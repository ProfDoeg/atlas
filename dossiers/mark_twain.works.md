## Works by Samuel Langhorne Clemens

### Long fiction and book-length narratives

- **1873:** *The Gilded Age: A Tale of Today*. A satirical novel co-made with Charles Dudley Warner; Clemens wrote roughly half of its alternating narrative strands. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1876:** *The Adventures of Tom Sawyer*. A novel of childhood, social performance, and adventure; its first publication was the 1876 British edition, followed by the American edition in December 1876. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1881–1882:** *The Prince and the Pauper: A Tale for Young People of All Ages*. A historical novel first issued in Canada in 1881 to secure copyright and in the United States in 1882; the differing dates belong to manifestations of one obra. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1884–1885:** *Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (Tom Sawyer’s Comrade)*. A novel published in Britain and Canada in 1884 and in the United States in 1885; the two dates should not be mistaken for two works. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1889:** *A Connecticut Yankee in King Arthur’s Court*. A time-travel novel and political-technological satire, published in Britain as *A Yankee at the Court of King Arthur*. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1892:** *The American Claimant*. A comic novel developed partly from Clemens’s earlier dramatic project *Colonel Sellers as a Scientist*. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1892–1893:** *Those Extraordinary Twins*. A comic narrative extracted from the manuscript that became *Pudd’nhead Wilson*; because Clemens split one developing project into two incompatible stories, it resists treatment as wholly independent in origin. [WMT]
- **1893–1894:** *The Tragedy of Pudd’nhead Wilson*. A novel first serialized in 1893–1894 and issued in a book together with *Those Extraordinary Twins* in 1894. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1894:** *Tom Sawyer Abroad*. A short novel narrated by Huck Finn and parodying technological adventure fiction. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1896:** *Personal Recollections of Joan of Arc, by the Sieur Louis de Conte*. A historical novel initially serialized anonymously and then published as a book under Clemens’s name. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1896:** *Tom Sawyer, Detective*. A novella or short novel based on a Scandinavian murder case and narrated by Huck Finn. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1906–1907:** *A Horse’s Tale*. A novella first serialized and then published as a book, combining animal narration with an anti-bullfighting argument. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]

### Travel books, memoirs, and argumentative books

- **1869:** *The Innocents Abroad; or, The New Pilgrims’ Progress*. A travel book remade from Clemens’s 1867 *Alta California* correspondence about the Quaker City excursion; it mixes reportage, comic performance, and retrospective reconstruction. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1872:** *Roughing It*. A semi-autobiographical travel narrative of Nevada, California, and Hawaiʻi whose episodes frequently transform experience into tall tale. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1875:** *Old Times on the Mississippi*. A sequence of autobiographical magazine chapters about learning to pilot the river; much of it was later absorbed and revised into *Life on the Mississippi*, so it is both an independent serial obra and material for another. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1877:** *Some Rambling Notes of an Idle Excursion*. A serialized travel narrative of a Bermuda journey, later collected in book form. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1880:** *A Tramp Abroad*. An illustrated European travel book combining journey narrative, burlesque, legend, essay, and invented episode. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1883:** *Life on the Mississippi*. A hybrid of memoir, history, travel writing, reportage, and recycled-and-revised *Old Times* material. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1897:** *Following the Equator: A Journey Around the World*. A travel book made from Clemens’s 1895–1896 lecture journey; its British manifestation bears the title *More Tramps Abroad*. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1907:** *Christian Science, with Notes Containing Corrections to Date*. A book-length polemic assembled from earlier articles and revised material; it is an argumentative intervention rather than neutral religious description. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]
- **1909:** *Is Shakespeare Dead? From My Autobiography*. A book-length anti-Stratfordian argument presented as autobiographical inquiry; its speculative attribution of Shakespeare’s works is Clemens’s position, not an accepted scholarly attribution. [Johnson; BAL; MBH]

### Story, sketch, and essay collections made or authorized as books

- **1867:** *The Celebrated Jumping Frog of Calaveras County, and Other Sketches*. Clemens’s first book collection; it is a designed book-object but also a manifestation gathering pieces that had earlier lives in newspapers and periodicals. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1871:** *Mark Twain’s (Burlesque) Autobiography and First Romance*. A slim composite book containing a mock autobiography and the unfinished burlesque romance *The House That Jack Built*. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1875:** *Mark Twain’s Sketches, New and Old*. An authorial collection of previously printed and new sketches; later editions altered its contents, so the title does not designate one perfectly stable textual object. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1877:** *A True Story, and the Recent Carnival of Crime*. A book pairing two previously published shorter works. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1878:** *Punch, Brothers, Punch! and Other Sketches*. A small collection issued as part of the Punch Library; bibliographers treat it as a distinct book manifestation rather than a newly composed unified work. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1882:** *The Stolen White Elephant, Etc.* A collection of stories and sketches, many previously printed. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1888:** *Mark Twain’s Library of Humor*. An anthology compiled with William Dean Howells and Charles Hopkins Clark, mixing Clemens’s selections and writings with work by many other authors; authorship of the container is therefore editorial and collaborative. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1892:** *Merry Tales*. A collection of six previously published humorous narratives. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1893:** *The £1,000,000 Bank-Note and Other New Stories*. A book collection headed by the title story; American and British manifestations differ in bibliographical detail. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1897:** *How to Tell a Story and Other Essays*. A collection whose title essay is part literary criticism, part comic demonstration, and part protocol for oral storytelling. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1900:** *The Man That Corrupted Hadleyburg and Other Stories and Essays*. A collection centered on the titular moral satire. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1903:** *My Début as a Literary Person, with Other Essays and Stories*. A collection and retrospective self-presentation, not a newly composed continuous book. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1906:** *The $30,000 Bequest and Other Stories*. Clemens’s last substantial lifetime story collection; most components had appeared previously. [Johnson; BAL]

### Individually made stories, sketches, satires, and manifestos

- **1852:** *The Dandy Frightening the Squatter*. An early comic sketch published under Clemens’s initials; it belongs to the juvenile newspaper writing established by the scholarly *Early Tales and Sketches*. [ETS]
- **1865:** *Advice for Good Little Girls*. A mock conduct-text whose form parodies the protocol while its action subverts it. [ETS]
- **1865:** *The Story of the Bad Little Boy Who Didn’t Come to Grief*. A burlesque of moralizing children’s literature. [ETS]
- **1865:** *Jim Smiley and His Jumping Frog*. The newspaper story later retitled *The Celebrated Jumping Frog of Calaveras County*; the changing title marks revision and republication, not a wholly separate narrative. [ETS; Johnson]
- **1868:** *Cannibalism in the Cars*. A political burlesque framed as an apparently factual railway conversation. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1868:** *General Washington’s Negro Body-Servant*. A satire of newspaper credulity and manufactured historical relics. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1868:** *My Late Senatorial Secretaryship*. A political sketch first printed in a newspaper. [Johnson]
- **1868:** *A Medieval Romance*. An intentionally unresolved or abandoned narrative whose narrator refuses to solve the plot; its incompleteness is part of the published joke, although its manuscript history also resists the category of finished story. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1870:** *A Ghost Story*. A comic supernatural tale concerning the Cardiff Giant hoax. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1874:** *A True Story, Repeated Word for Word as I Heard It*. A framed narrative based on Mary Ann Cord’s account of enslavement and family separation; Clemens made the literary frame and transcription, while the narrated life is Cord’s, making sole authorship ethically and formally insufficient. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1875:** *Some Learned Fables for Good Old Boys and Girls*. A sequence of scientific and institutional satires presented as animal fables. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1875:** *The Story of the Good Little Boy Who Did Not Prosper*. A companion burlesque to the earlier “bad little boy” story. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1876:** *A Literary Nightmare*. A comic sketch also known through its refrain-derived title *Punch, Brothers, Punch!*; the titles identify manifestations of substantially the same obra. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1876:** *A Murder, a Mystery, and a Marriage*. A story written for a proposed collaborative fiction game that never proceeded as planned; Clemens withheld it after another author used a similar plot, and it was not fully published until long after his death. [MTP-JF]
- **1876:** *The Canvasser’s Tale*. A comic tale of an obsessive collector. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1877:** *The Invalid’s Story*. A grotesque railway story written in 1877 but withheld from *A Tramp Abroad* and first published in 1882. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1879:** *The Great Revolution in Pitcairn*. A mock history and political fable. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1880:** *1601: Conversation, as It Was by the Social Fireside, in the Time of the Tudors*. A privately printed scatological pastiche; early printings were anonymous or pseudonymous, but Clemens’s authorship is established. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1882:** *The McWilliamses and the Burglar Alarm*. A domestic-technological sketch. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1882:** *The Stolen White Elephant*. A detective-story burlesque. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1882:** *On the Decay of the Art of Lying*. An essay originating as a paper for the Historical and Antiquarian Club of Hartford; it behaves partly as a mock manifesto defending cultivated lying. [BAL; WMT]
- **1885:** *The Private History of a Campaign That Failed*. A semi-autobiographical Civil War narrative whose comic memoir form does not erase its revision of Clemens’s brief Confederate service. [BAL; MTP-CW]
- **1891:** *Luck*. A short story about reputation, accident, and retrospective mythmaking. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1893:** *Is He Living or Is He Dead?*. A story about artists manufacturing value through a staged death. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1893:** *The £1,000,000 Bank-Note*. A comic economic fantasy first published as the leading work in a collection. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1895:** *Fenimore Cooper’s Literary Offenses*. A polemical comic essay that invents rules of narrative craft and applies them as a prosecutorial protocol. [BAL; LOA]
- **1898:** *Concerning the Jews*. An essay opposing antisemitic stereotypes while reproducing some generalizations of its period; its argument and its limitations both belong to the object. [BAL; LOA]
- **1899:** *My First Lie, and How I Got Out of It*. An autobiographical-political essay on collective falsehood. [BAL; LOA]
- **1899:** *The Man That Corrupted Hadleyburg*. A long story or novella about civic reputation and orchestrated moral collapse. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1900:** *A Salutation-Speech from the Nineteenth Century to the Twentieth*. An anti-imperialist address written for publication; it acts as a satirical indictment and therefore approaches manifesto. [LOA]
- **1901:** *To the Person Sitting in Darkness*. An anti-imperialist satire attacking missionary rhetoric and colonial violence; it is an intervention intended to act in public debate. [Johnson; BAL; LOA]
- **1901:** *To My Missionary Critics*. A documentary rebuttal extending the argument of *To the Person Sitting in Darkness*. [BAL; LOA]
- **1901:** *Edmund Burke on Croker and Tammany*. A political satire aimed at New York machine government. [BAL; LOA]
- **1901:** *The Battle Hymn of the Republic, Updated*. A satirical anti-imperialist lyric made by rewriting the structure of an existing song; it was not published in Clemens’s lifetime. [LOA]
- **1901:** *The United States of Lyncherdom*. An anti-lynching essay withheld during Clemens’s life and first published posthumously in 1923; its suppression is part of its status as an unrealized public intervention. [LOA]
- **1902:** *A Double-Barrelled Detective Story*. A novella-length parody that collides frontier melodrama with Sherlock Holmes fiction. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1903:** *A Dog’s Tale*. An antivivisection story narrated by a dog and later issued separately as a book. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1904:** *Extracts from Adam’s Diary, Translated from the Original MS.* A comic pseudo-translation and illustrated book developed from earlier Adam writings. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1904–1905:** *King Leopold’s Soliloquy: A Defense of His Congo Rule*. A pamphlet satire against atrocities in the Congo Free State; it is a manifesto-like political weapon voiced through an incriminating dramatic monologue. [Johnson; BAL; LOA]
- **1905:** *The War Prayer*. An antiwar prose poem withheld after magazine rejection and published posthumously; it is simultaneously parable, prayer, and counter-manifesto. [LOA]
- **1905–1906:** *Eve’s Diary*. A fictional diary first serialized and then issued as an illustrated book; its memorial relation to Olivia Clemens complicates its apparently comic form. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1906:** *What Is Man?*. A philosophical dialogue privately printed in 1906 and anonymously issued; it advances Clemens’s determinism through a teacher-pupil form rather than conventional exposition. [Johnson; BAL; WMT]
- **1908:** *Little Bessie*. A sequence of unfinished philosophical dialogues, also called *Little Bessie Would Assist Providence*; it is a manuscript project rather than a completed poem or book. [WMT]
- **1909:** *A Fable*. A short allegory about representation, mirrors, and collective judgment. [BAL]
- **1909:** *Extract from Captain Stormfield’s Visit to Heaven*. A book manifestation of part of a much older, repeatedly revised narrative project; “Extract” accurately signals that the larger work remained unfinished. [Johnson; BAL; FM]
- **written 1898; published 1912:** *My Platonic Sweetheart*. A dream narrative published posthumously. [BAL; LOA]

### Plays and dramatic projects

- **1874:** *Colonel Sellers*. A stage comedy made with Gilbert B. Densmore from Clemens and Warner’s *The Gilded Age*; the collaboration and adaptation make it a distinct dramatic obra, not merely the novel in another binding. [MDW]
- **1876–1877:** *Ah Sin*. A melodrama co-written with Bret Harte for actor Charles T. Parsloe; the surviving and performed play is collaborative, and scholarship disputes the partners’ precise shares. [MBH; MDW]
- **1878:** *Simon Wheeler, Detective*. An unfinished comedy built around the Jumping Frog narrator; surviving drafts make it a dramatic project rather than a completed play. [MDW]
- **1883–1884:** *Colonel Sellers as a Scientist*. An unsuccessful dramatic project subsequently cannibalized for *The American Claimant*. [MDW]
- **1884:** *The Prince and the Pauper* dramatization. Clemens prepared a stage version, but Abby Sage Richardson extensively revised the playable script; attribution of the produced drama is therefore collaborative and textually unstable. [MDW]
- **1887–1888:** *The American Claimant* dramatic versions. Successive scripts and revisions form a project leading toward, but not identical with, the 1892 novel. [MDW]
- **1890:** *The Death Wafer*. A short play derived from Clemens’s story *The Great Dark* or related death-disk material; its manuscript and production histories are incomplete. [MDW]
- **1895:** *Pudd’nhead Wilson* dramatization. Clemens collaborated with actor Frank Mayo, whose stage adaptation supplied much of the performed form; it should not be assigned to Clemens alone. [MDW]
- **1898:** *Is He Dead?*. A completed farce about a painter who profits by disguising himself and faking his death; unproduced in Clemens’s lifetime, it was first published in 2003 and staged in 2007. [MTP-JF]
- **1898:** *Quaker City Holy Land Excursion*. An unfinished play project based on the journey behind *The Innocents Abroad*. [MDW]

### Unfinished narrative and philosophical projects

- **c. 1884:** *Huck Finn and Tom Sawyer Among the Indians*. Nine surviving chapters of an abandoned sequel; it is a project whose incompleteness is constitutive, not a damaged finished novel. [HHT]
- **c. 1890:** *The Great Dark*. An unfinished dream narrative surviving in variant manuscript states. [WDT]
- **1897:** *Huck Finn*. A short sequel fragment, distinct from *Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* and never developed into a completed work. [HHT]
- **1897–1900:** *The Chronicle of Young Satan*. The first extended “Mysterious Stranger” manuscript, left unfinished. [MSM]
- **1898:** *Schoolhouse Hill*. An unfinished six-chapter Tom-and-Huck narrative that turns toward a supernatural stranger; editors associate it with the Mysterious Stranger project. [MSM; HHT]
- **c. 1899:** *Tom Sawyer’s Conspiracy*. An unfinished ten-chapter sequel project. [HHT]
- **c. 1900:** *Tom Sawyer’s Gang Plans a Naval Battle*. A brief unfinished fragment. [HHT]
- **1902–1908:** *No. 44, The Mysterious Stranger*. The longest and latest major Mysterious Stranger manuscript, repeatedly revised but not completed for publication. [MSM]
- **c. 1904–1905:** *Three Thousand Years Among the Microbes*. An unfinished satirical fantasy narrated by a human transformed into a cholera germ. [FM]
- **1905–1909:** *Letters from the Earth*. A group of related letters and papers rather than a lifetime-authorized book; Bernard DeVoto’s 1962 publication constructed a posthumous collection from Clemens’s manuscripts. [FM; MTP]
- **various dates, chiefly the later years:** *Fables of Man*. A body of fables, dialogues, philosophical tales, and fragments assembled posthumously by scholarly editors; the collection is an editorial manifestation, not a book Clemens finished or titled as such. [FM]
- **various dates:** *Which Was the Dream?* and related symbolic writings. A scholarly grouping of dream narratives and fragments; the title names one manuscript and, editorially, a larger unstable field of projects. [WDT]
- **1897–1908:** *The Mysterious Stranger* manuscripts. This is a project-family rather than a single finished obra: *The Chronicle of Young Satan*, *Schoolhouse Hill*, and *No. 44* remain separate unfinished attempts. The 1916 *Mysterious Stranger: A Romance* was constructed by Albert Bigelow Paine and Frederick A. Duneka through conflation, alteration, and newly supplied transitions, so it resists attribution to Clemens as a finished work. [MSM]

### Autobiographical project, notebooks, letters, and journalism

- **1870–1871:** *Memoranda*. Clemens’s signed monthly department for *The Galaxy*, comprising essays, sketches, notices, and editorial performances; it is a serial body of work rather than one stable text. [Johnson; LOA]
- **1877–1883 and other years:** *Notebooks and journals*. Working notebooks containing observations, travel records, inventions, accounts, drafts, and project seeds; they were not conceived as a single public work, and the scholarly edition preserves that resistance to unity. [MNJ]
- **1866:** *Sandwich Islands letters*. Newspaper correspondence from Hawaiʻi, later selectively republished as *Letters from Hawaii*; the posthumous book is an editorial manifestation of the original journalistic series. [Johnson; ETS]
- **1867:** *Quaker City correspondence*. Travel letters written for the *Alta California* and the *New York Tribune*, later transformed into *The Innocents Abroad*; the letters remain a distinct serial journalistic obra. [Johnson; BAL]
- **1851–1910:** *Tales, sketches, speeches, and essays*. Hundreds of separately published or delivered pieces constitute a dispersed body of work rather than a single title; the authoritative scholarly collections divide it into 1851–1890 and 1891–1910, while *Early Tales and Sketches* establishes the early texts. Individual attribution and dating sometimes remain editorial problems. [ETS; LOA]
- **1853–1910:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of outgoing letters form a documentary corpus rather than a work Clemens designed for publication; the Mark Twain Project catalogs known correspondence and provides critically edited texts, with more than 2,000 letters available online. [MTP-L]
- **1870–1909:** *Autobiography of Mark Twain*. A decades-long project made through experiments in written reminiscence and, especially, free-form dictation; Clemens never assembled it into a final book. Paine’s 1924 edition and other selections are editorial constructions, while the Mark Twain Project’s three-volume edition presents the surviving autobiographical materials in an authoritative sequence without pretending that Clemens completed a conventional autobiography. [AMT; MTP]
- **1906–1907:** *Chapters from My Autobiography*. Twenty-five installments selected and authorized by Clemens for the *North American Review*; they are a lifetime manifestation of the larger, unfinished autobiographical project. [AMT]

### Lectures, speeches, and performed texts

- **1866:** *Sandwich Islands lecture*. Clemens’s first major public lecture, developed from his Hawaiʻi reporting; the performed event survives through texts and reports but was repeatedly altered in delivery. [MTSp]
- **1868–1869:** *The American Vandal Abroad*. A lecture derived from the Quaker City journey and performed in numerous changing versions; it is best understood as a repeatable performance score, not a single fixed text. [MTSp]
- **1871–1872:** *Roughing It lecture*. A touring comic lecture related to, but not identical with, the book. [MTSp]
- **1873:** *Our Fellow Savages of the Sandwich Islands*. A revised Hawaiʻi lecture whose wording varied between performances. [MTSp]
- **1879:** *Some Thoughts on the Science of Onanism*. A privately delivered burlesque address to the Stomach Club; it is a performed comic provocation rather than a public scientific essay. [MTSp]
- **1882:** *Advice to Youth*. A humorous address that imitates and overturns the protocol of moral instruction. [MTSp; LOA]
- **1884–1885:** *Twins of Genius tour performances*. A collaborative lecture-and-reading program made and performed with George Washington Cable; the program is a performance project rather than a Clemens text alone. [MTSp]
- **1895–1896:** *Round-the-world lecture tour program*. A changing assemblage of readings, stories, and remarks performed to restore Clemens’s finances; it has no single definitive script and therefore resists classification as one finished literary work. [MTSp]
- **1906:** *The Horrors of the German Language*. A comic German-language address related to, but distinct in occasion and performance from, the earlier appendix “The Awful German Language.” [MTSp]
- **1910:** *Mark Twain’s Speeches*. A posthumously issued collection of recorded addresses; the volume is an editorial manifestation and cannot stand for the many lectures and extemporaneous remarks that were never recorded. [Johnson; MTSp]

### Patented devices and commercial designs

- **1871:** *Improvement in Adjustable and Detachable Straps for Garments* (U.S. Patent 121,992). A patented garment-fastening device devised under the name Samuel L. Clemens; it is a functional protocol embodied in hardware, not a literary text. [USPTO; Smithsonian]
- **1873:** *Mark Twain’s Patent Self-Pasting Scrap Book* (U.S. Patent 140,245). A scrapbook system with adhesive-coated pages activated by moisture; Clemens patented the mechanism and commercialized the designed object. [USPTO; Smithsonian]
- **1885:** *Memory-Builder: A Game for Acquiring and Retaining All Sorts of Facts and Dates* (U.S. Patent 324,535). A history-learning game using a board or chart, pegs, and chronological play; it is simultaneously educational protocol, game system, and manufactured object. [USPTO; Smithsonian]
