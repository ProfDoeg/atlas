## Works by Annie Besant

### Freethought, secularist, scientific, and social-reform texts

- **1870:** *Notes on Fasting, by a Layman*. Privately printed essay, composed in 1866 as *A Paper on the Duty of Fasting*; its survival and exact publication status are uncertain.
- **1873:** *On the Deity of Jesus of Nazareth*. Anonymous critical pamphlet, signed “By the Wife of a Beneficed Clergyman” and edited by Charles Voysey.
- **1873:** *A Comparison between the Fourth Gospel and the Three Synoptics*. The second part of Besant’s inquiry into Jesus, originally issued under the heading “According to St. John.”
- **1874:** *Civil and Religious Liberty*. A lecture-pamphlet using the French Revolution to argue for liberty.
- **1874:** *Liberty, Equality, Fraternity*. A political pamphlet.
- **1874:** *Natural Religion versus Revealed Religion*. A freethought theological critique.
- **1874:** *On the Atonement*. A theological critique.
- **1874:** *On Eternal Torture*. A theological critique later incorporated into *My Path to Atheism*.
- **1874:** *On Inspiration*. An anonymously published critique of scriptural inspiration.
- **1874:** *On the Political Status of Women*. A lecture-pamphlet advocating women’s political rights.
- **1874:** *On the Religious Education of Children*. A polemical pamphlet.
- **1875:** *Catholicism and Rationalism*. A review of a public debate, supplemented by Besant’s essay comparing secularism and Catholicism.
- **1875:** *Essays by Mrs. Besant*. A publisher-bound gathering of pamphlets rather than a newly composed unified book.
- **1875:** *Euthanasia*. An anonymously issued manifesto-like argument for legal medically administered euthanasia.
- **1875:** *On the Mediation and Salvation of Ecclesiastical Christianity*. An anonymous theological critique.
- **1875:** *On the Nature and Existence of God*. An anonymous philosophical pamphlet.
- **1875:** *On Prayer*. An anonymous freethought pamphlet.
- **1876:** *The Beauties of the Prayer-Book*. A three-part ironic critique of the Church of England’s liturgy.
- **1876:** *Constructive Rationalism*. An anonymously published rationalist pamphlet.
- **1876:** *Christianity*. Besant’s contribution to *The Freethinker’s Text-book*, made collaboratively with Charles Bradlaugh and Charles Watts.
- **1876–1883:** *History of the Great French Revolution*. A fourteen-lecture historical project: six lectures appeared in 1876 and eight continuations in 1883; later one-volume issues are manifestations of that earlier project.
- **1876:** *The Secular Song and Hymn Book*. An edited communal songbook containing Besant’s preface and four hymns; authorship of the whole collection is therefore not hers.
- **1877:** *Giordano Bruno*. A biographical freethought pamphlet.
- **1877:** *The Gospel of Christianity, and the Gospel of Freethought*. A comparative polemical lecture-text.
- **1877:** *Landlords, Tenant Farmers, and Labourers*. Political articles first signed “Ajax,” then issued as a pamphlet.
- **1877:** *English Republicanism*. A republican political pamphlet.
- **1877:** *The Fruits of Philosophy*. Besant and Charles Bradlaugh republished Charles Knowlton’s contraceptive tract with their own comments; it is an editorial-political act and the object of their obscenity prosecution, not an original book by Besant.
- **1877:** *The Gospel of Atheism*. A lecture-pamphlet.
- **1877:** *Is the Bible Indictable?* A legal and secularist polemic applying contemporary obscenity doctrine to the Bible.
- **1877:** *The Law of Population*. A birth-control manifesto assembled from articles in the *National Reformer*.
- **1877:** *The Queen versus Charles Bradlaugh and Annie Besant*. A specially reported trial record made with Bradlaugh; it documents their proceeding rather than functioning as a solely authored work.
- **1878:** *Christian Progress*. A freethought pamphlet.
- **1878:** *The Fruits of Christianity*. A National Secular Society tract.
- **1878:** *Marriage As It Was, As It Is, and As It Should Be*. A serialized essay turned reform pamphlet.
- **1878:** *My Path to Atheism*. A collection and reconfiguration of sixteen earlier pamphlets rather than sixteen newly made texts.
- **1879:** *England, India, Afghanistan*. An anti-imperial political pamphlet.
- **1879:** *The Story of Afghanistan*. A political pamphlet assembled from *National Reformer* articles.
- **1880:** *The English Land System*. A political pamphlet made from two newspaper articles.
- **1880:** *The Ethics of Punishment*. A reform pamphlet.
- **1880:** *The Jesus of the Gospels and the Influence of Christianity*. A verbatim debate record involving Besant and the Rev. A. Hatchard; dialogic rather than solely authored.
- **1880:** *Law Breakers and Law Makers*. A political leaflet known through later quotation; its independent bibliographical survival is uncertain.
- **1880:** *The Social Aspects of Malthusianism*. A birth-control and social-policy pamphlet.
- **1880:** *We Seek for Truth*. An address whose text is known through Besant’s later autobiography; it resists treatment as a securely surviving independent work.
- **1881:** *Free Trade versus “Fair Trade”*. Five separately published lectures bound as one economic work.
- **1881:** *God’s Views on Marriage, As Revealed in the Old Testament*. A polemical response to claims about freethought and “free love.”
- **1881:** *Henry Varley Exposed*. A political rebuttal defending Charles Bradlaugh.
- **1881:** *Light, Heat, and Sound*. A scientific examination manual.
- **1881:** *The Transvaal*. An anti-imperial political pamphlet.
- **1881:** *Vivisection*. An antivivisection pamphlet.
- **1882:** *Blasphemy*. A secularist pamphlet.
- **1882:** *Coercion in Ireland and Its Results*. A political plea made from a *National Reformer* article.
- **1882:** *Egypt: A Criticism of British Policy, 1875–82*. An anti-imperial pamphlet made from newspaper articles.
- **1882:** *Electricity*. A four-lecture scientific sequence issued in separate parts.
- **1882:** *Eyes and Ears: Six Chats on Seeing and Hearing*. A popular-science lecture series.
- **1882:** *Force No Remedy*. A political intervention prompted by the Phoenix Park murders.
- **1882:** *Physiology of the Home*. Four separately issued scientific lectures—*Digestion*, *Organs of Digestion*, *Circulation*, and *Respiration*—bound as a course.
- **1882:** *The True Basis of Morality*. A secular ethical pamphlet.
- **1883:** *The Argument from Design*. An unpublished lecture known through a contemporary discussion; the performance existed, but no independent Besant publication is established.
- **1883–1884:** *The Christian Creed; or, What It Is Blasphemy to Deny*. A two-part secularist work, initially issued in part and then complete.
- **1883:** *Christianity: Its Evidences, Its Origin, Its Morality, Its History*. A systematic freethought critique.
- **1884:** *Biblical Biology*. A satire subtitled “A Contribution to Religious Non-science.”
- **1884:** *The Myth of the Resurrection*. A lecture-pamphlet also incorporated into the collaborative series *The Atheistic Platform*.
- **1884:** *The Story of the Soudan*. A political lecture-pamphlet also incorporated into *The Atheistic Platform*.
- **1884:** *What Is the Use of Prayer?* A lecture-pamphlet also incorporated into *The Atheistic Platform*.
- **1884:** *Why Should Atheists Be Persecuted?* A civil-liberties manifesto also incorporated into *The Atheistic Platform*.
- **1885:** *August Comte: His Philosophy, His Religion, and His Sociology*. An intellectual-biographical study.
- **1885:** *Autobiographical Sketches*. Autobiographical articles from *Our Corner* reconstituted as a book.
- **1885:** *Gordon Judged out of His Own Mouth*. A political pamphlet made from two *National Reformer* articles.
- **1885:** *Is Christianity a Success?* A freethought pamphlet.
- **1885:** *The Legalisation of Female Slavery in England*. A feminist polemic first published as a newspaper article in 1876.
- **1885:** *Legends and Tales*. A collection of nine retold myths and historical narratives, including earlier pieces on Hypatia and Giordano Bruno.
- **1885:** *The Natural History of the Christian Devil*. A historical-religious critique.
- **1885:** *The Redistribution of Political Power*. A political essay first printed in *Our Corner*.
- **1885:** *Sin and Crime*. An ethical and penal-reform pamphlet.
- **1885:** *Woman’s Position According to the Bible*. A feminist critique of scripture.
- **1885:** *A World without God*. A reply to Frances Power Cobbe.
- **1886:** *Disestablish the Church; or, The Sins of the Church*. A composite political-religious campaign text whose constituent pamphlets also circulated separately.
- **1886:** *Why I Am a Socialist*. A political manifesto.
- **1887:** *Essays on Socialism*. A collaborative Fabian-era collection containing Besant’s work; it is not wholly hers.
- **1888:** *The Political Status of Women*. A revised manifestation of her women’s-rights argument.
- **1888:** *The Trades Union Movement*. A socialist account of organized labour.
- **1889:** *Industry under Socialism*. Besant’s contribution to the collaborative manifesto-volume *Fabian Essays in Socialism*.
- **1889:** *The Case for India*. A political pamphlet.
- **1890:** *Why I Became a Theosophist*. A conversion narrative and public declaration.
- **1891:** *The Political Status of Women*. A substantially revised edition; whether to count it as a new obra or a new manifestation of the 1874 project is interpretive.

### Translations

- **1875:** *The Legend of St. Christopher*. A translation from French.
- **1875:** *The Seven Sacraments*. A translation from French.
- **1876:** *The Christian Martyrs*. A translation from French.
- **1876:** *The Religious Revolution in the Nineteenth Century*. A translation from German.
- **1881:** *The Life and Writings of M. W. de Wette*. A translation from German; the bibliographical record treats these five translations as Besant’s work, though their source editions require separate textual study.

### Autobiographical and biographical works

- **1885:** *Autobiographical Sketches*. An episodic self-portrait first made serially; it overlaps with, but is not simply an edition of, her later autobiography.
- **1893:** *An Autobiography*. A book-length retrospective narrative.
- **1891:** *As I Knew Her*. Besant’s memorial essay on H. P. Blavatsky, contributed to the collaborative volume *H. P. B.: In Memory of Helena Petrovna Blavatsky*.
- **1893:** *The Theosophical Society and H. P. B.* A memorial-defense text first published in 1890 and subsequently manifested as article, pamphlet, and contribution.
- **1929:** *Annie Besant: An Autobiography—Illustrated*. An illustrated manifestation of the autobiography rather than an entirely new work.
- **1933:** *The Future of the Theosophical Society*. A late address subsequently incorporated into a convention volume.
- **Posthumous, various dates:** *Letters of Annie Besant*. Edited selections from correspondence are manifestations made partly by later editors; the letters themselves are Besant’s, but the titled collections are not wholly her constructions.

### Theosophical manuals, treatises, and collaborative investigations

- **1892:** *The Seven Principles of Man*. The first Theosophical Manual, initially serialized in *Lucifer*.
- **1893:** *Reincarnation*. The second Theosophical Manual, initially serialized.
- **1893:** *Death—and After?* The third Theosophical Manual, initially serialized.
- **1895:** *Karma*. The fourth Theosophical Manual, initially serialized.
- **1896:** *Man and His Bodies*. The seventh Theosophical Manual, initially serialized.
- **1897:** *The Ancient Wisdom*. A systematic exposition of Besant’s mature theosophical cosmology.
- **1901:** *Esoteric Christianity; or, The Lesser Mysteries*. A treatise distinct from her 1898 lecture series titled *Esoteric Christianity*.
- **1903:** *Theosophy and the New Psychology*. A psychological-theosophical study.
- **1904:** *A Study in Consciousness*. A speculative psychology.
- **1905:** *Thought-Forms*. An illustrated collaborative investigation with C. W. Leadbeater; its plates were executed by several artists, so the book is a small Gesamtkunstwerk of clairvoyant claim, diagram, colour theory, and image.
- **1907:** *Occult Chemistry*. A collaborative clairvoyant investigation with C. W. Leadbeater. Later editions expanded and rearranged it, complicating the boundary between original work and subsequent manifestation.
- **1908:** *An Introduction to Yoga*. An instructional lecture-book—a protocol for spiritual practice as well as an exposition.
- **1908:** *Thought Power: Its Control and Culture*. A practical psychological-spiritual protocol.
- **1910:** *The Universal Text Book of Religion and Morals, Part I*. A comparative-religion textbook assembled around scriptural examples.
- **1911:** *The Universal Text Book of Religion and Morals, Part II: Ethics*. The ethical continuation of the textbook project.
- **1913:** *Investigations into the Super-Physical*. A record of claimed occult investigations, made with C. W. Leadbeater.
- **1915:** *The Universal Text Book of Religion and Morals, Part III, vol. 1: Hinduism*. A revision of material from the 1904 *Advanced Textbook*; no further instalments appeared, leaving the projected series unfinished.
- **1919:** *Man’s Life in Three Worlds*. An introductory theosophical book first serialized in the *Adyar Bulletin*.
- **1921:** *Talks with a Class*. A book assembled from previously serialized instructional talks.
- **1924:** *The Lives of Alcyone*. A collaborative occult biography with C. W. Leadbeater; its claims and division of authorship are inseparable from their shared clairvoyant project.
- **1924:** *In the World’s Service*. A pseudonymous and collaborative collection whose contributors include Besant and Leadbeater; several pieces conceal authorship behind devotional personae.
- **1926:** *The Masters*. A theosophical study.
- **1927:** *The Inner Government of the World*. A theosophical lecture-book.
- **1931:** *Man’s Life in This and Other Worlds*. A late synthetic theosophical work.
- **1934:** *The Origin of the Aryan Race*. Published after Besant’s death; the bibliography places it among her theosophical books, but posthumous editorial intervention makes its final form a disputed manifestation.

### Theosophical convention lecture-works

- **1894:** *The Building of the Kosmos and Other Lectures*. Four convention lectures made into a book.
- **1895:** *The Self and Its Sheaths*. A four-lecture convention sequence.
- **1896:** *The Path of Discipleship*. A four-lecture instructional sequence.
- **1897:** *Four Great Religions*. Four comparative-religion lectures, later absorbed into *Seven Great Religions*.
- **1899:** *Evolution of Life and Form*. Four convention lectures.
- **1900:** *Avataras*. Four lectures on divine incarnation.
- **1901:** *Ancient Ideals in Modern Life*. Four lectures applying Hindu social ideals to modern life.
- **1902:** *The Religious Problem in India*. Four lectures on Islam, Jainism, Sikhism, and Theosophy.
- **1903:** *The Three Great Religions*. A comparative-religion convention sequence.
- **1904:** *The Pedigree of Man*. Four lectures combining spiritual, biological, and racial-evolutionary claims.
- **1905:** *A Study in Karma*. A convention lecture-book distinct from the shorter 1895 manual *Karma*.
- **1906:** *Theosophical Lectures*. A convention sequence.
- **1907:** *The Wisdom of the Upanishats*. A convention lecture-book.
- **1908:** *The Changing World*. A convention series later supplemented with an index.
- **1909:** *The Science of the Emotions*. A psychological-theosophical lecture project.
- **1910:** *The Path of Initiation*. An instructional convention sequence.
- **1911:** *The Laws of the Higher Life*. A spiritual protocol in lecture form.
- **1912:** *Theosophy and the New Psychology*. A convention manifestation related to Besant’s earlier work under the same title.
- **1913:** *Theosophy and the Theosophical Society*. Four lectures defining both a doctrine and an institution.
- **1913:** *Indian Social Reform*. An intended four-lecture convention work of which three lectures remained unpublished; it survives as an incomplete project rather than a stable book.
- **1916:** *Theosophy and Life’s Deeper Problems*. Four lectures on God, humanity, ethics, and brotherhood.
- **1917:** *Duties of the Theosophist*. Three lectures that function as an institutional and ethical protocol.
- **1919:** *Problems of Reconstruction*. Four lectures on religious, social, political, and educational reconstruction.
- **1919:** *The Duty of the Theosophical Society*. An unpublished convention address; the performed text is the work, though it resists bibliographical treatment as a publication.
- **1921:** *The Great Plan*. Four lectures describing cosmic evolution.
- **1931:** *The Future of the Theosophical Society*. Besant supplied one lecture to a multi-author convention volume; the whole book is not hers.

### Other lecture cycles and spoken projects

- **1893:** *Theosophical Congress at the Parliament of Religions*. Besant made six lectures within this collaborative congress record: *Theosophy as a System of Truths*, *Karma*, *Theosophy and Modern Social Problems*, *Theosophy and Ethics*, *The Evolution of Man*, and *The Supreme Duty*.
- **1894:** *Melbourne Lectures*. Five lectures survive as an unpublished typescript; this is a performed and archival project rather than a published book.
- **1895:** *The Birth and Evolution of the Soul*. A two-part lecture.
- **1895:** *In the Outer Court*. Five lectures forming a spiritual-training protocol.
- **1897:** *The Three Paths to Union with God*. Three Benares lectures.
- **1898:** *Esoteric Christianity*. A five-lecture sequence distinct from the 1901 book *Esoteric Christianity; or, The Lesser Mysteries*.
- **1909:** *The Changing World*. A fifteen-lecture international sequence whose individual occasions matter as much as its later book manifestation.
- **1910:** *Popular Lectures on Theosophy*. Six introductory lectures simultaneously made available as individual pamphlets.
- **1911:** *The Immediate Future*. A multi-venue lecture series on physical, religious, social, and political change.
- **1927:** *The New Civilisation*. Four Queen’s Hall lectures on religion, education, and economics.
- **1928:** *An Empire of Force, or a Federation of Free Peoples*. Four unpublished Queen’s Hall lectures; an unrealized book but a completed spoken project.
- **1929:** *Queen’s Hall lectures on death and rebirth*. Four lectures without a securely established collective title; three were later serialized, while the cycle as a whole remained unpublished in book form.
- **1929:** *Five Sermonettes*. Five addresses delivered in a Liberal Catholic church and published as a devotional sequence.
- **1930:** *Theosophical World Congress Lectures*. Two Chicago lectures published together.

### Hindu educational and interpretive works

- **1895:** *The Bhagavad-Gita, or the Lord’s Song*. Besant’s English translation.
- **1899:** *The Story of the Great War*. A school text retelling the *Mahabharata* for Hindu students.
- **1901:** *Sanatana Dharma: An Elementary Textbook of Hindu Religion and Ethics*. A collaborative textbook with Bhagavan Das.
- **1901:** *Shri Ramachandra: The Ideal King*. A school text drawing lessons from the *Ramayana*.
- **1903:** *Sanatana Dharma: Catechism for Boys and Girls in Hindu Religion and Ethics*. A collaborative instructional protocol with Bhagavan Das.
- **1904:** *Hindu Ideals*. Six lectures adapted for students.
- **1904:** *Sanatana Dharma: An Advanced Textbook of Hindu Religion and Ethics*. A collaborative textbook with Bhagavan Das, combining doctrine, ritual instruction, and ethics.
- **1909:** *The Bhagavad-Gita*. A translation accompanied by commentary.
- **1913:** *Wake Up, India!*. A collection of social-reform lectures functioning as a manifesto.
- **1932:** *Hindu Reform on National Lines*. A late social-religious reform argument.

### Indian political, historical, and educational works

- **1914:** *India and the Empire*. A collection of political speeches and reports rather than a continuously composed book.
- **1915:** *A Bird’s-Eye View of India’s Past as the Foundation for India’s Future*. A historical-political study repeatedly revised in 1917, 1923, and 1930.
- **1915:** *How India Wrought for Freedom*. A history of the Indian National Congress constructed from its official records.
- **1915:** *India: A Nation*. A manifesto for Indian self-government; British authorities prohibited its circulation in India.
- **1917:** *The Birth of New India*. A large retrospective collection of political, educational, social, and religious writings and speeches; it is an assembled political oeuvre as much as a single book.
- **1917:** *The Case for India*. A political advocacy text.
- **1917:** *India’s Rights*. A political manifesto.
- **1917:** *Congress Speeches*. A manifestation of public oratory rather than a separately composed book.
- **1918:** *India and the War*. A political intervention.
- **1918:** *The Commonweal*. A collection associated with Besant’s political journalism; its boundaries overlap the periodical of the same title.
- **1919:** *The Future of Indian Politics*. A political analysis.
- **1922:** *The Indian Problem*. A political study.
- **1925:** *Indian Art*. A cultural-nationalist study.
- **1926:** *India: Bond or Free?* A sustained argument for self-government.
- **1929:** *The Indian Village System*. A collaborative study with A. Ranganatham.
- **1932:** *India’s Struggle to Achieve Dominion Status*. A political manuscript issued in facsimile.
- **1932:** *Principles of Education, with a Scheme Applying Them to National Education in India*. An educational protocol combining general principles with an institutional plan.

### Periodicals as editorial obra

- **1878–1889:** *The National Secular Society’s Almanack*. Besant co-edited an existing annual with Charles Bradlaugh; she did not found it.
- **1881–1888:** *The National Reformer*. Besant co-edited this established weekly with Bradlaugh.
- **1883–1888:** *Our Corner*. A weekly founded and edited by Besant; the periodical as an ongoing forum is one of her clearest editorial obras.
- **1888:** *The Link*. A weekly founded and co-edited by Besant and W. T. Stead; it lasted from February through December.
- **1890–1897:** *Lucifer*. Besant first co-edited Blavatsky’s monthly, then edited it alone and with G. R. S. Mead; she inherited rather than created the journal.
- **1897–1907:** *The Theosophical Review*. The renamed successor to *Lucifer*, co-edited by Besant and Mead; whether it is a new periodical or a changed manifestation of *Lucifer* is bibliographically debatable.
- **1900–at least 1919:** *Central Hindu College Magazine*. An institutional journal associated with the college Besant founded; the bibliography records it among her edited periodicals but does not establish that every issue was personally edited by her.
- **1907–1933:** *The Theosophist*. Besant edited the established monthly from Olcott’s death until her own.
- **1908–1929:** *The Adyar Bulletin*. A monthly founded and edited by Besant, later merged into *The Theosophist*.
- **1908–1912:** *The Link*. A quarterly Esoteric School newsletter founded and edited by Besant; it is distinct from her 1888 weekly of the same title.
- **1914–1933:** *New India*. Besant transformed the purchased *Madras Standard* into a political daily; the paper is an editorial and activist obra, not a solely authored text.
- **1914–1920:** *The Commonweal*. A political weekly founded and edited by Besant.
- **1917–1933:** *Young India*. An editorial association reported by the bibliography; it should not be confused with Gandhi’s better-known periodical, and the precise limits of Besant’s control require issue-level verification.
- **1929–1933:** *The Adyar Theosophist*. The merged successor to *The Adyar Bulletin* and *The Theosophist*; institutionally new but materially continuous with both.
- **1930:** *The Hollywood Theosophist*. Besant contributed a ten-article sequence, but did not make the periodical as a whole.

### Selected articles, pamphlet manifestations, and resistant corpora

- **1889:** *Practical Work for Theosophists*. An essay in *Theosophical Siftings*, also circulated with another author’s text.
- **1893:** *A Word on Man, His Nature and His Powers*. A shipboard lecture subsequently manifested as journal article, collected essay, and pamphlet.
- **1893:** *Theosophy as a System of Truths Discoverable and Verifiable by Perfected Men*. A congress lecture also issued as a pamphlet.
- **1893:** *Karma, the Law of Causation, of Justice, and the Adjustment of Effects*. A congress lecture also issued separately.
- **1893:** *Theosophy and Modern Social Problems*. A congress lecture and pamphlet.
- **1893:** *Theosophy and Ethics*. A congress lecture repeatedly recollected under later titles.
- **1893:** *The Evolution of Man*. A congress lecture later absorbed into *Essays and Addresses*.
- **1893:** *The Supreme Duty*. A congress lecture later incorporated into several collections.
- **1904:** *Answers in the Vahan*. Besant’s responses form part of Sarah Corbett’s edited *Extracts from the Vahan*; the question-and-answer corpus is collaborative and dispersed.
- **1906:** *Occultism and Occult Training*. A congress paper accompanied by Besant’s presidential address.
- **1907:** *The Conditions of Occult Research*. A congress paper published in proceedings and posthumously reprinted.
- **1912:** *Thoughts Are Things*. A reprint of the 1896 article *Thought-Forms* inside Leadbeater’s book; it is a renamed manifestation, not a separate composition.
- **1916:** *The Theosophic View*. Besant’s contribution to the symposium *What Happens after Death?*
- **1889–1897:** *Articles in Lucifer*. The dedicated bibliography identifies 78 selected items. This resists honest conversion into “everything”: the bibliography expressly says “selected,” and many items are serial states of books or sources of later pamphlets.
- **1897–1907:** *Articles in The Theosophical Review*. The bibliography identifies 80 selected items, again mixing autonomous articles with serial manifestations of other works.
- **1889–1947:** *Articles in The Theosophist*. The bibliography identifies 344 selected items, including posthumous publications through the 1947 centenary issue; those posthumous editorial manifestations cannot all be treated uncritically as works completed by Besant.
- **1908–1929:** *Articles in The Adyar Bulletin*. The bibliography identifies 102 selected items, including presidential addresses and serial states of books.
- **1908–1912:** *Articles in The Link*. The bibliography identifies 35 selected Esoteric School texts; their institutional and sometimes restricted character complicates the category of public work.
- **1930:** *Articles in The Hollywood Theosophist*. Ten articles form a discrete periodical sequence, one later reissued as a pamphlet.
- **1891–1932:** *Theosophical pamphlets*. The dedicated bibliography lists 134 objects, but most are manifestations of already named lectures or articles rather than newly made works.
- **1895–1907:** *Benares pamphlet series*. Thirty-four recorded objects belong to two different, often conflated series; duplicated titles and numbering make a title-by-title “work” count unreliable.
- **1908–1916:** *Adyar Popular Lectures pamphlet series*. Thirty-one pamphlets include both first publications and simultaneous pamphlet states of lecture-books.
- **1911–1936:** *Adyar Library pamphlet series*. Seventy-three Besant-related pamphlets are mainly reprints; some appeared after her death and are editorial manifestations rather than new works.
- **1892–1920s:** *Miscellaneous theosophical pamphlet series*. Seventeen items are recorded, but the source explicitly calls the series incomplete.
- **1915–1930:** *Later political pamphlets*. Sixty-nine items are recorded across several incomplete series; many reproduce speeches or articles already represented above.
- **1934–2013:** *Posthumous Besant publications*. The bibliography records 218 items, including twelve volumes of the *Besant Spirit Series*, unreleased material, extracts, retitled works, and reprints. These resist collective attribution as works “made” by Besant because later editors selected, titled, recombined, or renamed much of the material.
