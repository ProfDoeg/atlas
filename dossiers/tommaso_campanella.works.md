## Works by Tommaso Campanella

### Philosophical and encyclopedic obra

- **1586–1588:** *Lectiones logicae, physicae et animasticae*. Lecture notes on logic, physics, and the soul; the work is lost and known through Campanella’s retrospective bibliographies. [Firpo, no. 53]
- **1587–1590:** *De investigatione rerum*. An early natural-philosophical investigation, completed according to Campanella’s own lists but now lost. [Firpo, no. 54]
- **1589:** *Philosophia sensibus demonstrata*. An eight-disputation defense of Bernardino Telesio’s sensory naturalism against Aristotelianism; written in 1589 and first published in 1591, it also circulated as *Pro Telesio contra Aristotelicos*. [Firpo, no. 1]
- **1591–1592:** *Philosophia Pythagorica*. A lost reconstruction or exposition of Pythagorean philosophy. Its independent existence rests principally on Campanella’s own inventories. [Firpo, no. 57]
- **1591–1592:** *De philosophia Empedoclis*. A lost treatment of Empedoclean philosophy, known only bibliographically. [Firpo, no. 58]
- **1591–1592:** *Metaphysicae novae exordium*. The lost beginning of an early “new metaphysics,” not to be confused with Campanella’s later, repeatedly rewritten *Metaphysica*. [Firpo, no. 59]
- **1591–1592:** *Encyclopaedia facilis dictata principibus*. A lost pedagogical encyclopedia intended for princes; it survives as a reported project rather than a recoverable text. [Firpo, no. 60]
- **1592–1593:** *De rerum universitate*. A twenty-book natural-philosophical synthesis, reconstructed after its first book was confiscated; the whole work was subsequently lost, although parts fed later versions of Campanella’s *Philosophia realis*. [Firpo, no. 8a]
- **1594:** *Fisiologia compendiosa*. A concise natural philosophy, apparently extracted or reworked from *De rerum universitate*; it is lost and resists secure separation from that larger project. [Firpo, no. 8a]
- **1595:** *Compendium de rerum natura*. A compact natural philosophy, later printed in 1617 as *Prodromus philosophiae instaurandae*; it is both an autonomous treatise and the prospectus for a larger reform of knowledge. [Firpo, no. 3]
- **1596:** *De modo sciendi*. A lost philosophical poem on method or knowing, paired in the evidence with *Physiologica*; whether the two were wholly separate works is uncertain. [Firpo, no. 69]
- **1596:** *Physiologica*. A lost philosophical poem or verse treatise on nature, reported alongside *De modo sciendi*. [Firpo, no. 69]
- **1596–1598:** *Epilogo magno*. An encyclopedic “great epilogue” of natural philosophy, repeatedly revised and later partly translated into Latin; it is a major surviving stage in the evolving *Philosophia realis* rather than a fixed final book. [Firpo, no. 8b/e/g]
- **1602–1638:** *Metaphysica*. Campanella’s metaphysical summa, successively composed in Italian, lost or confiscated, reconstructed in Latin, and revised through at least four redactions before publication in 1638; “the work” is therefore a long textual project, not one stable artifact. [Firpo, no. 23]
- **1609–1615:** *Quaestiones physiologicae, morales et politicae*. Sets of questions in natural, moral, and political philosophy that became structural components of the printed *Philosophia realis*. [Firpo, no. 8f–h]
- **1612–1638:** *Philosophia rationalis*. A comprehensive art of discourse and knowledge comprising grammar, dialectic, rhetoric, poetics, and historiography; begun in prison and published in 1638. [Firpo, no. 22]
- **1612–1613:** *Dialectica*. The logical division of *Philosophia rationalis*; it is a component treatise but also circulated conceptually as a work in its own right. [Firpo, no. 22]
- **1612–1613:** *Rhetorica*. The rhetorical division of *Philosophia rationalis*. [Firpo, no. 22]
- **1612–1613:** *Poetica*. The Latin poetics incorporated into *Philosophia rationalis*, distinct from Campanella’s earlier Italian *Poetica*. [Firpo, no. 22]
- **1613:** *Historiographia*. A theory and protocol for writing history, added as the fourth major division of *Philosophia rationalis*. [Firpo, no. 22]
- **1619:** *Grammatica*. The grammatical division added to *Philosophia rationalis*. [Firpo, no. 22]
- **1623; revised 1637:** *Philosophia realis epilogistica / Philosophia realis*. An encyclopedic Gesamtkunstwerk of natural philosophy, ethics, politics, economics, and utopia, assembled from works written and rewritten over decades; the 1623 and 1637 forms are materially different configurations. [Firpo, no. 8h–i]
- **1623; revised 1637:** *Physiologia*. The natural-philosophical portion of *Philosophia realis*, descended from several earlier, partly lost projects. [Firpo, no. 8h–i]
- **1623; revised 1637:** *Ethica*. A moral-philosophical treatise within *Philosophia realis*, developed from earlier *Quaestiones* and substantially revised. [Firpo, no. 8h–i]
- **1623; revised 1637:** *Oeconomica*. A treatise on household government within *Philosophia realis*. [Firpo, no. 8h–i]
- **1633–1638:** *Tomi X instauratarum scientiarum* / *Index librorum propriorum*. The continuously revised architecture for a projected ten-volume restoration of the sciences. It is less a completed work than a protocol and map for Campanella’s intended opera omnia. [Firpo, no. 24]

### Natural philosophy, astronomy, astrology, mathematics, and medicine

- **1590; rewritten 1604:** *De sensitiva rerum facultate / Del senso delle cose e della magia*. A treatise arguing that sensation pervades nature and joining natural philosophy to magic; the first version was lost, the Italian reconstruction was translated and repeatedly revised in Latin as *De sensu rerum et magia*. [Firpo, no. 6]
- **1591–1592:** *De sphera Aristarchi*. A lost astronomical work whose exact argument cannot now be recovered. [Firpo, no. 55]
- **1603–1611:** *De astronomia*. A substantial astronomical work, revised after 1610 and confiscated in 1611; it is lost. [Firpo, no. 73]
- **1604:** *De symptomatis mundi per ignem interituri*. An astrological-prophetic diagnosis of signs announcing the world’s destruction by fire; it is lost or not securely recoverable as an independent text. [Firpo, no. 73]
- **1604:** *Prognosticum astrologicum de his quae mundo imminent*. A lost astrological prognostication concerning impending world events. [Firpo, no. 74]
- **1610:** *De utilitate potus calidi*. A lost medical regimen on the usefulness of hot drink. [Firpo, no. 77]
- **1609–1635:** *Medicina*. A medical summa first made in two books and then rewritten in seven; its growth across more than two decades makes it a sustained project as well as a treatise. [Firpo, no. 13]
- **1613; revised through 1630:** *Astrologicorum libri VI/VII*. A systematic astrological work that grew from six to seven books; unauthorized and altered early printings complicate the identity of its authoritative form. [Firpo, no. 9]
- **1613:** *Quattro articoli sul discorso sui galleggianti di Galileo*. Four responses to Galileo’s discourse on floating bodies; the work is lost. [Firpo, no. 78]
- **before 1613:** *Disputatio contra graphomantum*. A lost disputation against divination or prediction through writing; its precise date and scope remain uncertain. [Firpo, no. 79]
- **1614:** *Mathematica*. A mathematical treatise or collection, surviving posthumously rather than through an authorized lifetime edition. [Firpo, no. 47]
- **1618:** *Calculus nativitatis D. Philiberti Vernati*. A natal calculation or horoscope for Philibert Vernat, an applied astrological object rather than a general treatise. [Firpo, no. 48]
- **1626:** *De siderali fato vitando*. A protocol for averting astral fate, incorporated into the astrological corpus and entangled with the controversial 1629 printing. [Firpo, nos. 9, 37]
- **1629:** *Apologeticus ad libellum De siderali fato vitando*. A defense of the preceding astrological protocol against criticism. [Firpo, no. 37]
- **before or during 1631:** *Scritti astrologici*. A bibliographically reported group of astrological writings that cannot be securely separated into individually titled works; it resists treatment as a single fixed text. [Firpo, no. 89]
- **1631:** *De conflagratione Vesuvii*. A lost or untraced treatment of an eruption or conflagration of Vesuvius, combining natural and prognostic interests. [Firpo, no. 90]
- **1637:** *Defensio libri sui De sensu rerum*. A polemical defense of *De sensu rerum et magia*, printed with the revised treatise. [Firpo, no. 21]
- **1638:** *Horoscopus serenissimi Delphini*. The horoscope calculated for the infant future Louis XIV; it is an astrological object made for a particular birth and was published only posthumously. [Firpo, no. 26]
- **1638–1639:** *Apologia Horoscopi et Poeseos suae*. A defense of both the Dauphin’s horoscope and Campanella’s celebratory poetry; it is known as a late work but was lost. [Firpo, no. 96]

### Political philosophy, counsel, and utopian manifestation

- **1593:** *Discorso sul modo delle fortificazioni*. A technical-military discourse on fortification; it survived posthumously. [Firpo, no. 49]
- **1593:** *De monarchia Christianorum*. A lost politico-religious treatise on Christian monarchy. [Firpo, no. 63]
- **1594–1595:** *Discorsi ai principi d’Italia*. Political counsels urging Italian rulers toward a pro-Spanish policy; they survive posthumously as a group rather than as a single conventionally composed book. [Firpo, no. 28]
- **1594–1595:** *Discorso sui Paesi Bassi / Discursus de Belgio sub Hispanicam potestatem redigendo*. Strategic advice for bringing the Low Countries under Spanish power; Campanella reworked it into the *Monarchia di Spagna*, while the independent Italian and Latin forms continued to circulate. [Firpo, no. 2]
- **1594:** *Orationes politicae pro saeculo praesenti*. A lost body of political orations; the plural title probably denotes a collection rather than one text. [Firpo, no. 65]
- **1596–1597:** *Consultazione alla Repubblica veneta / Discorso ai Veneziani*. A lost piece of political counsel addressed to Venice. [Firpo, no. 67]
- **1596–1597:** *Trattato dell’arte cavaglieresca*. A lost treatise on chivalric or military art. [Firpo, no. 68]
- **1600–1601:** *Monarchia di Spagna / De monarchia Hispanica*. A geopolitical treatise presenting Spain as the prospective instrument of universal order; it exists in Italian and authorially reworked Latin forms rather than as a simple translation pair. [Firpo, no. 5]
- **1601:** *Aforismi politici*. Political aphorisms later revised, Latinized, and incorporated into the *Politica* of *Philosophia realis*. [Firpo, no. 8c/h]
- **1602:** *La città del Sole / Civitas Solis*. A philosophical dialogue and utopian manifestation of an ideal solar commonwealth; repeatedly revised and Latinized, it became part of the *Politica* while retaining an independent identity. [Firpo, no. 8d/h]
- **1605:** *Del governo del regno*. A memorandum or tract offering governmental counsel for Naples; its existence and identification are conjectural, and Firpo cautiously associated it with promises made to the viceroy. [Firpo, no. 75]
- **1605:** *Discorso delle ragioni che ha il Re Cattolico sopra il Mondo Nuovo*. An argument concerning Spanish claims in the New World, attached to the *Monarchia del Messia*. [Firpo, no. 11]
- **1608:** *Arbitrii tre sopra l’aumento delle entrate del Regno di Napoli*. Three fiscal proposals for increasing Neapolitan royal revenue: practical instruments of statecraft rather than abstract political theory. [Firpo, no. 29]
- **1623; revised 1637:** *Politica*. The political division of *Philosophia realis*, incorporating the aphorisms and *Civitas Solis*. [Firpo, no. 8h–i]
- **1627:** *Discorsi della libertà e della felice suggettione allo Stato ecclesiastico*. Two political discourses arguing about liberty and advantageous submission to papal government; printed with the *Monarchia Messiae* but also an independent intervention. [Firpo, no. 12]
- **1632:** *Dialogo politico tra un Veneziano, Spagnuolo e Francese sopra i rumori passati di Francia*. A political dialogue interpreting recent French disturbances through three national voices. [Firpo, no. 39]
- **1635:** *Aforismi politici per le presenti necessità di Francia*. Aphoristic counsel tailored to the immediate needs of the French monarchy. [Firpo, no. 40]
- **1635:** *Se al tempo nostro possa e debba trasmutarsi l’impero romano, e per parte di chi*. A political tract asking whether the Roman imperial dignity should be transferred and by whose agency; it incorporated a related *Comparsa regia*. [Firpo, no. 41]
- **1635:** *Documenta ad Gallorum nationem*. Political admonitions or instructions to the French nation; the manuscript work survives but was not published during Campanella’s lifetime. [Firpo, no. 52]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *Quibus quotve modis pauci contra plures pugnare ac vincere possint*. A lost military-political protocol on how a few may fight and defeat a larger force. [Firpo, no. 85]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *De aulicorum technis*. A lost analysis of the stratagems of courtiers. [Firpo, no. 86]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *An Monarchia Hispanorum sit in augmento, vel in statu, vel in decremento*. A lost diagnostic disputation asking whether Spanish monarchy was rising, stable, or declining. [Firpo, no. 97]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *Cur Galli, cum sint potentiores, non dominantur; Hispani vero imbecilliores econtra*. A lost comparative political disputation on French power and Spanish dominion. [Firpo, no. 98]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *A quibus desiderari pax debet secundum politicam*. A lost political consultation on which parties ought to desire peace. [Firpo, no. 99]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *Politica consultatio contra praedeterminatores, ad Venetos*. A lost political-theological consultation addressed to Venice against predetermination. [Firpo, no. 100]

### Theology, ecclesiology, prophecy, and religious action

- **1593:** *Del governo ecclesiastico / De regimine ecclesiae*. A program for ecclesiastical government, later expanded as *Discorsi universali del governo ecclesiastico* and abridged in 1631; it is a reform project in several states, not one settled text. [Firpo, no. 34]
- **1595:** *Dialogo politico contro luterani, calvinisti ed altri eretici*. A confessional-political dialogue against Protestant movements, composed as an intervention in contemporary religious conflict. [Firpo, no. 44]
- **1598:** *Quaestiones contra Molinam*. Lost theological questions directed against Luis de Molina’s positions. [Firpo, no. 71]
- **1598–1599:** *De episcopo*. A lost treatment of episcopal office. [Firpo, no. 72]
- **1600–1608:** *Articuli prophetales*. A set of prophetic articles or theses, developed across several years; their status lies between theological propositions, political prophecy, and manifesto. [Firpo, no. 33]
- **1605–1607:** *Atheismus triumphatus*. A dialogue or apologetic designed to make atheism defeat itself and thereby vindicate religion; composed in Italian, rewritten in Latin, and repeatedly revised before the 1631 and 1636 printings. [Firpo, no. 10]
- **1605:** *Cur sapientes et prophetae nationum omnium nascuntur in regionibus certis*. A lost inquiry into why sages and prophets arise in particular regions. [Firpo, no. 76]
- **1605; Latin revision 1618:** *Monarchia del Messia / Monarchia Messiae*. A politico-theological project for the universal monarchy of Christ under papal leadership, accompanied by arguments about the New World. [Firpo, no. 11]
- **1609:** *De gentilismo non retinendo*. A theological-political disputation against retaining pagan practices, eventually printed with *Atheismus triumphatus*. [Firpo, no. 15]
- **1610–1617:** *Quod reminiscentur*. An immense, four-part project for the religious reunification or conversion of Christians, Muslims, Jews, and gentiles. It remained unfinished, so its projective ambition—not completeness—is part of the obra. [Firpo, no. 42]
- **1613–1624:** *Theologia*. A theological summa in twenty-nine books, completed after more than a decade of prison composition but published only in modern scholarly editions. [Firpo, no. 45]
- **1618:** *De conceptione Beatae Virginis*. A theological treatise on the Virgin’s conception; Firpo classed it among lost works, but a text was subsequently recovered and critically edited, so the 1940 status is obsolete.
- **1621:** *Ad Cardinalem Bellarminum contra censuram librorum meorum*. A lost defense addressed to Bellarmine against the ecclesiastical censure of Campanella’s books. [Firpo, no. 81]
- **1626:** *Osservazioni sul libello del Parlamento del Re Cristianissimo*. A response to a French parlementary pamphlet, combining ecclesiology and immediate political controversy; it is lost. [Firpo, no. 83]
- **1627:** *Orationes de laudibus Divi Thomae*. Orations in praise of Thomas Aquinas; the collection is lost. [Firpo, no. 84]
- **1631:** *Disputatio contra murmurantes citra et ultra montes in Bullas Sixti V et Urbani VIII adversus iudiciarios editas*. A defense of papal bulls against judicial astrology, later printed with the theological works. [Firpo, no. 14]
- **1631:** *Censure sopra il libro del P. Mostro*. Campanella’s answers to an opponent’s numerous censures of his writings; a polemical dossier rather than a conventional treatise. [Firpo, no. 38]
- **1631:** *De assistentia dominorum Cardinalium in Curia*. A lost ecclesiological memorandum on the cardinals’ attendance or assistance in the papal curia. [Firpo, no. 91]
- **1632:** *Expositio super caput IX Epistulae Sancti Pauli ad Romanos*. A theological exposition of Romans 9, printed in the 1636 theological collection. [Firpo, no. 17]
- **1632:** *Apologia pro Scholis Piis*. A defense of the Piarist schools: an institutional manifesto as much as an apologetic text. [Firpo, no. 46]
- **1636:** *De regno Dei*. A theological-political treatise on the kingdom of God, subsequently incorporated into the 1637 *Philosophia realis*. [Firpo, no. 19]
- **1636:** *De praedestinatione*. A treatise on predestination, printed with *Atheismus triumphatus*. [Firpo, no. 16]
- **1636:** *Opuscolo polemico*. An otherwise unidentified lost polemical pamphlet; because neither a secure title nor subject survives, it resists classification as a determinate work. [Firpo, no. 95]
- **date uncertain, before 1634:** *In quibus possunt communicare, et in quibus non, cum schismaticis et infidelibus*. A theological protocol defining permissible and impermissible dealings with schismatics and non-Christians; left behind in Rome and now lost. [Firpo, no. 93]
- **date uncertain, before 1634:** *De praecedentia, praesertim Religiosorum*. A lost treatise on precedence, especially among religious orders. [Firpo, no. 94]
- **date uncertain, before 1639:** *De canonisatione Sanctorum*. A lost treatment of the canonization of saints. [Firpo, no. 88]

### Apologies, legal defenses, memoranda, and manifestos

- **1593:** *Apologia pro Telesio*. A lost defense of Bernardino Telesio, probably distinct from but closely related to the earlier *Philosophia sensibus demonstrata*. [Firpo, no. 61]
- **1595:** *Defensio Telesianorum ad Sanctum Officium*. Campanella’s lost concluding defense in his Roman heresy proceedings; it was an operative legal text, not merely a philosophical essay. [Firpo, no. 66]
- **1599:** *Dichiarazione*. A statement extracted after Campanella’s arrest for the Calabrian conspiracy; because it was produced under prosecutorial pressure, its status as something freely “made” by him is compromised. [Firpo, no. 30]
- **1600:** *Prima delineatio defensionum fratris Thomae Campanellae*. The first formal outline of Campanella’s legal defense in the conspiracy and heresy trials. [Firpo, no. 32]
- **1600:** *Secunda delineatio defensionum fratris Thomae Campanellae*. A second, distinct defensive outline; Firpo grouped the defense dossier imperfectly, and modern editions distinguish the two *delineationes*. [Firpo, no. 32]
- **1600; appendix 1611:** *Ad amicum apologia*. A legal and autobiographical defense addressed to a friend, later supplemented by a *Nova appendix necessaria*. [Firpo, no. 31]
- **1620:** *Informatione sopra la lettura delli processi*. A procedural memorandum seeking reconsideration of Campanella’s case after nearly two decades of imprisonment. [Firpo, no. 27]
- **1620:** *Narratione della historia sopra cui fu appoggiata la favola della ribellione*. A counter-narrative disputing the story on which the charge of rebellion rested; it acts as forensic testimony and self-vindication. [Firpo, no. 27]
- **1623:** *Apologeticus pro carminibus Virginii Caesarini*. A defense of Virginio Cesarini’s poems. [Firpo, no. 82]
- **1623:** *Pro conclavi admonitio ad electores Summi Pontificis*. An admonition to the papal electors during the conclave: a manifesto intended to influence an election rather than neutrally describe it. [Firpo, no. 20]
- **1636:** *Disputatio in prologum instauratarum scientiarum*. A disputation serving as a methodological prologue to the projected restoration of the sciences. [Firpo, no. 18]

### Poetry, poetics, and literary commentary

- **c. 1577–1639:** *Poesie / La Cantica*. A lifetime body of Italian and Latin poems—sonnets, canzoni, madrigals, elegies, hymns, epigrams, and occasional verse—only partly selected by Campanella for the 1622 *Scelta*. It is a corpus rather than one composition, and the authenticity of individual poems has required critical adjudication. [Firpo, no. 4]
- **1585:** *Orazione in verso eroico con un’ode saffica*. An early recited verse oration with a Sapphic ode; it belongs to the poetic corpus and is not securely recoverable as a separate surviving object. [Firpo, no. 4]
- **1588:** *Elegia per Bernardino Telesio*. A funeral elegy reportedly placed on Telesio’s bier; its independent textual survival is uncertain. [Firpo, no. 4]
- **1596:** *Poetica secondo i propri principî*. An Italian poetics setting out Campanella’s own literary principles; unlike the later Latin *Poetica*, it survives in manuscript and was not printed during his lifetime. [Firpo, no. 50]
- **1596:** *Ars versificatoria de metro latino applicando vulgari linguae*. A lost technical protocol for applying Latin meter to the vernacular. [Firpo, no. 64]
- **1622:** *Scelta d’alcune poesie filosofiche*. Campanella’s authorially selected and expounded collection of philosophical poems; it is a deliberately constructed manifestation of the larger poetic corpus, not the corpus itself. [Firpo, no. 4]
- **1627–1632:** *Commentaria super poematis Urbani VIII*. Extensive commentaries on the poems of Pope Urban VIII; surviving in manuscript, they combine literary exegesis, philosophy, and courtly-political service. [Firpo, no. 51]
- **1633:** *Disticon pel Re di Francia*. A Latin distich for the king of France, an occasional poem rather than a book. [Firpo, no. 4, appendix]
- **1638–1639:** *Ecloga in portentosam Delphini nativitatem*. A Latin eclogue celebrating the birth of the future Louis XIV, dictated in December 1638 and printed in January 1639. [Firpo, no. 4, appendix]
- **date uncertain:** *Maria regina di Scozia*. A lost literary work—possibly dramatic or poetic—on Mary, Queen of Scots; neither its form nor date is securely known. [Firpo, no. 70]

### Autobiography, bibliography, and self-construction

- **1632:** *Syntagma de libris propriis et recta ratione studendi*. A dictated account of Campanella’s own writings joined to a program for correct study; it is simultaneously autobiography, bibliography, curriculum, and defense of his intellectual project. [Firpo, no. 25]
- **1632:** *Vita Campanellae*. A brief autobiographical life dictated to Gabriel Naudé; although transmitted in the third person, it is treated as Campanella’s self-authored account. [Firpo, no. 92]

### Correspondence and documentary actions

- **1590–1639:** *Lettere superstiti*. A corpus of letters, dedications, petitions, recommendations, and memorials addressed to rulers, popes, cardinals, scholars, patrons, and friends. Firpo catalogued 124 surviving items individually; the modern critical edition revises and enlarges the corpus, so “the correspondence” is an archival body of actions rather than one work. [Firpo, nos. 101–224]
- **1599–1638:** *Lettere perdute*. Sixty-eight letters or documentary communications known from references but no longer extant in Firpo’s census. They resist being called surviving texts: what remains is evidence that communicative acts occurred, sometimes with a recoverable recipient or purpose but without recoverable wording. [Firpo, nos. 225–292]
