## Works by Miguel de Cervantes Saavedra

### Prose fictions and composite books

- **1585:** *Primera parte de La Galatea*. A six-book pastoral romance interweaving prose narrative, verse, and the extensive *Canto de Calíope*; “Primera parte” also marks it as an unfinished project whose promised continuation never appeared. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1605:** *El ingenioso hidalgo don Quijote de la Mancha*. The first part of the composite prose fiction now called *Don Quijote*, incorporating poems, documents, speeches, and inset narratives rather than behaving as a single-form “novel.” [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1605:** *El curioso impertinente*. An inset novella within the first *Quijote*; it is both a separable narrative and part of the larger obra, so its independence is structurally uncertain. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1605:** *Historia del cautivo* (also *El capitán cautivo*). An inset captivity narrative in the first *Quijote*, drawing upon Mediterranean history and Cervantes’s experience but made as fiction within fiction. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1613:** *Novelas ejemplares*. A deliberately constituted book of twelve short prose fictions, prefaced and presented by Cervantes as a collective obra; the individual novelas remain distinct made things. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *La gitanilla*. A short prose fiction in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *El amante liberal*. A Mediterranean captivity-and-release novella in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **Before 1605; published 1613:** *Rinconete y Cortadillo*. A picaresque-inflected novella surviving in an earlier Porras-manuscript version as well as the revised printed text; its dating and textual development are therefore not reducible to 1613. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *La española inglesa*. A short romance of capture, separation, testing, and reunion in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *El licenciado Vidriera*. A short prose fiction organized around the sayings and delusion of a learned protagonist. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *La fuerza de la sangre*. A short prose fiction in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **Before 1605; published 1613:** *El celoso extremeño*. A novella surviving in materially different Porras-manuscript and printed versions; the revision changes its moral and narrative effects, so neither version can simply stand for the other. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *La ilustre fregona*. A short prose fiction in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *Las dos doncellas*. A short romance in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *La señora Cornelia*. An Italianate novella in *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *El casamiento engañoso*. A short prose fiction that also supplies the narrative frame for *El coloquio de los perros*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1613:** *El coloquio de los perros*. A dialogic animal fable presented as a written record inside *El casamiento engañoso*; it is simultaneously an autonomous novella and a nested, dubiously authenticated manuscript within another fiction. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1615:** *Segunda parte del ingenioso caballero don Quijote de la Mancha*. The authored continuation of the 1605 fiction and a response to the unauthorized sequel published under the name Alonso Fernández de Avellaneda. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **Completed 1616; published 1617:** *Los trabajos de Persiles y Sigismunda, historia septentrional*. A four-book Byzantine romance completed near Cervantes’s death and issued posthumously; the publication is posthumous, not the making of the text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)

### Full-length drama

- **c. 1581:** *Los tratos de Argel* / *El trato de Argel*. A captivity play preserved in two manuscripts under the singular title, although Cervantes himself twice used the plural; neither title should silently erase the other. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **c. 1583:** *La destrucción de Numancia* / *La Numancia* / *El cerco de Numancia* / *Tragedia de Numancia*. A tragedy of collective resistance and destruction; Cervantes’s names differ from the titles in its two surviving manuscripts, and it remained unprinted until 1784. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **Probably 1580s; attribution disputed:** *La conquista de Jerusalén por Godofre de Bullón*. An anonymous manuscript comedy plausibly identified with Cervantes’s lost *La Jerusalén*, but the attribution is not universally accepted and must not be treated as secure. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *Ocho comedias y ocho entremeses nuevos, nunca representados*. A printed theatrical collection and obra in its own right, containing sixteen separately named performance texts; its title’s “never performed” describes their market history, not their medium. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_obras_completas/)
- **1615:** *El gallardo español*. A full-length comedy in *Ocho comedias y ocho entremeses*. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *La casa de los celos y selvas de Ardenia*. A full-length chivalric and pastoral comedy. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *Los baños de Argel*. A full-length captivity comedy distinct from the earlier *Trato de Argel*. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *El rufián dichoso*. A full-length saint’s comedy dramatizing a criminal’s conversion. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *La gran sultana doña Catalina de Oviedo*. A full-length comedy set at the Ottoman court. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *El laberinto de amor*. A full-length comedy of disguises and entangled courtship. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *La entretenida*. A full-length urban comedy that deliberately frustrates conventional marriage-plot closure. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *Pedro de Urdemalas*. A full-length comedy centered on a shape-changing rogue who finally becomes an actor, making theatrical performance itself part of the obra’s subject. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)

### Entremeses: short performance works

- **1615:** *El juez de los divorcios*. A comic interlude structured as a sequence of marital petitions. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *El rufián viudo llamado Trampagos*. A comic interlude in verse. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *La elección de los alcaldes de Daganzo*. A comic interlude about grotesquely unsuitable candidates for civic office. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *La guarda cuidadosa*. A comic interlude built around rival suitors and street performance. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *El vizcaíno fingido*. A comic interlude of impersonation and deception. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *El retablo de las maravillas*. A comic interlude in which an empty puppet show becomes a social test: the supposed spectacle is a deliberately absent manifestation produced by collective fear. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *La cueva de Salamanca*. A comic interlude turning an adulterous household crisis into an improvised supernatural performance. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1615:** *El viejo celoso*. A comic interlude distinct from, though thematically related to, the novella *El celoso extremeño*. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)

### Long poem and hybrid supplement

- **1614:** *Viaje del Parnaso*. A long narrative and satirical poem in tercets, casting the contemporary literary world as an allegorical voyage and battle. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1614:** *Adjunta al Parnaso*. A prose supplement appended to *Viaje del Parnaso*; its dialogue, mock-documentary apparatus, and comic privileges make it a hybrid adjunct rather than another canto of the poem. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)

### Independently transmitted poems

- **Probably 1560s; first printed 1899:** *Soneto a la reina Isabel de Valois*. A laudatory sonnet preserved in manuscript; its exact date is not fixed. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1569:** *Soneto-epitafio a la reina Isabel de Valois*. A funeral sonnet printed in Juan López de Hoyos’s account of the queen’s death and obsequies. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1569:** *Copla real a la muerte de Isabel de Valois*. A ten-line funeral poem printed in López de Hoyos’s commemorative volume. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1569:** *Coplas reales a la muerte de Isabel de Valois*. Four funeral stanzas printed in the same commemorative book. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1569:** *Elegía al cardenal Espinosa*. An extended elegy ostensibly addressed to Cardinal Diego de Espinosa but occasioned by Isabel de Valois’s death. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1575–1580:** *Soneto en honor de Bartholomeo Ruffino di Chiambery*. A commendatory sonnet written during captivity and transmitted with Ruffino’s manuscript history of the fall of La Goleta and Tunis. - **1575–1580:** *Soneto en alabanza del libro de Bartholomeo Ruffino di Chiambery*. A second commendatory sonnet attached to the same captivity manuscript. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1577:** *Epístola a Mateo Vázquez*. A verse petition from captivity, autobiographical and instrumental as well as poetic; because its manuscript transmission and discovery generated debate, its status has been scrutinized more closely than that of ordinary commendatory verse. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, chronology](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/autor_cronologia/)
- **c. 1578–1580:** *Octavas a Antonio Veneziano*. Verse octaves addressed to the Sicilian poet and fellow captive; their exact date is not fixed. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1580:** *Soneto en honor de Pedro de Padilla*. A commendatory poem printed with Padilla’s *Tesoro de varias poesías*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1584:** *Soneto en honor de Juan Rufo Gutiérrez*. A commendatory sonnet printed with Rufo’s epic *La Austriada*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1585:** *Redondillas en honor de Pedro de Padilla*. Commendatory religious verse printed in Padilla’s *Jardín espiritual*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1585:** *Estancias en honor de Pedro de Padilla*. A second commendatory poem in Padilla’s *Jardín espiritual*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1585:** *Soneto al seráfico San Francisco*. A devotional sonnet printed in Padilla’s *Jardín espiritual*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1586:** *Soneto en honor de López Maldonado*. A commendatory sonnet printed with Gabriel López Maldonado’s *Cancionero*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1586:** *Quintillas en loor de López Maldonado*. An extended commendatory poem in the same *Cancionero*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1587:** *Soneto en honor de Alonso de Barros*. A commendatory sonnet printed in Barros’s *Filosofía cortesana moralizada*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **Late 1580s:** *Nuevo soneto a fray Pedro de Padilla*. A commendatory sonnet for Padilla’s Marian verse; the poem is conventionally dated through the book in which it appeared rather than through an autograph. - **1588:** *Soneto al doctor Francisco Díaz*. A commendatory sonnet prefacing Díaz’s surgical treatise; it is paratext made to act publicly on behalf of another book. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1588:** *Canción nacida de las varias nuevas que han venido de la Católica Armada que fue sobre Inglaterra*. A public-events canción responding to uncertain reports about the Armada, not a retrospective historical account. - **After 1588:** *Segunda canción a la Armada Invencible*. A second Armada poem; its precise date and relation to the preceding canción are not securely fixed. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **Date uncertain:** *Romance de la morada de los celos*. A separately transmitted lyric ballad whose date cannot be stated securely. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1595:** *Glosa a San Jacinto*. A devotional competition poem associated with the canonization festivities for Hyacinth of Poland. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1596:** *Soneto al marqués de Santa Cruz*. A commendatory sonnet printed in Cristóbal Mosquera de Figueroa’s account of the Azores campaign. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1596; attribution doubted:** *Soneto a la entrada del duque de Medina en Cádiz*. A satirical occasional sonnet preserved through a lost or untraced manuscript; its editor explicitly expressed doubts about its authenticity. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1598:** *Soneto al túmulo de Felipe II en Sevilla*. A burlesque sonnet on the king’s enormous funeral monument; Cervantes later singled it out among his poems. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1598:** *Quintillas a la muerte de Felipe II*. An elegiac occasional poem distinct in tone and form from the burlesque tomb sonnet. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1598:** *Soneto en honor de Lope de Vega*. A commendatory sonnet printed with Lope’s *La Dragontea*, before the later public antagonism between the writers. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **After 1597:** *Soneto a la muerte de Fernando de Herrera*. A memorial sonnet whose date follows Herrera’s death but whose first composition date is not fixed. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **Published 1610:** *Soneto a don Diego Hurtado de Mendoza y a su fama*. A posthumous commendatory sonnet attached to an edition of Mendoza’s poetry. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1613:** *Octavillas al secretario Gabriel Pérez del Barrio Angulo*. Commendatory verse for a practical book on secretarial service; it functions as literary paratext rather than an independent volume. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **Early 1610s:** *Soneto a don Diego Rosel y Fuenllana*. A highly mannered commendatory sonnet; its exact composition date is uncertain. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1614–1615:** *Canción a los éxtasis de Santa Teresa de Jesús*. A devotional canción associated with celebrations of Teresa’s beatification. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **1616:** *Soneto a Juan Yagüe de Salas*. A commendatory sonnet for Yagüe de Salas’s *Los amantes de Teruel*. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)
- **Date uncertain:** *Soneto a doña Alfonsa González*. A separately transmitted occasional sonnet for which the scholarly collection supplies no secure composition date. [Schevill and Bonilla, *Poesías sueltas*](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/poesias-sueltas--2/html/)

### Documentary and petitionary text

- **10–22 October 1580:** *Información de Argel*. A legal “información y probanza” assembled at Cervantes’s request from a petition, interrogatory, witness statements, and notarial certification. Cervantes made the proceeding possible and signed its initiating petition, but the whole document is collaborative and scribal; some scholars call portions Cervantes’s earliest prose, while others resist treating the dossier as a literary work by him. [Archivo de la Frontera](https://www.archivodelafrontera.com/archivos/archivos-mediterraneo/la-informacion-de-argel-de-1580/)

### Lost works and unfinished projects

- **By 1585; lost:** *La confusa*. A comedy Cervantes named among his successful early plays; only the title and his retrospective claim survive, so the lost performance text cannot be described as though extant. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **By 1585; lost:** *El trato de Constantinopla y muerte de Selín*. A lost play known from Cervantes’s own retrospective title-listing. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1580s; lost:** *La gran turquesca*. A lost play known by title; it must not be conflated automatically with the extant *Gran sultana*. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1580s; lost:** *La batalla naval*. A lost play known by title, probably responding to Mediterranean warfare, though no surviving text permits a secure description of its plot. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1580s; lost:** *La Amaranta, o la del Mayo*. A lost play whose alternative title is all that securely identifies it. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1580s; lost:** *El bosque amoroso*. A lost play known only through Cervantes’s retrospective list. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **1580s; lost; title division uncertain:** *La Única y La bizarra Arsinda*. The wording may preserve two titles or one compound title; because no text survives, the ambiguity itself is part of the obra’s status. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **After 1585; unfinished or lost:** *Segunda parte de La Galatea*. Cervantes repeatedly projected a continuation of his pastoral romance, but no completed text survives; it is therefore a project, not an extant book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, chronology](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/autor_cronologia/)
- **Announced by 1616; lost or never completed:** *Las semanas del jardín*. A projected work named by Cervantes near the end of his life. No authorial text survives, and later reconstructions cannot turn the announced project into a recovered Cervantine work. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **Announced by 1616; lost or never completed:** *El famoso Bernardo* / *Bernardo del Carpio*. A projected chivalric or heroic work mentioned by Cervantes; its genre, completeness, and even whether a substantial manuscript ever existed remain uncertain. [Oxford Handbook of Cervantes](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34247/chapter/290357941)
- **Date unknown:** *Other early comedies*. Cervantes claimed to have written roughly twenty or thirty plays, far more than the extant texts and recoverable titles. This unnamed remainder resists itemization: it is evidence of a lost dramatic body of work, not a license to invent titles. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, *Ocho comedias y ocho entremeses*](https://cervantes.library.tamu.edu/V2/CPI/textos/fsevilla/8comedias.htm)

### Contested attributions and things that resist inclusion as Cervantes’s works

- **Early seventeenth century; attribution disputed:** *La tía fingida*. An anonymous novella surviving in two textual traditions and long attributed to Cervantes. Stylometric and literary arguments continue on both sides, so it belongs in the attribution history but not among the secure *Novelas ejemplares*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_atribuciones/); [Anales Cervantinos](https://analescervantinos.revistas.csic.es/index.php/analescervantinos/article/view/502)
- **Seventeenth century; attribution disputed:** *Los habladores*. An anonymous entremés traditionally assigned to Cervantes but excluded from the secure group of eight; the attribution remains conjectural. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/miguel_de_cervantes/estados_atribuciones/)
- **Seventeenth century; attribution disputed:** *La cárcel de Sevilla*. An entremés sometimes attributed to Cervantes without consensus; it resists admission to his obra because no decisive authorial evidence survives. [Survey of disputed Cervantine entremeses](https://webs.ucm.es/BUCM/tesis/19911996/H/3/AH3005801.pdf)
- **Seventeenth century; attribution disputed:** *El hospital de los podridos*. An anonymous entremés historically assigned to Cervantes by some scholars but not securely his. [Survey of disputed Cervantine entremeses](https://webs.ucm.es/BUCM/tesis/19911996/H/3/AH3005801.pdf)
- **Seventeenth century; attribution disputed:** *Los mirones*. An anonymous entremés included in the wider field of Cervantine attributions, not in the authenticated dramatic corpus.
