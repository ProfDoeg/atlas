## Works by Muhammad Baha al-Din Naqshband Bukhari

### Devotional protocol of uncertain authorship

- **Before 1389; attribution uncertain:** *Awrād-i Bahāʾiyya* (“The Bahāʾī Litany”). A devotional protocol traditionally associated with Baha al-Din and the sole possible exception to the scholarly judgment that he left no writings; its authorship and date cannot be securely established, so it should not be treated as an authenticated composition. ([Encyclopaedia of Islam assessment cited by Hurqalya](https://hurqalya.ucmerced.edu/node/2781))

### Orally made teachings, preserved by others

- **Before 1389; recorded after oral delivery:** *Kalimāt-i Bahāʾ al-Dīn Naqshband* (“Sayings of Baha al-Din Naqshband”). This is not a book he wrote but a dispersed body of spoken dicta, instructions, and interpretations subsequently selected, classified, and explicated by his disciple Muhammad Pārsā in *Risāla-yi Qudsiyya*; authorship therefore belongs to Baha al-Din at the level of utterance, while the surviving textual obra is Pārsā’s. ([İbrahimoğlu, “Risāla al-Qudsiyya and Its Place in the Naqshbandī Culture”](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/sobad/article/1235559); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Bahāʾ-al-Dīn Naqšband”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baha-al-din-naqsband-kaja-mohammad-b/))

### Spiritual and communal protocol

- **Fourteenth century, especially before 1389:** *Silent dhikr as the defining discipline of his path*. This was an embodied protocol rather than a separately titled text: Baha al-Din made exclusive silent remembrance central to the lineage descending from him, while rejecting musical audition, seclusion, distinctive clothing, institutional hospice residence, and reliance upon charismatic feats. The practice was inherited from earlier Khwājagān precedents rather than invented wholly by him, but scholarship identifies his insistence upon it as his decisive formative contribution. ([Hamid Algar, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Bahāʾ-al-Dīn Naqšband”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baha-al-din-naqsband-kaja-mohammad-b/))

- **By 1389:** *The path later called the Naqshbandiyya*. This is a communal and pedagogical obra, not a authored book and not straightforwardly an organization founded at one moment: Baha al-Din reshaped an existing Khwājagān lineage so decisively that the enduring order took his name, although its extensive institutional development occurred after his death. ([Hamid Algar, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Bahāʾ-al-Dīn Naqšband”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baha-al-din-naqsband-kaja-mohammad-b/))

### Rejected or unverified attributions

- **Date unknown; attributed much later:** *Poems and short treatises attributed to Baha al-Din Naqshband*. These resist enumeration as authenticated works: the standard scholarly assessment is that the several poems and treatises placed under his name are almost certainly spurious, and no reliable contemporary corpus or fixed list supports their attribution. ([Encyclopaedia of Islam assessment cited by Hurqalya](https://hurqalya.ucmerced.edu/node/2781))

- **Compilation published in 1997; poems purportedly fourteenth-century:** *Rubāʿiyāt-i Khwāja Naqshband*. This collection assigns 137 Persian quatrains to Baha al-Din, assembled and commented upon by Muhammad Sadiq Qusuri, but the early sources reportedly preserve only a few quatrains and the large corpus lacks secure medieval attestation; it must therefore be catalogued as a modern attribution rather than an authenticated poetic obra. ([“The Essence and Meaning of the Rubai of Bahauddin Naqshband”](https://inlibrary.uz/index.php/international-scientific/article/view/70309); [Encyclopaedia of Islam assessment cited by Hurqalya](https://hurqalya.ucmerced.edu/node/2781))

### Works sometimes mistaken for his

- **Late fourteenth century:** *Risāla-yi Qudsiyya*. This is Muhammad Pārsā’s authored treatise, not Baha al-Din’s: it preserves and interprets Baha al-Din’s spoken teachings, making it a textual manifestation of his voice but an obra made by his disciple. ([İbrahimoğlu, “Risāla al-Qudsiyya and Its Place in the Naqshbandī Culture”](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/pub/sobad/article/1235559))

- **Late fourteenth or early fifteenth century:** *Maqāmāt-i Bahāʾ al-Dīn Naqshband*. This collection of Baha al-Din’s biography and dicta was first made by his successor ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn ʿAṭṭār; it is testimony about him, drawing upon orally transmitted material, rather than something Baha al-Din composed. ([Hamid Algar, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Boḵārī, ʿAlāʾ-al-Dīn Moḥammad”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bokari-ala-al-din-mohammad-b/))

- **Late fourteenth or early fifteenth century:** *Anīs al-ṭālibīn wa-ʿuddat al-sālikīn*. This early collection of Baha al-Din’s life, sayings, and instruction is traditionally associated with Ṣalāḥ ibn Mubārak and draws partly on testimony supplied by ʿAlāʾ al-Dīn ʿAṭṭār; it is a witness to Baha al-Din’s oral practice, not his own written work. ([Hamid Algar, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Boḵārī, ʿAlāʾ-al-Dīn Moḥammad”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/bokari-ala-al-din-mohammad-b/); [Hamid Algar, *Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Bahāʾ-al-Dīn Naqšband”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/baha-al-din-naqsband-kaja-mohammad-b/))
