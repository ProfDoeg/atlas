## Works by Max Moritz Warburg

### Published texts

- **25 April 1918:** *„Viod“ (Vereinigung jüdischer Organisationen Deutschlands)*. A political-organizational essay published in *Neue Jüdische Monatshefte*, vol. 2, no. 14, pp. 315–318; it proposes coordinated representation for German Jewish organizations and is closer to a programmatic intervention than to a descriptive article. Its attribution and publication data are recorded in the standard bibliography attached to Warburg’s biography, although no collected-works edition exists. ([bibliographical record](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Warburg))

### Speeches and public interventions

- **May 1933:** *Appeal to the Reich government for the equality of Jewish Germans in private enterprise*. A jointly authored political appeal by Warburg and several non-Jewish business leaders. It is an intervention—arguably a manifesto in the modest sense of a text intended to alter government conduct—but the surviving scholarly account supplies neither a formal title nor the names of every co-author; the government did not answer it. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **9 January 1938:** *Begrüßungsworte von Max Warburg zur Einweihung des Jüdischen Gemeinschaftshauses*. A 6½-page typescript and delivered inaugural address, preserved in the Max M. Warburg Collection at the Leo Baeck Institute. It simultaneously explains the house, inaugurates it, and publicly asserts Jewish cultural self-determination under surveillance; it therefore acts as well as describes. ([critical source edition](https://schluesseldokumente.net/quelle/jgo%3Asource-118); [scholarly interpretation](https://schluesseldokumente.net/beitrag/mueller-wesemann-eroeffnungsrede-gemeinschaftshaus))

### Memoir and posthumous textual obra

- **Written before 1946; privately printed 1952:** *Aus meinen Aufzeichnungen* (also cited as *Aus meinen Erinnerungen*). Warburg’s autobiographical recollections, issued posthumously in New York as a private printing by his son Eric M. Warburg. The inconsistent title in reference works—*Aufzeichnungen* in the *Neue Deutsche Biographie* and *Erinnerungen* in *Encyclopaedia Judaica*—should not be silently harmonized; this is one posthumous textual obra whose editorial extent and relation to Warburg’s original papers require examination of the private printing. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent); [Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/warburg-max-m))

### Institutional and financial projects

- **1893–1938:** *Leadership and remaking of M. M. Warburg & Co.* This is a sustained banking project rather than a single authored object: Warburg entered the family bank as a personally liable partner in 1893, directed it alone after 1910, and developed it into a major private bank specializing in securities, foreign-trade finance, and industrial credit. Because the institution pre-existed him and depended on partners and employees, it resists attribution as his individual “work.” ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **Before 1914:** *International loan and infrastructure financings*. Under Warburg’s leadership the bank participated in issues connected with the Baghdad Railway and in Japanese and Liberian sovereign loans. These are financial constructions—distributed contractual objects made by banks, states, and investors—not individually authored works, and the standard biography does not enumerate every transaction. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **1907:** *Hamburgische Wissenschaftliche Stiftung*. Warburg participated in founding this scientific foundation. It is a collective institutional obra rather than his sole creation. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **1908:** *Hamburgisches Kolonialinstitut*. Warburg and Werner von Melle actively brought about the institute’s foundation. This was a collaborative educational and colonial project, not a text, and its historical function cannot be separated from German colonial policy. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **June 1914:** *Anglo-German Bank*. A projected bank for investment in Morocco, initiated by Warburg and Lord Milner with German and British governmental approval. The outbreak of war prevented the intention from becoming a durable institution, so it belongs here principally as an unrealized or aborted project rather than a completed obra. ([biographical account](https://dewiki.de/Lexikon/Max_Warburg))

- **1914–1918:** *Reichseinkauf / Zentraleinkaufsgesellschaft*. Warburg and Albert Ballin were leading promoters and founders of this state-controlled organization for purchasing food abroad during the First World War. It is a collective logistical apparatus—part institution, part economic protocol—and not properly reducible to a personal “work.” ([Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/warburg-max-m))

- **1919:** *Economic and financial work for the German delegation at Versailles*. Warburg declined appointment as one of the principal delegates but served as an economic expert alongside his partner Carl Melchior; both advised against signing the treaty and withdrew from their delegations. This is advisory and negotiating labor rather than a stable authored object, and the sources consulted do not identify a discrete report that can safely be titled as his work. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **1919:** *University of Hamburg*. Warburg supported the university’s foundation after earlier helping establish the scientific foundation and Colonial Institute from which its institutional environment developed. “Made” is necessarily qualified here: he was a facilitator and financier among many founders, not the university’s sole creator. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **Summer 1933:** *Transfer agreement for emigrants to Palestine*. Warburg and Carl Melchior arranged an agreement between the German Reich and the Jewish Agency to facilitate emigrants’ transfer of capital to Palestine. This is a collectively negotiated financial protocol rather than a conventional work; the biographical source does not warrant treating Warburg as its sole author or equating it without qualification with every later mechanism known as the Haavara Agreement. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **1933:** *Removal of the Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg from Hamburg to London*. Warburg organized the relocation of the library created by his deceased brother Aby. The made thing here is a rescue and migration project, not the library collection itself: authorship of that collection remains Aby Warburg’s, while Max’s role was to help preserve it as an institution. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))

- **1933–1938:** *Financial assistance for Jewish emigration*. Through the Hilfsverein der deutschen Juden and other Jewish organizations, Warburg organized financial mechanisms enabling emigration from Nazi Germany, including work associated with the Palaestina-Treuhand GmbH. This is a body of rescue practice rather than one bounded work, and surviving accounts describe collective organizational action rather than sole authorship. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent); [Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/warburg-max-m))

- **1937–9 January 1938:** *Jüdisches Gemeinschaftshaus, Hartungstraße 9–11, Hamburg*. A cultural and communal project realized with Warburg’s financial and ideological support as a home for the Jüdischer Kulturbund Hamburg and other organizations; Fritz Block, Ernst Hochfeld, and Oscar Gerson designed the extensive architectural conversion. The house is therefore a collective obra, not a building designed by Warburg, while his inaugural speech is a distinct textual manifestation listed above. ([critical source edition](https://schluesseldokumente.net/quelle/jgo%3Asource-118); [project history](https://schluesseldokumente.net/beitrag/mueller-wesemann-eroeffnungsrede-gemeinschaftshaus))

- **1937–1938:** *Preservation and transfer of M. M. Warburg & Co. under persecution*. Warburg kept the bank operating as long as possible to protect Jewish employees and clients, then arranged its transfer to non-Jewish limited partners and managing partners Rudolf Brinckmann and Paul Wirtz. This resists being called a work: it was a defensive institutional project under coercion, subsequently distorted by state-imposed “Aryanization,” an unfair price, and the prohibition of the Warburg name. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118806173.html#ndbcontent))
