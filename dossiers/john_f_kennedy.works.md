## Works by John Fitzgerald Kennedy

### Obra: authored and collaborative books

- **1940:** *Appeasement at Munich: The Inevitable Result of the Slowness of Conversion of the British Democracy from a Disarmament to a Rearmament Policy*. Kennedy’s Harvard honors thesis, an academic manuscript rather than a commercial book; its substantially revised public manifestation became *Why England Slept*. [The thesis was later published under the editorially supplied title *Prelude to Leadership*, but that 1995 edition is not a separate Kennedy work.](https://www.jfklibrary.org/asset-viewer/archives/JFKPP/011/JFKPP-011-001)

- **1940:** *Why England Slept*. A political-history book developed from Kennedy’s Harvard thesis, analyzing Britain’s delayed rearmament before the Second World War; Kennedy made the argument, while Arthur Krock assisted in revising and arranging the manuscript for publication. [The Kennedy Library’s archival description identifies the thesis-to-book relationship.](https://static.jfklibrary.org/82r16wm7i81y1rjsa1ci6a708rsbenog.pdf)

- **1945:** *As We Remember Joe*. A privately printed memorial volume conceived and edited by Kennedy after the death of his brother Joseph P. Kennedy Jr.; its individual recollections belong to numerous contributors, so Kennedy’s obra here is chiefly editorial, commemorative, and material rather than sole authorship. [A complete copy is preserved among the Kennedy Library’s digitized papers.](https://static.jfklibrary.org/88216bvu6uwp4ctvb6t68il088d81i48.pdf)

- **1954–1955; published 1956:** *Profiles in Courage*. A collection of historical-political profiles issued under Kennedy’s name and awarded the 1957 Pulitzer Prize for biography; it resists uncomplicated single authorship because Theodore Sorensen conducted research and drafting, Jules Davids and Jacqueline Kennedy also assisted, and surviving drafts show Kennedy revising and directing a collaborative production. [The Kennedy Library describes Sorensen’s acknowledged role](https://www.jfklibrary.org/sites/default/files/2018-05/Issue_20_Winter_2016.pdf), while [its manuscript catalogue preserves edited chapter drafts](https://www.jfklibrary.org/asset-viewer/archives/jfkpp-028-002).

- **1958; expanded book published posthumously 1964:** *A Nation of Immigrants*. An essay commissioned by the Anti-Defamation League for its One Nation Library, subsequently revised and enlarged as a book during Kennedy’s presidency but not issued in that form until after his death; the 1964 volume is therefore the manifestation of an unfinished revision project rather than a wholly posthumous creation. [The ADL records the original commission and manuscript](https://www.adl.org/node/38334), and [the standard Kennedy bibliography records the 1964 book](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/john-f-kennedy-9780313281921/).

### Essays and journalistic texts

- **April–May 1945:** *Dispatches from the United Nations Conference on International Organization*. A series of newspaper reports Kennedy made as a Hearst special correspondent at the San Francisco conference; these are separate occasional articles rather than a book, and surviving bibliographies do not give the series a single authorial title.

- **Summer 1945:** *European diary*. An untitled manuscript diary kept during Kennedy’s journey through postwar Europe and later published under varying editorial titles; because Kennedy did not prepare it as a named work for publication, it resists classification as a finished obra.

- **October 1957:** *A Democrat Looks at Foreign Policy*. A programmatic essay in *Foreign Affairs* presenting Kennedy’s alternative to Eisenhower-era foreign policy; it is an essay, not a speech collection or book. [*Foreign Affairs* dates and attributes the article to Kennedy.](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/anthologies/2015-09-29/essays-presidency)

- **1959:** *A Force That Has Changed the Political Scene*. An article about television’s transformation of electoral politics, published in *TV Guide*; it is both media criticism and an intervention by a politician consciously fashioning a televisual public persona. [The Paley Center’s historical account identifies Kennedy’s article and its role in his campaign practice.](https://time.com/4795637/jfk-television/)

- **26 December 1960:** *The Soft American*. A *Sports Illustrated* essay calling physical fitness a matter of national policy; its manifesto-like purpose was not merely to describe American bodies but to initiate a federal fitness campaign. [The bibliographical record gives its publication date and venue.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-6443.2005.00253.x)

- **16 July 1962:** *The Vigor We Need*. A second *Sports Illustrated* essay on national fitness, now written from the presidency and functioning partly as policy advocacy; like most presidential prose, it belongs to a collaborative speechwriting and administrative apparatus even though published under Kennedy’s name. [The bibliographical record identifies the article and date.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-6443.2005.00253.x)

### Manifestations assembled from speeches and public statements

- **1960:** *The Strategy of Peace*. An Allan Nevins–edited selection of Kennedy’s pre-presidential speeches and statements; the volume is an editorial manifestation of earlier public texts, not a newly written continuous treatise. [The bibliographical record identifies Kennedy, Nevins, the year, and the volume’s extent.](https://www.mieder.de/images/the_truth_shall_make_you_free.pdf)

- **1962:** *To Turn the Tide*. John W. Gardner’s selection from Kennedy’s statements between his election and the close of the 1961 congressional session; it turns dispersed, frequently staff-assisted acts of presidential speech into a retrospective book-shaped obra. [The bibliographical record identifies its editor, scope, and publication details.](https://www.mieder.de/images/the_truth_shall_make_you_free.pdf)

- **1964:** *The Burden and the Glory: The Hopes and Purposes of President Kennedy’s Second and Third Years in Office as Revealed in His Public Statements and Addresses*. A posthumous collection edited by Allan Nevins, extending the sequence formed by *The Strategy of Peace* and *To Turn the Tide* and including the undelivered Dallas speech of 22 November 1963; Kennedy made the constituent public texts, but not the final book. [The publication record describes its contents and editorial responsibility.](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Burden_and_the_Glory.html?id=pBmQzwEACAAJ)

### Public-address and governmental text corpora

- **1946–1960:** *Congressional, campaign, and senatorial speeches and statements*. A large, distributed body of public texts rather than one fixed work: drafts survive across Kennedy’s campaign, House, Senate, personal, and speechwriters’ papers, and authorship ranges from Kennedy’s own composition to texts researched or drafted by staff and revised or authorized by him. [The Kennedy Library’s archival guide identifies the relevant campaign, House, Senate, personal, and Sorensen collections.](https://jfklibrary.libguides.com/JFKspeeches/archivalholdings)

- **1 August–7 November 1960:** *The Speeches, Remarks, Press Conferences, and Statements of Senator John F. Kennedy*. A government-printed corpus of the general-election campaign, edited by Warren G. Magnuson; it fixes campaign utterances—including remarks, releases, and press exchanges—whose composition was collaborative and whose boundaries depend upon editorial collection. [The standard bibliographical description records the date range and 1,440-page compilation.](https://www.mieder.de/images/the_truth_shall_make_you_free.pdf)

- **20 January 1961–22 November 1963:** *Public Papers of the Presidents of the United States: John F. Kennedy*. Three annual official volumes containing messages, speeches, statements, news conferences, letters, and other White House releases; this is the closest collected-works edition for Kennedy’s presidency, but it is an institutional corpus rather than proof that he personally drafted every text attributed to the presidential voice. [GovInfo describes the series and the 1961 volume’s coverage](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/PPP-1961-book1), while [the Federal Register describes the 1962 volume as containing speeches, conferences, messages, and other White House materials](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1964-01-31/pdf/FR-1964-01-31.pdf).

- **1961–1963:** *White House audio recordings of speeches and public remarks*. These recordings are manifestations of Kennedy’s public-address corpus in voice and performance, not merely duplicate written texts: timing, improvisation, accent, and delivery make the sounded event a distinct object. [The Kennedy Library identifies the fully digitized White House Communications Agency collection.](https://jfklibrary.libguides.com/JFKspeeches/archivalholdings)

- **1961–1963:** *Presidential news conferences*. Sixty-four live, largely improvisatory encounters whose questions and answers were subsequently textualized in the *Public Papers*; they resist sole authorship because reporters supplied the questions and Kennedy’s prepared knowledge depended upon extensive staff briefing. [The Paley Center records the number and Kennedy’s deliberate development of the televised form.](https://time.com/4795637/jfk-television/)

### Projects and protocols enacted through text

- **1960–1961:** *The New Frontier*. A political project articulated across the 1960 nomination address, campaign papers, inaugural rhetoric, and the first legislative program rather than a single stable text; its obra is the coordinated body of proposals and performances, many collaboratively drafted, through which Kennedy sought and began to exercise presidential power.

- **1961:** *Peace Corps project*. A governmental creation initiated by campaign remarks and made operative through Executive Order 10924 before receiving statutory form; its founding texts are protocols that established an institution, though neither the institution nor every word of those instruments can be treated as Kennedy’s sole-authored work. [The American Presidency Project’s Kennedy timeline records the executive order and subsequent message to Congress.](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/john-f-kennedy-event-timeline)

- **1961:** *Alliance for Progress*. A hemispheric political project announced and repeatedly defined in presidential addresses, diplomatic instruments, and administrative programs; it is an institutional and collaborative obra whose texts acted by organizing aid and inter-American commitments.

- **1961–1963:** *Project Apollo’s lunar objective*. Kennedy did not create the space program or its machines, but he made the crewed-lunar-landing objective into a national project through presidential messages, speeches, budgets, and administrative decisions; the “work” is therefore an intention enacted across media and institutions, not a Kennedy-authored technical artifact.

- **1963:** *Civil-rights legislative project*. A still-unfinished presidential project embodied in the 11 June televised address, the 19 June message and bill sent to Congress, and subsequent advocacy; the Civil Rights Act of 1964 was its later legislative manifestation, but it was enacted after Kennedy’s death and is not simply his work.

- **1963:** *Limited Nuclear Test Ban Treaty project*. A diplomatic and rhetorical project culminating in the signed treaty and its ratification campaign; the legal instrument was collectively negotiated by governments and cannot be classified as Kennedy’s individual text, although his administration and public addresses materially shaped its appearance.
