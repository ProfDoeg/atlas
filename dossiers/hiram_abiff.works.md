## Works by Hiram Abiff

### Identity and limits of the catalogue

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** *The temple bronzes attributed to Hiram/Huram*. The only ancient inventory of things made by this artisan is 1 Kings 7:13–47, partially repeated in 2 Chronicles 4:11–17; it presents sets of objects rather than a modern catalogue raisonné. “Hiram Abiff” is a later Masonic form of “Huram-abi,” and the biblical and Masonic figures cannot securely be treated as one historical maker; moreover, the Kings and Chronicles accounts disagree about his ancestry, abilities, and precise role. ([1 Kings 7:13–47](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A13-47&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 2:13–14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%202%3A13-14&version=NRSVUE); [Lexham Bible Dictionary](https://biblia.com/books/lbd/word/Hiram_the_Artisan))

### Monumental bronzework

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** *Jachin*. The southern or right-hand member of a pair of freestanding bronze temple pillars, named after its installation; the biblical narrative attributes the casting of both pillars to Hiram but does not preserve the object itself. ([1 Kings 7:15–22, 41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A15-22%2C41&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** *Boaz*. The northern or left-hand companion to *Jachin*, likewise a monumental bronze pillar rather than part of a surviving building; its attribution, dimensions, and appearance exist only through the textual tradition. ([1 Kings 7:15–22, 41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A15-22%2C41&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Two bowl-shaped capitals. Cast-bronze architectural crowns made for the pillars; Kings assigns a height of five cubits, whereas 2 Chronicles 3:15 gives five cubits for the capitals but differs from Kings over the pillars’ combined dimensions. ([1 Kings 7:16, 41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A16%2C41&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 3:15–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%203%3A15-17&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Two networks or latticeworks. Chain-like bronze ornament covering the two capitals; their exact construction is difficult to recover from the terminology and no physical example survives. ([1 Kings 7:17–18, 41](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A17-18%2C41&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Four hundred bronze pomegranates. Architectural ornaments arranged in two rows on each capital-network; they constitute a set of components rather than four hundred independently titled works. ([1 Kings 7:18–20, 42](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A18-20%2C42&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:12–13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A12-13&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** *The Sea*. A very large circular cast-bronze basin or reservoir for the temple court, conventionally called the Molten Sea; it is both a functional vessel and a monumental sculptural ensemble. Kings and Chronicles transmit slightly different statements about its capacity. ([1 Kings 7:23–26, 44](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A23-26%2C44&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:2–5, 15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A2-5%2C15&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Twelve bronze oxen beneath *the Sea*. A load-bearing sculptural base composed of twelve outward-facing cattle, grouped toward the four cardinal directions; whether it should be counted separately from the Sea or as part of that Gesamtkunstwerk is inherently uncertain. ([1 Kings 7:25, 44](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A25%2C44&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:4, 15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A4%2C15&version=NRSVUE))

### Mobile vessels and supports

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Ten bronze stands. Elaborately decorated wheeled carts or bases made to carry washing basins, with frames, panels, wheels, axles, and projecting supports; the inventory treats them as a coordinated set. ([1 Kings 7:27–37, 43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A27-37%2C43&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Ten bronze basins. Portable washing vessels mounted one to each wheeled stand; 2 Chronicles explains their ritual function as washing what was used for burnt offerings. ([1 Kings 7:38–39, 43](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A38-39%2C43&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:6, 14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A6%2C14&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Bronze pots. An unspecified number of small temple-service vessels; neither individual titles nor a secure total survive, so the textual class—not distinct recoverable objects—is the smallest honest catalogue entry. ([1 Kings 7:40, 45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A40%2C45&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:11, 16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A11%2C16&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Bronze shovels. An unspecified set of implements, probably for handling altar debris or ashes; the inventory supplies no individual descriptions. ([1 Kings 7:40, 45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A40%2C45&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:11, 16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A11%2C16&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Bronze bowls. An unspecified group variously translated as bowls, basins, or sprinkling bowls, demonstrating that even the objects’ modern categories are textually unstable. ([1 Kings 7:40, 45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A40%2C45&version=NRSVUE))

- **Tenth century BCE, traditionally:** Bronze forks. Chronicles includes forks or flesh-hooks among the utensils attributed to Huram, while the parallel summary in Kings instead explicitly names bowls; this is a variant inventory, not secure evidence for a separately preserved set. ([2 Chronicles 4:16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A16&version=NRSVUE); [1 Kings 7:45](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A45&version=NRSVUE))

### Disputed project rather than securely attributed object

- **Tenth century BCE in the story; Masonic form documented from the early eighteenth century:** *Solomon’s Temple*. Freemasonry transforms Hiram Abiff into the temple’s chief architect or master of works, making the whole building his project and effectively a Gesamtkunstwerk; the biblical inventory, however, calls him a bronze worker and attributes the temple’s construction to Solomon and a larger workforce, so the building should not be silently catalogued as Hiram’s historical obra. No identifiable fabric of Solomon’s temple or of Hiram’s bronzes survives. ([Lexham Bible Dictionary](https://biblia.com/books/lbd/word/Hiram_the_Artisan); [Encyclopedia of the Bible](https://www.biblegateway.com/resources/encyclopedia-of-the-bible/Hiram); [Jeremy D. Smoak, “Building Solomon’s Temple”](https://doi.org/10.1628/hebai-2021-0017))
