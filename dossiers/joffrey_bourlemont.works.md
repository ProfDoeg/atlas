## Works by Joffrey de Bourlémont

### Foundations and buildings

- **April 1250 onward:** Reconstruction and fortification of the Château de Bourlémont. This architectural project reused an older stronghold: Joffrey acknowledged receiving 100 livres from Thibaut of Champagne specifically for the work, but the precise extent of his surviving fabric—usually associated with the great round towers—remains uncertain (Poull, catalogue no. 89).
- **1261–1265:** Hôpital or Maison-Dieu de Gerbonvaux. An institutional and architectural project made jointly with his wife Sibille: they acquired Gerbonvaux in 1261, converted it into a hospital for poor people, travelers, and the sick, attached Bermont to it, and endowed it by the act of 1265. The hospital existed by 1263, so “1265” dates its surviving endowment record rather than necessarily its first operation (Poull, nos. 123–124, 130, 133–135).
- **1263–1265:** Union of the Maison de Bermont with Gerbonvaux. This was an institutional assemblage rather than a new building: Joffrey and Sibille acquired the existing religious house conditionally in 1263 and incorporated it into their hospital, requiring continuing worship in its chapel (Poull, nos. 133–134).

### Charters, dispositions, and other documentary acts

These documents are administrative acts as well as texts: they transfer property, establish obligations, or authenticate another person’s act. Most have no historical title, so descriptive labels are used rather than invented titles.

- **19 October 1231:** Declaration of liability to indemnify Duke Mathieu II of Lorraine. A financial obligation concerning the duke’s guarantee of 100 livres messins (Poull, no. 57).
- **19 April 1232:** Act appointing Duke Mathieu II as guarantor. A credit instrument for a debt of 180 livres provinois owed to two Sienese merchants (Poull, no. 58).
- **October 1232:** Foundation of Pierre de Bourlémont’s anniversary at Toul Cathedral. A liturgical memorial project for Joffrey’s father, preserved only through documentary notice (Poull, no. 59).
- **December 1232:** Grant of an annual rent to the chapter of Toul. The act is jointly attributed only to “F.,” lady of Bourlémont, and “J.,” her son; Poull identifies the latter as Joffrey, but the abbreviated original attribution should be retained as a qualification (Poull, no. 60).
- **1235:** Charter approving Ismer de Pargny’s alms to Mureau Abbey. An authentication of an annual grain rent rather than a gift originated by Joffrey (Poull, no. 61).
- **Eve of the Nativity of the Virgin, 1235:** Donation of the Domrémy tithe-share and the communal oven at Greux to Mureau. A dispositive charter made with the consent of Sibille, Félicité, and the bishop of Toul; it also confirms earlier family gifts (Poull, no. 62).
- **First Saturday after Easter, 1237:** Sealing of Hugues de La Fauche’s memorial donation. Joffrey and Guy de Joinville authenticate another lord’s foundation of an anniversary; it resists being called solely Joffrey’s work because the underlying disposition is Hugues’s (Poull, no. 63).
- **April 1238:** Donation of Joffrey’s possessions at Pargny-sous-Mureau. A jointly consented alms charter, excluding the homage owed by Pargny’s lords (Poull, no. 64).
- **1239:** Confirmation of Mureau Abbey’s mill at Frebécourt and earlier Bourlémont gifts. An original sealed charter survives or survived in the Château de Bourlémont archive (Poull, no. 67).
- **June 1239:** Guarantee for Pierre de Bauffremont toward Mureau Abbey. A surety act rather than a narrative text (Poull, no. 68).
- **12 June 1239:** Liege-homage declaration to the bishop of Toul. A performative feudal text recognizing inherited fiefs and the obligation to guard Brixey Castle (Poull, no. 69).
- **May 1242:** Notification of Hue de Mandres’s annual alms to Mureau. Joffrey’s charter authenticates the gift of one of his men (Poull, no. 71).
- **2 February 1244:** Notification of Gauthier de Pargny’s donation to Mureau. An authenticating seigneurial act (Poull, no. 73).
- **1244:** Notification of Damete de Bourlémont’s donation to Mureau. The charter records grain and other property held from Joffrey in fief (Poull, no. 74).
- **1248:** Approval of Regnier de Vaucouleurs’s donation to Mureau. A consent act known through a later manuscript collection (Poull, no. 77).
- **April 1248:** Consent and warranty for the sale of the Pargny tithes. Joffrey guarantees Mureau’s possession of a fief sold by Hugues de La Fauche (Poull, no. 78).
- **Ascension 1248:** Donation of revenues at Domrémy, Greux, and La Neuveville-en-Roises to Mureau. A surviving or formerly surviving original charter, subsequently confirmed by the count of Bar and bishop of Toul (Poull, nos. 79–81).
- **6 June 1249:** Guarantee for Duke Mathieu II’s obligations to Thibaut of Champagne. Joffrey issued one of several separate surety acts (Poull, no. 84).
- **6 June 1249:** Charter concerning Erart de Pargny’s grain rent to Mureau. A seigneurial notification and enforcement undertaking; the original survives at the Archives départementales de la Haute-Marne and is edited in the IRHT’s *Chartae Galliae* (Poull, no. 85; Gigot, no. 20).
- **September 1249:** Notification of Alexandre d’Ambonville’s sale of Bréchainville tithes. An authenticating charter for property held from Joffrey (Poull, no. 86).
- **October 1249:** Notification of Jean d’Ymer de Pargny’s sale of a close to Mureau. Another seigneurial authentication rather than Joffrey’s own conveyance (Poull, no. 87).
- **April 1250:** Donation of the Pargny tithes to Mureau. Joffrey is the donor, although the surviving catalogue notice derives from the bishop of Toul’s confirmation and records Sibille’s consent (Poull, no. 88).
- **February 1251:** Donation of tithes, wine, and other revenues to Mureau. A jointly consented alms charter covering La Neuveville-en-Roises, Corbesans, and wine from Charmes-la-Côte (Poull, nos. 92–93).
- **July 1251:** Undertaking to defend the Saint-Elophe family’s donation of Cirfontaine tithes. The principal charter is Joffrey de Joinville’s; Joffrey de Bourlémont’s contribution is the enforceable promise of guardianship, not authorship of the entire transaction (Poull, no. 94).
- **Octave of Saints Peter and Paul, 1251:** Approval of a donation to Chaumousey Abbey. A consent charter for lands at Ménil-en-Saintois given by Joffrey’s dependants Gosoin de Gironcourt and Marguerite (Poull, no. 95).
- **29 April 1252:** Guarantee for the Lorraine–Luxembourg–Bar treaty. A diplomatic surety undertaken for the duchess of Lorraine and Ferry III (Poull, no. 96).
- **April 1252:** Donation of the greater and lesser tithes of Pargny to Mureau. A perpetual alms grant made with Sibille’s consent (Poull, no. 97).
- **April 1252:** Consent and warranty for La Fauche property sold to Mureau. A legal authentication promising the abbey peaceful possession (Poull, no. 98).
- **October 1252–Candlemas 1253:** Exchange with Mureau at Rorthey-sous-Midrevaux. The transaction and Joffrey’s notification of it survive through two related acts; the October text is the chapter of Toul’s confirmation and therefore is not itself his creation (Poull, nos. 99–101).
- **February 1253:** Notification of Aubry d’Arches’s donation at Pargny. A seigneurial charter authenticating property held in Joffrey’s fief (Poull, no. 100).
- **July 1254:** Donation of property at Housselmont to Mureau. A dispositive alms charter (Poull, no. 102).
- **Thursday after Saint Gengoul, 1254:** Notification and approval of the Pargny family’s donation to Mureau. An authentication of another family’s gift (Poull, no. 103).
- **23 July 1255:** Act concerning Montbras Castle and lordship. Joffrey and Pierre de Villouxel jointly took the property back as a fief from the bishop of Toul; this is a feudal disposition, not evidence that Joffrey built the castle (Poull, no. 105).
- **19 December 1255:** Agreement to serve as seneschal of Navarre. A performative service contract made at Estella for a two-year term, with stipulated wages and loan (Poull, no. 107).
- **1256:** Approval of Errard and Joffrey de Pargny’s donation to Mureau. Joffrey’s act confirms a donation first notified by Sibille (Poull, nos. 108–109).
- **1256:** Mortgage or pledge of property to Thibaut of Champagne. The transaction is Joffrey’s, although Poull catalogues it through Sibille’s approval before the bishop of Toul (Poull, no. 110).
- **13 November 1257:** Treaty with James I of Aragon. A diplomatic act concluded by Joffrey in his capacity as seneschal of Navarre; it is a jointly negotiated instrument, not a privately authored composition (Poull, no. 111).
- **Late 1258:** Arbitration project concerning Champagne and Burgundy. Joffrey and Anseau de Traînel were appointed to settle the dispute; the catalogue establishes the commission but does not identify a surviving award, so this remains a project rather than a securely extant text (Poull, no. 112).
- **April 1259:** Exchange of dependent persons at Domrémy with Mureau Abbey. A transactional act that resists modern separation into “text” and “thing,” since the written instrument enacted an exchange of people under seigneurial dependency (Poull, no. 113).
- **February 1260:** Notification of a sale to Thibaut, count of Bar. A charter recording Joffrey’s conveyance of property previously acquired from Wauthier Berlouin of Domgermain (Poull, no. 114).
- **July 1260:** Notification of Jean de La Fauche’s grain donation and testament. Joffrey authenticates an alms gift involving property in his fief (Poull, no. 115).
- **September 1260:** Notification and consent concerning the Bréchainville tithes. A family-consented charter authenticating their sale to Mureau (Poull, nos. 116–117).
- **28 September 1260:** Mediation of the Choiseul–Bar truce. Joffrey was one of three mediators; the resulting truce therefore cannot be treated as his sole work (Poull, no. 120).
- **February 1261:** Notification of Conin de Domrémy’s donation of Gouécourt tithes. An authenticating seigneurial charter (Poull, no. 121).
- **February 1261:** Receipt for 80 livres from Mureau. A quittance acknowledging payment for Joffrey’s consent to the abbey’s purchase of the Bréchainville tithes (Poull, no. 122).
- **February 1262:** Confirmation of Mureau’s possessions and settlement of its woodland claims. A jointly consented charter that also cedes Joffrey’s claims in the woods of Domrémy and Greux (Poull, no. 126).
- **April 1262:** Three acts concerning Guillaume and Hodierne de Pargny’s conveyance. Joffrey successively notified the donation, identified its guarantors, and recorded their promise to obtain the heirs’ approval; these are distinct documentary acts, although closely bound parts of one transaction (Poull, nos. 127–129).
- **March 1263 or 1264:** Acknowledgment of a debt to Simon Pagnier. A credit instrument for 300 livres provinois, guaranteed by Thibaut of Champagne; the year depends on whether its March date fell before or after the contemporary New Year (Poull, no. 131).
- **1265:** Endowment act for the Hôpital de Gerbonvaux. A foundation protocol enumerating revenues and dependencies, regulating worship at Bermont, restricting alienation, and reserving appointment of the hospital’s master; Poull knew it through an archival analysis rather than an identified surviving original (Poull, no. 134).
- **July 1265:** Approval of Errard de Pargny’s donation to Gerbonvaux. An authenticating charter in favor of Joffrey’s hospital (Poull, no. 135).
- **September 1265:** Liege-homage declaration to Ferry III of Lorraine. A performative feudal act covering Hamainville and other augmented fiefs (Poull, no. 136).
- **December 1265:** Further liege-homage declaration to Ferry III. The catalogue treats this as a distinct act concerning several Lorraine fiefs (Poull, no. 138).
- **August 1266:** Notification and approval of Menissier and Sibille de Brixey’s donation to Gerbonvaux. A surviving or copied charter authenticating a gift to Joffrey’s foundation (Poull, no. 139).
- **October 1266:** Donation of one-third of the Bruley tithes to Mureau. A collective family charter made by Joffrey, Sibille, Pierre, and the younger Joffrey (Poull, no. 140).
- **October 1266:** Sale to Vaux-en-Ornois Abbey. The sale was made jointly by Joffrey and Sibille; its documentary form is known through a vidimus issued by Jean de Joinville (Poull, no. 141).
- **1230–1266:** Undertaking to serve the bishop of Metz. An imprecisely dated feudal commitment reported by de Pange; its wording and documentary survival are not specified by Poull (Poull, no. 143).
- **Before October 1266:** Testamentary gift of a dependent man from Housselmont to Mureau. The testament itself is not catalogued as surviving: Sibille and her sons executed this particular provision in April 1269, after Joffrey’s death (Poull, no. 147).

### Spoken counsel preserved by another writer

- **First half of 1248:** Counsel to Jean de Joinville before the Seventh Crusade. An oral admonition about a knight’s responsibility toward poorer crusaders, later preserved in Joinville’s memoir; it is Joffrey’s utterance but not an independently surviving text authored or written down by him (Poull, no. 82).

### Heraldic and sigillographic manifestations

- **Attested 1239:** Seal of Joffrey de Bourlémont. A brown-wax seal attached on a double tail to his confirmation of Mureau’s mill at Frebécourt; it is a material authentication device and self-representation, not a separately titled work (Poull, no. 67).
- **Attested 1248:** Burelé arms of silver and red. A heraldic manifestation attributed to “Geoffroy Gravier de Bourlémont” in a roll reportedly owned by the comte de Pange around 1900; because Poull cites it indirectly through de Pange, both the object’s present location and the exact transmission require caution (Poull, no. 76).

### Posthumous completion and limits of the corpus

- **April 1269:** Execution of Joffrey’s testamentary gift to Mureau. The charter was made by Sibille and their sons, not by Joffrey, and therefore resists inclusion as his work; it belongs here only as the posthumous manifestation of one of his projects (Poull, no. 147).
- **March 1269 or 1270:** Confirmation of the Gerbonvaux endowment. Joffrey’s sons acknowledged their father’s donations before Jean de Joinville and other ecclesiastical and secular authorities. This is evidence for Joffrey’s foundation, not a work authored by him (Poull, no. 148).
- **1456:** References to the later Bourlémont family in Joan of Arc’s rehabilitation proceedings. These concern Béatrice de Bourlémont and traditions surrounding the “Fairies’ Tree,” not anything made by Joffrey; the date is therefore a coda in the family’s documentary afterlife, not an extension of his oeuvre.
