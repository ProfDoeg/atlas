## Works by Theodor W. Adorno

### Philosophical books, dissertations, and essay-constellations

- **1924:** *Die Transzendenz des Dinglichen und Noematischen in Husserls Phänomenologie*. Doctoral dissertation: an early phenomenological study, later incorporated into the collected philosophical writings as a distinct text (GS 1).
- **1927:** *Der Begriff des Unbewußten in der transzendentalen Seelenlehre*. Philosophical habilitation thesis submitted to Hans Cornelius and then withdrawn after criticism; it survives as an abandoned academic project rather than an authorized book (NS I.1).
- **1931:** *Die Aktualität der Philosophie*. Inaugural lecture and philosophical program, not originally a book; it announces a materially oriented, interpretive philosophy (GS 1).
- **1932:** *Die Idee der Naturgeschichte*. Lecture-text developing “natural history” as a critical constellation rather than a systematic philosophy of history (GS 1).
- **1930s, exact date uncertain:** *Thesen über die Sprache des Philosophen*. Undated typescript of theses on philosophical language; its fragmentary, propositional form resists classification as a finished treatise (GS 1).
- **1933:** *Kierkegaard: Konstruktion des Ästhetischen*. Adorno’s completed habilitation study and first philosophical monograph; it appeared on the day Hitler became chancellor (GS 2).
- **1944–1947:** *Dialektik der Aufklärung: Philosophische Fragmente*. Collaborative philosophical obra with Max Horkheimer, first circulated as *Philosophische Fragmente* in 1944 and published in revised form in 1947; individual authorship cannot be cleanly divided throughout, although some excursuses and notes have identified principal authors (GS 3).
- **1942:** *Das Schema der Massenkultur*. A substantial draft intended for the culture-industry complex of *Dialektik der Aufklärung* but excluded from the published book; an autonomous fragment only by subsequent editorial constitution (GS 3).
- **1944–1947:** *Aufzeichnungen und Entwürfe*. Short philosophical, political, and anthropological pieces forming the final section of *Dialektik der Aufklärung*; collectively a constellation rather than a conventional chapter sequence (GS 3).
- **1944–1949; published 1951:** *Minima Moralia: Reflexionen aus dem beschädigten Leben*. A book of 153 numbered philosophical miniatures and aphorisms; each unit is a made text, but the ordered constellation is also the obra (GS 4).
- **1956:** *Zur Metakritik der Erkenntnistheorie: Studien über Husserl und die phänomenologischen Antinomien*. Philosophical monograph assembled from work begun in Oxford during the 1930s and extensively revised after the war (GS 5).
- **1963:** *Drei Studien zu Hegel*. Book-constellation comprising “Aspekte,” “Erfahrungsgehalt,” and “Skoteinos oder Wie zu lesen sei”; the three essays remain distinguishable works within the made whole (GS 5).
- **1964:** *Jargon der Eigentlichkeit: Zur deutschen Ideologie*. Polemical philosophical book directed at the language and social function of German existential authenticity (GS 6).
- **1966:** *Negative Dialektik*. Adorno’s principal completed philosophical monograph: deliberately anti-systematic, though constructed as a sustained book rather than a miscellany (GS 6).
- **1966–1969; published 1970:** *Ästhetische Theorie*. Unfinished book-project left in multiple manuscript layers and edited posthumously by Gretel Adorno and Rolf Tiedemann. It is an obra whose final sequence and textual boundaries are partly editorial rather than authorially settled (GS 7).
- **1967:** *Die Kunst und die Künste*. Essay and lecture on the erosion of boundaries among the arts; conceptually it addresses the Gesamtkunstwerk while remaining a discrete prose text (GS 10.1).
- **1969:** *Zu Subjekt und Objekt*. Philosophical essay published in *Stichworte*; a compact intervention rather than a systematic treatise (GS 10.2).
- **1969:** *Marginalien zu Theorie und Praxis*. Philosophical-political essay whose “marginal” form is intentional: it resists becoming a protocol for action (GS 10.2).

### Literary criticism and essay books

- **1955:** *Prismen: Kulturkritik und Gesellschaft*. Author-assembled essay collection; its essays retain separate identities while the juxtaposition makes a second-order critical obra (GS 10.1).
- **1958:** *Noten zur Literatur I*. First author-assembled constellation of literary essays, including *Der Essay als Form*; neither anthology nor unified treatise adequately describes its form (GS 11).
- **1959:** *Noten zur Literatur II*. Second literary-essay constellation, containing studies of Proust, Valéry, George, Eichendorff, and others (GS 11).
- **1961:** *Noten zur Literatur III*. Third constellation of literary criticism, including *Versuch, das Endspiel zu verstehen*; the individual essays and the ordered volume are both relevant made objects (GS 11).
- **1965:** *Noten zur Literatur IV*. Fourth author-published constellation of literary essays (GS 11).
- **Posthumously constituted:** *Noten zur Literatur: Nachgelassene Schriften*. Additional literary studies collected by editors in GS 11; this is not an author-planned fifth volume and should not be silently treated as one.
- **1963:** *Eingriffe: Neun kritische Modelle*. Nine essays configured by Adorno as a critical “model” book, spanning philosophy, education, culture, and social criticism (GS 10.2).
- **1967:** *Ohne Leitbild: Parva Aesthetica*. Author-assembled collection of aesthetic and cultural interventions; the Latin subtitle identifies its deliberately “small” critical form (GS 10.1).
- **1969:** *Stichworte: Kritische Modelle 2*. Second book of critical models and one of Adorno’s last author-published collections (GS 10.2).

### Music philosophy, criticism, and composer portraits

- **1932:** *Zur gesellschaftlichen Lage der Musik*. Extended two-part essay: simultaneously sociology, criticism, and a program for interpreting musical production under capitalism (GS 18).
- **1938:** *Über den Fetischcharakter in der Musik und die Regression des Hörens*. Music-sociological essay conceived in dialogue with Walter Benjamin’s work on technical reproducibility (GS 14).
- **1940–1941; left unfinished:** *Current of Music: Elements of a Radio Theory*. A projected English-language radio-theory book assembled from Princeton Radio Research Project manuscripts. Adorno never completed or authorized it as a finished book; the modern volume is an editorial reconstruction of the project (NS I.3).
- **1941:** *The Radio Symphony*. Study of symphonic broadcasting and altered listening, written within the Princeton Radio Research Project; part of the unfinished radio-theory project but also a self-contained research text (NS I.3).
- **1941:** *A Social Critique of Radio Music*. Radio-research essay joining empirical observation to social theory (NS I.3).
- **1941:** *On Popular Music*. Collaborative study credited to Adorno, with George Simpson participating in its English formulation; exact contribution should therefore not be represented as uncomplicated sole authorship (GS 14/NS I.3).
- **1941:** *The Problem of a New Type of Human Being*. Research proposal or project-outline addressed to Paul Lazarsfeld, not a completed empirical study (NS I.3).
- **1948–1949:** *Philosophie der neuen Musik*. Philosophical diptych opposing studies of Schoenberg and Stravinsky; its first half originated independently before the book was conceived (GS 12).
- **1937–1938; published 1952:** *Versuch über Wagner*. Critical composer monograph written largely in exile and later revised for publication (GS 13).
- **1956:** *Dissonanzen: Musik in der verwalteten Welt*. Author-assembled collection of essays on contemporary musical life and administered culture; enlarged editions altered its contents (GS 14).
- **1959:** *Klangfiguren: Musikalische Schriften I*. Collection of music-critical essays and composer studies (GS 16).
- **1960:** *Mahler: Eine musikalische Physiognomik*. Composer monograph that calls itself a “physiognomy,” combining analysis, philosophy, memory, and social interpretation rather than offering a biography (GS 13).
- **1962:** *Einleitung in die Musiksoziologie: Zwölf theoretische Vorlesungen*. A book made from twelve lectures: simultaneously lecture-cycle and systematic music-sociological project (GS 14).
- **1963:** *Quasi una fantasia: Musikalische Schriften II*. Music-essay constellation whose title signals a form between free fantasy and organized criticism (GS 16).
- **1963:** *Vers une musique informelle*. Lecture-essay proposing an “informal music”; programmatic but not a manifesto in the strict collective or declarative sense (GS 16).
- **1964:** *Der getreue Korrepetitor: Lehrschriften zur musikalischen Praxis*. Studies and practical reflections on performance and interpretation; they guide practice without constituting a simple protocol (GS 15).
- **1968:** *Berg: Der Meister des kleinsten Übergangs*. Composer portrait of Alban Berg, blending memoir, technical analysis, and philosophical criticism (GS 13).
- **1920s–1969; unfinished:** *Zu einer Theorie der musikalischen Reproduktion*. Long-running project of notes, drafts, and schemas for a theory of performance. Adorno never converted it into a finished treatise; the published work is an editorial construction from the papers (NS I.2).
- **1934–1969; unfinished:** *Beethoven: Philosophie der Musik*. Lifelong project of fragments and working notes toward a Beethoven book. The intention is unmistakable, but no completed Adornian book existed at his death (NS I.1).
- **1928–1962; posthumously assembled:** *Musikalische Schriften I–III*. The rubric in the collected writings gathers numerous separately made reviews, lectures, introductions, analytical essays, and broadcasts; it is an editorial corpus, not one work (GS 16).
- **1920–1968; posthumously assembled:** *Musikalische Schriften IV–V*. Further criticism, reviews, dictionary-like contributions, concert introductions, and occasional texts; many are brief manifestations of radio or periodical work rather than autonomous books (GS 17–18).
- **Posthumously assembled:** *Musikalische Schriften VI*. Miscellaneous and previously uncollected music writings; its unity belongs to the collected edition, not to an authorial project (GS 19).

### Sociology, social psychology, and empirical research

- **1943:** *The Psychological Technique of Martin Luther Thomas’ Radio Addresses*. Psychoanalytic content study of a right-wing American radio preacher, produced within the Institute of Social Research’s antisemitism research (GS 9.1).
- **1944–1945:** *Anti-Semitism and Fascist Propaganda*. Social-psychological essay and research intervention, not a standalone monograph (GS 8).
- **1945:** *Social Science and Sociological Tendencies in Psychoanalysis*. Essay connecting psychoanalytic revisionism to social accommodation (GS 8).
- **1949:** *The Stars Down to Earth: The Los Angeles Times Astrology Column*. Content analysis of newspaper astrology. Written from a 1952–1953 research series in its familiar expanded form, it is an empirical-critical study rather than a work about astrology as belief (GS 9.2).
- **1950:** *The Authoritarian Personality*. Large collaborative empirical study by Adorno, Else Frenkel-Brunswik, Daniel J. Levinson, and R. Nevitt Sanford, with a wider research staff. Adorno wrote identifiable portions, but the book as a whole must not be attributed to him alone (GS 9.1 contains only his contributions).
- **1950:** *Types and Syndromes*. Adorno’s principal typological chapter in *The Authoritarian Personality*; a component text within a collective research obra (GS 9.1).
- **1950:** *Freudian Theory and the Pattern of Fascist Propaganda*. Social-psychological essay using psychoanalysis to interpret fascist mass address (GS 8).
- **1952–1953:** *The Psychological Technique of the Los Angeles Times Astrology Column*. Research project underlying *The Stars Down to Earth*; its archive, manuscript forms, and later publication history do not yield a single uncomplicated publication date (GS 9.2).
- **1955:** *Gruppenexperiment*. Collective Institute for Social Research report on German political consciousness, edited with Friedrich Pollock and others. Adorno directed and interpreted important parts, but the empirical obra is institutionally collaborative.
- **1955:** *Schuld und Abwehr*. Adorno’s qualitative interpretation of material from the *Gruppenexperiment*, published as a separately identifiable study (GS 9.2).
- **1955:** *Zum Verhältnis von Soziologie und Psychologie*. Extended essay connecting social theory and psychoanalysis; subsequently supplemented by a postscript (GS 8).
- **1959:** *Theorie der Halbbildung*. Essay on socially produced pseudo-culture or “half-education,” not an educational protocol (GS 8).
- **1960:** *Kultur und Verwaltung*. Lecture-essay on the contradiction between cultural production and administration (GS 8).
- **1962:** *Zur Logik der Sozialwissenschaften*. Adorno’s principal paper in the German sociology “positivism dispute,” later embedded in a multi-author debate-volume (GS 8).
- **1962:** *Die revidierte Psychoanalyse*. Critique of revisionist psychoanalysis, first published in a collaborative lecture collection with Horkheimer (GS 8).
- **1965:** *Soziologische Exkurse*. Introductory sociology materials produced with the Institute for Social Research. It is an institutional teaching obra, not straightforwardly Adorno’s sole-authored book (GS 8).
- **1968:** *Soziologische Schriften I*. Author-era collection bringing together major social-theoretical essays; its contents are individually made texts, while the volume is a retrospective constellation (GS 8).
- **1969:** *Spätkapitalismus oder Industriegesellschaft?*. Congress address diagnosing contemporary social structure; also a framing contribution to the German Sociological Association proceedings Adorno edited (GS 8).
- **Posthumously assembled:** *Soziologische Schriften II*. Editorial collection of empirical studies, research memoranda, institutional work, and shorter sociology; it does not represent a single author-designed book (GS 9.1–9.2).

### Education, politics, public speech, and broadcast

- **1959–1969:** *Erziehung zur Mündigkeit*. A posthumously assembled series of broadcasts, talks, and conversations—many with Hellmut Becker—on education and democratic autonomy. The familiar book is not an author-composed treatise.
- **1959:** *Was bedeutet: Aufarbeitung der Vergangenheit*. Public lecture and broadcast intervention on the political misuse of “coming to terms” with the Nazi past (GS 10.2).
- **1960:** *Der Sinn der Aufarbeitung der Vergangenheit*. A related public intervention whose title and transmission overlap with the better-known 1959 lecture in some publication histories; editions must be checked rather than silently conflated.
- **1965:** *Tabus über dem Lehrberuf*. Lecture-broadcast on the social and psychological constraints surrounding teaching (GS 10.2).
- **1966:** *Erziehung nach Auschwitz*. Radio lecture subsequently published as an essay; a normative public intervention, not a curriculum or protocol (GS 10.2).
- **1967:** *Erziehung – wozu?*. Conversation with Hellmut Becker; authorship is dialogical and the printed text derives from a broadcast event.
- **1967:** *Aspekte des neuen Rechtsradikalismus*. Freely delivered lecture, preserved through recording/transcription and published posthumously; its later book form is an editorial manifestation, not the original object (NS V.1).
- **1968:** *Erziehung zur Entbarbarisierung*. Conversation with Hellmut Becker; a jointly produced broadcast-text rather than a sole-authored essay.
- **1968:** *Einleitung zu “Der getreue Korrepetitor” and other radio introductions*. Broadcast manifestations accompanying Adorno’s musical pedagogy; many survive as scripts or recordings and resist separation into stable literary works.
- **1969:** *Keine Angst vor dem Elfenbeinturm*. *Der Spiegel* interview: a jointly made journalistic conversation, not an Adorno-authored essay, despite its inclusion in the collected writings (GS 20.1).
- **1949–1968:** *Vorträge 1949–1968*. The posthumously edited corpus contains twenty surviving public lectures, including *Städtebau und Gesellschaftsordnung*, *Die Aktualität der Soziologie*, *Ad Proust*, *Zur Einführung in die neue Musik*, *Kultur und Culture*, *Probleme der Musikkritik*, *Die autoritäre Persönlichkeit*, *Ist Aberglaube harmlos?*, *Richard Strauss – Fragen der kompositorischen Technik*, and *Aspekte des neuen Rechtsradikalismus*. The corpus is an archival manifestation; each lecture originated as an event before becoming a text (NS V.1).

### University lecture-cycles

- **1957–1958:** *Erkenntnistheorie*. Recorded university lecture-cycle, published posthumously from transcripts; a pedagogical event made textual by editors (NS IV.1).
- **1958:** *Einführung in die Dialektik*. Lecture-cycle, not an introduction Adorno prepared as a book (NS IV.2).
- **1958–1959:** *Ästhetik*. First surviving extended aesthetics lecture-cycle (NS IV.3).
- **1959:** *Kants “Kritik der reinen Vernunft”*. Lecture-cycle on Kant, transmitted through recordings and editorial transcription (NS IV.4).
- **1959–1960:** *Einleitung in die Philosophie*. Introductory university course, distinct from any author-published monograph (NS IV.5).
- **1960:** *Philosophie und Soziologie*. Lecture-cycle articulating relations between the two disciplines (NS IV.6).
- **1960–1961:** *Ontologie und Dialektik*. Lecture-cycle centered on Heidegger and ontology (NS IV.7).
- **1961–1962:** *Ästhetik*. A second, distinct aesthetics lecture-cycle; it should not be merged with either the 1958–1959 course or the unfinished *Ästhetische Theorie* (NS IV.8).
- **1962–1963:** *Philosophische Terminologie*. Two-semester lecture-cycle later issued in volumes; the published division is editorial (NS IV.9).
- **1963:** *Probleme der Moralphilosophie*. Lecture-cycle known from transcript, not the moral-philosophy book Adorno never completed (NS IV.10).
- **1963–1964:** *Fragen der Dialektik*. Lecture-cycle, surviving as a mediated oral text (NS IV.11).
- **1964–1965:** *Philosophische Elemente einer Theorie der Gesellschaft*. Lecture-cycle toward a social theory Adorno never completed as a systematic book (NS IV.12).
- **1964–1965:** *Zur Lehre von der Geschichte und von der Freiheit*. Lecture-cycle joining philosophy of history to the problem of freedom (NS IV.13).
- **1965:** *Metaphysik: Begriff und Probleme*. Lecture-cycle, distinct from the metaphysical “meditations” in *Negative Dialektik* (NS IV.14).
- **1965–1966:** *Vorlesung über Negative Dialektik*. Fragmentarily preserved lecture-cycle associated with the book in progress; only part of the course survives (NS IV.16).
- **1968:** *Einleitung in die Soziologie*. Adorno’s final surviving sociology lecture-course (NS IV.15).
- **1950s–1960s:** *Kranichsteiner Vorlesungen*. Darmstadt lectures on new music, composition, form, and musical interpretation; an event-series and teaching practice subsequently constituted as a textual corpus (NS IV.17).
- **Dates vary; incompletely preserved:** *Stichworte und Stenogramm-Fragmente zu den nicht erhaltenen Vorlesungen*. Notes for lost lecture-cycles. These are material traces of projects, not substitute transcripts of the missing performances (NS IV.18).

### Musical compositions: works with opus numbers

- **1925–1928:** *Vier Gedichte von Stefan George für Singstimme und Klavier, op. 1*. Song-cycle for voice and piano; individual songs have separate dates, while the opus is the constructed whole.
- **1925–1926:** *Zwei Stücke für Streichquartett, op. 2*. Chamber-music diptych comprising *Bewegt* and *Variationen*; the movement dates do not follow their final order.
- **1928:** *Vier Lieder für eine mittlere Stimme und Klavier, op. 3*. Song-cycle for medium voice and piano.
- **1920–1929:** *Sechs kurze Orchesterstücke, op. 4*. Orchestral cycle assembled from miniatures composed across nearly a decade; “Sehr langsam” survives in versions associated with 1920 and 1928.
- **1938–1941:** *Klage: Sechs Lieder für Singstimme und Klavier, op. 5*. Song-cycle setting several poets; the extended date reflects composition and revision across the exile years.
- **1923–1942:** *Sechs Bagatellen für Singstimme und Klavier, op. 6*. Song-cycle assembled over an unusually long span rather than conceived in a single compositional period.
- **1944:** *Vier Lieder nach Gedichten von Stefan George für Singstimme und Klavier, op. 7*. Late song-cycle for voice and piano.

### Musical compositions: unnumbered, unfinished, and arranged works

- **1919:** *Für Sebastian Wedler*. Early piano piece; the dedication functions as its surviving title.
- **1920:** *Sechs Studien für Streichquartett*. Early chamber cycle predating the numbered quartet pieces.
- **1921:** *Streichquartett*. Early quartet, distinct from *Zwei Stücke für Streichquartett, op. 2*.
- **1921:** *Klavierstück*. Untitled piano composition conventionally identified by medium and date; the generic description should not be mistaken for an authorially distinctive title.
- **Early 1920s:** *Sonate für Violoncello solo*. Unfinished solo-cello sonata whose surviving beginning is marked “Durchaus rhapsodisch”; it is a fragmentary project, not a completed sonata.
- **1923–1945:** *Drei Gedichte von Theodor Däubler für vierstimmigen Frauenchor a cappella*. Choral cycle comprising *Dämmerung*, *Winter*, and *Oft*; its very broad date range reflects separate compositional stages.
- **1932–1933:** *Der Schatz des Indianer-Joe*. Planned Singspiel after Mark Twain, abandoned after two surviving orchestral songs. The project—not a completed opera—is the relevant work.
- **1932–1933:** *Totenlied auf den Kater*. Orchestral song surviving from the projected *Der Schatz des Indianer-Joe*.
- **1932–1933:** *Hucks Auftrittslied*. Song with chamber orchestra surviving from the projected *Der Schatz des Indianer-Joe*.
- **1941:** *Kinderjahr: Sechs Stücke aus op. 68 von Robert Schumann, für kleines Orchester gesetzt*. Orchestral arrangement of six Schumann piano pieces; it is Adorno’s made orchestration, not an original composition in the same sense as his opus works.
- **Dates vary:** *Einzelne Lieder und Klavierstücke*. The critical musical edition preserves additional juvenilia, occasional pieces, drafts, and fragments. Several lack stable authorial titles or completed states and therefore resist honest enumeration as fully bounded works.

### Correspondence, notebooks, and archival writing

- **1928–1940:** *Briefwechsel Theodor W. Adorno / Walter Benjamin*. A correspondence jointly made by two writers and only posthumously constituted as a book; individual letters are communications rather than literary works in the ordinary sense.
- **1923–1966:** *Theodor W. Adorno–Siegfried Kracauer: Briefwechsel*. Joint correspondence whose book form is editorial and retrospective.
- **1925–1935:** *Theodor W. Adorno–Alban Berg: Briefwechsel*. Collaborative epistolary record, important also to Adorno’s compositional apprenticeship.
- **1938–1969:** *Briefwechsel Theodor W. Adorno / Max Horkheimer*. Large working correspondence documenting jointly developed theoretical and institutional projects; neither author made the later published book.
- **1943–1955:** *Briefwechsel Theodor W. Adorno / Thomas Mann*. Correspondence, including Adorno’s musical counsel during the making of Mann’s *Doktor Faustus*. That counsel does not make Adorno co-author of Mann’s novel, though the novel’s musical conception bears his uncredited contribution.
- **Dates vary:** *Notizbücher*. Working notebooks containing ideas, observations, drafts, and records. They are made material objects and project-reservoirs, but many entries resist being called autonomous works.
- **Posthumously assembled:** *Vermischte Schriften*. Reviews, memorial pieces, prefaces, political statements, interviews, occasional prose, and previously uncollected texts gathered in GS 20. The editorial corpus documents many small manifestations but was never one Adorno-designed obra.
