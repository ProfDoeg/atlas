## Works by Dr Carl Jung of Mainz

### No securely attributable obra

- **Seventeenth century, if historical:** No named text, medical or legal treatise, alchemical preparation, building, image, instrument, or other made thing can presently be attributed to Dr Carl Jung of Mainz. The standard bibliography of seventeenth-century German-area printing, VD 17, supplies no identifiable work under this attribution; this negative result is not proof that he made nothing, only that no surviving or documented *obra* has been established. [VD 17 describes its scope and cataloguing principles here.](https://www.vd17.de/en/)

### Biographical tradition—not his work

- **1962–2026:** *Dr Carl Jung of Mainz*. This is a posthumous biographical manifestation rather than an *obra* by its subject: C. G. Jung’s memoir presents an otherwise unknown “Dr. med. et jur.” in Mainz and conjectures that he knew the writings of Michael Maier and Gerard Dorn. Even the death date is unstable—1645 in Hereward Tilton’s scholarly account, but 1654 in the published English translation of the memoir—and neither source supplies a work, fixed birth date, documentary genealogy, or evidence of alchemical authorship; Tilton accordingly calls him a “supposed ancestor” and treats Jung’s connection with Maier and Dorn as autobiographical imagining. [Tilton, pp. 22–23](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783110896572_A20712434/preview-9783110896572_A20712434.pdf); [Jung, *Memories, Dreams, Reflections*, “The Tower”](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/EB/I-J-K/Jung%20-%20Memories%2C%20Dreams%2C%20Reflections.pdf). It therefore resists inclusion as a work “by” Carl Jung of Mainz: what survives is a twentieth- and twenty-first-century tradition made about him.
