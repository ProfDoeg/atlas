## Works by Clovis

### Royal text and protocol

- **c. 507–508:** *Letter of Clovis to the bishops of Aquitaine* (*Epistolae Austrasicae* 2). A royal letter and protocol explaining how bishops could obtain protection for ecclesiastical property and intercede for captives during the Visigothic campaign; preserved only in a later copy, it is the sole surviving sincere act issued in Clovis’s name. ([Brühl](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1996_num_154_1_450817); [Halfond](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9781614510994.151/pdf?licenseType=open-access))

### Legal project

- **probably 507–511:** *Pactus legis Salicae* (“Pact of Salic Law,” earliest sixty-five-title form). A collective legal compilation traditionally treated as legislation produced under Clovis, not a text personally written by him. The attribution and date remain disputed: the surviving manuscripts begin only in the eighth century, the text names no identifiable king, and even the existence of a single recoverable “original” has been questioned. ([Renard](https://doi.org/10.3406/bec.2009.463964); [Bibliotheca legum](https://www.leges.uni-koeln.de/lex/lex-salica/))

### Ecclesiastical project

- **511:** *First Council of Orléans*. Clovis convened the council and presented matters for consideration, making it a royal-episcopal project rather than his authored text; the bishops, not Clovis, composed its thirty-one canons and their covering letter to him. The resulting legislation regulated asylum, clerical discipline, church property, royal ecclesiastical gifts, captives, and the integration of former Arian clergy. ([Halfond](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9781614510994.151/pdf?licenseType=open-access); [conciliar letter](https://scholarly.romanletters.org/letters/epistulae_merowingici/5/))

### Foundation and building

- **before 511:** *Basilica of the Holy Apostles Peter and Paul, Paris* (later Sainte-Geneviève). A church, royal mausoleum, and joint foundation associated with Clovis and Clotild rather than a solely authored object; Clovis was buried there before the building was complete, and the Merovingian structure itself no longer survives. Gregory of Tours supplies the principal early testimony, while the precise construction history and division of patronage remain unrecoverable. ([Gregory of Tours, *Histories* II](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/gregory-clovisconv.asp); [Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève](https://www.bsg.univ-paris3.fr/iguana/uploads/file/History%20and%20buildings.pdf))

### Later attributions that resist inclusion as works

- **date claimed as Clovis’s reign; actually later:** *Diploma or diplomas for Saint-Mesmin of Micy*. Two acts, surviving in variant recensions, attribute the monastery’s foundation or endowment to Clovis. Modern diplomatics rejects every surviving Clovis diploma as forged; Micy’s Clovis foundation is therefore a later institutional tradition, not a securely attributable obra, although a lost historical grant has sometimes been conjectured. ([Brühl](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1996_num_154_1_450817); [critical study of the cartularies](https://bibliographienumeriquedhistoiredudroit-ifg.univ-lorraine.fr/s/droit/item/133719))

- **498 as claimed by the document:** *Diploma for Saint-Jean de Réomé*. A purported royal grant to the monastic community associated with John of Réomé; the attribution to Clovis is rejected, so this is evidence for later memory and forgery rather than a work by the king. ([Kölzer, no. 3, as cited in the Semur manuscript study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/30457495_Le_manuscrit_1_de_la_Bibliotheque_municipale_de_Semur-en-Auxois_provenant_de_l%27abbaye_de_Saint-Jean_de_Reome_Moutiers-Saint-Jean_programme_pedagogique_de_recherche); [Brühl](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1996_num_154_1_450817))

- **date claimed as Clovis’s reign; actually later:** *Diploma for Saint-Pierre-le-Vif at Sens*. A purported foundation or privilege issued in Clovis’s name; it belongs to the rejected diplomatic corpus and cannot be counted as his work. ([Kurth’s inventory](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/JKL/Kurth/Clovis/Clovis_51.htm); [Brühl](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1996_num_154_1_450817))

- **date claimed as Clovis’s reign; actually later:** *Diploma for Sainte-Marie de Bethléem in the Gâtinais*. A supposed royal ecclesiastical privilege known only through later transmission; diplomatic criticism denies its authenticity, making it a work of later forgers rather than Clovis. ([Kurth’s inventory](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/JKL/Kurth/Clovis/Clovis_51.htm); [Brühl](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1996_num_154_1_450817))

- **date claimed as Clovis’s reign; actually later:** *Diploma for Saint-Hilaire of Poitiers*. A purported grant or privilege to the church of Saint Hilary; no sincere royal act survives behind its present form, so attribution to Clovis must be rejected rather than silently accepted. ([Kurth’s inventory](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/JKL/Kurth/Clovis/Clovis_51.htm); [Brühl](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1996_num_154_1_450817))

- **date claimed as Clovis’s reign; attested only much later:** *Grant or foundation associated with Fridolin*. A lost diploma inferred from a medieval saint’s Life was included among the *deperdita* of the modern MGH catalogue, but recent analysis argues that the described legal form belongs to the seventh century and is fictional in relation to Clovis. It therefore resists being called even a lost work of his. ([MGH catalogue discussion](https://www.hist.uzh.ch/dam/jcr%3Ab79ea925-223d-4d27-a78a-d5ec4abf9e13/Fridolinheute.pdf))
