# Emma Kunz: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary note

Emma Kunz left a limited conventional paper trail. She did not publish an autobiography, title or date most of her drawings, systematically record her patients, or leave a full written key to her methods. Much of the standard life story derives from oral testimony gathered after her death—especially from Anton C. Meier, whom she treated as a child and who later acquired much of her work, founded the Emma Kunz Centre, marketed AION A, and became her principal biographer.

This creates several overlapping records:

- **Documented fact:** supported by civil, bibliographical, institutional, photographic, business, or comparable records.
- **Reported fact:** stated by named witnesses or reputable secondary sources but not independently established here.
- **Claim by Kunz or her adherents:** a claim about clairvoyance, prophecy, healing, energy, or related phenomena whose occurrence or interpretation is not independently verified.
- **Allegation or disputed claim:** contested assertions, including later legal and financial disputes.
- **Myth or legend:** a story central to Kunz’s public image but supported primarily by retrospective testimony, repetition, or hagiographic literature.

Descriptions such as “artist,” “healer,” “researcher,” “radiesthesist,” “visionary,” “medium,” and “outsider artist” reflect different periods and interpreters. Kunz reportedly regarded herself principally as a researcher and natural healer, not as an artist in the ordinary professional sense.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and economic background

**Documented or consistently reported fact:** Emma Kunz was born into a poor weaving family in Brittnau. Her father is identified in standard biographies as **Stephan Oswald Kunz**, a hand weaver, and her mother as **Rosina Kunz, née Gubelmann**.

The family is usually described as having ten children, Emma being one of them. Accounts state that several siblings died young. The household belonged to the declining rural hand-weaving economy rather than the urban professional or artistic class.

No reliable evidence located for this dossier traces the Kunz or Gubelmann families beyond Emma’s parents, establishes noteworthy ancestry, or identifies inherited property or family wealth.

### Deaths in the family

The sources do not give a completely stable account:

- A frequently repeated biography says that **three siblings died in childhood**.
- A 2025 mathematical study, drawing on prior biographical literature and information associated with the Emma Kunz Foundation, states that **two sisters died in 1909 and her father died by suicide that year**.
- Other summaries say that her **father and a brother died by suicide when she was seventeen**.

These statements may describe separate deaths, but the presently consulted sources do not reconcile the names, exact dates, or sequence. The safest conclusion is that **Kunz experienced several sibling deaths and her father’s suicide during adolescence; the reported suicide of a brother remains insufficiently documented here**.

Later accounts refer to sisters with whom she lived again at Brittnau and to a brother near whom she subsequently resided. Their complete names and dates have not been established from the accessible sources.

### Marriage, partners, and descendants

No marriage, long-term partner, or child is documented. The story of her youthful attachment to a young man in America is discussed below because it is central to later biography but rests on retrospective reporting.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1892–1910

Emma Kunz grew up in Brittnau, an Aargau municipality near Zofingen. She attended the local primary school. No secondary, university, medical, scientific, or art-school education is documented.

Later biographers and the Emma Kunz Centre describe her as clairvoyant or unusually perceptive from childhood. Claims include telepathy, precognition, sensitivity to plants and minerals, and knowledge obtained without ordinary reading. One frequently repeated story says that she could place a hand upon a closed book and know its contents.

**Evidentiary status:** these are retrospective paranormal claims, not independently documented childhood observations. They became foundational to her posthumous identity.

The family deaths around 1909 constituted a major formative crisis. Shortly afterward—variously dated to age eighteen, 1910, or 1911—she reportedly began systematic work with a pendulum. Her early interests are described as prophecy, telepathy, diagnosis, and the perception of otherwise invisible forces.

There is no evidence that she joined a Spiritualist church, Theosophical lodge, Anthroposophical society, Rosicrucian organization, or other formal occult movement. Later curators have placed her beside Spiritualist and esoteric artists, but institutional comparison is not proof of organizational affiliation.

## Education and Formation

### Formal education

- Primary school in Brittnau.
- No documented professional qualification in medicine, pharmacy, botany, geology, psychology, art, mathematics, or engineering.
- No documented apprenticeship to a licensed physician or formally trained artist.

### Self-formation

Kunz’s education appears to have been largely autodidactic. Her working vocabulary combined:

- radiesthesia and pendulum divination;
- number symbolism;
- geometrical form;
- ideas of rhythm, polarity, transformation, and energy;
- natural remedies;
- an organic or holistic conception of relations among humans, plants, minerals, landscape, and cosmos.

Her surviving publications do not amount to a conventional medical or scientific system with replicable experimental protocols. Her notebooks and working materials remain important because she rarely explained individual drawings in writing.

### Teachers and influences

No securely documented personal teacher has emerged.

Jakob Friedrich Welti, in whose household she worked from 1923 to 1939, was a figurative painter and art critic. His proximity may have given Kunz sustained contact with an artistic household, but the sources do not establish that Welti formally taught her, directed her later geometric practice, or regarded her as a pupil.

Claims connecting her directly to Rudolf Steiner, C. G. Jung, Theosophy, Sri Aurobindo, Paracelsian medicine, or particular occult schools are generally interpretive or contextual unless otherwise specified. Her use of the pendulum belongs to a wider European radiesthetic culture that was already organized by the 1920s and 1930s.

---

## The American Journey, Reportedly 1911–1912

The standard story says that nineteen-year-old Kunz traveled to the United States in 1911 to follow a youthful romantic attachment. She supposedly failed to meet—or was disappointed by—the young man and returned to Brittnau in 1912.

The Waldstatt Emma Kunz path brochure cautiously says that she followed a young man but **“allegedly” never met him**. Other accounts simply state that she returned disappointed.

**Evidentiary status:** reported fact with legendary elaboration. The journey is consistently repeated, but the consulted sources do not supply a passenger list, passport record, immigration entry, destination, the man’s name, correspondence, or precise dates. Whether she actually met him cannot be established. It has subsequently functioned as the only major romantic episode in her biography and as a narrative of disappointed worldly attachment preceding a life devoted to healing and research.

---

## Factory Work and Early Healing, 1912–1923

After returning to Switzerland, Kunz supported herself through industrial employment. Sources variously call the workplace a knitting mill, hosiery factory, or embroidery/needlework concern in **Strengelbach**, near Brittnau. The usual period is **1912–1923**.

During these years she reportedly continued experimenting with the pendulum and increasingly advised or treated others. The surviving accounts do not provide a patient register, fees, tax records, advertisements, medical disciplinary file, or clear date for the beginning of regular practice.

Claims that her advice produced “miraculous” results belong principally to testimonial tradition. Kunz herself is said to have rejected the term *miracle*, arguing that her abilities reflected natural human capacities latent in everyone.

---

## The Welti Household and Literary Work, 1923–1939

From 1923 through 1939 Kunz spent at least the summer seasons in **Engelberg**, working in the household of painter and art critic **Jakob Friedrich Welti (1871–1952)**. Her role is variously described as:

- housekeeper;
- domestic employee;
- companion;
- increasingly trusted member of the household.

The exact transition from employee to companion is unclear. There is no good evidence that Kunz and Welti were romantic partners.

Her position in an artist’s household may be biographically significant. Nevertheless, her later drawings differ sharply from Welti’s figurative art, and no documentary record has been located showing that he devised her method.

### *Leben* (1930)

In 1930, Kunz published a small volume of poetry titled **_Leben_** (“Life”), through **Hans A. Gutzwiller AG, Zürich**. This is her earliest securely identifiable publication.

The book shows that she sought public expression before beginning the large graph-paper drawings. It does not establish a literary career: no substantial sequence of periodical publications, readings, reviews, or later poetry books has been located.

### Development of radiesthetic work

Accounts differ over whether she began pendulum practice in 1910 or 1933. The likely distinction is between early private use and later systematic investigation. By 1933, she is said to have used the pendulum to investigate “earth currents” or terrestrial forces.

---

## Beginning of the Large Drawings, 1938–1939

Around **1938**, aged about forty-six, Kunz began the large geometric drawings for which she is now known. She worked principally with:

- graphite pencil;
- colored pencil;
- carpenter’s pencil;
- chalk or oil crayon;
- large sheets or rolls of millimeter graph paper;
- straightedge and, according to some observers, compass;
- a pendulum used to locate determining points.

The reported procedure was:

1. She formulated a question concerning a person, illness, political matter, philosophical problem, or natural process.
2. The pendulum indicated points on the graph paper.
3. She connected and extended those points through linear construction.
4. She developed geometrical structures and color relations.
5. The completed image became a diagnostic, meditative, interpretive, or research instrument.

Anton C. Meier later recalled that she located points rapidly and without obvious calculation. The presence of regularly spaced line systems, reflections, rotations, symmetries, and mathematical envelopes has led later mathematicians to analyze her procedures geometrically. This analysis establishes mathematical structure in the finished work, not paranormal causation.

Some drawings allegedly required twenty-four to forty-eight hours of concentrated labor. Stories say that she worked through the night with little or no food, taking only liquids. These details come largely from Meier and local recollection.

Kunz did not title or date most sheets. She reportedly believed that fixing an individual explanation would prevent future reinterpretation. Their chronology is consequently difficult to reconstruct.

### “Penta”

Around this period, Kunz reportedly called herself or was called **Penta**, derived from the pentagram/fivefold form. The extent to which this was a public pseudonym, private working name, or nickname within her circle remains uncertain.

---

## Return to Brittnau and the Wartime Period, 1939–1947

Kunz left the Welti household around 1939 and returned to or near her sisters in Brittnau. Published chronologies commonly place her in Brittnau from approximately **1940 to 1947**.

She increasingly received people seeking help with physical or psychological problems. Her remedies reportedly included herbs, baths, compresses, dietary recommendations, work with the drawings, and pendulum diagnosis.

No reliable aggregate exists for:

- number of patients;
- fees or income;
- success and failure rates;
- adverse outcomes;
- clinical records;
- referrals to physicians;
- legal complaints.

That evidentiary absence is important. Testimonials cannot by themselves establish medical efficacy.

## Anton C. Meier, Würenlos, and AION A, 1941–1943

### The patient

Anton C. Meier (1936–2017), son of the proprietor of the Würenlos stoneworks, developed paralysis attributed in later accounts to poliomyelitis. His father sought Kunz’s help when Anton was about five or six.

Dates vary:

- 1941 in some summaries;
- 1942 in the Emma Kunz Centre narrative and Republik;
- 1943 in Blick and some retellings.

This may reflect onset, treatment, and recovery occurring over more than one year, but the discrepancy is not resolved.

### The quarry

Kunz traveled to the family’s historical sandstone or shell-limestone quarry at **Würenlos**. Using her pendulum, she identified a grotto as an unusually powerful location and selected a mineral material that was ground into powder. She named it **AION A**.

“Aion” is a Greek term connected with age, eternity, or an unlimited span. Promotional literature interprets the name as “boundless.”

### The cure narrative

Meier later wrote that Kunz applied the powdered stone mixed with water in compresses. He reported that strength returned to his legs after weeks, that he began with crutches, and that after several months he could walk and run.

**Documented facts:**

- Meier survived into adulthood and became closely associated with Kunz.
- He later attributed his recovery to her treatment and AION A.
- He acquired much of her artistic estate and founded the Emma Kunz Centre in 1986.
- AION A continues to be extracted and commercially distributed.

**Unresolved medical questions:**

- The dossier has not located the original diagnosis, hospital chart, virological confirmation, prognosis, rehabilitation record, or treating physician’s account.
- Poliomyelitis can produce outcomes ranging from transient weakness to lasting paralysis, with varying degrees of spontaneous and rehabilitative recovery.
- No controlled clinical evidence located here demonstrates that the quarry powder caused Meier’s recovery.
- The recovery is therefore **a documented testimonial**, not a medically established cure attributable to AION A.

Kunz’s relationship with the Meier family continued. Anton later became the central guardian and promoter of her legacy, creating both an unusually rich source of eyewitness information and a potential conflict of interest: his identity, businesses, foundation, and public standing were bound to the cure story.

---

## Later Central-Swiss Residences, 1947–1951

Sources place Kunz in **Lungern**, Canton Obwalden, from roughly **1948 to 1951**. A recent scholarly summary says that after leaving her sisters she first moved closer to a brother.

The precise sequence of addresses and household arrangements remains incompletely published. The Waldstatt municipal brochure states that she felt obstructed in Obwalden in the free exercise of her research and natural-healing methods.

This assertion should be treated as a reported explanation. The consulted sources do not reproduce an administrative order, prosecution, licensing decision, or medical complaint naming Kunz.

---

## Waldstatt, 1951–1963

### Move and house

In **1951**, Kunz built or had built a house in **Waldstatt**, Appenzell Ausserrhoden, and moved there. Appenzell Ausserrhoden had a comparatively permissive environment for non-physician natural healing, making it a destination for practitioners unable to work as freely elsewhere.

She reportedly regarded the nearby **Säntis** as a sacred mountain. From her Waldstatt house she continued private consultations, drawing, plant experiments, and preparation of remedies.

No evidence located indicates that she became wealthy. Her early life was poor and working-class; her later house represents a significant material achievement, but construction cost, ownership financing, debts, patient fees, and estate value at death are not established.

### Plant experiments

Kunz experimented with marigolds or calendulas. She claimed that she could “polarize” plants or give them a precise task, including producing multiple daughter blossoms. Photographs from 1953 show unusual multi-headed flower forms and have become part of her archive and exhibition history.

**Evidentiary status:** the photographed plants are documentary objects; the claim that pendular or psychic direction produced their morphology is unverified. No controlled botanical protocol, independent replication, genetic analysis, or blinded comparison has been located.

### Healing practice

Kunz used drawings as diagnostic and contemplative devices. Visitors reportedly brought questions concerning illness, psychology, personal decisions, and public affairs. Her activities remained private and personal rather than institutional: no clinic, school, formal professional organization, or documented staff is known.

The record is dominated by successful testimonials. Failed treatments, patients who did not improve, and possible harms are not systematically documented. This may reflect both the informal practice and the selective preservation of admiring testimony.

### Political prophecy

Later literature says that some drawings answered political questions or prophesied historical events. Claims include anticipation of war, the atomic bomb, or future recognition of her art.

Apart from the prediction that her drawings belonged to the twenty-first century, the sources generally do not provide securely dated, sealed, pre-event interpretations. Post-event readings are therefore vulnerable to retrospective matching.

No evidence located establishes party membership, electoral activity, political office, wartime resistance, collaboration, espionage, or intelligence connections.

## Publications of 1953

In Waldstatt in 1953, Kunz self-published two small works explaining aspects of her conceptions:

1. **_Neuartige Zeichnungsmethode: Gestaltung und Form als Mass, Rhythmus, Symbol und Wandlung von Zahl und Prinzip_**  
   Approximate English: “New Method of Drawing: Configuration and Form as Measure, Rhythm, Symbol, and Transformation of Number and Principle.”

2. **_Das Wunder schöpfender Offenbarung: Gestaltung und Form als Mass, Rhythmus, Symbol und Wandlung von Zahl und Prinzip_**  
   Approximate English: “The Miracle of Creative Revelation: Configuration and Form as Measure, Rhythm, Symbol, and Transformation of Number and Principle.”

Catalogues sometimes print **_schöpferischer Offenbarung_** rather than **_schöpfender Offenbarung_**, and some reproduce “Offenbarung” as part of the subtitle to the first work. Copies should be collated before treating these variants as distinct editions.

These publications constitute Kunz’s principal first-person theoretical evidence. They are dense, schematic, and not a comprehensive key to individual drawings. Her formulation joined form, number, rhythm, transformation, and principle in a system she treated as research rather than aesthetic production alone.

## Artistic and Intellectual Development

### Relationship to art

Kunz did not exhibit her drawings during her lifetime. She reportedly did not consider them autonomous art objects. They were:

- records of questions and responses;
- maps of energetic relations;
- diagnostic aids;
- instruments for meditation or concentration;
- models of universal order;
- sometimes sources of prophecy.

The modern art world later detached or partially detached the sheets from this working setting and presented them as geometric abstraction, visionary art, outsider art, mediumistic art, or an alternative modernism.

### Formal characteristics

Recurring features include:

- squares and rectangles;
- crosses;
- triangles and pyramidal arrangements;
- circular and radial structures;
- bilateral and rotational symmetry;
- grids and diagonals;
- layered transparent-looking planes;
- repeated line families producing curved envelopes;
- strongly organized color fields.

A 2025 article in *The Mathematical Intelligencer* analyzes the envelope curves created by families of straight lines in Kunz’s work. It demonstrates that their apparent curves can arise from systematic linear constructions and that reflection, translation, rotation, and color yielded considerable variation.

### Gender symbolism

A critical doctoral analysis notes that Kunz associated men with odd numbers, verticality, fire, and spiritual rays, while women were associated with even numbers, two, water, matter, earth, and the horizontal. The author argues that at least some works operate more as schematic doctrine than as art.

This is a substantive art-historical criticism, not evidence of misconduct. It complicates later presentations of Kunz as automatically emancipatory because she was an overlooked woman.

### Spiritualism and mediumship

Curators frequently group Kunz with Georgiana Houghton and Hilma af Klint because all three produced abstract forms in connection with hidden forces or higher knowledge. Important differences remain:

- Houghton identified with Spiritualism and attributed works to spirits.
- Af Klint had academic art training and worked within organized esoteric settings.
- Kunz had no formal art training and described the pendulum as a research and healing instrument.

Calling Kunz a “Spiritualist” in a strict institutional sense therefore goes beyond the available evidence. “Radiesthesist,” “visionary healer,” or “practitioner of an individual esoteric-natural system” is more precise.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Jakob Friedrich Welti

**Documented relationship:** employer and household head, Engelberg, 1923–1939. Kunz served as housekeeper and later reportedly as companion. Welti’s profession placed her in proximity to artistic life, but no formal teacher-pupil relationship is established.

### Anton C. Meier

**Documented relationship with contested medical interpretation:** patient from childhood, friend, witness, collector, biographer, and posthumous promoter.

Meier acquired approximately 400 drawings, developed the Würenlos site, founded the Emma Kunz Centre in 1986, promoted AION A, and published a major biography. His testimony preserves otherwise unavailable information but is inseparable from his belief in Kunz and from later commercial and institutional interests.

### Emil Schmidlin and the Meier stoneworks

Accounts identify the quarry proprietor who approached Kunz as part of the Meier/Schmidlin stone-industry family structure. Naming varies in popular retellings. The legal and commercial history of the companies should not be conflated with Kunz’s personal ownership: no evidence shows that she owned shares in the quarry or the later healing-products business.

### Patients and villagers

Kunz’s network included patients, relatives, neighbors, and local witnesses. Most remain unnamed in the published summaries. Heiny Widmer’s 1973 catalogue reportedly invoked unnamed informants, limiting independent verification.

### Harald Szeemann and Heiny Widmer

Neither knew Kunz as an active artist in the ordinary sense before her death. Widmer presented the first public exhibition at the Aargauer Kunsthaus in 1973. Szeemann helped situate her within “individual mythologies,” outsider positions, and expanded definitions of art.

### Patronage

No conventional lifetime patron is documented. The major patronage structure arose posthumously through Anton C. Meier, the quarry businesses, the Centre, and the Foundation.

## Companies, Institutions, Projects, Property, and Financial Interests

### Kunz’s lifetime

No company, incorporated practice, patent, investment portfolio, or industrial enterprise is documented in her name.

Her material interests appear to have included:

- income from factory work;
- domestic employment;
- possible patient payments or gifts;
- her Waldstatt house;
- copyright and physical ownership interests in her drawings and publications;
- possible interests associated with AION A, although no lifetime licensing agreement has been identified.

### AION A

AION A is powdered mineral material extracted from the Würenlos quarry and sold as a natural remedy. Promotional claims have included use for joint, muscular, tendon, connective-tissue, rheumatic, and inflammatory skin conditions.

The continued commercialization is posthumous. Modern corporate and trademark activity includes **Emma Kunz Heilprodukte AG**. A United States trademark application filed in December 2023 contained extremely broad lists of proposed pharmaceutical and medical uses; the application was recorded as abandoned in February 2025 for failure to respond or late response. A trademark filing is not evidence that the listed treatments are clinically effective or approved for those indications.

### Emma Kunz Centre and Museum

- **1986:** Anton C. Meier founded the Emma Kunz Centre at the Würenlos quarry.
- **1991:** the museum officially opened.
- The institution maintains the grotto, displays rotating selections of Kunz’s drawings, preserves archives, and supports the sale or distribution of AION A.
- The local municipality treats the Centre as an important cultural destination.
- Approximately seventy works may be displayed in rotating presentations.

### Emma Kunz Foundation

The Foundation controls or manages much of the artistic estate and associated rights. It is developing an online catalogue raisonné intended to record every known drawing.

A Swiss cultural-heritage distinction praised the Foundation’s preservation and responsible stewardship of the estate. The 2021 exhibition catalogue *Kosmos Emma Kunz* received recognition among the most beautiful German books of that year.

### Ownership after Anton Meier

After Meier’s death in 2017, control of the estate, companies, quarry property, and foundation became entangled in an inheritance dispute described under controversies.

---

## Death

Emma Kunz died at her Waldstatt home or residence on **16 January 1963**. She was buried at Brittnau.

The consulted institutional and scholarly sources do not state a verified medical cause of death. No death certificate or medical report was located. Assertions about a particular illness should therefore not be supplied without further archival evidence.

She died before any public exhibition of her drawings and before their recognition as art. No reliable valuation of her estate at death has been found.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, and Financial Questions

### 1. Medical efficacy of AION A

**Claim:** Kunz discovered a healing mineral and used it to reverse Anton C. Meier’s poliomyelitis-related paralysis.

**Evidence for the claim:**

- Meier’s detailed retrospective testimony.
- His lifelong conviction that Kunz and the powder enabled his recovery.
- Continued family and institutional preservation of the story.
- Subsequent testimonials and continuing commercial use.

**Evidence limitations:**

- No original clinical records were located.
- No controlled trial establishes causation.
- Natural recovery and rehabilitation are not excluded.
- Promotional accounts use definitive words such as “healed” where the available evidence supports only “recovered following treatment.”

**Classification:** documented treatment and recovery; causal efficacy unverified.

### 2. Broader healing and paranormal claims

Claims include accurate diagnosis through drawings, telepathy, prophecy, reading closed books, perception of energy fields, psychically directed plant growth, and therapeutic effects of the grotto.

No criminal fraud finding, medical malpractice judgment, or contemporary exposure of deliberate deception was located. Equally, no independently replicated scientific evidence validates the paranormal mechanisms.

**Classification:** testimonial claims, not established scientific facts; no evidence located of adjudicated fraud by Kunz.

### 3. Absence of negative outcome records

Kunz’s preserved narrative is highly selective. Published accounts emphasize successful cases, while failures and adverse outcomes are virtually absent. This is a classic survivorship and archive-selection problem. It does not prove fraud, but it prevents meaningful assessment of efficacy or safety.

### 4. Art-world reclassification

Kunz did not present the drawings publicly as fine art. Posthumous institutions have exhibited and marketed them within modernism and contemporary art.

Critics have raised questions about:

- removing functional diagrams from their healing context;
- describing Kunz as an “artist” against her apparent self-understanding;
- assimilating her to celebrated women abstractionists despite significant historical differences;
- treating unverifiable esoteric biography as museum narrative;
- commercial coexistence between art exhibitions and sales of AION A.

This is an interpretive and institutional controversy, not a legal accusation against Kunz.

### 5. The Meier inheritance litigation

This controversy arose decades after Kunz’s death and concerned Anton C. Meier’s estate rather than misconduct by Kunz.

Reported facts include:

- Meier died in July 2017.
- His estate included or controlled the Emma Kunz collection, approximately 130,000 square metres of property around the Würenlos quarry, nine historically significant buildings, and companies connected with stoneworks and healing products.
- Meier had three reported children: Michael Gandola, born outside marriage in 1958, and two children of a later marriage.
- His testament sought creation of a foundation for Kunz’s art while property was assigned within the family structure.
- Gandola was not named and sued for recognition and a compulsory share.
- The dispute concerned the former Swiss institution of *Zahlvaterschaft*, under which a biological father could pay maintenance without creating ordinary legal filiation and inheritance rights.
- Gandola filed litigation in March 2019 after settlement efforts failed.
- Press reports in 2021 placed the dispute before the Baden District Court and described the overall assets as potentially worth several million Swiss francs, while stressing that transaction values were undisclosed.
- Foundation and company officers reportedly overlapped, including Andreas Ritter, Markus Siegrist, and Beat Curti.
- Counsel for Gandola alleged that the Foundation would benefit from historically discriminatory law. Opposing parties reportedly maintained that Gandola was not a legal heir and had used the wrong procedural action.

**Classification:** documented litigation and competing legal claims. The sources consulted do not provide a final judgment or reliable post-2021 disposition. No conclusion about wrongdoing by the Foundation or corporate officers should be inferred without the court record.

### 6. Corporate and trademark claims

The 2023 AION A trademark application listed an unusually broad range of pharmaceutical uses, including serious diseases and psychiatric conditions. The application’s 2025 abandonment is an administrative trademark outcome, not a regulatory adjudication of medical efficacy and not a fraud finding.

### 7. Adversarial-search result

Dedicated searches in German and English for controversy, scandal, accusation, lawsuit, fraud, conspiracy, criticism, criminal conduct, and recent reporting located:

- criticism of unverified healing and paranormal claims;
- lack of scientific evidence for AION A;
- art-historical criticism of museum framing;
- posthumous inheritance litigation and opaque asset values;
- expansive posthumous commercial claims in trademark documentation.

They did **not** locate reliable evidence that Kunz was arrested, imprisoned, formally investigated for fraud, convicted, sued during her lifetime, involved in money laundering, political violence, intelligence work, or an organized criminal enterprise.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, and Disputed Narratives

### The clairvoyant child

**Story:** Kunz exhibited telepathy and clairvoyance from early childhood.

**Appearance and circulation:** posthumous biographies, Centre literature, exhibition texts, press profiles.

**Evidence:** retrospective testimony; no contemporary childhood record located.

**Reputational effect:** establishes her as innately gifted and frames later practice as discovery rather than learned occult technique.

### Reading a book by touch

**Story:** She could place her hand on a book and know its contents.

**Circulation:** Foundation representatives and popular media.

**Evidence:** no controlled demonstration or contemporary report located.

**Classification:** paranormal legend.

### The failed American romance

**Story:** Kunz crossed the Atlantic for a young man but either never met him or returned rejected.

**Evidence:** the journey itself is consistently reported; passenger and immigration documentation were not supplied by the consulted sources. The emotional details vary.

**Effect:** supplies a renunciation narrative in an otherwise celibate biography.

### The miraculous cure of Anton Meier

**Story:** A paralyzed boy walked after Kunz treated him with AION A compresses.

**Evidence:** strong first-person retrospective testimony from Meier; absence of medical documentation sufficient to establish diagnosis, prognosis, or causation.

**Effect:** central founding myth of the Centre, quarry, commercial remedy, and estate. It is simultaneously a patient testimony, institutional origin story, and marketing narrative.

### The power grotto

**Story:** Kunz’s pendulum detected extraordinary harmonizing energy in the Würenlos grotto; visitors still experience physiological or spiritual effects there.

**Evidence:** visitor testimony, Kunz tradition, and anecdotal experiences. An SRF report playfully noted microphone interference but did not establish an anomalous physical field.

**Effect:** turned an industrial quarry into a pilgrimage, wellness, and cultural destination.

### Prophecy of the atomic bomb

**Story:** a drawing anticipated the atomic bomb or nuclear age.

**Evidence:** no securely dated, pre-Hiroshima interpretive document located. The drawings are generally untitled and undated, encouraging retrospective attribution.

**Classification:** post-event interpretive claim.

### “My work is for the twenty-first century”

**Story:** Kunz predicted that her drawings would only be understood or recognized in the twenty-first century.

**Evidence:** attributed by Meier and repeated extensively; no contemporaneous recording located in the consulted sources.

**Against/qualification:** she was already exhibited successfully in 1973 and internationally before 2000, although major twenty-first-century exhibitions did substantially expand recognition.

**Effect:** has become the signature prophecy validating her posthumous success.

### The invitation from Sri Aurobindo’s circle

**Story:** representatives of the Indian mystic Sri Aurobindo traveled to Brittnau and invited Kunz to India as a master; she declined, saying that each person should work within their own culture.

**Appearance:** Juri Steiner’s 1996 *Parkett* essay, apparently dependent on Kunz biographical tradition.

**Evidence:** no names, date, correspondence, travel record, or Aurobindo-ashram documentation located.

**Classification:** unverified report, possibly hagiographic.

**Effect:** casts Kunz as a spiritual equal of an internationally recognized mystic who consciously chose local rootedness over discipleship and fame.

### The “artist who was not an artist”

**Story:** Kunz was a major neglected pioneer of abstraction.

**Evidence:** the drawings unquestionably exist and predate their public exhibition. Their aesthetic importance is an art-historical judgment. She had no formal training and apparently did not understand them as conventional art.

**Effect:** central to feminist recovery, alternative-modernist, outsider-art, and spiritual-abstraction narratives. It can illuminate her exclusion while also retroactively imposing a category she did not prioritize.

## Works by Emma Kunz

### Authored publications

1. **_Leben_**. Zürich: Hans A. Gutzwiller AG, 1930.  
   Poetry collection. Genuine lifetime publication.

2. **_Neuartige Zeichnungsmethode: Gestaltung und Form als Mass, Rhythmus, Symbol und Wandlung von Zahl und Prinzip_**. Waldstatt, self-published, 1953.  
   Genuine lifetime publication. The precise punctuation and inclusion of “Offenbarung” vary in later catalogues.

3. **_Das Wunder schöpfender Offenbarung: Gestaltung und Form als Mass, Rhythmus, Symbol und Wandlung von Zahl und Prinzip_**. Waldstatt, self-published, 1953.  
   Genuine lifetime publication. Some later sources give *schöpferischer* rather than *schöpfender*.

### Drawings

Approximately 500–600 works are often cited, although the physical and catalogue record is still being consolidated. Most are:

- untitled;
- undated;
- identified posthumously by catalogue numbers such as *Work No. 013*, *095*, *099*, *307*, *396*, or *507*;
- executed on millimeter paper with graphite, colored pencil, carpenter’s pencil, chalk, or oil crayon.

The numbers are posthumous inventory designations, not titles assigned by Kunz.

The Foundation’s online catalogue raisonné is the principal evolving resource. A 2020 Kornfeld sale entry for *Werk Nr. 507* stated that the Foundation knew the work and would include it in the catalogue, illustrating the continuing discovery and registration of sheets outside the core holding.

### Notebooks and research material

Surviving material includes:

- notebooks or workbooks;
- pendulums;
- an organizing wooden board;
- photographs of experimental plants;
- remedy containers and related objects;
- photographic negatives and portraits;
- archival testimony collected after her death.

Some working notebooks remain unpublished. Their existence does not mean that they supply a complete decoding system.

### Botanical photographs

Photographs dated about 1953 document marigolds associated with her “polarization” experiments. Kunz’s authorship of every photograph is not always stated; these should be described as archive photographs documenting her project rather than automatically as photographs taken by her.

### AION A

AION A is a named material or remedy associated with Kunz’s discovery and instructions, not a conventional authored artwork or patented invention. No patent in her name was located.

## Exhibitions and Posthumous Reception

### 1973–1976: discovery by the art world

Kunz’s drawings were first publicly exhibited in **1973**, ten years after her death, at the **Aargauer Kunsthaus**, Aarau, under the title **_Der Fall Emma Kunz_**. Director Heiny Widmer’s use of “case” suited a period interested in outsider art and individual mythology.

Exhibitions followed in Vaduz and at the Musée d’Art Moderne in Paris in 1975–1976. Harald Szeemann helped place her within an expanded art-historical field.

### 1986–1993: institutional consolidation

- Emma Kunz Centre founded, 1986.
- Museum opened, 1991.
- A Swiss postage stamp using *Work No. 095* appeared in 1993.
- The major Centre-sponsored *Leben. Werk* publication appeared the same year.

### 1999: Zurich

The Kunsthaus Zürich exhibition **_Steiner, Beuys, Belyi, Kunz: Richtkräfte für das 21. Jahrhundert_** placed her alongside Rudolf Steiner, Joseph Beuys, and Andrei Bely.

This was a curatorial association, not evidence that Kunz met or collaborated with them.

### 2005–2006: international abstraction history

The Drawing Center’s **_3 x Abstraction_** joined Kunz with Hilma af Klint and Agnes Martin. It traveled from New York to the Santa Monica Museum of Art and the Irish Museum of Modern Art in Dublin.

The exhibition argued that the three used abstraction to structure philosophical, scientific, and transcendental ideas rather than pursuing formalism alone.

### 2009–2013

Works appeared in Japan at the Kawamura Memorial Museum of Art, at the 2010 Gwangju Biennale, at Zentrum Paul Klee, the New Museum in New York, and Fundación “la Caixa” in Barcelona and Madrid.

Kunz was included in Massimiliano Gioni’s **_The Encyclopedic Palace_** at the 55th Venice Biennale in 2013.

### 2018–2022

- **_Weltempfänger_**, Lenbachhaus, Munich, 2018–2019.
- **_Emma Kunz: Visionary Drawings_**, Serpentine Galleries, London, 23 March–19 May 2019; more than forty drawings and the first British solo exhibition.
- Muzeum Susch, 2019.
- **_Zahl, Rhythmus, Wandlung_**, Kunsthalle Ziegelhütte, Appenzell, 2020.
- **_Kosmos Emma Kunz_**, Aargauer Kunsthaus, 2021, followed by Tabakalera, San Sebastián, 2022.

These exhibitions increasingly placed Kunz in dialogue with contemporary artists rather than treating her only as outsider or medium.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

Kunz’s reputation passed through several stages:

1. **Local healer and eccentric researcher:** her principal lifetime identity.
2. **A “case” of outsider production:** the framing used in 1973.
3. **Individual mythology and expanded art:** reinforced by Szeemann.
4. **Spiritual or mediumistic abstraction:** comparisons with Houghton, af Klint, and Martin.
5. **Internationally exhibited modernist precursor:** strengthened by New York, Venice, London, Munich, and Susch exhibitions.
6. **Contemporary artists’ artist:** recent exhibitions emphasize responses by living artists.
7. **Commercial-spiritual brand:** AION A, the grotto, museum, workshops, and healing narratives.
8. **Object of mathematical study:** analysis of line envelopes and transformations.
9. **Contested institutional legacy:** questions concerning medical claims, art-world reframing, estate control, inheritance litigation, and the boundary between cultural stewardship and commercial promotion.

Her obscurity during life is real, but the claim that she was simply suppressed by the art world requires qualification: she did not seek ordinary artistic recognition, did not exhibit, and understood the sheets as working instruments. Her exclusion from canonical abstraction and her nonparticipation in its institutions are related but not identical facts.

The survival of a large body of fragile graph-paper works is chiefly due to posthumous preservation by Meier and the Foundation. That concentration has preserved the ensemble but has also allowed the principal biographical, commercial, and interpretive narratives to develop within a single interdependent institutional structure.

## Atlas Connections

Only connections supported by the consulted sources are listed. Most names in the atlas roster have no documented crossing with Kunz.

### Emma Kunz

- **Nature:** the present subject.
- **Evidence:** documented fact.

### Hilma af Klint

- **Nature:** posthumous curatorial association, not a lifetime meeting. Kunz and af Klint were exhibited together in *3 x Abstraction* (New York, Santa Monica, Dublin, 2005–2006) and *Weltempfänger* (Munich, 2018–2019).
- **Evidence:** documented fact.
- **Qualification:** the artists worked independently; no correspondence, meeting, or acknowledged lifetime influence has been established.

### Rudolf Steiner

- **Nature:** posthumous shared exhibition in *Steiner, Beuys, Belyi, Kunz: Richtkräfte für das 21. Jahrhundert*, Kunsthaus Zürich, 1999.
- **Evidence:** documented fact.
- **Qualification:** no documented meeting, correspondence, organizational affiliation, or direct influence has been found.

### Paul Klee

- **Nature:** Kunz’s works were exhibited at Zentrum Paul Klee in Bern in 2012.
- **Evidence:** documented institutional connection.
- **Qualification:** this is a posthumous venue connection only; no evidence establishes that Kunz met Klee or studied his work.

Sri Aurobindo is not in the supplied atlas roster, but the alleged invitation from his representatives is sufficiently prominent to record elsewhere in the dossier. It remains unverified.

Jung, Rudolf Steiner during life, Hilma af Klint during life, Johannes Itten, Paul Klee during life, Wassily Kandinsky, Paracelsus, Helena Blavatsky, G. I.
