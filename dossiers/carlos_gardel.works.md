## Works by Carlos Gardel

### Recorded-performance corpus

- **1912:** *Fifteen Columbia acoustic recordings*. Gardel’s first commercial recording project comprised fifteen matrices—fourteen are known to survive—of estilos, canciones, valses and other canción criolla forms. This is a body of recorded performances rather than fifteen compositions by Gardel.
- **1917:** *Twenty-five Odeón acoustic recordings*. Solo and Gardel–Razzano performances; the catalogue warns that the music credited to Gardel for *Yo soy como el ave cisne* was actually by Santiago Rocca.
- **1918:** *Five Odeón acoustic recordings*. A small group of Gardel–Razzano vocal-duet manifestations.
- **1919:** *Thirteen Odeón acoustic recordings*. These include Gardel’s recorded performance of *De vuelta al bulín*; authorship of the repertory belongs variously to Gardel, Razzano and other songwriters.
- **1920:** *Twenty-three Odeón acoustic recordings*. A mixed canción-criolla and emerging tango-song corpus.
- **1921:** *Twenty-three Odeón acoustic recordings*. Recorded performances, chiefly with guitars, not an “album” in the later LP sense.
- **1922:** *Forty-four Odeón acoustic recordings*. Individual matrices and alternate performances constitute the made objects.
- **1923:** *Fifty-two Odeón acoustic recordings*. A year’s recording corpus spanning tangos and rural or criollo song forms.
- **1924:** *Fifty-nine Odeón acoustic recordings*. The corpus includes Gardel’s vocal manifestation of *La cumparsita*; it is not authorship of Gerardo Matos Rodríguez’s composition.
- **1925:** *Ninety-one Odeón acoustic recordings*. One catalogue item is presently unavailable, illustrating that the historical discography and the surviving accessible sound corpus are not identical.
- **1926:** *One hundred six Odeón recordings*. Only eighty-nine are presently represented by accessible audio in the Fundación catalogue; matrices, issued sides and surviving sound therefore resist being treated as one uncomplicated set of “works.”
- **1927:** *Eighty-two recordings*. Seventy-one are represented by accessible audio; this corpus crosses the transition from acoustic to electrically recorded sound.
- **1928:** *One hundred seventeen electrical recordings*. Made in Buenos Aires, Barcelona and Paris contexts; ninety-eight are represented by accessible audio.
- **1929:** *Ninety-six electrical recordings*. Paris and Buenos Aires sessions; eighty-eight are represented by accessible audio.
- **1930:** *Ninety-two electrical recordings*. Eighty-eight are represented by accessible audio; these phonographic performances are distinct from the sound-film performances made the same year.
- **1931:** *Thirty-three electrical recordings*. Thirty are represented by accessible audio.
- **1932:** *Twelve electrical recordings*. All twelve are represented by accessible audio in the consulted catalogue.
- **1933:** *Sixty-two electrical recordings*. Fifty-eight are represented by accessible audio; this was Gardel’s last extensive Buenos Aires recording year.
- **1934:** *Ten Victor electrical recordings*. New York studio performances, including music connected with Gardel’s American films.
- **1935:** *Twenty-three Victor electrical recordings*. Gardel’s final recording project; twenty are represented by accessible audio, and the last sessions ran through 20 March 1935.

### Film performances and audiovisual works

Gardel generally acted and sang in these works; he did not direct them and should not be treated as their sole maker. The American features are closer to collaborative Gesamtkunstwerke: Gardel helped shape their musical material and, through Éxito’s Spanish Pictures, had a production interest, while Alfredo Le Pera supplied scripts and lyrics and Paramount supplied production and distribution.

- **1917:** *Flor de durazno*. Silent feature based on Hugo Wast’s novel, with Gardel as actor; only a fragment of his performance is known through the museum compilation.
- **1930:** *El carretero*. Sound musical short directed by Eduardo Morera, built around Gardel’s performance of Arturo de Nava’s song.
- **1930:** *Añoranzas*. Sound musical short directed by Morera, featuring Gardel and his guitarists.
- **1930:** *Rosas de otoño*. Sound musical short: a staged, optically recorded song performance.
- **1930:** *Mano a mano*. Sound musical short combining Gardel’s performance with dialogue involving lyricist Celedonio Flores.
- **1930:** *Yira, yira*. Sound musical short combining Gardel’s performance with dialogue involving Enrique Santos Discépolo.
- **1930:** *Tengo miedo*. Sound musical short; one of the ten successfully released Morera films.
- **1930:** *Padrino pelao*. Sound musical-comedy short centered on Gardel’s rendition of the Delfino–Cantuarias song.
- **1930:** *Enfundá la mandolina*. Sound musical-comedy short centered on Gardel’s performance.
- **1930:** *Canchero*. Sound musical short with a dramatic setting and Gardel as singer-performer.
- **1930:** *Viejo smoking*. Sound musical short with a more developed dramatic scene, including Inés Murray and César Fiaschi.
- **1930:** *El quinielero*. A filmed short that failed to enter the original released group but was rediscovered in 1995; it therefore resists the simple status “lost.”
- **1930:** *Leguisamo solo*. One of five shorts reportedly spoiled in laboratory processing. Its title is known, but the work survives principally as documentation of an unrealized public manifestation.
- **1930:** *Four other damaged Morera shorts, titles not securely established in the consulted filmography*. These were made as part of the fifteen-film project but destroyed or discarded during processing; forcing titles onto them would invent evidence.
- **1931:** *Las luces de Buenos Aires*. Gardel’s first Paramount sound feature, filmed at Joinville in France; he appeared as actor-singer rather than sole author.
- **1932:** *Espérame*. Paramount feature filmed in France, built around Gardel’s acting and musical performances; released in 1933 in some markets.
- **1932:** *La casa es seria*. Approximately twenty-five-minute comic musical short with Gardel and Imperio Argentina; commonly dated 1932 by production chronology but listed as 1933 in the Fundación catalogue.
- **1932–1933:** *Melodía de arrabal*. Paramount musical feature with Gardel and Imperio Argentina; filmed in France in 1932 and released in 1933, hence the competing dates.
- **1934:** *Cuesta abajo*. New York musical feature starring Gardel, produced in association with his Éxito’s Spanish Pictures and distributed by Paramount; it integrates acting, newly composed songs and recorded performance.
- **1934:** *El tango en Broadway*. New York musical feature starring Gardel. The Fundación table labels it 1935, while production and standard filmographies date it 1934.
- **1935:** *El día que me quieras*. New York musical feature starring Gardel, filmed in January 1935; a collaborative cinematic Gesamtkunstwerk incorporating songs by Gardel and Le Pera.
- **1935:** *Tango Bar*. Gardel’s final completed starring feature, combining drama, dance and songs including *Por una cabeza*.
- **1934–1935:** *Cazadores de estrellas / The Big Broadcast of 1936*. Gardel reportedly made material for this Paramount revue project, but he does not appear in the released 1935 feature; some Gardel catalogues nevertheless list it posthumously under 1936. It therefore resists classification as a completed Gardel film.

### Institutional and production projects

- **1930:** *Unión Argentina, Sociedad Difusora de Obras Musicales y Cinematográficas*. A company/project constituted by Gardel, José Razzano and Francisco Canaro to administer, acquire and disseminate musical works through records, printed editions, piano rolls and cinema; it is an intentional production protocol as much as a discrete obra.
- **1934–1935:** *Éxito’s Spanish Pictures*. Gardel and his associates’ production project for the New York films. It gave him greater artistic and financial participation but does not make him the sole author of the resulting features.
