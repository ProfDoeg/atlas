## Works by Ettore Ferrari

### Sculptures and reliefs

- **1868:** *Labano accoglie amorevolmente Giacobbe*. Terracotta relief sketch made for the Accademia di San Luca’s Albacini competition and preserved there; some accounts date the competition to 1867, so the year is not fully settled. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **c. 1868–1870:** *Eudoro e Cimodicea* (also called *Martiri*). Relief inspired by Chateaubriand’s *Les Martyrs*; the variant titles appear to identify the same early sculpture rather than two securely distinct works. ([Treccani, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/))
- **1870:** *Bruto*. Sculpture of the republican tyrannicide, conceived as an image of victorious rebellion; its political function makes it close to a sculptural manifesto. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Cimiteri Capitolini](https://visite.cimitericapitolini.it/volti-memorie/personaggio-180.html))
- **1871:** *Stefano Porcari*. Historical sculpture rejected by the Accademia because of its rebellious political tenor; drawings and sketches preceded the final conception. ([Treccani, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/))
- **1874:** *Ermengarda* (also printed as *Ildegonda* in some accounts). Literary-historical sculpture; the inconsistent naming in the secondary record should not be silently normalized. ([Treccani, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/))
- **1877:** *Jacopo Ortis morente*. Romantic literary sculpture, apparently left in plaster, which received an honorable mention at the National Fine Arts Exhibition in Naples. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1880:** *Cum Spartaco pugnavit* (also reported as *Cum Spartaco pugno nit* or *Cum Spartaco pugnavi*). Plaster group awarded a first prize at Turin’s 1880 National Exhibition amid accusations of plagiarism; even its transmitted Latin title varies. ([Treccani, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Dizionario d’arte Sartori](https://dizionariodartesartori.it/artisti/ferrari-ettore))
- **1882:** *Lesbia*. Ideal figure subsequently recorded in Chicago; published summaries do not establish its present location or survival securely. ([Dizionario d’arte Sartori](https://dizionariodartesartori.it/artisti/ferrari-ettore))
- **1883:** *La morte di Emilio Morosini* (also exhibited as *Morte di A. Morosini – Assedio di Roma 1849*). Bronze historical group concerning the defense of the Roman Republic, now recorded in the Galleria Nazionale d’Arte Moderna; the initial in an 1890 exhibition title conflicts with the usual identification as Emilio Morosini. ([Galleria Recta](https://www.galleriarecta.it/autore/ferrari-ettore/?lang=italian); [Fondazione CRTrieste](https://www.fondazionecrtrieste.it/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Umberto_Veruda.pdf))
- **1883:** *Ritrovamento del Laocoonte*. Architectural bas-relief beside the main entrance of Rome’s Palazzo delle Esposizioni. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1884:** *Apollo*. Rooftop architectural statue for Rome’s Palazzo delle Esposizioni. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1884:** *Fidia*. Companion rooftop statue for Rome’s Palazzo delle Esposizioni; some modern summaries misprint the title as *Fizia*. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **c. 1890:** *Figura femminile velata con urna*. Funerary or commemorative female figure; the national catalogue attributes it to Ferrari but its precise original identity remains uncertain. ([Catalogo Generale dei Beni Culturali](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/1200827777))
- **undated:** *Episodio di storia antica*. Terracotta attributed to Ferrari’s early activity on stylistic grounds, rather than a firmly documented commission; both title and attribution are descriptive and provisional. ([Carlo Virgilio & C.](https://www.carlovirgilio.it/autore/ettore-ferrari-1845-1929/))

### Public monuments

- **1875–1877:** *Monumento a Ion Heliade Rădulescu*. Bronze public monument commissioned for Bucharest in 1875 and completed in the foundry in 1877. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1879:** *Monumento a Ovidio*. Standing public statue made for Constanța, Romania; later sources sometimes date its public realization or exhibition to 1884 or 1887, although Ferrari completed the work in 1879. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Carlo Virgilio & C.](https://www.carlovirgilio.it/autore/ettore-ferrari-1845-1929/))
- **1880–1887:** *Monumento a Vittorio Emanuele II*. Equestrian bronze and allegorical sculptural ensemble for Venice, won in competition in 1880 and inaugurated on the Riva degli Schiavoni in 1887. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [monument record](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_a_Vittorio_Emanuele_II_%28Venezia%29))
- **1886–1887:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Vicenza*. Standing marble civic monument; authorities differ between 1886 and 1887 when assigning the work’s date. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1887–1889:** *Monumento a Giordano Bruno*. Bronze figure, pedestal reliefs, and commemorative program in Campo de’ Fiori, Rome: a public monument that also acts as an anticlerical manifesto. Ferrari completed the final plaster model in 1887 and the monument was inaugurated in 1889. ([Archivio Centrale dello Stato](https://acs.cultura.gov.it/dantedi/); [Carlo Virgilio & C.](https://www.carlovirgilio.it/autore/ettore-ferrari-1845-1929/))
- **1888:** *Monumento ad Abraham Lincoln*. Monumental figure made for New York and reported in later catalogues as being at the Metropolitan Museum; that stated location requires verification, and the object’s present status is unclear. ([Dizionario d’arte Sartori](https://dizionariodartesartori.it/artisti/ferrari-ettore))
- **1887:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Verdi*. Commemorative statue or monument made for Philadelphia; accessible summaries establish the commission but not its current survival or precise form. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1892:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Pisa*. Standing bronze monument with narrative reliefs representing “Patria e Umanità,” the departure of the Thousand, Aspromonte, and Garibaldi’s entry into Pisa. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Dizionario d’arte Sartori](https://dizionariodartesartori.it/artisti/ferrari-ettore))
- **1893:** *Monumento a Carlo Cattaneo*. Public commemorative monument in Milan. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1893:** *Monumento a Quintino Sella*. Public sculptural ensemble inaugurated in Rome, combining Sella’s figure with allegories of Law and the Genius of Finance. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [University of Messina study](https://cisu.unime.it/wp-content/uploads/sites/55/2022/08/2.-Poeti-pittori.pdf))
- **1893–1895:** *Monumento equestre a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Rovigo*. Bronze equestrian monument begun in 1893 and inaugurated in 1895; the story that its inverted crowns caused its removal from an intended Roman destination is traditional rather than securely demonstrated. ([monument history](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_a_Giuseppe_Garibaldi_%28Rovigo%29))
- **1895:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Macerata*. Public monument to Garibaldi inaugurated in Macerata. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1895:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Tortona*. Public commemorative monument; the concise scholarly biography records the commission without describing its constituent sculpture. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1895:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Cortona*. Public commemorative monument whose exact dating and later physical history are not detailed by the accessible catalogue summaries. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1896:** *Monumento a Terenzio Mamiani*. Public monument in Pesaro. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1897:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Rovigo*. Treccani gives 1897 for Ferrari’s Rovigo monument, while the monument’s local history dates its making to 1893–1895; this is a bibliographical dating conflict, not evidence for another work. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [monument history](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monumento_a_Giuseppe_Garibaldi_%28Rovigo%29))
- **1897:** *Monumento ad Alberto Mario*. Public monument at Lendinara. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1899:** *Monumento a Engel*. Public monument at Vicosoprano, Switzerland; accessible summaries identify neither Engel’s full name nor the monument’s precise medium. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1900:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Bevagna*. Public Garibaldi monument recorded in the scholarly biography without a secure date. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1902–1929; inaugurated 1949:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Mazzini*. Gesamtkunstwerk-like civic ensemble of architecture, sculpture, relief, allegory, and political commemoration on Rome’s Aventine; Ferrari completed the sculptural portions before his death, but the state inaugurated the monument only in 1949. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Archivio Centrale dello Stato](https://acs.cultura.gov.it/dantedi/))
- **1904:** *Monumento a Giuseppe Garibaldi, Massa Marittima*. Public commemorative monument. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1909:** *Medaglione di Felice Orsini*. Public portrait medallion inaugurated at Meldola on 9 May 1909; it is a memorial object rather than a free-standing monument. ([biographical overview](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Ferrari))
- **1912–1945:** *Monumento ad Antonio Panizzi*. Commissioned commemorative monument for Brescello; the long date range reflects a work whose conception and eventual installation belong to different moments, resisting a single “completion” date. ([Regione Emilia-Romagna](https://patrimonioculturale.regione.emilia-romagna.it/arti-visuali/cultura-in-mostra/monumenti-tricolori/monumenti-tricolori.pdf/%40%40download/file))
- **1914:** *Monumento a Gabriele Rosa*. Public monument at Iseo. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1914:** *Monumento a Giovanni Bovio*. Public monument at Trani. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1923:** *Monumento ad Antonio Meucci*. Public commemorative monument on Staten Island, New York. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1924:** *Monumento ai caduti della guerra mondiale*. War memorial at Alexandria, Egypt. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1925:** *Monumento a Ovidio, Sulmona*. Bronze replica or recasting of Ferrari’s earlier Constanța figure, donated for Ovid’s birthplace; it is a new manifestation of an existing model rather than a wholly independent invention. ([University of Chieti-Pescara study](https://ricerca.unich.it/retrieve/7af0ae78-6691-4820-9865-4ea7d77f6fdc/la%2Bcostruzione%2Bdel%2Bregime.pdf))
- **1927:** *Traiano*. Monumental portrait statue or bust made for Cluj, Romania, as part of a linked group of Roman-Dacian commemorative objects. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1927:** *Decebalo*. Companion monumental portrait made for Cluj. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1927:** *Traiano sulla Lupa Capitolina*. Relief made for Cluj; the available summary separates it from the statues of Trajan and Decebalus. ([biographical overview](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Ferrari))
- **1927:** *Monumento ai caduti*. War memorial at Marino. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Funerary monuments

- **date unknown:** *Monumento Pacheco*. Tomb or funerary monument made for Buenos Aires; its date and present condition are not supplied by the standard concise biography. ([Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/))
- **date unknown:** *Monumento Dolci*. Funerary monument in Verona’s Cimitero Monumentale. ([Cimitero Monumentale di Verona](https://www.agec.it/cimiteromonumentale/autori/ettore-ferrari/))
- **date unknown:** *Monumento Lugo*. Funerary monument in Verona’s Cimitero Monumentale. ([Cimitero Monumentale di Verona](https://www.agec.it/cimiteromonumentale/autori/ettore-ferrari/))

### Portrait busts, herms, and medallions

- **date unknown:** *Busto di Francesco Carrara*. Memorial portrait in Pisa’s Camposanto. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **date unknown:** *Busto di Jakob Moleschott*. Portrait bust made for the University of Rome. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **date unknown:** *Busto di Pietro Cossa*. Portrait bust in the Campidoglio according to an early reference, while a later study places a 1929 Cossa bust at the Verano; whether these are one displaced object, replicas, or distinct manifestations is unresolved in the accessible sources. ([Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/); [Ca’ Foscari study](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/article/mdccc-1800/2020/1/art-10.14277-MDCCC-2280-8841-2020-01-002.pdf))
- **date unknown:** *Busto di Sante*. Portrait bust recorded at Pola, without a securely expanded name or date in the consulted source. ([Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/))
- **date unknown:** *Erma del generale Giuseppe Avezzana*. Commemorative herm combining Avezzana’s portrait with an allegorical element at its base. ([Cronaca Numismatica](https://www.cronacanumismatica.com/la-medaglia-per-linaugurazione-del-monumento-a-garibaldi-a-orbetello/))
- **date unknown:** *Busto di Giuseppe Mazzini*. Portrait bust or element of a larger ensemble; the national catalogue records an attributed example but does not establish that every surviving cast is a separate authored work. ([Catalogo Generale dei Beni Culturali](https://www.catalogo.beniculturali.it/Agent/16d809e347fad1b7391a8799c1d57926))
- **date unknown:** *Busto di Michelangelo Caetani*. Portrait bust in the Campidoglio. ([Treccani, Enciclopedia Italiana](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/))
- **1898–1899:** *Ritratto di Felice Cavallotti*. Commemorative portrait sculpture catalogued by the Italian cultural-heritage authority; the catalogue’s life dates for Ferrari contain an evident erroneous death year, but the object record itself attributes the work to him. ([Catalogo Generale dei Beni Culturali](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/2000148533))

### Unfinished or rejected projects

- **1879:** *Primo progetto per il monumento a Giordano Bruno*. Rejected project showing Bruno as an active orator with raised hand; it survives conceptually and through a published design, and should be counted as a project rather than conflated with the quieter monument installed in 1889. ([Roma ieri oggi](https://www.info.roma.it/personaggi_dettaglio.asp?ID_personaggi=302); [Archivio Centrale dello Stato](https://acs.cultura.gov.it/dantedi/))
- **from 1890:** *Progetto per il monumento a Giuseppe Mazzini*. The intention and preliminary design long preceded the formal 1902 commission and the completed sculptural ensemble; it is the project-state of the later monument, not simply an alternative title for a finished object. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1905:** *Bozzetto per il monumento a Giuseppe Mazzini*. Finished design model for the Aventine ensemble; it is simultaneously an autonomous exhibition object and a stage within the larger project. ([biographical overview](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ettore_Ferrari))
- **1906:** *Frammento pel monumento a Mazzini*. Sculptural fragment or exhibition manifestation of the Mazzini project, awarded the Premio Principe Umberto at Brera; it resists classification as either merely preparatory material or a wholly separate work. ([Galleria Recta](https://www.galleriarecta.it/autore/ferrari-ettore/?lang=italian))

### Editorial and political creation

- **1923–1925:** *Lux*. Militant periodical founded by Ferrari in November 1923 to unite political, secular, and esoteric argument. It is better classified as an editorial creation and continuing political manifestation than as a single authored “text”: Ferrari created and directed the platform, but its issues were collective objects. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ettore-ferrari_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
