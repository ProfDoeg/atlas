# Raúl Veroni: Research Dossier

## Scope and identification

This dossier concerns **Raoul Veroni**, also routinely catalogued as **Raúl Veroni**, the Italian-born Argentine-based engraver, illustrator, printer, typographer, editor, and maker of bibliophile books (1913–1992).

He must be distinguished from his son, **Ral Veroni**—born Raúl Veroni in Buenos Aires in 1965—who is also an artist, engraver, author, and editor. Search results and institutional catalogues sometimes conflate their names and works. The father normally used **Raoul** professionally; the son uses **Ral** or **Ralveroni**.

The surviving online documentary record is concentrated on Veroni’s artistic and editorial career. Basic civil and private-life documentation—parents’ names, marriage record, complete household, citizenship status, exact death date, estate, and medical history—was not located in accessible sources. Those absences are noted rather than filled by inference.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Preferred professional name:** Raoul Veroni.
- **Alternative form:** Raúl Veroni.
- **Occasional catalogue forms:** Raul Veroni; Veroni, Raúl.
- **Full birth name:** Not established beyond Raoul/Raúl Veroni.
- **Born:** **8 August 1913**, Milan, Kingdom of Italy.
- **Died:** **1992**, Buenos Aires, Argentina.
- **Exact death date:** Not established in the consulted sources.
- **Age at death:** Seventy-eight or seventy-nine, depending on whether he died before or after 8 August 1992.
- **Nationality:** Italian by birth. Sources describe him variously as an Italian artist established in Argentina and as an Argentine artist. No naturalization document was found.
- **Principal occupations:** Engraver, draftsman, illustrator, lithographer, watercolorist, printer, hand compositor, typographer, book designer, binder, editor, and newspaper artist.
- **Known artistic techniques:** Etching, drypoint, woodcut, lithography, drawing, pen and watercolor, hand coloring, typography with movable lead type, and artisanal book production.
- **Principal places of residence:** Milan in infancy; Tafí del Valle, Tucumán, during childhood; Buenos Aires from adolescence through the end of his life; periodic stays or vacations in Villa Gesell during the 1970s.
- **Known Buenos Aires address:** His house-workshop and graphic studio stood at **Senillosa 944**, in or near Parque Chacabuco.
- **Principal employers and institutions:** Escuela de Bellas Artes Manuel Belgrano; Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Cárcova; Francisco A. Colombo’s printing establishment; the newspaper *La Prensa*; Ediciones Urania; Estudio Gráfico Raoul Veroni.
- **Known child:** Ral Veroni, born in Buenos Aires in 1965.
- **Spouse or partner:** A recollection by Ral says that his mother was also an artist and accompanied the family to Villa Gesell. Her name was not found in the consulted sources.

## Source Base and Evidentiary Limits

The principal sources used here are:

1. The Biblioteca Nacional de la República Argentina’s catalogue for the 2012 exhibition **“El libro como arte: Raoul Veroni y las ediciones de bibliófilo.”** It contains an essay on his techniques and workshop history and the most substantial available bibliography of books issued in his four principal series.
2. An article by Ral Veroni and Linda Neilson published in *Página/12* in connection with that exhibition.
3. A 2011 *Página/12* profile, based substantially on family knowledge, which supplies the most precise early-life and employment chronology.
4. A 2015 *Página/12* article containing Ral Veroni’s first-person recollections of the house-workshop, his father’s work schedule, family vacations, and watercolor practice.
5. A 2024 *El Litoral* article drawing upon the contemporary newspaper *El Orden* for the 1941 Santa Fe prize.
6. Institutional and specialist studies and catalogues concerning Argentine fine printing, bibliophile books, and particular editions.
7. Auction and bookseller records used cautiously for edition statements and surviving examples.

No comprehensive scholarly biography, published memoir by Veroni, accessible personnel file, oral-history transcript, civil registry file, probate file, or complete catalogue raisonné was located.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and Italian background

**Documented or consistently reported fact:** Veroni was born into an Italian family in Milan on 8 August 1913. When he was approximately three months old, his parents took him to Argentina and settled in **Tafí del Valle, Tucumán**.

The names, professions, ancestry, financial circumstances, political affiliations, and religious identities of his parents were not established. The reason for the family’s migration likewise remains undocumented in the consulted record.

### Childhood household

Veroni spent his childhood among the mountains and vegetation of Tafí del Valle. Later family and exhibition texts connect that landscape with the recurring botanical and rural character of his art. This connection is an interpretation by his family and later commentators, not a documented statement by Veroni himself.

No siblings have been identified.

### Marriage, partner, and children

Ral Veroni’s recollection of childhood vacations refers to “mamá” and says that she, too, was an artist. She reportedly objected when Raoul discarded or left unsigned the watercolors he made for pleasure in Villa Gesell, insisting that he preserve and sign them because they were art.

No source consulted supplied her name, the date or legal status of the relationship, or her own artistic biography.

One child is firmly documented:

- **Ral Veroni**, born in Buenos Aires in **1965**, artist, engraver, writer, bookseller, gallerist, and editor. He later preserved his father’s workshop materials, exhibited his father’s work, participated in the 2012 Biblioteca Nacional retrospective, and revived the Urania imprint in 2010.

Whether Raoul Veroni had other children could not be determined.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1913–c. 1929

Raoul Veroni was born in Milan on 8 August 1913. According to the biographical account published by his son and Linda Neilson, his family crossed to Argentina when he was three months old. They settled in Tafí del Valle, in the province of Tucumán.

The available sources describe the environment as rural and mountainous. Later works—landscapes, plant studies, flowers, fruit, branches, and rural imagery—have consequently been read as continuations of his Tucumán childhood. There are no surviving school records, childhood letters, or dated juvenilia in the sources consulted.

As an adolescent he moved from Tucumán to Buenos Aires to study drawing. The date is not reported. If he followed a conventional secondary-school age, the move probably occurred in the late 1920s, but this is an inference and should not be treated as established.

## Education and Formation

### Escuela Manuel Belgrano

**Reported fact:** Veroni first studied drawing in Buenos Aires at the **Escuela de Bellas Artes Manuel Belgrano**. The dates and diploma status were not located.

### Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Cárcova

He continued at the **Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Cárcova**, an advanced training institution for the visual arts.

**Documented teacher:** **Alfredo Guido**, painter, engraver, illustrator, and book artist. Multiple biographical and bookseller records describe Veroni as Guido’s student.

The Cárcova training gave Veroni a formal command of printmaking. His subsequent work encompassed etching, drypoint, wood engraving, and lithography. Later accounts emphasize not only his ability to design an image but his understanding of the complete printing process: preparing a plate or block, proofing it, adjusting ink and pressure, and pulling the edition.

### Francisco A. Colombo as professional mentor

From **1936 through 1961**, Veroni worked in the graphic establishment of **Francisco A. Colombo**. The Biblioteca Nacional catalogue describes Colombo as his central practical teacher in the craft of the fine book.

At Colombo’s establishment Veroni progressed through the connected trades of:

- illustration and original printmaking;
- proofing and edition printing;
- hand composition with movable type;
- page design;
- paper selection and handling;
- press operation;
- and binding.

Colombo therefore functioned as employer, master printer, and technical mentor. The workshop was also Veroni’s point of access to authors, artists, bibliophiles, and patrons of limited editions.

## Early Career, 1936–1942

### Printmaking prizes

Sources associated with Galería Mar Dulce and the 2023 *Revista del Hospital de Niños* study report that Veroni received a **printmaking prize at the Salón de Acuarelistas, Pastelistas y Grabadores in 1936, 1937, 1938, and 1939**.

The exact title and category of each annual award, the works submitted, jury membership, and official catalogue entries were not recovered. These awards are consistently reported in later institutional and exhibition texts, but the original salon catalogues should be consulted for verification.

### Salón Nacional

Veroni participated in the **Salón Nacional in 1938**. The work or works exhibited were not identified in the accessible source.

### *La Prensa*

A family-based account states that Veroni worked as a draftsman or illustrator for the Buenos Aires newspaper ***La Prensa*** from **1938 until 1979**. He normally worked the evening shift.

This salaried employment reportedly supported his household. His private artistic and editorial work was conducted in the morning, followed by his *La Prensa* shift from approximately 6 p.m. until midnight.

No personnel file or comprehensive inventory of his newspaper illustrations was found. Consequently, individual unsigned newspaper drawings cannot safely be attributed to him solely on stylistic grounds.

### 1941 Santa Fe acquisition prize

On **25 May 1941**, at the XVIII Salón Anual organized by the Comisión Provincial de Bellas Artes in Santa Fe, Veroni’s etching ***El Niño*** received the **Premio Adquisición** in the print section.

*El Orden* described it as the best print presented, noting its line, expression, precision, and close observation. Unlike the four earlier salon prizes, this award can be tied to a named work, date, exhibition, and contemporary press report.

An acquisition prize ordinarily meant that the work entered a public collection, but its present inventory location was not established.

## Chronological Life History

### 1943: foundation of Urania

In **1943**, aged thirty, Veroni financed and produced his first independent bibliophile book: Rafael Alberti’s ***Tres recuerdos del cielo***.

The edition consisted of only **24 copies**, printed on handmade Fabriano Perusia paper. It included five etchings by Veroni and was composed in movable type. This publication became the beginning of **Ediciones Urania**.

The financing is significant. Later accounts state that Veroni paid for the first book himself. Urania was not founded as a high-volume commercial publisher; it was a vehicle through which he could control text, typography, illustration, paper, impression, and binding as parts of a single work.

### 1943–1954: first Urania sequence

During this period Veroni issued limited editions of poetry by Alberti, Gabriele d’Annunzio, Camille Flammarion, Giacomo Leopardi, and Edgar Allan Poe.

The runs were extremely small—generally between 24 and 87 copies. Such production restricted income and audience but enabled close control over materials and impression quality.

Veroni continued working at both Francisco Colombo’s establishment and *La Prensa*. Urania was therefore an independent artistic undertaking maintained alongside salaried employment.

### Mid-1940s exhibitions and contemporary reception

A contemporary press item preserved in a digitized periodical describes an exhibition of approximately forty prints and book illustrations by “Raúl Veroni” at **Amigos del Arte** when he was a little over thirty. The reviewer described him as tall, thin, shy, and birdlike, and discussed works including ***La rama***, ***Pequeño paisaje***, and ***La mariposa***.

A separate Uruguayan periodical index records an article titled **“Grabados de Raúl Veroni”** on 11 May 1947. Without the complete article, it cannot be determined whether this referred to a Montevideo exhibition, a review of Argentine work, or a general profile.

These records indicate that Veroni’s recognition was not confined to book collectors, although he later retreated from the competitive salon system.

### 1955–1961: diversification of editorial series

In 1955 Veroni initiated **La Cabellera**, a poetry series beginning with Percy Bysshe Shelley’s ***A una alondra*** and Tulio Carella’s ***Intermedio***.

In 1956 he began **El camino a Ocloyas**, devoted to Argentine rural or criollo writing. Its titles included Roberto J. Payró, Juan Carlos Dávalos, and Jorge Calvetti.

In 1961 he opened **El Mato**, a series for Argentine poets, with Silvina Ocampo’s ***Tríptico de un jazmín***.

The series identities allowed him to arrange texts by language, theme, and national tradition while maintaining a recognizable workshop practice.

### Important collaborative editions of the 1950s

Veroni’s work was not confined to books bearing his own imprint.

Known examples include:

- Leopoldo Lugones’s ***Al rastro*** (Sociedad de Bibliófilos Argentinos, 1955): Jorge Argerich supplied pen illustrations, which were etched and printed by Veroni at Francisco Colombo’s establishment.
- Carlos Manuel Muñiz’s ***Desde esta tierra*** (1956): contained a Veroni illustration colored by the Planograf process and was printed by Colombo.
- Jorge Luis Borges’s ***Límites*** (1958): Veroni pulled the original etchings by Leopoldo Presas for a private edition printed by Francisco Colombo.
- John Keats’s ***Odas*** (1958): translated by Juan Rodolfo Wilcock and issued in a run of 52 copies; later bookseller records describe 20 on Fabriano Magnani and 32 on Ingres paper.

These production roles must be distinguished from literary authorship. Veroni did not write the underlying poems or prose. Depending on the edition, he acted as illustrator, plate printer, typographer, designer, compositor, pressman, or publisher.

### 1960–1962: establishment of his own printing house

Sources disagree slightly on the date:

- Galería Mar Dulce and later biographical summaries say that Veroni installed his own press in **1960**.
- The Biblioteca Nacional catalogue says he opened the studio in **1962**, at **Senillosa 944**.
- His employment at Colombo is consistently given as ending in **1961**.

The most plausible reconstruction is that machinery and financing were acquired or installation began in 1960, his Colombo collaboration ended in 1961, and the independent house-workshop was fully operating by 1962. This remains a reconciliation rather than a directly documented sequence.

The press was financed through **credits from the Italian government and Argentina’s Fondo Nacional de las Artes**. No loan files, amounts, repayment schedules, or collateral records were found.

His home and workshop were integrated. A surviving photograph shows him there in 1969.

### Workshop technology and routine

Ral Veroni remembered waking to the sound of a **German Phoenix Press II platen press**, commonly called a Minerva. Etchings were printed in the ground-floor room adjoining the garden.

Lithography required a separate small room on the terrace. Raoul carried stones weighing approximately forty kilograms down the stairs whenever they had to be wetted for printing.

A typical handmade edition involved:

1. choosing and editing the literary text;
2. designing page proportions and typographic treatment;
3. drawing and developing the illustrations;
4. engraving plates or blocks;
5. proofing and correcting;
6. printing the images;
7. hand-setting the text in lead type;
8. printing the letterpress sheets;
9. cutting or preparing the paper;
10. assembling and binding the copies;
11. and designing the colophon.

His son estimated that a single book could require roughly six months.

### 1960s: mature book art

Veroni’s mature books often incorporated the colophon as an independent visual composition. Rather than placing a conventional rectangular paragraph at the end, he made the type follow the contour of an image. Doing so with movable lead type required exact calculations of line length, leading, spacing, and image position.

Representative projects included:

- Basilio Uribe’s ***Corona de María*** (1960);
- T. S. Eliot’s ***Poemas de Ariel*** (1961);
- Silvina Ocampo’s ***Tríptico de un jazmín*** (1961);
- Horacio Esteban Ratti’s ***Para un adiós*** (1962);
- Tulio Carella’s ***Preta*** (1963);
- Silvina Ocampo’s ***La casa natal*** (1964);
- Enrique Banchs’s ***Cantos del anochecer*** (1965);
- versions of Sappho in 1968;
- Delmira Agustini’s ***Poemas*** (1969).

Proofs and letters surviving with ***Tríptico de un jazmín*** document direct communication between Veroni and Silvina Ocampo. Ocampo wrote that nothing could please her more than seeing the images inspired by one of her poems and thanked him for choosing it.

### 1965: birth of Ral Veroni

Veroni’s son Ral was born in Buenos Aires in 1965. Ral grew up in the Senillosa house-workshop amid presses, paper, ink, type, plates, and stones.

This constituted an informal apprenticeship. Ral later became a printmaker and editor and eventually revived Urania, although he did not do so immediately after his father’s death.

### 1968: Sappho editions

Veroni produced two small Sappho-related editions in 1968:

- ***Sapfó***, in Miguel Alfredo Olivera’s Spanish version, 22 copies;
- ***Poemas de Safo***, translated by Héctor Miguel Angeli, 24 copies.

The colophon of the latter became a frequently reproduced example of his typographic art: the text curves around a standing female figure lifting a veil.

Catalogues sometimes compress or confuse the two Sappho editions. They should be treated as distinct publications.

### 1970s: double working life

Through most of the 1970s Veroni maintained two jobs:

- private editorial and printing work in the morning;
- newspaper illustration at *La Prensa* in the evening.

This arrangement provided a stable wage but limited the time available for independent work.

The survival of such labor-intensive editions despite low profitability is explained in family accounts as a personal commitment to the art of the book. There is no evidence that the private press generated substantial wealth.

### Villa Gesell watercolors

During family vacations in **Villa Gesell** in the 1970s, Veroni walked through the dunes and forest and collected stems and flowers. At the hotel he drew them directly in pen and added watercolor.

His son remembered Veroni holding a plant in his left hand at arm’s length and drawing it rapidly with his right. The paper was often handmade stock left over from book projects.

He did not originally make these works for sale or exhibition. Some were unsigned; some were reportedly discarded. His partner urged him to preserve and sign them.

The subjects included crowns of thorns, roses, laurel, olive, poppies, ivy, fruit, stems, and leaves. Twenty-five of these small works were first publicly presented in the 2015 exhibition ***Naturaleza*** at Galería Mar Dulce.

### Other 1970s production

Veroni continued publishing, printing for others, and experimenting with collaborative editions. Documented examples outside the four-series bibliography include:

- Pedro Miguel Obligado, ***El andén*** (1971), with an original Veroni print; 120 special copies, produced at Estudio Gráfico Raúl Veroni.
- Oscar Hermes Villordo, ***La oscuridad como una rosa*** (1972), 41 copies.
- Rainer Maria Rilke, ***Primera elegía de Duino*** (1973), 47 copies.
- Dino Campana, ***Poemas*** (1973), selected and annotated by Horacio Armani, with Veroni lithographs; 21 copies.
- Francesco Petrarca, selected versions and notes by Luis Arena (1975), 30 copies.
- Oscar Cerruto, ***Reverso de la transparencia*** (1975), with woodcuts, hand composition, and printing by Veroni; 406 copies, including six special Ingres-paper copies.
- Héctor Dante Cincotta, ***A la Pampa*** (1976), hand-composed by Veroni, with an original Veroni drawing; 38 copies.
- Edgar Allan Poe, ***Annabel Lee*** (1976), Miguel Alfredo Olivera translation; 25 copies.
- ***Poesía portuguesa*** (1977), an edition prepared by Veroni with an original drawing by Norah Borges; 45 numbered and hand-colored copies.
- Alfonsina Storni, ***Poemas*** (1977), 50 copies.
- Leopardi selection (1978), with versions and notes by Luis Arena and prints by Víctor Rebuffo; 15 copies.
- Héctor J. Puglia, ***Dos poemas*** (1978), 35 copies.

### 1974 PEN Club recognition

In **1974**, the **PEN Club Argentino** honored Veroni for his editorial work. The title of the tribute, speakers, venue, and surviving program were not located.

### 1979: departure from *La Prensa*

The family-based chronology states that Veroni’s work at *La Prensa* ended in **1979**, after approximately forty-one years.

The circumstances are not documented. No evidence was found of dismissal, political removal, labor dispute, or disciplinary action.

### 1981: last title in the principal catalogue

The Biblioteca Nacional bibliography ends with Ricardo Molinari’s ***Dos poemas*** in **1981**, an edition of fifteen copies.

Later descriptions say that Veroni devoted roughly forty or forty-five years to fine books. Whether he continued to produce isolated commissions after 1981 is not fully established.

### 1984 Buenos Aires Book Fair

In **1984**, Veroni was invited to exhibit at the **10th Buenos Aires International Book Fair** with artist **Líbero Badii**. The form of their joint presentation and the works displayed were not identified.

### Final years and death

Very little has been published about Veroni’s life between 1984 and 1992. The consulted record contains no documented illness, retirement date, financial crisis, accident, addiction, psychological breakdown, or legal problem.

He died in **Buenos Aires in 1992**. Neither the exact date nor cause of death was established.

## Companies, Institutions, Technologies, Projects, and Financial Interests

### Francisco A. Colombo printing establishment

Veroni worked there from 1936 to 1961. The relationship combined wage labor, craft apprenticeship, and collaboration on commissioned fine books.

Colombo’s establishment served affluent private patrons and associations such as the Sociedad de Bibliófilos Argentinos. This environment connected Veroni with the economic infrastructure of Argentine bibliophilia: wealthy collectors financed short runs, while printers and artists supplied hand labor and costly materials.

No evidence indicates that Veroni owned equity in Colombo’s firm.

### Ediciones Urania

- **Founded:** 1943.
- **Founder:** Raoul Veroni.
- **Original purpose:** Limited bibliophile editions integrating important literary texts, original graphic art, typography, handmade or fine paper, and artisanal printing.
- **First publication:** Rafael Alberti, ***Tres recuerdos del cielo***, 24 copies.
- **Business model:** Very small, numbered runs; high labor per copy; little apparent ability to scale.
- **Later history:** Production ceased after Veroni’s death. His son Ral revived the name in 2010.

Urania was both a publishing imprint and Veroni’s authorial framework for treating the entire book as an artwork.

### Other imprints or series

Four principal series are documented:

- **Urania**, international poetry;
- **La Cabellera**, poetry;
- **El camino a Ocloyas**, Argentine rural or criollo texts;
- **El Mato**, Argentine poetry.

Sources also refer generically to his “collections” as independent seals. Their exact legal or commercial registration status was not determined.

### Estudio Gráfico Raoul Veroni

The independent press operated from the Senillosa 944 house-workshop after approximately 1960–1962.

Known equipment included:

- Phoenix Press II platen press;
- an etching press or related intaglio-printing apparatus;
- a Krauss lithographic press later acquired by another printer in 2008;
- lithographic stones;
- movable lead type;
- woodblocks, clichés, initial letters, and engraving tools.

### Financing

The press was established with credit from:

- the **Italian government**;
- the **Fondo Nacional de las Artes**.

No amounts or contractual records were found.

### Property and estate

The house-workshop at Senillosa 944 was the principal property associated with him. Ownership status, mortgage status, acquisition date, and probate disposition are unknown.

After his death, equipment, papers, books, art, and workshop materials passed at least partly into family custody. Ral Veroni later described his father’s works as his inheritance and used the collection in establishing Galería Mar Dulce.

### Wealth

No reliable valuation of Veroni’s income, estate, or collection was found. Available evidence suggests:

- regular household income from *La Prensa*;
- independent earnings from printing and illustration;
- credit-financed workshop equipment;
- fine books whose tiny editions were unlikely to yield industrial-scale profits;
- posthumous value in his books, proofs, and original prints.

Auction asking prices and estimates do not establish Veroni’s lifetime wealth.

### Patents and inventions

No patents, registered industrial inventions, or proprietary printing technologies were found. His importance lay in mastery and combination of established techniques rather than invention of a new mechanical process.

## Political, Religious, Intellectual, and Artistic Development

### Politics

No party membership, public office, campaign activity, exile, censorship case, political imprisonment, intelligence relationship, or participation in political violence was found.

His long employment at *La Prensa* placed him inside a major and politically influential newspaper, but employment alone does not establish agreement with its editorial positions.

### Religion

His editions included Christian or Marian titles, notably Basilio Uribe’s ***Corona de María***, but this is insufficient evidence of personal religious conviction. No statement of faith or religious affiliation was found.

### Intellectual orientation

Veroni’s editorial choices show broad engagement with European and Argentine poetry:

- classical Greek lyric through Sappho;
- Italian poetry through Dante-era and modern authors including Petrarch, Leopardi, Carducci, D’Annunzio, and Campana;
- English-language Romantic and modern poetry through Shelley, Keats, Poe, and Eliot;
- Argentine poetry through Ocampo, Banchs, Molinari, Storni, Dávalos, Payró, and others.

His practice privileged correspondence between text, typography, material, and image. The Biblioteca Nacional catalogue treats the colophon as the place where his personal typographic imagination was most visible.

### Artistic development

A provisional sequence can be reconstructed:

1. Academic drawing and printmaking in Buenos Aires.
2. Salon-oriented engravings and prizes in the late 1930s and early 1940s.
3. Apprenticeship and professional work at Colombo.
4. Increasing concentration on illustration and bibliophile editions after 1943.
5. Independent control of the complete book-making process after 1960–1962.
6. Continued fine printing accompanied by private pen-and-watercolor studies during the 1970s.
7. Withdrawal from salaried newspaper work in 1979 and reduced documented production after 1981.

His son states that Veroni became uncomfortable with the competitiveness of prizes and the struggle for exhibition space and instead “fell in love” with the rigor of books. This is a reported family explanation, not a surviving statement by Veroni.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Alfredo Guido

**Documented teacher-student relationship.** Guido taught Veroni at the Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Cárcova and provided the principal named link to Argentina’s academic printmaking tradition.

### Francisco A. Colombo

**Documented mentor-employer relationship, 1936–1961.** Colombo’s establishment trained Veroni in printing, composition, and book construction and gave him access to bibliophile publishing.

### Ral Veroni

**Documented father-son and artistic transmission.** Ral grew up in the workshop, inherited art and equipment, curated or supported posthumous exhibitions, and revived Urania.

### Veroni’s unnamed artist partner

**Reported family relationship.** Ral recalled her presence during Villa Gesell vacations and her efforts to make Raoul preserve and sign his watercolors. Her identity remains unresolved.

### Silvina Ocampo

**Documented author-editor-illustrator relationship.** Veroni produced ***Tríptico de un jazmín*** and ***La casa natal***. Surviving letters from Ocampo discuss his images and thank him for selecting her poem.

### Tulio Carella

**Documented repeated collaboration.** Veroni issued Carella’s ***Intermedio*** and ***Preta***. The nature of their friendship beyond these books is not established.

### Basilio Uribe

**Documented repeated collaborator.** Uribe was involved as author, translator, or designer in several editions, including ***Corona de María*** and works by Eliot and Leopardi.

### Líbero Badii

**Documented exhibition association.** Both were invited to the 1984 Buenos Aires Book Fair presentation. A deeper friendship is not established.

### Artists and authors whose work he printed

His professional network included, among others, Leopoldo Presas, Jorge Argerich, Víctor Rebuffo, Norah Borges, Ricardo Molinari, Enrique Banchs, Horacio Armani, Miguel Alfredo Olivera, Héctor Miguel Angeli, Luis Arena, Juan Rodolfo Wilcock, and Héctor Dante Cincotta.

The degree of personal intimacy varied and usually cannot be reconstructed from colophons alone.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

No substantial political mythology, hostile legend, conspiracy tradition, or scandal narrative about Veroni was found. The posthumous mythology surrounding him is principally artisanal and familial.

### “Renaissance man”

**Story:** Exhibition texts describe Veroni as a man of “Renaissance spirit,” combining artist, printer, typographer, editor, binder, and naturalist sensitivity.

**Origin and circulation:** The phrase appears in posthumous gallery and newspaper writing, especially accounts associated with his family.

**Evidence:** His documented command of multiple book trades supports the underlying description. “Renaissance man” remains an honorific metaphor, not a historical title.

**Reputational effect:** It presents him as an integrated maker standing against the fragmentation and automation of modern publishing.

### Technique comparable to Dürer

**Story:** Ral Veroni recalled that his father’s discipline and technical ability allowed him to reach a line and technique comparable to Albrecht Dürer.

**Evidence:** This is an admiring assessment by his son. Veroni’s award-winning printmaking and technically demanding books demonstrate high skill, but no comparative scholarly study establishing equivalence with Dürer was found.

**Classification:** Family evaluation or hagiographic comparison, not an objective rank.

**Effect:** It reinforces the posthumous image of Veroni as an old-master craftsman.

### The selfless maker unconcerned with exhibition

**Story:** Veroni supposedly made his Villa Gesell watercolors solely for pleasure, left many unsigned, and sometimes discarded them.

**Evidence:** Direct recollection by his son, including the mother’s objections. The unsigned surviving sheets and their delayed exhibition are consistent with the account.

**Classification:** Reported family fact, credible but not independently documented at the time.

**Effect:** The story distinguishes his private pleasure in making art from professional competition and commercial exhibition.

### The book as “a work of love”

**Story:** Later accounts depict Veroni as continuing to make fine books even when no client existed, accepting their low profitability for the sake of beauty.

**Evidence:** The minuscule editions, lengthy production process, continued salaried newspaper work, and surviving workshop record support the claim. Veroni’s own financial accounts or diary were not found.

**Classification:** Supported interpretive narrative rather than a directly documented self-description.

### Name confusion as a source of false biography

Because both father and son are visual artists and editors named Raúl Veroni, later catalogues and event notices occasionally attribute the son’s post-1980s work to “Raúl Veroni” without clearly distinguishing him from Raoul.

Works involving graffiti, altered banknotes, *El Lápiz Japonés*, the Teatrito rioplatense, Mar Dulce’s management, or post-1992 exhibitions by a living artist generally belong to **Ral Veroni**, not Raoul Veroni.

## Death and Later Life

The public chronology becomes sparse after his 1984 Book Fair appearance.

He died in Buenos Aires in 1992. The following were not established:

- exact date;
- hospital or place of death;
- immediate and underlying cause;
- funeral and burial location;
- will or probate proceedings;
- estate valuation;
- disposition of the Senillosa house;
- complete distribution of his presses, type, art, and correspondence.

Urania entered a period of inactivity after his death. Ral Veroni later said that he did not immediately feel able to assume the legacy. The birth of Ral’s daughter in 2009 contributed to his decision to revive the imprint in 2010.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

Veroni’s posthumous reputation rests on three connected bodies of work:

1. his independent prints and drawings;
2. his artisanal editions;
3. his role in transmitting manual printing culture.

His books preserve a production system in which one person could select a text, design the page, create and print its images, set metal type, print the sheets, and supervise binding.

The tiny original editions made the books scarce from the outset. They now circulate through specialist dealers, auction houses, libraries, galleries, and private collections. Market descriptions sometimes overstate rarity or simplify Veroni’s exact role, making colophons and institutional copies especially important for attribution.

Ral Veroni has been the principal custodian of his father’s public legacy. He:

- inherited works and workshop materials;
- mounted or supported exhibitions;
- revived Urania;
- supplied family testimony;
- and connected the elder Veroni’s press tradition with contemporary artist’s books.

The 2012 Biblioteca Nacional exhibition was the most important institutional reassessment. It presented Veroni not merely as an illustrator but as an integrated book artist.

The later exhibition of the Villa Gesell watercolors expanded his reputation beyond printmaking and typography, revealing a private practice centered on direct observation and botanical subjects.

No evidence was found of a political cult, formal school bearing his name, major public monument, or broadly popular mythology. His influence remains strongest among Argentine engravers, fine printers, bibliophiles, book artists, and specialist collectors.

## Atlas Connections

Only connections supported by the consulted sources are included. Shared nationality, chronology, or appearance in the same general historical discussion is not counted.

### Jorge Luis Borges

**Evidentiary label: Documented fact.**

In 1958 Francisco Colombo printed a private edition of Borges’s ***Límites*** containing six poems and six original etchings by Leopoldo Presas. Raoul Veroni pulled the etchings. The connection was therefore a production relationship through the Colombo workshop; no personal friendship or meeting has been established.

### Silvina Ocampo

**Evidentiary label: Documented fact.**

Veroni edited and illustrated Ocampo’s ***Tríptico de un jazmín*** (Buenos Aires, 1961; 29 copies) and ***La casa natal*** (1964; 20 copies). Surviving letters from Ocampo to Veroni discuss the images inspired by her poem and thank him for selecting it. This is the strongest documented author-editor correspondence located for the dossier.

### Alfonsina Storni

**Evidentiary label: Documented posthumous artistic connection.**

Veroni produced ***Poemas*** by Storni in 1977, thirty-nine years after her death. The edition ran to fifty copies. There could have been no collaboration or meeting; the connection consists of Veroni’s posthumous selection, design, illustration, and/or production of her poetry.

### Edgar Allan Poe

**Evidentiary label: Documented posthumous artistic connection.**

Veroni issued ***Dos poemas*** in 1952 and ***Annabel Lee*** in 1976. Poe died in 1849, so this was an editorial and artistic engagement with his writings, not a personal relationship.

### Gabriele d’Annunzio

**Evidentiary label: Documented posthumous or indirect artistic connection.**

Veroni issued ***Dos poemas*** in 1945 and ***La lluvia en el pinar*** in 1954 and contributed signed etchings to an edition of ***La estrofa grande***. D’Annunzio died in 1938. No evidence was located that Veroni met him during Veroni’s Italian infancy or later life.

### Domingo Faustino Sarmiento

**Evidentiary label: No direct connection established.**

Sarmiento’s ***Facundo*** was an important 1933 Sociedad de Bibliófilos Argentinos production illustrated by Veroni’s teacher Alfredo Guido and printed by Francisco Colombo. Veroni entered Colombo’s workshop in 1936. The edition formed part of his professional environment, but no evidence shows that he participated in it.

### Adolfo Bioy Casares and Victoria Ocampo

**Evidentiary label: No direct connection established.**

Both belonged to Silvina Ocampo’s immediate family and literary milieu, but the located correspondence and book records connect Veroni only to Silvina. No meeting, correspondence, collaboration, or commission involving Raoul Veroni and either Bioy Casares or Victoria Ocampo was found.

### Remaining atlas roster

No other documented crossing with the supplied atlas roster was found in the consulted sources. In particular, the presence of various atlas figures in Argentine literary, artistic, or political life was not treated as evidence of contact. For most roster names, absence of a connection is the expected result given Veroni’s specialized professional sphere.

## Chronology

- **8 August 1913:** Born in Milan, Italy.
- **Late 1913:** At approximately three months old, migrates with his parents to Argentina.
- **1910s–1920s:** Childhood in Tafí del Valle, Tucumán.
- **Late 1920s or early 1930s:** Moves to Buenos Aires as an adolescent; exact year unknown.
- **1930s:** Studies at Escuela Manuel Belgrano and Escuela Superior de Bellas Artes Ernesto de la Cárcova; student of Alfredo Guido.
- **1936:** Begins work at Francisco A. Colombo’s establishment; receives print prize at Salón de Acuarelistas, Pastelistas y Grabadores.
- **1937:** Receives second reported print prize at the same salon.
- **1938:** Receives another salon prize; participates in the Salón Nacional; begins work at *La Prensa* according to the family chronology.
- **1939:** Receives fourth consecutive reported salon print prize.
- **25 May 1941:** ***El Niño*** receives the Premio Adquisición at the XVIII Salón Anual in Santa Fe.
- **1943:** Establishes Urania; publishes Alberti’s ***Tres recuerdos del cielo*** in 24 copies.
- **1945:** Publishes D’Annunzio’s ***Dos poemas***.
- **Mid-1940s:** Exhibits approximately forty prints and illustrations at Amigos del Arte.
- **11 May 1947:** Uruguayan press index records “Grabados de Raúl Veroni.”
- **1948:** Publishes Flammarion’s ***Urania, la musa del cielo***.
- **1951:** Publishes Leopardi’s ***L’antico amor***.
- **1952:** Publishes Poe’s ***Dos poemas***.
- **1954:** Publishes D’Annunzio’s ***La lluvia en el pinar***.
- **1955:** Begins La Cabellera with Shelley and Carella editions; works on Lugones’s ***Al rastro***.
- **1956:** Begins El camino a Ocloyas; contributes to Muñiz’s ***Desde esta tierra***.
- **1958:** Produces Keats editions; pulls Presas’s etchings for Borges’s ***Límites***.
- **1960:** Reported beginning of independent press installation, financed by Italian-government and Fondo Nacional de las Artes credits.
- **1961:** Ends formal collaboration with Colombo; publishes Eliot and Ocampo; begins El Mato.
- **1962:** Biblioteca Nacional chronology dates opening of Estudio Gráfico Raoul Veroni at Senillosa 944.
- **1965:** Son Ral Veroni born in Buenos Aires.
- **1968:** Produces two separate Sappho editions.
- **1969:** Photographed in the Senillosa workshop; publishes Delmira Agustini.
- **1970s:** Continues morning workshop and evening *La Prensa* schedule; makes private botanical watercolors during Villa Gesell vacations.
- **1971:** Produces ***El andén*** and a new edition of Shelley’s ***A una alondra***.
- **1973:** Produces books by Campana, Rilke, and David Martínez.
- **1974:** Honored by PEN Club Argentino.
- **1975:** Produces Petrarch and Cerruto editions.
- **1976:** Produces ***A la Pampa*** and Poe’s ***Annabel Lee***.
- **1977:** Produces Alfonsina Storni’s ***Poemas*** and ***Poesía portuguesa*** with an original Norah Borges drawing.
- **1978:** Produces editions involving Leopardi, Víctor Rebuffo, and Héctor J. Puglia.
- **1979:** Ends employment at *La Prensa*.
- **1981:** Publishes Ricardo Molinari’s ***Dos poemas***, last title in the Biblioteca Nacional’s four-series list.
- **1984:** Invited to exhibit at the 10th Buenos Aires International Book Fair with Líbero Badii.
- **1992:** Dies in Buenos Aires; exact date and cause unknown.
- **2007:** Work shown at ***Artistas y Bibliófilos***, 33rd Buenos Aires Book Fair.
- **2010:** Ral Veroni revives Ediciones Urania.
- **2011:** Galería Mar Dulce mounts ***Herencia***.
- **2012:** Biblioteca Nacional presents ***El libro como arte*** and an accompanying documentary.
- **2015:** Villa Gesell watercolors publicly exhibited in ***Naturaleza***.
- **2024:** *El Litoral* revisits his career and the contemporary documentation of the 1941 prize.
