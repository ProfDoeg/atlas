## Works by Herbert Osborn Yardley

### Cryptographic organizations and systems

- **1917–1919:** *MI-8*. An institutional project: the U.S. Army’s first permanent codebreaking section, conceived, organized, and directed by Yardley. Its collective solutions cannot ordinarily be assigned to him alone, so MI-8 is better treated as his organizational obra than as a collection of individually authored texts. ([Kahn](https://books.google.com/books?id=NMS69lns1_UC); [NSA](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Digital-Media-Center/Biographies/Biography-View-Page/Article/1623030/herbert-o-yardley/))

- **1919–1929:** *The Cipher Bureau / American Black Chamber*. An institutional continuation of MI-8, organized and directed by Yardley for the Army and State Department. Its reconstruction of foreign diplomatic systems was collaborative and secret; the surviving products resist classification as Yardley’s individual works. ([NSA Yardley Collection](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Yardley-Collection/smdpage14698/2/); [Kahn](https://books.google.com/books?id=NMS69lns1_UC))

- **1920:** *Code Compiling Company*. A commercial cover organization created by Yardley with Charles J. Mendelsohn, both to publish commercial codes and to house the clandestine Cipher Bureau. It is simultaneously a business, an intelligence apparatus, and a project concealing another project. ([Kahn, pp. 56–57](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1921:** *Universal Trade Code*. A commercial telegraphic codebook compiled under the supervision of Yardley and Charles J. Mendelsohn, containing 100,000 usable codewords and mechanisms for detecting transmission errors. It is a protocol and working instrument rather than a narrative book. ([Kahn, bibliography and pp. 56–57](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1933:** *Secret-ink advertising system*. A commercial product comprising invisible ink, developer, treated paper, and instructions, developed and marketed through Yardley’s short-lived business. Formulas and production involved experimentation and associates, so it is not securely an individual invention; the business was eventually sold to Virgil Vandeventer. ([Kahn, pp. 146–148](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1938–1940:** *Chinese codebreaking organization*. An institutional project established and trained by Yardley for Chiang Kai-shek’s government to attack Japanese military communications. Its operational products were collective and classified, not discrete public works attributable solely to Yardley. ([Kahn](https://books.google.com/books?id=NMS69lns1_UC); [NSA biography](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Digital-Media-Center/Biographies/Biography-View-Page/Article/1623030/herbert-o-yardley/))

- **1941:** *Examination Unit*. Canada’s wartime cryptanalytic organization, founded and initially directed by Yardley. Like his earlier bureaus, it is an institutional obra whose intercepted messages, solutions, and procedures were made collectively. ([Kahn](https://books.google.com/books?id=NMS69lns1_UC); [NSA biography](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Digital-Media-Center/Biographies/Biography-View-Page/Article/1623030/herbert-o-yardley/))

### Memoirs, technical books, and protocols

- **1931:** *The American Black Chamber*. A cryptologic memoir, exposé, and technical popularization describing MI-8 and the Cipher Bureau. Contemporary insiders disputed numerous details: Charles Mendelsohn identified exaggerations and fictionalized episodes, although he still regarded its larger account as substantially valuable. ([Kahn, pp. 104–120](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html); [NSA](https://www.nsa.gov/Press-Room/Digital-Media-Center/Biographies/Biography-View-Page/Article/1623030/herbert-o-yardley/))

- **1932:** *Yardleygrams*. A hybrid puzzle book of short espionage narratives, cryptograms, worksheets, and solutions—a game-like protocol for learning elementary cryptanalysis. Clem Koukol ghostwrote the book; Kahn concludes that Yardley probably contributed the cryptanalytic material and story concepts, making its authorship collaborative and concealed. ([Kahn, pp. 139–141 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1945:** *The Achievements of the Cipher Bureau (MI-8) During the First World War*. A retrospective technical-historical report prepared for the Office of the Chief Signal Officer; it later appeared posthumously in *Cryptologia* as “From the Archives” in 1984. The 1984 publication is a later manifestation, not a new Yardley work. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html); [Lou Kruh collection bibliography](https://apprendre-en-ligne.net/crypto/references/LouKruh.html))

- **1957:** *The Education of a Poker Player, Including Where and How One Learns to Win*. A combined memoir, sequence of poker stories, probability-based strategy manual, and protocol for play. Its mingling of autobiography, fiction-like narration, and instruction makes “poker book” more accurate than any single literary genre. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html); [PokerNews](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2017/08/poker-pop-culture-061-herbert-o-yardley-codebreaker-28804.htm))

- **date of composition not established; published 1983:** *The Chinese Black Chamber: An Adventure in Espionage*. A memoir of Yardley’s Chinese cryptanalytic mission, withheld during his lifetime and manifested posthumously with an introduction by James Bamford and an afterword by Edna Ramsaier Yardley. The publication date therefore dates the manifestation, not necessarily Yardley’s manuscript. ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/the-spy-who-exposed-the-secrets-of-the-black-chamber-one-of-americas-first-code-breaking-organizations-180985947/); [1983 edition record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA19199861))

### Essays, exposés, and technical articles

- **4 April 1931:** *Secret Inks*. A popular technical article in the *Saturday Evening Post* explaining clandestine-writing methods through Yardley’s intelligence experience. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **18 April 1931:** *Codes*. A popular explanatory article in the *Saturday Evening Post* on code systems and their analysis. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **9 May 1931:** *Ciphers*. A popular explanatory article in the *Saturday Evening Post* on cipher systems and cryptanalysis. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **12 July 1931:** *How They Captured the German Spy, Mme. Maria de Victorica, Told at Last*. A historical espionage article in *Every Week Magazine*. Its account belongs to the same memoir-exposé project as *The American Black Chamber*. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **26 July 1931:** *Secrets of America’s Black Chamber*. A magazine exposé in *Every Week Magazine* about American cryptanalytic operations. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **10 October 1931:** *Double-Crossing America*. A *Liberty* article recounting Yardley’s solution of Japanese diplomatic messages concerning the 1927 intervention in Shantung. It is memoir and disclosure rather than an independent technical study. ([Kahn, pp. 137–139 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **21 November 1931:** *Cryptograms and Their Solution*. A popular cryptanalytic article in the *Saturday Evening Post*. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **19 December 1931:** *Are We Giving Away Our State Secrets?*. A *Liberty* article incorporating two of Yardley’s earlier letters urging the State Department to improve its cryptographic security. It functions partly as a public warning and partly as a republished policy intervention. ([Kahn, pp. 137–139 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **8 April 1934:** *Spies inside Our Gates*. A nonfiction espionage article published simultaneously in the *Sunday Washington Star Magazine* and the *New York Herald Tribune Magazine*. These are two manifestations of one text, not two works. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

### Cryptographic puzzle series

- **26 December 1931–5 March 1932:** *Yardleygrams*. Six short *Liberty* features, each combining an anecdote, an elementary cryptogram, and a challenge to the reader; solutions appeared in following issues. The shared title names a serial puzzle format rather than six securely titled individual works. ([Kahn, bibliography and pp. 139–141](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **27 May–12 August 1933:** *Yardleygrams*. A second, twelve-part *Liberty* series consisting of letter squares traversed by an unbroken route to reveal concealed messages. It is a redesigned puzzle protocol, distinct from the anecdotal first series despite retaining its title. ([Kahn, bibliography and pp. 147–148](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

### Fiction

- **30 December 1933:** *The Beautiful Secret Agent*. An espionage short story in *Liberty*, introducing codebreaker Nathaniel Greenleaf. Kahn judges it harshly but documents it as one of Yardley’s first published attempts at fiction. ([Kahn, pp. 148–150 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **April 1934:** *The Blonde Countess*. An espionage novel developed from the “H-27” story and an earlier dramatic project. Published under Yardley’s name alone, it was substantially written by Carl Grabo, who claimed 99 percent of the prose; its credited and actual authorship therefore diverge sharply. ([Kahn, pp. 151–153 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **21 April 1934:** *H-27, the Blonde Woman from Antwerp*. An espionage short story in *Liberty*, featuring Nathaniel Greenleaf and a fictionalized German agent. It supplied material for *The Blonde Countess* and the film project *Stamboul Quest*. ([Kahn, pp. 150–151 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **November 1934:** *Red Sun of Nippon*. An espionage novel involving Japanese codebreaking and the recurring Nathaniel Greenleaf. Kahn’s bibliography assigns it jointly to Yardley and Carl Grabo, although stylistic evidence led Kahn to conclude that Yardley wrote this novel largely without Grabo. ([Kahn, pp. 153–154 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1945:** *Crows Are Black Everywhere*. An espionage novel about an American reporter and a Japanese spy ring in wartime Chungking, made with Carl Grabo. It transforms Yardley’s Chinese experience into fiction rather than recording it as memoir. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

### Radio and screen work

- **21 January–12 July 1935:** *Stories of the Black Chamber*. A three-part NBC radio serial made with D. Thomas Curtin: *Secret Ink* (twenty-one installments), *The Spy Exchange* (twenty-two), and *The Girl from Soho* (twenty-nine). It is a broadcast Gesamtkunstwerk of script, performance, sound, espionage fiction, advertising, and promotion for Yardley’s secret-ink product; surviving scripts are documented, but no audio recordings are known. ([Kahn, pp. 154–157](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html); [National Cryptologic Foundation](https://cryptologicfoundation.org/ncf-history/stories-from-the-black-chamber-manuscript/))

- **1934–1935:** *Stamboul Quest*. A screen project derived from “H-27” for which Yardley checked factual plausibility and supplied written memoranda while employed by MGM. The completed film cannot straightforwardly be called his work: he was a contributing writer and technical adviser, not its sole author. ([Kahn, Hollywood chapter](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1935:** *Rendezvous*. A film marketed as based on Yardley’s work, with Yardley employed in Hollywood as writer and technical adviser. Kahn states that the finished film had essentially nothing to do with *The American Black Chamber* or Yardley’s stories, so it resists inclusion as a Yardley work despite the screen credit and publicity. ([Kahn, pp. 173–182](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

### Unpublished, suppressed, or abandoned projects

- **1931:** *Codes, Ciphers and Their Solution*. A projected textbook for young readers, begun and then abandoned; Yardley subsequently sent his agent a 2,000-word piece related to the idea, but it was never published. Its status is project rather than completed obra. ([Kahn, pp. 137–138](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1932–1933:** *Japanese Diplomatic Secrets*. A 970-page manuscript assembled from intercepted Japanese dispatches with connective prose, ghostwritten by Marie Stuart Klooz for Yardley. Federal authorities seized it before publication; subsequent legislation effectively prohibited its disclosure, making it a suppressed collaborative documentary compilation rather than a published book. ([Kahn, pp. 159–172](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1933–1934:** *Dramatization of the American Black Chamber*. An unpublished 108-page dramatic typescript made with Carl Grabo. It began as a play, remained untitled beyond this descriptive designation, and metamorphosed into material for *The Blonde Countess*. ([Kahn, bibliography and pp. 151–153](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1934:** *Eaters of Men*. An unpublished approximately 75,000-word serial or novel about arms manufacturers, made with Charles E. Whittaker. Its manuscript is lost or unlocated; its existence rests on Yardley’s 1 December 1934 letter to George Bye, so completeness and final form cannot now be verified. ([Kahn, bibliography and pp. 151–152](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1938:** *Shadows in Washington: A Story of Intrigue*. A completed, unpublished 275-page typescript described by Yardley as a peacetime Washington spy story. Multiple editors rejected it, but the manuscript survives sufficiently to count as an obra rather than merely an intention. ([Kahn, pp. 184–186 and bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **1940 or later:** *Japanese Military Codes and Ciphers in Occupied China: Period 1938–1940*. A classified technical-historical report on Yardley’s Chinese cryptanalytic work. It is an operational record rather than a literary publication, and its precise composition date is not established by the consulted bibliography. ([Kahn, index and China/Canada chapters](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))

- **date not established:** *On Pearl Harbor*. An unpublished nineteen-page manuscript preserved in the Herbert O. Yardley Collection, National Archives Record Group 457, box 3. Its form is an argumentative historical essay; Alexander Hagerty later examined it in *Cryptologia*, but that article is not Yardley’s work. ([Kahn, bibliography](https://dokumen.pub/the-reader-of-gentlemens-mail-herbert-o-yardley-and-the-birth-of-american-codebreaking-9780300129885.html))
