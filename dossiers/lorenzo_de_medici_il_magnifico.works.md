## Works by Lorenzo di Piero de' Medici

### Lyric obra and authorial collections

- **c. 1460s–1480s:** *Rime*. An authorially shaped lyric corpus comprising 108 numbered sonnets, eight canzoni, five sestine, and a ballata; many individual poems have no original titles and are therefore best identified by number or first line, not by editorial subject-headings. ([Simioni, vol. I, “Rime”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/III._Rime); [Guarino, *Complete Literary Works*, “Lyrics”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **c. 1480–1485:** *Comento del magnifico Lorenzo de’ Medici sopra alcuni de’ suoi sonetti*. A prose-and-verse self-commentary on selected lyrics: neither simply a poetry collection nor merely criticism, but an autobiographical and philosophical remaking of his own poems. Its date and degree of completion remain uncertain. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. I](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “Commentary on Some of His Sonnets”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Selva d’Amore I*. The first of two extended sylvae in mixed lyric and narrative modes; it survives as part of the larger, apparently unfinished *Selve d’Amore* project. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. I](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “Sylva I”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Selva d’Amore II*. A second and substantially longer sylva; its relation to the first makes *Selve d’Amore* an incomplete poetic project rather than a securely finished book. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. I](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “Sylva II”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Elegia*. An elegiac poem transmitted among Lorenzo’s longer vernacular compositions; the title is generic rather than evidence of a separately published book. ([Ross and Hutton, *Poesie volgari*, vol. II](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzone in morte della Nencia*. A short lament attached in the textual tradition to the Nencia material; its attribution is not as secure as that of the central lyric corpus. ([Ross and Hutton, *Poesie volgari*, vol. II](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))

### Pastoral, mythological, and descriptive poems

- **date uncertain:** *Corinto*. A pastoral eclogue, also known through the descriptive title *Innamoramento di Lorenzo de’ Medici*; it is one of the two texts grouped as *Egloghe* in the collected edition. ([Simioni, “Egloghe”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/VI._Egloghe); [Guarino, “Eclogues”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Apollo e Pan*. A mythological-pastoral eclogue and the second work in the collected *Egloghe*. ([Simioni, “Egloghe”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/VI._Egloghe))
- **probably 1480s:** *Ambra*. A mythological and topographical poem about the landscape around Lorenzo’s villa at Poggio a Caiano; it turns a place, a river, and a metamorphosis into a dynastic landscape-poem. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. I](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “Ambra”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Amori di Venere e Marte*. A short mythological poem on Venus and Mars, transmitted separately in the collected corpus. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. II](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “The Loves of Venus and Mars”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **after 1473, date uncertain:** *La caccia col falcone*. A comic-descriptive hunting poem in ottava rima, tied to an actual social performance but made into a narrative poetic obra. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. II](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “The Hunt with Falcons”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))

### Comic and narrative obra

- **probably late 1460s:** *Nencia da Barberino*. A rustic mock-pastoral in ottava rima. Its authorship, extent, and textual boundaries are disputed: manuscripts and early print transmit materially different versions, and the long vulgate form may contain additions by Lorenzo or by other hands. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. II, including the doubtful vulgate](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Ross and Hutton, editorial discussion](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf); [Guarino, “Nencia da Barberino I–II”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **before 1474, probably unfinished:** *Simposio, ovvero I beoni*. A comic catalogue-poem in terza rima portraying Florentine drinkers. It survives in nine capitoli and resists being called a finished work: its abrupt state is commonly treated as incompletion rather than a designed ending. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. II](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “Symposium”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **probably late 1460s:** *Novella di Giacoppo*. A completed prose novella modeled on the Boccaccian tale; recent scholarship has reopened the dating of this and the accompanying *Ginevra*. ([Orvieto, *Tutte le opere*, bibliographic record](https://renovella.unisi.it/corpus/biblio/view?id=427); [Zanato, “Ancora sulla datazione delle novelle”](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-653-4/978-88-6969-653-4_WUVsoAs.pdf); [Guarino, “Short Stories”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **probably late 1460s; unfinished:** *Novella di Ginevra*. An incomplete prose novella concerned with Luigi Lanfranchi’s love for Ginevra de’ Griffi. It is explicitly a fragment or abandoned project, not a finished short story. ([Zanato, “Ancora sulla datazione delle novelle”](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-653-4/978-88-6969-653-4_WUVsoAs.pdf); [Guarino, “Short Stories”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))

### Philosophical and devotional obra

- **c. 1473–1474:** *Altercazione*. A philosophical disputation in terza rima on the highest good, staged as dialogue and informed by Lorenzo’s engagement with Ficinian Platonism. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. II](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Guarino, “Disputation”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Capitolo dove eccito ed esorto me medesimo*. A moral-philosophical capitolo in terza rima, an act of self-exhortation rather than a treatise. ([Simioni, “Capitoli”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/VII._Capitoli))
- **date uncertain:** *Capitolo fatto a Giovanfrancesco Ventura per la morte di una sua figliuola*. A consolatory verse epistle or capitolo addressed to a bereaved father. ([Simioni, “Capitoli”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/VII._Capitoli))
- **date uncertain:** *Sonetti spirituali*. A small body of devotional sonnets; the collective heading is editorial, while the individual poems generally lack stable authorial titles. ([Simioni, “Rime spirituali”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XII._Rime_spirituali); [Guarino, “Devotional Poems”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Capitoli spirituali* (also called *Orazioni* in parts of the tradition). A group of verse prayers or devotional capitoli—five in the older complete poetic compilation—whose generic identity shifts between prayer, poem, and spiritual exercise. ([Simioni, “Rime spirituali”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XII._Rime_spirituali); [Ross and Hutton, *Poesie volgari*, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **date uncertain:** *Laude*. A body of spiritual songs intended for devotional singing; they are lyric texts and potential performances, not books, and the collected tradition contains attribution problems at its edges. ([Simioni, “Rime spirituali”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XII._Rime_spirituali); [Guarino, “Hymns of Praise”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **date uncertain:** *Sonnets to Ginevra de’ Benci*. A small occasional sequence conventionally separated from Lorenzo’s other verse; its status is that of a linked group rather than a demonstrably authorial book. ([Guarino, “Sonnets to Ginevra de’ Benci”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))

### Sacred theatre as Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1491:** *Rappresentazione di san Giovanni e Paolo*. A sacred play in verse, written for performance and therefore simultaneously text, spectacle, speech, costume, music, and civic devotion—a genuine Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a book. The early printed tradition varies the title and sometimes adds Saint Costanza. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. II](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)); [Simioni, survey of editions](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/Nota/II._Edizioni); [Guarino, “The Mystery Play of Saints John and Paul”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))

### Canzoni a ballo: texts made for singing and dancing

- **dates uncertain:** *Canzoni a ballo*. A corpus of thirty-two dance-songs in Simioni’s edition, beginning with *Amor, c’hai visto ciascun mio pensiero* and ending with *Le sette allegrezze d’amore*. The corpus is a performative manifestation rather than a single book, and several pieces entered Lorenzo’s canon through late or unstable attribution. ([Simioni, complete contents](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XV._Canzoni_a_ballo))
- **date uncertain:** *La confessione*. A comic canzonetta transmitted as the thirty-first *canzone a ballo*; its separate title and relatively late printed witnesses make its attribution less secure than the central lyric canon. ([Simioni, “Canzoni a ballo”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XV._Canzoni_a_ballo); [Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **date uncertain:** *Le sette allegrezze d’amore*. A canzonetta and the thirty-second item in Simioni’s dance-song corpus; its recovery from rare later printing leaves its authorship less firmly grounded than manuscript-authorized poems. ([Simioni, “Canzoni a ballo”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XV._Canzoni_a_ballo); [Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))

### Carnival manifestations and procession-songs

- **date uncertain:** *Canzona de’ confortini*. A carnival song voiced by sellers of medicinal or confectionery comforts; it is a script for a public musical manifestation. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona de’ profumi*. A carnival song for perfumers, built for collective singing and comic double meaning. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona de’ cialdoni*. A carnival vendors’ song for wafer-sellers, simultaneously lyric text and performance protocol. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona degl’innestatori*. A carnival song for grafters, exploiting the trade as a basis for theatrical and erotic equivocation. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona dello zibetto*. A carnival song for sellers of civet perfume; the made thing includes an imagined occupational troupe and its public voice. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona delle forese*. A carnival song assigned to countrywomen, made for a staged collective persona rather than private reading. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **probably 1490:** *Canzona di Bacco*, also called *Trionfo di Bacco e Arianna*. Lorenzo’s best-known carnival-procession song; the poem survives, but its original realization was a multimedia civic manifestation of bodies, costume, music, movement, and text. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi); [Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona de’ sette pianeti*, also called *Trionfo dei sette pianeti*. A cosmological procession-song whose full obra was the moving spectacle as much as the surviving words. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi); [Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona delle cicale*. A carnival song for a costumed or impersonated group of cicadas. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona de’ visi addrieto*. A carnival song for performers walking or appearing with their faces turned backward; its identity depends upon bodily staging and thus resists reduction to text alone. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzona de’ fornai*. A carnival trade-song for bakers, designed for collective performance. ([Simioni, “Canti carnascialeschi”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVI._Canti_carnascialeschi))
- **dates and attributions uncertain:** *Other carnival songs transmitted under Lorenzo’s name*. Early collections also assign him songs for hermits, gold-spinners, girls and cicadas, beggars, bericuocolai, young wives and old husbands, shoemakers, women of San Cresci, muleteers, oil-makers, cesspit-emptiers, and resellers; Simioni excludes or quarantines many of these as doubtful, so they resist inclusion as secure Lorenzo obra. ([Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf); [Simioni, “Rime varie o di dubbia autenticità”](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVII._Rime_varie_o_di_dubbia_autenticit%C3%A1))

### Epistles, letters, and political protocols

- **date uncertain:** *Epistola a don Federico d’Aragona*. A literary verse epistle placed first in Simioni’s collected works; it is an occasional address, not a freestanding book. ([Simioni, *Opere*, vol. I](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)))
- **dates uncertain:** *Three comic epistles*. A group of burlesque verse letters preserved among the comic works; the grouping is editorial rather than an authorial collection. ([Guarino, “Three Epistles”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt1t88tg3))
- **1460–1492:** *Letters*. A vast working corpus of personal, diplomatic, financial, patronal, and governmental communications—often dictated, copied, registered, or issued through secretaries. These letters are acts and instruments as much as texts; the national scholarly edition remains incomplete, currently publishing material through April 1491 with gaps in the volume sequence, so no honest entry can pretend that the surviving corpus is closed. ([Istituto Nazionale di Studi sul Rinascimento, *Lettere di Lorenzo de’ Medici*](https://www.insr.it/lettere-di-lorenzo-de-medici/))
- **1491:** *Instruction to Giovanni de’ Medici on going to Rome*. A paternal-political protocol within the correspondence, instructing Lorenzo’s young cardinal son how to conduct himself; it acts upon future behavior rather than merely describing it. It belongs to the letter corpus, not to a separately authored “book.” ([Istituto Nazionale di Studi sul Rinascimento, volume chronology](https://www.insr.it/lettere-di-lorenzo-de-medici/))

### Doubtful, unstable, or editorially created obra

- **dates uncertain:** *Rime varie o di dubbia autenticità*. This is not an authorial work but an editorial quarantine containing sonnets, capitoli, laude, dance-songs, carnival songs, and the expanded vulgate *Nencia* that have circulated under Lorenzo’s name without secure attribution. Its resistance to being called “a work” is precisely its value: it records the unstable historical boundary of Lorenzo’s oeuvre. ([Simioni, complete doubtful section](https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Opere_(Lorenzo_de%27_Medici)/XVII._Rime_varie_o_di_dubbia_autenticit%C3%A1))
- **date uncertain:** *Le donne ciarlone*. A burlesque canzone printed under Lorenzo’s name in later collections; its late textual basis leaves the attribution uncertain. ([Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **dates uncertain:** *Amante sventurato*, *La capricciosa*, *La pietosa*, and *Io vi vo’, donne, insegnare*. Four canzonette admitted by older editors from late printed sources rather than strong contemporary witnesses; they belong to the attributed tradition, not unreservedly to the secure oeuvre. ([Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
- **date uncertain:** *Canzone in morte della Nencia* and *Amori di Marte e Venere* as separately transmitted versions. Although included by older collected editions, their particular textual forms depend on relatively late or derivative witnesses; “Lorenzo’s work” here means a contested textual tradition, not one fixed authorial object. ([Ross and Hutton, preface](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/enh460b2447643A.pdf))
