## Works by Saint Thecla of Iconium

### Attributed prayers and ritual acts

- **Undated; attributed in a late-second-century narrative:** Prayer at the pyre. An oral prayer placed in Thecla’s mouth before her first attempted execution in Iconium; it survives only as speech composed for the anonymous *Acts of Paul and Thecla*, not as an independently transmitted text by a demonstrably historical Thecla. ([Barrier, commentary on *Acts of Paul and Thecla*](https://repository.tcu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fddaed7-46c6-46e6-bab9-5d7d6c338eb2/content))

- **Undated; attributed in a late-second-century narrative:** Prayer for Falconilla. This is an intercessory oral act through which the narrative says Thecla transfers Tryphaena’s deceased daughter to the place of the righteous. It behaves more like an efficacious manifestation than a literary work, and scholarship treats Thecla herself as probably fictional. ([Trumbower, “Thecla’s Prayer for Falconilla”](https://doi.org/10.1093/0195140990.003.0004))

- **Undated; attributed in a late-second-century narrative:** Self-baptism in the arena. By entering the water and invoking Jesus Christ, Thecla makes herself baptized: a ritual-performative act rather than an authored text. Its attribution is inseparable from the *Acts* and became controversial because readers used Thecla as a precedent for women baptizing and teaching. ([Barrier, *The Acts of Paul and Thecla*](https://repository.tcu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fddaed7-46c6-46e6-bab9-5d7d6c338eb2/content); [Kraemer, “Thecla of Iconium, Reconsidered”](https://academic.oup.com/book/9896/chapter-abstract/157211306))

### Teaching as an attributed project

- **Undated; attributed in a late-second-century narrative:** Admonition or proclamation to Theocleia. After Paul commissions her to teach, Thecla returns to Iconium and addresses her mother; this is presented as preaching “the word of God,” not as a titled or preserved composition, and the narrative does not report whether it succeeds. ([Barrier, commentary, especially APTh 4.18](https://repository.tcu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fddaed7-46c6-46e6-bab9-5d7d6c338eb2/content))

- **Undated; attributed in variant endings of the *Acts*:** Teaching ministry at Seleucia. Some witnesses say only that Thecla enlightened many before dying; the longer Codex Baroccianus ending makes her a cave-dwelling teacher of women and healer. This is an open-ended apostolic project rather than a recoverable body of authored texts, and the endings are later, mutually divergent expansions rather than part of the earliest recoverable conclusion. ([Barrier, discussion and text of APTh 44–45 and Codex G](https://repository.tcu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fddaed7-46c6-46e6-bab9-5d7d6c338eb2/content))

### Material making attributed in the narrative

- **Undated; attributed in a late-second-century narrative:** Altered tunic made into a man’s cloak. The *Acts* says that Thecla sewed or refashioned her tunic before travelling to find Paul. It is an ordinary garment and has no surviving material identity or fixed title, but it is the clearest tangible object the tradition expressly represents her as making. ([Barrier, *The Acts of Paul and Thecla*](https://repository.tcu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fddaed7-46c6-46e6-bab9-5d7d6c338eb2/content); [Jacobs, *The Life and Passion of Saint Thecla*, introduction](https://www.nasscal.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Jacobs-Life-of-Thecla-Preview.pdf))

### Posthumous manifestations, not securely her works

- **Fifth century onward:** Healings and other interventions collected as the *Miracles of Saint Thecla*. These manifestations are narrated as acts performed by the living or posthumously active saint, but the collection itself is an anonymous fifth-century literary work, not a book made by Thecla; treating its individual miracle stories as her authored oeuvre would confuse the saint’s cultic agency with the hagiographer’s composition. The standard critical edition contains forty-six miracle narratives. ([Johnson, *The Life and Miracles of Thekla: A Literary Study*](https://chs.harvard.edu/book/johnson-scott-fitzgerald-the-life-and-miracles-of-thekla-a-literary-study/); [Dagron edition described in *The Journal of Theological Studies*](https://academic.oup.com/jts/article-abstract/58/1/310/2931905))

- **Second century onward:** No securely attributable written oeuvre. Neither the *Acts of Paul and Thecla* nor the later *Life and Miracles* claims to be written by Thecla: the former was attributed by Tertullian to an Asian presbyter writing from love of Paul, while the latter is anonymous. Because Thecla’s historical existence cannot be established independently of her legendary dossier, even the prayers, teaching, baptism, garment, and miracles above resist classification as works by a recoverable historical maker. ([Barrier, *The Acts of Paul and Thecla*](https://repository.tcu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6fddaed7-46c6-46e6-bab9-5d7d6c338eb2/content); [Johnson, *The Life and Miracles of Thekla*](https://chs.harvard.edu/book/johnson-scott-fitzgerald-the-life-and-miracles-of-thekla-a-literary-study/))
