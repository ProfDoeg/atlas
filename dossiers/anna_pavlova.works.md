## Works by Anna Matveyevna Pavlova

### Choreographic miniatures

- **c. 1909–1915:** *The Dragonfly* (*La Libellule*). A solo choreographic miniature set to Fritz Kreisler’s *Schön Rosmarin*; Pavlova devised both choreography and costume. Its date is reported variously: the lyrical miniatures apparently began around 1909, while surviving New York documentation dates *Dragonfly* to 1915. ([Pavlova Project](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/credits); [Lazzarini and Lazzarini, *Pavlova: Repertoire of a Legend*](https://search.worldcat.org/title/5564846))

- **c. 1909–1915:** *The Butterfly* (*Le Papillon*). A solo choreographed by Pavlova, performed to music variously identified as by Léo Delibes, Ludwig Minkus, or Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov; this instability suggests a mutable touring number rather than a composition with one fixed score. A 1915 Los Angeles performance is documented, but not necessarily the première. ([Pavlova Project](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/credits); [Lazzarini and Lazzarini, *Pavlova: Repertoire of a Legend*](https://search.worldcat.org/title/5564846))

- **c. 1909 or later:** *Night* (*La Nuit*). A lyrical solo included among Pavlova’s own “choreographic melodclamations.” Its authorship is not entirely secure: some accounts treat it as Pavlova’s composition, while another tradition derives her solo from choreography by Nikolai Legat; no reliable première date should therefore be imposed. ([Russian biographical account](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0%2C_%D0%90%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%9F%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%BD%D0%B0); [Lazzarini and Lazzarini, *Pavlova: Repertoire of a Legend*](https://search.worldcat.org/title/5564846))

- **1913:** *Gavotte Pavlova*. A theatrical gavotte to Paul Lincke’s music, originated or arranged for herself by Pavlova and subsequently treated as a distinct dance. Sources sometimes credit Ivan Clustine, so it is safer to regard it as a Pavlova–Clustine touring creation rather than assign uncontested sole authorship. ([contemporary 1914 report](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5b/Victoria_Daily_Times_%281914-01-16%29_%28IA_victoriadailytimes19140116%29.pdf); [University of Surrey study](https://s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/eu-st01.ext.exlibrisgroup.com/44SUR_INST/upload/1762717126092/27557476.pdf))

- **1915:** *California Poppy* (*The Californian Poppy*). A flower solo to Tchaikovsky’s *Mélodie* in E-flat, with choreography and costume attributed to Pavlova; the documented San Francisco presentation belongs to 1915, although later sources sometimes give about 1916. ([Pavlova Project](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/credits); [Pointe](https://pointemagazine.com/tbt-anna-pavlova-circa-1916/))

- **1918 or 1919:** *Autumn Leaves* (*Feuilles d’automne*). A one-act “choreographic poem,” with scenario and choreography by Pavlova and music assembled from Chopin, in which she embodied the last chrysanthemum of autumn. It is her only documented ensemble ballet under sole choreographic authorship; sources disagree between a 1918 and probably 1919 Rio de Janeiro première. ([Alberto Testa repertory entry](https://www.informadanza.com/la-danza/storia-della-danza-storie-di-danze/le-trame-dei-balletti/il-mondo-del-ballettoil-balletto-dalla-a-alla-z/autumn-leaves/); [National Library of Australia News](https://michellepotter.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/As-light-as-air.pdf); [Arts Centre Melbourne](https://stories.artscentremelbourne.com.au/divas-in-transit/index.html))

### Social-dance protocols

- **1914–1915:** *The Pavlowna*. A social dance developed by Pavlova from prize-winning music and published as photographed, step-by-step instruction in the *Ladies’ Home Journal*. It is both choreography and protocol: a dance designed for reproduction by ordinary social dancers rather than a unique stage manifestation. ([contemporary instructional feature](https://www.libraryofdance.org/manuals/1915-Pavlowa-Pavlowana_%28Hathi%29.pdf); [*Dance Index* bibliography](https://books.google.com/books?q=%22Dance+Index%22+%22Pavlowana%22))

- **1914–1915:** *Czarina Waltz*. The second of three social dances devised by Pavlova in 1914 and issued with music, photographs, and instructions in early 1915. It was deliberately made as a practicable participatory dance, so the work resides partly in its protocol rather than in any one performance. ([1914–15 tour publication](https://www.scribd.com/document/162191803/Mlle-Anna-Pavlowa-00); [*Dance Index* bibliography](https://books.google.com/books?q=%22Dance+Index%22+%22Czarina+Waltz%22+Pavlova))

- **1914–1915:** *Gavotte Renaissance*. The third dance in Pavlova’s social-dance series, explicitly distinguished in its contemporary description from the earlier *Gavotte Pavlova*. Its obra is a repeatable sequence of partnered figures disseminated through illustrated instructions. ([1914–15 tour publication](https://www.scribd.com/document/162191803/Mlle-Anna-Pavlowa-00); [*Dance Index* bibliography](https://books.google.com/books?q=%22Dance+Index%22+%22Gavotte+Renaissance%22+Pavlova))

### Ballet adaptations and collaborative Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1911:** *Snowflakes*. A touring condensation of the pine-forest journey and snow scene from Tchaikovsky’s *The Nutcracker*, staged principally by Ivan Clustine for Pavlova’s company and shaped around Pavlova as Snow Queen. It belongs here as a collaborative adaptation she commissioned and embodied, not as securely documented sole choreography by Pavlova. ([history of *Nutcracker* productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_productions_of_The_Nutcracker); [New Zealand performance study](https://paperspast.natlib.govt.nz/periodicals/TLR20090101.2.12))

- **date uncertain, before 1931:** *Christmas*. A company ballet using the December movement from Tchaikovsky’s *The Seasons*, credited jointly to Pavlova, Ivan Clustine, and Alexandre Volinine. Its divided authorship and combination of music, ensemble choreography, costume, and seasonal stage picture make it a small collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an autonomous Pavlova solo. ([Pavlova Project](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/credits); [Chicago Film Archives](https://collections.chicagofilmarchives.org/Detail/objects/9161))

- **1917:** *Mexican Dances*. A group of dances developed after Pavlova’s encounter with Mexican performance traditions and danced initially with Mikhail Domyslawski. Sources document the manifestation but do not preserve a stable inventory or establish whether Pavlova, her partner, or local teachers fixed every step; it therefore resists treatment as a single, closed ballet. ([Pavlova Project](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/credits); [Fischer, “The Swan Brand”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767710000234))

- **1923:** *Ajanta Frescoes* (*Les Fresques d’Ajanta*). A miniature ballet project prompted by Pavlova’s visit to the Ajanta caves, with music by Alexander Tcherepnin and choreography usually assigned to Ivan Clustine under Pavlova’s conception and direction. It is best classified as her project and company production, not her sole choreography; contemporary and later critics also disputed the authenticity of its imagined “Indian” movement. ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/anna-pavlova); [Nottingham study](https://eprints.nottingham.ac.uk/65186/1/Geographies%20of%20Indian%20Dance%2C%20Sawjani%20P.%20%5Bcorrected%5D.pdf))

- **1923:** *Oriental Impressions*. A triptych or compilation of short Asian-themed ballets conceived and presented by Pavlova after touring Japan and India. Its components were collaborative—Uday Shankar choreographed material, while other dances drew on teachers encountered during the tour—so the whole is a Pavlova-produced Gesamtkunstwerk but not a work of sole authorship. ([Silvia Mei, “Anna Pavlova, danzatrice eurasiana?”](https://doi.org/10.6092/issn.2039-2281/3284); [Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/anna-pavlova))

- **1923:** *Krishna and Radha*. A duet commissioned by Pavlova and created and choreographed by Uday Shankar, who danced Krishna opposite her Radha. Pavlova made the project possible and helped determine its theatrical frame, but the choreography is Shankar’s; calling it simply “a Pavlova work” would erase that authorship. ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/anna-pavlova); [Making Britain](https://www5.open.ac.uk/research-projects/making-britain/taxonomy/term/686/ram-gopal))

- **1923:** *A Hindu Wedding*. A ballet commissioned and produced within Pavlova’s Indian project, with music by the composer Meera Chatterjee and design/choreographic participation by Uday Shankar. The surviving accounts divide its making among several people, so it is a company production and collaborative manifestation rather than secure Pavlova choreography. ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/anna-pavlova); [Cornell study of Uday Shankar](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstreams/c65944ce-e55c-4ae7-8930-743d7c103611/download))

### Created performance roles

- **1905:** *The Swan*, subsequently *The Dying Swan* (*La Mort du cygne*). Mikhail Fokine choreographed this solo to Saint-Saëns specifically for Pavlova, but her body, interpretation, revisions in performance, and thousands of repetitions created its defining manifestation. It is emphatically not her authored choreography, despite frequent shorthand that she “created” it: she created the role, while Fokine made the choreographic score. ([Fokine attribution and work history](https://www.lodance.app/limelight/the-dying-swan); [Lazzarini and Lazzarini, *Pavlova: Repertoire of a Legend*](https://search.worldcat.org/title/5564846))

- **1915–1916:** *The Dumb Girl of Portici*. Pavlova created the screen performance of the mute Fenella, including a danced prologue and epilogue, in her only feature film. The film itself was directed by Lois Weber and Phillips Smalley and written by Weber, so it is not Pavlova’s film obra; her made thing is the bodily, mimetic performance preserved within it. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Film/18203-THE-DUMB-GIRL-OF-PORTICI?cxt=filmography); [San Francisco Silent Film Festival](https://silentfilm.org/the-dumb-girl-of-portici/))

### Visual studies

- **dates and titles not securely catalogued:** *Small paintings and modeled statuettes of dancing figures and Pavlova’s roles*. Witnesses and later scholarship describe Pavlova making paintings and small sculptures, often as studies of her own bodily positions. No accessible catalogue raisonné establishes a complete item-by-item corpus, titles, dates, or present locations; these objects therefore resist honest enumeration as separately titled works. ([Pavlova Project historical notes](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/historical-notes); [Portnova, *Plastic Arts Role in the Creative Process of the Choreographer*](https://ppublishing.org/media/uploads/journals/monograph/Portnova.pdf))

### Institutional project

- **1910/1911–1931:** *Anna Pavlova’s touring ballet company*. More than an administrative organization, the company was Pavlova’s continuing project: a mutable vehicle combining abridged classics, commissioned ballets, her own miniatures, costumes, décor, musicians, and encounters with local dance traditions across six continents. It has no single fixed textual form or definitive repertoire and thus resists being called one “work,” but the sustained global project is among the largest things she made. ([London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/the-great-ballet-dancer-anna-pavlova/); [Pavlova Project historical notes](https://www.pavlovaproject.com/historical-notes); [Lazzarini and Lazzarini, *Pavlova: Repertoire of a Legend*](https://search.worldcat.org/title/5564846))
