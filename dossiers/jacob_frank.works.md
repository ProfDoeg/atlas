## Works by Jacob Frank

### Dictated teachings and visionary records

- **June–December 1784, with later additions through 1790:** *Zbiór słów Pańskich w Brünnie mówionych* (*Collection of the Words of the Lord Spoken in Brünn*, also *Księga Słów Pańskich* / *Words of the Lord*). This is not a book Frank wrote: followers recorded, translated, arranged, and repeatedly recopied his oral sayings, parables, dreams, reminiscences, and doctrinal lectures. The surviving Polish manuscripts are incomplete and textually divergent; Frank apparently did not command written Polish, but Chone Shmeruk concludes that the words themselves are authentically his, mediated through unnamed scribes. ([Shmeruk](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/68029/PDF/WA248_73391_P-I-2524_shmeruk-ksiega_o.pdf); [Lenowitz](https://www.scribd.com/document/51890818/jacob-frank-mss-lenowitz); [Michaelson](https://academic.oup.com/mj/article-abstract/37/3/338/4108091))

- **Recorded by 1790:** *Zbiór słów Pańskich w Offenbach mówionych* (*Collection of the Words of the Lord Spoken in Offenbach*). A continuation of Frank’s dictated teaching after his move to Offenbach, therefore an oral-scribal obra rather than an autograph text; only its concluding portion, numbered 2119–2188 in the Lublin manuscript, and fragments quoted by nineteenth-century scholars survive, while the last known complete manuscript of the larger corpus was lost during the Second World War. ([Shmeruk](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/68029/PDF/WA248_73391_P-I-2524_shmeruk-ksiega_o.pdf); [Lenowitz](https://www.scribd.com/document/51890818/jacob-frank-mss-lenowitz))

- *Dodatek Słów Pańskich w Brünnie mówionych* (*Supplement to the Words of the Lord Spoken in Brünn*). An undated supplementary group of sayings preserved after the Offenbach colophon in the Lublin manuscript; it resists independent-work status because it is a scribal appendix of older Brünn material rather than a text separately issued or titled by Frank. ([Shmeruk](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/68029/PDF/WA248_73391_P-I-2524_shmeruk-ksiega_o.pdf))

- *Dodatek do Zbioru słów Pańskich w Offenbach mówionych* (*Supplement to the Collection of the Words of the Lord Spoken in Offenbach*). This is another manuscript supplement within the transmitted corpus, not a securely autonomous composition; its boundaries and dating depend upon the surviving copies and modern editorial reconstruction. ([Jan Doktór’s collected edition as described in *Adlojada*](https://adlojada.muzeum.szczecin.pl/images/pliki/Adlojada-Kultura-i-Profanacje-2016.pdf))

- *Widzenia Pańskie* (*The Lord’s Visions*). Records of visions attributed to or recounted before Frank and included by Jan Doktór in the collected textual corpus; their scribal form and uncertain individual authorship make them manifestations of Frank’s visionary court rather than straightforward authored prose. ([Doktór’s collected edition as described in *Adlojada*](https://adlojada.muzeum.szczecin.pl/images/pliki/Adlojada-Kultura-i-Profanacje-2016.pdf); [Kraushar, vol. 2](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/74413/WA248_96677_F-22-143-2_kraushar-frank_o.pdf))

### Letters and proclamations of disputed attribution

- **Allegedly 1760–1773; circulated in 1798–1800:** *Two letters allegedly written during Frank’s imprisonment at Częstochowa*. Their original titles and dates are not securely established. They survive embedded in the posthumous Frankist circulars called the *Czerwone Listy* (*Red Letters*), so Frank’s authorship is a claim made by the later Offenbach community, not a settled attribution. ([YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Frankism))

- **1798–1800:** *Czerwone Listy* (*Red Letters*). These red-ink circular epistles acted as manifestos, warning of catastrophe and urging Jews to accept baptism, but they were composed and dispatched after Frank’s death by the Offenbach leadership; only the two embedded Częstochowa letters were attributed to him. The cycle therefore belongs to Frankism’s posthumous reception, not securely to Frank’s own works. ([YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Frankism); [Encyclopaedia Judaica article](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/frank-jacob-and-frankists))

### Collective records that resist attribution as works by Frank

- *Rozmaite adnotacje, przypadki, czynności i anekdoty pańskie* (*Various Notes, Events, Deeds, and Anecdotes of the Lord*). This Frankist manuscript collects observations, memories, actions, and anecdotes concerning Frank; it is an indispensable witness to his performed teachings but is better classified as a communal record made around him than as a work authored by him. Jan Doktór published the first complete scholarly edition in 1996. ([Acta Poloniae Historica](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/29565/PDF/WA303_48021_1997-76_APH_04_o.pdf); [Polish Literary Bibliography](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/62638/PDF/WA248_PDLW_82378_pbl-monog-rec-2013_o.pdf))

- *Kronika frankistowska* (*The Frankist Chronicle*). Compiled by members of the movement at Offenbach, this detailed internal history records Frank’s life, court, and ritual conduct but was not written by him; only fragments survive, and the manuscript used by earlier scholars subsequently disappeared. It belongs in the atlas as a collective manifestation generated by Frank’s community, while resisting inclusion among his authored works. ([Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities](https://www.academy.ac.il/ShopEng/Entry.aspx?entryId=21238&nodeId=1534); [Encyclopaedia Judaica article](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/frank-jacob-and-frankists))

- **Circa 1800:** *Proroctwa Izajaszowe* (*The Prophecy of Isaiah*). A Polish Frankist reinterpretation of Isaiah surviving only through excerpts published by Aleksander Kraushar; scholarship identifies its author as an anonymous Frankist and dates it after Frank’s death. It is therefore a manifestation of his movement’s revolutionary expectations, not a work by Frank, despite its frequent association with his textual corpus. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica article](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/frank-jacob-and-frankists); [Kraushar, vol. 2](https://rcin.org.pl/Content/74413/WA248_96677_F-22-143-2_kraushar-frank_o.pdf))

### Religious and social projects

- **1755–1791:** *The Frankist movement*. Frank’s largest obra was a continuing messianic project rather than a discrete text: a community, doctrine, hierarchy, itinerary, and sequence of deliberately transformative religious acts assembled from Sabbatean, Jewish, Islamic, and Christian materials. Because followers, opponents, and later generations continually remade it—and because distinct Polish, personal, and Bohemian formations were all called “Frankist”—it resists treatment as a single finished work. ([Maciejko](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt3fhpbw); [Oxford Bibliographies](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554974474))

- **1759:** *The collective passage through Catholic baptism*. Frank directed his followers’ mass conversion as a strategic religious project and enacted manifesto: baptism was presented not as the terminus of conversion but as entry into “Edom” on the way toward a still-future religion or “knowledge.” It was collective, negotiated with church authorities, and therefore cannot be attributed to Frank alone, but its performative structure was central to what he made. ([Maciejko](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt3fhpbw); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Frankism))

- **1773–1791:** *The Brünn and Offenbach courts*. These successive households combined family, sect, ceremonial costume, military display, oral teaching, visionary performance, finance, and political self-presentation; “Gesamtkunstwerk” fits better than “institution” or “book,” although the court was collectively sustained and sometimes coercively governed. It was also the workshop in which Frank’s sayings were recorded and his daughter Eva fashioned as a female messianic figure. ([Maciejko](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt3fhpbw); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Frankism); [Michaelson](https://academic.oup.com/book/43750))
