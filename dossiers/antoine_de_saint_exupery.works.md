## Works by Antoine de Saint-Exupéry

### Juvenilia: stories, poems, and fragments

- **c. 1914:** *Odyssée d’un chapeau haut de forme*. A schoolboy prose tale preserved among the juvenilia; its date is editorially inferred rather than supplied by Saint-Exupéry. [The Pléiade edition prints it among the *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **c. 1914:** *Le son du cor s’élève et baisse lentement*. An untitled juvenile poem, conventionally identified by its opening words; the title therefore belongs to the editor’s descriptive apparatus, not securely to the work. [The Pléiade dates it “vers 1914.”](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **c. 1914:** *Les ailes frémissaient sous le souffle du soir*. An untitled poem about flight, again named here by its opening words and dated approximately by the Pléiade editors. [Pléiade text and date.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1915–1916:** *Le Parapluie*. A juvenile verse satire or song-like fragment; its textual boundaries and precise date are not entirely secure. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1915–1916:** *Soleil d’or*. A juvenile poem reflecting on war and a dying civilization. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1915–1916:** *Quelques profils de Boches*. A wartime schoolboy caricature combining prose and verse; its period language and stereotypes are integral to the historical object. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1915–1916:** *Amertume*. A juvenile wartime poem. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **November 1915:** *Reims*. A juvenile war poem whose manuscript bears a place and month but an imperfectly legible year, editorially understood as 1915. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **December 1915:** *Printemps de guerre*. A sonnet-like juvenile war poem; the year is editorially reconstructed from the manuscript. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1915–1916:** *Désenchantement*. A short juvenile poem or fragment. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1918:** *Ballade du petit bureau*. A comic ballade spoken by a school desk. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **October 1921?:** *Adieu de la maîtresse à l’enfant prodigue*. A poem dated conjecturally by the editors from a Casablanca manuscript. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **November 1921?:** *À mon amie*. A lyric poem with a conjectural Casablanca date. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1921?:** *Sonnet*. A love poem whose generic heading is its only known title; both title specificity and date resist certainty. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **early 1920s:** *L’Adieu*. A multipart poetic sequence comprising “Avant l’adieu,” “L’Adieu,” and “Après l’adieu”; it is better understood as a developing poetic project than as a securely finished publication. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1923?:** *La Lune*. A playful poem with a conjectural date. [Pléiade, *Textes de jeunesse*.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)
- **1920s:** *Manon, danseuse*. A novella or extended prose narrative shown in manuscript to Louise de Vilmorin, Renée de Saussine, and Jean Prévost but not published in Saint-Exupéry’s lifetime; the surviving text is a completed-looking manuscript rather than an authorially released work. [The Saint-Exupéry succession records its posthumous publication in 2007.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)

### Fiction, testimony, meditation, and illustrated Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1926:** *L’Aviateur*. A short story published in *Le Navire d’argent* on 1 April 1926, developed from a larger abandoned manuscript sometimes called *L’Évasion de Jacques Bernis*; it is Saint-Exupéry’s first published literary text. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1929:** *Courrier Sud*. A novel assembled partly from the earlier Bernis project, joining aviation narrative, love story, and meditation; it was his first book. [The Pléiade-based BnF work authority dates and classifies it as a novel.](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb18145734q)
- **1931:** *Vol de nuit*. A novel about the organization, risks, and ethics of night airmail flight. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1939:** *Terre des hommes*. A composite work of memoir, reportage, essay, and philosophical meditation, substantially reworking earlier journalism; its resistance to the category “novel” was already visible when it received the Académie française’s Grand Prix du roman. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1939:** *Wind, Sand and Stars*. The authorized American recasting of *Terre des hommes*, not simply a mechanically translated edition: Saint-Exupéry and his translators altered its selection and organization for the American publication. It therefore occupies an ambiguous position between manifestation and distinct version of the obra.
- **1942:** *Pilote de guerre* / *Flight to Arras*. A testimonial narrative and wartime meditation based on Saint-Exupéry’s 23 May 1940 reconnaissance mission; French and English versions appeared in the United States in February 1942. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1942:** *Lettre à un otage*. An essay in epistolary form, addressed through the figure of Saint-Exupéry’s Jewish friend Léon Werth; begun as a preface to Werth’s *Trente-trois jours*, it became an autonomous public text. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1943:** *Le Petit Prince*. A Gesamtkunstwerk of tale, philosophical fable, handwriting-like typography, and authorial watercolors: the images do not merely decorate an independently complete verbal text. It first appeared in French and English in New York in April 1943; the French publication followed posthumously in 1946. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1936–1944:** *Citadelle*. An immense, unfinished project of parables, dialogues, meditations, and imagined pronouncements, which Saint-Exupéry himself treated as his prospective posthumous work. The 1948 book was constituted by editors from manuscript sheets found in a suitcase, so that its ordering and apparent completeness must not be mistaken for a final authorial form. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)

### Journalism and public testimony

- **26 October 1932:** *Pilote de ligne (1)*. An article defending the work and human value of Aéropostale’s pilots during the company’s financial and political crisis. [The succession’s Pléiade-based inventory of the fifteen *Marianne* articles.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **2 November 1932:** *Pilote de ligne (2)*. The continuation of the preceding article, using episodes from the Casablanca–Dakar route. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **30 November 1932:** *Escales en Patagonie*. A report-memoir about the extension of the South American airmail network toward Tierra del Fuego. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **14 December 1932:** *Princesses d’Agrigente*. A travel recollection later reworked into the “Oasis” chapter of *Terre des hommes*. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **25 January 1933:** *Mermoz*. A journalistic portrait of Jean Mermoz. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **17 May 1933:** *Bark, esclave maure*. A report or narrative portrait subsequently reworked in *Terre des hommes*. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **22 August 1933:** *À propos de Codos et Rossi*. An aviation article occasioned by Paul Codos and Maurice Rossi’s record flight. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **24 January 1934:** *La Fin de l’Émeraude*. A memorial account connected with the crash of the Air France airliner *Émeraude*. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **28 February 1934:** *Servitude et grandeur de l’aviation*. An article of aviation recollections, distinct from Saint-Exupéry’s similarly themed preface to Maurice Bourdet’s book. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **11 April 1934:** *Souvenirs de Mauritanie*. A memoir-reportage later reused in *Terre des hommes* and republished in *Air France Revue*. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **21 November 1934:** *Le 14e Salon de l’aviation*. An aviation report for *Marianne*. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **May 1935:** *Moscou*. A series of reports written for *Paris-Soir* around Pierre Laval’s visit to the Soviet Union; “Moscou” names a journalistic cycle rather than a book conceived as such. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **7 August 1935:** *Mermoz, pilote de ligne*. A portrait of Mermoz for *Marianne*. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **30 January–4 February 1936:** *Le Vol brisé, Prison de sable*. A six-installment newspaper récit—“Un avertissement du destin,” “Soudain un formidable craquement,” “La Soif,” “Le Délire,” “Le Supplice du troisième jour,” and “Résurrection”—about the failed Paris–Saigon raid; it was later transformed into *Terre des hommes*. [The succession documents all six installments and their dates.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/le-vol-brise-prison-de-sable-1936/)
- **August 1936:** *L’Espagne ensanglantée*. A five-article reportage cycle from Barcelona during the Spanish Civil War; the collective title describes a serial journalistic manifestation, not a separately completed book. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/reportages-sur-la-guerre-despagne-1936-1937/)
- **15 December 1936:** *Mermoz, le héros vaincu*. A memorial article published in *La Vie aérienne* after Mermoz’s disappearance. [Succession inventory of the Mermoz writings.](https://dev.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-consacres-a-mermoz-1933-1937/)
- **16 December 1936:** *À Jean Mermoz…*. A memorial article written while Saint-Exupéry still resisted accepting Mermoz’s death. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **16 January 1937:** *À Jean Mermoz*. A further memorial article, published in *Le Flambeau*; it should not be silently merged with the similarly titled *Marianne* piece. [Succession inventory of the Mermoz writings.](https://dev.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-consacres-a-mermoz-1933-1937/)
- **22 January 1937:** *Mermoz avait défriché les sables, la montagne, la nuit et la mer*. A memorial article in *L’Intransigeant*, later reused in *Terre des hommes*. [Succession inventory of the Mermoz writings.](https://dev.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-consacres-a-mermoz-1933-1937/)
- **May–June 1937:** *Madrid*. A Spanish Civil War reportage cycle for *Paris-Soir*, produced during Saint-Exupéry’s second reporting journey. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/reportages-sur-la-guerre-despagne-1936-1937/)
- **7 December 1937:** *Adieu à Mermoz*. A commemorative address delivered on the first anniversary of Mermoz’s disappearance: an oral public act as well as a text. [Succession inventory of the Mermoz writings.](https://dev.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-consacres-a-mermoz-1933-1937/)
- **before or during 1937:** *Tu es large comme une génération…*. An unfinished, unpublished draft tribute to Mermoz, known by its opening words rather than a fixed authorial title; it resists being called a finished work. [Succession inventory of the Mermoz writings.](https://dev.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-consacres-a-mermoz-1933-1937/)
- **October 1938:** *La Paix ou la guerre ?*. A three-article *Paris-Soir* cycle prompted by the Munich Agreement; it is political intervention and diagnosis rather than a retrospectively assembled “book.” [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/la-paix-ou-la-guerre-1938/)
- **1938:** *Aventures et escales*. An illustrated aviation contribution or publication project drawing on Saint-Exupéry’s experience as pilot and reporter; its collaborative magazine-like form makes “book” an inadequate category. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **16 August 1939:** *Le Pilote et les puissances naturelles*. An article for *Marianne* on the pilot’s encounter with natural forces. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **23 August 1939:** *Anne Lindbergh*. An article on Anne Morrow Lindbergh, adjacent to Saint-Exupéry’s preface to the French edition of her book but not identical with it. [*Marianne* inventory.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/ouvrage/articles-parus-dans-marianne-1932-1939/)
- **1939:** *Pilotes d’essai*. A contribution to Jean-Marie Conty’s special issue of *Document*; because the issue was collaborative, Saint-Exupéry made a contribution rather than the whole object. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1939–1944:** *Écrits de guerre*. Not one authorially designed work but a posthumously named corpus of letters, interviews, messages, memoranda, and radio addresses from the war years; the 1982 compilation is an editorial manifestation and should not be mistaken for a book Saint-Exupéry completed. [Saint-Exupéry succession description.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **29 November 1942:** *Lettre aux Français*. A radio address broadcast from New York, intended to act politically by calling for unity; it is closer to a public manifesto than to a descriptive essay and survives within the wartime corpus.
- **1943:** *Lettre au général X*. An unfinished or unsent meditation addressed to an unnamed general and published only after Saint-Exupéry’s death; the received title is editorial, and the addressee has been variously identified. It resists treatment as a finalized autonomous work.

### Prefaces and introductions

- **1932:** *Préface à Le Destin de Joseph-Marie Le Brix*. A prefatory essay for José Le Boucher’s biography of the aviator Le Brix; the obra is Saint-Exupéry’s preface, not the book it accompanies. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1933:** *Préface à Grandeur et servitude de l’aviation*. A prefatory essay for Maurice Bourdet’s book, setting out Saint-Exupéry’s understanding of aviation and human responsibility. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1939:** *Préface à Le Vent se lève*. Saint-Exupéry’s introduction to the French translation of Anne Morrow Lindbergh’s *Listen! The Wind*; it is a framing text embedded in another author’s book. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1942:** *Préface à La France que j’aime*. An introductory text for Helen Mackay’s book; authorship extends only to the prefatory component, not to the volume as a whole. [Saint-Exupéry succession bibliography.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)

### Screen works and unrealized cinema projects

- **1935–1936:** *Anne-Marie*. An original screenplay by Saint-Exupéry, adapted for production by André Lang and director Raymond Bernard; the released film is therefore a collaborative manifestation rather than Saint-Exupéry’s sole obra. [The surviving contract calls it his original scenario, while production credits identify the adapters.](https://www.gazette-drouot.com/lots/6563469-antoine-de-saint-exupery----)
- **1936–1937:** *Courrier Sud*. A screenplay and dialogue adapted by Saint-Exupéry from his novel, further shaped by Pierre Billon, Hans G. Lustig, and Robert Bresson before filming. The screenplay and the released film must be distinguished from both the 1929 novel and from one another. [The production contract assigns Saint-Exupéry scenario and dialogue.](https://www.gazette-drouot.com/lots/6563469-antoine-de-saint-exupery----)
- **1930s:** *Atlantique-Sud*. A film project to which Saint-Exupéry contributed, apparently in connection with Air France publicity; the surviving evidence supports collaboration or development work, not attribution of a completed sole-authored film. [Paule Bounin’s study of Saint-Exupéry’s cinematic work.](https://fr.readkong.com/page/l-oeuvre-cinematographique-de-saint-exupery-paule-bounin-1493505)

### Drawings, watercolors, and letters-drawings

- **childhood–1944:** *Drawings, watercolors, pastels, and pen-and-pencil sketches*. A lifelong visual corpus dispersed through notebooks, manuscripts, letters, books, and separate sheets; it cannot honestly be reduced to a finite list of consistently titled works, since many sheets are untitled, undated, or inseparable from writing. Delphine Lacroix and Alban Cerisier’s catalogue raisonné gathers the known corpus, while the BnF describes works originating from collections across France, Morocco, and New York. [BnF description of the catalogue raisonné.](https://salleovale.bnf.fr/fr/selections-thematiques/antoine-de-saint-exupery-dessins-aquarelles-pastels-plumes-et-crayons)
- **1942–1943:** *Watercolors for Le Petit Prince*. A sequence of authorial images made as structural parts of the tale, including drawings whose meaning depends on discrepancies between seeing, reading, and interpretation. Their proper obra is both the individual sheets and the illustrated book they collectively constitute. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1943:** *Lettres à l’inconnue*. A group of illustrated love letters to a young French Red Cross ambulance worker encountered in North Africa; writing and drawing form indivisible letters-drawings rather than a book. Their collective title and 2008 publication are posthumous editorial manifestations. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)

### Notebooks, correspondence, and posthumously constituted corpora

- **1923–1931:** *Lettres de jeunesse*. Letters chiefly to Renée de Saussine, later selected and constituted as a 1953 book; the individual letters are works of correspondence, whereas the collective title is editorial. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **throughout his life:** *Lettres à sa mère*. A lifelong correspondence with Marie de Saint-Exupéry, posthumously selected and published in 1954. It is a documentary and epistolary corpus rather than an authorially planned book, and published selections do not establish completeness. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1935–1940:** *Carnets*. One agenda and five surviving notebooks containing calculations, sketches, observations, drafts, and philosophical notes, often used concurrently. They are working objects and reservoirs for projects, not a finalized continuous work; *Carnets* is the posthumous title of their 1953 publication. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **1943:** *Correspondence with “the unknown woman”*. The individual letters and drawings later published as *Lettres à l’inconnue* survive as an incomplete private exchange; uncertainty about the recipient’s identity is part of the archive, not a gap to be filled. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/oeuvre-old/)
- **dates varying:** *Other correspondence*. Thousands of professional, familial, romantic, military, and literary letters survive in public and private collections. They are things Saint-Exupéry made, but neither the Pléiade correspondence nor later themed volumes establish a closed catalogue of every letter; treating each published selection as a separate authorial work would confuse editorial manifestations with the underlying documents. [The Pléiade volume explicitly organizes a correspondence corpus alongside personal writings.](https://media.electre-ng.com/extraits/extrait-id/80f50144d20db65b484bcebc12cde868d3a0c86a3aed24cb3263e1df1324b099.pdf)

### Inventions, protocols, and technical projects

- **c. 1908:** *Motorized flying bicycle*. A childhood technical project drawn and built with a village carpenter but never made to fly; its project-status—not successful operation—is the work. [Saint-Exupéry succession account of his scientific projects.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/personne/les-techniques-scientifiques/)
- **c. 1908:** *Motorized irrigation system*. A childhood machine improvised from the flying bicycle’s engine; it reportedly exploded and injured his brother François. It is known as an episode and prototype, not through a stable authorial title. [Saint-Exupéry succession account.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/personne/les-techniques-scientifiques/)
- **1934–1941:** *Aeronautical patent corpus*. Eleven French patent applications, four additions, one application filed under the pseudonym “Max Ras,” and one United States patent concerning such problems as blind landing and aircraft navigation. Each document is both protocol and project—an instruction for a possible apparatus rather than evidence that the apparatus was manufactured—and the succession warns that none produced income. [Saint-Exupéry succession inventory of the patents.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/personne/les-brevets-dinventions/)
- **1934:** *System for landing aircraft without visibility*. Saint-Exupéry’s first recorded patent project, devising technical assistance for blind landing; the cited source supplies its subject but not a fixed short authorial title, so none is invented here. [Saint-Exupéry succession patent history.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/personne/les-brevets-dinventions/)
- **1939–1940:** *Anti-freezing proposal for aircraft machine guns*. A wartime technical protocol proposing a methyl-glycol solution to prevent weapons from freezing at high altitude. The succession records the proposal and Saint-Exupéry’s campaign for its adoption, but it is not presented as one of the formally titled literary works. [Saint-Exupéry succession patent history.](https://www.antoinedesaintexupery.org/personne/les-brevets-dinventions/)
