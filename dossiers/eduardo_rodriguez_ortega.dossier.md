# Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega: Research Dossier

## Scope, Identification, and Evidentiary Note

This dossier identifies the subject as **Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega**, the Argentine video-game programmer, designer, producer, educator, and industry organizer associated with Sabarasa Entertainment, QB9, the Asociación de Desarrolladores de Videojuegos Argentinos (ADVA), and Eclipse Entretainment S.R.L.

The requested form **“Eduardo S. Rodríguez Ortega”** could not be corroborated. The subject’s own professional curriculum vitae, ADVA’s institutional history, Argentine corporate records, conference programs, game credits, and professional profiles consistently identify him as **Eduardo Rodríguez Ortega**, without the middle initial “S.” The meaning or source of the initial therefore remains unresolved.

The exact name also belongs to several unrelated people, including a Cuban film still photographer born in Camagüey in 1965, a Spanish technology executive, and professionals in Mexico. Those records have not been merged into this biography.

Research was conducted through 3 September 2026. The surviving public record is overwhelmingly professional. It supports a detailed career history but provides very little reliable information about childhood, ancestry, private family life, health, religion, personal wealth, or intimate relationships.

### Evidentiary labels

- **Documented fact:** established by an official record, institutional document, published game credit, or directly attributable primary source.
- **Reported fact:** stated by a reputable institutional or journalistic source but not independently confirmed by an underlying record.
- **Self-reported claim:** found in Rodríguez Ortega’s CV or professional profile.

This work overlapped chronologically with his later employment at Sabarasa and, according to his CV, continued until 2007.

## 2002–2005: Sabarasa Entertainment and *Mazes of Fate*

Rodríguez Ortega worked as a **game developer/programmer at Sabarasa Entertainment** from 2002 to 2005.

His principal surviving credit from this period is *Mazes of Fate*, a first-person role-playing game for Nintendo’s Game Boy Advance. Published credits list him among the programmers with **Santiago Segade, Nicolás Vinacur, and Lucas Wall**. The game was directed by **Javier F. Otaegui**.

Development began around March 2003. The Game Boy Advance edition was released in North America in December 2006, after Rodríguez Ortega’s stated Sabarasa tenure had ended. A Nintendo DS edition followed in 2008 and preserved him in the credits as a member of the original team.

His CV also reports development of an unidentified 3-D chess game for **Cardoza Entertainment**. No title or release record was established.

### Was he a founder of Sabarasa?

A 2019 event biography calls him “one of the founders” of Sabarasa. His own CV merely calls him a game developer there, while available *Mazes of Fate* materials identify Javier Otaegui as Sabarasa’s director. No incorporation record or contemporaneous founding document naming Rodríguez Ortega was located.

His status as a Sabarasa founder is therefore a **reported/promotional claim, not securely documented**.

## 2003–2004: Founding ADVA

ADVA’s historical membership page lists Rodríguez Ortega among the organization’s founders. His own CV dates the founding to **2004**, while ADVA labels its first provisional board **2003–2004**.

He was not listed as an officer on that first provisional board, but later served in elected or oversight roles.

ADVA became a central professional network in his later career: industry advocacy, conferences, judging, mentoring, and training recur throughout his public record.

## 2005–2006: Move to QB9

Rodríguez Ortega joined **QB9 Entertainment** around 2005. The Inter-American Development Bank’s survey of Latin American game studios lists **Juan Pablo Cornejo, Eduardo Rodriguez, and Ariel Nader** as QB9’s founders.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

## “The first South American/Latin American console game”

### Story

*Mazes of Fate* has repeatedly been called:

- the first South American game developed for a console;
- the first Latin American portable-console game;
- the first Latin American game for a Nintendo console.

### Appearance and circulation

The claim appears in:

- Rodríguez Ortega’s CV;
- Sabarasa promotional interviews;
- contemporary game journalism;
- later biographies and retrospectives.

### Evidence

The game was unquestionably developed in Argentina and commercially released for a Nintendo handheld. Contemporary reporting in 2006–2007 repeated the Nintendo/Latin America “first” claim.

### Qualification

The formulations are not identical. “First console game,” “first portable-console game,” and “first Nintendo-console game” are different claims. No exhaustive census of every earlier Latin American console-development project was located.

### Reputation effect

The “first” narrative became a central origin story both for Rodríguez Ortega’s professional biography and for histories of Argentina’s modern game industry. The safest formulation is that *Mazes of Fate* was **widely reported contemporaneously as the first Latin American-developed game for a Nintendo platform**, while broader versions remain insufficiently audited.

## Founder narratives

Rodríguez Ortega is variously described as:

- a founder of ADVA;
- a founder of QB9;
- a founder of Sabarasa;
- a co-founder of Eclipse.

The evidence differs:

- **ADVA:** documented by ADVA’s own historical list.

- **Sabarasa:** appears in a later event biography but not in his CV or the consulted contemporaneous project credits; unresolved.

The accumulation of founder labels contributes to a public image of Rodríguez Ortega as a serial institution-builder, but each claim should be evaluated separately.
