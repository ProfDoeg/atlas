## Works by Joshua Norman Haldeman

### Political manifestos, arguments, and legislative texts

- **13 September 1934:** *Social Credit*. A letter published in the “Open Forum” of *The Western Producer*, arguing for social credit as a solution to failures of economic distribution. The archival chronology treats the signed letter as Haldeman’s earliest identified publication. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 2–3](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **26 June 1940:** *Statement of Patriotism by Those Who Were Technocrats*. A newspaper declaration protesting Canada’s prohibition of Technocracy Inc.; it functioned as a public manifesto and generated three charges against Haldeman. The surviving evidence establishes that he signed and promoted it, but does not establish that he alone composed it. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 4–5](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1941–1943:** *Total War & Defence*. Both an organization directed by Haldeman and a newsletter expressing its program of voluntary wartime research. Because organization, research project, and serial publication shared the name, it resists classification as a single work; surviving accounts also do not identify every issue or establish that Haldeman personally wrote every text. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 5, 27](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1943:** *Saskatchewan Chiropractic Act*. A legislative protocol regulating chiropractic practice. The scholarly biography and the underlying Rehm notes credit Haldeman with drawing up its wording, but the enacted statute was necessarily a collective institutional product, so sole authorship should not be inferred. [Keating and Haldeman, “Joshua N Haldeman, DC: the Canadian Years, 1926–1950”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2485067/); [archival chronology, pp. 5, 27](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **21 December 1946:** *Dr. Haldeman’s Reply*. A signed political reply published in the Saskatoon *Star-Phoenix*. The bibliographic record confirms its title and date, but the available scholarly chronology does not supply enough information to characterize its full argument safely. [Contemporary-paper citation reproduced in the biographical references](https://2.zoppoz.workers.dev/https/pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_Norman_Haldeman)

- **May 1960:** *The International Conspiracy to Establish a World Dictatorship and the Menace to South Africa*. A 42-page, self-published political tract: simultaneously a manifesto for white-minority rule and an antisemitic, anti-communist conspiracy text produced immediately after the Sharpeville massacre. The University of the Free State’s pamphlet inventory dates it May 1960, while the imprint itself is recorded as undated; therefore “May 1960” describes the work’s stated date rather than an unambiguous publication date. [University of the Free State pamphlet inventory](https://scholar.ufs.ac.za/bitstreams/9b218841-6f23-4564-ac5e-9093ff7b5d1a/download); [description of the original Pretoria edition](https://antiquarianauctions.com/lots/the-international-conspiracy-to-establish-a-world-dictatorship-and-the-menace-to-south-africa-1960)

- **Undated; apparently later than 1960:** *The International Conspiracy in Health*. A self-published polemical pamphlet attacking fluoridation, vaccination, health insurance, and state or professional control of health care. Some secondary notices assign it to the 1950s and others to 1969; no dependable dated original has been identified, so its date remains unresolved. [Extant-pamphlet description](https://ultimatumeditions.com/bookstore/the-international-conspiracy-in-health/); [discussion of its subjects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_N._Haldeman)

### Chiropractic communications and protocols

- **1938:** *News Concerning My Trip This Summer*. A one-page circular to Haldeman’s patients describing his attendance at chiropractic conventions and postgraduate courses and converting that travel into a professional advertisement. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 3–4](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1942:** Untitled weekly CKCK chiropractic broadcasts. A recurring body of spoken work transmitted Wednesdays at 4:15 p.m.; the programs’ individual titles, scripts, and completeness are not known, so this is better understood as a broadcast practice than a recoverable set of discrete texts. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, p. 5](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **April 1950:** *Chiropractic: The Safest, Quickest, Surest Way to Health*. A self-published patient pamphlet combining professional promotion, autobiographical material, credentials, photographs, and health claims. [Keating and Haldeman, bibliography and discussion](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/26103300_Joshua_N_Haldeman_DC_the_Canadian_Years_1926-1950); [archival chronology, p. 16](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **May 1951:** *Chiropractic in South Africa: Former ICA Board Member Reports Excellent Opportunities for Chiropractors*. A letter dated 6 March 1951 and published under an editorial title in the *ICA Review*; it is at once professional report, recruitment appeal, and account of Haldeman’s new practice. Because the heading appears editorial, it should not automatically be treated as Haldeman’s own title. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 17–18](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1953 onward:** *The Haldeman Bulletin*. A clinic bulletin or newsletter reporting staff appointments, new chiropractic offices, and clinic events. At least one issue existed by May 1953, but no complete run or stable publication dates are documented; it therefore resists enumeration as a set of individually titled works. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, p. 18](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **7 May 1957:** Untitled letter to the *Canadian Chiropractic Journal*. A professional letter congratulating the journal and recalling Haldeman’s role in founding the Canadian Chiropractic Association and Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College; it appeared in volume 1, number 3, pages 16–18. [Haldeman chronology](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/HaldemanJoshua-chrono.pdf)

### Journey narratives and travel books

- **6 August 1951:** *A New Life for Haldeman*. A dispatch to Regina’s *Leader-Post* describing the family’s move to Pretoria and defending apartheid in explicitly racist terms. It is a migration narrative, but also political advocacy presented through the form of a personal travel report. [CBC account citing the original newspaper article](https://bankwatch.ca/2025/03/21/the-canadian-roots-of-elon-musk-conspiracist-grandpa/); [contemporary citation and summary](https://nmafpublic.s3.ca-central-1.amazonaws.com/entries/2026/01/entry_8966/25_08_TorontoLife_7692d471-90a4-4adf-ac3c-0d45ce68ee47.pdf)

- **September 1952:** *Flying…Two Continents*. An illustrated article in *ICA Review*, volume 7, number 3, recounting Haldeman and Winnifred Haldeman’s approximately 25,000-mile flight through Africa and Europe. Although it records a journey, its venue and professional audience also make it chiropractic-association journalism. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 17–18, 22](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **Undated, probably mid-1950s:** *The Flying Haldemans: “Pity the Poor Private Pilot”*. A self-published book co-authored with Winnifred Haldeman about their private flying. The standard scholarly bibliography explicitly calls it undated, so later descriptions placing it generally in the mid-1950s should not be converted into a precise publication year. [Keating and Haldeman bibliography, p. 22](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

### Journey-projects and performed works

- **1952:** Flight around Africa and through Europe. A roughly 25,000-mile aerial journey reaching Scotland and Norway, made with Winnifred Haldeman and subsequently manifested in *Flying…Two Continents*. The flight itself was a made itinerary and performed project, not merely the subject of a text. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 17–18](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1953:** Central African aerial expedition. An approximately 8,000-mile journey undertaken by Joshua and Winnifred Haldeman with their son Scott; no fixed title or independent narrative is documented. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, p. 18](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1954:** South Africa–Australia–South Africa flight. A roughly 30,000-mile journey in a single-engine aircraft, routed up Africa and across Asia before circling Australia and returning. Claims that it was the first or longest such private flight are qualified in the source as matters of contemporary belief rather than formally verified records. [Keating and Haldeman chronology](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/HaldemanJoshua-chrono.pdf)

- **1953–1960s:** Expeditions in search of the Lost City of the Kalahari. A body of twelve family expeditions by air and ground: an unfinished research-and-exploration project whose intended object was never found and whose individual journeys are not fully titled or dated. It resists being called a conventional “work,” but its repeated itinerary-making, field searches, and later incorporation into books by F. Goldie and A. J. Clement make it a consequential project. [Keating and Haldeman, account of the twelve expeditions](https://www.chiropractic.ca/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/JCCA-Sept-1995-Vol-39-No-3-PP-172-186.pdf)

- **1956:** Algiers–Cape Town motor rally journey. A competitive trans-African performance in which Haldeman drove a Ford Ranch-Wagon and tied for first place in the third Algiers–Cape Town Rally. It was an enacted journey rather than an authored text, and the surviving scholarly bibliography does not attach a separate title of his making to it. [Biographical summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_N._Haldeman)

### Organizations, institutions, and collective projects

- **1931–1934:** Research and Open Forum Debating Society. A political-economy research and public-debate project directed by Haldeman. The record does not establish that he founded it or preserve a catalogue of its discussions, so it should not be treated as a wholly authored œuvre. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 2, 27](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1941–1943:** Total War and Defence organization. Haldeman organized and directed this voluntary research body of approximately sixty-five participants to investigate aspects of Canada’s war effort. It was a collective political project rather than a discrete authored work and overlapped with the newsletter of the same name. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, pp. 5, 27](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1944–1950:** Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College. Haldeman was an original sponsor and founding-board participant, not its sole creator. His contribution belongs to a collective institution-building project and should not be inflated into authorship of the college itself. [Keating and Haldeman](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2485067/); [CMCC chronology](https://www.chiro.org/Plus/History/Colleges/CanadianMemorialCC/CMCC_chrono.pdf)

- **1950:** Pretoria Pistol Club. Haldeman helped form the club upon arriving in South Africa. This is an organizational creation, but the source does not assign him sole founding authorship or describe a surviving charter. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, p. 17](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **1950s–1960s:** Haldeman Chiropractic Clinic, Pretoria. A large clinical institution developed by Haldeman, eventually occupying more than 4,000 square feet and reportedly seeing as many as 600 patients weekly. “Clinic” here denotes a practice, staff, premises, and public program rather than a single architectural work, and no architect or construction attribution is supplied. [Keating and Haldeman chronology, p. 27](https://chiro.org/Plus/History/Persons/Haldeman/Haldeman_Chronology.PDF)

- **During his 1959–1969 SACA presidency:** South African Chiropractic Research Foundation. A collective research project formed under the South African Chiropractors Association and briefly operating a small animal laboratory. The scholarly account treats it as an institutional achievement of Haldeman’s presidency, not as his sole creation. [Keating and Haldeman](https://www.chiropractic.ca/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/JCCA-Sept-1995-Vol-39-No-3-PP-172-186.pdf)
