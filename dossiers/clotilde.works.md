## Works by Sainte Clotilde

### Joint architectural obra

- **c. 507–511:** *Basilica of the Holy Apostles, Paris*. A votive church and royal funerary complex made jointly with Clovis; a seventh-century Parisian tradition assigns the initial project to Clotilde, while construction began only in Clovis’s final years and was apparently completed after his death. Later called Sainte-Geneviève, it cannot securely be treated as Clotilde’s independent design. ([Kurth, pp. 32–34](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf); [Bibliothèque Sainte-Geneviève](https://www.bsg.univ-paris3.fr/iguana/uploads/file/History%20and%20buildings.pdf))

### Monastic and ecclesiastical foundations

- **c. 520:** *Saint-Georges community and oratory at Chelles*. A small religious community—probably female—with an oratory dedicated to Saint George, founded on a royal estate. The attribution comes from the later *Vita Balthildis*, but archaeological and documentary scholarship accepts an early Clotildian establishment while distinguishing it from the much larger double monastery created or rebuilt by Queen Balthild in the seventh century. ([Cudorge, pp. 85–87](https://bdr.parisnanterre.fr/theses/internet/2017/2017PA100144/2017PA100144.pdf); [IRHT](https://bmf.irht.cnrs.fr/37319))

- **First half of the sixth century; traditional attribution:** *Notre-Dame des Andelys*. A women’s monastery and sanctuary attributed to Clotilde by her tenth-century *Vita*; the accompanying story of a miraculous spring is hagiography, not construction evidence. Bede independently confirms the later monastery’s prominence, but not Clotilde’s authorship, so this remains a traditional rather than securely contemporary attribution. ([Kurth, pp. 49–50](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

- **Undated; traditional attribution:** *Women’s monastery at Saint-Pierre-le-Puellier, Tours*. A monastic foundation assigned to Clotilde by local tradition and later scholars, without a surviving contemporary foundation record. It therefore resists classification as an authenticated obra and is better treated as a remembered project. ([Kurth, pp. 48–49](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

- **Undated; late attribution:** *Church of Saint-Pierre and community of canons outside Laon*. Clotilde’s tenth-century biographer credits her with founding this church and installing a collegiate community. No contemporary evidence cited by the standard biography confirms the claim. ([Kurth, p. 49](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

### Reconstruction and enrichment

- **Early sixth century:** *Basilica of Saint-Germain at Auxerre*. A reconstruction of the modest funerary oratory of Saint Germanus as a basilica approximately fifty metres long, together with its decoration and endowment. The evidence consists of a ninth-century miracle collection and the recorded epitaph of Bishop Lupus; the building’s Clotildian phase is archaeologically recognized, although the narrative sources are later. ([French Ministry of Culture](https://archeologie.culture.gouv.fr/auxerre/fr/la-basilique-de-clotilde); [Kurth, p. 48](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

- **Undated; substantially altered tradition:** *Enlargement and enrichment of Saint-Pierre at Reims*. A patronage project said by the tenth-century *Vita Chrothildis* to commemorate Clovis’s baptism. Even the standard biographer warns that the surviving tradition has been notably altered, so the scope—and possibly Clotilde’s responsibility—cannot be recovered confidently. ([Kurth, p. 49](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

- **Undated; substantially altered tradition:** *Rebuilding of Saint-Pierre/Holy Apostles outside Rouen*. A reconstruction attributed only by Clotilde’s tenth-century biography; the account connects it with an older sanctuary supposedly consecrated by Saint Denis. Its historical core may concern royal patronage, but neither the date nor the actual work can be securely established. ([Kurth, p. 49](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

- **Undated; local tradition:** *Saint-Martin at Auch*. An abbey traditionally said to have been built by Clovis at Clotilde’s request. This is therefore best classified as her project or intervention rather than a building made independently by her; the attribution rests on local memory, not a surviving sixth-century act. ([Kurth, p. 32](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

### Documentary and material acts

- **Before 545; document lost:** *Grant of land to the priest Anastasius of Clermont*. A landed donation embodied in a royal diploma issued by Clotilde; neither the deed’s date, wording, nor property description survives. Gregory of Tours preserves indirect evidence of the act through the later dispute over Anastasius’s title, making this a lost legal instrument rather than a surviving text. ([Gregory of Tours, *Histories* IV.12, discussed by Kurth, p. 50](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

- **During her widowhood, 511–545:** *Endowments to churches, monasteries, and holy places*. This is a dispersed body of material patronage rather than one titled work: Gregory of Tours states that Clotilde transferred estates wherever religious institutions were in need. Except for the Anastasius grant, the individual acts and diplomas are lost, so completeness is impossible and invented titles would misrepresent the evidence. ([Gregory of Tours, *Histories* III.18](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/basis/gregory-hist.asp))

- **During her residence at Tours, after 511:** *Adornment and enrichment of Saint-Martin’s shrine*. A continuing patronage practice directed toward the tomb and basilica of Saint Martin, not a separately titled building or text. Its boundaries and constituent gifts are unrecorded, so it resists treatment as a single finished work. ([Kurth, pp. 50–51](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))

### Religious-political project

- **Late fifth–early sixth century:** *Catholic formation of the Frankish royal household*. A sustained project manifested in the baptism of Clotilde’s sons, her exhortations to Clovis, and her influence on his adoption of Catholic Christianity. Clovis’s conversion was not a thing she made alone, and Gregory’s providential narrative cannot establish every detail, but it records intentional action rather than mere association. ([Gregory of Tours, *Histories* II.29–31](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/gregory-clovisconv.asp); [Kurth, pp. 17–18](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/JKL/Kurth/Clotilde.pdf))
