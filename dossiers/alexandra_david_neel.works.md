## Works by Alexandra David-Néel

### Political, feminist, and philosophical interventions

- **1895–1907:** *Pour la vie et autres textes libertaires*. An anarchist and individualist body of articles and pamphlets rather than a single contemporaneous book; *Pour la vie* itself is variously dated 1898 and 1901, while the larger corpus was constituted editorially in 1998. [1][2]
- **1898–1900:** *Articles pour La Fronde*. Feminist journalism later gathered under the title *Le Féminisme rationnel*; the collection is a posthumous editorial manifestation, not a book conceived in that form by David-Néel. [1][2]
- **1907:** *Le Philosophe Meh-Ti et l’idée de solidarité*. A philosophical study and presentation of Mozi, made partly through existing translations and with the assistance of an unnamed Chinese collaborator. [3][4]
- **1911:** *Le Modernisme bouddhique et le bouddhisme du Bouddha*. A comparative doctrinal study; later editions extracted and republished *Le Bouddhisme du Bouddha*, so the shorter title should not automatically be counted as a wholly separate original work. [1][4]
- **1939:** *Le Bouddhisme, ses doctrines et ses méthodes*. A synthetic exposition of Buddhist doctrine and practice. [1]
- **1951:** *Les Enseignements secrets dans les sectes bouddhistes tibétaines: la vue pénétrante*. A doctrinal exposition based on Tibetan oral teaching; its 1961 revised edition is a later state of the same work. [1][4]
- **1958:** *La Connaissance transcendante, d’après le texte et les commentaires tibétains*. A translation-commentary made jointly with Lama Yongden from the *Prajñāpāramitā* tradition; its collaborative authorship should be retained. [4][5]
- **1961:** *Immortalité et réincarnation: doctrines et pratiques, Chine, Tibet, Inde*. A comparative study of doctrines concerning survival, rebirth, and immortality. [1][4]
- **1964:** *Quarante siècles d’expansion chinoise*. A large-scale historical and geopolitical essay. [1][4]

### Travel narratives and works of cultural observation

- **1927:** *Voyage d’une Parisienne à Lhassa: à pied et en mendiant, de la Chine à l’Inde à travers le Thibet*. A first-person journey narrative incorporating photographs and a map; it transforms the 1921–1924 expedition into a deliberately shaped literary obra rather than serving as a raw itinerary. [1][4]
- **1929:** *Mystiques et magiciens du Tibet*. A hybrid of travel narrative, ethnographic observation, religious exposition, and accounts of extraordinary practices. [1][4]
- **1933:** *Au pays des brigands-gentilshommes* (first issued as *Grand Tibet: au pays des brigands-gentilshommes*). A travel and adventure narrative; the shorter title is the continuing identity of the work, not a separate creation. [1][4]
- **1940:** *Sous des nuées d’orage*. A narrative of travel through China during the Sino-Japanese War. Some later covers incorrectly give the singular *Sous une nuée d’orage*, an edition-level error rather than an alternative authorial title. [1]
- **1947:** *À l’Ouest barbare de la vaste Chine*. A travel narrative and description of western China. [1][4]
- **1949:** *Au cœur des Himalayas: le Népal*. A retrospective account of Nepal combining personal travel, geography, customs, and religion. [1][4]
- **1951:** *L’Inde, hier, aujourd’hui, demain*. A cultural and political study of India before and after independence. Its expanded 1969 recasting, *L’Inde où j’ai vécu: avant et après l’indépendance*, is a revised manifestation rather than an entirely unrelated work. [1][4]
- **1953:** *Le Vieux Tibet face à la Chine nouvelle*. A work of travel observation and political interpretation addressing Tibet’s encounter with Communist China. [1][4]
- **1972:** *Le Sortilège du mystère: faits étranges et gens bizarres rencontrés au long de mes routes d’Orient et d’Occident*. A posthumously published collection of autobiographical and anomalous encounters; because its final editorial formation occurred after David-Néel’s death, it resists treatment as an entirely author-fixed book. [1][4]
- **1975:** *Vivre au Tibet: cuisine, traditions et images*. A posthumous illustrated work of everyday-life observation and practical description; its assembly as a volume was editorial. [1]
- **1994:** *Grand Tibet et vaste Chine: récits et aventures*. A posthumous collected-work manifestation comprising five earlier travel books and selected documents, arranged by Marie-Madeleine Peyronnet; it is not a newly authored journey narrative. [6]

### Fiction and lyric theatre

- **1897–1900:** *Lidia*. A one-act lyric tragedy whose libretto David-Néel wrote under her stage identity Alexandra Myrial, with music by guitarist Jean Haustont. Its performance and publication history is obscure, and the surviving evidence does not justify a more exact date. [7]
- **1902:** *Le Grand Art*. A completed autobiographical novel about the operatic world, left unpublished during David-Néel’s lifetime and first issued in 2018; the publication is posthumous, but the manuscript is an early, finished obra rather than an editor-created compilation. [8][9]
- **1931:** *La Vie surhumaine de Guésar de Ling, le héros thibétain, racontée par les bardes de son pays*. A literary retelling of the Tibetan Gesar epic made with Lama Yongden; it mediates an oral and manuscript tradition and therefore is neither simply an authored novel nor a literal transcription. [1][4]
- **1935:** *Le Lama aux cinq sagesses*. A “Tibetan novel” jointly credited to Lama Yongden and David-Néel; accounts of their working practice do not permit David-Néel to be treated silently as sole author. [5][10]
- **1938:** *Magie d’amour et magie noire: scènes du Tibet inconnu*. A novelistic sequence in which ethnographic knowledge, the marvelous, and Tibetan storytelling conventions are deliberately interwoven. [1][4]
- **1954:** *La Puissance du néant*. A Tibetan novel attributed on the original publication to Lama Yongden, with David-Néel identified as translator and annotator; later cataloguing sometimes treats it as jointly authored, so its attribution remains unstable. [10][11]

### Translations, adaptations, and scholarly mediations

- **1923:** *L’Iliade thibétaine et ses bardes*. A study of the Gesar epic that precedes the fuller 1931 retelling; it is related to, but not merely an edition of, *La Vie surhumaine de Guésar de Ling*. [4]
- **1927:** *L’Entraînement psychique chez les Thibétains*. The printed manifestation of a lecture delivered to the Institut général psychologique; it is a lecture-text rather than a conventional monograph. [4]
- **1951:** *Astavakra Gîtâ: discours sur le Vedânta advaita*. A French translation and interpretive presentation of the Sanskrit philosophical poem, not an original poem by David-Néel. [1]
- **1952:** *Textes tibétains inédits*. An anthology of translated Tibetan writings, including poems attributed to the sixth Dalai Lama; its creative act lies in selection, translation, and mediation rather than primary authorship. [1][4]
- **1958:** *Avadhuta Gîtâ de Dattatreya: poème mystique Vedânta advaita*. A translation and presentation of a Sanskrit Advaita text; it was later combined editorially with the *Astavakra Gîtâ*. [1]
- **1970:** *En Chine: l’amour universel et l’individualisme intégral—les maîtres Mo-Tsé et Yang-Tchou*. A posthumous philosophical study bringing together David-Néel’s work on two Chinese thinkers; its publication date is not evidence of when every component was composed. [1][3]
- **Publication date not securely established:** *Grammaire de la langue tibétaine parlée*. A posthumously issued linguistic manuscript prepared for publication by Marie-Madeleine Peyronnet; the surviving bibliographies do not consistently establish either its completion date or the degree of editorial intervention. [1]

### Correspondence, notebooks, and editorially constituted texts

- **1904–1941:** *Correspondance avec Philippe Néel*. Thousands of letters to her husband forming an epistolary corpus rather than a book project. Selections appeared as *Journal de voyage* in 1975–1976 and an “édition intégrale” in 2000, but both titles and divisions are posthumous editorial manifestations. [1][4][12]
- **1928–1929:** *Correspondance relative au projet de voyage en Asie centrale*. Thirteen surviving archival pieces concerning a proposed expedition; they document a project, including intention and negotiation, rather than constituting a literary work in the ordinary sense. [13]
- **Dates various:** *Carnets personnels*. Working notebooks containing observations, reflections, drafts, and records. They resist identification as one finished obra: extracts were later recomposed with correspondence and unpublished writings in *La Lampe de sagesse*. [14]
- **1986:** *La Lampe de sagesse*. A posthumous anthology assembled from notebooks, correspondence, and mostly unpublished fragments; the words are David-Néel’s, but the titled book as a whole is an editorial creation. Some catalogues date its first edition 1986, while the BnF describes a 1991 edition. [14][15]
- **2021:** *Voyages et aventures de l’esprit: textes et documents inédits*. A posthumous editorial collection drawn from papers found in David-Néel’s house; it is a new manifestation of pre-existing texts, not a book whose final architecture she authorized. [16]
- **2021:** *Milarepa, le yogi-poète tibétain*. A posthumously published study and presentation of Milarepa, edited with an introduction and notes by Françoise Bonardel; its manuscript history and degree of authorial completion are not fully disclosed in the bibliographical notice. [1]

### Photographic and archival manifestations

- **1910s–1940s:** *Asian travel photographs*. David-Néel made and collected photographic records during her journeys, later using some as book illustrations. The archive does not support treating every print as an individually titled artwork, and maker, sitter, collector, and printer are not always separable roles. [9][17]
- **1926:** *Six photographs of Tibet donated to the Société de géographie*. A documented surviving group of photographic objects rather than a titled photographic suite; the catalogue identifies David-Néel as donor but does not securely establish that she exposed every negative herself. [4]
- **Dates various:** *Manuscripts and working versions*. The Maison Alexandra David-Néel reports thirty-seven original manuscripts and sixty-four working versions in the bequest. This is an archival body of process—drafts, variants, and unfinished states—not sixty-four additional works that can safely be given invented titles. [9]

### Prefatory and paratextual works

- **1949:** *Preface to Léa Lafugie, Au Tibet*. A commissioned paratext attached to another traveller’s book, not a book by David-Néel. [1]
- **1954:** *Preface to André Migot, Caravane vers Bouddha: un Français à travers la Haute-Asie mystique*. A paratext that frames another author’s journey and spiritual interpretation. [1]
- **1960:** *Notes to Essais sur le bouddhisme en général et sur le zen en particulier*. Scholarly annotations made with Paul Masson-Oursel; these are contributory texts rather than a freestanding obra. [15]
