## Works by Paulo Reglus Neves Freire

### Theses, reports, and instructional protocols

- **1959:** *Educação e atualidade brasileira*. Freire’s habilitation thesis for the chair of History and Philosophy of Education at the University of Recife; it is an academic project rather than an ordinary trade book and supplied the basis for *Educação como prática da liberdade*. [1]
- **1961:** *A propósito de uma administração*. An administrative report on João Alfredo Gonçalves da Costa Lima’s rectorship of the University of Recife, interwoven with Freire’s argument for a democratic university. [1]
- **1961:** *Livros de exercícios, nos. 1–3*. Three instructional workbooks for literacy practice; the standard bibliography supplies neither a named publisher nor individual subtitles, so their identities as separate works remain indistinct. [1]
- **1961:** *Primeiro livro do monitor*. A protocol for the literacy monitor or culture-circle coordinator, issued without a named publisher. [1]
- **1963:** *Alfabetização e conscientização*. A literacy manual or programmatic booklet joining the acquisition of written language to critical consciousness. [1]
- **1978:** *Cartas aos animadores e às animadoras culturais*. Instructional letters prepared for cultural workers in São Tomé and Príncipe; they resist classification as a conventional book because they originated as working communications within a literacy project. [1]
- **1980:** *Quatro cartas aos animadores de círculos de cultura de São Tomé e Príncipe*. A further epistolary protocol for culture-circle practice, subsequently circulated as a small publication. [1]

### Individually authored books and book-length obras

- **1967:** *Educação como prática da liberdade*. A book-length theoretical statement developed from the 1959 thesis, arguing for dialogical education amid Brazil’s democratic and authoritarian transitions. [1][2]
- **1968–1970:** *Pedagogia do oprimido*. A philosophical-pedagogical obra written in Portuguese in Chile in 1968, first published in Spanish and English in 1970 and only later in Brazil; publication dates therefore vary when editions are mistaken for the work’s date. [1][2][3]
- **1969:** *Extensión o comunicación? La concientización en el medio rural*. A polemical essay first issued in Spanish in Chile and later in Portuguese as *Extensão ou comunicação?*; it rejects agricultural “extension” as transmission and proposes communication as reciprocal knowledge-making. [1]
- **1969:** *Educación para la concientización*. A Chilean book or booklet assembling Freire’s account of conscientization; its overlap with other early collections makes it resistant to treatment as an entirely independent textual composition. [1]
- **1970:** *Cultural Action for Freedom*. A monograph made from essays written in exile; later Portuguese collections rearranged and supplemented this material, so it should not be conflated with every edition titled *Ação cultural para a liberdade*. [1][2]
- **1970:** *Cambio*. A Spanish-language compilation of previously dispersed articles together with material on cultural action; as an editorial assemblage, its unity is bibliographical rather than that of a newly written continuous book. [1]
- **1971:** *Conscientisation: recherche de Paulo Freire*. A French assemblage prepared with the INODEP team from Freire’s texts; later Spanish and Portuguese versions appeared under several titles, including *Conscientização: teoria e prática da libertação*. Its status is partly Freirean obra and partly editorial manifestation. [1]
- **1975:** *Ação cultural para a liberdade e outros escritos*. A Portuguese collection of essays and interventions rather than a single newly composed treatise; it incorporates texts whose earlier manifestations had separate lives. [1][2]
- **1977:** *Cartas à Guiné-Bissau: registros de uma experiência em processo*. An epistolary account made during an unfinished educational experiment; its letters document and actively guide the project rather than merely describing it. [1][2]
- **1978:** *Os cristãos e a libertação dos oprimidos*. A short theological-political intervention addressed to Christian participation in liberation; some bibliographies treat it as a booklet and others as an essay. [1]
- **1979:** *Educação e mudança*. A compact collection of Freirean texts on education and social transformation; its familiar Portuguese form is an editorially gathered book, not a wholly new continuous manuscript. [1]
- **1981:** *Ação cultural para a liberdade*. A reconfigured publication of earlier cultural-action writings; it is best understood as another manifestation of that textual body, not a separate intellectual project merely because its contents and title vary by edition. [1]
- **1982:** *A importância do ato de ler: em três artigos que se completam*. A deliberately integrated triptych of essays, making one book from three related interventions. [1][2]
- **1985:** *The Politics of Education: Culture, Power, and Liberation*. An English-language collection selected and translated by Donaldo Macedo, with an introduction by Henry Giroux; it is an editorial constellation of Freire’s essays rather than an independently composed English monograph. [1][2]
- **1991:** *A educação na cidade*. A “spoken book” assembled principally from interviews concerning Freire’s work as São Paulo’s municipal education secretary; it is simultaneously testimony, policy account, and dialogic obra. [1]
- **1992:** *Pedagogia da esperança: um reencontro com a Pedagogia do oprimido*. A reflective sequel and critical return to *Pedagogia do oprimido*, with notes by Ana Maria Araújo Freire. [1][4]
- **1993:** *Política e educação*. A thematically organized collection of essays and talks on the political character of education, rather than a continuous treatise written as one manuscript. [1][4]
- **1993:** *Professora sim, tia não: cartas a quem ousa ensinar*. An epistolary pedagogical intervention: letters in literary form, but also protocols urging teachers toward professional and political agency. [1][4]
- **1994:** *Cartas a Cristina: reflexões sobre minha vida e minha práxis*. An autobiographical sequence of letters addressed to Freire’s niece Cristina; memoir, intellectual history, and pedagogical reflection remain deliberately inseparable. [1][4]
- **1995:** *À sombra desta mangueira*. A late meditative book joining memory, place, politics, and education, with notes by Ana Maria Araújo Freire. [1][4]
- **1996:** *Pedagogia da autonomia: saberes necessários à prática educativa*. Freire’s final book completed and published during his lifetime, structured as a set of ethical and practical propositions about teaching. Some chronologies give 1997 because of a later imprint or launch, but the first edition is conventionally dated 1996. [2][4]

### Dialogic and collaborative books

- **1970:** *Witness to Liberation*, in *Seeing Education Whole*. A collaborative report and reflection by Freire, Tom Paxton, Jacques Prévert, Charles Hurst, Martin Conway, and Ellis Nelson arising from a World Council of Churches consultation; it is not solely Freire’s book. [1]
- **1978:** *Freire–Illich: The Pedagogy of the Oppressed—The Oppression of Pedagogy*. A documentary publication made from encounters and interviews involving Freire and Ivan Illich; the editors’ construction of the record complicates individual authorship. [1]
- **1980:** *Vivendo e aprendendo: experiências do IDAC em educação popular*. A collective IDAC obra credited to Freire, Claudius Ceccon, Rosiska Darcy de Oliveira, and Miguel Darcy de Oliveira, joining institutional reflection to accounts of practice. [2]
- **1982:** *Sobre educação (Diálogos), volume 1*. A transcribed book-dialogue with Sérgio Guimarães; conversation is its generative medium, so neither speaker is adequately described as the sole author. [1][2]
- **1984:** *Sobre educação (Diálogos), volume 2*. The second Freire–Guimarães dialogue volume, continuing the jointly made oral-textual form. [1]
- **1984:** *A África ensinando a gente: Angola, Guiné-Bissau, São Tomé e Príncipe*. A dialogic reflection with Sérgio Guimarães on African educational experiences; the conversational record is the obra. [1]
- **1985:** *Essa escola chamada vida: depoimentos ao repórter Ricardo Kotscho*. Parallel life testimonies by Freire and Frei Betto elicited and organized by journalist Ricardo Kotscho; it is an interview construction, not a conventionally co-written treatise. [1][2]
- **1985:** *Por uma pedagogia da pergunta*. A dialogue with Antonio Faundez in which questioning is both subject and method; it exists as a jointly made conversational book. [1][2]
- **1986:** *Fazer escola conhecendo a vida*. A collaborative pedagogical book with Adriano Nogueira and Débora Mazza, developed from interdisciplinary popular-education practice. [1]
- **1986:** *Na escola que fazemos: uma reflexão interdisciplinar em educação popular*. A collectively voiced reflection associated with Freire, Adriano Nogueira, and Débora Mazza; bibliographies vary in how they assign authorship and editorial responsibility. [1][2]
- **1987:** *Aprendendo com a própria história*. A book-dialogue with Sérgio Guimarães reconstructing Freire’s political and pedagogical trajectory through recorded testimony. [1][2]
- **1987:** *Medo e ousadia: o cotidiano do professor* / *A Pedagogy for Liberation*. A dialogue with Ira Shor, developed from recorded and repeatedly edited conversations; the English and Portuguese titles are manifestations of the same collaborative obra. [1][2]
- **1987:** *Alfabetização: leitura do mundo, leitura da palavra* / *Literacy: Reading the Word and the World*. A coauthored book with Donaldo Macedo linking literacy to cultural and political interpretation; translation and dialogic composition make priority between its linguistic manifestations difficult to reduce to a single “original.” [1]
- **1988:** *Que fazer: teoria e prática em educação popular*. A dialogue with Adriano Nogueira whose interrogative title signals a practical-political protocol as much as a theoretical book. Some bibliographies date editions to 1989, so the first-publication year is not reported consistently. [1][2]
- **1990:** *We Make the Road by Walking: Conversations on Education and Social Change*. Conversations between Freire and Myles Horton, edited by Brenda Bell, John Gaventa, and John Peters; the editors shaped recorded speech into the published object, making authorship necessarily distributed. [1][2]
- **1990:** *Conversando con educadores*. A Spanish-language collection of Freire’s conversations with educators; it is an editorially constituted spoken book, and the standard bibliography does not present it as a new continuous manuscript. [1]
- **1991:** *Paulo Freire en El Salvador*. A published record of Freire’s encounters and discussions in El Salvador; its institutional production by CIAZO makes it closer to documentary manifestation than sole-authored monograph. [1]
- **1992:** *Pedagogia: diálogo e conflito*. A collaborative intervention with Moacir Gadotti and Sérgio Guimarães; editions and bibliographies differ over whether to foreground Freire as coauthor or treat the volume as a set of conversations. [1]
- **1994:** *O caminho se faz caminhando*. The Portuguese manifestation of the Freire–Horton conversations published earlier as *We Make the Road by Walking*, not a separate dialogue merely because it bears another title and date. [1]

### Essays, articles, lectures, interviews, and letters as a dispersed corpus

- **1955–1996:** *Articles, essays, conference papers, lectures, interviews, prefaces, presentations, and correspondence*. The standard biobibliography inventories these as several separate chronological classes beginning in 1955, including named texts such as “Educação e atualidade brasileira,” “The Political Literacy Process—An Introduction,” “Education, Liberation and the Church,” and “The Importance of the Act of Reading.” This is a large, incompletely recoverable dispersed corpus: texts recur under translated titles, lectures become articles, and interviews or letters later become chapters, so claiming a definitive one-bullet-per-title catalogue would falsely stabilize editions as works. [1]
- **1970:** *The Political Literacy Process—An Introduction*. An essay first published in German periodical form and then in French, Italian, and Portuguese manifestations; it later entered *Ação cultural para a liberdade*. [1][2]
- **1973:** *Education, Liberation and the Church*. A theological-political essay first appearing in *Study Encounter*; it is an article, not a freestanding book, despite later incorporation into collections. [1]
- **1983:** *The Importance of the Act of Reading*. A lecture-derived article published in the *Journal of Education* and also incorporated into the Portuguese triptych *A importância do ato de ler*. [1][2]
- **1988:** *Letter to Adult Education Workers*. A public letter published in *Adult Education and Development*; it is both correspondence and pedagogical exhortation, resisting a clean boundary between letter, essay, and protocol. [2]

### Posthumous editorial manifestations

- **2000:** *Pedagogia da indignação: cartas pedagógicas e outros escritos*. A posthumous collection organized by Ana Maria Araújo Freire from unfinished pedagogical letters and other texts. Freire made the component writings and the unfinished project, but not the final editorial book in the form published. [4]
- **2001:** *Pedagogia dos sonhos possíveis*. A posthumous collection organized by Ana Maria Araújo Freire from lectures, interviews, and other writings; its title and book-level unity are editorial rather than a completed Freire manuscript. [4]
- **2003:** *O educador: vida e morte*. A later collection containing Freirean material alongside other contributors; it should not be counted without qualification as a book authored by Freire. [1]
- **2005:** *Pedagogia da tolerância*. A posthumous collection organized by Ana Maria Araújo Freire from seminars, talks, and writings; Freire authored the underlying texts, while the published obra was made editorially after his death. [4]
- **2007:** *Daring to Dream: Toward a Pedagogy of the Unfinished*. An English posthumous selection of speeches, essays, and interviews, edited into a new book after Freire’s death; it is a manifestation of archival material, not a lifetime-authored monograph. [2][4]

### Pedagogical and institutional projects

- **1947–1957:** *SESI Pernambuco adult-education work*. A sustained pedagogical project through which Freire developed practices of consultation, dialogue, and work with workers’ families. It resists identification as one authored work: its obra consisted in meetings, institutional arrangements, research, and changing educational relations. [1][5]
- **1958:** *Adult Education and Marginal Populations: The Mocambos Problem*. A report and intervention presented at Brazil’s Second National Congress of Adult Education; it was a project statement for education grounded in learners’ social realities rather than a book. [1][5]
- **1960–1964:** *Movimento de Cultura Popular do Recife literacy and culture circles*. A collective educational creation in which Freire directed the Division of Research and helped develop culture circles, thematic investigation, and generative-word practice. Calling it simply the “Paulo Freire method” obscures its collaborative construction and the participants who remade it in use. [1][5]
- **1962–1963:** *Angicos literacy experiment*. A short, intensive adult-literacy project in Rio Grande do Norte, commonly summarized as teaching approximately 300 workers in forty hours. It was a collective political-pedagogical event rather than a text, and the famous numerical formula can conceal preparation, research, staff, and participants. [5][6]
- **1963–1964:** *Programa Nacional de Alfabetização*. A federal project intended to establish thousands of culture circles across Brazil under Freire’s coordination; the 1964 military coup terminated it before nationwide realization. Its incompletion is integral to the obra. [5][6]
- **1964–1969:** *Chilean agrarian-reform education*. A body of literacy, peasant education, and rural communication work undertaken with Chilean institutions during exile. It generated *Extensión o comunicación?* and other texts but cannot be reduced to those publications. [1][5]
- **1970–1980:** *World Council of Churches educational work*. A transnational advisory project based in Geneva through which Freire worked with liberation movements, churches, and newly independent states; its manifestations include consultations, letters, reports, and national programs rather than one bounded work. [1][5]
- **1971–1980:** *Instituto de Ação Cultural (IDAC)*. A collective institution co-created in Geneva by Freire and other exiles to practice and rethink popular education. Because IDAC’s reports and books were collaboratively made, attribution to Freire alone is often inappropriate. [1][5]
- **1975–1980:** *Guinea-Bissau literacy collaboration*. An advisory and pedagogical project conducted by Freire and the IDAC team with the new state’s education authorities; *Cartas à Guiné-Bissau* records an experiment explicitly still “in process,” not a completed system imposed by a sole author. [1][5]
- **1976–1980:** *São Tomé and Príncipe literacy and culture-circle program*. A collaborative national project involving workbooks, cultural notebooks, and letters to animators. Its protocols survive as texts, but the made thing was the educational process enacted by learners and cultural workers. [1][5]
- **1980–1989:** *Post-exile popular-education advising*. A dispersed body of work with unions, church organizations, universities, and popular movements after Freire’s return to Brazil. It resists cataloguing as a single obra because its substance lay in counsel, workshops, conversations, and institutional relationships. [1][5]
- **1989–1991:** *São Paulo municipal education project*. As municipal secretary of education, Freire led a collective transformation involving democratic school governance, building repair, curriculum reorientation, teacher development, and popular participation. Authorship belongs to an administration and its communities as well as to Freire. [1][5][7]
- **1989–1991:** *MOVA-SP (Movimento de Alfabetização de Jovens e Adultos)*. A partnership between São Paulo’s government and civil-society organizations for youth and adult literacy. It is a pedagogical-political network, not a proprietary “method” or sole-authored work. [1][7]
- **1991–1992:** *Instituto Paulo Freire*. An international network conceived through discussions among Freire, Moacir Gadotti, Carlos Alberto Torres, Francisco Gutiérrez, and others and formally created in 1992. Freire helped make the project, but the institution is a collective and continuing obra, not an object attributable to him alone. [1]
