## Works by Polybius of Megalopolis

### Historical obra

- **Begun after 167 BCE; expanded and revised after 146 BCE:** *Histories* (*Historiai*). A forty-book “pragmatic” universal history explaining Rome’s Mediterranean ascendancy, originally planned to cover 220–168/167 BCE and subsequently extended backward to 264 and forward to 146; Books 1–5 survive complete, while Books 6–39 survive unevenly in excerpts and fragments, and Book 40—apparently a summary or index—is lost. The work’s long composition and revision make a single completion date misleading. ([Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548090484), [McGing, introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191612602_A23588695/preview-9780191612602_A23588695.pdf), [Büttner-Wobst critical edition, vol. IV](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783110936957))

### Biography

- **Date unknown, after 183 BCE and before the corresponding portion of the *Histories*:** *Life of Philopoemen*. A three-book biographical and commemorative obra, now lost; Polybius himself distinguishes it from the *Histories* and says that it treated Philopoemen’s upbringing and celebrated actions. Its conventional English title is descriptive rather than securely transmitted as an ancient title. ([Polybius, *Histories* 10.21](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/10%2A.html), [McGing, introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191612602_A23588695/preview-9780191612602_A23588695.pdf))

### Military protocol

- **Date unknown; written before the relevant passage of the *Histories*:** *Notes on Tactics* (often titled *Tactics*). A lost technical treatise or collection of memoranda defining, analyzing, and instructing military organization; Polybius refers readers to it for a fuller treatment of camp geometry, while Arrian and Aelian preserve evidence of its later reception. Neither its exact title nor its extent is certain. ([Polybius, *Histories* 9.20.4](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/9%2A.html), [McGing, introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191612602_A23588695/preview-9780191612602_A23588695.pdf))

- **Date unknown; incorporated into *Histories* 10.43–47:** *Alphabetic fire-signalling protocol*. This is not an independently transmitted work but an operational system: five-by-five letter groups, tablets, and paired torch signals for communicating unrestricted messages. Polybius explicitly credits Cleoxenus and Democleitus with the underlying invention and claims only to have perfected it, so it resists attribution as his creation alone. ([Polybius, *Histories* 10.43–47](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Polybius/10%2A.html), [Hau, “Polybius and the Progress of History”](https://histos.org/index.php/histos/article/download/366/360/369))

### Lost historical monograph

- **After 133 BCE, if the usual identification is correct:** *History of the Numantine War*. A lost historical monograph known only from Cicero’s statement that Polybius wrote about the Numantine war; its customary title, extent, and precise date are modern reconstructions, and Polybius’s own presence at Numantia remains an inference rather than a securely documented fact. ([Cicero, *Letters to Friends* 5.12.2, cited in the ancient evidence summarized by Smith](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0104%3Aentry%3Dpolybius-bio-4), [McGing, introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191612602_A23588695/preview-9780191612602_A23588695.pdf))

### Geographical inquiry of uncertain independence

- **Date unknown:** *On the Habitability of the Equatorial Region*. A lost geographical argument cited by Geminus and conventionally listed as a separate treatise. Its independence is disputed: it may instead have been material from the almost wholly lost geographical Book 34 of the *Histories*, so it may resist classification as a separate obra at all. ([Smith, “Polybius,” *Dictionary of Greek and Roman Biography and Mythology*](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0104%3Aentry%3Dpolybius-bio-4), [McGing, introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191612602_A23588695/preview-9780191612602_A23588695.pdf))

### Institutional and legal work

- **146–145 BCE:** *Postwar civic settlements for the Greek cities*. Not a securely identifiable authored text but a practical constitutional project: after Rome’s defeat of the Achaean League, Polybius helped explain, draft, implement, and adjudicate the cities’ new laws and constitutions. Because the individual enactments are not preserved as a Polybian corpus and authority ultimately came from the Roman commission, this resists being called either a single work or solely his creation. ([Polybius, *Histories* 39.5](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0234%3Abook%3D39), [McGing, introduction](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191612602_A23588695/preview-9780191612602_A23588695.pdf))
