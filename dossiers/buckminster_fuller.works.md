## Works by R. Buckminster Fuller

### Lifelong Gesamtkunstwerk and archives

- **1907–1983:** *Dymaxion Chronofile*. Fuller’s continuously accumulated record of correspondence, drawings, photographs, manuscripts, clippings, models, and daily life is simultaneously archive, autobiography, design instrument, and unfinished Gesamtkunstwerk; it resists classification as a single “work.” The surviving papers occupy roughly 1,400 linear feet at Stanford, and neither the archive nor any checklist can turn every component into a separately titled obra. ([Stanford Libraries](https://findingaids.stanford.edu/agents/people/5593); [Estate chronology](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf))
- **1927–1983:** *Guinea Pig B*. Fuller’s name for the project of treating his own life as an experiment in what one individual, without wealth or institutional power, might do for humanity; its manifestations include inventions, lectures, books, companies, correspondence, and the Chronofile, so it is an intention rather than a bounded object. ([Buckminster Fuller Institute biography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/biography/))
- **c. 1944–1983:** *Synergetics*. A body of geometrical investigation—models, diagrams, terminology, lectures, manuscripts, and two major books—rather than merely the title of those books; its unstable boundary makes it closer to a lifelong research project than a discrete text. ([Synergetics online edition](https://www.rwgrayprojects.com/synergetics/); [Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1951–1983:** *Comprehensive Anticipatory Design Science*. Fuller’s overarching project for using empirically tested design to improve humanity’s resource performance; it is a method, manifesto, and umbrella for many subsequent works, not a single artifact. ([Buckminster Fuller Institute biography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/biography/))
- **1961–1975:** *World Design Science Decade*. A proposed international ten-year program, launched through the International Union of Architects, for redirecting design education and world resources; it survives through speeches, research, documents, maps, and the World Game and therefore resists treatment as one text. ([Buckminster Fuller Institute](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))

### Books, poem-books, compilations, and graphic texts

- **1928:** *4D Time Lock*. A privately printed manifesto for an industrially produced, resource-efficient dwelling system; only 200 copies of the first printing were made, and later editions vary in date and title styling. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1938:** *Nine Chains to the Moon*. A book-length synthesis of technology, history, economics, and Fuller’s principle of doing more with less; unlike many later volumes assembled from lectures or shorter texts, it was conceived substantially as a book. ([Annotated bibliography](https://rwgrayprojects.com/SynergeticsDictionary/Additional/Biblio.pdf))
- **1960:** *The Dymaxion World of Buckminster Fuller*. An illustrated survey of Fuller’s designs and ideas made with Robert W. Marks; authorship is collaborative rather than Fuller’s alone. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1962:** *Untitled Epic Poem on the History of Industrialization*. A long visual poem—also circulated as *The Unfinished Epic of Industrialization*—whose typography, diagrams, and historical argument make it neither an ordinary poem nor an ordinary technological treatise. The variant titles and publication histories should not be silently collapsed into separate works. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/); [World Design Science Decade bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/wdsd_phase1_doc3_thinking.pdf))
- **1962–1963:** *Education Automation: Freeing the Scholar to Return to His Studies*. A manifesto and educational proposal developed from Fuller’s Southern Illinois University lectures; bibliographies variously date it 1962 or 1963 because lecture, copyright, and publication dates differ. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1963:** *Ideas and Integrities: A Spontaneous Autobiographical Disclosure*. An autobiographical compilation of essays, talks, and design arguments rather than a continuous memoir. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1963:** *No More Secondhand God and Other Writings*. A hybrid collection of poems, essays, geometrical speculation, and manifestos; its changing media and modes make it a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a conventional essay collection. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1968:** *“How Little I Know.”* Fuller’s contribution to the multi-author volume *What I Have Learned*; the containing book is not his obra, so only the chapter belongs here. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1969:** *Operating Manual for Spaceship Earth*. A compact planetary manifesto that treats Earth as a finite craft requiring collective operation; some estate listings give an earlier copyright date, but the recognized first book publication is 1969. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/); [exhibition catalogue](https://www.buckyverse.org/en/buckminster_fuller_starting_with_the_universe/index))
- **1969:** *Utopia or Oblivion: The Prospects for Humanity*. A compilation of lectures and essays functioning as a design-science manifesto; parts had appeared in the World Design Science Decade documents. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/); [World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1969:** *50 Years of the Design Science Revolution and the World Game*. A book/document joining an autobiographical account of Fuller’s design program to the protocol and rationale of the World Game. ([Whitney exhibition catalogue](https://www.buckyverse.org/en/buckminster_fuller_starting_with_the_universe/index))
- **1970:** *The Buckminster Fuller Reader*. James Meller’s edited selection of Fuller’s earlier texts; it is a post-selection manifestation of a dispersed body of writing, not a wholly new continuous work by Fuller. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1970:** *I Seem to Be a Verb*. A collaboratively made graphic book with Jerome Agel and Quentin Fiore, combining Fuller’s language with montage, typography, photographs, and parallel page sequences; it is a Gesamtkunstwerk whose authorship cannot properly be assigned to Fuller alone. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1970:** *Intuition*. A philosophical and geometrical essay-book developed from Fuller’s lectures and reflections on cognition. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1972:** *Buckminster Fuller to Children of Earth*. A photographic and textual book compiled and photographed by Cam Smith from Fuller’s words; Fuller supplied the text, but the book-object is collaborative. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1973:** *Earth, Inc.* A brief planetary-economic manifesto treating Earth’s resources as a common inventory rather than national property. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1975:** *Synergetics: Explorations in the Geometry of Thinking*. A numbered, illustrated geometrical system assembled with E. J. Applewhite; it is book, reference structure, neologistic language, and manifestation of the larger Synergetics project. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/); [online edition](https://www.rwgrayprojects.com/synergetics/))
- **1975–1976:** *Tetrascroll: Goldilocks and the Three Bears—A Cosmic Fairy Tale*. A cosmological narrative first manifested as a suite of twenty-one interlocking tetrahedral lithographs and subsequently as a conventional codex; publication sources consequently give both 1975 and 1976. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1976:** *And It Came to Pass—Not to Stay*. A collection of essays, lectures, and autobiographical reflections rather than a single newly composed argument. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1979:** *Synergetics 2: Further Explorations in the Geometry of Thinking*. A continuation and expansion of *Synergetics*, again made with E. J. Applewhite and inseparable from the larger research project. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/); [online edition](https://www.rwgrayprojects.com/synergetics/))
- **1979:** *R. Buckminster Fuller on Education*. Robert D. Kahn and Peter H. Wagschal’s edited assembly of Fuller’s educational talks and texts; the editorially constructed book is distinct from the prior occasions on which its component texts appeared. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1979:** *Synergetics Folio: A Collection of Ten Posters with Introductory Essay by Buckminster Fuller*. A portfolio of geometrical posters and an essay, not simply a book; its separable sheets are essential to what kind of thing it is. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1981:** *Critical Path*. A late synthesis of autobiography, technological history, global economics, and planetary strategy, made with Kiyoshi Kuromiya as “adjuvant”; estate records sometimes use the 1980 copyright date, while standard bibliographies date publication to 1981. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1981:** *Buckminster Fuller Sketchbook*. A facsimile-like visual notebook of drawings, annotations, and design ideas; it presents thinking-in-process rather than a finished verbal treatise. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1983:** *Grunch of Giants*. A political-economic allegory and manifesto whose title abbreviates “Gross Universal Cash Heist.” ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1983:** *Humans in Universe*. A dialogic or collaboratively assembled book made with Anwar Dil, combining Fuller’s conversations and thought with Dil’s editorial structure. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))
- **1983:** *Inventions: The Patented Works of R. Buckminster Fuller*. A documented portfolio and retrospective catalogue edited by Robert Snyder; it reproduces and explains Fuller’s patent corpus, so it is simultaneously Fuller’s late self-presentation and an editorial work by Snyder. ([digitized edition](https://buckyverse.org/en/inventions/inventions.pdf))
- **1992, posthumous:** *Cosmography: A Posthumous Scenario for the Future of Humanity*. A posthumously shaped book made from Fuller’s material with Kiyoshi Kuromiya as adjuvant; its authorship and final form are therefore shared, and Fuller did not approve the published object after completion. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/books-by-fuller/))

### Journals, lectures, and document series

- **1930–1932:** *Shelter*. An architectural magazine that Fuller acquired from *T-Square*, renamed, edited, designed, and published with collaborators; the run is a periodical project rather than one text, and individual unsigned items cannot all be securely attributed to him. ([Estate history](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html); [BFI biography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/biography/))
- **1938–1940:** *Fortune science-and-technology studies*. Charts, research, and editorial work produced while Fuller served as *Fortune*’s science and technology consultant; because magazine production was corporate and frequently unsigned, the body of work resists item-by-item attribution to Fuller. ([BFI biography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/biography/))
- **1963:** *Inventory of World Resources, Human Trends and Needs*. Phase I, Document 1 of the World Design Science Decade, made with John McHale; it is an inventory and planning instrument, not merely an essay. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1964:** *The Design Initiative*. Phase I, Document 2 of the World Design Science Decade, developed from Fuller’s address to the International Union of Architects; it acts as a manifesto calling institutions into a program of work. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1965:** *Comprehensive Thinking*. Phase I, Document 3 of the World Design Science Decade; a methodological manifesto for approaching world problems without disciplinary fragmentation. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1965:** *The Ten Year Program*. Phase I, Document 4 of the World Design Science Decade, authored by John McHale rather than Fuller; it belongs to Fuller’s project but should not be misattributed as his text. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1967:** *Comprehensive Design Strategy*. Phase II, Document 5 of the World Design Science Decade, comprising *Man with a Chronofile*, *Introduction to Design Strategy*, and *Design Strategy*; it is part autobiography, part manifesto, and part protocol. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1967:** *The Ecological Context: Energy and Materials*. Phase II, Document 6 of the World Design Science Decade, authored by John McHale; it is a manifestation of the collaborative program but not Fuller’s individual obra. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1971:** *The World Game: Integrative Resource Utilization Planning Tool*. The first World Game series document: simultaneously research report, operating protocol, and invitation to collective play. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1975:** *Everything I Know*. A twelve-session, approximately forty-two-hour extemporaneous lecture series recorded in Philadelphia; it is an audiovisual oral summa, subsequently transcribed and digitized, rather than a book Fuller wrote. ([Buckminster Fuller Institute](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/everything-i-know/))
- **1920s–1983:** *Articles, speeches, telegrams, interviews, and occasional poems*. Fuller made hundreds of shorter texts and thousands of hours of speech, but the estate’s published article list describes itself as a selection rather than a complete scholarly bibliography; unsigned collaborative pieces and variant transcripts make “one bullet per text” impossible without inventing stable works where the archive does not establish them. ([BFI bibliography](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/); [articles list](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/articles-by-fuller/))

### Housing, vehicles, buildings, and city projects

- **1922–1927:** *Stockade Building System*. A lightweight building method developed with James Monroe Hewlett using vertically set fiber-composite blocks; it became both a construction business and two patentable inventions, so the project exceeds either patent document. ([Estate shelter catalogue](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html); [BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1927:** *4D Tower House*. An unbuilt hexagonal, mast-suspended apartment tower conceived for aerial delivery by dirigible; it survives as project, drawings, and models rather than architecture occupied on a site. ([Estate shelter catalogue](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html))
- **1927–1930:** *Dymaxion House*. A mass-production dwelling project suspended from a central mast and conceived as a service system rather than a conventional building; changing versions and names make it a design lineage, not one fixed house. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/2030-r-buckminster-fuller); [Estate shelter catalogue](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html))
- **1929:** *Proposed Dymaxion Hanging Restaurant for Romany Marie*. An unrealized suspended restaurant documented by drawings; as a commission proposal it remains an intention rather than a building. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/2030-r-buckminster-fuller))
- **1933–1934:** *Dymaxion Car*. A rear-steered, three-wheeled transport experiment manifested in three differing prototypes; Fuller directed the project, while Starling Burgess and a team of engineers and fabricators contributed materially, making sole authorship misleading. ([BFI patent catalogue](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/); [Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1936–1940:** *Dymaxion Bathroom*. A compact, prefabricated bathroom cell intended for industrial production; it was prototyped and patented but did not become the universal product Fuller envisaged. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1940–1941:** *Dymaxion Deployment Unit*. A circular corrugated-steel shelter derived from grain-bin manufacture and produced with Butler Manufacturing; unlike several Fuller housing projects, it entered limited wartime production. ([Estate chronology](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf); [BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1944–1946:** *Dymaxion Dwelling Machine / Wichita House*. A full-scale aluminum housing prototype developed with Beech Aircraft; Fuller’s disagreement with investors prevented mass production, leaving two prototypes and an unfinished industrial project rather than a commercial house type. ([Estate shelter catalogue](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html); [Henry Ford museum](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections-and-research/digital-collections/artifact/20965/))
- **1948–1949:** *Black Mountain College dome experiments*. Student-built experiments in great-circle structures; the first Venetian-blind-slat version collapsed or lay flat before later versions stood, making failure an informative manifestation of the project rather than a non-work. ([Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1950:** *R. Buckminster Fuller and Anne Hewlett Dome Home, Carbondale*. Fuller’s family residence made from a commercial geodesic-dome kit and modified for domestic use; it is a lived building as well as a test of dome habitation. ([Fuller Dome Home](https://fullerdomehome.com/))
- **1953:** *Ford Rotunda Dome*. A lightweight geodesic roof for Ford’s Dearborn Rotunda, devised when conventional framing was considered too heavy; it was a built industrial commission, later destroyed with the Rotunda by fire in 1962. ([Henry Ford](https://www.thehenryford.org/collections-and-research/digital-resources/popular-topics/ford-rotunda/))
- **1954:** *Milan Triennale Geodesic Dome*. A built demonstration dome awarded the Triennale’s Gran Premio; temporary exhibition structure, engineering prototype, and public manifesto coincide in one object. ([Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1954:** *U.S. Marine Corps helicopter-transportable domes*. Lightweight geodesic shelters developed and tested for military deployment; they are a family of built prototypes rather than one uniquely titled building. ([Estate chronology](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf))
- **1958:** *Tokyo Tower proposal*. An unselected project rather than the Tokyo Tower that was built; treating it as a completed Fuller building would be an attribution error. ([Estate chronology](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf))
- **1958:** *Tokyo Olympic Stadium proposal*. An unselected design and therefore a project without a built manifestation. ([Estate chronology](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf))
- **c. 1958–1962:** *Cloud Nine*. A proposal for enormous, heated geodesic spheres that might become buoyant and support aerial communities; no sphere was built, and the work persists as speculative calculation and image. ([Whitney exhibition catalogue](https://www.buckyverse.org/en/buckminster_fuller_starting_with_the_universe/index))
- **1960:** *Dome over Manhattan*. A proposal made with Shoji Sadao for a climate-controlling dome over midtown Manhattan; its monumental photomontage is the principal manifestation of an intentionally speculative project. ([Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1967:** *United States Pavilion, Expo 67*. The large geodesic sphere in Montreal, designed through Fuller & Sadao with Geometrics and other engineers; Fuller’s is therefore leading but collaborative authorship. The acrylic skin burned in 1976, while the steel framework survives as the Montréal Biosphère. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2011630541/); [Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1967:** *Triton City*. A floating tetrahedral city proposed with Shoji Sadao and studied for possible construction in Baltimore; federal interest and detailed studies did not produce the city, so the project—not a building—is the obra. ([Estate shelter catalogue](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html))
- **1971:** *Old Man River’s City*. A vast terraced, crater-like community project for East St. Louis; it remained an urban proposal and set of representations rather than a realized city. ([Estate shelter catalogue](https://buckminsterfuller.net/images/shelter.html))
- **1965–1981:** *Fly’s Eye Dome*. A family of perforated fiberglass dome prototypes developed as autonomous dwelling components; several differently sized manifestations exist, so the title denotes a system rather than one object. ([Whitney Museum](https://whitney.org/exhibitions/buckminster-fuller))
- **1974–1985:** *Dewan Tunku Geodesic Dome, Penang*. A geodesic auditorium project associated with Fuller and Shoji Sadao, proposed during Fuller’s lifetime but completed after his death; its long realization and collaborative engineering make a simple 1983 attribution inadequate. ([Estate chronology](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf))

### Maps, games, geometry, and planetary systems

- **1943–1946:** *Dymaxion Map / Airocean World Map*. A cartographic projection developed through several polyhedral versions to show the continents with reduced visible distortion and without a fixed “up”; the 1946 patent marks one manifestation, not the beginning or end of the map project. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/2030-r-buckminster-fuller))
- **c. 1948–1959:** *Jitterbug Transformation*. A manipulable geometrical model in which a cuboctahedron contracts through an icosahedral phase toward an octahedron; it is demonstration, toy-like object, and dynamic theorem rather than a static sculpture. ([Synergetics online edition](https://www.rwgrayprojects.com/synergetics/))
- **1949 onward:** *Tensegrity*. A structural principle and family of models balancing discontinuous compression elements within continuous tension; Fuller coined and patented the term and systems, but Kenneth Snelson developed crucial early structures and disputed Fuller’s priority, so sole invention remains contested. ([Smithsonian Archives of American Art](https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/interviews/oral-history-interview-kenneth-snelson-13591); [BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1950s onward:** *Vector Equilibrium*. Fuller’s name and interpretive framework for the cuboctahedron as an energetic-geometrical model; he did not invent the ancient solid itself, and the obra lies in the models, terminology, and system of relations he built around it. ([Synergetics online edition](https://www.rwgrayprojects.com/synergetics/))
- **1950s onward:** *Isotropic Vector Matrix*. A conceptual and modelled lattice of tetrahedra and octahedra used throughout Synergetics; it is a representational system rather than a single artifact. ([Synergetics online edition](https://www.rwgrayprojects.com/synergetics/))
- **1961 onward:** *World Game*. A participatory research simulation for allocating planetary resources so that all humanity might prosper without ecological damage; it is at once game, pedagogical protocol, data project, and political manifesto, with later versions made by many collaborators. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1960s–1983:** *World Resources Inventory*. A changing body of global resource data, charts, maps, and planning documents supporting the World Design Science Decade and World Game; it resists being called one authored work because researchers and students continually assembled it. ([World Design Science Decade](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/world-design-science-decade/))
- **1969 onward:** *World-around Energy Grid*. A proposal for interconnecting regional electrical networks across national boundaries, especially through the Bering Strait; it remained a planetary infrastructure project rather than a constructed system. ([Global Energy Network Institute](https://www.geni.org/globalenergy/library/buckminster_fuller/world-energy-grid.shtml))

### Patented inventions and abandoned applications

- **1924–1927:** *Stockade: Pneumatic Forming Process* (U.S. Patent 1,634,900). A process patent filed in 1924 and issued in 1927 for forming the Stockade building blocks; it is one legal manifestation of the collaborative Stockade project. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1926–1927:** *Stockade: Building Structure* (U.S. Patent 1,633,702). A patented wall-and-building system developed through the Stockade enterprise with James Monroe Hewlett. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1928:** *4D House* (application file 1,793). An abandoned patent application, not a granted patent; its abandonment leaves evidence of an unfinished legal and architectural project. ([Estate patent list](https://www.buckminsterfuller.net/ewExternalFiles/RBF%20Basic%20Bio.pdf))
- **1933–1937:** *Dymaxion Car* (U.S. Patent 2,101,057). The patent manifestation of the three-wheeled vehicle project, filed in 1933 and granted in 1937. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1938–1940:** *Dymaxion Bathroom* (U.S. Patent 2,220,482). A patent for the prefabricated bathroom unit, distinct from its physical prototypes. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1941–1944:** *Dymaxion Deployment Unit—Sheet* (U.S. Patent 2,343,764). A patent covering the sheet enclosure of the deployment unit. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1941–1944:** *Dymaxion Deployment Unit—Frame* (U.S. Patent 2,351,419). A companion structural-frame patent; the legal separation does not mean that two independent shelters were made. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1944–1946:** *Dymaxion Map* (U.S. Patent 2,393,676). A cartographic patent for laying out the world on a polyhedral surface and unfolding it in different arrangements. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1951–1954:** *Geodesic Dome* (U.S. Patent 2,682,235). Fuller’s patent for a geodesic structural framework; Walther Bauersfeld’s earlier Zeiss planetarium dome is prior built work, so “inventor of the geodesic dome” is historically disputed even though Fuller’s particular patent and development program are not. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/technology/geodesic-dome))
- **1955–1959:** *Paperboard Dome* (U.S. Patent 2,881,717). A lightweight dome system using paperboard elements. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1957–1959:** *Plydome* (U.S. Patent 2,905,113). A dome assembled from overlapping sheet or plywood components. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1957–1959:** *Catenary / Geodesic Tent* (U.S. Patent 2,914,074). A tensioned shelter whose form joins tent construction to Fuller’s geometrical systems. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1956–1961:** *Octet Truss* (U.S. Patent 2,986,241). A space-frame system based on alternating tetrahedra and octahedra. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1959–1962:** *Tensegrity Structures* (U.S. Patent 3,063,521). A patent for discontinuous compression members held by continuous tension; Kenneth Snelson’s independent contribution and priority claim must remain attached to its attribution. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/); [Smithsonian oral history](https://www.aaa.si.edu/collections/interviews/oral-history-interview-kenneth-snelson-13591))
- **1959–1963:** *Submarisle / Undersea Island* (U.S. Patent 3,080,583). A floating or submersible marine platform conceived as an artificial island. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1961–1964:** *Aspension / Suspension Building* (U.S. Patent 3,139,957). A mast-and-cable building system in which floors are suspended rather than conventionally stacked. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1961–1965:** *Monohex / Geodesic Structures* (U.S. Patent 3,197,927). A geodesic construction system using repeated modular elements. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1960–1965:** *Laminar Dome* (U.S. Patent 3,203,144). A dome composed as a continuous intersecting laminar network. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1965:** *Octa Spinner*. An abandoned patent application rather than a granted patent; the estate catalogue preserves it as an unfinished invention. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1964–1967:** *Star Tensegrity / Octahedral Truss* (U.S. Patent 3,354,591). A tensegrity-derived octahedral space-frame system. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1970:** *Rowing Needles* (U.S. Patent 3,524,422). A rowing device whose filing and issue dates are both recorded as August 18, 1970 in the estate catalogue. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1973–1975:** *Floatable Breakwater* (U.S. Patent 3,863,455). A floating marine structure intended to dissipate wave energy. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1973–1975:** *Non-symmetrical Tensegrity* (U.S. Patent 3,866,366). A patent extending tensegrity beyond symmetrical arrangements. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1977–1979:** *Floating Breakwater* (U.S. Patent 4,136,994). A later floating-breakwater system, legally and technically distinct from the 1975 patent despite the near-identical title. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1978–1980:** *Tensegrity Truss* (U.S. Patent 4,207,715). A truss system applying tension-integrity principles. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))
- **1981–1983:** *Hanging Storage Shelf Unit* (U.S. Patent 4,377,114). A suspended domestic storage system and Fuller’s last patent listed by the estate. ([BFI patents](https://www.bfi.org/about-fuller/bibliography/patents/))

### Portfolios and retrospective self-catalogues

- **1981:** *Inventions: Twelve Around One*. A portfolio combining prints and texts for thirteen selected inventions; it translates engineering and patent projects into graphic art and retrospective self-portraiture. It should not be confused with the 1983 book *Inventions*. ([Whitney exhibition catalogue](https://www.buckyverse.org/en/buckminster_fuller_starting_with_the_universe/index))
- **1983:** *Inventions: The Patented Works of R. Buckminster Fuller*. As an illustrated patent compendium, this is also the closest thing Fuller authorized to a catalogue raisonné of his inventions; because Robert Snyder documented and edited it, its catalogue form is collaborative. ([digitized edition](https://buckyverse.org/en/inventions/inventions.pdf))
