## Works by John Maynard Keynes

### Books and book-length treatises

- **1913:** *Indian Currency and Finance*. A monograph on India’s monetary system, written after Keynes’s India Office service and later reprinted as volume I of the *Collected Writings* (CW A1).  
- **1919:** *The Economic Consequences of the Peace*. A polemical work of political economy attacking the Versailles settlement; it is analysis but also an intervention intended to change policy (CW A2).
- **1921:** *A Treatise on Probability*. A philosophical and mathematical treatise developed from Keynes’s earlier fellowship dissertation; the surviving drafts are substantially more extensive than the material selected for the *Collected Writings*.
- **1922:** *A Revision of the Treaty*. A sequel to *The Economic Consequences of the Peace*, proposing revision of reparations and inter-Allied debts.
- **1923:** *A Tract on Monetary Reform*. A monetary-policy treatise assembled partly from earlier journalism but revised into a distinct book.
- **1930:** *A Treatise on Money*. A two-volume economic treatise—volume I, *The Pure Theory of Money*, and volume II, *The Applied Theory of Money*—whose analytical project was superseded rather than simply continued by the *General Theory*.
- **1931:** *Essays in Persuasion*. An author-shaped collection of previously published books, pamphlets, articles, broadcasts, and speeches; as a collection it is a new obra, but many of its components have independent textual lives.
- **1933:** *Essays in Biography*. A collection of biographical and memorial essays, subsequently enlarged; its subjects include politicians, economists, and members of Keynes’s intellectual circle.
- **1936:** *The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money*. A theoretical treatise designed to overturn the prevailing account of employment; the special German, Japanese, and French prefaces are separate authorial texts attached to translations.
- **1972, posthumous:** *Essays in Biography*, enlarged edition. An editorially expanded manifestation incorporating further biographical writings; it was not constituted in this form by Keynes during his lifetime and therefore resists treatment as an unqualified Keynes-made book.
- **1972, posthumous:** *Essays in Persuasion*, expanded collected-writings text. An editorial manifestation restoring or adding material, not a separate lifetime obra by Keynes.

### Pamphlets and compact policy interventions

- **1925:** *The Economic Consequences of Mr. Churchill*. A polemical pamphlet opposing Britain’s return to gold at the pre-war parity; it acts as a public-policy intervention as much as it describes monetary conditions.
- **1925:** *A Short View of Russia*. A political and economic travel essay first issued through periodical publication and then as a pamphlet; later incorporated into *Essays in Persuasion*.
- **1926:** *The End of Laissez-Faire*. A published lecture and political-economic manifesto arguing that neither doctrinaire laissez-faire nor comprehensive state socialism supplied an adequate agenda.
- **1927:** *Are Books Too Dear?* A publishing-economics essay also issued in the Hogarth Press pamphlet *Books and the Public*; its separate and collected appearances are manifestations of one text, not distinct works.
- **1929:** *Can Lloyd George Do It? An Examination of the Liberal Pledge*. A jointly made election pamphlet with Hubert D. Henderson advocating loan-financed public works; undifferentiated joint authorship prevents secure allocation of every passage to Keynes.
- **1933:** *The Means to Prosperity*. A policy pamphlet proposing coordinated public expenditure and credit expansion against depression.
- **1933:** *An Open Letter to President Roosevelt*. A newspaper manifesto addressed directly to Roosevelt and intended to influence the New Deal; its original object was an open letter rather than a book.
- **1940:** *How to Pay for the War: A Radical Plan for the Chancellor of the Exchequer*. A policy pamphlet proposing compulsory deferred pay and related anti-inflationary machinery; it functions partly as a fiscal protocol.
- **1942:** *The Objective of International Price Stability*. A pamphlet manifestation of Keynes’s wartime proposals concerning currencies, commodities, and post-war stability.
- **1944:** *The International Monetary Fund*. A wartime explanatory pamphlet associated with the emerging Bretton Woods settlement; it belongs to a negotiated institutional project rather than standing wholly apart from Keynes’s official work.

### Academic essays, dissertations, and theoretical papers

- **1904:** *The Political Doctrines of Edmund Burke*. A Cambridge student essay; it survives as juvenilia rather than as a lifetime publication.
- **1905:** *The Moral Sciences Club paper on probability*. An early philosophical paper without a stable lifetime-publication identity; it belongs to the project that eventually became *A Treatise on Probability*.
- **1906:** *Recent Economic Events in India*. An early economic paper arising from Keynes’s India Office work.
- **1908:** *The Principles of Probability*. Keynes’s unsuccessful first fellowship dissertation; an unfinished manifestation of the probability project, not identical with the 1921 treatise.
- **1909:** *A Treatise on Probability*. The revised fellowship dissertation accepted by King’s College; despite sharing the later book’s title, it is an earlier manuscript state rather than the 1921 publication.
- **1909:** *The Method of Index Numbers with Special Reference to the Measurement of General Exchange Value*. The Adam Smith Prize essay, unpublished in Keynes’s lifetime and first printed from manuscript in the *Collected Writings*.
- **1910:** *The Principal Averages and the Laws of Error which Lead to Them*. A technical statistical article.
- **1911:** *The Influence of Parental Alcoholism*. A statistical article examining data on heredity and social conditions.
- **1912:** *The Foreign Trade of the United Kingdom at Prices of 1900*. A statistical-economic article.
- **1914:** *The City of London and the Bank of England, August 1914*. An analytical article about the financial crisis at the outbreak of war.
- **1921:** *A Treatise on Probability*–related replies and corrections. Short academic texts defending and amending the treatise; they resist being treated as wholly independent works because they are continuations of its argument.
- **1924:** *Discussion on Monetary Reform*. A printed speech delivered during the Royal Economic Society’s annual meeting; the spoken event and printed record are different manifestations.
- **1924:** *A Comment on Professor Cannan’s Article*. An academic intervention in the *Economic Journal*.
- **1925:** *Discussion on the National Debt*. A printed contribution to a Royal Economic Society discussion.
- **1928:** *The Amalgamation of the British Note Issues*. A monetary-policy article assembled partly from an earlier memorandum and correspondence, making its textual boundaries unusually visible.
- **1930:** *Economic Possibilities for Our Grandchildren*. A speculative essay about technology, accumulation, work, and leisure; first a periodical text and later a component of *Essays in Persuasion*.
- **1930:** *Discussion on Mr Hawtrey’s “Money and Index Numbers”*. A printed oral intervention.
- **1931:** *The Pure Theory of Money: A Reply to Dr Hayek*. A controversial academic response defending the *Treatise on Money*.
- **1932:** *The Economic Prospects 1932*. A public lecture or address circulated in print; its identity varies among spoken, journal, and collected manifestations.
- **1933:** *A Monetary Theory of Production*. A lecture/article announcing the research programme that led toward the *General Theory*.
- **1934:** *Poverty in Plenty: Is the Economic System Self-Adjusting?* A broadcast argument against confidence in automatic full-employment equilibrium.
- **1936:** *The General Theory of Employment*. An article clarifying the central argument of the book after publication; it is a distinct explanatory text, not an abridged edition.
- **1937:** *Alternative Theories of the Rate of Interest*. An academic article refining and defending the liquidity-preference theory.
- **1937:** *The “Ex Ante” Theory of the Rate of Interest*. An academic controversy article.
- **1937:** *The Theory of the Rate of Interest*. A further theoretical intervention whose proximity to Keynes’s other interest-rate papers makes it part of an argumentative sequence rather than a wholly isolated work.
- **1938:** *Mr Keynes and “Finance”: Comment*. A reply to D. H. Robertson.
- **1938:** *The Theory of Employment*. A reply to Jacob Viner and other critics of the *General Theory*.
- **1939:** *Professor Tinbergen’s Method*. A methodological review-essay criticizing early econometric model testing.
- **1940:** *Comment on Tinbergen’s Reply*. A continuation of the econometrics controversy.
- **1941:** *The Theory of Money and the Foreign Exchanges*. An economic paper connected with wartime and post-war monetary planning.
- **1943:** *The Long-Term Problem of Full Employment*. A policy-theoretical text circulated within government; its initial status was a memorandum rather than public authorship.
- **1946:** *The Balance of Payments of the United States*. Keynes’s last substantial economic article, published posthumously; its posthumous publication makes the printed article a manifestation he did not finally supervise.

### Reviews as critical works

- **1906:** *Essays on Economics, by H. Stanley Jevons*. Keynes’s first identified published review.
- **1908:** *West Ham: A Study in Social and Industrial Problems*. A statistical and social-policy review.
- **1911:** *Publications Issued by and in Preparation for the National Monetary Commission of the United States*. A collective bibliographical review.
- **1912:** *Calcul des probabilités* and associated probability works. A composite review discussing several books; it is one review-text rather than one work per reviewed volume.
- **1912:** *Report of the Commission on the Cost of Living in New Zealand*. A review of an official report.
- **1913:** *Gold, Prices and Wages, by J. A. Hobson*. A critical economic review.
- **1914:** *What Is Money?, by A. Mitchell Innes*. A monetary review.
- **1925:** *The Committee on the Currency*. A review of the official currency report with substantive policy argument.
- **1927:** *The Colwyn Report on National Debt and Taxation*. A review-essay on an official report.
- **1928:** *The Great Villiers Connection*. A review of W. T. Gun’s *Studies in Hereditary Ability*, later remade as a biographical essay.
- **1928:** *Crete and Greece in the Bronze Age*. An unsigned composite review; its attribution to Keynes rests on the collected-writings editors’ bibliographical work.
- **1928:** *The United States Balance of Trade in 1927*. A review of Ray Hall’s official study.
- **1928:** *The Behaviour of Prices, by Frederick C. Mills*. A review of statistical price research.
- **1930:** *The Economic Development of the Byzantine Empire*. A review of André Andreades’s work.
- **1931:** *Logic*. A review of F. P. Ramsey’s *Foundations of Mathematics*, later recast as part of Keynes’s memoir of Ramsey.
- **1932:** *Member Bank Reserves in the United States*. A review of an official Federal Reserve report.
- **1936:** *The Supply of Gold*. A composite review-essay using several publications.
- **1939:** *Professor Tinbergen’s Method*. Although conventionally catalogued as a review, its scale and methodological argument also make it an original academic essay.

### Biographical, philosophical, and literary writings

- **1903:** *The Character of William III*. A student historical essay; juvenilia rather than a lifetime-published work.
- **1904:** *The Political Doctrines of Edmund Burke*. A student political essay, also classifiable as juvenilia.
- **1905–06:** *Miscellaneous Apostles papers*. Philosophical and autobiographical papers written for the Cambridge Apostles; these often lack stable public titles and resist classification as finished works.
- **1924:** *Alfred Marshall, 1842–1924*. An obituary and intellectual biography later expanded for *Memorials of Alfred Marshall* and *Essays in Biography*; the versions are related but not textually identical.
- **1924:** *Bibliographical List of the Writings of Alfred Marshall*. A scholarly bibliography prepared for the *Economic Journal* and reprinted in *Memorials of Alfred Marshall*.
- **1925:** *A Tract on Monetary Reform*–era memoir of Mary Paley Marshall. A biographical piece whose archival and published manifestations do not always carry a single stable title.
- **1925:** *Am I a Liberal?* A political address, serialized article, and later collected essay; it is part self-classification, part party manifesto.
- **1928:** *Melchior: A Defeated Enemy*. A biographical-political portrait of Carl Melchior.
- **1929:** *Lloyd George: A Fragment*. A political character study.
- **1930:** *H. H. Asquith*. A political-biographical essay.
- **1931:** *F. P. Ramsey*. A memorial essay combining personal recollection with an assessment of Ramsey’s philosophical work.
- **1931:** *Newton, the Man*. A biographical essay presenting Newton as an alchemical and theological thinker as well as a scientist; Keynes revised it for a projected Newton commemoration.
- **1933:** *Thomas Robert Malthus*. An intellectual biography.
- **1933:** *Robert Malthus: The First of the Cambridge Economists*. A lecture-derived manifestation related to, but not simply identical with, the biographical essay.
- **1936:** *William Stanley Jevons*. An intellectual-biographical essay.
- **1938:** *My Early Beliefs*. A privately delivered autobiographical memoir of the Bloomsbury milieu; it was not published in Keynes’s lifetime and resists being treated as a public work.
- **1942:** *Mary Paley Marshall, 1850–1944*. A memorial notice and biographical assessment.
- **1946:** *Dr Melchior: A Defeated Enemy*. A revised or retitled manifestation of Keynes’s earlier Melchior portrait.

### Journalism, broadcasts, and public argument

- **1923–24:** *Anonymous contributions to The Nation and Athenaeum*. At least ten short unsigned political pieces identified by the *Collected Writings* bibliography; because they originally appeared anonymously and often under generic departmental headings, treating each as a securely titled autonomous work would manufacture identities the sources do not supply.
- **1923:** *The American Debt*. A periodical intervention on inter-Allied obligations.
- **1923:** *The Reconstruction of Europe*. A sequence of articles in the *Manchester Guardian Commercial* reconstruction supplements; it is both a serial journalistic project and source-material for later monetary writing.
- **1924:** *The Balance of Political Power at the Elections*. A political article also circulated internationally under altered titles.
- **1925:** *The Balfour Note and Inter-Allied Debts*. A policy article.
- **1925:** *The Arithmetic of the Sterling Exchange*. A journalistic monetary calculation.
- **1926:** *Coal: A Suggestion*. A proposed settlement of the coal dispute.
- **1926:** *Back to the Coal Problem*. A continuation of Keynes’s intervention in the industrial dispute.
- **1926:** *The Autumn Prospects for Sterling*. A syndicated monetary article published under different translated titles.
- **1926:** *Bagehot’s Lombard Street*. A historical-financial essay.
- **1927:** *Liberalism and Industry*. A speech to Liberal candidates subsequently published as a book contribution.
- **1928:** *A Liberal Programme*. A series of articles, produced with other Liberal thinkers, whose collective authorship and serial form resist reduction to a single-authored book.
- **1929:** *The Economic Chaos of Europe*. A contribution to a multivolume popular history.
- **1931:** *The Consequences to the Banks of the Collapse of Money Values*. An article prepared for *Vanity Fair*, revised for *Essays in Persuasion*, and published in a somewhat different form; no single manifestation exhausts the work.
- **1931:** *The Great Slump of 1930*. A public explanation of the depression, first issued as periodical journalism and then collected.
- **1931:** *The Problem of Unemployment*. A broadcast and printed discussion with other speakers; the broadcast event is jointly made, while Keynes’s contribution remains separately identifiable.
- **1931:** *A Reply to Sir William Beveridge*. A public-policy controversy text.
- **1931:** *The Abandonment of the Gold Standard*. Newspaper and broadcast interventions responding to Britain’s suspension of convertibility.
- **1932:** *The World’s Economic Outlook*. A public address and printed essay on depression and recovery.
- **1933:** *The Chaos of the Foreign Exchanges*. The first of five *Daily Mail* articles on the World Economic Conference.
- **1933:** *Can We Co-operate with America?* The second article in that conference series.
- **1933:** *The Conference Gets to Work*. The third article in the series.
- **1933:** *President Roosevelt’s Experiment*. The fourth article in the series.
- **1933:** *A Chapter of Miscalculations at the Conference*. The fifth article in the series.
- **1934:** *Can America Spend Its Way into Recovery?* An American magazine article advocating expansion.
- **1936:** *Art and the State*. A broadcast and printed cultural-policy manifesto.
- **1939:** *Democracy and Efficiency*. A political broadcast or address considering democratic government under crisis conditions.
- **1940:** *How to Pay for the War* broadcasts and articles. Preparatory and explanatory manifestations of the pamphlet’s fiscal project rather than wholly independent theoretical works.
- **1943:** *The Arts in War-Time*. A newspaper statement defending cultural provision during war.
- **1945:** *The Arts Council: Its Policy and Hopes*. A broadcast manifesto for the newly formed Arts Council.

### Official reports, memoranda, and institutional designs

- **1906–08:** *India Office memoranda*. Administrative and economic memoranda produced in official employment; many lack stable titles and were not public works, although selected examples survive in the *Collected Writings*.
- **1911:** *Recent Developments of the Indian Currency Question*. A Royal Economic Society paper printed by the Indian government for internal circulation.
- **1913:** *Memorandum on Proposals for an Indian State Bank*. An official policy memorandum whose object is institutional design.
- **1914–18:** *Treasury wartime financial memoranda*. A large body of individually dated official documents on foreign exchange, Allied finance, borrowing, and wartime supply; the collected edition is expressly selective, so this body of work cannot honestly be converted into a complete list of autonomous titled works.
- **1915:** *Memorandum on the Inflation Tax*. An official analytical memorandum, later important to Keynes’s account of inflation.
- **1919:** *Paris Peace Conference memoranda*. Drafts, calculations, position papers, and reparations proposals made as part of British official work; authorship sometimes involved departmental collaboration, and their project-status is often more accurate than calling each a finished work.
- **1924:** *Memorandum on Monetary Reform*. An official or quasi-official policy text related to the published *Tract*.
- **1929–30:** *Macmillan Committee evidence and memoranda*. Testimony, statistical submissions, and policy arguments made for the Committee on Finance and Industry; these are procedural interventions rather than a single authored book.
- **1930:** *Proposals for a Revenue Tariff*. A policy project associated with Keynes’s movement away from unconditional free trade.
- **1931:** *The Committee of Economists reports and memoranda*. Collaborative official documents on the economic crisis; individual responsibility is disputed or shared, so the committee texts should not be attributed wholly to Keynes.
- **1939–40:** *Compulsory saving and deferred-pay scheme*. A fiscal design developed through memoranda, articles, broadcasts, and *How to Pay for the War*; the project, rather than any one manifestation, is the made thing.
- **1940–45:** *Treasury wartime-finance memoranda*. Official papers on budgeting, inflation, lend-lease, external finance, and the transition from war; many were collaborative, confidential, repeatedly revised, or untitled.
- **1941:** *Proposals for an International Currency Union*. The first developed form of Keynes’s post-war monetary project.
- **1941–43:** *International Clearing Union*. A succession of plans and drafts for a multilateral clearing system using the proposed international unit “bancor”; it is an evolving institutional project, not one fixed text.
- **1942:** *Proposals for an International Clearing Union*. A circulated government paper and revised manifestation of the clearing-union project.
- **1943:** *Plan for an International Clearing Union*. The British government’s published or officially circulated version; revisions and interdepartmental negotiation make singular authorship qualified rather than absolute.
- **1943–44:** *Commodity-control and buffer-stock plans*. Memoranda designing international commodity arrangements; these are unfinished or unrealized institutional projects.
- **1943–45:** *Full-employment policy memoranda*. Drafts contributing to British employment policy and the 1944 white paper; the final government publication was collective and cannot be treated simply as Keynes’s work.
- **1944:** *Bretton Woods negotiating texts*. Statements, drafts, speeches, and amendments made as leader of the British delegation; the resulting agreements were negotiated creations, not works authored by Keynes alone.
- **1944–45:** *Anglo-American financial negotiations papers*. Briefs, drafts, calculations, and negotiating protocols concerning American financial assistance.
- **1945:** *British Loan Agreement negotiating project*. Keynes helped make the settlement through negotiation and official texts, but neither the agreement nor its final wording is solely his obra.
- **1945–46:** *International Monetary Fund and World Bank implementation papers*. Speeches, reports, and institutional proposals; these are contributions to collectively made institutions.
- **1946:** *Savannah speeches*. Keynes’s addresses at the inaugural meetings of the IMF and World Bank; oral performances subsequently preserved through official records.

### Editorial and publishing projects

- **1911–45:** *The Economic Journal under Keynes’s editorship*. A thirty-three-year editorial project involving selection, revision, commissioning, correspondence, and unsigned editorial matter. It is a body of work, but not a text wholly “written by Keynes,” and the collected edition prints only selected editorial correspondence.
- **1914–46:** *Royal Economic Society secretaryship*. An institutional and administrative body of work comprising reports, minutes, correspondence, publishing decisions, and financial arrangements; it resists classification as a discrete authored work.
- **1922–36:** *Cambridge Economic Handbooks*. A series Keynes conceived and edited, supplying introductions or prefaces to several volumes; the books themselves remain the works of their named authors.
- **1926:** *Official Papers by Alfred Marshall*. An edited volume for which Keynes supplied an editor’s preface; the Marshall papers are not Keynes’s works.
- **1925:** *Memorials of Alfred Marshall*. An edited memorial volume made with A. C. Pigou and containing Keynes’s memoir and bibliography; responsibility for the composite book is shared.
- **1923–30:** *The Nation and Athenaeum*. Keynes’s work as chairman, financial backer, and active editorial participant made the periodical institutionally possible, but the journal as a whole cannot be attributed to him as author.
- **1931 onward:** *The New Statesman and Nation*. A periodical formed through the merger in which Keynes participated as proprietor and director; this is an institutional creation with collective authorship.

### Cultural institutions and material projects

- **1932–46:** *Cambridge Arts Theatre project*. Keynes organized, financed, and helped govern the theatre, which opened in 1936. The building and institution are an obra made collaboratively with architects, performers, donors, and administrators, not a Keynes-authored “text.”
- **1936:** *Cambridge Arts Theatre*. The realized theatre building and producing organization; architectural authorship is not Keynes’s, while the entrepreneurial and institutional project substantially is.
- **1942–46:** *Council for the Encouragement of Music and the Arts*. Keynes chaired and shaped CEMA’s wartime cultural programme; the programme is a collective institutional work rather than his individual creation.
- **1946:** *Arts Council of Great Britain*. Keynes helped transform CEMA into a permanent public body and became its first chairman. The council is a collectively constituted institution, although Keynes’s cultural-policy project materially shaped it.
- **1930s–1946:** *Keynes’s collection of paintings, drawings, manuscripts, and Newton papers*. A deliberately assembled collection rather than a set of objects made by Keynes; selection and arrangement are his curatorial work, while the component objects retain their own makers.
- **1936:** *Acquisition of the Newton alchemical manuscripts*. A collecting and preservation act, not authorship; Keynes subsequently organized and interpreted the papers in *Newton, the Man*.
- **1925–46:** *Investment portfolios for King’s College, the National Mutual, and other institutions*. Strategies and portfolios made through repeated transactions rather than stable texts. Their status as “works” is contestable, but excluding them would conceal an important practical form of Keynes’s economic activity.

### Correspondence, drafts, and things that resist being called works

- **c. 1897–1946:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters survive across the Keynes Papers and other archives. Individual letters are made texts, but ordinary correspondence was not consistently intended as autonomous work, and the *Collected Writings* is selective rather than complete.
- **1906–46:** *Lectures and lecture notes*. Surviving notes include economics teaching, public lectures, and speaking outlines; some are preparatory fragments, some records of performances, and many have no fixed title.
- **1908–21:** *Probability drafts, proofs, and correspondence*. A large manuscript ecology behind *A Treatise on Probability* that the collected edition deliberately did not reproduce comprehensively.
- **1920s–1936:** *Treatise on Money and General Theory drafts*. Manuscripts, discarded chapters, proofs, tables, and correspondence documenting changing theoretical projects; a draft is a made text without necessarily being a finished work.
- **1930s–1946:** *Investment memoranda*. Reports and recommendations written for institutions Keynes advised; authorship may be individual, secretarial, or committee-based.
- **1940–46:** *Treasury office papers*. Carbon copies, annotations, calculations, briefs, and collaborative drafts preserved through bureaucratic filing. The editors warn that a genuinely complete edition would be several times the size of the thirty-volume collection.
- **Undated or uncertain:** *Untitled notes and fragments*. The archive contains texts for which no defensible title or exact date exists; naming or dating them more precisely would invent bibliographical facts.
