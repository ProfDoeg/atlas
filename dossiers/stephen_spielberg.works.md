## Works by Steven Allan Spielberg

### Amateur films and early cinematic works

- **c. 1957–58:** *Untitled train-wreck film*. An 8-mm home movie constructed from footage of Spielberg crashing his model trains; sources disagree on whether this experiment preceded or formed part of his first titled film.
- **1959:** *The Last Gun*. A nine-minute 8-mm Western made for a Boy Scout photography badge. Its title is variously remembered as *The Last Gunfight*, *The Last Shootout*, *Gunfight*, and *Gunsmog*, so even the work’s name is unstable.
- **1959–61:** *Fighter Squad*. A black-and-white amateur war film—also called *Fighter Squadron* or *Battle Squad*—combining staged material with documentary aircraft footage.
- **1961–62:** *Escape to Nowhere*. A roughly forty-minute 8-mm war film made with schoolmates; accounts date it variously to 1961 and 1962.
- **c. 1958–63:** *Untitled amateur adventure and family films*. Spielberg recalled making fifteen to twenty 8-mm films, most of which have no reliably catalogued title or date; they resist enumeration as separate surviving works.
- **1964:** *Firelight*. A self-financed, approximately 135–140-minute science-fiction feature exhibited once in a Phoenix cinema. Only fragments survive, making the obra materially incomplete.
- **1967:** *Slipstream*. An unfinished feature project about bicycle racers; production stopped when its financing was exhausted, and the surviving material has not circulated as a completed film.
- **1968:** *Amblin’*. A 35-mm short road film written and directed by Spielberg; its successful festival and industry circulation led to his Universal contract and later supplied the name of his production company.

### Television films and episodes directed

- **1969:** *Night Gallery: “Eyes.”* One segment of Rod Serling’s television-anthology pilot; it was Spielberg’s first professional directing assignment.
- **1970:** *Marcus Welby, M.D.: “The Daredevil Gesture.”* An episode of the medical drama.
- **1971:** *The Name of the Game: “L.A. 2017.”* A feature-length dystopian episode of an otherwise contemporary television series.
- **1971:** *The Psychiatrist: “The Private World of Martin Dalton.”* An episode—derived from the series’ pilot formation—of the short-lived psychiatric drama.
- **1971:** *The Psychiatrist: “Par for the Course.”* A second episode of the series directed by Spielberg.
- **1971:** *Night Gallery: “Make Me Laugh.”* A supernatural-comedy anthology segment.
- **1971:** *Columbo: “Murder by the Book.”* The first regular-series episode of *Columbo*, distinct from its earlier pilot films.
- **1971:** *Owen Marshall, Counselor at Law: “Eulogy for a Wide Receiver.”* An episode of the legal drama.
- **1971:** *Duel*. A television feature subsequently lengthened for theatrical distribution; the television and expanded cinema cuts are manifestations of the same film rather than wholly separate obras.
- **1972:** *Something Evil*. A made-for-television supernatural horror film.
- **1973:** *Savage*. A television film conceived as a potential series pilot; the contemplated series was not made.
- **1985:** *Amazing Stories: “Ghost Train.”* The premiere episode of Spielberg’s anthology series, directed by its creator.
- **1985:** *Amazing Stories: “The Mission.”* A feature-length wartime-fantasy episode directed by Spielberg from his story idea.

### Theatrical films directed

- **1974:** *The Sugarland Express*. Spielberg’s first theatrically released feature made within the Hollywood studio system; he also shares story credit.
- **1975:** *Jaws*. A feature film adapted from Peter Benchley’s novel.
- **1977:** *Close Encounters of the Third Kind*. A science-fiction feature written and directed by Spielberg; the 1980 *Special Edition* and 1998 *Director’s Cut* are substantially revised manifestations, not independent narratives.
- **1979:** *1941*. A large-scale ensemble comedy about wartime panic in Los Angeles.
- **1981:** *Raiders of the Lost Ark*. An adventure feature and the first released Indiana Jones film.
- **1982:** *E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial*. A science-fiction feature; Spielberg’s digitally altered 2002 edition was later disavowed in favor of preserving the original manifestation.
- **1983:** *Twilight Zone: The Movie—“Kick the Can.”* Spielberg directed one of the anthology feature’s four principal episodes; the whole film is collaborative and cannot accurately be assigned to him alone.
- **1984:** *Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom*. An adventure feature conceived as a prequel to *Raiders of the Lost Ark*.
- **1985:** *The Color Purple*. A feature adaptation of Alice Walker’s novel.
- **1987:** *Empire of the Sun*. A feature adaptation of J. G. Ballard’s autobiographical novel.
- **1989:** *Indiana Jones and the Last Crusade*. The third released Indiana Jones feature.
- **1989:** *Always*. A romantic-fantasy feature remaking *A Guy Named Joe*.
- **1991:** *Hook*. A fantasy feature extending the Peter Pan story rather than directly adapting one single text.
- **1993:** *Jurassic Park*. A feature adaptation of Michael Crichton’s novel and a hybrid of photographic, animatronic, and computer-generated cinema.
- **1993:** *Schindler’s List*. A historical feature adapted from Thomas Keneally’s documentary novel.
- **1997:** *The Lost World: Jurassic Park*. A sequel loosely adapted from Crichton’s second Jurassic Park novel.
- **1997:** *Amistad*. A historical courtroom drama based on the 1839 revolt aboard *La Amistad* and its legal aftermath.
- **1998:** *Saving Private Ryan*. A fictional war feature framed by historical events.
- **2001:** *A.I. Artificial Intelligence*. A science-fiction feature developed for years by Stanley Kubrick and ultimately written and directed by Spielberg. Its authorship is deliberately composite: Kubrick’s project, Brian Aldiss’s originating story, Ian Watson’s treatments, and Spielberg’s screenplay all remain perceptible.
- **2002:** *Minority Report*. A science-fiction feature adapted from Philip K. Dick’s story.
- **2002:** *Catch Me If You Can*. A biographical crime feature whose source memoir’s factual reliability has subsequently been heavily disputed.
- **2004:** *The Terminal*. A comedy-drama loosely prompted by stories of people stranded in international transit zones rather than a strict representation of one life.
- **2005:** *War of the Worlds*. A contemporary feature adaptation of H. G. Wells’s novel.
- **2005:** *Munich*. A historical-political thriller adapted from George Jonas’s *Vengeance*; the source book and aspects of the film’s reconstruction have been disputed.
- **2008:** *Indiana Jones and the Kingdom of the Crystal Skull*. The fourth Indiana Jones feature.
- **2011:** *The Adventures of Tintin*. A performance-capture animated feature combining material from several Hergé albums; Spielberg also operated the virtual camera without a separate screen credit.
- **2011:** *War Horse*. A feature derived from Michael Morpurgo’s novel and its theatrical adaptation.
- **2012:** *Lincoln*. A historical feature principally adapted from portions of Doris Kearns Goodwin’s *Team of Rivals*.
- **2015:** *Bridge of Spies*. A historical espionage feature combining the Rudolf Abel exchange with the capture and release of Francis Gary Powers.
- **2016:** *The BFG*. A feature adaptation of Roald Dahl’s children’s novel.
- **2017:** *The Post*. A historical feature about the publication of the Pentagon Papers.
- **2018:** *Ready Player One*. A feature adaptation of Ernest Cline’s novel.
- **2021:** *West Side Story*. A film musical reinterpreting the 1957 stage work rather than simply remaking its 1961 screen manifestation.
- **2022:** *The Fabelmans*. A semi-autobiographical feature co-written with Tony Kushner; it transforms Spielberg family history into fiction rather than claiming documentary status.
- **2026:** *Disclosure Day*. A science-fiction feature directed and produced by Spielberg from his story, with a screenplay by David Koepp.

### Short, civic, and commissioned moving-image works

- **1999:** *The Unfinished Journey*. A short historical film made for the millennium celebration at the Lincoln Memorial. Its ceremonial projection and limited circulation make it closer to a civic manifestation than an ordinary theatrical film.
- **2008:** *A Timeless Call*. A short documentary tribute to United States military veterans, commissioned for the Democratic National Convention.
- **2022:** *Cannibal*. A one-take music video for Marcus Mumford, directed and recorded by Spielberg on a mobile telephone; it was his first music video.

### Screen stories and scripts made for other directors

- **1973:** *Ace Eli and Rodger of the Skies*. A feature directed by John Erman from a story and initial screenplay by Spielberg; revisions and the finished film departed sufficiently from his work that his credit was reduced to “story.”
- **1982:** *Poltergeist*. A supernatural feature directed by Tobe Hooper from a story and screenplay co-written by Spielberg, who also produced it. Contemporary reports and later testimony disagree over how much practical direction Spielberg exercised, so sole directorial authorship cannot responsibly be reassigned to him.
- **1985:** *The Goonies*. A feature directed by Richard Donner from Spielberg’s story; Spielberg also reportedly performed uncredited second-unit direction and editing, making his authorship more extensive than the story credit alone but still collaborative.
- **1985:** *Amazing Stories: “The Mission.”* Spielberg supplied the story as well as directing the episode; the teleplay is credited to Menno Meyjes.
- **1985:** *Amazing Stories: “The Wedding Ring.”* An episode directed by Danny DeVito from a Spielberg story.
- **1985:** *Amazing Stories: “Ghost Train.”* Spielberg directed the episode; its teleplay and originating story are credited to Frank Deese, so it is not a Spielberg-written text.
- **1986:** *Amazing Stories: “Vanessa in the Garden.”* An episode directed by Clint Eastwood from Spielberg’s teleplay.
- **2001:** *A.I. Artificial Intelligence*. Spielberg’s screenplay reworked a long-developed Kubrick project; it belongs both to his directed cinema and to his small body of credited feature writing.
- **2022:** *The Fabelmans*. An original screenplay written by Spielberg and Tony Kushner.
- **2026:** *Disclosure Day*. Spielberg receives story credit, while David Koepp receives screenplay credit.

### Serial and institutional creations

- **1985–87:** *Amazing Stories*. A television anthology created by Spielberg. It is a serial Gesamtkunstwerk assembled through numerous writers, directors, composers, and craftspeople; Spielberg originated and produced the whole framework but personally directed only two episodes.
- **1994–present:** *USC Shoah Foundation Visual History Archive*. A continuing audiovisual testimony project initiated by Spielberg after *Schindler’s List*. It resists classification as his individual “work”: the archive is an institutional, collaborative creation whose primary makers and speakers are tens of thousands of survivors and witnesses.
- **2007:** *On the Lot*. A filmmaking-competition television format created with Mark Burnett and David Goffin; it is a serial production protocol as much as a conventional program.

### Interactive works and game projects

- **1996:** *Steven Spielberg’s Director’s Chair*. An interactive movie-making simulation in which users edit and stage filmed material; Spielberg served as designer-director, but the resulting sequences vary with the user and therefore have no single fixed form.
- **1999:** *Medal of Honor*. A historical first-person video game developed from a concept proposed by Spielberg. The finished game is a collaborative software work rather than a personally directed film.
- **2008:** *Boom Blox*. A physics-based video game conceived through Spielberg’s collaboration with Electronic Arts.
- **2009:** *Boom Blox Bash Party*. A sequel expanding the design of *Boom Blox*; Spielberg remained associated with the originating concept rather than functioning as its sole designer.
- **2005–10:** *LMNO*. An ambitious interactive science-fiction project developed with Electronic Arts and ultimately cancelled. Because no finished game appeared, it survives as a project—design work, prototypes, and intention—not as a completed public obra.
