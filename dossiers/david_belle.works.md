## Works by David Nicolas Belle

### Embodied discipline and collective projects

- **Late 1980s–1990s:** *Parkour*. An embodied movement discipline and continuing project developed at Lisses and Évry from Belle’s training, his father Raymond Belle’s teaching, and the experiments of a larger circle. Belle is often called its founder and coined or established the name, but scholarship also attributes its early grammar to Sébastien Foucan and the Yamakasi collectively; it therefore resists treatment as Belle’s sole obra. ([Thorpe and Ahmad](https://doi.org/10.1080/09523367.2023.2195632); [Angel, *Ciné Parkour*](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/6119/1/FulltextThesis.pdf); [Atkinson, “Parkour Fails and Hébertisme”](https://doi.org/10.1080/17409292.2023.2152587))

- **1990s:** *Yamakasi*. A training collective and social project formed by Belle and eight other practitioners; accounts disagree over leadership, the precise chronology, and whether “parkour,” *l’art du déplacement*, and the group’s collective practice were identical. The collective is not properly a work by Belle alone, and his departure after its early television exposure makes its authorship especially resistant to singular attribution. ([Angel, *Ciné Parkour*](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/6119/1/FulltextThesis.pdf); [Atkinson, “Parkour Fails and Hébertisme”](https://doi.org/10.1080/17409292.2023.2152587))

- **2014–:** *Mouvement International du Parkour, Freerunning et de l’Art du Déplacement (MIPFA)*. An organizational project established by Belle, Williams Belle, Châu Belle, Malik Diouf, Sébastien Foucan, and Charles Perrière on 3 September 2014 to represent and transmit related movement disciplines; it is a collective institution, not Belle’s individual obra. ([Thorpe and Ahmad](https://doi.org/10.1080/09523367.2023.2195632))

### Moving-image self-manifestation

- **1997 or 1998:** *Speed Air Man*. A self-produced promotional showreel assembling footage of Belle’s movement practice, made both to communicate parkour and to seek screen work. Sources give both 1997 and 1998, so its date is not securely fixed; although commonly called a film or video, it is closer to a performed manifesto and professional demonstration than a conventional authored short. ([Angel, *Ciné Parkour*](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=72f8b08356e73fee83f7daaace95127cd2e0c9bf&repid=rep1&type=pdf); [Söderberg, interview study](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A529582/FULLTEXT01.pdf))

### Books and protocols

- **2009:** *Parkour*. A photographic and interview-based book credited to David Belle, with text and interviews by Sabine Gros La Faige and a preface by Luc Besson. It presents Belle’s history and philosophy of the discipline, but its divided textual responsibility prevents treating every word as Belle’s sole-authored text. ([Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/affiner.do?afficheRegroup=false&critereRecherche=&index=GFRMConstr&listeAffinages=GFRMConstr_11976851_%3BFacLocal_Lcl2DRdjRich&motRecherche=&nbResultParPage=100&numNotice=11932135_&triResultParPage=0&trouveDansFiltre=NoticePUB&typeNotice=))

- **2014:** *Parkour: des origines à la pratique / From the Origins to Practice*. A bilingual instructional manual co-authored with Charles Perrière: simultaneously a history, training protocol, and statement of the discipline’s values. It is expressly collaborative rather than Belle’s sole obra. ([Cité de l’architecture et du patrimoine](https://citedelarchitecture.fr/fr/minimaousse/article/bibliographie-0); [publisher record reproduced by Decitre](https://www.decitre.fr/livres/parkour-9782851808844.html))

### Choreographies and designed action

- **2004:** *Les Rivières pourpres II: Les Anges de l’Apocalypse*. Belle’s stunt work or choreography within Olivier Dahan’s feature film. The movement contribution is his made thing; the film itself is not his obra, and sources vary between describing him as stunt performer and choreographer. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en); [AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2005:** *Le Transporteur 2 / Transporter 2*. Stunt choreography or coordination contributed to Louis Leterrier’s film. It survives only as part of a collaborative cinematic Gesamtkunstwerk and should not be mistaken for a film authored by Belle. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2008:** *Babylon A.D.* Belle devised or coordinated parkour and stunt action while also appearing on screen. Sources disagree over the exact credit—stunt coordinator, parkour-stunts coordinator, or consultant—so the attributable obra is the action design rather than the whole film. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en); [AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2009:** *Banlieue 13: Ultimatum*. Belle contributed stunt arrangement as well as the performed role of Leïto. AlloCiné credits him as *régleur cascades*, while his agency filmography lists only the acting role; the extent and formal status of his action authorship are therefore not fully settled. ([AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/); [ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2010:** *Prince of Persia: The Sands of Time*. A body of movement and action design credited variously as stunt coordination and choreography. It is an embedded choreographic contribution rather than a separately titled autonomous work. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en); [AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2011:** *Colombiana*. Parkour choreography created for Olivier Megaton’s feature. Belle made its movement sequences, not the film as a whole. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2014–2015:** *Dying Light*. Parkour choreography, motion-capture performance, stunt coordination, and movement consultation created for Techland’s video game; the agency dates the work to 2014, while the game was released in 2015. This distributed authorship across body, animation, and software makes the contribution closer to one layer of a digital Gesamtkunstwerk than a freestanding Belle work. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

### Screen and digital performances

- **2000:** *Louis Page: “Les gens du voyage”*. A television performance as Laurent. The episode is not Belle’s authored work; the made thing attributable to him is the performance. ([AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2001:** *L’Engrenage*. A screen performance as the creditor in Frank Nicotra’s film, not an independently authored Belle obra. ([AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2002:** *Femme Fatale*. A small performance as a French police officer in Brian De Palma’s film. It belongs in a performance record but resists classification as a “work by” Belle. ([AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2002:** *Intervention divine / Yadon ilaheyya*. A screen performance credited as “Marksman 1” in Elia Suleiman’s film; the film itself is Suleiman’s obra, not Belle’s. ([AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2004:** *Banlieue 13*. Belle’s leading performance as Leïto, built around his parkour vocabulary. The character and embodied action are a manifestation of his practice, though the film was directed by Pierre Morel and written and produced by others. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2006:** *Un monde meilleur*. A performance as a soldier in Igor Pejic’s short film. Sources differ slightly in naming the role, but agree that this is a performed contribution rather than a Belle-authored film. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2008:** *Babylon A.D.* A screen performance alongside Belle’s action-design contribution; role descriptions vary among sources. The performance is separable from, but entangled with, his choreography. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en); [AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2009:** *Banlieue 13: Ultimatum*. A second performance as Leïto, combined with a disputed or inconsistently recorded stunt-arrangement credit. It is a collaborative manifestation, not a solely authored Belle work. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en); [AlloCiné filmography](https://www.allocine.fr/personne/fichepersonne-93828/filmographie/))

- **2012:** *Métal Hurlant Chronicles*. A performance in one television episode, identified in filmographies as “Red Light.” The series and episode are not Belle’s obra. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2013:** *Malavita / The Family*. A performance as Mezzo in Luc Besson’s film, with Belle’s official site also describing him as a production consultant. The available professional filmography records the acting but not a distinct choreographic work, leaving the scope of the consultation unclear. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en); [official David Belle site](https://davidbelleparkour.com/fr/pages/about))

- **2014:** *Brick Mansions*. A leading performance as Lino Duppré in the English-language remake of *Banlieue 13*. Belle’s embodied movement is central to the film, but neither the film nor its screenplay is his authored obra. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2014–2015:** *Dying Light*. A motion-captured performance associated with the character Antoine Merpe, in addition to Belle’s choreography and consultation. It is a mediated bodily manifestation distributed among performer, animators, designers, and software. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2016:** *Super Express*. A screen performance in Song Xiao’s feature; the agency filmography supplies no role name. It is a performance credit, not a film authored by Belle. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2017:** *Brutal: A Taste of Violence*. A performance in the Studio+ serial production. Its exact release status and episode-level title are inconsistently reported, so it is safest treated as a screen manifestation rather than a securely bounded Belle work. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2020:** *Bronx / Rogue City*. A performance as Zach Damato in Olivier Marchal’s film. The made thing attributable to Belle is the performance, not the film. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))

- **2022:** *Dying Light 2: Stay Human*. Voice and performance work as Hakon, with motion and stunt consultation also reported in filmographic sources. It is another distributed digital manifestation rather than a freestanding Belle obra. ([ECI Talents filmography](https://www.eci.net/talent/david_belle.htm?lng=en))
