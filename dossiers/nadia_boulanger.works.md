## Works by Nadia Boulanger

### Compositions: songs and vocal cycles

- **1901:** *Extase* (NB 1). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Victor Hugo; completed 16 December. [Laederich, p. 315.]
- **1902:** *Aubade* (NB 2). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Louis Tiercelin; completed 1 April. [Laederich, p. 315.]
- **1902:** *Désespérance* (NB 3). Unpublished mélodie for voice and piano, setting Paul Verlaine; composed 2–14 April. [Laederich, p. 316.]
- **1905:** *Allons voir sur le lac d’argent* (NB 5). Unpublished mélodie for two voices and piano, setting Armand Silvestre; completed 28 February. [Laederich, p. 317.]
- **1905:** *Écoutez la chanson bien douce* (NB 7). Mélodie for voice and piano or orchestra, setting Paul Verlaine; composed October–4 December. [Laederich, p. 318.]
- **1905:** *Soleils couchants* (NB 8). Mélodie for voice and piano, with an orchestral version, setting Paul Verlaine; completed in December. [Laederich, p. 318.]
- **1906:** *Versailles* (NB 9). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Albert Samain; composed 17–21 April. [Laederich, p. 319.]
- **1906:** *Élégie* (NB 10). Mélodie for voice and piano or orchestra, setting Albert Samain; composed 9–21 April. [Laederich, p. 319.]
- **1906:** *Ilda* (NB 13). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Albert Samain; completed in August. [Laederich, p. 320.]
- **1906:** *Mon cœur* (NB 14). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Albert Samain; completed 12 August. [Laederich, p. 320.]
- **1906:** *Mon âme est une infante* (NB 15). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Albert Samain; composed 29 August–1 September. [Laederich, p. 320.]
- **1906:** *Un grand sommeil noir* (NB 16). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Paul Verlaine; completed 1 September. The title is conventionally supplied from the poem’s opening rather than securely inscribed as a title. [Laederich, p. 321.]
- **1906–1907:** *Histoire* (NB 17). Unpublished mélodie for voice and orchestra, setting Henry Bataille. [Laederich, p. 321.]
- **1907:** *Poème d’Amour* (NB 21). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Armand Silvestre; completed 26 September. [Laederich, p. 323.]
- **1908:** *O Schwöre nicht (Ne jure pas)* (NB 22). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Heinrich Heine; completed 10 March. [Laederich, p. 324.]
- **1908:** *À la mer* (NB 27). Mélodie for voice and piano extracted from *La Sirène*, setting Eugène Adenis and Gustave Desveaux-Vérité; its May–June date is approximate. [Laederich, p. 327.]
- **1908:** *Was will die einsame Thräne (Larme solitaire)* (NB 28). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Heinrich Heine; completed 1 August. [Laederich, p. 327.]
- **1908:** *Ach, die Augen sind es wieder (Mélancolie)* (NB 29). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Heinrich Heine; completed 11 September. [Laederich, p. 328.]
- **1909:** *Prière* (NB 30). Mélodie for voice and piano or orchestra, setting Henry Bataille; completed 22 January. [Laederich, p. 328.]
- **1909:** *Cantique* (NB 31). Mélodie for voice and piano or orchestra, setting Maurice Maeterlinck; completed early in the year. [Laederich, p. 329.]
- **1909:** *Les Heures claires* (NB 33). Cycle for voice and piano, setting Émile Verhaeren, jointly composed with Raoul Pugno between 13 April and 13 August; it is a genuinely collaborative obra whose individual shares cannot simply be separated. [Laederich, pp. 330–331.]
- **c. 1910:** *Heures ternes* (NB 38). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Maurice Maeterlinck; the manuscript supplies only an approximate date. [Laederich, p. 335.]
- **1910:** *Le Beau Navire* (NB 39). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Georges Delaquys; completed 21 March. [Laederich, p. 335.]
- **1910:** *La Mer* (NB 42). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Paul Verlaine; the December date is approximate. [Laederich, p. 338.]
- **before 1913:** *Chanson* (NB 46). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Georges Delaquys; its exact date is unknown. [Laederich, p. 341.]
- **1914–1915:** *Soir d’hiver* (NB 49). Mélodie for voice and piano or orchestra; unusually, Boulanger also made its poetic text. [Laederich, pp. 343–344.]
- **before 1921:** *Doute* (NB 52). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Camille Mauclair; the catalogue cannot date it more precisely. [Laederich, p. 346.]
- **before 1921:** *Au bord de la route* (NB 53). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Camille Mauclair. [Laederich, p. 346.]
- **before 1921:** *L’Échange* (NB 54). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Camille Mauclair. [Laederich, p. 346.]
- **before 1921:** *Le Couteau (populaire)* (NB 55). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Jean-François Bourguignon. [Laederich, p. 347.]
- **before 1921:** *J’ai frappé* (NB 56). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting “Jean-François Bourguignon,” the pseudonym of Renée de Marquein. [Laederich, p. 347.]
- **before 1921:** *Chanson* (NB 57). Mélodie for voice and piano, setting Camille Mauclair; it is distinct from the earlier NB 46 of the same generic title. [Laederich, pp. 347–348.]

### Compositions: choral, dramatic, and orchestral works

- **1905:** *Allegro* (NB 4). Orchestral composition completed 17 January. [Laederich, p. 316.]
- **1905:** *Les Sirènes* (NB 6). Unpublished choral work for four-part women’s chorus and orchestra, setting Charles Grandmougin; completed 18 April. [Laederich, p. 317.]
- **1906:** *À l’aube* (NB 12). Unpublished work for four-part mixed chorus and orchestra, setting Armand Silvestre; made for the Prix de Rome preliminary competition, with the dates 5–11 May reconstructed rather than certain. [Laederich, p. 320.]
- **1907:** *Soleils de septembre* (NB 19). Unpublished work for four-part mixed chorus and orchestra, setting Auguste Lacaussade; a Prix de Rome preliminary exercise dated approximately 6–10 May. [Laederich, p. 322.]
- **1907:** *Selma* (NB 20). Unpublished Prix de Rome cantata for three soloists and orchestra, setting Georges Spitzmuller; composed 18 May–17 June. [Laederich, pp. 322–323.]
- **1908:** *Deux Chœurs* (NB 23). Two unpublished unaccompanied choruses for four-part women’s voices, setting Heinrich Heine; the bracketed “Deux” is cataloguing description rather than necessarily a fixed authorial title. [Laederich, p. 324.]
- **1908:** *À l’hirondelle* (NB 25). Unpublished Prix de Rome preliminary chorus for four-part mixed voices and piano or orchestra, setting Sully Prudhomme; its 3–7 May dates are reconstructed. [Laederich, p. 325.]
- **1908:** *La Sirène* (NB 26). Prix de Rome scène musicale for three soloists and orchestra, setting Eugène Adenis and Gustave Desveaux-Vérité; composed approximately 19 May–30 June and left unpublished. [Laederich, pp. 325–327.]
- **1909:** *Soir d’été* (NB 35). Unpublished four-part mixed chorus with orchestra, made for the Prix de Rome preliminary competition; the catalogue gives an approximate completion date of 6 May. [Laederich, p. 332.]
- **1909:** *La Roussalka* (NB 36). Unpublished Prix de Rome cantata for three soloists and orchestra, setting Fernand Boissier and Eugène Adenis; composed from mid-May to 8 June. [Laederich, pp. 332–333.]
- **1909–1910:** *Dnégouchka* (NB 37). Cantata for three soloists and orchestra, or piano, setting Georges Delaquys; it is a substantial revision of *La Roussalka*, and therefore both a new manifestation and a reworking of NB 36. [Laederich, pp. 333–335.]
- **1910–1912:** *La Ville morte* (NB 41). Four-act opera on Gabriele D’Annunzio’s text, jointly composed with Raoul Pugno. It survives as an incomplete collaborative project rather than an uncomplicated finished opera: Pugno’s death interrupted preparation for performance, and the surviving sources complicate attribution of individual passages. [Laederich, pp. 336–338.]
- **c. 1920:** *Lux aeterna* (NB 32). Sacred arrangement of *Cantique* for voice, violin, cello, harp, and organ, replacing Maeterlinck’s poem with a Latin text; it is a new manifestation of NB 31 rather than wholly independent musical material. [Laederich, pp. 329–330.]

### Compositions: keyboard, chamber, and concertante works

- **1910:** *Pièce pour deux pianos* (NB 40). Unpublished two-piano work completed 25 April; the descriptive title is bracketed because no secure authorial title survives. [Laederich, p. 336.]
- **1911:** *Prélude–Petit Canon–Improvisation* (NB 43). Tripartite work for organ or harmonium, completed in September. [Laederich, pp. 338–339.]
- **1911–1913:** *Trois Pièces* (NB 45). Chamber-music set for cello and piano; its movements originated at different moments, making the collected set a later composite obra. [Laederich, pp. 340–341.]
- **1912:** *Fantaisie variée* (NB 44). Concertante work for piano and orchestra, composed 7 November–13 December and orchestrated 23–24 December. [Laederich, pp. 339–340.]
- **1914:** *Morceaux à déchiffrer* (NB 47). Two piano sight-reading pieces—one for the men’s preparatory class and one for the women’s competition at the Paris Conservatoire—published in an unidentified periodical; they are functional examination protocols as well as compositions. [Laederich, pp. 341–342.]
- **1914–1916:** *Petites Pièces* (NB 48). Group of short piano works composed over an approximate two-year span. [Laederich, pp. 342–343.]
- **1915:** *Pièce sur des airs populaires flamands* (NB 50). Organ composition based on Flemish popular tunes, completed early in the year. [Laederich, p. 344.]
- **1915:** *Vers la vie nouvelle* (NB 51). Piano work completed 19 November. Some authority records date it approximately 1916, but Laederich dates the autograph to 1915. [Laederich, pp. 344–346; BnF authority record.]

### Conservatoire exercises

- **June 1899:** *Concours d’harmonie (1899)*. Surviving harmony-competition work; it resists the autonomous “work” category because it was made as an assessed exercise. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **2 July 1900:** *Concours d’harmonie (1900)*. Harmony-competition submission and pedagogical object rather than a public composition. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **8 July 1901:** *Concours d’harmonie (1901)*. Harmony-competition submission. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **June 1902:** *Concours d’harmonie (1902)*. Harmony-competition submission; the day is unknown. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **17 January–May 1903:** *Devoirs d’harmonie (1903)*. A body of harmony assignments, not a single autonomous composition. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **May 1903:** *Concours d’harmonie (1903)*. Harmony-competition submission; the precise date is unknown. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **18 January 1904:** *Fugue on a subject by Ambroise Thomas*. Scholastic fugue identified by its supplied subject rather than by an independent title. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **5 April 1904:** *Fugue in G major*. Scholastic fugue whose key functions as its identifying description. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **24 June 1904:** *Fugue on a subject by André Gedalge*. Scholastic fugue identified by its supplied subject. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **July 1904:** *Concours de contrepoint et fugue (1904)*. Counterpoint-and-fugue competition submission; the exact day is unknown. [Laederich, pp. 348–350.]
- **5–8 May 1906:** *Fugue* (NB 11). Prix de Rome preliminary exercise on a subject by Camille Saint-Saëns; the opening date is uncertain. [Laederich, p. 319.]
- **4–5 May 1907:** *Fugue* (NB 18). Prix de Rome preliminary exercise on a subject by Théodore Dubois. [Laederich, p. 322.]
- **2–5 May 1908:** *Fugue* (NB 24). Four-string fugue on a subject by Théodore Dubois, made for the Prix de Rome preliminary competition. [Laederich, pp. 324–325.]
- **c. 5 May 1909:** *Fugue* (NB 34). Prix de Rome preliminary exercise on a subject by Théodore Dubois; its completion day is uncertain. [Laederich, p. 332.]

### Lost, unfinished, and undated projects

- **1909:** *Pour Elle*. A mélodie known from its premiere by Rodolphe Plamondon with Boulanger at the piano on 19 February; the composition has not been located, so the documented performance—not a surviving score—is its principal manifestation. [Laederich, p. 350.]
- **1910:** *Pour Toi*. A mélodie listed by SACEM and premiered by Plamondon and Boulanger on 9 December, but not subsequently located. [Laederich, p. 350.]
- **Undated:** *Fugue à quatre parties*. Surviving or documented four-part fugue without a secure date; its generic designation may be descriptive rather than a fixed title. [Laederich, pp. 350–351.]
- **Undated:** *Fugue à trois parties*. One of two separately catalogued three-part fugues bearing the same generic description. [Laederich, pp. 350–351.]
- **Undated:** *Fugue à trois parties*. A second, distinct three-part fugue; neither chronology nor a more individual title is established. [Laederich, pp. 350–351.]
- **Undated:** *Alyssa*. Unfinished cantata: a project whose incompletion is part of its surviving identity. [Laederich, pp. 350–351.]
- **Undated:** *Jean des Bois s’en va-t-en guerre*. Unfinished mélodie; no reliable completion date exists. [Laederich, pp. 350–351.]

### Transcriptions, reductions, and realizations

- **2 October 1904:** *Messidor: entracte symphonique*. Organ transcription of Alfred Bruneau’s music; it is Boulanger’s arrangement, not authorship of the underlying composition. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **1922:** *Fantaisie dans l’ambiance espagnole*. Reduction for two pianos, eight hands, of Pierre Menu’s work. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **Date unknown:** *Le Tombeau de Couperin*. Organ transcription of Maurice Ravel’s work; the manuscript has not been located, so its details remain dependent on documentary testimony. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **1933:** *Widerstehe doch der Sünde*, BWV 54. Reduction for two pianos, eight hands, of Johann Sebastian Bach’s cantata. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **1936:** *Concerto for organ and orchestra*. Arrangement attributed to Antonio Vivaldi’s music and declared to SACEM on 4 November; the catalogue treats it as a transcription, not an original Boulanger concerto. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **c. 1940:** *Symphonie en ut: Adagio*. Two-piano transcription of the slow movement from Igor Stravinsky’s symphony. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **11 June 1942:** *Bohémienne*. SACEM-declared arrangement of an otherwise unidentified work; both the underlying object and the exact nature of Boulanger’s intervention remain uncertain. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **1950:** *Musical Offering*. Realization of the bass in Bach’s work, jointly made by Dinu Lipatti and Boulanger; it is collaborative editorial-performance work, not a newly authored composition. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]
- **Undated:** *Untitled work for piano four hands*. A transcription or arrangement whose source work and title have not been identified. [Laederich, pp. 351–352.]

### Manifestations of Lili Boulanger’s works

- **Date unknown:** *Cortège*. Nadia Boulanger’s organ transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 32; an arrangement and new instrumental manifestation, not a claim to the underlying obra. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Dans l’immense tristesse*. Transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 38 for voice and organ. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Dans l’immense tristesse*. Separate transcription of LB 38 for voice, string quartet, harp, and organ. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Nocturne*. Transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 14 for small orchestra. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Pie Jesu*. Transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 43 for voice and piano. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Pie Jesu*. Separate transcription of LB 43 for small orchestra without organ. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Pour les funérailles d’un soldat*. Organ transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 26. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Psaume 24*. Transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 36 for large orchestra without organ. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Psaume 129*. Unison-chorus transcription of Lili Boulanger’s LB 37. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **Date unknown:** *Psaume 130*. Two-piano reduction of the orchestra in Lili Boulanger’s LB 39. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **1970:** *Clairières dans le ciel*. Nadia Boulanger’s revision of the voice-and-piano score of Lili Boulanger’s LB 30 for Durand; an editorial manifestation whose underlying authorship remains Lili’s. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
- **1970:** *Faust et Hélène*. Nadia Boulanger’s revision of the voice-and-piano score of Lili Boulanger’s LB 29 for Durand. [Laederich, pp. 352–353.]
