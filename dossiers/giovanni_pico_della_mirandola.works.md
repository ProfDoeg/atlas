## Works by Giovanni Pico della Mirandola

### Philosophical and theological interventions

- **1485:** *Letter to Ermolao Barbaro*. An extended humanist epistle defending scholastic philosophical language against the demands of classical eloquence; it functions as a polemical essay within Pico’s correspondence. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

- **1486:** *Letter to Lorenzo de’ Medici*. A substantial literary-philosophical epistle, conventionally treated as an independent minor work rather than merely routine correspondence. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1486:** *Conclusiones DCCCC publice disputandae* (“Nine Hundred Conclusions for Public Disputation”). Neither a continuous treatise nor simply a book: it is a printed apparatus of 900 theses—402 attributed to earlier authorities and 498 advanced under Pico’s responsibility—made simultaneously as philosophical synthesis, advertisement, challenge, and script for a disputation. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1486–1487, unrealized:** *The Roman public disputation*. This was a project and intended intellectual spectacle rather than a text: Pico invited scholars to Rome to debate the *Conclusiones*, but ecclesiastical proceedings prevented it from taking place. Its non-occurrence is part of what the work is. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

- **1486–1487:** *Oratio quaedam elegantissima*, later called *Oratio de hominis dignitate*. A projected inaugural speech for the unrealized disputation, and thus an unperformed speech rather than a manifesto actually delivered; Pico neither published nor publicly pronounced it. Two redactions survive—a lacunose earlier version and a revised Roman version probably cut by his nephew—and the familiar title “On the Dignity of Man” is posthumous. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

- **1487:** *Apologia conclusionum suarum*. A juridical-theological self-defense composed against the condemnation of thirteen theses: more an intervention in an inquisitorial proceeding than a detached philosophical treatise. Its prefatory material reuses part of the undelivered *Oratio*. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

### Commentaries and exegetical constructions

- **1485–1486:** *Commento sopra una canzona de amore composta da Girolamo Benivieni*. An Italian philosophical commentary built around another person’s love poem; Pico left it unfinished and unpublished, so it survives as an incomplete interpretive project rather than an authorized book. It was first printed posthumously in Benivieni’s works in 1519. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

- **1488–1489:** *Expositiones in Psalmos*. A projected threefold—literal, moral, and allegorical—commentary on the Psalms, now surviving only in expositions of Psalms 6, 10, 11, 15, 17, 18, 47, and 50. It resists treatment as a completed work: these are remnants of a larger lost exegetical construction, and only the exposition of Psalm 15 entered the 1496 collected edition. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1489:** *Heptaplus de septiformi sex dierum Geneseos enarratione*. A sevenfold commentary on the opening creation narrative of Genesis, combining Christian exegesis, philosophy, numerological architecture, and concealed Kabbalistic interpretation. It was one of only three works Pico himself sent to print. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1494; published posthumously before 1523:** *Expositio singularis in Orationem dominicam*. A short exposition of the Lord’s Prayer, transmitted outside the principal 1496 collection and printed at Bologna without a date shortly before 1523. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Treatises, fragments, and polemical continuations

- **Late 1490:** *De ente et uno*. A philosophical treatise-fragment intended as part of a much larger project to reconcile Plato and Aristotle. Pico did not publish it, and calling it a finished independent “book” obscures its status as one surviving piece of an unrealized concordance. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

- **1491–1492:** *Responsiones to Antonio Cittadini’s objections*. Three polemical responses extending *De ente et uno* into an exchange with the Ferrarese philosopher Antonio Cittadini. The manuscript and posthumous printed traditions do not transmit exactly the same dossier, and Cittadini’s four *Obiectiones* are not Pico’s creations. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1492–1494:** *Disputationes adversus astrologiam divinatricem*. A large attack on predictive astrology, found unfinished among Pico’s papers and edited posthumously by his nephew Gianfrancesco. Twelve books survive although Pico projected thirteen; the nephew’s intervention in preparing the text makes its final textual form—and therefore the boundary between Pico’s work and its posthumous manifestation—problematic. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

### Spiritual protocols and devotional texts

- **Before 1494:** *Duodecim regulae partim excitantes, partim dirigentes homines in spirituali pugna*. Twelve rules for Christian life: a devotional protocol meant to prompt and direct conduct, not a descriptive treatise. It is transmitted in the 1496 posthumous collection. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1494:** *Duodecim arma spiritualis pugnae, quae in promptu haberi debent cum peccandi libido mentem subdit*. Twelve “weapons” for moments of temptation: a compact spiritual protocol or mnemonic arsenal, preserved in the 1496 collection. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1494:** *Duodecim conditiones amantis*. Twelve conditions of the lover, converting the language of love into a devotional exercise. It is a sequence of spiritual maxims rather than a conventional book and survives through the 1496 collection. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Poetry

- **Mostly after 1486:** *Sonetti*. An obra consisting of more than fifty Italian sonnets rather than a securely author-arranged book: forty-five survive together in Paris, Bibliothèque nationale de France, MS Italien 1543, while others have separate transmission. Because the corpus is dispersed, its exact boundary is editorial rather than entirely authorial. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

- **Date uncertain, probably after 1486:** *Capitolo in terza rima*. An Italian poem in linked tercets preserved separately from the principal sonnet manuscript. It is an individual poetic object, but its date is not securely fixed. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Dates uncertain:** *Carmina latina*. A dispersed body of Latin poems, conventionally gathered as a corpus rather than established as a collection designed by Pico. The modern editions supplement rather than duplicate one another, which makes the limits of this obra textual and editorial. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1494:** *Deprecatoria ad Deum*. A devotional prayer in elegiac verse, hence simultaneously poem and performed prayer; it was printed in the 1496 posthumous collection. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [1519 *Omnia Opera* contents](https://sourcelibrary.org/la/book/ioannis-pici-mirandulae-omnia-opera-mirandola)

### Correspondence as an unstable obra

- **Before 1494:** *Epistulae / Lettere*. Pico made numerous Latin and vernacular letters, including philosophical essays, political messages, petitions, and familiar correspondence; collectively they form an epistolary obra, not a single authored book. Gianfrancesco’s 1496 selection was cut, censored, and arranged to support his pious portrait of Pico, while Vatican MS Capponiano 235 and later discoveries preserve excluded material, so the early printed “work” is partly the nephew’s construction. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

### Lost works and works known only by testimony

- **1493; lost:** *Quo quisque pacto per se philosophus evadat*. Pico’s Latin translation of Ibn Ṭufayl’s Arabic philosophical narrative *Ḥayy ibn Yaqẓān*. The translation is attested because Pico cites it in the *Disputationes*, but no text is presently known to survive; its title is Pico’s, not Ibn Ṭufayl’s. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date unknown; lost:** *Liber de vera temporum supputatione*. A work on the correct computation of time, cited by Pico himself in the *Disputationes*. It exists for the atlas only as an attested lost work; its completeness and exact form cannot be recovered. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date unknown; lost:** *De veritate translationis Hieronymi adversus Hebraeorum calumnias*. A defense of Jerome’s biblical translation reported by Gianfrancesco in his biography of Pico. No surviving text is identified, so even its status as completed work rather than projected work depends upon the nephew’s testimony. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date unknown; lost:** *Defensio pro septuaginta interpretibus quantum ad Psalmos attinet adversus Hebraeos*. A defense of the Septuagint translators concerning the Psalms, likewise known from Gianfrancesco’s testimony rather than a surviving text. Its title records an obra, but its extent and completion remain unknown. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1494; unrealized or fragmentary:** *Concordia between Plato and Aristotle*. Pico repeatedly projected a systematic reconciliation of the two philosophers, of which *De ente et uno* survives as a fragment. The intention is securely attested, but no complete work bearing a stable authorial title survives; it should therefore be classified as a project, not silently promoted into a lost finished book. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pico-della-mirandola/)

### Unidentified manuscript manifestations

- **Recorded in 1649; present status unknown:** *A manuscript book on Leviticus*. An inventory of the Mirandola ducal wardrobe described an autograph book by Pico “on Leviticus.” The notice does not establish whether this was an independent work, notes, or part of another exegetical project, so it resists classification as a securely identifiable obra. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Recorded in 1649; present status unknown:** *A manuscript book on the first book of Moses*. The same inventory recorded an autograph manuscript on Genesis, but the description is too imprecise to determine whether it was the *Heptaplus*, preparatory material, or another work. It is therefore evidence of a material manifestation, not firm evidence for an additional title. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Recorded in 1649; identification uncertain:** *Varia opuscula manuscripta in uno volumine*. An autograph miscellany listed in the same inventory, probably—but not demonstrably—the surviving manuscript containing the Psalm commentaries. Because the identification is conjectural, it cannot safely be counted as a separate work. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pico-giovanni-conte-della-mirandola-e-concordia_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)
