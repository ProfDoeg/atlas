## Works by James "Jamie" Dimon

### Corporate epistles

- **2000:** *Letter to Shareholders, Bank One Annual Report 2000*. Dimon’s first annual shareholder letter as Bank One’s chief executive: a signed corporate epistle and managerial account, produced with institutional assistance rather than an independently authored literary work.

- **2001:** *Letter to Shareholders, Bank One Annual Report 2001*. A signed corporate epistle embedded in Bank One’s annual report; the report itself is a collective regulatory object and should not be attributed wholly to Dimon.

- **2002:** *Letter to Shareholders, Bank One Annual Report 2002*. A signed corporate epistle. JPMorganChase’s archive identifies the heritage Bank One report but supplies older reports only on request, so public access to this item is incomplete.

- **2003:** *Letter to Shareholders, Bank One Annual Report 2003*. Dimon’s final Bank One shareholder letter before the company’s merger with JPMorgan Chase; it remains part of an institutionally produced annual report.

- **2005:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2005*. Dimon’s first letter in JPMorgan Chase’s official annual-report sequence. Its conventional title is generic, but the separately issued, signed letter is the attributable obra—not the complete annual report.

- **2006:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2006*. A signed corporate epistle combining performance accounting, strategy, and exhortation.

- **2007:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2007*. A signed corporate epistle whose account of the bank belongs to Dimon rhetorically, although its data and production are collective.

- **2008:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2008*. A crisis-era corporate epistle and public defense of the firm’s conduct during the financial collapse.

- **2009:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2009*. A corporate epistle that also functions as a policy argument about the post-crisis financial system.

- **2010:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2010*. A signed corporate epistle: part report, part strategic program, and part intervention in debates over financial regulation.

- **2011:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2011*. A corporate epistle and managerial account; like the rest of the sequence, it resists uncomplicated sole-authorship because employees supplied research, figures, and drafting support.

- **2012:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2012*. A signed corporate epistle addressing firm performance and the “London Whale” trading losses; it is simultaneously an account, defense, and corrective program.

- **2013:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2013*. A corporate epistle shaped by the firm’s legal and regulatory settlements and therefore also an exercise in institutional self-description.

- **2014:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2014*. A signed corporate epistle that expands beyond financial reporting into public-policy advocacy.

- **2015:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2015*. A corporate epistle and strategic manifesto for the bank, although “manifesto” describes its action in the corporate world rather than a formally designated genre.

- **2016:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2016*. A question-organized corporate epistle explaining the firm’s strategy, competitive position, and policy views.

- **2017:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2017*. A corporate epistle increasingly operating as a broad argument about government, growth, work, and social policy.

- **2018:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2018*. A signed corporate epistle that incorporates or republishes other writings, including the Hobson–Dimon op-ed listed separately below; those embedded texts should not be mistaken for newly made works.

- **2019:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2019*. A corporate epistle completed amid the opening phase of the COVID-19 crisis and published as an account of both institutional resilience and public obligations.

- **2020:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2020*. A corporate epistle and extensive policy manifesto addressing the pandemic, inequality, institutional competence, and American competitiveness.

- **2021:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2021*. A corporate epistle combining financial narration with a program for economic growth and geopolitical leadership.

- **2022:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2022*. A corporate epistle written after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine; it incorporates ideas—and excerpts—from Dimon’s separately published *The West Needs America’s Leadership*.

- **2023:** *Letter to Shareholders, JPMorgan Chase Annual Report 2023*. A corporate epistle marking twenty years since the Bank One–JPMorgan Chase merger and presenting a retrospective account of the resulting institution.

- **2024:** *Chairman and CEO Letter to Shareholders, Annual Report 2024*. Dated April 7, 2025, this is a corporate epistle, strategic account, and policy intervention; its year is the reporting year rather than its publication year.

- **2025:** *Jamie Dimon’s Letter to Shareholders, Annual Report 2025*. Published in 2026, this corporate epistle explicitly describes the annual letter as work undertaken by Dimon “and by many people in the company,” making its collaborative production unusually explicit.

### Co-authored public arguments

- **2018:** *Short-Termism Is Harming the Economy*. An opinion essay co-authored with Warren E. Buffett, published in *The Wall Street Journal* on June 7, 2018; it is a manifesto-like intervention urging public companies to reduce or abandon quarterly earnings guidance.

- **2019:** *Black Americans Are Still Worse Off Financially. Businesses Can Help*. An opinion essay co-authored with Mellody Hobson for CNN Business and subsequently incorporated into Dimon’s 2018 annual-report letter. It is both diagnosis and corporate-action manifesto; the dual publication does not create two distinct works.

### Sole-authored public arguments

- **2017:** *Let’s Get to Work*. A short signed essay introducing JPMorgan Chase’s inclusive-growth program. Because it appears within an institutionally produced corporate-responsibility publication, it is an attributable contribution rather than a freestanding book or report.

- **2022:** *The West Needs America’s Leadership: How the U.S. Can Marshal Its Strengths—Not Only Military but Also Moral, Economic and Diplomatic*. A *Wall Street Journal* opinion essay and geopolitical manifesto advocating coordinated Western military, economic, diplomatic, and moral leadership; its later reproduction or quotation in the annual report is a manifestation of the same text.

- **2024:** *The Next President Must Restore Our Faith in America*. A signed *Washington Post* opinion essay published August 2, 2024. It is an electoral-season policy manifesto rather than a campaign platform: Dimon prescribes a governing program without presenting himself as a candidate.

- **2026:** *From the Desk of Jamie Dimon: Policy Matters*. A signed introductory essay launching JPMorganChase’s “From the desk of” policy forum. It is both a policy argument and a performative institutional act: the text creates the recurring forum it announces.

- **2026:** *A Plan for the Western World’s Revival*. A signed *Wall Street Journal* opinion essay published September 28, 2026. It is a geopolitical-economic manifesto proposing a renewed transatlantic trade and strategic compact.

### Institutional projects associated with Dimon

- **2000–present:** *The annual shareholder-letter project*. The individual letters above also form an unfinished serial project: Dimon describes the recurring exercise as a means by which he, management, employees, and communities analyze what the company is trying to accomplish. The project is his in an executive and rhetorical sense, but its research and production are expressly collaborative.

- **2025–2035:** *Security and Resiliency Initiative*. A JPMorganChase project announced under Dimon’s leadership to facilitate or finance $1.5 trillion in industries considered important to economic security, including up to $10 billion in direct equity and venture-capital investment. It resists classification as Dimon’s personal obra: he announced and chairs aspects of it, but the maker and implementing subject is the corporation.

- **2026–present:** *From the desk of*. An ongoing JPMorganChase public-policy forum introduced by Dimon for writings from the firm’s senior leaders. It is a project rather than a single authored text, and it cannot properly be counted as Dimon’s sole creation merely because his inaugural essay established it.

### Scope and attribution note

- **No fixed date:** *No standard scholarly bibliography or collected-works edition is known*. The closest authoritative corpus is JPMorganChase’s official annual-report archive, supplemented by the SEC’s filed Bank One and JPMorgan Chase reports and the original publications of signed essays. Speeches, interview transcripts, congressional testimony, earnings-call answers, press-release quotations, internal memoranda, deal documents, and corporate products are not counted as authored works merely because Dimon spoke, signed in an official capacity, or supervised the institution that produced them; no authoritative catalogue establishes such material as a complete personal oeuvre.
