## Works by Ahmad al-Majriti

### Mathematical and astronomical obras of Maslama ibn Aḥmad al-Majrīṭī

- *Kitāb fī thimār al-ʿadad*. A practical-arithmetic treatise whose title may be rendered “Book on the Fruits of Number”; no secure composition date is supplied by the standard work-list. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- *Abwāb lā yastaghnī man yarūmu ʿamal al-asṭurlāb ʿanhā wa-istashaltuhā min al-shakl al-qaṭṭāʿ*. A protocol-like set of indispensable propositions for constructing an astrolabe, derived from the geometry of the transversal figure; its date is not securely known. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- *Fī ʿamal al-asṭurlāb wa-l-ʿamal bihi*. A practical treatise or protocol on making and using the astrolabe; it is catalogued independently, although its precise relationship to the preceding astrolabe text is not settled in the consulted work-list. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- *Taʿlīq ʿalā kitāb Baṭlamyūs fī tasṭīḥ basīṭ al-kura*. A commentary and revision of the Arabic *Planisphaerium* of Ptolemy, not simply a translation: it adds improved procedures for drawing an astrolabe’s fundamental lines and locating the stars of its rete. ([Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm); [ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- *Revision of Thābit ibn Qurra’s work on the figure of secants*. A geometrical revision or body of notes employing Menelaus’s theorem; no independent fixed title or date is preserved in the standard work-list, so the descriptive title should not be mistaken for an authorial one. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599); [Casulleras](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

- *Risālat masqaṭ al-nuṭfa wa-kayfiyyat taʾthīr al-kawākib fī al-mawlūd min Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ*. An astrological epistle concerning conception and stellar influence at birth, transmitted in relation to the *Epistles of the Brethren of Purity*; whether it is an original composition, an excerpt, or a recension is not made secure by the catalogue record. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- *Astronomical treatises from the Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ*. A selection or recension rather than an independently conceived book; the catalogue’s supplied descriptive title signals that the boundaries and original title of this thing remain uncertain. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- *Revision of the astronomical and astrological treatises of the Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ*. A redactional project applied to inherited texts. It may overlap with the preceding selection, and therefore resists confident counting as a separate obra even though the scholarly database records it separately. ([ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- **Late tenth century:** *Revision of al-Khwārizmī’s Sindhind zīj* (also called the *Zīj of al-Khwārizmī–Maslama*). A collaborative astronomical handbook and table-making project with Ibn al-Ṣaffār: inherited tables were recalculated for Córdoba, the Persian solar calendar was replaced by the Islamic lunar calendar, and new or substituted chronological, trigonometric, astronomical, and astrological tables were incorporated; al-Khwārizmī’s original is lost, while this recension survives through a twelfth-century Latin translation. ([Casulleras](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm); [ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

### Observational and pedagogical projects

- **979:** *Observation of Regulus and recalculation of stellar longitudes*. This was an observational result rather than a book: al-Majrīṭī measured Regulus at an ecliptic longitude of 135°40′ and used it to establish revised longitudes for the fixed stars and a precession value of 13°10′ relative to Ptolemy’s catalogue; the resulting star table entered his *Planisphaerium* commentary. ([Casulleras](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

- **Late tenth–early eleventh century:** *The Córdoba school of mathematical astronomy*. A pedagogical project and intellectual lineage rather than a bounded text: al-Majrīṭī joined the mathematical traditions of inheritance calculation and the philosophical exact sciences and trained a multigenerational group that included Ibn al-Samḥ, Ibn al-Ṣaffār, and Ibn Bargūth. Calling the school solely his “work” risks turning collective teaching and reception into an individual obra. ([Casulleras](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm); [ISMI](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/person/50599))

- **1006–1007:** *Forecast attached to the conjunction of Saturn and Jupiter*. An astrological pronouncement predicting dynastic change, destruction, slaughter, and famine; it is an attributed act of professional practice, not a securely identified surviving text. ([Casulleras](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

### Obras of Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī carried under al-Majrīṭī’s name

- **950–953:** *Rutbat al-ḥakīm*. An alchemical treatise and experimental protocol, composed before the *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm*. Manuscripts and centuries of bibliography attached it to Maslama ibn Aḥmad al-Majrīṭī, but Maribel Fierro reassigned it to the earlier traditionist and esoteric scholar Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī (905/906–964); subsequent manuscript research has strongly supported, though not rendered logically unassailable, that attribution. ([Fierro, 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/1595996); [Kacimi, 2013](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=10012935); [de Callataÿ, survey](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=ast-002%3A2017%3A71%3A%3A1470))

- **954–959:** *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm* (“The Aim of the Sage”; later Latin *Picatrix*). A four-book compilation of astral theory, talismanic operations, prayers, images, substances, and inherited occult authorities—simultaneously an encyclopedic obra and a set of protocols. Medieval attribution made “al‑Majrīṭī” its author, but modern scholarship variously calls the maker anonymous or pseudo-al‑Majrīṭī and, following Fierro, usually identifies Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī; the work’s stated completion in 348 AH/959 makes attribution to the astronomer born around 950 chronologically untenable. ([Fierro, 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/1595996); [Pingree’s critical Latin edition, Warburg Institute](https://commons.warburg.sas.ac.uk/downloads/8g84mm241); [Kacimi, 2013](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=10012935))

### Attribution-manifestation: the name in transit

- **After 1360–2026:** *“Aḥmad al-Majrīṭī” as maker of the Picatrix corpus*. This is not another obra and probably not one historical person: the name is a durable attribution-manifestation produced by manuscript copying, Ibn Khaldūn’s influential report, Latin transmission, bibliography, and modern cataloguing. It conflates Maslama ibn Aḥmad al-Majrīṭī with Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī and sometimes turns the patronymic “ibn Aḥmad” into the author’s given name; scholarship since 1996 has largely dismantled the identification, while catalogues and editions continue to reproduce it. ([Fierro, 1996](https://doi.org/10.2307/1595996); [de Callataÿ and Moureau’s manuscript reassessment](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315634461_De_nuevo_sobre_Maslama_Ibn_Qasim_al-Qurtubi_los_Ijwan_al-Safa%27_e_Ibn_Jaldun_Nuevos_datos_de_dos_manuscritos_de_la_Rutbat_al-hakim); [Spiritus Mundi attribution dossier](https://spiritusmundi.com/en/actores/maslama-al-mayriti))
