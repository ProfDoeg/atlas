## Works by Peter von Cornelius

### Monumental paintings and fresco projects

- **1806–1808:** *Decoration of the choir and dome of St. Quirinus, Neuss*. A cycle in glue paint depicting Moses, Isaiah, Jeremiah, apostles, and angels; damaged by damp and overpainted in 1865, so Cornelius’s work no longer survives in an independently legible state.

- **1809–1811:** *Mythological decorations for the Schmitt–Mumm house, Frankfurt*. A room-sized oil-or-tempera cycle, now lost; seven surviving or historically recorded designs included *Demeter Giving the Grain of Wheat to Triptolemus*.

- **1814:** *Justice and Strength Crowned by the Genius of Victory*. A collaborative transparent festival picture celebrating the capture of Paris: Friedrich Overbeck painted Justice and Cornelius Strength. It is no longer extant and resists treatment as Cornelius’s autonomous work.

- **1815–1818:** *The Story of Joseph in Egypt (Casa Bartholdy cycle)*. Cornelius contributed *Joseph Interpreting Pharaoh’s Dreams* and *Joseph Recognized by His Brothers* to the collaborative fresco room in Rome; the frescoes were detached in 1887 and transferred to Berlin. Because several Nazarene artists made the room, the ensemble is closer to a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk than a single-author painting.

- **1817–1819:** *Dante’s Paradise for the Villa Massimo*. Designs and cartoons for a projected ceiling painting based on Dante; Cornelius left Rome before execution, and Philipp Veit painted the room from different compositions. The surviving designs—not the executed ceiling—are Cornelius’s unfinished project.

- **1818:** *Festival decorations for Crown Prince Ludwig at the Villa Schultheiss*. Designs for three transparent pictures—an allegory of the arts, the old masters, and patrons of art—with predella scenes of Jericho, Hercules cleansing the Augean stables, and Samson fighting the Philistines. The original drawings are lost, and the temporary painted manifestation was executed collectively by artists in Rome.

- **1820–1830:** *Fresco cycle of the Munich Glyptothek*. A monumental mythological Gesamtkunstwerk comprising the Hall of the Gods and Hall of the Trojan Heroes, designed by Cornelius and executed partly by him and partly by Clemens Zimmermann, Joseph Schlotthauer, Eugen Neureuther, and other pupils. The frescoes were destroyed during the Second World War; cartoons and other designs preserve portions of the project.

- **1820–1826:** *Hall of the Gods*. The first Glyptothek subcycle comprised three large lunettes and thirty-two smaller ceiling pictures organizing water, earth, air, fire, dawn, day, evening, and night around scenes of the Olympian gods. Individual execution was divided among Cornelius and his workshop, so authorship belongs variously to design, cartoon, and painted surface.

- **1826–1830:** *Hall of the Trojan Heroes*. The second Glyptothek subcycle traced the Trojan story from the marriage and abduction of Helen through the war and destruction of Troy, with Prometheus and Pandora imagery at the room’s ends. Cornelius supplied the program and designs, while pupils painted much of it.

- **1827–1836; executed 1831–1840:** *History of Art in the Loggias of the Alte Pinakothek*. Forty-eight designs forming paired Italian and northern-European histories of art, from the union of religion and the arts through Raphael, Dürer, Rubens, and their predecessors. Clemens Zimmermann executed the frescoes, making Cornelius’s obra the governing project and drawings rather than every painted mark.

- **1830–1840:** *Fresco decoration of the Ludwigskirche, Munich*. A church-wide theological Gesamtkunstwerk extending from Creation through the action of the Holy Spirit and Redemption to the Last Judgment. Cornelius designed the program and principal cartoons, painted the vast *Last Judgment* himself, and delegated or shared many other sections with assistants.

- **1836–1839:** *The Last Judgment*. The monumental altar-wall fresco in the Ludwigskirche, painted personally by Cornelius from a cartoon made in Rome in 1834–1835. Preparatory outline and watercolor versions also survive as distinct works.

- **1836–1839:** *God the Father as Creator and Sustainer of the World*. The high-choir ceiling composition, flanked by Gabriel and Michael; assistants transferred and painted it from Cornelius’s connected cartoons.

- **1836–1840:** *The Governance of the Holy Spirit*. The Ludwigskirche transept-vault program of prophets, patriarchs, apostles, martyrs, doctors, founders, evangelists, and other witnesses. Some cartoons were drawn by Cornelius, others were developed by assistants from his sketches, and several are lost.

- **1831–1836:** *Christ on the Cross*. A monumental cartoon drawn in Rome for the Ludwigskirche and subsequently translated into fresco; the distinction between Cornelius’s cartoon and the workshop’s wall painting is essential.

- **1833–1836:** *Adoration of the Magi and Shepherds*. A monumental Ludwigskirche cartoon and its fresco realization, accompanied on the wall by Annunciation panels painted by Hermann.

- **1843:** *Frederick William III and Queen Louise Laying Their Crowns before Christ*. A semicircular design intended for the apse of the Charlottenburg mausoleum. It remained a project; Karl Gottfried Pfannschmidt’s eventual fresco retained only its general premise and is not Cornelius’s work.

- **1843–1844:** *Seven cartoons for the windows of the princely crypt in Schwerin Cathedral*. Colored oil-on-paper designs representing Moses, Peter, Mary, the Ascension, John, Paul, and Isaiah. Ernst Gillmeister executed the stained glass, so Cornelius made the cartoons and iconographic project, not the glass objects alone.

- **1843–1866:** *Campo Santo or Royal Burial Hall at Berlin Cathedral*. Cornelius’s largest project: a four-wall Christian cycle of Incarnation, Redemption, the Church, Judgment, the Beatitudes, and works of mercy. The building and frescoes were never realized; preliminary schemes and monumental cartoons are therefore the completed manifestations of an unfinished architectural-pictorial Gesamtkunstwerk.

- **1843–1845:** *First four-wall scheme for the Campo Santo*. Small-scale outline drawings for the east, west, south, and north walls, now associated with Weimar. Cornelius revised some proposed subjects when preparing the later cartoons, so this scheme records one state of the project rather than a fixed final program.

- **1846:** *The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse*. Monumental Campo Santo cartoon; formerly in Berlin’s Nationalgalerie and reported missing after 1945. Its status is both autonomous cartoon and component of an unrealized fresco cycle.

- **1847:** *The Seven Angels with the Bowls of Wrath*. Lunette cartoon paired with *The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse* in the Campo Santo scheme.

- **1847:** *Visiting Prisoners, Comforting Mourners, and Guiding the Lost*. Predella cartoon of works of mercy for the Campo Santo’s north wall.

- **1848:** *Feeding the Hungry and Giving Drink to the Thirsty*. Campo Santo predella cartoon.

- **1848:** *Blessed Are Those Who Hunger and Thirst for Righteousness*. Monumental niche-group cartoon; Cornelius also produced a small oil version in 1851, with underpainting by F. Schubert.

- **1849:** *The Descent of the New Jerusalem*. Monumental Campo Santo cartoon.

- **1849:** *The Fall of Satan into the Abyss*. Campo Santo lunette cartoon accompanying *The Descent of the New Jerusalem*.

- **1851:** *The Resurrection of the Flesh*. Monumental Campo Santo cartoon.

- **1851–1852:** *The Fall of Babylon*. Monumental Campo Santo cartoon, completed in 1852 after a prolonged design process.

- **1853–1856:** *The Expectation of the Last Judgment*. Finished tempera design for a colossal apse fresco in the projected Berlin Cathedral. The cathedral scheme was abandoned, so this is an unrealized project whose surviving design functions as its obra.

- **1857:** *Christ the Judge*. Campo Santo lunette cartoon associated with *The Fall of Babylon*.

- **1857:** *Clothing the Naked and Sheltering the Homeless*. Campo Santo predella cartoon.

- **1859–1860:** *The Appearance of God the Father with the Symbols of the Evangelists*. Campo Santo lunette cartoon.

- **1860–1861:** *Caring for the Sick and Burying the Dead*. Campo Santo predella cartoon.

- **1860–1861:** *Blessed Are Those Persecuted for Righteousness*. Monumental Campo Santo niche-group cartoon.

- **1862–1863:** *The Parable of the Wise and Foolish Virgins*. Auxiliary cartoon for the central Judgment composition on the Campo Santo’s north wall.

- **1863–1865:** *The Incredulity of Thomas*. Campo Santo niche-group cartoon.

- **1865–1866:** *The Descent of the Holy Spirit*. Auxiliary cartoon for a Campo Santo niche group and one of Cornelius’s last works.

### Easel paintings

- **1803:** *Polyphemus in His Cave*. Grisaille competition painting for the Weimar Prize; lost.

- **1804:** *Humankind Assailed by the Element of Water*. Black-and-white ink cartoon submitted for the Weimar Prize; lost after passing into private ownership.

- **1804:** *The Fourteen Holy Helpers*. A pair of paintings, each showing seven saints with two angels, commissioned for the infirmary chapel at Essen.

- **1808 or 1809:** *Pallas Athena as Inventor of Weaving*. Oil painting; its exact year is not securely fixed.

- **1809:** *Psyche Returning from the Dark Earthly Realm*. Memorial oil painting commissioned after the sudden death of a child; the identification of the ascending child as Psyche is interpretive rather than a firmly documented original title.

- **1809–1811:** *The Holy Family*. Painting made for Karl Theodor von Dalberg, showing the young John offering grapes to Christ while an angel plays a harp.

- **1809–1811:** *Portrait of the Art Dealer Willmann*. Life-size portrait.

- **1809–1811:** *Portrait of Mrs. Willmann*. Life-size pendant portrait.

- **1809–1811:** *Portrait of Mrs. J. D. Scheel, née Silbermann*. Life-size portrait.

- **1809–1811:** *Portrait of Gottfried Malss*. Bust-length portrait.

- **1813–1818:** *The Wise and Foolish Virgins*. Large but unfinished oil painting, conceived in or about 1813 and worked on principally in Florence. Its incompleteness is part of the object’s historical identity rather than grounds for excluding it.

- **1818 or 1819:** *The Entombment*. Small oil on wood based on Cornelius’s related outline drawing.

- **about 1818:** *The Flight into Egypt*. Small collaborative oil: Cornelius painted the figures and Joseph Anton Koch the landscape.

- **1815–1822:** *The Three Marys at the Tomb*. Commissioned oil painting; its cartoon was reportedly complete by 1815, but the finished picture was not delivered until 1822.

- **1840–1843:** *Christ in Limbo*. Oil painting commissioned by Count Atanazy Raczyński and completed in Berlin.

- **1851:** *Blessed Are Those Who Hunger and Thirst for Righteousness*. Small oil realization of the Campo Santo niche composition; F. Schubert supplied the underpainting, making its execution collaborative.

- **1858:** *Pietà: Mourning over the Body of Christ*. Tempera painting based on the corresponding Campo Santo composition.

- **1859:** *Hagen Sinking the Nibelung Treasure in the Rhine*. Oil painting developed from Cornelius’s 1855 ink drawing.

### Independent drawings, cartoons, and lithographs

- **1804–1808:** *Theseus and Pirithous in the Underworld*. Known in a large sepia competition drawing and a separate pen version; these are related but materially distinct objects.

- **1804–1808:** *Jacob Blessing Joseph’s Sons*. Pen drawing.

- **1804–1808:** *Brutus Ordering the Execution of His Sons*. Wash drawing.

- **1804–1808:** *Head of a Greek Hero (Odysseus?)*. Pencil outline; the identification as Odysseus is uncertain.

- **1804–1808:** *Anchises Refusing to Flee with Aeneas*. Sepia drawing.

- **1804–1808:** *Portrait of a Man*. The sitter’s identity is disputed: Alfred von Wolzogen called him Cornelius’s father, while Herman Riegel identified him as the owner’s father.

- **1809–1811:** *Saint Michael Casting Down the Dragon*. Pen-outline design commissioned by Dalberg.

- **1809–1811:** *The Guardian Angel Leading a Child to Church*. Pen-outline design commissioned by Dalberg.

- **1809–1811:** *Portrait of a Lady in a Straw Hat*. Pencil drawing.

- **1809–1811:** *A Knight Confronting Two Armed Men*. Pen-outline drawing without a securely established narrative title.

- **1811:** *Journey in the Taunus*. An illustrated travel manuscript comprising fifty-six written pages and six drawings—an apparition, river bathing, a fight, crossing a brook, and two portraits. It is simultaneously travel text, sketchbook, and personal record rather than simply a “book.”

- **1811–1819:** *Romeo and Juliet drawings*. A six-sheet group comprising paired treatments of Romeo’s farewell, Juliet apparently dead, and Romeo’s death; some sheets are finished compositions and others outlines or unfinished studies.

- **1811–1819:** *Departure for the Flight into Egypt*. Two related drawings, one partly sketched and one lightly outlined for execution.

- **1811–1819:** *The Entombment*. Several independent pen, pencil, and gold-heightened treatments survive or are recorded; identical subject matter does not make them editions of one object.

- **about 1815:** *Pietà*. Pen-and-sepia composition of the mourners around Christ, with a separate outline version.

- **1811–1819:** *The Descent from the Cross*. Unfinished pencil outline.

- **1812 or 1813:** *The Arrest of Christ*. Drawing.

- **1812–1819:** *Christ with Eight Disciples at the House of Mary and Martha*. Pen-outline drawing.

- **1812–1819:** *Paul Taking Leave of the Ephesians at Miletus*. Drawing known in at least two versions; one occupies the reverse of an *Entombment* sheet.

- **1812:** *Double portrait of Friedrich Overbeck and Peter Cornelius*. A collaborative drawing: Cornelius drew Overbeck, while Overbeck drew Cornelius. It resists single authorship.

- **1815:** *Eros Instructing Erato*. Pen drawing, later repeated by Cornelius in wash around 1843.

- **1811–1819:** *Allegory of Tyrol*. Pen outline representing Tyrol between personifications of Church and State.

- **1818:** *Madonna of the Rose*. Cornelius’s autograph lithograph, rather than merely a reproductive print after his design.

- **1818–1819:** *Portrait of an Italian Country Girl*. Pencil drawing.

- **1818–1819:** *Portrait of the Painter Carl Philipp Fohr*. Pencil drawing.

- **1818–1819:** *View of Santi Giovanni e Paolo, Rome*. Unfinished pencil outline.

- **mid-1820s:** *Portrait of Cornelius’s First Wife and Their Two Daughters*. Pencil family portrait.

- **about 1830:** *Three Small Portraits*. A sheet or group of drawings, one portrait identifying the sculptor Konrad Eberhard.

- **about 1830:** *Portrait of Sulpiz Boisserée*. Drawing.

- **1830:** *Venus Anadyomene; Pygmalion; Prometheus; Birth of Athena*. Four ceiling designs for the Thorvaldsen festival in Rome, executed as decorations by younger artists; the present location of Cornelius’s drawings was already unknown to the historical catalogue.

- **1841:** *The Seasons*. Calendar-title drawing representing the year through Christmas, Easter, Midsummer, and harvest festivals.

- **1842:** *Antigone with the Urn at the Household Altar*. Pencil drawing subsequently adapted by Karl Pfeuffer for a commemorative medal.

- **1843:** *Germania Victorious over Discord*. Pencil design commemorating the supposed thousandth anniversary of the German realm; adapted by Karl Fischer for a medal.

- **1843:** *Six Scenes from Tasso’s Jerusalem Delivered*. Outline designs made for tableaux vivants at a Berlin court festival. Their intended manifestation was performed living pictures, not a conventional illustrated book.

- **1847:** *The Shield of Faith*. Complex outline design for a silver christening shield presented by Frederick William IV to the Prince of Wales; August Fischer modeled the physical shield. Cornelius’s obra is the iconographic and linear design, while the finished ceremonial object is collaborative.

- **1850:** *Eight Martyr Saints*. Pencil-outline series representing James of Jerusalem, Polycarp, Ignatius, Stephen, Justin, Boniface, Kilian, and Cecilia, made to assist Heinrich Lengerich’s chapel paintings.

- **1850:** *The Coronation of the Virgin*. Design for a five-part stained-glass window in Aachen Cathedral; the royal stained-glass workshop translated it into glass.

- **1852:** *Saint Elizabeth of Thuringia Showing Her Husband a Sick Man She Has Nursed*. Wash drawing.

- **1852:** *Peter and Simon Magus in Samaria*. Wash drawing.

- **1853:** *The Baptism of Christ in the Jordan*. Pencil design translated by Wilhelm Kullrich into a relief on a gold baptismal ewer; the drawing’s later location is unrecorded.

- **1855:** *Hagen Sinking the Nibelung Treasure in the Rhine*. Ink drawing made for the *Rheinlandsalbum* and later reworked as an oil painting.

- **date not firmly recorded:** *Lady Macbeth Sleepwalking*. Wash drawing showing Lady Macbeth observed by the physician and gentlewoman.

- **1859:** *Faust and Margaret in Martha’s Garden (“Do You Believe in God?”)*. Wash drawing revisiting Cornelius’s early Faust imagery.

- **1860:** *Christ Healing the Woman with an Issue of Blood*. Wash drawing.

- **date unknown, probably 1811–1816:** *Holy Family*. Pencil-outline drawing; the proposed dating is Riegel’s inference rather than a documented date.

- **date unknown:** *Head of Christ*. Cartoon whose date is not fixed in the historical work catalogue.

### Illustration cycles and sequences

- **1811–1815:** *Twelve Drawings to Goethe’s Faust*. Pen-drawing cycle conceived as an autonomous sequence and published in engravings in 1816. Besides the twelve canonical sheets, Cornelius made unengraved variants and at least nineteen recorded studies; the prints are reproductive manifestations, not interchangeable with the drawings.

- **1811–1815:** *Gretchen with Faust and Mephistopheles at Her Door*. Unengraved pen drawing related to the *Faust* cycle.

- **1811–1815:** *Gretchen and Lieschen at the Well*. Unengraved pen drawing related to the *Faust* cycle.

- **1812–1813:** *Twelve Drawings for Amalie von Helwig’s Taschenbuch der Sagen und Legenden*. Designs translated into engravings by several printmakers; Cornelius’s original drawings are lost.

- **1812–1817:** *Seven Drawings for the Nibelungenlied*. Pen-drawing cycle comprising a dedication title, the queens’ greeting, Hagen’s deceit, Siegfried’s farewell, Siegfried capturing a bear, Siegfried’s death, and Kriemhild discovering his body. Engraved publication made the cycle public, but the drawings remain the primary obra.

- **1811–1819:** *Departure for the Saxon War*. Nibelungen outline drawing known through a lithographic reproduction; the original is lost.

- **1811–1819:** *The Nibelungs’ Journey on the Danube*. Incompletely finished pencil outline, accompanied historically by a related sketch of Hagen and the Danube nixies.

### Designs for medals and ceremonial objects

- **1844:** *Portrait of Duke Albert of Prussia*. Design for a commemorative medal marking his foundation of the Albertina at Königsberg; its existence is reported by Wolzogen, while the historical catalogue supplies no documented present location.

- **1847:** *The Genius of Prussia and the Four Estates*. Two pencil designs for a medal commemorating the first United Diet of Prussia; their whereabouts are unknown.

- **1847:** *The Genius of Science Unveiling Ephesian Artemis*. Pencil design used by Karl Fischer for the reverse of a medal honoring Humboldt’s *Kosmos*.

- **1848:** *Cronus Conquered by Eros*. Pencil design adapted for a medal commemorating the silver wedding of Frederick William IV and Queen Elisabeth.

- **1849:** *Saint Michael Casting the Bound Dragon into the Abyss*. Pencil design used by Wilhelm Kullrich for a medal celebrating the suppression of the uprisings in Baden and the Palatinate.

- **1849:** *Goethe*. Drawing for an unrealized medal celebrating the centenary of Goethe’s birth; the finished drawing is lost, although a sketch was recorded.

- **1850:** *The Protecting Goddess of Prussia on a Rising Eagle*. Design for an industrial-award medal, known from Wolzogen’s record.

- **1850:** *Athena and Hephaestus Joining Hands beneath Tyche*. Pencil design used by Karl Pfeuffer for an industrial-award medal; the finished drawing is lost, though a sketch was recorded.

- **1851:** *Athena Entrusting the Helm of the Ship of State to the Genius of Count Brandenburg*. Pencil design translated by Karl Fischer into a medal reverse; its location is unknown.

- **1853:** *Eros Leading the Bridegroom to the Seated Bride*. Pencil design used by Wilhelm Kullrich for a medal celebrating the marriage of Princess Anna of Prussia and Prince Frederick of Hesse.
