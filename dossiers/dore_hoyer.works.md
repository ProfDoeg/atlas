## Works by Dore Hoyer

### Solo dances, suites, and cycles

- **1933:** *Afrikanisches Kriegerlied*. Solo choreography to music by Samuel Coleridge-Taylor, premiered in Hoyer’s first independent dance programme on 30 March 1933; the programme misspelled the composer’s name. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Vierteilige Studie*. Four-part solo study to music by Granville Bantock, premiered in the first programme. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Stiller Tanz*. Solo to music by Erik Satie, premiered in the first programme; it should not be confused with the later *Stille*. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Drei Gesichte*. Three-part solo sequence to music by Fidelio F. Finke. “Gesichte” here means visions or inwardly seen images, not portraits. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Ballade*. Solo choreography to music by Finke. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Gotisches Lied*. Solo to Satie’s music; the title apparently follows the musical source rather than naming a narrative subject devised by Hoyer. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1932–1933:** *Zwei ernste Gesänge*. Two dances composed with Peter Cieslak, who wrote the music in December 1932; Hoyer retained them in her second programme and later judged them among the few dances of her first programme to have lasting value. ([Peter, ch. 3](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4); [German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/))
- **1933:** *Tanz in Weiß*. Solo named for its white costume rather than for a literary subject, with music by Cieslak. ([Peter, ch. 3](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Tanz in Schwarz*. Companion solo named for its black costume, with music by Cieslak. ([Peter, ch. 3](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Allegro ritmico*. Abstract solo identified by musical tempo and character, to music by Engel; repeated in Hoyer’s second programme. ([Peter, pp. 83–84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Masken I–V*. Five-part solo cycle to music by Gian Francesco Malipiero. Parts of *Masken* remained in Hoyer’s repertory in 1936, but the surviving programme evidence leaves the exact selection uncertain. ([Peter, pp. 84, 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4); [Peter, ch. 6](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1933:** *Drei stille Tänze*. Three-part Satie suite in Hoyer’s second programme, probably related to but not safely identical with the earlier singular *Stiller Tanz*. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Flackernd*. Solo to music by Cieslak. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Fragment*. Solo to music by Cieslak; its title marks it as a fragmentary form, not necessarily an unfinished project. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1933:** *Barlumi*. Five-part Malipiero cycle whose Italian title means glimmers or flashes. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1935:** *Fünf namenlose Tänze*. Five deliberately unnamed dances to music by Cieslak. The third programme survives only through reviews, so this reconstructed collective description resists treatment as five securely titled works. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1935:** *Zweite stille Suite*. Three-part Satie suite; “second” relates it to Hoyer’s earlier quiet dances, but the surviving evidence does not establish exact choreographic continuity. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1935:** *Sieben Gesichte*. Seven-part sequence to music by Finke. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1935:** *Wechselnde Lichter*. Solo or multipart choreography to music by Malipiero. ([Peter, p. 84](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/03_kap3.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=4))
- **1936:** *Treibend*. Abstract solo originally performed with noise accompaniment; Wiatowitsch subsequently composed music for it. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1936:** *Ranken*. Solo to music by Alexander Scriabin. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1936:** *Gesichte I–V*. Five-part Scriabin cycle, distinct from the 1933 and 1935 sequences carrying related titles; it remained in Hoyer’s repertory through 1939 and reappeared after the war. ([Peter, pp. 187–189, 207](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1936:** *Schrei*. Approximately three-minute, musicless solo inspired by Edvard Munch’s image and built around percussive foot rhythm and an abstract choreography of the scream. Some later accounts dated it to 1933 or 1938, but Peter demonstrates from Hoyer’s records and programmes that it was conceived in 1935 and premiered in 1936. ([Peter, pp. 189–196](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1936:** *Schnelltanz*. Solo to a folk tune, documented in the reconstructed fourth programme. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1936:** *Fragmente I–V*. Five-part sequence to music by Hanns Hasting; the plural title distinguishes it from the singular Cieslak *Fragment* of 1933. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1936–1937:** *Der Flamme entgegen*. Solo project mentioned by Hoyer while she was working on it in December 1936 and associating it with an etching by Lea Grundig. Its performance and final form are not securely established, so it resists classification as a completed work. ([Peter, p. 192](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1937:** *Ballade* / *Tänze vom Alltag*. Six-figure cycle, five parts to folk music and the sixth to Bartók’s *Allegro barbaro*; Hoyer annotated the programme with the alternative collective title *Tänze vom Alltag*. It may reuse a title from 1933, but the evidence does not justify identifying the two choreographies. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1937:** *Hymnus*. Solo to music by Philipp Jarnach. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1937:** *Stille*. Solo performed to a folk tune, although Hoyer’s notebooks also associate it with Satie; neither the music nor its relation to the earlier “quiet” dances is wholly stable. ([Peter, pp. 187–188, 209](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1937:** *Tanzende Frau*. Four-part cycle, the first part to a folk tune and the others to Bartók. Hoyer had also worked on a two-part project called *Arbeiterfrau*, which Peter cautiously identifies as a developmental stage of this cycle rather than a securely separate work. ([Peter, pp. 187–188, 212](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1937, project:** *Arbeiterfrau* / *Arbeiterfrau singt an der Wiege*. A projected cycle, apparently inspired by Brecht’s lullabies, documented in two- and three-part conceptions. Its exact relation to *Tanzende Frau* and *Enge der Großstadt* is unresolved, so the project should not be silently converted into a completed choreography. ([Peter, pp. 212–213](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1938:** *Weite des Landes*. Rural cycle comprising *Auf dem Felde*, *Heimweg* or *Heimweg vom Felde*, *Feierabend*, and *Froher Tag*; the 1939 version added *Bitte* and *Vor dem Hause*. The programmes call some accompaniments folk tunes, while surviving music suggests that Satie may have been concealed because Nazi authorities proscribed him as Jewish. ([Peter, pp. 187–189, 207–209](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1938:** *Enge der Großstadt*. Socially critical cycle comprising *Angst*, *Verzweifelte Freude*, *Sorge*, *Empörung*, and *Schrei*. Its working title was *Ballade von der Armut*; it also formed the second half of the larger programme conception *Tanzende Frau*. ([Peter, pp. 187–188, 207–212](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1938:** *Tänze der Verlorenheit I–III*. Three-part solo cycle, with two sections to Debussy and one to Ravel. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1938:** *Allegro Barbaro*. Solo to Bartók, presented independently in the 1938 programme after having supplied the music for the sixth figure of the 1937 *Ballade*. Whether it was precisely the same choreography is not established. ([Peter, pp. 187–188](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1938–1939:** *Nach einem Bolero* / *Bolero* / *Drehtanz*. Long-form turning solo to Ravel, progressively reduced from broad floor paths to rotation largely on one spot. The altered titles and choreography mark an evolving obra rather than several securely distinct works; it remained in Hoyer’s repertory for decades. ([Peter, pp. 187–189, 218–219](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1939:** *Kinder ihrer Erde*. Rural cycle comprising *Ein Mädchen wirbt*, *Braune Erde*, *Der Schnitter*, *Brotbacken*, and probably the reused *Froher Tag*. Its component list is reconstructed because no programme sheet has been found. ([Peter, pp. 188–189, 207–208](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1941:** *Jeanne d’Arc*. Solo choreography or dramatic character study from Hoyer’s wartime period; the concise scholarly work list supplies the title and date but not a secure account of its surviving form. ([Sächsische Biografie](https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Dore_Hoyer_%281911-1967%29))
- **1941:** *Ophelia*. Solo character study from the same period; documentation cited in the available work lists is sparse. ([Sächsische Biografie](https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Dore_Hoyer_%281911-1967%29))
- **1941–1952:** *Biblische Gestalten*. A changing cycle of biblical figures rather than a single fixed text; *Ruth* and *Judith* are documented components, and the broad date range indicates continuing revision and accretion. ([Sächsische Biografie](https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Dore_Hoyer_%281911-1967%29); [German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/))
- **1944:** *Amazone*. Wartime solo choreography recorded in the Tanzfilminstitut’s work list. Its extant state and exact premiere circumstances are not specified there. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1944:** *Der Geliebten*. Wartime solo choreography recorded in the same work list; its title may describe a dedication or figure, but the source does not establish which. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1945:** *Drehtanz*. A title used for Hoyer’s turning dance in the postwar chronology, probably a manifestation of the evolving Ravel *Bolero* rather than a wholly separate work. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/); [Peter, ch. 6](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/06_kap6.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=7))
- **1947:** *Gesichte*. Postwar manifestation of Hoyer’s recurring “visions” choreography; the source lists it separately, but its degree of identity with the 1936 *Gesichte I–V* is uncertain. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1947:** *Mütter*. Cycle or suite centred on mothers, associated with Hoyer’s Käthe-Kollwitz-inflected postwar repertory. The surviving descriptions do not always distinguish an autonomous cycle from related material in *Tänze für Käthe Kollwitz*. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1947:** *Rebellion*. Solo choreography recorded in the postwar chronology. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1948:** *Gesichte unserer Zeit*. Four-part contemporary cycle comprising *so fern – so weit*, *unabwendbar*, *gezeichnet*, and *motorisch*, to music associated with Bartók. It revisits the “Gesichte” category but is a separately titled postwar work. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1948:** *Der große Gesang*. Major solo cycle, generally listed as seven dances: *Tanz der göttlichen Besessenheit*, *Tanz der lauteren Einfalt*, *Tanz der brutalen Gewalt*, *Tanz der erhabenen Trauer*, *Tanz der klärenden Besinnung*, *Tanz der zwei Gesichter*, and *Tanz der menschlichen Besessenheit*. Sources disagree over whether to call it a five-part suite or a seven-dance cycle, suggesting changing programme groupings rather than a single uncontested structure. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1949:** *Lyrik*. Solo or suite listed in the Tanzfilminstitut chronology; the generic title and presently available record do not define its component structure. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1952:** *Judith*. Biblical solo and a late component or independent manifestation of *Biblische Gestalten*. Its double status—as a named solo and part of a changing cycle—should be retained. ([Sächsische Biografie](https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Dore_Hoyer_%281911-1967%29))
- **1953:** *Südliche Impressionen*. Travel-derived solo suite or programme, preceding the better-documented *Südamerikanische Reise*. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1954:** *Südamerikanische Reise*. Travel cycle created from Hoyer’s South American experience; documented parts include *Die Erde singt*. It is a choreographic suite with an accompanying programme note by Hoyer, not a travel book. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/); [Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1954:** *Raum atmen*. Solo, also listed as a section in the sequence *Monolog*. The available catalogues do not make entirely clear whether *Monolog* was an overarching cycle, a later grouping, or an alternative programme title. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1954:** *Dämon Maschine*. Solo whose asymmetric, body-concealing costume made the dancing body resemble an abstract image; it is therefore a choreographic and visual object, though not a full Gesamtkunstwerk in the strict sense. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/); [German Dance Archive, dance-and-painting lexicon](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/www.tanzarchiv-koeln.de/Archivalie_des_Monats/Nele_Lipp/Nele_Lipp_Tanz_Malerei-Ein_Lexikon.pdf))
- **1955:** *Zwischen gestern und morgen*. Solo or cycle whose title frames a temporal passage; the catalogue record supplies no secure component list. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1956:** *Mit Trommel und Gong*. Percussion-based solo or suite; the title identifies its sonic apparatus rather than a literary subject. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1956:** *Auf schwarzem Grund*. Solo cycle to music by Giselher Klebe, Maurice Ohana, and Dimitri Wiatowitsch; documented sections include *Ostinato*. Its unifying “black ground” is both programme concept and visual condition. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1958:** *Mosaico*. Solo or composite suite; the title itself proposes a mosaic of parts, but the accessible catalogue does not enumerate them. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1962:** *Bach-Präludien* / *Präludien*. Solo sequence to Bach, documented photographically under both shortened and expanded titles; its precise premiere date and internal division are not consistently supplied in the consulted catalogues. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/))
- **1962:** *Affectos humanos*. Five-solo cycle to music by Dimitri Wiatowitsch, comprising *Eitelkeit* (also catalogued as *Ehre*), *Begierde*, *Hass*, *Angst*, and *Liebe*. The cycle survives unusually well through Hoyer’s notes and a film recording, but later stage versions are reconstructions or embodied transmissions, not identical repetitions of Hoyer’s 1962 manifestation. ([University of Washington](https://dance.washington.edu/people/dore-hoyer); [undo, redo and repeat](https://www.undo-redo-repeat.de/wege-der-weitergabe/die-zeitzeuginnen/martin-nachbar.html))
- **1965:** *Nocturno*. Late solo choreography; the catalogue’s stray punctuation after the title has no evident authorial significance. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1966:** *Großstadt*. Late choreography on the metropolis, distinct by date from the 1938 cycle *Enge der Großstadt* unless further archival evidence proves a revision. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1967:** *Asien-Suite*. Late suite listed under Hoyer’s final year. The accessible catalogue does not establish whether she completed or publicly performed it before her death, so it may be better understood as a project than as a securely manifested work. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))

### Group choreographies and dance-theatre works

- **1946:** *Tänze für Käthe Kollwitz*. Six-part group dance cycle developed from Hoyer’s choreographic directions and her pupils’ improvisations, with music by Ulrich Kessler. Inspired by Kollwitz prints, its documented parts include *Carmagnole*, *Inspiration*, and *Losbruch*; because creation was collaborative, it resists attribution to Hoyer as sole bodily author even though the choreography and governing project were hers. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/); [German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1947:** *Schießbude*. Group choreography in the repertory of Hoyer’s Dresden ensemble. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1948:** *Es war ein Bursche und sein Mädchen*. Choreographic work listed in the postwar chronology; the accessible record does not settle whether it was a solo, duet, or larger ensemble piece. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1950:** *Anfang und Ende*. Hamburg State Opera choreography, documented in photographs with Hoyer and Bico von Larsky; some sources connect its music or conception to the evolving Ravel *Bolero*, but the duet/group manifestation warrants its own entry. ([German Dance Archive catalogue record](https://faust.sk-kultur.de/hzeig.FAU?desk=%2300000132%23&dm=1&sid=028B38FC225091&thes=1); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1950:** *Der Fremde*. Group choreography for the Hamburg State Opera, a dance-of-death-like treatment of the individual’s relation to society. Hoyer regarded it as one of her most important group works. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1950:** *Zigeuner*. Hamburg ensemble choreography to Bartók, named in the State Opera’s historical repertory account. The dated institutional source does not clarify whether this is one of the three group works premiered in February 1950 or a reused programme designation. ([Hamburg State Opera](https://hamburgballett.die-hamburgische-staatsoper.de/de/ueberuns/hamburg-ballett/historie))
- **1951:** *Kontraste*. Choreographic work from Hoyer’s Hamburg period, probably an ensemble composition; the available catalogue does not define its cast or structure. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1951:** *Sonate für 2 Klaviere und ein Schlagzeug*. Abstract choreography organized around an instrumental sonata ensemble; the title describes the musical apparatus rather than a narrative. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1961:** *Cadena de Fugas* / *Sieben Fugen (Spiel der Kräfte)*. Group choreography structured as a chain of seven fugues to Bach. The Spanish and German titles appear to designate the same or closely related work, but the consulted catalogues do not explicitly resolve the equivalence. ([Sächsische Biografie](https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Dore_Hoyer_%281911-1967%29); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))
- **1961:** *La Idea*. Socially critical group choreography based on Frans Masereel’s wordless woodcut sequence *Die Idee*, with music by Paul Constantin. As movement derived from a graphic narrative and re-manifested by dancers, it crosses media without erasing their separation. ([Sächsische Biografie](https://saebi.isgv.de/biografie/Dore_Hoyer_%281911-1967%29); [German Dance Archive, dance-and-painting lexicon](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/www.tanzarchiv-koeln.de/Archivalie_des_Monats/Nele_Lipp/Nele_Lipp_Tanz_Malerei-Ein_Lexikon.pdf))
- **1964:** *Androclus und der Löwe*. Dance-theatre or stage choreography recorded in the work chronology; the accessible entry does not specify whether Hoyer originated the complete production or only its movement component. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))

### Stage direction and choreographic staging

- **1933–1934:** *Untitled dances for operas and operettas at the Oldenburg State Theatre*. Hoyer was required to make dance insertions for repertory productions. These are genuine commissioned choreographic manifestations, but they resist listing as autonomous titled works because the archival summary supplies neither titles nor a complete production list. ([German Dance Archive, dance-and-painting lexicon](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/www.tanzarchiv-koeln.de/Archivalie_des_Monats/Nele_Lipp/Nele_Lipp_Tanz_Malerei-Ein_Lexikon.pdf))
- **1952:** *Johanna auf dem Scheiterhaufen*. Hoyer’s Mannheim staging and choreography of Arthur Honegger and Paul Claudel’s dramatic oratorio; authorship of the underlying music and text is not hers, so her obra is the theatrical and choreographic realization. ([Nationaltheater Mannheim](https://www.nationaltheater-mannheim.de/kacheln/artikel/zeitfenster-50/))
- **1952:** *Maria Magdalena*. Stage direction or choreographic staging listed among Hoyer’s works. The underlying dramatic text is not hers, and the accessible catalogue does not identify the version, so the made thing attributable to her is the production rather than the named play. ([German Dance Film Institute](https://dancefilm.info/dore-hoyer/))
- **1950s–1960s:** *Other dramatic productions at Mannheim, Ulm, Athens, Berlin, Salzburg, and Frankfurt*. Archival biographies establish that Hoyer repeatedly worked as a stage director and connected speech with dance, but the accessible scholarly summaries do not provide a complete, reliably titled production list. This therefore resists entry as a single work: it records a documented body of practice whose individual manifestations still require theatre-archive reconstruction. ([German Dance Archive](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/archiv/nachlaesse-sammlungen/h/dore-hoyer/); [New German Biography](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118553887.html))

### Choreographic notebooks and protocols

- **1933–1960s:** *Choreographic notebooks*. Twenty-two surviving notebooks combine verbal movement descriptions, floor-path diagrams, figurative sketches, and photographs. They are neither merely preparatory texts nor autonomous dances: they are working protocols through which ephemeral choreographies were fixed, revised, and made transmissible. ([Academy of Arts press dossier](https://www.adk.berlin/de/presse/pressedossiers/adk19_WdKe_Pressedossier_EN.pdf); [German Dance Archive finding aid](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/www.tanzarchiv-koeln.de/Texte/Bestandsuebersicht_001_Dore_Hoyer_45.pdf))
- **1935 onward:** *Retrospective records of the early solo programmes*. After Cieslak’s death, Hoyer began writing down dances she had made with him. These records are protocols and acts of preservation, not new performances; their mixture of notation systems also resists treatment as a conventional written score. ([Peter, introduction](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/01_kap1.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2))
- **undated and various dates:** *Choreographic notes and work manuscripts*. The German Dance Archive preserves descriptions, title drafts, thematic notes, and working materials beyond the bound notebooks. The archive explicitly includes unidentified and undated dances, so no complete list can honestly turn every surviving trace into a securely titled, dated work. ([German Dance Archive finding aid](https://www.deutsches-tanzarchiv.de/fileadmin/user_upload/www.tanzarchiv-koeln.de/Texte/Bestandsuebersicht_001_Dore_Hoyer_45.pdf); [archive database](https://faust.sk-kultur.de/hzeig.FAU?desk=%2300000191%23&dm=1&sid=98A58B9D1108030&thes=4))

### Essays, programme texts, and public interventions

- **1948:** *Warum moderner Ausdruckstanz?* Essay arguing for modern expressive dance; as an intervention in the status and history of the form, it approaches a manifesto rather than functioning only as description. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1951:** *Ein Leben für den Tanz. Zu Mary Wigmans 65. Geburtstag am 13. November 1951*. Commemorative essay on Wigman and a life organized through dance. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1954:** *Südamerikanische Reise*. Programme note for the dance cycle of the same name. It is a paratext to a choreography, not a separate travel narrative, though publication gives it a second manifestation as prose. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1956:** *Dank an Mary Wigman*. Published tribute to Wigman. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1957:** *New York – Hochburg des “Modern Dance”. Eindrücke aus amerikanischen Tanz-Studios*. Report and critical essay on American modern-dance studios, produced after Hoyer’s encounter with the United States. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1960:** *Noch zu jung für ein Begräbnis – zum Thema “Freier Tanz”*. Newspaper intervention defending free dance against its premature institutional burial; its polemical action makes “manifesto” an appropriate secondary category. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
- **1965:** *Wort und Tanz*. Essay on the relation between verbal language and dance, published in the yearbook of Hamburg’s Freie Akademie der Künste. ([Peter, bibliography](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/7840/08_kap8910.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=9))
