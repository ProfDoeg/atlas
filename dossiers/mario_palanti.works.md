## Works by Mario Palanti

### Built architecture and architectural interventions

- **1910:** *Italian Pavilion for the International Railway Exhibition of the Argentine Centennial*. Temporary exhibition architecture in Buenos Aires, designed under Gaetano Moretti; Palanti’s precise share is disputed between collaboration, construction management, and authorship claimed in his later curriculum vitae. [1][2]

- **1911:** *New Faculty of Law, University of Buenos Aires*. Neo-Gothic institutional project undertaken in the office of Arturo Prins and Oskar Ranzenhofer, with Francisco Gianotti; Palanti was a collaborator, not its sole author, and the building was never completed as designed. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Residence for Davide Costaguta*. A Buenos Aires petit hôtel on Calle Uruguay, recorded in Palanti’s retrospective list and early exhibition catalogue; published chronologies assign it to 1913. [1][3]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Rental building for Francesco Cassottana*. Residential building on Calle Tucumán, Buenos Aires; published chronologies date it to 1913, while Palanti’s own list supplies no year. [1][3]

- **1914:** *Edificio de los Atlantes / Giuseppe Peretti Building*. Mixed-use or rental building at Avenida Rivadavia 1916, Buenos Aires, distinguished by sculpted atlantes; Palanti’s 1958 list calls it the building of Giuseppe Peretti & Co. and dates it 1916, so both its name and date vary in the sources. [1][3]

- **1914:** *Rental building for Bernardo or Pietro Vila*. Residential building at Avenida Rivadavia 2625, Buenos Aires. The patron appears as Bernardo Vila in one chronology and Pietro Vila in Palanti’s own later list. [1][3]

- **1914:** *Cine Presidente Roca*. Cinema at Avenida Rivadavia 3736, Buenos Aires; subsequently demolished. Scholarship notes that Palanti designed the façade and that its execution may have occurred later, making the extent and date of the realized obra uncertain. [1][4]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Garage for Giuseppe Peretti & Co.*. Commercial garage on Calle Bartolomé Mitre, Buenos Aires, known from Palanti’s retrospective inventory; no secure construction date is supplied there. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Silvio J. Merlo*. Urban residence at Azcuénaga and Arenales, Buenos Aires; the consulted scholarly catalogue preserves the patron and location but no fixed date. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Pedro Canduglia*. Residence on Calle Arenales, Buenos Aires, documented in Palanti’s list of realized South American works without a precise date. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Building for Nicolás Bruzzone e Hijos*. Building on Calle Libertad, Buenos Aires; its function and exact date remain unspecified in the principal documentary list. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Building for Cesare Baragli*. Building on Avenida Rivadavia, Buenos Aires; Palanti’s inventory may distinguish more than one commission for Cesare Baragli, but the surviving descriptions do not securely establish whether these are separate obras or versions of one. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Building for Angelo Baragli*. Building on Avenida Rivadavia, Buenos Aires, known from Palanti’s retrospective inventory without a fixed date or street number. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Building for Odine Bazzani*. Building on Avenida Rivadavia, Buenos Aires; neither function nor precise date is securely stated in the consulted catalogue. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Carmelo Novello*. Residence on Avenida Montes de Oca, Buenos Aires, recorded without a fixed date. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Cesare Rossi*. Residence in Barracas, Buenos Aires, recorded without a precise address or date. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Industrial building for J. Manzanares & Co.*. Industrial architecture in Barracas, Buenos Aires; its dating and subsequent fate are not specified in Palanti’s list. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Aurelio Devoto*. Residence on Calle Juncal, Buenos Aires, documented without a fixed date. [1]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Adrián Beccar Varela*. Residence on Avenida Las Heras, Buenos Aires; the patron’s surname is inconsistently transcribed in later sources. [1]

- **1915:** *Chalet for Alberto Grimoldi*. House at Avenida Rivadavia 5050, Buenos Aires; subsequently demolished. [1][3]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Villa Vasena*. Suburban residence for Alfredo Vasena at San Isidro, Buenos Aires Province; the catalogue confirms realization but does not establish a precise date. [1][4]

- **c. 1910–1916:** *Petit hôtel for Eduardo Resta*. Residence in Palermo Chico, Buenos Aires, distinct from the later Resta automobile palace; no fixed date is given in Palanti’s inventory. [1]

- **1919–1923:** *Pasaje Barolo / Palacio Barolo*. Multifunctional office-and-shopping tower at Avenida de Mayo 1370, Buenos Aires, commissioned by Luis Barolo. Its architecture, lighthouse, ornament, and Dantean program form a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a commercial building. [1][5]

- **1919–1924:** *Giovanni Battista Uboldi Building*. Rental housing at Avenida Rivadavia 2450–2460, Buenos Aires; the patron also appears in sources as Juan Uboldi. [1][3]

- **c. 1919–1924:** *Palanti rental complex at Victoria and Sarandí*. Five independent residential blocks commissioned or owned by Palanti himself in Buenos Aires; the documentary description does not provide an accepted title. [1]

- **c. 1919–1924:** *Palanti building on Avenida Corrientes*. A Buenos Aires building made for or owned by Palanti; function, address, and date remain insufficiently identified, so it resists treatment as a securely individuated work. [1]

- **1919–1924:** *Palacio Comercial Emilio Valsecchi*. Commercial building at San José and Alsina, Buenos Aires. Palanti’s own inventory instead locates a Valsecchi palace at Victoria and Alsina, leaving an unresolved address discrepancy. [1][3]

- **1919–1924:** *Headquarters of the Banco Francés e Italiano para la América del Sur*. Bank building at San Martín and the present Teniente General Perón, Buenos Aires; later occupied by successor banking institutions. [1][3]

- **1920:** *Andrés Roccatagliata Building*. Large rental-apartment building at Avenida Santa Fe and Callao, Buenos Aires. [1][3]

- **1921:** *Second Andrés Roccatagliata Building*. Residential building at Avenida Santa Fe 1769–1771, Buenos Aires; catalogues treat it separately from the corner building. [1][3]

- **1922:** *Casa Palanti*. Private residence at Eduardo Costa 3079–3099, Buenos Aires, designed in association with an architect whose name is variously rendered as Alguier; present-day symbolic readings should not be mistaken for securely documented original intentions. [1][3]

- **1922–1928:** *Palacio Salvo*. Multifunctional tower on Plaza Independencia, Montevideo, commissioned by the Salvo brothers and developed with engineer Gori Salvo. Officially inaugurated in 1928, it belongs with the Barolo and unrealized Mole Littoria to Palanti’s “unfinished trilogy” of tower-Gesamtkunstwerke. [1][6][7]

- **1924–1929:** *Edicola Palanti / Civico Mausoleo Palanti*. Funerary architecture in Milan’s Cimitero Monumentale, conceived for Palanti’s parents, built in 1924–1928, and inaugurated in 1929; it later became the civic mausoleum bearing his name. [8]

- **1927:** *London–Paris department store*. Commercial building occupying a site between Avenida 18 de Julio, Zorrilla de San Martín, and Rincón in Paysandú, Uruguay; its attribution is documented in the Palanti monograph, while readily available summaries give little information about its subsequent fabric. [3][9]

- **1927–1928:** *Palacio Automotor Resta Hermanos / Chrysler Palace / Palacio Alcorta*. Automobile showroom and service complex at Avenida Figueroa Alcorta 3351, Buenos Aires, incorporating a rooftop test track; later converted into housing, so the surviving object is materially a transformation of Palanti’s obra. [1][10]

- **1927–1928:** *Hotel Excelsior, now Hotel Castelar*. Grand hotel at Avenida de Mayo 1142–1148, Buenos Aires; Palanti’s curriculum describes a 300-room, 300-bath establishment. [1][3]

- **date uncertain:** *Apostolic Nunciature, Buenos Aires*. Ecclesiastical-diplomatic residence included among Palanti’s realized works and illustrated in *Quattro anni di lavoro*; the consulted catalogue does not settle its exact date. [1]

- **date uncertain:** *Grand Cinema Rosa*. Cinema on Avenida Rivadavia, Buenos Aires; Palanti’s retrospective list supplies neither a street number nor a secure date, and it should not automatically be identified with the Cine Presidente Roca. [1]

- **date uncertain:** *Unspecified South American buildings*. Palanti claimed further designs and constructions in Argentina’s interior, Brazil, Uruguay, Chile, Venezuela, and Colombia. Because his 1958 document neither names nor separates them, this is evidence of an incompletely recoverable body of work, not a defensible list of individual obras. [1]

- **after 1945:** *Postwar reconstruction work in Europe*. Palanti’s curriculum claims professional work on European reconstruction after the Second World War but names no buildings; the claim therefore resists classification as a discrete work. [1]

### Unrealized architectural projects and competition entries

- **c. 1910s:** *Church for Tigre*. An unrealized ecclesiastical project represented by multiple volumetric and detail studies; the surviving scholarship does not establish a definitive version or title. [4]

- **c. 1910s–1920s:** *Natural History Museum, Buenos Aires*. Unrealized museum project; known through Palanti’s published designs rather than a completed building. [4]

- **c. 1910s–1920s:** *Theatre for a mutual-aid society*. Unrealized theatre project in Buenos Aires; the consulted account does not securely identify the commissioning society or date. [4]

- **c. 1910s–1920s:** *Expansion of the Banco de la Provincia de Buenos Aires*. Project for enlarging the bank building originally designed by Henry Hunt and Hans Schroeder; it is recorded as a design rather than a securely realized alteration. [4]

- **c. 1919:** *Housing for the Unión Católica Argentina*. Project for compact houses, an antecedent of Palanti’s later standardized dwellings; whether any units were built is not established. [1]

- **c. 1919–1924:** *Hotel Montevideo*. Hotel project for Montevideo; it remained at the project stage. [4]

- **c. 1919–1924:** *Grand Hotel at Pueyrredón and Mitre*. Unrealized Buenos Aires hotel project known through published drawings. [4]

- **date uncertain:** *Club del Orden, Santa Fe*. Project for a new headquarters of the Club del Orden; the CEDODAL monograph treats it specifically, but available evidence does not show that Palanti’s design was executed. [9]

- **1924–1926:** *L’Eternale / Mole Littoria*. Repeatedly revised proposal for a colossal multifunctional tower in Rome, conceived as a fascist monument and never built. Its identity lies in the project, models, drawings, publicity, and book rather than in a material building. [7][11]

- **1933–1934:** *Palazzo del Littorio / Torre Littoria*. Competition project for the Fascist Party headquarters on Via dell’Impero, Rome; unrealized and published by Palanti as a portfolio. A surviving drawing titled *Sarcofago dei martiri* belongs to its proposed chapel, not to an autonomous built monument. [8][12]

- **1935:** *Auditorium for Rome*. Utopian competition project documented through plans, sections, details, models, and a privately printed portfolio; it was not built. [8][13]

- **c. 1948:** *Standardized Palandomus houses*. Designs for compact, low-cost houses using Palanti’s dry-construction system; they are a family of prototypes rather than one stable building. [1][14]

- **c. 1950–1952:** *Housing for Ciudad Evita*. Compact 65-square-metre prototype house for a four-person family, designed for construction in Palandomus blocks. The project survives in drawings, but the dissertation does not establish that Palanti’s version was adopted or built. [1]

### Construction systems, inventions, and technical projects

- **1919–c. 1970:** *Palandomus*. Patented modular dry-wall construction system based on a single interlocking cement block, repeatedly revised and patented in several countries. It is simultaneously an invention, a protocol for construction, and a long-duration project rather than one building. [14]

- **by 1922:** *Demountable house*. Technical-commercial housing project mentioned in correspondence between Mario and Giuseppe Palanti; insufficient surviving identification prevents it from being confidently equated with a particular Palandomus prototype. [1]

- **1920s, dates uncertain:** *Technical inventions and industrial schemes*. Family correspondence names work involving cinema machinery or films, automobiles, the Italcable system, Pavesi tractors, block-making machines, tiles, and a submarine. These references show inventive activity but do not define stable titles, dates, or Palanti’s precise authorship; they resist entry as fully recoverable individual works. [1]

### Exhibitions and architectural self-representations

- **1916:** *Prima esposizione personale d’architettura nella Repubblica Argentina*. An exhibition mounted in the Comisión Nacional de Bellas Artes, Buenos Aires; it was a curated manifestation of Palanti’s architecture and studies, distinct from the 1917 book that documented it. Palanti’s later curriculum dates both exhibition and publication to 1916, whereas the surviving volume is dated 1917. [1][15]

- **1917:** *Prima esposizione personale d’architettura nella Repubblica Argentina*. A book-catalogue containing 146 plates of built work and projects; it is both a text and a deliberately constructed public image of an architectural oeuvre. [15]

- **1924, title disputed:** *Quattro anni di lavoro* / *Cinque anni di lavoro*. Illustrated architectural portfolio with 152 plates. Library records and surviving copies support *Quattro anni di lavoro*, while later bibliographies also circulate *Cinque anni di lavoro*; the titles should not be silently merged without noting this bibliographic instability. [16][17]

### Architectural books and project-portfolios

- **1926:** *L’Eternale: mole littoria, Roma, MCMXXVI*. Illustrated project-book devoted to the unbuilt Mole Littoria. It is not merely a description: as propaganda addressed to the Fascist government, it attempts to bring the building and its political symbolism into being. [11][18]

- **1934:** *Palazzo del Littorio: progetto*. Privately produced architectural portfolio presenting Palanti’s competition design for Rome; a manifestation of an unrealized project rather than a record of a completed palace. [8][12]

- **1935:** *Auditorium: progetti*. Privately printed portfolio of 33 pages and 42 plates presenting Palanti’s unrealized Roman auditorium; sources report an edition of 300 copies. [13]

- **1946 or 1947:** *Architettura per tutti / Architecture for All*. Bilingual architectural and urbanistic book bringing together Palanti’s buildings, projects, construction ideas, and polemical reflections. Catalogues disagree between 1946 and 1947, so the publication year is not silently normalized here. [1][19]
