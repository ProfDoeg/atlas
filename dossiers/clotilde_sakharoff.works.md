## Works by Clotilde Sakharoff

### Choreographic obras and dance manifestations

- **1910:** *First solo-dance programme*. Sakharoff’s Munich début comprised dances of her own making to music by Schubert, Schumann, and Grieg, but the surviving scholarly account does not supply stable individual titles; the programme therefore resists division into securely named works. ([Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd121579190.html); [Fotografia e Danza](https://www.fotografiaedanza.it/performers/clotilde-von-derp-sakharoff/))

- **1912 or earlier:** *May Day Dance* (*Maientanz*; later *Danse de mai*). A solo choreographic interpretation of music by Arnold Krug, already documented in her early touring repertory and subsequently listed among the Sakharoffs’ 1920 works. Its dating and later joint attribution are not entirely consistent across the sources. ([Latvian Academy of Culture study](https://lka.edu.lv/media/cms_page_media/2021/2/19/PROMOCIJAS%20DARBS_Vidzemniece_08_02_2021.pdf); [Deutsches Tanzarchiv Köln](https://faust.sk-kultur.de/hzeig.FAU?desk=%2300000115%23&dm=1&sid=98A58B9D1026101&thes=1))

- **1916:** *Danseuse de Delphes*. A solo dance to Debussy, conceived as an archaic-Greek apparition rather than a narrative ballet. Sources securely associate the manifestation and costume with Clotilde, but the division of choreographic authorship between her and Alexandre remains uncertain. ([Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/sacharov-alexander-1886-1963-and-von-derp-clotilde-born-von-der-planitz-1892-1974%26gt); [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/downloadPDF?url=sfz109341.pdf))

- **1920:** *Visions du Moyen Âge* (*Moyen Age Dances*). A choreographic suite made with Alexandre Sakharoff and presented as a stylized evocation of the Middle Ages, not a reconstruction governed by historical-dance protocol. The reference repertory credits it jointly, while some archival descriptions attribute the couple’s choreography and costume conception chiefly or wholly to Alexandre. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974); [Deutsches Tanzarchiv Köln](https://faust.sk-kultur.de/hzeig.FAU?desk=%2300000115%23&dm=1&sid=98A58B9D1026101&thes=1); [Harvard finding aid](https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/catalog/hou01561))

- **1922:** *Humoresque*. A Clotilde solo to Max Reger, made with Alexandre Sakharoff according to the standard repertory list and repeatedly documented in contemporary programmes. It was a dance-image or musical embodiment rather than a plot-bearing ballet; exact choreographic responsibility is disputed by the sources. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974); [Stader Kunst-Buch-Kabinett catalogue](https://www.staderkunstbuchkabinett.de/images/kunstbuch/home/Katalog_Sacharoff_2016.pdf))

- **1922:** *Chanson nègre* (also documented as *Poème nègre*). A solo choreographic manifestation performed by Clotilde to music by David Guion. The historically transmitted title uses racial terminology now recognized as objectionable; sources disagree over *Chanson* versus *Poème*, and attribution oscillates between Clotilde, the couple, and Alexandre as overall deviser. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974); [mdwPress](https://www.mdw.ac.at/mdwpress/mdwp009-011/); [Teatro e Storia](https://www.teatroestoria.it/materiali/Il_caso_GUALINO.pdf))

- **by 1923:** *Poème printanier* (also *Primavera*). A Clotilde solo to Arnold Krug that made a pastoral “spring poem” through gesture, costume, and musical interpretation. It may be related to, or a retitling of, her earlier Krug-based *May Day Dance*; the consulted sources do not resolve whether these are one obra or two. ([Centre national de la danse](https://mediatheque.cnd.fr/ressources/ressourcesEnLigne/la_danse/DAN_23_32.pdf); [National Diet Library](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/view/download/digidepo_14606036_po_geppo2601.pdf?contentNo=1); [Teatro e Storia](https://www.teatroestoria.it/materiali/Il_caso_GUALINO.pdf))

- **1923:** *Valses rouges* (also *Valse rouge*). A duet made and danced with Alexandre Sakharoff to Chopin. It is best understood as a joint choreographic obra whose bodies, music, costumes, and theatrical image form a small Gesamtkunstwerk; individual authorship cannot be cleanly separated. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974); [Teatro e Storia](https://www.teatroestoria.it/materiali/Il_caso_GUALINO.pdf))

- **1926:** *Plein Bonheur*. A choreographic work credited jointly to Clotilde and Alexandre in the standard repertory summary. The accessible catalogue record establishes its existence and date but does not securely disclose its music, cast, or whether it was a solo or duet. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974))

- **1930:** *Prélude et Fugue*. A duet to J. S. Bach made with Alexandre Sakharoff, structured through corresponding, alternating, and simultaneous movements of the two bodies. Contemporary criticism treats the danced relation between the bodies and Bach’s contrapuntal form as the work itself. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974); [La Tribune de la danse](https://mediatheque.cnd.fr/ressources/ressourcesEnLigne/la_tribune_de_la_danse/TRI_35_20.pdf))

- **by 1935:** *La Jeune Fille au Jardin*. A Clotilde solo to Federico Mompou, described by contemporary criticism as one of her characteristic creations. The consulted standard summary omits it, so its premiere date and formal authorship remain unconfirmed; it survives most securely as a performed manifestation rather than a fully documented choreographic score. ([La Tribune de la danse](https://mediatheque.cnd.fr/ressources/ressourcesEnLigne/la_tribune_de_la_danse/TRI_35_20.pdf); [Routledge, *Modern Dance in France*](https://www.scribd.com/document/662919869/Choreography-and-Dance-Studies-Series-Jacqueline-Robinson-Modern-Dance-in-France-1920-1970-Routledge-1998))

- **1933:** *Papillon*. A choreographic work listed as jointly made with Alexandre Sakharoff in the standard repertory summary. Later performance evidence assigns a *Papillon* to Alexandre alone, so Clotilde’s authorship or participation—and possibly the identity of the two similarly titled dances—remains disputed. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974); [Cine Radio Actualidad, 1942](https://anaforas.fic.edu.uy/jspui/bitstream/123456789/77592/1/CRA_n305_1942-04-17.pdf))

- **1937:** *Danse de Destin*. A choreographic work credited to Clotilde and Alexandre in the standard repertory summary. The accessible sources establish the title and date but provide too little evidence to classify it more narrowly as solo, duet, or extended ballet. ([Dictionary of Women Worldwide](https://www.encyclopedia.com/women/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/sakharoff-clotilde-1892-1974))

### Textual obras

- **1935; revised or republished 1939:** *Ce que la danse est pour nous*. An aesthetic declaration published first as an article and later documented as a conference text: part essay, part artistic credo, and close to a manifesto because it states what the Sakharoffs’ dance is and what it should accomplish. The publication history is not fully settled—the 1935 source says it reproduces portions of an article from *Le Jour*, while later scholarship cites the 1939 *Conferencia* version. ([Archives internationales de la danse](https://mediatheque.cnd.fr/ressources/ressourcesEnLigne/aid/aid/AID35_04/AID35_04.pdf); [Routledge, *Modern Dance in France*](https://www.scribd.com/document/662919869/Choreography-and-Dance-Studies-Series-Jacqueline-Robinson-Modern-Dance-in-France-1920-1970-Routledge-1998); [Fotografia e Danza](https://www.fotografiaedanza.it/performers/clotilde-von-derp-sakharoff/))

- **undated:** *Les Sakharoff—La vie que nous avons dansée*. An unpublished, 301-page French typescript autobiography. It is a completed archival text but an incomplete public work: extracts appeared in the 2002 exhibition catalogue, while the whole manuscript remains unpublished and its date is not fixed. ([Deutsches Tanzarchiv Köln](https://faust.sk-kultur.de/hzeig.FAU?desk=%230000012A%23&dm=1&sid=028B38FC240009&thes=4); [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd121579190.html))
