# H. G. Wells: Research Dossier

**Subject:** Herbert George Wells (1866–1946)  
**Prepared:** 20 August 2026  
**Method:** Chronological documentary reconstruction using Wells's autobiography and published works; archival catalogues; contemporaneous records; court judgments; institutional histories; modern biographies and scholarship. Wikipedia was used only for orientation and bibliographic cross-checking, not as the principal authority.

## Evidentiary Note

This dossier distinguishes among: (1) events documented in contemporary records or Wells's papers; (2) Wells's own retrospective testimony; (3) biographical reconstruction; (4) allegations rejected by a court; and (5) myths or later cultural claims. Wells's *Experiment in Autobiography* (1934) is exceptionally detailed but is a self-authored reconstruction with deliberate omissions, especially concerning sex. His posthumously published *H. G. Wells in Love* (1984), edited by his son G. P. Wells from the private “Postscript to an Experiment in Autobiography,” supplies Wells's own account of those omissions; it is testimony, not neutral corroboration.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full legal name:** Herbert George Wells.
- **Usual published name:** H. G. Wells.
- **Family name:** “Bertie.”
- **Pseudonyms and signatures:** **Reginald Bliss** for *Boon* (1915); **Septimus Browne** for “A Talk with Gryllotalpa” and other juvenile work; **Sosthenes Smith** (the initials “S.S.”) for “A Vision of the Past”; **D. P.** for *The Elements of Reconstruction* (1916). Some early journalism was anonymous or unsigned. The quantity of unidentified early periodical work cannot now be established.
- **Birth:** 21 September 1866, Atlas House, then 162 High Street, Bromley, Kent, England. Modern renumbering places the site at **47 High Street**.
- **Death:** 13 August 1946, 13 Hanover Terrace, Regent's Park, London, aged 79.
- **Nationality:** British; English by birth and cultural identification, although his political program became explicitly cosmopolitan.
- **Occupations:** Draper's apprentice; pupil-teacher; schoolteacher; correspondence-college lecturer; biologist and science textbook writer; journalist; critic; novelist; short-story writer; historian; political pamphleteer; broadcaster; screenwriter; public intellectual.
- **Fields:** “Scientific romance” (later grouped within science fiction), realistic and comic fiction, utopian and dystopian writing, popular science, world history, education, socialism, international organization, human rights, war and peace, futurology.
- **Public offices and institutional roles:** Fabian Society member and briefly an executive reform insurgent; Labour parliamentary candidate for London University in 1922 and 1923; president of International PEN, 1933–1936; president of PEN's Wartime International Presidential Committee, 1941–1946; first president and co-founder of the Diabetic Association, 1934.
- **Education and degrees:** Normal School of Science, South Kensington, 1884–1887; University of London external BSc, 1890 (first class in zoology, second class in geology); D.Litt., University of London, 1936.
- **Children known and acknowledged:** George Philip “Gip” Wells (1901–1985), Frank Richard Wells (1903–1982), Anna-Jane Blanco White (1909–2010), and Anthony West (1914–1987).

## Family and Ancestry

### Paternal line

Wells wrote that his paternal grandfather, also Joseph Wells, was head gardener to Lord De L'Isle at Penshurst Place in Kent. He listed his father's siblings as Charles Edward, Henry, Edward, William, Lucy, Elizabeth, and Hannah and believed the family had been established in Kent for several generations. His father, **Joseph “Joe” Wells (1827–1910)**, worked as a gardener at Uppark before becoming a shopkeeper and professional cricketer. Joe bowled for Kent and for private clubs; cricket income supplemented a chronically weak retail business. A broken thigh/femur in 1877 ended his effective cricket career.

### Maternal line

Wells's mother, **Sarah Neal (1822–1905)**, came from a Sussex service background. She had worked as a lady's maid at Uppark, the great house near South Harting, before marrying Joseph in 1853. She was conventionally Protestant and concerned with respectability and salvation. Joe became a freethinker. Religion, poverty, and the failure of the shop widened the distance between them. They did not divorce and, according to the family account, did not take other partners, but they lived mostly apart after Sarah returned to Uppark as resident housekeeper.

### Siblings

Herbert was the fourth child and third surviving son.

- **Frances Sarah “Fanny” Wells** (c. 1855–1864), the eldest child, died before Herbert's birth, reportedly of appendicitis. The family story that Herbert was conceived as a “replacement” is a later interpretive claim, not a demonstrable parental declaration.
- **Francis Charles “Frank” Wells** (born 1857; died 1933) entered the drapery trade.
- **Frederick Joseph “Freddy” Wells** (1862–1954) was also apprenticed to drapers, emigrated to Johannesburg in 1902, returned to England around 1921, and married a maternal cousin, Edith Neal. His papers are held at Illinois.

### Marriages, partners, and children

1. **Isabel Mary Wells (1865–1931)** — Wells's cousin. They married in 1891. Wells later described intellectual and sexual incompatibility. He left her in 1894 for Amy Catherine Robbins; divorce followed in 1895. Isabel later married again and became Isabel Mary Smith. No children are known from the marriage.
2. **Amy Catherine Robbins (1872–1927)** — Wells met her as a student at the University Tutorial College. He renamed her “Jane,” the name by which she became known. They lived together from 1894 and married at St Pancras register office in October 1895. She typed, edited, criticized, researched, managed household and family arrangements, and tolerated or negotiated a marriage in which Wells had other relationships. Their sons were:
   - **George Philip “Gip” Wells** (1901–1985), later a zoologist and co-author, with his father and Julian Huxley, of *The Science of Life*.
   - **Frank Richard Wells** (1903–1982), later father of a line that includes film director Simon Wells.
3. **Amber Reeves (1887–1981)** — New Zealand-born Cambridge student, Fabian, writer, and daughter of William and Maud Pember Reeves. The affair began in 1908 while she was preparing for her Cambridge examinations. Their daughter **Anna-Jane** was born in December 1909. Reeves married the barrister George Rivers Blanco White in July 1909 under an arrangement made amid the scandal; Wells remained Anna-Jane's biological father. Illinois's “Kennard Collection” preserves Wells–Reeves–Anna-Jane materials.
4. **Rebecca West**, born Cicely Isabel Fairfield (1892–1983) — journalist and novelist, twenty-six years younger than Wells. Their relationship began in 1913 after West had attacked Wells's novel *Marriage* in print and he wrote to meet her. Their son, **Anthony West (1914–1987)**, became a writer. The Wells–West relationship was passionate, recurrently hostile, and prolonged through correspondence and disputes about money, access, parenting, and recognition. Anthony's later *H. G. Wells: Aspects of a Life* is both family testimony and a son's adversarial reassessment.
5. **Moura Budberg**, born Maria Ignatievna Zakrevskaya (c. 1891/92–1974) — Russian/Estonian aristocrat, translator, sometime associate and lover of Maxim Gorky. Wells met her during his 1920 visit to Soviet Russia. Their sexual relationship began then and became central again after she moved to London in 1933. She refused Wells's repeated proposals of marriage but attended him during his last illness.

Other documented or strongly supported relationships include **Dorothy Richardson** (an affair in 1906–07, followed by a pregnancy and miscarriage according to later accounts), **Elizabeth von Arnim** (c. 1910–13), **Margaret Sanger** (1920–21 and intermittently afterward), and **Odette Keun** (a domestic and sexual partnership, principally 1924–33, at Lou Pidou near Grasse). Wells's posthumous sexual memoir also names or alludes to Violet Hunt, Rosamund Bland, Constance Coolidge, and others. The exact list and boundaries of sexual relationships remain partly dependent on Wells's self-report.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1866–1884

### Bromley and Atlas House

Joseph and Sarah used a small inheritance to acquire Atlas House, a china, glass, and sporting-goods shop with living quarters. The location and aging stock were poor. Wells grew up in what he later presented as lower-middle-class insecurity: not destitution, but perpetual fear of falling out of respectability. The shop, the servants' world at Uppark, and later drapery employment supplied the class geography of *Kipps*, *The History of Mr Polly*, *Tono-Bungay*, and “Bladesover” in other fiction.

In 1874 Wells broke a leg and spent an extended period in bed. His father supplied books from the Bromley Literary Institute. Wells made this episode a foundation myth of his reading life: enforced immobility opened alternative worlds and the prospect of becoming a writer.

He attended **Thomas Morley's Commercial Academy** from 1874 to 1880. Wells later described a narrow curriculum dominated by copperplate handwriting, bookkeeping, and commercial arithmetic. His memories are hostile and comic; they document his later judgment more securely than the school's objective quality.

### Family economic collapse and juvenile labor

Joseph's 1877 injury removed the family's most reliable supplementary income. Sarah became the effective “commander-in-chief” of her sons' placements.

- **1879:** A brief placement as pupil-teacher at the National School in Wookey, Somerset, under “Uncle” Arthur Williams. Williams's qualifications proved defective and the appointment collapsed within months. Wells later admitted beating pupils and struggling with discipline; he nevertheless discovered that teaching was more mentally tolerable than shop work.
- **1880:** A short, failed drapery apprenticeship at **Rodgers and Denyer**, opposite Windsor Castle. He remained only a few months.
- **1880–83:** Apprenticed to **Southsea Drapery Emporium (Hyde's)**. He worked extremely long days, slept in a communal dormitory, packed parcels, served customers badly, and rebelled before completing the four-year indenture. His mother had contracted to pay a £50 premium, of which £40 had already been paid, making his departure an economic blow. The “shop assistant” experience became central to his fiction and socialism.
- Between placements came a short stay connected with a chemist at Midhurst and exposure to **Midhurst Grammar School**. Headmaster **Horace Byatt** recognized his ability and later enabled his escape from drapery.
- **1883–84:** Wells returned to Midhurst as a pupil-teacher. He studied Latin and science, taught, and prepared for scholarship examinations.

Uppark mattered as both physical place and mental archive. Sarah lived in the servants' quarters while the estate contained a large library. Wells read Plato, Thomas More, Defoe, Enlightenment and Victorian science, and works outside the commercial curriculum intended for him. His position—socially below the owners but with access to their intellectual property—became a recurring model of class asymmetry.

## Education and Formation, 1884–1893

### Normal School of Science

In 1884 Wells won a one-guinea-a-week scholarship to the **Normal School of Science**, South Kensington (later the Royal College of Science, ultimately absorbed into Imperial College). His first year under **Thomas Henry Huxley** was decisive. Huxley's teaching joined Darwinian evolution, comparative anatomy, disciplined observation, agnosticism, and public education. Wells's later histories, fictions of species divergence, and belief that humanity could redirect evolution all grew from this formation.

Wells excelled initially in biology, zoology, and mathematics. Huxley's illness and retirement, Wells's increasingly broad political and literary interests, inadequate food, and his own loss of focus contributed to poorer later performance. He passed biology and physics but failed geology, lost his scholarship, and left in 1887 without completing the Normal School course.

He helped found and edit the **Science Schools Journal**. Under juvenile pseudonyms he published “A Talk with Gryllotalpa,” “A Tale of the Twentieth Century,” “A Vision of the Past,” and, in 1888, “The Chronic Argonauts,” an early and substantially different ancestor of *The Time Machine*. He participated in debating societies, read Plato and contemporary socialism, and encountered William Morris's lectures and the Fabian milieu.

### Teaching, injury, and degree

- **1887–88:** Teacher at **Holt Academy**, near Wrexham. Wells found the school disorganized and impoverished. During a football game he sustained a serious internal injury, later described as a crushed kidney followed by hemorrhages. Doctors suspected tuberculosis/consumption. The precise retrospective diagnosis is uncertain, but the illness was real, recurrent, and important: he returned to Uppark to convalesce and for years organized residence and work around respiratory health.
- **1888:** He spent time in Basford, Stoke-on-Trent. Potteries furnaces and industrial landscapes fed “The Cone” and imagery later used in *The War of the Worlds*.
- **1889–90:** Science and general teacher at **Henley House School**, Kilburn, run by J. V. Milne. One pupil was the future writer A. A. Milne, who later remembered Wells as clever and impatient rather than an effective schoolmaster.
- He qualified through the College of Preceptors and taught for **William Briggs's University Correspondence College/University Tutorial College**.
- **1890:** He took the University of London external BSc, earning first-class honors in zoology and second-class honors in geology.
- **1891:** Published scientific essays including “Zoological Retrogression” and “The Rediscovery of the Unique.”
- **1893:** Published *Text-Book of Biology* and, with R. A. Gregory, *Honours Physiography*. He married Isabel during this transitional period, continued tutoring, and wrote journalism for income.

## Early Career and Literary Breakthrough, 1893–1901

### Illness and transition to writing

Recurrent hemorrhage and exhaustion made full-time teaching precarious. Wells increasingly wrote reviews, scientific journalism, humorous sketches, and stories. His earliest periodical work was frequently unsigned; David C. Smith and other bibliographers conclude that an unknown number of items are lost or unidentifiable.

In 1893 Wells met Amy Catherine Robbins as a student. He left Isabel in 1894 and lived with Amy/Jane. The divorce and remarriage in 1895 took place just as his literary career broke open.

### 1895: the breakthrough year

*The Time Machine* evolved through several serial forms, including material published anonymously in the *National Observer* and *New Review*. Its 1895 book publication converted the machine—not dream, magic, or accidental sleep—into a durable technological narrative device. It joined cosmic time, entropy, Darwinian divergence, and class allegory in the Eloi and Morlocks.

Also in 1895 he published *The Wonderful Visit*, *The Stolen Bacillus and Other Incidents*, and *Select Conversations with an Uncle*. His short fiction appeared heavily in the *Pall Mall Gazette*, *Pall Mall Budget*, *New Budget*, *Idler*, and related magazines.

### Woking, Worcester Park, Sandgate

Wells and Jane lived at **Lynton, 141 Maybury Road, Woking**, from May 1895 for roughly eighteen months. This was one of his most concentrated creative periods: he completed *The Island of Doctor Moreau*, wrote *The Wheels of Chance*, planned *The War of the Worlds*, and began material that became *When the Sleeper Wakes* and *Love and Mr Lewisham*. They moved to **Worcester Park** in 1896 and, because of Wells's health, to **Sandgate** on the Kent coast in 1898.

The sequence of scientific romances established him internationally:

- *The Island of Doctor Moreau* (1896): vivisection, plasticity, animal/human boundaries, evolutionary reversion.
- *The Wheels of Chance* (1896): bicycle mobility, class, courtship.
- *The Invisible Man* (1897): experimental isolation, criminality, and bodily absence.
- *The War of the Worlds* (serialized 1897; book 1898): technologically superior imperial invasion reversed onto southern England; the narrator explicitly compares Martian treatment of humans with human treatment of colonized peoples and animals.
- *When the Sleeper Wakes* (1899; revised as *The Sleeper Awakes*, 1910): finance, accumulation, mass society, and oligarchic future.
- *Love and Mr Lewisham* (1900): education and lower-middle-class aspiration.
- *The First Men in the Moon* (1901): space travel through the fictional gravity-screening substance “cavorite,” with a differentiated lunar society.

These were literary technologies, not engineering prototypes. Wells held no relevant patent and did not claim experimentally to have produced invisibility, time travel, antigravity, tanks, or atomic weapons.

### Spade House and family establishment

Architect **C. F. A. Voysey** designed **Spade House** at Sandgate, completed in 1901. The house's name arose from an inverted-heart motif. Wells's increased earnings allowed a purpose-built residence, staff, travel, and family life. George Philip was born in 1901 and Frank Richard in 1903.

## Chronological Life History, 1901–1914

### Anticipations, sociology, and the “New Republic”

With *Anticipations* (1901), *Mankind in the Making* (1903), and *A Modern Utopia* (1905), Wells shifted from fiction containing politics to explicit social forecasting. He argued that railways, automobiles, communications, urban expansion, and technical expertise were dissolving nineteenth-century institutions. His imagined “New Republic” or world state would be administered by trained, scientifically literate elites.

This program contained incompatible-seeming elements that remained together in Wells's work:

- opposition to inherited rank, nationalist sovereignty, and unaccountable private wealth;
- advocacy of education, women's economic independence, birth control, and a universal basic social minimum;
- suspicion of parliamentary party politics and mass opinion;
- attraction to elite administration—the “Samurai” of *A Modern Utopia*;
- “negative eugenics”: preventing categories defined as hereditarily diseased, “defective,” addicted, or antisocial from reproducing, sometimes by coercive sterilization, segregation, or death.

The eugenic language in *Anticipations* is not merely later hostile paraphrase. Wells wrote of nations that might “sterilize,” “export,” or “poison” their “People of the Abyss” and of the “euthanasia of the weak and sensual.” In *A Modern Utopia* he preferred “the sterilisation of failures” to selective breeding of an approved type, while also proposing exclusionary islands and state killing of severely diseased births. Later genetic knowledge and the Nazi program altered his language and some conclusions, but did not make the earlier texts disappear.

### Fabian Society struggle, 1903–1908

Wells joined the **Fabian Society** in 1903. Sponsored and encouraged by George Bernard Shaw, he soon attacked its small membership, limited propaganda apparatus, and rule by the “Old Gang”—Sidney and Beatrice Webb, Shaw, Edward Pease, and their circle. His 1906 “Faults of the Fabian” and Committee of Enquiry proposed mass recruitment, local groups, a weekly review, a publications department, parliamentary candidates, a large election fund, and renaming the organization the British Socialist Party.

The contest became personally a **Wells-versus-Shaw** struggle for control. Wells was a powerful recruiter but erratic administrator; the old executive accepted fragments of reform while resisting his leadership. His proposal for an election fund drew promises of £2,637 against a £5,000 target. By 1908 the reform campaign had failed and Wells withdrew from sustained Fabian management, although he remained socialist and maintained friendships and quarrels with Fabians for decades. In *The New Machiavelli* (1911) he satirized Sidney and Beatrice Webb as Oscar and Altiora Bailey, intensifying the breach.

### United States and European travel

In 1906 Wells traveled in the United States, met **President Theodore Roosevelt**, observed New York, Washington, Chicago, and industrial America, and published *The Future in America*. His later travel writing repeatedly used elite interviews and industrial tours as evidence, but retained the form of a writer's reconnaissance rather than systematic field research.

### Fiction, domestic politics, and sexual crises

Wells's major books of the period included *Kipps* (1905), *In the Days of the Comet* (1906), *The War in the Air* (1908), *Tono-Bungay* (1909), *Ann Veronica* (1909), *The History of Mr Polly* (1910), *The New Machiavelli* (1911), *Marriage* (1912), and *The Passionate Friends* (1913). They converted autobiographical people and situations into recognizable fiction, sometimes without effective disguise. Friends and partners could identify themselves; the books functioned as art, political argument, and public airing of private conflict.

The affair with Amber Reeves became a public Fabian scandal. Beatrice Webb condemned what she called the “sordid intrigue.” The pregnancy, Blanco White marriage, and birth of Anna-Jane occurred around the publication of *Ann Veronica*, whose independent Cambridge heroine was widely read through the Reeves affair even though the novel was not a literal transcript.

Wells's “free love” rested on negotiated toleration by Jane, but it was asymmetrical. He was older, famous, married, and often a teacher, mentor, or senior political associate of younger women. His own memoir portrays the affairs as reciprocal encounters among sexual equals and calls them “digressions.” Surviving correspondence shows that women exercised agency, sometimes pursued him, and often remained friends; it also records jealousy, pregnancy, secrecy, and unequal burdens. No criminal charge or court finding of sexual violence is known. The historically supportable controversy concerns adultery, power imbalance, emotional damage, public fictionalization, and Wells's self-serving presentation—not a proved criminal offense.

### Little wars and “inventions”

*Floor Games* (1911) and *Little Wars* (1913) formalized imaginative floor play and miniature-wargame rules developed with his sons. *Little Wars* is an important ancestor of modern tabletop miniature gaming. It was a published rules project, not a patented technology.

Wells's 1903 story “The Land Ironclads” imagined armored cross-country fighting machines with pedrail-like wheels, influenced by Bramah Joseph Diplock's wheel concepts. Later accounts call this a prediction or invention of the tank. It anticipated tactical features but did not supply the engineering lineage of the British tank; Wells was not a tank designer.

### First Russian visit and Rebecca West

Wells visited tsarist Russia in January 1914, including St Petersburg and Moscow, and returned sympathetic to Russia as a potential counterweight to Germany. In 1913 his relationship with Rebecca West began; Anthony was born in 1914.

## War, Propaganda, and World Organization, 1914–1920

### First World War position

Wells supported Britain's war against Imperial Germany and published *The War That Will End War* (1914). The phrase became a symbol of liberal-war idealism and later disillusionment. He advocated defeating German militarism but argued that victory was defensible only if it produced democratic international organization.

His war books included *The World Set Free* (1914), *The War and Socialism* (1915), *The Peace of the World* (1915), *What Is Coming?* (1916), *Mr Britling Sees It Through* (1916), *War and the Future* (1917), and *In the Fourth Year* (1918). *Mr Britling* became an especially large American success and materially strengthened his finances.

### Government propaganda work

In May 1918 **Lord Northcliffe** recruited Wells to direct preparation of British propaganda against Germany at the Department of Propaganda in Enemy Countries, headquartered at **Crewe House** and reporting to the War Cabinet. Wells worked with historian J. W. Headlam-Morley, joined Crewe House's advisory committee, and drafted a memorandum aligning propaganda with stated Allied war aims. He left day-to-day direction of the German Section in July but remained on the committee; Hamilton Fyfe succeeded him.

This is a documented state-propaganda role. Crewe House coordinated with the Foreign Office, War Office, Air Ministry, Admiralty, and military and naval intelligence. That institutional proximity does not by itself establish Wells as a clandestine intelligence officer. No evidence consulted shows formal MI5 or MI6 employment.

### League of Nations projects

Wells collaborated with Edward Grey, Lionel Curtis, William Archer, Wickham Steed, A. E. Zimmern, J. A. Spender, James Bryce, Gilbert Murray, and others on *The Idea of a League of Nations* and *The Way to the League of Nations* (1919). He wanted an organization superior to sovereign states, armed or empowered to enforce world law. The actual League disappointed him as a diplomatic association of governments without sufficient executive power.

### *The Outline of History*

Wells undertook a universal history after the war. Jane typed, researched, organized correspondence, and criticized; specialists advised on portions; J. F. Horrabin supplied maps and illustrations. It first appeared in twenty-four illustrated fortnightly parts beginning in November 1919 and as volumes in 1920. It relied heavily on the 1911 *Encyclopaedia Britannica* and standard secondary works, presented human history as one species-wide story, minimized kings and battles, and foregrounded biological and social development.

The work sold more than two million copies, generated translations and spin-offs, and made Wells wealthy. Professional historians criticized errors, compression, and trespass into specialized fields. Hilaire Belloc attacked its treatment of Catholicism; Wells answered in *Mr. Belloc Objects to “The Outline of History”* (1926). The commercial “Outline” model produced *A Short History of the World* (1922), *The Science of Life* (1930), and *The Work, Wealth and Happiness of Mankind* (1931).

## World Figure, 1920–1933

### Soviet Russia, Lenin, Gorky, and Budberg

In 1920 Wells traveled to devastated Soviet Russia, stayed in Maxim Gorky's circle, met Lenin, and began his relationship with Moura Budberg. *Russia in the Shadows* described economic and institutional collapse more sharply than many sympathetic visitors did, while treating Lenin as the one figure capable of imposing a reconstructive plan. Wells rejected Bolshevik class-war doctrine and mocked Marxist orthodoxy, but hoped the regime could evolve toward technical reconstruction. Lenin reportedly regarded Wells as a bourgeois or philistine; the exact private wording comes through secondary recollection.

Moura later acquired the mythology of the “Red Mata Hari.” She had connections across Soviet, émigré, British diplomatic, and later Cambridge-spy social worlds. British security services investigated her. Writers have alleged that she worked for Soviet intelligence, British intelligence, or both. No public court judgment or conclusive released file proves a defined dual-agent service. Wells suspected espionage; suspicion is not proof.

### Washington, world lectures, and elections

Wells attended and wrote about the 1921–22 Washington Naval Conference, using it to press for international disarmament and world institutions. He lectured in Britain and abroad and became an interview-level interlocutor of presidents, prime ministers, revolutionary leaders, scientists, and writers.

He stood for Parliament as **Labour candidate for London University**:

- **1922:** 1,427 votes (19.18%), third behind Unionist Sydney Russell-Wells (3,833) and Liberal A. F. Pollard (2,180).
- **1923:** 1,420 votes (17.64%), again third behind Russell-Wells (4,037) and Pollard (2,593).

These were defeats, not symbolic appointments. Wells never sat in Parliament and increasingly argued that territorial electoral politics could not solve global technological problems.

### Residences and personal reorganization

In spring 1912 Wells had taken **Little Easton Rectory**, Essex, renamed **Easton Glebe**; it remained the family base until Jane's death in 1927. *Mr Britling Sees It Through* uses a fictionalized version. From 1924 Wells also lived extensively with **Odette Keun** at **Lou Pidou**, a house they built near Grasse in southern France. The domestic partnership ended by 1933. These overlapping residences reflected both international work and a life divided among Jane, lovers, children, and writing establishments.

Jane died on 6 October 1927 at Dunmow, aged 55, and was cremated at Golders Green. Wells described genuine devastation, while continuing other relationships. Her intellectual and logistical labor had been foundational but was long subordinated in the public author-brand “H. G. Wells.” *The Book of Catherine Wells* (1928), assembled after her death, preserved some of her writings.

### The Open Conspiracy and elite world administration

*The Open Conspiracy* (1928; revised 1930, 1931, and 1933) called on scientists, teachers, administrators, industrial organizers, and other “competent” people to coordinate openly across borders until a world commonwealth displaced sovereign-state rivalry. Wells described this not as a secret cabal but as an explicit, distributed movement.

The text later became raw material for conspiracy literature about a Wellsian “New World Order.” The documentary fact is that Wells publicly advocated a planned socialist world state and used phrases such as “open conspiracy” and “world revolution.” The unsupported extension is that he directed a hidden government or disclosed an already-existing cabal. His program was propaganda for a desired future, not evidence of concealed present control.

### Florence Deeks litigation

Canadian writer **Florence Amelia Deeks** alleged that Macmillan of Canada had transmitted her unpublished feminist world history, *The Web of the World's Romance*, to Wells and that he used it for *The Outline of History*. She sought $500,000 and sued Wells and several Macmillan/Newnes/Cassell entities for copyright infringement and breach of confidence.

- **Allegation:** Deeks had left her manuscript with Macmillan Canada on 8 August 1918; she later identified structural parallels and shared errors in Wells's book.
- **Wells's evidence:** He testified that he had never seen or heard of her manuscript.
- **Publisher evidence:** Macmillan witnesses testified that the manuscript remained in Canada and was never sent to Wells.
- **Trial and Ontario appeal:** Her case was dismissed; judges rejected the expert comparison as insufficient to overcome direct evidence of non-access.
- **Final result:** On 3 November 1932 the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council dismissed her appeal in *Deeks v Wells*, [1932] UKPC 66. Lord Atkin held that the manuscript had not been shown to leave Canada or reach Wells and that similarities were explicable through subject matter and common sources. This was a final judicial rejection, not a settlement or unresolved indictment.
- **Later dispute:** A. B. McKillop's *The Spinster and the Prophet* (2000) argued that gender and institutional bias distorted Deeks's treatment and found the plagiarism theory persuasive. Legal scholar Denis Magnusson replied that, whatever discriminatory experiences surrounded the case, the access and copying evidence remained legally weak and the result would probably be the same under modern doctrine. No later judgment reversed the 1932 decision.

## International Presidency and Final Political Phase, 1933–1946

### PEN, Nazi Germany, and the Black Book

Wells succeeded John Galsworthy as president of **International PEN** in 1933 and served through 1936. At the 1933 Dubrovnik congress he insisted that exiled German writer Ernst Toller be heard and participated in the removal of the Nazi-controlled German PEN center after it failed to oppose book burnings and excluded Jewish and dissident writers. Nazi students burned *The Outline of History* in May 1933; his works were banned.

The Reich Security Main Office included “H. G. Wells” in the **Sonderfahndungsliste G.B.**, later called the Black Book, a 1940 list of persons to be arrested after a German invasion of Britain. A surviving copy at the Hoover Institution confirms his inclusion. This was a German state targeting record, not later legend.

### Roosevelt and Stalin, 1934

Wells met President **Franklin D. Roosevelt** in the United States and then interviewed **Joseph Stalin** for about three hours in Moscow on 23 July 1934. The published transcript, recorded by Konstantin Umansky, shows Wells trying to interpret the New Deal as movement toward planned economy and disputing Stalin's two-class Marxist analysis. He told Stalin, provocatively, that he might be “more to the Left.”

Wells challenged elements of Stalin's doctrine but underestimated the dictatorship's violence. The interview occurred amid collectivization, famine's aftermath, police terror, and before the public climax of the Great Purge; Wells did not expose those structures. Critics treated the encounter as political credulity. It is inaccurate to call him a Stalinist: he rejected Marxism, class dictatorship, censorship, and later denounced Soviet repression. It is accurate that he granted Stalin legitimacy as a planning interlocutor and temporarily hoped Soviet and New Deal planning might converge.

### International PEN and India/Australia

Wells served as PEN president until 1936 and later chaired its wartime presidential committee from 1941 until death. He traveled to Australia in 1938–39, stopping in Bombay on 16 December 1939 during the return route according to PEN archival description. In Australia he antagonized Prime Minister Joseph Lyons by calling Hitler and Mussolini “criminal Caesars” and criticizing Australian policy, including racial exclusion and the treatment of Aboriginal people. He left Australia in February 1939; chronologies sometimes conflate the outward and homeward legs, so exact travel sequence should be checked against passenger records for any finer reconstruction.

### *Things to Come* and film work

Producer Alexander Korda's **Things to Come** (1936), directed by William Cameron Menzies, gave Wells unusual control as screenwriter and public theorist. Based on *The Shape of Things to Come* and related works, it dramatized world war, civilizational collapse, technocratic “Wings Over the World,” and a future conflict over space travel. Wells also scripted **The Man Who Could Work Miracles** (1936). His earlier *The King Who Was a King* (1929) was a published film scenario never produced as intended; “The New Faust” remained unmade.

The films were collaborations, not solely Wells's creations. Korda, Menzies, actors, designers, composers, and studio institutions transformed his scenarios. Wells's conflicts with filmmakers illustrate the limit of authorial control in cinema.

### World Brain

In lectures from 1936 onward and in *World Brain* (1938), Wells proposed a permanent, continually revised, authoritative world encyclopedia, widely accessible and integrated with education. He discussed microfilm and distributed documentary centers—not digital packet networks or personal computing. It is reasonable to call the project an ancestor in the history of universal knowledge systems and to compare it with the internet or Wikipedia. It is not literally a technical prediction of the World Wide Web.

### Diabetes and the Diabetic Association

Wells developed diabetes in later life; sources vary on the exact diagnosis date. With physician **R. D. Lawrence**, himself kept alive by insulin, he founded the **Diabetic Association** in 1934 at a meeting in Wells's London home. In a February 1934 *Times* appeal, Wells proposed an association open to diabetics “rich or poor,” supporting mutual aid, knowledge, research, and proper treatment. Wells became its first president. It became the British Diabetic Association in 1954 and Diabetes UK in 2000. The modern H. G. Wells Medal recognizes eighty years lived with diabetes.

### World Brain, world state, and authoritarian vocabulary

In *The Shape of Things to Come*, *The Open Conspiracy*, *After Democracy*, *The New World Order*, and allied texts, Wells repeatedly used “dictatorship,” “world control,” “world police,” “air dictatorship,” and rule by competent elites. Sometimes “dictatorship” meant emergency technical administration rather than personal tyranny. Nonetheless, the texts authorize coercion, centralized force, suppression of nationalist resistance, and rule not dependent on ordinary party democracy.

Countervailing texts are also documentary facts: Wells insisted on open criticism, free speech, science's corrigibility, and, after observing fascist and Soviet dictatorship, enforceable individual rights against centralized power. The tension between expert world government and accountable liberty was never completely solved.

### Race, empire, eugenics, Jews, and Zionism

Wells's record changed over time and is internally contradictory.

#### Race and empire

- *The War of the Worlds* reversed imperial extermination and explicitly asked readers to compare Martian conduct with British and Tasmanian colonial violence.
- Wells attacked racial prejudice as pseudo-science and argued in *A Modern Utopia* that supposed racial types were mixed, unstable categories.
- He nevertheless wrote with evolutionary hierarchies, civilizational rankings, and population-management assumptions common to imperial social science. His world state could treat resistant cultures as administrative problems.
- By the Second World War he attacked phrases about “backward races and inferior peoples” and rejected racial nationalism.

#### Eugenics

- **Documented:** From *Anticipations* through *A Modern Utopia* and into *The Science of Life*, Wells supported forms of negative eugenics—restrictions on reproduction by people classed as hereditarily diseased or “defective,” and in early work sterilization, segregation, euthanasia, or killing.
- **Qualification:** He opposed the “positive eugenic” breeding of a standardized superior type and argued that human variation and environmental reform mattered. His approach increasingly shifted toward voluntary birth control, maternal endowment, education, and individual reproductive choice.
- **Late change:** In *The Rights of Man* (1940) he called eugenics an unsupported speculation of theorists, and after knowledge of Nazi crimes became available he repudiated racial eugenics. Scholars disagree whether this constitutes a fundamental conversion or a late narrowing of earlier commitments.

#### Jews and Zionism

- Wells opposed antisemitic persecution and racial theories of Jewish inferiority; he defended Jewish and dissident writers through PEN and was himself targeted by the Nazis.
- He also wrote hostile generalizations. *In the Days of the Comet* describes Jews as social parasites; *The Fate of Homo Sapiens* (1939) treated Judaism and Jewish collective continuity as obstacles to assimilation into a world state, called Nazism “inverted Judaism,” and employed stereotypes of Jewish influence.
- His early world-state universalism was anti-Zionist because it opposed national separatism in general. After the Holocaust, he supported a Jewish state more directly and corresponded with Chaim Weizmann. The change did not erase prior statements.
- Therefore neither “Wells was simply an antisemite” nor “Wells was consistently a defender without anti-Jewish ideas” describes the documentary record.

### Religion

Wells was confirmed in the Church of England but wrote that at thirteen he expelled the punitive Christian God from his mind. Huxleyan agnosticism and scientific naturalism dominated his formation. During the First World War he experienced what he called a religious phase. *God the Invisible King* (1917) declared that he was “neither atheist nor Buddhist nor Mohammedan nor Christian” and proposed a finite, struggling, non-creator God arising in human minds and history. He rejected Trinity, providence, revelation, magic, clerical sexual morality, and an organized church.

This “finite God” was closer to collective ethical purpose than orthodox theism. Wells's later work returned toward secular world-humanism. *Crux Ansata* (1943) attacked the Roman Catholic Church as a political institution, especially the papacy's accommodations with fascism. Christianity nevertheless remained an enduring narrative and ethical vocabulary even when he repudiated its theology.

### Human rights campaign

In 1939–40 Wells used *The New World Order*, newspaper debate, and *The Rights of Man; or, What Are We Fighting For?* to demand a legally enforceable world declaration. Under former Lord Chancellor **John Sankey**, a committee revised the proposals into the **Sankey Declaration of Rights**. Wells promoted versions internationally, sent them to political leaders, distributed them through hundreds of editors, and arranged wartime dissemination into occupied Europe.

The later UN drafting process had many sources and authors. John Humphrey, principal drafter in the UN Secretariat, recorded Wells/Sankey material among documents consulted. Thus Wells's campaign was a documented precursor and input to the 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights; the stronger claim that he single-handedly “wrote the UDHR” is false.

### Second World War and final pessimism

Wells remained at 13 Hanover Terrace during much of the Blitz. He supported defeating Nazism but attacked the Allied leadership for lacking a sufficiently radical postwar program. George Orwell's 1941 “Wells, Hitler and the World State” argued that Wells's rational nineteenth-century categories could not comprehend fascist irrationalism, nationalism, and military barbarism. Wells and Orwell shared hostility to Nazism but differed over explanatory method and the prospects of rational world organization.

In *'42 to '44*, *Phoenix*, and *Mind at the End of Its Tether* (1945), Wells's confidence collapsed. The last book suggested that *Homo sapiens* might be reaching an evolutionary dead end and be replaced. It is an intellectual position written during illness, war, and disappointment; biographers also read it as late-life depression, but no clinical psychiatric diagnosis should be inferred solely from the text.

## Companies, Institutions, Technologies, Projects, and Financial Interests

### Publishing and literary business

Wells operated as a professional author through publishers including Heinemann, Macmillan, George Newnes, Cassell, Methuen, Chapman & Hall, Secker & Warburg, Penguin, and American firms. **A. P. Watt & Son** handled literary-agency business; archival files include contracts, memoranda, and financial records. Serial rights, British and American book editions, translations, newspaper syndication, collected editions, film rights, lectures, and broadcasting formed the revenue structure.

No evidence consulted indicates that Wells created a major incorporated business, speculative investment empire, or patent portfolio. His principal capital was copyright and public reputation.

### Financial trajectory

- Childhood and youth: chronic lower-middle-class insecurity, apprenticeship premiums, scholarships, low teaching salaries, and dependence on relatives.
- 1893–95: textbook and journalism income, followed by rapid security after *The Time Machine* and successive scientific romances.
- 1901 onward: sufficient income to commission Spade House and support a household, children, travel, and multiple residences.
- 1916–20: *Mr Britling* and especially *The Outline of History* transformed him from a successful novelist into a wealthy international author. Exact annual income and net worth varied with royalties, taxes, foreign rights, and family obligations and are not established by the public sources consulted.
- He supported Jane's household, children by Jane, Anna-Jane and Amber Reeves-related arrangements, Rebecca West and Anthony in contested ways, travel, and residences in England and France. Correspondence and legal/financial papers at Illinois and NYPL offer the primary route to exact amounts.

### Projects with technological afterlives

- **Time machine:** a literary device that consolidated mechanical time travel as a genre convention.
- **Land ironclads:** anticipatory fiction, not a tank patent.
- **Atomic bombs:** *The World Set Free* (written 1913, published 1914) used the phrase for continuously exploding radioactive weapons. The fictional physics was wrong in mechanism and scale. Physicist Leó Szilárd later stated that reading Wells informed his apprehension of what a nuclear chain reaction could mean and his desire for secrecy. This is evidence of influence, not evidence that Wells discovered nuclear fission.
- **World Brain:** institutional knowledge architecture using print, microfilm, documentation, and international educational administration; comparable to later networked encyclopedias but not a digital specification.
- **Little Wars:** influential miniature-wargame rule system.

## Political, Religious, Intellectual, and Artistic Development

### Persistent intellectual axes

1. **Evolution without guaranteed progress:** Huxley's Darwinism taught Wells that extinction and regression were possible. His optimism was therefore political, not biological inevitability.
2. **Education versus catastrophe:** Knowledge had to become an organized planetary function; otherwise technical power magnified tribal conflict.
3. **World state versus sovereign nation:** Nearly every political project after 1900 aimed at transferring force, law, transport, finance, education, or information above the nation-state.
4. **Individual liberty versus functional collectivism:** Wells wanted planned provision and coordinated expertise, yet increasingly recognized the danger of unchecked administration.
5. **Sexual freedom versus domestic dependence:** He argued for women's independence and freer unions while relying heavily on Jane's unpaid labor and imposing unequal consequences on partners.
6. **Species thinking:** Human beings appear as an animal species subject to ecology, selection, disease, adaptation, and possible extinction. This produced both anti-racist universalism and coercive eugenic proposals.

### Artistic phases

- **1887–94:** scientific journalism, comic sketches, experimental tales, *The Chronic Argonauts*.
- **1895–1901:** concentrated scientific romances and global literary breakthrough.
- **1900–16:** realistic “condition of England” novels and direct social prophecy.
- **1914–24:** war, world order, history, religion, popular synthesis.
- **1925–36:** encyclopedic systems, long novels of ideas, political manifestos, cinema, international celebrity.
- **1937–46:** World Brain, war aims, rights, anti-fascist polemic, and terminal pessimism.

## Important Relationships and Networks

- **Thomas Henry Huxley:** foundational scientific teacher; supplied evolution, agnosticism, and the model of public scientific education.
- **Horace Byatt:** Midhurst headmaster who identified Wells's ability and helped him escape drapery.
- **J. V. Milne:** Henley House proprietor and respected educational colleague.
- **George Bernard Shaw:** sponsor into Fabianism, political rival, enduring friend and antagonist. Their correspondence spans more than forty years.
- **Sidney and Beatrice Webb:** collaborators and then Fabian adversaries; targets of *The New Machiavelli*.
- **Joseph Conrad:** close literary friend during the 1890s and early 1900s; Wells helped efforts to obtain financial support for him. Political and aesthetic differences, including the war, cooled the friendship.
- **Henry James:** early admirer who called Wells the most interesting literary man of his generation. Their debate opposed Wells's socially purposeful, capacious novel to James's art of consciousness and form. Wells's caricature of James as a hippopotamus laboring to pick up a pea in *Boon* ended the friendship despite attempted apology.
- **Arnold Bennett, Ford Madox Ford, George Gissing:** major fin-de-siècle/Edwardian literary associates; relationships combined mutual aid, rivalry, and satire.
- **G. K. Chesterton and Hilaire Belloc:** religious, political, and historical opponents; Wells debated distributism, Catholicism, history, and social planning with them.
- **Virginia Woolf and modernists:** Woolf used Wells, Bennett, and Galsworthy as foils for “materialist” fiction. Later literary history often reproduced this modernist demotion.
- **Winston Churchill:** admirer and ideological opponent. Correspondence began by 1901; Churchill borrowed language and social-policy ideas from Wells and acknowledged a debt before a 1906 speech. Both addressed aviation, mechanized war, atomic danger, and world order from different politics.
- **Maxim Gorky, Lenin, Stalin:** successive Soviet literary and political interlocutors; Wells was never a Communist Party member.
- **Franklin D. Roosevelt and Theodore Roosevelt:** presidential interview partners in different American phases.
- **Julian Huxley and G. P. Wells:** co-authors of *The Science of Life*.
- **R. D. Lawrence:** physician, fellow diabetic, and co-founder of the Diabetic Association.
- **John Sankey, Ritchie Calder, Leonard Woolf, Gilbert Murray, Lionel Curtis:** collaborators in rights, League, propaganda, and world-order projects.
- **Alexander Korda and William Cameron Menzies:** cinematic collaborators and sources of conflict over translating Wellsian programs into film.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, Crimes, Fraud, Corruption, or Financial Questions

### Documented conduct

- Adultery and numerous overlapping relationships occurred over decades and were substantially admitted by Wells.
- He used students, friends, lovers, and political associates as fictional models, sometimes recognizably and against their preferences.
- He advocated coercive negative-eugenic measures in published works.
- He directed British enemy propaganda for part of 1918.
- He lost two parliamentary elections.
- He publicly underestimated aspects of Stalinism in 1934.

### Allegations not established as fact

- **Plagiarism from Florence Deeks:** formally alleged, litigated, and finally rejected by the Privy Council in 1932. Later historical controversy persists, but there was no finding against Wells.
- **Moura Budberg as double agent:** widely alleged and investigated; not conclusively proved in the public record consulted. Her possible intelligence work does not demonstrate Wells's knowing participation.
- **Wells as intelligence agent:** his official propaganda committee had intelligence liaison. No evidence consulted establishes clandestine employment as an intelligence officer.
- **Criminal sexual conduct:** no known charge, indictment, or conviction. Criticism of power imbalance and exploitation should not be converted into an undocumented criminal allegation.
- **Financial crime/corruption/money laundering:** none found in the consulted archival descriptions, judgments, biographies, or institutional histories.

### Public and intellectual disputes

- Fabian struggle with Shaw/Webbs, 1906–08.
- Amber Reeves scandal, 1908–09.
- Henry James quarrel after *Boon*, 1915.
- Catholic criticism of *The Outline of History* and Wells's exchanges with Belloc.
- Deeks litigation, 1926/28–32.
- Criticism of Stalin interview, 1934.
- PEN confrontation with Nazi German center, 1933–34.
- Orwell's attack on Wellsian rationalism, 1941.
- Continuing controversy over eugenics, race, Judaism, Zionism, technocracy, and sexual autobiography.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### “Father of science fiction”

1. **Story:** Wells founded science fiction.
2. **Appearance/circulation:** Twentieth-century genre histories, publishers, film promotion, and the H. G. Wells cult; often shared with Jules Verne and Hugo Gernsback.
3. **Evidence:** Wells decisively shaped time travel, invasion, future war, biological engineering, planetary travel, and the British scientific romance. Mary Shelley, Poe, Verne, Bulwer-Lytton, and many others preceded him; Gernsback institutionalized the later magazine category.
4. **Effect:** The title secures Wells's canonical centrality but obscures collective genre origins and his non-fiction, realism, and politics.

### The literal prophet of tanks, atomic bombs, airplanes, television, and the internet

1. **Story:** Wells accurately invented or predicted a list of modern technologies.
2. **Circulation:** Obituaries, science-fiction publicity, technology journalism, documentaries, and internet lists.
3. **Evidence:** He imaginatively extrapolated armored vehicles, aerial war, radioactivity-based bombs, mass visual communication, suburbs, and world knowledge systems. Many concepts had scientific or technical predecessors; details were often wrong; prediction was embedded in satire and political scenario. Szilárd's testimony supplies unusually strong evidence of real-world influence for *The World Set Free*.
4. **Effect:** “Prophet” became the central Wells persona, sometimes eclipsing failed forecasts—short wars, weak Nazism in *The Shape of Things to Come*, rapid world government, and technological administration defeating nationalism.

### “I told you so, you damned fools” as epitaph

1. **Story:** Wells's grave bears this sentence.
2. **Origin:** Wells proposed a version in the 1941 preface to *The War in the Air* and in conversation: “I told you so. You damned fools.”
3. **Evidence:** The remark is authentically Wellsian, but he has no conventional grave inscription; he was cremated and his ashes scattered at sea.
4. **Effect:** It condensed the prophet myth into a posthumous catchphrase.

### Orson Welles's *War of the Worlds* caused nationwide American panic

1. **Story:** The 30 October 1938 CBS broadcast convinced the United States that Martians had landed and produced mass flight or social collapse.
2. **Circulation:** Immediate newspaper coverage—at a moment of competition between newspapers and radio—later film, radio, and anniversary retellings.
3. **Evidence:** Some listeners were frightened and authorities received calls; later audience research indicates the national audience and scale of panic were greatly exaggerated. H. G. Wells himself later spoke with Orson Welles and joked about the publicity and sales.
4. **Effect:** The broadcast permanently fused Wells/Welles, Martian invasion, media power, and mass credulity.

### Wells as architect of a secret “New World Order”

1. **Story:** Wells disclosed or directed an elite conspiracy for global domination.
2. **Circulation:** Anti-globalist and conspiracy literature drawing titles and phrases from *The Open Conspiracy* and *The New World Order*.
3. **Evidence:** Wells openly advocated a socialist world state led initially by scientifically and administratively competent networks. His conspiracy was explicitly “open,” published, debated, and aspirational. No evidence shows his control of a hidden state.
4. **Effect:** His own provocative terminology allowed later enemies to turn public utopian propaganda into alleged confession.

### Wells as uncomplicated progressive humanist or uncomplicated totalitarian

Both are posthumous selection myths. The first foregrounds anti-imperial reversals, women's independence, anti-Nazism, education, public health, and rights while omitting eugenics and elite coercion. The second foregrounds Samurai, world police, sterilization, and dictatorship while omitting open criticism, anti-racism, and enforceable individual rights. The contradiction is in the archive, not merely in later reputation.

## Death and Final Status

Wells lived at **13 Hanover Terrace** from 1933 until death. Diabetes, declining strength, and a liver tumor/cancer are frequently cited in biographies. Public sources also repeat heart attack or “natural causes.” The accessible sources consulted do not reproduce a death certificate establishing a single immediate medical cause; the most responsible formulation is that he died in his sleep on 13 August 1946 while suffering diabetes and a documented/reported liver tumor, with the immediate cause variously reported.

He was cremated at **Golders Green Crematorium on 16 August 1946**. G. P. and Frank Wells scattered his ashes in the English Channel off **Old Harry Rocks**, near Swanage, Dorset. There is therefore no inscribed grave bearing the famous proposed epitaph.

Moura Budberg cared for him during the final illness but did not marry him. His estate consisted chiefly of property, papers, copyrights, and publication interests; exact probate valuation was not established in the sources consulted.

## Honors, Awards, Offices, and Commemoration

- University of London BSc, 1890; D.Litt., 1936.
- President, International PEN, 1933–36; wartime presidential committee, 1941–46.
- First president, Diabetic Association, 1934.
- Nobel Prize in Literature nominations in **1921, 1932, 1935, and 1946**; he never received the prize. The official Nobel archive confirms individual nominations, including Henrik Schück's 1921 nomination; later compiled records supply the four-year total.
- Reported to have declined appointment to the British **Order of Merit** during George V's reign. Publicly accessible secondary lists support the refusal, but the underlying offer file was not located for this dossier.
- Nazi book burning and Black Book targeting were hostile state “honors” in the sense of political reputation, not awards.
- Greater London Council blue plaque at 13 Hanover Terrace, erected 1966; Bromley plaque at the modern birthplace site.
- Impact craters named **Wells** on the Moon and Mars; commemorative statues, societies, conferences, and awards use his name.
- The H. G. Wells Medal of Diabetes UK recognizes people who have lived with diabetes for eighty years.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### Literary canon

Wells's status split after death. *The Time Machine*, *The War of the Worlds*, *The Invisible Man*, *The Island of Doctor Moreau*, and selected stories became permanent popular classics. Much of his realistic fiction, world history, and political system-building fell out of ordinary reading. Modernist criticism, especially Woolf's categorization of Wells as a “materialist,” helped demote him within high-literary canons even as science fiction elevated him.

Later scholarship restored the unity of the archive: the scientific romances, sex novels, Fabian politics, world histories, film, rights campaigns, and World Brain all investigate how knowledge and power organize human beings.

### Science fiction and futurism

British scientific romance, American magazine science fiction, dystopia, invasion narrative, time-travel fiction, future war, planetary romance, mad-scientist fiction, and posthuman speculation all drew from Wells. Olaf Stapledon, J. D. Beresford, J. B. S. Haldane, C. S. Lewis (by opposition), George Orwell, Arthur C. Clarke, Brian Aldiss, John Wyndham, Christopher Priest, Stephen Baxter, and many others acknowledged or displayed the influence.

### Politics and institutions

The world-state movement failed in Wells's lifetime. The League of Nations disappointed him and the UN did not become the functional sovereign he imagined. Yet international organization, global education, public-health advocacy, rights declarations, knowledge networks, and transnational professional bodies developed along lines that make his questions durable even when his proposed machinery did not prevail.

### Historical reinterpretation

- **Mid-century:** prophet, rationalist, and founding science-fiction master.
- **Modernist literary history:** prolific Edwardian “materialist,” artistically inferior to James, Joyce, and Woolf.
- **1960s–90s scholarship:** recovery of scientific romance, utopia, world-state thought, and correspondence.
- **Post-1984:** sexual memoir and Anthony West's biography transformed public knowledge of the “free-love” life.
- **Twenty-first century:** sharper focus on eugenics, race, gendered labor, technocracy, human rights, media systems, ecological and extinction thinking, and the Deeks dispute.

No single reinterpretation cancels the others. Wells remains simultaneously an archive of emancipation and control: class mobility and social provision; imperial reversal and civilizational hierarchy; sexual freedom and domestic asymmetry; universal rights and coercive planning; scientific skepticism and prophet mythology.

## Atlas Connections

The roster was checked conservatively. The entries below are supported by identifiable writings, correspondence, recorded conversations, photographs, or documented meetings. Mere contemporaneity, thematic similarity, later influence, and incidental name-checks have been excluded.

### Abraham

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells treated Abraham as an ancestral Hebrew patriarch in the chapters on early Jewish history in *The Outline of History* (1919–20) and its condensation, *A Short History of the World* (1922). This is a later writer’s historical treatment of an earlier figure, not a personal crossing. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Alexander the Great

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells devoted a substantial section of *The Outline of History* to Alexander and an entire chapter of *A Short History of the World* to “The Empire of Alexander the Great,” interpreting his conquests as a portent—though an unsuccessful one—of political world-unification. [Primary text and contents](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Aristotle

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells discussed Aristotle’s Lyceum, philosophy, scientific work, and position as Alexander’s tutor in *The Outline of History* and *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Chaim Weizmann

- **[T3]** **Documented fact:** During Wells’s late movement from anti-national assimilationism toward support for a Jewish homeland, he opened a correspondence with Zionist leader Chaim Weizmann. The exchange belongs to Wells’s final years, after his earlier hostile statements about Jewish collective nationalism; it documents written engagement, not a demonstrated friendship or meeting. The Wells correspondence archive is extensive but only representatively indexed online, so exact letter dates should be checked in the published correspondence or Illinois originals. [University of Illinois Wells archive](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/Wellsdesc.html)

### Charlemagne

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells treated Charlemagne’s conquests, imperial coronation, and the subsequent fragmentation of his empire in the medieval chapters of *The Outline of History* and *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Charles Darwin

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells repeatedly wrote about Darwin as a decisive maker of the modern evolutionary worldview—in his histories of science, biological works, and autobiography. Darwinian evolution was also fundamental to Wells’s own intellectual formation, although Wells learned it most directly through T. H. Huxley rather than from Darwin personally.

### Christopher Columbus

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells narrated Columbus’s westward voyage and its place in European overseas expansion in *The Outline of History* and *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Constantine the Great

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells discussed Constantine’s conversion, imperial religious policy, and role in the political establishment of Christianity in *The Outline of History* and its shorter adaptation. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Albert Einstein

- **[T4]** **Documented fact:** Einstein and Wells were present together at Wells’s 15 April 1929 address, “The Common Sense of World Peace,” in the Reichstag building in Berlin. A contemporary photograph places both men with German officials at the event. This securely establishes shared time and place; the available record does not justify enlarging it into a close scientific collaboration. [Contemporary report of the Reichstag address](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2021/apr/16/danger-of-drifting-into-war-hg-wells-addresses-reichstag-1929) [Photographic record](https://www.granger.com/results.asp?image=0196457)

### Federico García Lorca

- **[T3]** **Documented fact:** As president of International PEN, Wells sent a telegram in 1936 seeking information about García Lorca after the poet disappeared in Nationalist-held Granada. The inquiry was a one-sided written intervention on behalf of a persecuted writer; Lorca had already been murdered, although Wells and PEN did not yet know it.

### Genghis Khan

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells wrote a sustained account of Genghis Khan and the Mongol conquests in *The Outline of History* and in the chapter “The Mongol Conquests” in *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Mahatma Gandhi

- **[T3]** **Documented fact:** In April 1940 Wells solicited Gandhi’s opinion of his proposed declaration of human rights. Gandhi replied by cable that Wells should begin with a declaration of the “duties of man,” after which rights would follow. The exchange was published during the international debate surrounding Wells’s rights campaign; it establishes direct correspondence, not a documented personal meeting. [Gandhi’s cable and editorial note](https://www.gandhipedia150.in/static/data/highlighted_pdfs_output/Ahmedabad_volume71_book_414.pdf) [Wells’s rights text in the Illinois collection](https://www.library.illinois.edu/rbx/hgwells2016/2016/09/the-rights-of-man-or-what-are-we-fighting-for-2/)

### Hannibal

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells narrated Hannibal’s invasion of Italy, victories over Rome, and eventual defeat in his account of the Roman–Carthaginian struggle in *The Outline of History* and *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Jesus of Nazareth

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells devoted substantial sections of *The Outline of History* and a chapter titled “The Teaching of Jesus” in *A Short History of the World* to Jesus, distinguishing the historical teacher and his ethical message from later doctrinal Christianity. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Julius Caesar

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells treated Caesar’s military career, dictatorship, assassination, and place in Rome’s transition from republic to empire in both *The Outline of History* and *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Justinian I

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells discussed Justinian’s eastern Roman empire, wars of reconquest, legal codification, and relationship with Theodora in his account of the Byzantine and Sassanid worlds. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Karl Marx

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells repeatedly analyzed and criticized Marx’s doctrines, historical method, class analysis, and political afterlife. Extended treatments appear in *The Outline of History*, *The Work, Wealth and Happiness of Mankind*, *The Open Conspiracy*, and the published 1934 Stalin–Wells discussion. Wells identified as a socialist but rejected Marxist orthodoxy and class dictatorship.

### Martin Luther

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells narrated Luther’s challenge to the papacy and his role in the Protestant Reformation in *The Outline of History* and the chapter “The Reformation of the Latin Church” in *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Moses

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells discussed Moses, the Exodus tradition, Mosaic law, and the formation of Hebrew monotheism in his chapters on early Jewish history. He treated the surviving narrative historically and critically rather than simply reproducing it as scripture. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Muhammad

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells devoted a major portion of *The Outline of History* and a full chapter of *A Short History of the World*, “Muhammad and Islam,” to Muhammad’s preaching, political leadership, and the emergence of Islam. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Napoleon Bonaparte

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells wrote at length about Napoleon’s rise, wars, empire, defeat, and political legacy in *The Outline of History* and in the concluding portions of *A Short History of the World* dealing with the French Revolution and post-Napoleonic settlement. [Primary text and contents](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### George Orwell

- **[T5]** **Documented fact:** Orwell and Wells met socially in wartime London, including a contentious 1941 supper associated with Inez Holden. Their larger crossing included Orwell’s August 1941 essay “Wells, Hitler and the World State,” Wells’s angry written response, and further criticism by Orwell. The meeting is therefore documented, while descriptions of the argument’s exact wording depend on participants’ recollections. [Orwell’s essay in his collected writings](https://lust-for-life.org/Lust-For-Life/_Textual/GeorgeOrwell_TheCollectedEssaysJournalismAndLettersOfGeorgeOrwell-VolumeII-MyCountryRightOrLeft-1940-1943_SoniaOrwell-IanAngus-editors_1970_327pp/GeorgeOrwell_TheCollectedEssaysJournalismAndLettersOfGeorgeOrwell-VolumeII-MyCountryRightOrLeft-1940-1943_SoniaOrwell-IanAngus-editors_1970_327pp.pdf) [Oxford research discussing the meeting and Wells’s letter](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A32db08e5-47ce-4b45-9a80-abcbb37d1f9e/files/ma216ef7afec61fd61602cb99b72e504e)

### Plato

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells wrote about Plato and the Academy in *The Outline of History* and engaged directly with Platonic political philosophy in *A Modern Utopia*. Plato supplied both a precedent for utopian social construction and a model against which Wells defined his own modern, evolutionary utopia. [Wells works index and *Outline* contents](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/57490/57490-h/57490-h.htm)

### Rabindranath Tagore

- **[T5]** **Documented fact:** Wells and Tagore met in Geneva in early June 1930 and conducted a recorded conversation about cultural difference, language, mechanization, education, and the prospect of a universal civilization. The published dialogue preserves direct reciprocal speech and establishes a personal meeting rather than merely parallel internationalism. [Dialogue reproduced from *A Tagore Reader*](https://schoolofwisdom.com/about/rabindranath-tagore-one-of-the-school-of-wisdoms-most-notable-teachers/tagore-and-h-g-wells/) [Tagore manuscript record referring to his meeting with Wells](https://bichitra.jdvu.ac.in/manuscript/manuscript_viewer.php?manid=449&mname=RBVBMS_329)

### Thomas More

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells explicitly engaged More’s *Utopia* in *A Modern Utopia* (1905), treating it as an important predecessor while distinguishing Wells’s mobile, planetary, evolutionary state from More’s static island commonwealth.

### Jules Verne

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells wrote retrospectively about Verne while distinguishing Verne’s extrapolative adventure machinery from his own “fantasies of possibility.” The comparison became central to later accounts of the origins of science fiction, but no well-supported personal meeting between Wells and Verne has been established.

### William Morris

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells wrote retrospectively about Morris’s socialist lectures, personality, aesthetic politics, and influence on his student generation in *Experiment in Autobiography*. Wells had also attended Morris’s lectures during his South Kensington years; the surviving evidence supports attendance and later written recollection, but not a personal friendship.

### George Washington

- **[T1-]** **Documented fact:** Wells treated Washington’s military and political role in the American Revolution in *The Outline of History* and the chapter “The American War of Independence” in *A Short History of the World*. [Primary text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/35461/35461-h/35461-h.htm)

### Absence and exclusion note

No secure T2 prophecy/hyperstition or T9 shared-object connection was established with a roster member. In particular, Wells’s fictional “atomic bombs” should not automatically be assigned as a T2 connection to Albert Einstein, Enrico Fermi, or J. Robert Oppenheimer: the documented causal testimony runs principally through Leó Szilárd, who is not on this roster, and does not by itself prove that Wells’s story caused any listed individual’s later work. Likewise, influence claims involving Borges, Lovecraft, Orwell, Tolkien, Philip K. Dick, Frank Herbert, Steven Spielberg, or later futurists do not constitute crossings under the specified taxonomy without direct written engagement, meeting, prophecy-causation, or a shared-object chain.
