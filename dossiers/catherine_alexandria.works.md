## Works by Catherine of Alexandria

### Historically attributable works

- **No secure date:** No surviving object, composition, or project can be historically attributed to Catherine. Her existence itself is unverified: no contemporary source records her, and her *passio* emerged centuries after the traditionally assigned martyrdom; consequently there is no historical Catherine corpus comparable to a collected-works edition or catalogue raisonné. ([Walsh](https://www.routledge.com/The-Cult-of-St-Katherine-of-Alexandria-in-Early-Medieval-Europe/Walsh/p/book/9781315240428); [Larison](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/246/files/Larison_uchicago_0330D_13197.pdf))

### Utterances made by the legendary Catherine

- **Traditionally c. 305–308:** Untitled rebuke of the emperor and defense of Christianity. This is an oral manifesto in the legend: Catherine publicly condemns imperial sacrifice and persecution, thereby precipitating her arrest; its words belong to successive anonymous hagiographers, not to a recoverable historical speaker. ([Walsh](https://www.routledge.com/The-Cult-of-St-Katherine-of-Alexandria-in-Early-Medieval-Europe/Walsh/p/book/9781315240428); [Huygens Institute](https://art-of-reasoning.huygens.knaw.nl/women-disputation.html))

- **Traditionally c. 305–308:** Untitled disputation with the pagan philosophers. A legendary performance of dialectic rather than an independently transmitted treatise: Catherine’s arguments convert her opponents, but survive only as dialogue embedded in much later versions of her life and passion. ([Huygens Institute](https://art-of-reasoning.huygens.knaw.nl/women-disputation.html); [National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/glossary/st-catherine-of-alexandria))

- **Traditionally c. 305–308:** Untitled prison exhortations and prophecies. The legends place speeches to the empress, Porphyry, soldiers, and other converts in Catherine’s mouth; these resist treatment as separate works because their number and wording change with the recension of the *passio*. ([Walsh](https://www.routledge.com/The-Cult-of-St-Katherine-of-Alexandria-in-Early-Medieval-Europe/Walsh/p/book/9781315240428); [Larison](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/246/files/Larison_uchicago_0330D_13197.pdf))

- **Traditionally c. 305–308:** Untitled prayer before execution. This is a legendary oral protocol of intercession—asking benefits for those who remember or invoke Catherine—rather than an authorial text; one early form also concerns the future disposition of her body, a passage omitted by some later redactions as her relic cult developed. ([Larison](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/record/246/files/Larison_uchicago_0330D_13197.pdf))

### Legendary performative acts

- **Traditionally c. 305–308:** Sign of the cross made over the converted philosophers. This is a gesture that acts rather than describes—a nonverbal blessing and confirmation before their execution—but it is an episode of the developed legend, not a historically documented object or artwork.

- **Traditionally c. 305–308:** Conversion of the philosophers and members of the imperial household. These conversions are “made” by Catherine only in the causal idiom of hagiography: they are effects attributed to her argument and example, not works separable from the narrative that reports them. ([National Gallery](https://www.nationalgallery.org.uk/paintings/glossary/st-catherine-of-alexandria); [Huygens Institute](https://art-of-reasoning.huygens.knaw.nl/women-disputation.html))

### Posthumous manifestations

- **c. 1424–1431:** Apparitions, voices, and counsels reported by Joan of Arc. Joan identified Catherine as one of the saints who appeared, spoke, comforted, instructed, and helped direct her mission; these are manifestations claimed by Joan, not securely attributable creations by Catherine, and the 1431 record filters them through judicial questioning and scribal transmission. ([Hobbins, *The Trial of Joan of Arc*](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.4159/9780674038684/html?lang=en); [International Joan of Arc Society](https://www.joanofarcsociety.org/joan-resources/texts-related-to-joan-of-arc/))

### Works wrongly implied by later imagery

- **Medieval period onward:** The book carried by Catherine in visual art. It is an iconographic attribute signifying learning and doctrine, not evidence that she composed a book; no title, manuscript, or ancient attribution supports treating it as an obra. ([*Encyclopedia of Ancient Christianity*](https://www.thetbs.org/study-materials/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Encyclopedia-of-Ancient-Christianity-by-Angelo-Di-Berardino-Thomas-C.-Oden-Joel-C.-Elowsky-James-Hoover-z-lib.org_.pdf); [Huygens Institute](https://art-of-reasoning.huygens.knaw.nl/women-disputation.html))
