## Works by Walter Elias Disney

### Early films and silent series

- **1921:** *Newman Laugh-O-Grams*. A series of topical animated inserts made for Kansas City exhibitor Frank Newman; Disney drew and directed them, but surviving records and prints are incomplete, so the series resists reconstruction as a fixed corpus. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Little Red Riding Hood*. An animated fairy-tale short made by Laugh-O-gram Films; it survives, although later retitling and sound reissue complicate its textual identity. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *The Four Musicians of Bremen*. An animated Laugh-O-gram fairy-tale short; Disney was producer-director within a small collaborative studio rather than its sole maker. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Jack and the Beanstalk*. An animated Laugh-O-gram fairy-tale short, now lost. Its entry is therefore a documented production rather than an extant film-object. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Jack the Giant Killer*. An animated Laugh-O-gram fairy-tale short whose survival status is uncertain or fragmentary in filmographies. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Goldie Locks and the Three Bears*. An animated Laugh-O-gram fairy-tale short, generally treated as lost. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Puss in Boots*. An animated Laugh-O-gram fairy-tale short; the surviving print circulated under later titles, making the manifestation distinct from the original release. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Cinderella*. An animated Laugh-O-gram fairy-tale short, documented but lost. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html))
- **1922:** *Tommy Tucker’s Tooth*. A commissioned dental-health film combining instruction and entertainment—a protocol in advertising-film form rather than an ordinary theatrical cartoon. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/030114_MultiplaneGuideFINAL.pdf))
- **1923:** *Martha*. A Laugh-O-gram production known from documentation but apparently unfinished or unreleased; as an abandoned project, it resists being called a completed film. ([Russell Merritt and J. B. Kaufman, *Walt in Wonderland*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Walt_in_Wonderland.html?id=syA3AQAAIAAJ))
- **1923:** *Alice’s Wonderland*. A live-action/animation hybrid made in Kansas City and used as the demonstration film for the subsequent Alice series. Its images were collectively animated, so Disney’s authorship is directorial, entrepreneurial and conceptual rather than solitary. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/blog/hollywood-cartoonland-walt-disneys-alice-comedies))
- **1923–1927:** *Alice Comedies*. A corpus of fifty-seven released live-action/animation shorts initiated by Disney and produced through the Disney Brothers studio; individual films were collaborative and several are lost or incomplete. The series is a hybrid obra rather than a single text. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/rr/mopic/findaid/animate.html); [Merritt and Kaufman](https://books.google.com/books/about/Walt_in_Wonderland.html?id=syA3AQAAIAAJ))
- **1927:** *Poor Papa*. The pilot Oswald cartoon; Universal rejected it initially, and its later release means that production date and public manifestation do not coincide. ([Merritt and Kaufman](https://books.google.com/books/about/Walt_in_Wonderland.html?id=syA3AQAAIAAJ))
- **1927–1928:** *Oswald the Lucky Rabbit*. A series of twenty-six silent cartoons produced by Disney’s studio for Universal. Disney created the project with Ub Iwerks and collaborators but lost contractual control of the character; authorship and ownership therefore sharply diverge. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/030114_MultiplaneGuideFINAL.pdf); [Merritt and Kaufman](https://books.google.com/books/about/Walt_in_Wonderland.html?id=syA3AQAAIAAJ))
- **1928:** *Plane Crazy*. The first Mickey Mouse film produced, though not the first released; its initial silent preview and later synchronized-sound release are distinct manifestations. Mickey’s visual creation is jointly attributable to Disney and Ub Iwerks rather than securely to Disney alone. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/030114_MultiplaneGuideFINAL.pdf); [Disney Publishing](https://books.disney.com/book-author/j-b-kaufman/))
- **1928:** *The Gallopin’ Gaucho*. The second Mickey film produced and another initially silent production subsequently issued with synchronized sound. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/030114_MultiplaneGuideFINAL.pdf))
- **1928:** *Steamboat Willie*. A synchronized-sound animated short directed by Disney and animated principally by Ub Iwerks; it was Mickey Mouse’s first widely released manifestation, not the character’s first film in production. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/030114_MultiplaneGuideFINAL.pdf))
- **1928–1953:** *Mickey Mouse theatrical cartoons*. A collaborative corpus built around a character co-created by Disney and Iwerks; Disney initially directed and voiced Mickey but did not personally make every cartoon bearing his name. Treating the entire series as his individual œuvre would reproduce the studio’s branding rather than its production history. ([Disney Publishing](https://books.disney.com/book-author/j-b-kaufman/); [AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1929:** *The Skeleton Dance*. The inaugural Silly Symphony, directed by Disney, animated by Iwerks and scored by Carl Stalling; it is an audiovisual construction rather than simply an illustrated musical composition. ([Museum of Modern Art](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/press_archives/285/releases/MOMA_1935-37_0008.pdf))
- **1929–1939:** *Silly Symphonies*. A seventy-five-film experimental cartoon series produced under Disney’s supervision. Direction, animation and music belong to changing collaborators, so the corpus is properly a studio obra and testing ground, not seventy-five solo works by Disney. ([J. B. Kaufman](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/J-B-Kaufman/404344155))

### Animated feature films and audiovisual Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1937:** *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs*. A feature-length animated film produced and creatively supervised by Disney but made by hundreds of studio artists, musicians and technicians; its customary attribution to “Walt Disney” names an organizing authorship, not sole execution. ([J. B. Kaufman, *The Fairest One of All*](https://www.jbkaufman.com/fairest-one-all))
- **1940:** *Pinocchio*. An animated feature produced under Disney’s supervision, combining adaptation, character animation, music, voices, painted backgrounds and photographic technology. It is closer to a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk than to an authored text. ([J. B. Kaufman](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/J-B-Kaufman/404344155))
- **1940:** *Fantasia*. A concert film combining classical recordings, animation and the experimental Fantasound system; Disney conceived it as an evolving program, so the released feature is one manifestation of a larger, partly unrealized project. ([J. B. Kaufman](https://www.simonandschuster.com/authors/J-B-Kaufman/404344155))
- **1941:** *The Reluctant Dragon*. A live-action tour of the Disney studio containing animated episodes; its staged documentary surface makes it simultaneously self-portrait, publicity object and anthology film. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1941:** *Dumbo*. An animated feature produced by Disney and made collectively at his studio; the title identifies the studio production rather than a film personally drawn or directed by him. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1942:** *Bambi*. An animated feature produced under Disney’s supervision after a prolonged development process; its naturalistic movement and multiplane environments make it an integrated work of animation, sound and photographic technique. ([Walt Disney Family Museum timeline](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/Walt%20Disney%20Timeline_0.pdf))
- **1942:** *Saludos Amigos*. A hybrid feature of animation, documentary travel footage and music resulting from Disney’s South American goodwill expedition. It is both a film and one manifestation of a government-linked cultural-diplomacy project. ([J. B. Kaufman, *South of the Border with Disney*](https://mail.jbkaufman.com/south-border-disney))
- **1943:** *Victory Through Air Power*. A wartime animated/live-action argument advocating Alexander de Seversky’s strategic-bombing doctrine; it functions as a cinematic manifesto rather than neutral description. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1944:** *The Three Caballeros*. A hybrid feature integrating live action, animation, popular music and travel imagery; it continued the Good Neighbor project while resisting separation into documentary, musical and cartoon. ([J. B. Kaufman, *South of the Border with Disney*](https://mail.jbkaufman.com/south-border-disney))
- **1946:** *Make Mine Music*. An animated anthology assembled from musically organized segments; Disney was producer and editorial authority, while the component films had numerous directors and designers. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1946:** *Song of the South*. A live-action/animation feature produced by Disney. Its romanticized representation of the post–Civil War South is historically disputed and has led Disney to withhold it from ordinary modern distribution; that contested status belongs to the work’s history. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1947:** *Fun and Fancy Free*. An anthology joining *Bongo* and *Mickey and the Beanstalk* with live-action framing; it is a composite manifestation made partly from projects originally conceived at greater length. ([J. B. Kaufman](https://www.jbkaufman.com/guest-appearances))
- **1948:** *Melody Time*. A seven-part animated musical anthology, produced by Disney but directed and executed by several studio units. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1948:** *So Dear to My Heart*. A live-action feature with animated interludes; Disney regarded its rural material as personally resonant, but credited directors, writers, actors and animators carried out the production. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1949:** *The Adventures of Ichabod and Mr. Toad*. A two-part animated feature adapting Kenneth Grahame and Washington Irving; its paired structure reflects the studio’s postwar package-film economy. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1950:** *Cinderella*. An animated feature produced under Disney’s supervision and collectively designed, animated, voiced and scored. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1951:** *Alice in Wonderland*. An animated feature assembled after decades of abandoned Disney Alice projects; the released film should not be confused with those unrealized versions. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1953:** *Peter Pan*. An animated feature produced by Disney after a long and interrupted development history; authorship remains divided among J. M. Barrie’s source, studio story artists, directors, animators and songwriters. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1955:** *Lady and the Tramp*. An animated feature produced by Disney and released in CinemaScope; its authorship is studio-collaborative despite the possessive “Walt Disney’s” used in promotion. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1959:** *Sleeping Beauty*. An animated feature whose stylized design, music adapted from Tchaikovsky and large-format Technirama photography make it a particularly explicit Gesamtkunstwerk. Disney produced it but did not personally direct or animate it. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1961:** *One Hundred and One Dalmatians*. An animated feature produced under Disney’s authority; its xerographic line was substantially shaped by technical and artistic collaborators, especially Ub Iwerks and Ken Anderson. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1963:** *The Sword in the Stone*. An animated feature produced by Disney and directed by Wolfgang Reitherman. Calling it simply “a Walt Disney film” records the production brand but obscures delegated creative labor. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1966:** *Winnie the Pooh and the Honey Tree*. An animated short featurette produced during Disney’s lifetime; it became a component of the posthumous compilation *The Many Adventures of Winnie the Pooh*. ([D23](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/s/))
- **1967:** *The Jungle Book*. An animated feature substantially shaped and supervised by Disney but released after his death. It belongs to his final production program while resisting a simple lifetime date. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))

### Characters and serial creations

- **1928:** *Mickey Mouse*. An animated character and reproducible corporate persona rather than a single bounded work. Disney supplied the initiating concept and voice, while Ub Iwerks’s decisive visual design makes “created by Walt Disney” an incomplete attribution. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/030114_MultiplaneGuideFINAL.pdf); [Disney Publishing](https://books.disney.com/book-author/j-b-kaufman/))
- **1928:** *Minnie Mouse*. A serial character first manifested alongside Mickey; her creation belongs to the same disputed and collaborative Disney–Iwerks production context. ([Disney Publishing](https://books.disney.com/book-author/j-b-kaufman/))
- **1930:** *Pluto*. A serial animated character whose identity emerged gradually from canine figures in successive Mickey cartoons; no single image or film entirely constitutes the creation. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1932:** *Goofy*. A serial character developed collectively from “Dippy Dawg”; the familiar persona was stabilized across performances by Pinto Colvig and work by studio writers, directors and animators rather than created in one authorial act. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1934:** *Donald Duck*. A serial character introduced in *The Wise Little Hen* and subsequently reshaped by performers and artists, notably Clarence Nash; Disney’s claim is institutional and supervisory rather than exclusive. ([Disney Publishing](https://books.disney.com/book-author/j-b-kaufman/))

### Documentary, educational and wartime film projects

- **1941–1945:** *Disney wartime and training films*. A large body of propaganda, military instruction, public-health and industrial films made under government or corporate contracts. Many are protocols—films intended to teach conduct or procedure—and individual authorship belongs to production teams, not to Disney alone. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney); [Walt Disney Family Museum timeline](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/Walt%20Disney%20Timeline_0.pdf))
- **1942:** *The New Spirit*. An animated tax-payment film commissioned by the United States Treasury; it acts on its audience by soliciting compliance and is therefore both propaganda and civic protocol. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1943:** *Education for Death*. An animated anti-Nazi propaganda short produced by Disney; its political function is inseparable from its narrative and visual form. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1943:** *Der Fuehrer’s Face*. An animated propaganda short featuring Donald Duck, produced by Disney and directed by Jack Kinney; its Oscar-era attribution to Disney should not erase its credited makers. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1943:** *Reason and Emotion*. An animated wartime allegory and behavioral argument produced by Disney. It is less a neutral entertainment than a persuasive model of mental discipline. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1946:** *The Story of Menstruation*. An educational animated film made for International Cellucotton Company; as a classroom protocol about bodily development, it is not adequately classified as a cartoon entertainment. ([D23](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/s/))
- **1948–1960:** *True-Life Adventures*. A fourteen-film nature-documentary series initiated and produced by Disney, encompassing shorts and features. Its photographed animals are creations only in the editorial sense: narration, music and montage construct stories from events the studio did not make. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1948:** *Seal Island*. The first True-Life Adventure, a documentary short produced by Disney from Alaskan wildlife footage. Its status as “nature” is complicated by narrative editing and anthropomorphic narration. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1953:** *The Living Desert*. The first feature-length True-Life Adventure; it is a constructed documentary manifestation built from field photography, editorial selection, music and narration. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1957–1960:** *People and Places*. A documentary-travel series produced by Disney. It shares the studio’s crafted narration and spectacle but applies them to human communities, raising questions about whose culture is being authored and displayed. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))

### Live-action cinema

- **1950:** *Treasure Island*. Disney’s first wholly live-action feature, produced in Britain; Disney was producer, while Byron Haskin directed and Lawrence Edward Watkin wrote the screenplay. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1954:** *20,000 Leagues Under the Sea*. A large-scale live-action adaptation produced by Disney, combining performance, design, underwater photography and effects. Its Nautilus environments make it a cinematic Gesamtkunstwerk, but Richard Fleischer and a large credited team executed the film. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1957:** *Old Yeller*. A live-action feature produced by Disney and directed by Robert Stevenson; its frequent identification with Disney denotes studio authorship, not personal direction. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1959:** *The Shaggy Dog*. A live-action fantasy-comedy produced under Disney’s supervision and the first of the studio’s cycle of contemporary family comedies. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1959:** *Darby O’Gill and the Little People*. A live-action fantasy feature produced by Disney, integrating location work, folklore, forced-perspective design and effects. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1960:** *Pollyanna*. A live-action adaptation produced by Disney and directed by David Swift. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1960:** *Swiss Family Robinson*. A live-action adventure feature produced under Disney’s authority; its island world is an elaborate collective construction rather than an individual text. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1961:** *The Absent-Minded Professor*. A live-action fantasy-comedy produced by Disney and directed by Robert Stevenson. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1961:** *The Parent Trap*. A live-action feature produced by Disney and directed by David Swift, with its doubled central performance created through photographic and editorial effects. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1964:** *Mary Poppins*. A live-action/animation musical produced by Disney, combining adaptation, songs, choreography, performance, effects and animation. P. L. Travers contested Disney’s transformation of her material, so even its source authorship forms part of the work’s disputed status. ([AFI Catalog](https://catalog.afi.com/Person/153163-Walt-Disney))
- **1965:** *That Darn Cat!*. A live-action comedy produced under Disney’s supervision and directed by Robert Stevenson. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1966:** *The Ugly Dachshund*. A live-action comedy released during Disney’s final year and produced within his studio system. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1967:** *The Happiest Millionaire*. A live-action musical begun under Disney’s supervision but released after his death; it belongs to his production slate without being a completed lifetime manifestation. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))
- **1968:** *The One and Only, Genuine, Original Family Band*. A live-action musical developed with Disney’s personal involvement but completed and released posthumously; its inclusion in his œuvre is consequently qualified. ([D23 film list](https://d23.com/list-of-disney-films/))

### Television works and broadcast forms

- **1950:** *One Hour in Wonderland*. A Christmas television special and Disney’s first network television production; it combined live presentation, studio promotion and excerpts from existing films. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1951:** *The Walt Disney Christmas Show*. A television special mixing live action and animation, made partly to promote *Peter Pan*. It is simultaneously program and advertisement. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/w/))
- **1954–1958:** *Disneyland*. A weekly anthology television series hosted and produced by Disney. It was entertainment, a financing mechanism and an architectural preview of the park, making broadcast and built project parts of one Gesamtkunstwerk. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html))
- **1955–1959:** *The Mickey Mouse Club*. A weekday television serial combining children’s variety performance, animation, newsreels and continuing dramas. Disney conceived and produced the format, but its episodes were collective works. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1957–1959:** *Zorro*. A live-action television series produced by Walt Disney Productions; Disney’s role was executive and supervisory, not that of sole writer or director. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1958–1961:** *Walt Disney Presents*. The retitled continuation of the anthology series, using Disney’s on-screen persona to join films, serials, nature programs and park promotion. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/w/))
- **1961–1966:** *Walt Disney’s Wonderful World of Color*. The NBC color incarnation of Disney’s anthology during his lifetime. The series is not one bounded work: it is a changing broadcast container, promotional system and body of individually produced episodes. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/w/))

### Built environments and experiential works

- **1952–1955:** *Disneyland project*. Disney’s intention to make a new kind of permanent themed environment preceded the completed park and generated plans, models, financing schemes and television programs. The project itself is therefore part of the obra, not merely preparation for it. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html))
- **1955:** *Disneyland*. A continuously changing park integrating architecture, landscape, rides, theatrical performance, music, food, merchandise and scripted behavior. It is a collaborative and mutable Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a stable object personally built by Disney. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html))
- **1955:** *Main Street, U.S.A.* A themed urban environment within Disneyland, idealizing a turn-of-the-century American town. Disney supplied memories and programmatic direction, while designers and craftspeople translated them into architecture and spectacle. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html))
- **1955:** *Adventureland*. A themed Disneyland environment built from landscaping, architecture, performance and colonial adventure imagery; it is a designed world rather than a single ride. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Frontierland*. A themed environment converting selected American frontier histories into participatory entertainment. Its historical claims and omissions make it an ideological construction as well as a built work. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Fantasyland*. A themed environment materializing the studio’s animated fairy-tale repertoire as architecture, rides and performance. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Tomorrowland*. A themed environment repeatedly rebuilt as the imagined future changed; no single physical state exhausts the work. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Jungle Cruise*. A scripted boat attraction combining mechanical figures, landscape and live comic narration. Disney initiated the attraction, but its text changes with performers and revisions, so it has no single final form. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Mark Twain Riverboat and Rivers of America*. A moving vessel and designed landscape conceived as one experiential environment; their status lies between architecture, machine, theater and transportation. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Autopia*. A participatory driving attraction whose riders partly produce each performance. Its changing vehicles and roadways make it a continuing system rather than a fixed work. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1955:** *Storybook Land Canal Boats*. A boat attraction through miniature architectural manifestations of animated-film settings, expanded and altered after its initial opening. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/s/))
- **1959:** *Disneyland Monorail*. A working transportation system staged as a vision of urban mobility; it is both infrastructure and futurist manifesto. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1959:** *Submarine Voyage*. An attraction combining vehicles, underwater scenery, effects and guided narrative; its experienced “voyage” exists only through the coordinated system. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/s/))
- **1959:** *Matterhorn Bobsleds*. A mountain, roller coaster and themed landscape developed by Disney and WED Enterprises; it cannot be reduced to either sculpture or machine. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1963:** *Enchanted Tiki Room*. A restaurant-adjacent theatrical attraction composed of architecture, programmed sound and Audio-Animatronic performers. Its automated performance makes it a recurrent manifestation rather than a static object. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1964:** *Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln*. An Audio-Animatronic theater presentation created for the New York World’s Fair and subsequently installed at Disneyland. Its successive revisions are distinct manifestations of a patriotic performance project. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1964:** *Progressland / Carousel of Progress*. A rotating theater and corporate-sponsored account of technological domestic progress created for General Electric. It acts as an industrial manifesto while combining architecture, machines, music and performance. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1964:** *Ford’s Magic Skyway*. A World’s Fair ride in Ford automobiles through prehistoric and futuristic scenes; dismantling and redistribution of its figures mean that the original experiential work no longer exists intact. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1964:** *“it’s a small world”*. A boat ride created for UNICEF and Pepsi-Cola at the New York World’s Fair and transferred to Disneyland. Architecture, figures, movement, lighting and an endlessly repeated song form an inseparable Gesamtkunstwerk. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1966:** *New Orleans Square*. A Disneyland land opened shortly before Disney’s death; it is an urban scenic environment and container for attractions rather than one conventionally bounded work. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1963–1966:** *Pirates of the Caribbean*. An Audio-Animatronic boat attraction developed under Disney but opened after his death in 1967. It is a posthumous manifestation of a lifetime project, collectively designed and never personally completed by him. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))
- **1961–1966:** *The Haunted Mansion*. A prolonged attraction project that Disney repeatedly redefined but did not live to see opened. Because competing concepts remained unresolved during his lifetime, the project—not the 1969 realization alone—is his attributable work. ([D23, *Disney A to Z*](https://d23.com/disney-a-to-z/))

### Institutions, systems and unrealized projects

- **1922:** *Laugh-O-gram Films, Inc.* A production company and collaborative workshop founded by Disney in Kansas City. It is an institutional creation rather than a text, and its bankruptcy is part of its completed historical form. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/blog/mrs-davis-letter-display-walt-disney-companys-100th-anniversary))
- **1923:** *Disney Brothers Cartoon Studio*. The production institution founded by Walt and Roy Disney; later names and corporate reorganizations are manifestations of an enterprise neither brother made alone. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/blog/100-years-ago-first-disney-bros-movie))
- **1940:** *Burbank studio campus*. A purpose-designed production environment integrating animation’s departments and workflows. Its authorship belongs to architects, engineers, workers and the Disney organization as well as to Disney’s programmatic direction. ([Walt Disney Family Museum timeline](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/Walt%20Disney%20Timeline_0.pdf))
- **1952:** *WED Enterprises*. Disney’s privately controlled design-and-development organization, created to make Disneyland and later attractions. As an institution that made other works, it is an enabling obra rather than a discrete artwork. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html))
- **1960–1966:** *California Institute of the Arts*. A proposed interdisciplinary arts institution formed through the merger of existing schools and championed by Disney. He did not live to see the present campus completed, so his attributable contribution is the educational project and institutional model, not the later building alone. ([Steve Mannheim, *Walt Disney and the Quest for Community*](https://www.routledge.com/Walt-Disney-and-the-Quest-for-Community/Mannheim/p/book/9781138253537))
- **1964–1966:** *The Florida Project*. A land-development, resort and urban-planning project initiated by Walt and Roy Disney in central Florida. Since its realized components were largely constructed after Walt’s death, the plans, acquisitions and organizational intention are more securely his work than the later resort as a whole. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html))
- **1966:** *Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow (EPCOT)*. Disney’s unfinished proposal for a planned city organized around transportation, industry and controlled urban development. It is a project and manifesto preserved in models, plans and a promotional film—not the theme park named EPCOT that opened in 1982. ([Walt Disney Family Museum](https://www.waltdisney.org/sites/default/files/walt-disney/walt-disney.html); [Steve Mannheim](https://www.routledge.com/Walt-Disney-and-the-Quest-for-Community/Mannheim/p/book/9781138253537))
