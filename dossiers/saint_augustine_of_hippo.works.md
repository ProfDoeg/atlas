## Works by Aurelius Augustinus

### Philosophical dialogues and liberal-arts projects

- **c. 380–381:** *De pulchro et apto* (*On the Beautiful and the Fitting*). A philosophical treatise in two or three books written during Augustine’s Manichaean period; it was already lost when he reviewed his writings, so its form and date remain approximate. [Retractationes 1.1; O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **386:** *Contra Academicos* (*Against the Academics*). A three-book philosophical dialogue attacking Academic skepticism, composed at Cassiciacum. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **386:** *De beata vita* (*On the Happy Life*). A Cassiciacum dialogue, shaped from a recorded group discussion, about happiness and the knowledge of God. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **386:** *De ordine* (*On Order*). A two-book dialogue concerning providence, evil, education, and the intelligibility of the world. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **386–387:** *Soliloquia* (*Soliloquies*). A two-book dialogue between “Augustine” and “Reason”; the projected inquiry remained unfinished. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **387:** *De immortalitate animae* (*On the Immortality of the Soul*). An unfinished working draft or argumentative memorandum intended to continue the *Soliloquia*, rather than a polished dialogue. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 387:** *Disciplinarum libri*. A project for manuals on the seven liberal disciplines; most components were abandoned or lost, so this is better classified as an educational project than as one completed obra. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 387:** *De grammatica* (*On Grammar*). The surviving abbreviated text may preserve Augustine’s contribution to his liberal-arts project, but its textual form and direct authorship are disputed. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 387:** *De dialectica* (*On Dialectic*). A technical liberal-arts manual traditionally attributed to Augustine; the extent to which the transmitted text represents his original work remains disputed. [Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **c. 387–391:** *De rhetorica* (*On Rhetoric*). A fragmentary school text transmitted under Augustine’s name; attribution is doubtful, and it cannot safely be treated as an authenticated work. [Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **388–390:** *De musica* (*On Music*). Six surviving books on rhythm, number, perception, and ascent toward immaterial order; a projected treatment of melody was never completed or does not survive. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **387–388:** *De quantitate animae* (*On the Magnitude of the Soul*). A philosophical dialogue examining the soul’s incorporeality, powers, and ascent. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **388–395:** *De libero arbitrio* (*On Free Choice*). A dialogue in three books begun in Rome and completed years later in Africa; its long composition makes it both an early philosophical work and a later Christian reconsideration of evil and responsibility. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **389–390:** *De magistro* (*On the Teacher*). A dialogue with Augustine’s son Adeodatus concerning signs, speech, learning, and the interior teacher. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)

### Apologetic, catechetical, and doctrinal works

- **390–391:** *De vera religione* (*On True Religion*). A systematic apologetic treatise, written before Augustine became a priest, presenting Christianity as the fulfillment of philosophical inquiry. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **391–392:** *De utilitate credendi* (*On the Usefulness of Believing*). An apologetic letter-treatise addressed to Honoratus, defending trust in religious authority against Manichaean claims. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **393:** *De fide et symbolo* (*On Faith and the Creed*). A written exposition developed from Augustine’s address to an African episcopal council; it is both conciliar speech and doctrinal treatise. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **394–395:** *De mendacio* (*On Lying*). A moral treatise classifying lies and arguing against their deliberate use; Augustine initially withheld or wished to suppress it but did not cease to regard it as his work. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **394–395:** *De continentia* (*On Continence*). A sermon-like moral treatise about inward restraint and the struggle against disordered desire. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **396:** *De agone christiano* (*On the Christian Struggle*). A compact catechetical and polemical handbook written for readers without advanced education. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **396–397; completed 426–427:** *De doctrina christiana* (*On Christian Teaching*). A four-book protocol for discovering and communicating scriptural meaning; Augustine left it incomplete after Book III and returned roughly thirty years later to finish it. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 398:** *De disciplina christiana* (*On Christian Discipline*). A surviving sermon published as an independent catechetical work; its boundary between sermon and treatise is therefore unstable. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 399:** *De catechizandis rudibus* (*On Catechizing Beginners*). A protocol for the deacon Deogratias on shaping introductory Christian instruction, including model narratives rather than a fixed liturgical script. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 400:** *De fide rerum invisibilium* (*On Faith in Things Unseen*). A sermon-treatise defending belief in realities that cannot be directly observed. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **399–405 or c. 408:** *De utilitate ieiunii* (*On the Usefulness of Fasting*). A preached exhortation subsequently transmitted as a separate work; both its date and its classification as sermon or treatise vary. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **406–408:** *De divinatione daemonum* (*On the Divination of Demons*). A short explanatory dialogue or treatise on how demons can appear to predict events without possessing divine knowledge. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **411:** *Sermo de excidio urbis Romae* (*Sermon on the Sack of Rome*). A homiletic response to the capture of Rome in 410; it survives as an individually catalogued sermon rather than merely a number in the general sermon corpus. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **413:** *De fide et operibus* (*On Faith and Works*). A pastoral-doctrinal treatise opposing admission to baptism without corresponding moral instruction. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **417–418:** *De patientia* (*On Patience*). A sermon-like moral treatise distinguishing Christian patience from endurance pursued for corrupt ends. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **420:** *Contra mendacium ad Consentium* (*Against Lying, to Consentius*). A second treatise on lying, written against proposals to infiltrate Priscillianist groups by deception. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 422:** *Enchiridion ad Laurentium de fide, spe et caritate* (*Handbook on Faith, Hope, and Love*). A compact doctrinal handbook organized around the Creed, Lord’s Prayer, and theological virtues. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **422–425:** *De octo Dulcitii quaestionibus* (*Eight Questions of Dulcitius*). A dossier of answers, partly adapted from earlier writings, assembled for the imperial official Dulcitius. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 428:** *De haeresibus ad Quodvultdeum* (*On Heresies, to Quodvultdeus*). A catalogue of eighty-eight heresies assembled from predecessors and Augustine’s own knowledge; the promised fuller account of what constitutes heresy was never supplied. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **428–429:** *Adversus Iudaeos* (*Against the Jews*). A polemical sermon or tract; its modern title and genre should not obscure that it originated as Christian preaching directed principally to a Christian audience. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)

### Scriptural interpretation

- **388–396:** *De diversis quaestionibus octoginta tribus* (*Eighty-Three Diverse Questions*). A retrospectively ordered collection of answers produced on different occasions, not a single continuously composed treatise. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **388–390:** *De Genesi contra Manichaeos* (*On Genesis against the Manichaeans*). A two-book allegorical commentary and anti-Manichaean polemic on the opening chapters of Genesis. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **393–394; revisited 426–427:** *De Genesi ad litteram imperfectus liber* (*Unfinished Literal Commentary on Genesis*). An abandoned commentary preserved precisely as an unfinished project; Augustine later added a note but did not turn it into the completed commentary. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **393–396:** *De sermone Domini in monte* (*On the Lord’s Sermon on the Mount*). A two-book exegetical treatise on Matthew 5–7. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **394:** *Expositio quarundam propositionum ex epistula ad Romanos* (*Exposition of Certain Propositions from Romans*). A sequence of answers to selected Pauline propositions, formed from discussions with Augustine’s clerical community. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **394–395:** *Epistulae ad Galatas expositio* (*Exposition of the Letter to the Galatians*). A continuous commentary on Galatians. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **394–395:** *Epistulae ad Romanos inchoata expositio* (*Unfinished Exposition of the Letter to the Romans*). A commentary abandoned near the beginning because the intended undertaking proved too extensive. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **396:** *Ad Simplicianum de diversis quaestionibus* (*Diverse Questions to Simplicianus*). Two books of exegetical responses—seven questions in all—especially important for Augustine’s developing account of grace. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 399:** *Adnotationes in Iob* (*Annotations on Job*). Notes assembled from oral exposition by listeners without Augustine’s authorization; he complained about their publication, so the text resists being called a deliberately made book. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **395–400:** *Quaestiones evangeliorum* (*Questions on the Gospels*). Two books of heterogeneous exegetical questions on Matthew and Luke rather than a continuous Gospel commentary. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **Date uncertain, probably late fourth or early fifth century:** *Quaestiones XVII in Matthaeum* (*Seventeen Questions on Matthew*). A small question-collection whose date cannot be securely fixed. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **399–400, possibly extending later:** *De consensu evangelistarum* (*On the Agreement of the Evangelists*). A four-book comparison and harmonization of the four Gospel narratives; scholarly chronologies differ over how long composition continued. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **401–415:** *De Genesi ad litteram* (*The Literal Meaning of Genesis*). A twelve-book commentary and theological inquiry into creation; its “literal” method includes philosophical and figurative reasoning rather than modern literalism. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **406–421?:** *In Iohannis evangelium tractatus CXXIV* (*124 Tractates on the Gospel of John*). A long hybrid of preached homilies and dictated exposition; dates proposed for its sections vary substantially. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **406–407 or c. 413:** *In epistulam Iohannis ad Parthos tractatus decem* (*Ten Tractates on the First Letter of John*). A preached series on 1 John; both its conventional Latin title and its date present unresolved problems. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **392–c. 422:** *Enarrationes in Psalmos* (*Expositions of the Psalms*). A lifetime corpus of dictated commentaries and preached sermons covering the Psalter, sometimes more than once; it is a body of work rather than one composition made at a single moment. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **419–420:** *Locutiones in Heptateuchum* (*Expressions in the Heptateuch*). A philological notebook cataloguing unusual turns of phrase in the first seven biblical books. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **419–420:** *Quaestiones in Heptateuchum* (*Questions on the Heptateuch*). Seven books of exegetical problems and proposed resolutions, companion to the *Locutiones*. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **Date uncertain, perhaps c. 419:** *De octo quaestionibus ex Veteri Testamento* (*Eight Questions from the Old Testament*). A small exegetical collection whose title is established but whose date remains uncertain. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **427:** *Speculum quis ignorat* (*Mirror: “Who Does Not Know”*). A compilation of biblical commandments selected as a moral mirror; it is an editorial construction from Scripture as much as an authored treatise. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)

### Anti-Manichaean polemics and debate records

- **388–389:** *De moribus Ecclesiae catholicae et de moribus Manichaeorum* (*On the Morals of the Catholic Church and the Morals of the Manichaeans*). A paired comparison of Catholic and Manichaean teaching and communal life. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **391–392:** *De duabus animabus contra Manichaeos* (*On Two Souls, against the Manichaeans*). A polemical treatise rejecting the Manichaean doctrine of opposed souls or natures. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **392:** *Acta contra Fortunatum Manichaeum* (*Proceedings against Fortunatus the Manichaean*). A stenographic record of a public debate; Augustine performed within it, but the surviving documentary object was made collaboratively by disputants and recorders. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **393–394:** *Contra Adimantum, Manichaei discipulum* (*Against Adimantus, a Disciple of Mani*). A point-by-point refutation of alleged contradictions between the Old and New Testaments. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 394:** *Contra epistulam Donati haeretici*. A lost anti-Manichaean work against a letter by a Manichaean named Donatus; it should not be confused with Augustine’s Donatist polemics. Its existence is known from Augustine’s retrospective catalogue. [Retractationes; Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **396:** *Contra epistulam Manichaei quam vocant Fundamenti* (*Against the Manichaean Letter Called the Foundation*). A detailed but unfinished refutation of a foundational Manichaean text. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **397–399:** *Contra Faustum Manichaeum* (*Against Faustus the Manichaean*). Thirty-three books alternating excerpts or summaries of Faustus’s objections with Augustine’s replies; it is structured as a textual dispute with an absent opponent. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 398:** *De natura boni contra Manichaeos* (*On the Nature of the Good, against the Manichaeans*). A compact metaphysical polemic arguing that all created natures are good insofar as they exist. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **398–399:** *Contra Secundinum Manichaeum* (*Against Secundinus the Manichaean*). A reply to Secundinus’s letter urging Augustine to return to Manichaeism; the transmitted dossier includes the opponent’s text and is therefore not wholly Augustine’s creation. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **404:** *Acta contra Felicem Manichaeum* (*Proceedings against Felix the Manichaean*). The documentary record of two public debate sessions, ending in Felix’s recantation; as “acts,” it is a jointly produced manifestation rather than a conventional authored book. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)

### Anti-Donatist interventions

- **393–394:** *Psalmus contra partem Donati* (*Psalm against the Party of Donatus*). An alphabetic, rhythmical anti-Donatist song intended for collective memory and performance; it is not simply a book and may have functioned as a popular manifesto. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 393:** *Contra epistulam Parmeniani, liber primus*. Augustine reports an initial reply to Parmenian which he abandoned when fuller materials became available; it is a lost or absorbed project and should not automatically be identified with the later three-book work. [Retractationes; Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **c. 395:** *Contra partem Donati*. A lost anti-Donatist work whose publication Augustine later regretted because some bishops were individually named; only the retrospective notice securely survives. [Retractationes; Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **c. 397:** *Contra epistulam Donati*. A lost reply to a Donatist letter concerning baptism, distinct from the similarly titled lost anti-Manichaean work. [Retractationes; Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **c. 400:** *Contra epistulam Parmeniani* (*Against the Letter of Parmenian*). A three-book refutation of the Donatist bishop Parmenian. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **400–401:** *De baptismo contra Donatistas* (*On Baptism, against the Donatists*). Seven books arguing that baptism’s validity does not depend on the moral status or ecclesial allegiance of its minister. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **400–403:** *Contra litteras Petiliani* (*Against the Letters of Petilian*). A three-book dossier produced in stages as Augustine acquired more of Petilian’s polemic. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **401–405:** *Epistula ad catholicos de secta Donatistarum* (*Letter to Catholics on the Donatist Sect*, also *De unitate Ecclesiae*). An extended open letter and ecclesiological manifesto arguing for the worldwide communion of the Church. Its attribution to Augustine was once questioned but is generally accepted. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 405–406:** *Contra Cresconium grammaticum et Donatistam* (*Against Cresconius the Grammarian and Donatist*). A four-book rebuttal engaging Cresconius’s rhetoric as well as his ecclesiology. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 410–411:** *De unico baptismo contra Petilianum* (*On the One Baptism, against Petilian*). A polemical treatise now lost except for fragments or testimonia; its existence and subject are recorded in Augustine’s catalogue. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **411:** *Gesta collationis Carthaginiensis* (*Proceedings of the Conference of Carthage*). The official stenographic acts of the Catholic–Donatist conference: Augustine was a principal speaker, not the sole author, so the record resists inclusion as his obra even though his spoken interventions belong to what he made. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **411:** *Breviculus collationis cum Donatistis* (*Summary of the Conference with the Donatists*). Augustine’s partisan digest and finding aid to the official conference proceedings. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **411:** *Contra partem Donati post gesta* (*Against the Party of Donatus after the Proceedings*). A public argumentative text written immediately after the conference; it is also known as *Ad Donatistas post collationem*. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **418:** *De gestis cum Emerito Donatistarum episcopo* (*Proceedings with Emeritus, Donatist Bishop*). A documentary record of Augustine’s confrontation with Emeritus at Caesarea; like other *acta*, it depends on stenographic mediation. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **418:** *Sermo ad Caesariensis ecclesiae plebem* (*Sermon to the People of the Church at Caesarea*). A separately preserved public address connected with the encounter with Emeritus. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **419–420:** *Contra Gaudentium Donatistarum episcopum* (*Against Gaudentius, Bishop of the Donatists*). Two books opposing Gaudentius’s defense of schism and threatened collective suicide; the first book circulated before the second was added. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)

### Anti-Pelagian works on grace, sin, and freedom

- **411–412:** *De peccatorum meritis et remissione et de baptismo parvulorum ad Marcellinum* (*On the Merits and Forgiveness of Sins and the Baptism of Infants*). A three-book intervention that became Augustine’s first large anti-Pelagian treatise. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **412:** *De spiritu et littera* (*On the Spirit and the Letter*). A reply to Marcellinus explaining grace, law, and justification. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **413–415:** *De natura et gratia* (*On Nature and Grace*). A response to Pelagius’s *De natura*, defending the necessity of healing grace. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **415:** *De perfectione iustitiae hominis* (*On the Perfection of Human Righteousness*). A collection of replies to propositions attributed to Caelestius concerning sinlessness. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **417:** *De gestis Pelagii* (*On the Proceedings concerning Pelagius*). Augustine’s interpretation of the acts of the Synod of Diospolis; it is commentary on a documentary record, not the record itself. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **418:** *De gratia Christi et de peccato originali* (*On the Grace of Christ and Original Sin*). Two linked books addressed to Albina, Pinianus, and Melania, answering Pelagius and Caelestius. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **419–421:** *De nuptiis et concupiscentia ad Valerium* (*On Marriage and Concupiscence, to Valerius*). Two books composed in successive stages to distinguish the good of marriage from disordered desire and to answer Julian of Eclanum. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **419–420:** *De natura et origine animae* (*On the Nature and Origin of the Soul*). A four-book dossier directed against Vincentius Victor and framed through replies to several correspondents; also called *De anima et eius origine*. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **420–421:** *Contra duas epistulas Pelagianorum* (*Against Two Letters of the Pelagians*). Four books addressed to Pope Boniface, replying to letters circulated by Julian and other Pelagian bishops. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **421–422:** *Contra Iulianum* (*Against Julian*). Six books answering Julian of Eclanum’s four-book polemic and preserving much of the opponent’s argument within Augustine’s response. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **426–427:** *De gratia et libero arbitrio* (*On Grace and Free Choice*). A treatise for the monks of Hadrumetum seeking to hold divine grace and human responsibility together. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **426–427:** *De correptione et gratia* (*On Reprimand and Grace*). A companion intervention explaining why correction remains meaningful if perseverance is a divine gift. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **428–429:** *De praedestinatione sanctorum* (*On the Predestination of the Saints*). The first of two late responses to monks in southern Gaul concerning the beginning of faith and election. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **428–429:** *De dono perseverantiae* (*On the Gift of Perseverance*). The companion treatise arguing that perseverance to the end is itself a divine gift. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **429–430:** *Contra Iulianum opus imperfectum* (*Unfinished Work against Julian*). Six surviving books of a projected reply to Julian’s eight books *Ad Florum*; Augustine was still composing it when he died, so incompletion is constitutive of the obra. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)

### Other controversies and recorded disputations

- **c. 415:** *Ad Orosium contra Priscillianistas et Origenistas* (*To Orosius, against the Priscillianists and Origenists*). A compact response to questions brought from Spain by Orosius. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **418–419:** *Contra sermonem Arianorum* (*Against an Arian Sermon*). A line-by-line refutation of an anonymous Arian theological statement. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **420:** *Contra adversarium legis et prophetarum* (*Against the Adversary of the Law and the Prophets*). A two-book defense of the Old Testament against an anonymous opponent with Marcionite affinities. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **427–428:** *Conlatio cum Maximino Arianorum episcopo* (*Conference with Maximinus, Arian Bishop*). A stenographic record of a public theological debate; it preserves Augustine’s performance but is not solely authored by him. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **427–428:** *Contra Maximinum Arianum* (*Against Maximinus the Arian*). A two-book written response composed after the debate because Augustine judged the allotted oral exchange inadequate. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)

### Pastoral, ascetic, and social protocols

- **c. 395–400:** *Ordo monasterii* (*Order of the Monastery*). A brief monastic protocol associated with Augustine’s communities; whether Augustine composed the transmitted form is disputed. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 397:** *Praeceptum* (*Rule*). A monastic protocol traditionally known as the Rule of Augustine; a substantial Augustinian core is widely accepted, but its original addressees, relation to Letter 211, and textual history remain contested. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **Date uncertain:** *Regularis informatio*. A female-form adaptation of the monastic rule transmitted within the Augustinian rule complex; whether Augustine himself made this version is disputed. [Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **c. 401:** *De bono coniugali* (*On the Good of Marriage*). A moral-theological treatise identifying fidelity, offspring, and sacramental permanence as goods of marriage. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 401:** *De sancta virginitate* (*On Holy Virginity*). A companion work praising consecrated virginity while warning against pride. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 401:** *De opere monachorum* (*On the Work of Monks*). A practical and exegetical intervention requiring able-bodied monks to work rather than claim scriptural exemption. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 414:** *De bono viduitatis* (*On the Good of Widowhood*). A pastoral letter-treatise addressed to Juliana about vowed widowhood and freedom from coercion. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **c. 420–421:** *De adulterinis coniugiis* (*On Adulterous Marriages*). Two books responding to Pollentius about separation, remarriage, and difficult Pauline texts. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **422–425:** *De cura pro mortuis gerenda* (*On Care for the Dead*). A pastoral response to Paulinus of Nola about burial near saints, prayer for the dead, dreams, and resurrection; proposed dates differ. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)

### Large synthetic and self-interpreting works

- **397–c. 401:** *Confessiones* (*Confessions*). Thirteen books combining prayer, autobiographical narrative, philosophical inquiry, and scriptural exegesis; it resists reduction to autobiography because its direct addressee is God. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **399–c. 422/426:** *De trinitate* (*On the Trinity*). Fifteen books composed and revised over many years; unauthorized circulation of incomplete books complicated Augustine’s own publication project, and its completion date remains disputed. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **413–426/427:** *De civitate Dei* (*The City of God*). A twenty-two-book apologetic, historical, exegetical, and political-theological construction produced in installments after the sack of Rome. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **426–427:** *Retractationes* (*Reconsiderations* or *Revisions*). A chronological catalogue in which Augustine names, contextualizes, criticizes, and corrects ninety-three works comprising 232 books; it is at once bibliography, intellectual autobiography, and protocol for rereading his corpus. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/augustine/)

### Letters and documentary interventions

- **386–430:** *Epistulae* (*Letters*). More than three hundred letters survive, including jointly authored, received, and later discovered dossiers; they are individual interventions rather than one book, but the numbered corpus is the standard scholarly way of cataloguing this dispersed body of work. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 423:** *Epistula 211 / Obiurgatio*. A rebuke and monastic protocol addressed to a women’s community, later transmitted together with the feminine Rule; its incorporation into the rule tradition makes its boundary as a separate work uncertain. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **Late fourth–early fifth century:** *Epistulae Divjak*. Twenty-nine letters rediscovered by Johannes Divjak and published in CSEL 88 in 1981; they are not a project Augustine named as a set, but a modern manuscript discovery within his epistolary production. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **Late fourth–early fifth century:** *Epistulae Dolbeau*. A small group of letters identified by François Dolbeau in a newly studied manuscript; the collective name describes modern recovery, not an Augustinian collection. [Zentrum für Augustinus-Forschung, Epistulae project](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/epistulae-projekt/)

### Preaching and orally made texts

- **392–430:** *Sermones ad populum* (*Sermons to the People*). Nearly six hundred authentic sermons or substantial sermon fragments survive, generally through shorthand transcription rather than authorial publication; each began as an oral event, so the corpus is a body of performed manifestations, not one book. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/augustine/)
- **Date uncertain:** *Sermo de symbolo ad catechumenos* (*Sermon on the Creed to Catechumens*). An independently catalogued catechetical sermon whose precise occasion and date are unknown. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **Newly identified in the twentieth century:** *Sermones Guelferbytani*. A modern grouping of authentic sermons recovered from Wolfenbüttel manuscripts; the label belongs to their manuscript location, not to an Augustinian project. [Clavis sermonum discussion, Library of Latin Texts manual](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)
- **Newly identified in the twentieth century:** *Sermones Caillau–Saint-Yves*. Authentic sermons embedded in an older mixed collection containing genuine and non-genuine material; attribution must therefore be made sermon by sermon. [Clavis sermonum discussion, Library of Latin Texts manual](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)
- **Newly identified in the twentieth century:** *Sermones Denis*. A manuscript-defined group of sermons recognized as Augustinian; it was not assembled as a collection by Augustine. [Clavis sermonum discussion, Library of Latin Texts manual](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)
- **Newly identified in the twentieth century:** *Sermones Mai*. A group recovered and published by Angelo Mai from manuscript witnesses; the collection contains attribution problems and is a modern scholarly object rather than Augustine’s own compilation. [Clavis sermonum discussion, Library of Latin Texts manual](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)
- **Discovered 1990; preached c. 397–418:** *Sermones Dolbeau*. Twenty-six sermons identified by François Dolbeau in a Mainz manuscript, several previously unknown; their collective title records rediscovery, while each sermon remains a distinct oral-textual event. [Clavis sermonum discussion, Library of Latin Texts manual](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)
- **Discovered 2007; preached early fifth century:** *Sermones Erfurt*. Six sermons found in an Erfurt manuscript, four previously unknown and two known incompletely; this is again a modern discovery-group, not an authored cycle. [Clavis sermonum discussion, Library of Latin Texts manual](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)

### Lost, unfinished, and unrealized projects

- **Before 387:** *Translations or extracts from the “Platonic books.”* Augustine reports reading Latin translations of Neoplatonic works but does not claim to have made them; they belong to his intellectual formation, not his oeuvre, and should be excluded despite later speculation. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/augustine/)
- **c. 387:** *Books on arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, and philosophy*. These were intended components of the liberal-disciplines project; no securely identifiable completed texts survive, and it is uncertain which progressed beyond plans or drafts. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **c. 393–395:** *Exposition of the whole Letter to the Romans*. Augustine attempted but abandoned this larger commentary after completing only its opening; the intention survives as a project embodied in the *Epistulae ad Romanos inchoata expositio*. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **Late 390s:** *Contra Hilarium*. A lost work against a lay critic named Hilary who objected to liturgical practices at Carthage; its title and existence are known from Augustine’s retrospective account, but its full contents cannot be reconstructed. [Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **c. 400:** *De trinitate, unauthorized partial edition*. Several unfinished books circulated without Augustine’s consent after copies were removed from his control. This manifestation resists classification as a separate work: it was neither his intended edition nor wholly independent of the eventual *De trinitate*. [O’Donnell chronology](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/augustine/chrontable.html)
- **Before 427:** *Admonitio concerning the works of Tyconius*. Augustine mentions material connected with the Donatist exegete Tyconius, but no securely separable work survives under a fixed title; it should remain an uncertain documentary trace rather than be promoted into a named book. [Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **429–430:** *Projected completion of Contra Iulianum*. Augustine intended a fuller answer to all eight books of Julian’s *Ad Florum* but died after producing six incomplete books. The unmade remainder is a project, not a lost text, and should not be assigned invented titles or dates. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **Unfixed dates:** *Unrecorded sermons and letters*. Possidius’s *Indiculus* implies a production much larger than the surviving corpus, while ancient shorthand practices ensured that not every preached text became an authorized book. These cannot be individually named and resist being counted as recoverable works. [Vatican account of the *Indiculus*](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2008/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20080220.html)

### Works attributed to Augustine but not securely his

- **Date uncertain:** *Regulae sancti Augustini* (*Rules of Saint Augustine*). The transmitted rule complex combines texts, adaptations, and later editorial arrangements; the *Praeceptum* has the strongest claim to Augustine, while attribution of the whole complex is controversial. [O’Donnell](https://faculty.georgetown.edu/jod/twayne/twaynebib.html)
- **Date uncertain:** *De grammatica, De dialectica,* and *De rhetorica* as transmitted. These school texts stand at different levels of authenticity: *Dialectica* and the grammatical tradition may preserve Augustinian material, whereas *Rhetorica* is especially doubtful. They should not be treated as an uncontested trilogy. [Augustinus-Lexikon work list](https://www.augustinus.de/projekte-des-zaf/augustinus-lexikon/werkeliste/)
- **Medieval:** *Soliloquia animae ad Deum*, *Meditationes*, and *Manuale*. These devotional works circulated under Augustine’s name but are medieval pseudo-Augustinian creations, not things made by Aurelius Augustinus. [New Advent, works of Augustine](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02089a.htm)
- **Medieval:** *Sermones ad fratres in eremo*. This sermon collection is pseudo-Augustinian and substantially later than Augustine; its traditional attribution is part of its reception history, not evidence of authorship. [Pseudo-Augustine overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-Augustine)
- **Date uncertain:** Numerous individual sermons in the traditional numbered corpus. Modern criticism distinguishes authentic, doubtful, and spurious items, so the presence of “Augustine” in a manuscript heading or old edition does not by itself make a sermon his obra. [Library of Latin Texts sermon-catalogue methodology](https://www.sba.unipi.it/sites/default/files/manual_en_1.pdf)
