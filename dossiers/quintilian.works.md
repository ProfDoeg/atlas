## Works by Marcus Fabius Quintilianus

### Authored works

- **c. 93–95 CE:** *Institutio oratoria* (*The Orator’s Education*). An extant twelve-book obra combining educational theory, rhetorical treatise, literary criticism, and practical protocol for forming an orator from childhood through public life; the chronology remains approximate, with scholarly estimates ranging from 92/93–94/95 to 93–96 CE. ([Oxford Handbook of Quintilian](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/37084/chapter-abstract/323178122); [chronological survey](https://dokumen.pub/the-oxford-handbook-of-quintilian-2021933377-9780198713784-0198713789.html))

- **Date unknown, before the completion of the *Institutio oratoria*:** *De causis corruptae eloquentiae* (*On the Causes of Corrupted Eloquence*). A published but lost rhetorical-critical obra about the deterioration of eloquence, known from Quintilian’s own retrospective references; its form and full contents cannot now be recovered. The old proposal that it survives as the *Dialogus de oratoribus* is disputed and generally rejected, the latter work usually being assigned to Tacitus. ([Oxford Handbook of Quintilian](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/37084/chapter-abstract/323177695); [Oxford Handbook preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191022876_A42767480/preview-9780191022876_A42767480.pdf))

### Forensic oratory

- **Date unknown:** *Speech in defence of Naevius Arpinianus* (conventional title: *Pro Naevio Arpiniano*). A published forensic speech defending a man accused of killing his wife by throwing her from a window; it is lost, and Quintilian identifies it as the only speech he himself released for publication. ([*Institutio oratoria* 7.2.24 and scholarly introduction](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Quintilian/Institutio_Oratoria/Introduction%2A.html); [Oxford Handbook preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191022876_A42767480/preview-9780191022876_A42767480.pdf))

- **Dates and titles unknown:** *Other forensic speeches*. Quintilian made and delivered further courtroom orations, but the surviving evidence does not permit a work-by-work list. Shorthand versions circulated without his authorization and, by his account, preserved very little of what he had actually said; these therefore resist classification as authored texts and are better treated as derivative manifestations of lost performances. ([*Institutio oratoria* 7.2.24 and scholarly introduction](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Quintilian/Institutio_Oratoria/Introduction%2A.html))

### Unauthorized manifestations of teaching

- **Before c. 93–95 CE:** *Untitled record of a two-day discussion on rhetoric*. One of two books circulating under Quintilian’s name before the *Institutio*: his pupils had taken down a two-day oral presentation. Quintilian expressly says that he neither published nor designed the book, so the teaching was his performance but the textual object was his pupils’ unauthorized manifestation rather than his obra. ([*Institutio oratoria*, preface 7](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/28413/pg28413-images.html))

- **Before c. 93–95 CE:** *Untitled record of several days of rhetorical teaching*. A second unauthorized book assembled by admiring students from as much as they could capture in notes over several days. Like its companion, it resists inclusion as a work authored by Quintilian even though it derived from something he made orally. ([*Institutio oratoria*, preface 7](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/28413/pg28413-images.html))

### Works attributed to Quintilian but probably not made by him

- **Date unknown; probably after Quintilian:** *Declamationes minores* (*Minor Declamations*). A pedagogical collection of model and outline controversiae: 145 pieces survive from an original sequence of 388. The manuscript tradition attributes it to Quintilian, and a minority of scholars has entertained his authorship or a connection with his unauthorized lecture books, but prevailing scholarship treats its author and date as unknown and associates it at most with his school or rhetorical tradition. ([Cambridge repository study](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/8792ab3b-5fa4-451b-92b3-887ce6cdd982); [Winterbottom, *Papers on Quintilian and Ancient Declamation*](https://academic.oup.com/book/36510); [BMCR review of the Loeb edition](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2007/2007.02.27/))

- **Date unknown; later imperial period:** *Declamationes maiores* (*Major Declamations*). A collection of nineteen elaborate fictional forensic speeches transmitted under Quintilian’s name. Modern scholarship regards the attribution as false and the collection as the production of one or more later declaimers; it belongs in the atlas only as a pseudonymous manifestation attached to Quintilian, not as something securely made by him. ([Winterbottom, *Papers on Quintilian and Ancient Declamation*](https://academic.oup.com/book/36510); [Oxford survey of Quintilian editions](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/37084/chapter-abstract/323177837))
