## Works by Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai

### Transmitted teachings

- **Second century CE; recorded from ca. 200 onward:** *Halakhic and aggadic teachings attributed to Rabbi Shimon*. This is not a self-authored book but a dispersed body of oral rulings, scriptural interpretations, arguments, and ethical sayings preserved under “Rabbi Shimon” or “Shimon ben Yochai” in the Mishnah, Tosefta, halakhic midrashim, and later Talmudic and midrashic compilations. The individual attributions belong to layers of rabbinic transmission and cannot all be authenticated as his exact words; the corpus therefore resists treatment as a fixed, complete obra ([*Encyclopaedia Judaica*, “Simeon Bar Yoḥai”](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/simeon-bar-yohai); [*Jewish Encyclopedia*, “Simeon ben Yoḥai”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13723)).

### Rabbinic compilations traditionally connected with him

- **Redacted after the second century; exact date disputed:** *Mekhilta de-Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai*. A halakhic midrash on Exodus from the interpretive school of Rabbi Akiva, named for Rabbi Shimon because an exposition attributed to him stands near its beginning—not because scholarship regards him as its redactor. The work was subsequently lost in its integral form and has been only partially reconstructed from Cairo Genizah fragments, medieval quotations, and later midrashim; much of the standard Epstein–Melamed edition is conjectural, and scholars generally place its redaction substantially after Rabbi Shimon’s lifetime ([Menahem I. Kahana, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*, “Mekhilta of R. Simeon ben Yoḥai”](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/mekhilta-r-simeon-ben-yohai)).

- **Redacted after the second century; no single secure date:** *Sifrei to Numbers and Sifrei to Deuteronomy*. Older tradition credited Rabbi Shimon with “the Sifrei,” and some anonymous material—especially in *Sifrei Deuteronomy*—reflects views associated with him or Rabbi Akiva’s school. Modern scholarship treats these as composite, anonymously redacted works: *Sifrei Numbers* is principally associated with Rabbi Ishmael’s school, while *Sifrei Deuteronomy* belongs principally to Rabbi Akiva’s; neither can securely be called an obra made by Rabbi Shimon ([*Jewish Encyclopedia*, “Sifre”](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13647-sifre.html); [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*, “Midreshei Halakhah”](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0014_0_13855.html)).

### Medieval pseudepigrapha

- **Late thirteenth century:** *Sefer ha-Zohar* (*The Book of Splendor*). A multilingual constellation of kabbalistic narratives, homilies, biblical exegesis, and mystical teaching that makes Rabbi Shimon its central revelatory teacher and was traditionally presented as his creation. Its most characteristic compositions arose in late-thirteenth-century Castile, roughly a millennium after him; scholarship variously associates them with Moses de León or a circle of authors, so Rabbi Shimon is a literary protagonist and attributed authority, not their historical maker ([Jeremy Philip Brown, “Zohar,” *Oxford Bibliographies in Jewish Studies*](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554969931)).

- **Written form shortly after the mid-eighth century, with earlier materials possible:** *Nistarot de-Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai* (*The Secrets of Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai*). A pseudepigraphic apocalypse cast as Rabbi Shimon’s dialogue with the angel Metatron, interpreting the rise of Islam and subsequent imperial history. Its references to seventh- and eighth-century events exclude second-century authorship, although some constituent traditions may be earlier than the surviving compilation ([John C. Reeves, “The Secrets of Rabbi Šimʿōn ben Yoḥai”](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Reeves-J-%E2%80%93-The-Secrets-.pdf)).

- **Post-eighth century; surviving recension’s date not securely fixed:** *ʿAtidot de-Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai* (*Future Things of Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai*). An apocalyptic manifestation related to, and sometimes treated as a variant recension of, the *Secrets*, preserved within the larger *Midrash of the Ten Kings*. Its Islamic-era historical horizon makes Rabbi Shimon’s authorship impossible; even its independence as a separate “work,” rather than a recension within a textual cycle, is debatable ([John C. Reeves, “The Secrets of Rabbi Šimʿōn ben Yoḥai”](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Reeves-J-%E2%80%93-The-Secrets-.pdf)).

- **Final redaction after 1099 CE:** *Tefillat Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai* (*Prayer of Rabbi Shimon ben Yochai*). Despite its title, this is an apocalyptic historical narrative rather than simply a liturgical protocol: it extends through Fatimid and Carmathian events and the Crusaders’ capture of Jerusalem. It belongs to the medieval Rabbi-Shimon apocalypse cycle and is pseudepigraphic, not a surviving prayer made by the second-century sage ([*Late Antique Responses to the Arab Conquests*](https://dokumen.pub/late-antique-responses-to-the-arab-conquests-2021046471-2021046472-9789004500617-9789004500648-9004500618.html); [John C. Reeves, “The Secrets of Rabbi Šimʿōn ben Yoḥai”](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Reeves-J-%E2%80%93-The-Secrets-.pdf)).
