## Works by Alejandro Christophersen

### Architectural works and realized designs

- **1889–1892:** *Buildings for the Bahía Blanca and North Western Railway*. A dispersed body of railway architecture made in partnership with Carl August Kihlberg; the surviving scholarly inventories do not securely identify every station or service building, so this is a corpus rather than a single obra.
- **1890–1891:** *Compañía Nueva de Gas de Buenos Aires works, Barracas*. Industrial complex designed with Carl August Kihlberg; much of it was later demolished, leaving only fragments of the original obra.
- **c. 1892:** *Residence for Nicholas Bouwer (later Palacio Mihanovich)*. Picturesque suburban house in Belgrano, designed with Carl August Kihlberg and subsequently demolished.
- **1893:** *Unzué residence and Café Tortoni building, Avenida de Mayo 825*. Mixed residential and commercial building; the café survives, but alteration complicates treating the present interior and Christophersen’s original obra as identical.
- **1893–1895:** *Building for María Carranza de Lawson (later Hotel Astoria)*. Hotel or income-producing urban building at Avenida de Mayo 902/916, surviving in modified form.
- **1893–1896:** *Hospital de Niños Dr. Ricardo Gutiérrez*. Hospital complex whose long construction history and later enlargement make Christophersen’s original architectural contribution only one layer of the present institution.
- **1894:** *Escuela No. 19 Leandro N. Alem*. Public-school building at Fray Cayetano Rodríguez 95.
- **1895:** *Palace for the Argentine National Congress*. Competition project rather than a realized building; it remains an obra as architectural intention even though Víctor Meano’s design was constructed instead.
- **1898:** *Panteón de la Sociedad Española de Socorros Mutuos*. Funerary monument in Chacarita Cemetery.
- **1898:** *Petit hôtel for Vicente Peralta Alvear*. Residence at Callao 1332, later demolished.
- **1898:** *Petit hôtel for Alfredo Martínez de Hoz*. Residence at Santa Fe 1026, demolished for the opening of Avenida 9 de Julio.
- **1898:** *House for the Mayol family*. Residence at Viamonte 726, demolished.
- **c. 1898:** *Marcel rental houses*. Three adjoining income houses on Venezuela 2763–2775; their precise construction date is not securely fixed.
- **1898–1901:** *Russian Orthodox Church of the Holy Trinity*. Christophersen directed the Buenos Aires construction, but the design was by Mikhail Timofeyevich Preobrazhensky; it therefore resists attribution as a building “by” Christophersen in the same sense as his independent designs.
- **1899:** *Palacio Tomás Devoto*. Eclectic residence at Callao 1025, demolished in 1938.
- **1899:** *Colegio and chapel of the Sagrados Corazones*. Educational and religious complex on Avenida Córdoba; later changes make its original extent difficult to separate from subsequent fabric.
- **c. 1900:** *Residence for Alberto Leloir*. House at Córdoba 624, subsequently demolished.
- **c. 1902:** *Christophersen residence, Guido 1565*. The architect’s own hôtel particulier, later demolished.
- **1902:** *Braceras residence*. Urban residence at Belgrano 2852, later used by the Argentine pulp-and-paper manufacturers’ association.
- **1903:** *Christophersen residence, Juncal 712*. Hôtel particulier designed for his own use.
- **1903:** *Residence for Domingo Acuña*. House at Arenales 1107, demolished.
- **1904:** *Benjamin Williams residence*. Hôtel particulier on Libertad; its recorded street number is inconsistent in published reproductions.
- **by 1904:** *Compañía Nueva de Gas administrative building, Alsina 1169*. Office building whose date is reported imprecisely in the inventories; it survives in institutional use.
- **1905:** *Sociedad Hipotecaria Belga América building*. Commercial building at Bartolomé Mitre 226, now greatly altered.
- **c. 1905:** *Christophersen residence, Juncal 1661*. Hôtel particulier made for the architect himself.
- **c. 1905:** *Petit hôtel for Antonio Santamarina*. Residence at Santa Fe 958, demolished.
- **1905:** *Residence for Alfredo T. Fernández*. House at Avenida Alvear 1494, demolished.
- **1905:** *Faculty of Exact Sciences/Engineering project*. Academic building designed in collaboration with engineer Pedro Coni Molina; the project belongs to Christophersen’s oeuvre even where its realized status is described inconsistently.
- **from 1905:** *Banco de la Nación Argentina branch buildings*. A distributed body of bank architecture in Buenos Aires Province and elsewhere—including branches catalogued at Chacabuco, Dolores, Mercedes, Santa Rosa and other locations—rather than one building; individual attribution and survival vary across the series.
- **1906:** *Residence for Pedro Méndez*. Hôtel particulier at Cerrito 1250, now the Centro Argentino de Ingenieros.
- **1906:** *Petit hôtel for the Gómez family*. Residence at Quintana 71, demolished.
- **1908:** *Hôtel Leloir, later Círculo Italiano*. Residence at Libertad 1264/1270; published inventories vary slightly in the street number.
- **c. 1908:** *Hôtel C. Rosetti*. Residence at Libertad 1370/1372.
- **1908:** *Hôtel E. Frías*. Residence on Avenida Quintana, subsequently altered or lost; catalogues do not consistently supply its number.
- **1908–1910:** *Palacio Anchorena*. A three-residence urban palace for Mercedes Castellanos de Anchorena, now Palacio San Martín; it is both building and coordinated decorative ensemble, approaching a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a bare architectural shell.
- **1909:** *Hôtel José Arce*. Residence at Arenales 1295.
- **1909:** *Hôtel Juan Zeballos*. Residence at Cerrito 1314.
- **1909:** *Residence for Héctor Cobo*. House at Suipacha 1258, demolished.
- **c. 1910:** *Hôtel Jacobi de Ayerza*. Residence at Talcahuano 1060.
- **1910:** *Hôtel Labougle*. Residence at Arenales 1612/1616.
- **1910:** *Hôtel J. Williams*. Residence at Libertad 1606.
- **1912:** *Hôtel Ledesma*. Residence at Montevideo 1545.
- **1912:** *Sanatorio Castro*. Private hospital at Ayacucho 1556.
- **1912:** *Hôtel Máximo Castro*. Urban residence identified in the scholarly chronology, although its exact address and present status are not consistently supplied.
- **1913–1916:** *Buenos Aires Stock Exchange*. Monumental institutional building at Sarmiento 299, won through a restricted competition; architecture, sculpture and interior decoration form a coordinated civic ensemble.
- **1914:** *Argentine Actors’ Association building*. Institutional building at Alsina 1762.
- **1918:** *Norwegian Seamen’s Church*. Church at Avenida Huergo 1267, later demolished; Christophersen also painted its interior frescoes, making the lost building a cross-media Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **c. 1922:** *Petit hôtel for Tomás Mackey*. Residence at Córdoba 609, Rosario, later demolished.
- **1922:** *Argentine Pavilion for the Rio de Janeiro International Exposition*. Temporary exhibition architecture that received a prize; its planned impermanence makes it a manifestation as much as a conventional building.
- **1923:** *Banco de la Nación Argentina, Once branch*. Public bank building associated with his larger branch-building programme.
- **1924:** *Residence on Pocitos beach*. Uruguayan seaside house discussed and illustrated by Christophersen; the inventories do not clearly distinguish the realized residence from his later model beach-house project.
- **1926:** *El Portazgo estancia house*. Country house at Melilla, Uruguay, interpreting colonial forms through modern domestic planning.
- **1927:** *Carlos Dupont rental building*. Income-producing apartment building at Callao 626, with documented interiors.
- **1928:** *Fernando Estrugamou residence*. Hôtel particulier at Callao and Arenales.
- **1928:** *San Ambrosio estancia*. Transformation and enlargement of a country estate near Gualeguay, Entre Ríos; it is an intervention in an older building rather than an entirely new autonomous object.
- **1928–1934:** *National Sanctuary and Basilica of Santa Rosa de Lima*. Church at Belgrano and Pasco, including dome, crypt and an oratory for María Unzué de Alvear and Ángel J. de Alvear; its architecture and liturgical spaces form a composite religious obra.
- **1929:** *Model house for Pocitos beach*. Published architectural project whose construction has not been established; it should be retained as a project, not silently converted into a building.
- **early 1930s:** *Two designs for economical houses*. Prototype housing projects published in 1932; their significance lies in the repeatable protocol for inexpensive dwelling rather than in a securely identified pair of constructed houses.
- **1934:** *Parish Church of Santa Magdalena Sofía Barat*. Church in Villa Devoto.
- **date uncertain:** *Parish Church of the Nativity of Mary*. Modest church at San Antonio 555; the online catalogue attributes it to Christophersen but does not establish a firm design date.
- **date uncertain:** *Church of Nuestra Señora del Carmen*. Religious building included in the scholarly pictorial inventory, without a consistently reported date.
- **date uncertain:** *Lincoln Cathedral*. Ecclesiastical design included in the scholarly inventory, but its chronology and precise extent of Christophersen’s intervention require further archival confirmation.
- **date uncertain:** *Hall of Honour for a club*. Surviving architectural sketch; no securely identified client, site or evidence of construction is known, so it resists classification beyond an undated project.
- **1940:** *Transradio Internacional building*. Office and communications building at Corrientes 500, among Christophersen’s late works and markedly more restrained than his earlier hôtels particuliers.

### Books and portfolios

- **1913:** *La arquitectura colonial y su origen* (also cited as *Los orígenes de la arquitectura colonial*). Published lecture or essay on colonial architecture; scholarly sources vary over the exact title, and the discrepancy should not be silently normalized.
- **1914:** *Petits Hôtels, casas y otros edificios*. Architectural portfolio presenting houses and other designs; it is both a book and a curated self-manifestation of professional work.
- **1919:** *Ideas sobre arte*. Collection of essays and lectures advancing Christophersen’s aesthetic position; several chapters originated as previously delivered or published texts.

### Essays, lectures, polemics and professional texts

- **1906:** *Conmemoración del gran Centenario*. Periodical essay.
- **1906:** *De actualidad: Patria y Arte*. Essay linking art and national culture.
- **1906:** *De actualidad: VII Congreso Internacional de Arquitectos*. Professional report or commentary.
- **1915:** *Rumbos nuevos*. Programmatic essay on future directions in architecture; although not formally labelled a manifesto, it acts like one by prescribing a cultural direction.
- **1917:** *Siluetas de antaño*. Historical or memorial essay.
- **1917:** *La arquitectura colonial y su origen*. Published extract from a lecture delivered at the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes; related to, but bibliographically not identical with, the 1913 book.
- **1917:** *Los concursos*. Professional essay on architectural competitions.
- **1917:** *El balance de un siglo de arquitectura*. Historical-critical essay.
- **1917:** *Una exposición de retratos*. Art-critical review.
- **1918:** *La Bolsa de Comercio de Buenos Aires*. Architectural account of his own building.
- **1918:** *A propósito de arte colonial*. Polemical essay.
- **1918:** *Los arquitectos y el Salón Anual*. Professional and art-critical commentary.
- **1918:** *Iglesia para Marineros Noruegos*. Presentation of his church design.
- **1923:** *Edificios públicos y monumentales: Edificio de Sucursal 11 de Setiembre del Banco de la Nación Argentina*. Project presentation.
- **1923:** *Pabellón Argentino en la Exposición Internacional de Río de Janeiro de 1922*. Retrospective account of the temporary pavilion.
- **1923:** *Los rascacielos y las construcciones gigantescas*. Critical essay on tall buildings and metropolitan scale.
- **1924:** *Una residencia en la Playa de Pocitos*. Architectural project presentation.
- **1924:** *Semejanza de la música y de la arquitectura*. Comparative aesthetic essay.
- **1925:** *Fundamentos de la arquitectura colonial*. Theoretical essay.
- **1925:** *Reflexiones sobre la reglamentación de la profesión de arquitecto*. Professional-policy argument; in function it approaches a protocol for regulating architectural practice.
- **1925:** *Casa de renta: Uruguay esquina Juncal*. Project presentation.
- **1925:** *Un concurso de planos hace 30 años*. Historical recollection of an architectural competition.
- **1926:** *Salpicón profesional*. Professional commentary.
- **1926:** *Casa en la estancia Melilla*. Presentation of the El Portazgo country-house project.
- **1926:** *Las nuevas tendencias arquitectónicas*. Polemical response to architectural modernism.
- **1926:** *Salpicón arquitectónico*. Architectural commentary.
- **1927:** *Un rato de charla con un futurista*. Polemical dialogue or essay directed at avant-garde aesthetics.
- **1927:** *Las diversas influencias arquitectónicas en la edificación de Buenos Aires*. Historical survey.
- **1927:** *Propiedad de renta: Callao esquina Tucumán (Sr. Carlos Dupont)*. Project presentation.
- **1928:** *Residencia particular: Callao esquina Arenales (Sr. Fernando Estrugamou)*. Project presentation.
- **1929:** *Modelo para una casa en la Playa de Pocitos, Montevideo*. Published project for a prototype seaside house.
- **1929:** *Hacia un lógico renacimiento arquitectónico*. Programmatic argument for architectural renewal; it functions as a conservative manifesto rather than neutral description.
- **1930:** *Transformación y ampliación de una vieja estancia en Entre Ríos*. Account of the San Ambrosio intervention.
- **1931:** *Monsieur Ángel en Montparnasse*, by Pierre-Gilles Veber. Spanish translation by Christophersen rather than an independently authored work; it belongs to his made textual output but resists classification as his literary creation.
- **1931:** *Santuario de Nuestra Señora de Santa Rosa de Lima*. Presentation of the sanctuary project.
- **1931:** *Apuntes para una lógica orientación de la enseñanza de la arquitectura*. Pedagogical prescription—effectively a protocol for architectural education.
- **1931:** *Inconvenientes de la arquitectura a sueldo del Estado*. Professional-policy essay.
- **1931:** *La guardia vieja*. Cultural or professional commentary.
- **1931:** *A propósito de la teoría del mecanismo*. Theoretical polemic.
- **1932:** *Dos interesantes proyectos de casas económicas*. Presentation of repeatable low-cost-house designs.
- **1933:** *Algunas modificaciones sobre enseñanza de la arquitectura*. Pedagogical proposal.
- **1933:** *Las casas económicas*. Housing-policy and design essay.
- **1933:** *Tres cosas viejas en el arte nuevo*. Polemical art criticism.
- **1934:** *La construcción de la casa propia*. Practical architectural guidance, approaching a protocol for prospective householders.
- **1934:** *El cuarto artefacto*. Architectural or cultural commentary.
- **1934:** *Santuario Nacional de Santa Rosa de Lima*. Later presentation of the completed or nearly completed sanctuary.
- **1935:** *¿Qué es un arquitecto?*. Definition and defence of the profession.
- **1935:** *La arquitectura en el siglo XIX*. Historical essay.
- **1935:** *Reflexiones sobre los concursos*. Late reconsideration of architectural competitions.
- **1936:** *Visión de Buenos Aires a vuelo de pájaro*. Urban essay or imaginative survey.
- **1940:** *Edificio de la Transradio Internacional*. Presentation of his late office building.
- **date uncertain:** *Adiós a los planos*. Public valedictory announcing his retirement from architectural practice; surviving references establish the utterance or text but do not provide a secure publication date or stable bibliographical form, so it resists treatment as a conventional titled work.

### Painting, drawing and integrated decoration

- **c. 1907–1911:** *French-period paintings*. A body of oils and watercolours made during and after study with Tony Robert-Fleury and Jules-Joseph Lefebvre, shown at Bordeaux, the Salon des Artistes Français and the Paris Salon d’Automne. The scholarly survey estimates Christophersen’s pictorial production at nearly 300 works but supplies no catalogue raisonné, making a responsible object-by-object list impossible.
- **1918:** *Frescoes for the Norwegian Seamen’s Church*. Monumental wall paintings integrated with his church architecture; both paintings and building were destroyed, so the obra survives through documentation rather than as a physical object.
- **date uncertain:** *Niña leyendo*. Signed watercolour; an auction catalogue records the object but supplies no secure creation date.
- **date uncertain:** *Mujer con flores y aves*. Signed oil on panel; known through the art market, without a secure date or documented exhibition history.
- **date uncertain:** *Mujer con mantón*. Signed oil painting; undated.
- **date uncertain:** *En el atelier*. Signed watercolour on paper; undated.
- **date uncertain:** *Portrait of a woman*. Painting sold under differing generic descriptions; its unstable title is descriptive rather than necessarily authorial.
- **date uncertain:** *Coastal landscape with figures*. Painting attributed to Christophersen in the international art market; the generic title and attribution history do not establish an original title.
- **throughout his career:** *Portraits, landscapes, oils and watercolours*. A pictorial corpus held across the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Norwegian diplomatic collections, Argentine provincial museums, the University of Buenos Aires, the Sociedad Central de Arquitectos and private collections. This body is unquestionably an obra, but it resists enumeration because no standard scholarly catalogue identifies all works, titles and dates.
