## Works by The Comte de Saint-Germain

### Musical obras

- **1747–1750:** *English Songs*. Four separately published vocal compositions—*The Maid That’s Made for Love and Me (O Wouldst Thou Know What Sacred Charms)*, *It Is Not That I Love You Less*, *Gentle Love, This Hour Befriend Me*, and *Jove, When He Saw My Fanny’s Face*—survive; treating them as one obra describes a small publication group, not a cycle conceived as a whole. ([Chrissochoidis, pp. 6–7](https://www.secm.org/newsletter/volume16.pdf); [Overton-Fuller inventory](https://findingcountstgermain.blogspot.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1747:** *The Favourite Songs in the Opera Call’d L’Incostanza Delusa*. A printed manifestation of Saint-Germain’s contributions to an operatic pasticcio rather than an opera composed wholly by him. David Hunter and Ilias Chrissochoidis count three contributed arias, while Jean Overton-Fuller’s inventory marks four—*Senza pietà mi credi*, *Dille che l’amor mio*, *Digli, digli*, and *Per pietà, bell’idol mio*—so the extent of his contribution remains disputed. ([Chrissochoidis, pp. 6–7](https://www.secm.org/newsletter/volume16.pdf); [Hunter, “The Great Pretender”](https://doi.org/10.2307/3650726); [Overton-Fuller inventory](https://findingcountstgermain.blogspot.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **c. 1750:** *Musique raisonnée selon le bon sens, aux Dames Angloises qui aiment le vrai goût en cet art*. An obra comprising forty-two Italian arias for voice, strings, and continuo: *Padre perdona; oh pene!*, *Non piangete amati*, *Intendo il tuo rossor*, *Senza pietà mi credi*, *Già, già che morir deggio*, *Dille che l’amor mio*, *Mio ben ricordati*, *Digli, digli, che un infedele*, *Per pietà, bell’idol mio*, *Non so, con dolce moto*, *Piango, è ver*, *Dal labbro che t’accende*, *Se mai riviene*, *Parlerò; non è permesso*, *Se tutti i miei pensieri*, *Guardalo, guardalo in volto*, *Oh Dio, mancarmi*, *Digli che son fedele*, *Pensa che sei crudele*, *Torna, torna innocente*, *Un certo non so che*, *Guardami, guardami prima in volto*, *Parto se vuoi così*, *Volga’l ciel, felici amanti*, *Guarda pria se in questa fronte*, *Quanto mai felici siete*, *Ah che nel dirti addio*, *Dopo un tuo sguardo, ingrata*, *Serberò fra’ ceppi ancora*, *Figlio, se più non vivi*, *Quando parto, e non rispondo*, *Povero cor, perché palpiti*, *Se de’ tuoi lumi al foco*, *Non v’è più barbaro*, *Se tosto mi sdegno*, *Hai negli occhi*, *Come potesti, oh Dio?*, *Che sorte crudele d’un povero amante*, *Se almen potesse il pianto*, *Se viver non poss’io*, *Fedel sarò, sarò cara*, and *Non ha ragione, ingrato*. The title suggests a didactic protocol, but the surviving object is principally a collected score; several titles vary between modern inventories because their first lines have been transcribed differently. ([Chrissochoidis, pp. 6–7](https://www.secm.org/newsletter/volume16.pdf); [facsimile and contents](https://imslp.org/wiki/Musique_Raisonn%C3%A9e_selon_le_bon_sens_%28Comte_de_Saint_Germain%29))

- **1750:** *Six Sonatas for Two Violins with a Bass for the Harpsichord or Violoncello*. A chamber-music obra containing sonatas in F major, B-flat major, E-flat major, G minor, G major, and A major; the frequently reproduced “Op. 47–52” labels belong to a modern sequential inventory, not an eighteenth-century opus scheme. ([Chrissochoidis, pp. 6–7](https://www.secm.org/newsletter/volume16.pdf); [Overton-Fuller inventory](https://findingcountstgermain.blogspot.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1758:** *Seven Solos for a Violin*. A collection of violin sonatas with figured bass, in B-flat major, E major, C minor, E-flat major, E-flat major, A major, and B-flat major. The “Op. 53–59” numbers sometimes attached to them likewise come from the modern inventory rather than the original print. ([Chrissochoidis, pp. 6–7](https://www.secm.org/newsletter/volume16.pdf); [Overton-Fuller inventory](https://findingcountstgermain.blogspot.com/p/bibliography.html))

### Poetry

- **Date unknown:** *Sonnet philosophique* (*Philosophical Sonnet*). A short French alchemical or mystical poem traditionally attributed to Saint-Germain; its documentary basis and date are insufficiently established for it to be treated as securely authenticated. ([Hall, *Sages and Seers*, attribution discussed here](https://amorc.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/2020-09_RC_Beacon.pdf))

### Attributed illuminated and cipher manuscripts

- **Eighteenth century; precise date unknown:** *La Très Sainte Trinosophie*. An illuminated, twelve-part initiatory or alchemical narrative preserved as Troyes MS 2400—a Gesamtkunstwerk of prose, cipher, diagrams, colour, and allegorical images rather than merely a “book.” Its attribution resists acceptance: the manuscript is a copy, and scholars have variously proposed Saint-Germain, Cagliostro, or an unidentified author; association with Saint-Germain depends substantially on a later ownership or bookseller’s note. ([Raphael Patai, *The Jewish Alchemists*, pp. 475–476](https://rexresearch1.com/AlchemyArchives/PataiJewishAlchemists.pdf); [Troyes-manuscript account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Tr%C3%A8s_Sainte_Trinosophie))

- **c. 1750:** *Untitled triangular cipher manuscript* (Getty Research Institute, MS 210). A triangular vellum object combining ciphered French, magical diagrams, and ritual instructions: simultaneously an obra, protocol, and deliberately shaped material artefact. Its opening presents it as Saint-Germain’s gift, which establishes association or provenance but does not by itself prove that he authored or fabricated the manuscript. ([Getty catalogue record](https://hdl.handle.net/10020/950053b35); [manuscript description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Triangular_Book_of_St._Germain))

- **c. 1775:** *No. Soixante & Seize de la collection maçonnique* (Getty Research Institute, MS 209). A second triangular cipher manuscript, made for the Freemason Antoine-Louis Moret and bearing an *ex dono* inscription naming Saint-Germain. Because “given by” is not equivalent to “written or made by,” this object resists inclusion as his work and is recorded here only as a disputed attribution or manifestation of a Saint-Germain-associated protocol. ([Getty catalogue record](https://hdl.handle.net/10020/950053b34); [manuscript history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Triangular_Book_of_St._Germain))

### Material experiments and manufactures

- **c. 1757–1760:** *Chambord colour and dye laboratory*. A royal experimental project supported by Louis XV for producing dyes and improving the colours of French textiles. Contemporary reports establish the laboratory and activity more securely than any surviving named formula or product, so the project—not an undocumented “invention”—is the obra that can responsibly be listed. ([Franco, “The Count of St. Germain”](https://doi.org/10.1093/mq/XXXVI.4.540); [historical summary](https://www.britannica.com/biography/comte-de-Saint-Germain))

- **1779–1784:** *Eckernförde silk-dyeing manufactory*. A collaborative project with Prince Charles of Hesse-Kassel intended to apply Saint-Germain’s colouring processes commercially. The enterprise and dyed samples were reported, but no stable catalogue of attributable products or surviving secret formula permits its output to be divided into individual works. ([Charles of Hesse-Kassel, *Mémoires de mon temps*](https://archive.org/details/mmoiresdemonte00hesgoog); [historical account](https://iniziazioneantica.altervista.org/1700-1800/saint_germain/The_Count_of_Saint-Germain02.pdf))

- **Dates unknown:** *Paintings, pigments, treated gemstones, oils, medicines, and metallic preparations reported under Saint-Germain’s name*. Witnesses and later biographies describe such objects and processes, including unusually brilliant pigments and the alleged removal of flaws from gems, but no catalogue raisonné securely identifies surviving individual examples. These are reported manifestations or demonstrations, not presently recoverable obras; claims that he manufactured large diamonds, gold, an elixir of life, or similarly miraculous substances remain anecdotal and should not be converted into works. ([Franco, “The Count of St. Germain”](https://doi.org/10.1093/mq/XXXVI.4.540); [Patai, *The Jewish Alchemists*, Saint-Germain chapter](https://rexresearch1.com/AlchemyArchives/PataiJewishAlchemists.pdf))
