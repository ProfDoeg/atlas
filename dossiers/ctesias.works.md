## Works by Ctesias of Cnidus

### Historical obra

- **After 398/397 BCE; exact date unknown:** *Persica* (*Persiká*, “Persian Matters”). A twenty-three-book historical narrative extending from the legendary Assyrian king Ninus through the eighth regnal year of Artaxerxes II; *Assyriaka* names its first six books, not a securely independent work. The original is lost: it survives through Photius’s epitome and quotations or adaptations by later authors, whose mediation makes both wording and attribution fragmentary. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ctesias-/); [Nichols, pp. 11–13](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))

### Ethnographic and paradoxographical obra

- **Probably soon after 398/397 BCE; exact date unknown:** *Indica* (*Indiká*, “Indian Matters”). A one-book prose monograph describing the Indus region, its peoples, animals, plants, medicines, and marvels; it is ethnography entwined with paradoxography rather than a “history of India” in the modern sense. Ctesias had not visited India, and the lost original is recoverable only through Photius’s epitome and later quotations. ([Lenfant, “L’Inde de Ctésias,” pp. 309–336](https://www.persee.fr/doc/topoi_1161-9473_1995_num_5_2_1572); [Nichols, pp. 9–11, 18](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781472519979_A24067884/preview-9781472519979_A24067884.pdf))

### Administrative and geographical treatises

- *On the Tributes of Asia* (*Peri tōn kata tēn Asian phorōn*). A lost prose treatise apparently concerning the peoples subject to the Persian king and their contributions, including provisions for the royal table; only two testimonia in Athenaeus survive, so its full scope and its relationship to *Persica* remain uncertain. ([Nichols, F53–F54 and discussion, pp. 46, 124](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))

- *Periodos* (*Circuit of the Earth*), also cited as *Periegesis*, *Periplous*, and *Periplous of Asia*. A lost geographical obra of at least three books, apparently treating Egypt, the Black Sea region, and Asia; scholarship generally understands the several ancient labels as variant titles for one work, although the assignment of individual fragments is sometimes uncertain. ([Nichols, F55–F60 and discussion, pp. 46, 125–126, 226–228](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))

### Possible medical writings

- *Treatise on Hellebore* (conventional descriptive title). Oribasius preserves a passage explicitly introduced as Ctesias’s treatment of hellebore, but whether it came from an independent medical treatise cannot now be established. It therefore resists counting as a securely separate obra. ([Nichols, F68 and discussion, pp. 47, 127](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))

- Untitled discussion of reducing a dislocated hip. Galen reports Ctesias’s criticism of Hippocrates, but preserves neither a title nor proof that the statement belonged to an independent medical work; Dominique Lenfant allows that it may instead have occurred in *Persica* or *Indica*. This is evidence of Ctesias’s medical writing or argument, not evidence for a countable separate book. ([Nichols, F67 and discussion, pp. 47, 127](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))

### Doubtful or falsely titled works

- *On Mountains*. Pseudo-Plutarch attributes a medicinal notice to Book II of a work under this title, but the title occurs nowhere else and Felix Jacoby rejected it as spurious. The underlying material may ultimately derive from Ctesias, yet the alleged treatise may be no more than a later label for a section of another work; it resists recognition as an independent obra. ([Nichols, F73 and discussion, pp. 47–48, 128, 229–230](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))

- *On Rivers*. Pseudo-Plutarch similarly assigns a medicinal plant notice to Book I of this otherwise unattested title. Its Ctesian content is possible, but its existence as a separate treatise is disputed and Jacoby classified the fragment as false. ([Nichols, F74 and discussion, pp. 47–48, 128, 230](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/UF/E0/02/25/21/00001/nichols_a.pdf))
