# Miguel Serrano: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary method

This dossier concerns **Miguel Joaquín Diego del Carmen Serrano Fernández** (1917–2009), the Chilean writer, diplomat, Antarctic traveler, political activist, and principal theorist of “esoteric Hitlerism.” It does not concern other writers or public figures named Miguel Serrano.

The following labels are used where the status of a claim requires clarification:

- **Documented fact:** supported by institutional records, publications, surviving correspondence, photographs, or multiple independent contemporary accounts.
- **Reported fact:** stated by a generally credible secondary source but not independently verified here from the underlying record.
- **Self-report:** stated by Serrano in memoirs, interviews, or autobiographical writing.
- **Allegation or theory:** a claim advanced without sufficient evidence to establish it.
- **Rumor or myth:** a legendary or propagandistic story important to Serrano’s identity or reception but unsupported or contradicted by the historical record.

The official Miguel Serrano archive maintained by EB Libros is exceptionally detailed, but it is a **posthumous, sympathetic estate source**. It preserves useful dates, publications, photographs, and excerpts; its ideological interpretations require independent control. The Biblioteca Nacional de Chile, contemporary newspapers, published correspondence, academic studies, and government material provide that control where available.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full name:** Miguel Joaquín Diego del Carmen Serrano Fernández.
- **Usual name:** Miguel Serrano.
- **Occasional forms:** Miguel Serrano Fernández; Don Miguel Serrano.
- **Honorifics and descriptions used by followers:** “Maestro,” “poet of esoteric Hitlerism,” “initiate,” and “last avatar’s herald.” These are sectarian or literary designations, not public offices.
- **Pseudonyms:** No consistently documented literary pseudonym has been established. His principal publications appeared under Miguel Serrano.
- **Born:** 10 September 1917.
- **Birthplace:** the home of his paternal grandparents at Santo Domingo 661, Santiago, Chile.
- **Died:** 28 February 2009, Santiago, Chile, aged ninety-one.
- **Cause of death:** cerebral hemorrhage or stroke.
- **Burial:** Cementerio General de Santiago, following a service that combined Catholic, familial, Nordic, Mapuche, and National Socialist elements.
- **Nationality:** Chilean.
- **Principal occupations:** writer, editor, journalist, Antarctic expedition member, career diplomat, ambassador, political propagandist, lecturer, and independent publisher.
- **Principal diplomatic appointments:** India, with accreditation extending to neighboring Asian states; Yugoslavia; Romania; Bulgaria; and Austria. The precise rank and dates evolved: he began in India as a diplomatic representative and subsequently held ambassadorial rank.
- **Principal ideological designation:** National Socialist from the late 1930s onward; after the 1970s, the chief systematizer of a syncretic doctrine he called **hitlerismo esotérico**.
- **Principal residences:** Santiago and the Claro valley in childhood; New Delhi; Belgrade; Vienna; Montagnola in Ticino, Switzerland; Valparaíso; and Santiago in his final years.

A Biblioteca Nacional chronology mistakenly gives his death as “29 February 2009.” No such date existed in 2009. Its prose account and independent obituaries give the correct date, **28 February 2009**.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and childhood household

Serrano was the son of:

- **Diego Serrano Manterola**, his father.
- **Berta Fernández Fernández**, his mother.

His mother died when he was approximately five. His father died several years later, leaving Miguel and his siblings orphaned while still young. The Biblioteca Nacional chronology places the transfer to his grandmother’s care in 1924, although some biographical summaries state that his father died when Miguel was about eight. The exact sequence within 1922–1925 should therefore be treated cautiously.

He reportedly had **two younger brothers and a sister**. Published summaries generally do not provide a reliably documented complete sibling list.

The children were raised principally by their paternal grandmother, **Fresia Manterola de Serrano**. They lived first in central Santiago and later on rural family property in the Claro valley. Serrano’s memoirs assign great symbolic importance to the family houses, gardens, animals, mountains, and an ancestral sapphire ring. These memories became components of his later “mythical autobiography.”

His estate chronology identifies **Pedro Fernández Recio**, builder of the family chapel of San Pedro, as a great-great-grandfather. Serrano represented his ancestry as old Chilean, landed, Catholic, and partly connected with the country’s political and literary elite. Exact claims of remote European, Visigothic, or “Aryan” lineage appearing in ideological presentations are not established genealogical facts.

### Vicente Huidobro

Serrano called the avant-garde poet **Vicente Huidobro** his uncle. The relationship is reported in the Biblioteca Nacional’s account of Serrano’s political development and in his memoirs. Huidobro’s challenge that Serrano should go fight in the Spanish Civil War reportedly helped end Serrano’s brief communist alignment. The exact genealogical degree of “uncle” is not consistently explained in accessible biographical sources.

### Marriage and children

Serrano’s first wife was **Carmen Rosselot Bordeau**. A January 1957 family photograph preserved by his estate identifies the couple with three children:

- **Carmen Serrano Rosselot**
- **José Miguel Serrano Rosselot**
- **Cristián Serrano Rosselot**

Carmen Rosselot died in the late 1980s; the estate chronology records the death in its 1985–1989 section. Serrano’s bibliography notes that he wrote a preface for his daughter Carmen’s book *El despertar* in 1961.

A grandson, **Miguel Serrano Spaltman**, later entered Chilean public discussion and was reported as a prospective presidential candidate in 2013. Film director **Sebastián Araya** was identified in funeral reporting as another grandson.

In 2000 Serrano married **María Isabel Pérez Quintela**, usually known as **Sabela P. Quintela** or **Sabela Quintela**. They had reportedly met in Spain in 1989; she moved to Chile in 1994. They shared a house at Avenida Alemania 5558 in Valparaíso during part of the 1990s. She became his literary executor and an organizer of the posthumous archive. Reports concerning the estate state that she inherited two Santiago apartments, but no complete probate file, property valuation, or verified inventory was located in the consulted sources.

### Intimate and symbolic relationships

Serrano’s books use women as both biographical persons and symbolic figures. A particularly important relationship was with **Irene Klatt Getta**, whom he met in Santiago in 1951. She became an inspiration for the “She,” “Queen of Sheba,” or absent feminine figure recurring in his writing. The factual extent and physical character of their relationship are difficult to extract from Serrano’s deliberately initiatory and mythologized prose. It is safest to describe her as a documented acquaintance and literary-spiritual muse.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1917–1935

The early deaths of Serrano’s parents were the central trauma of his childhood. His memoirs repeatedly return to abandonment, the lost mother, the divided self, ancestral memory, and attempts to recover an original unity. Animals, rural landscapes, mountains, stones, rings, and dreams acquired an enduring symbolic role.

His secondary schooling was unusually fragmented. Reported institutions and approximate dates are:

- Colegio de los Sagrados Corazones de Santiago, 1927–1928.
- Instituto de Humanidades Luis Campino, 1929–1930.
- Internado Nacional Barros Arana, approximately 1930–1933.
- Liceo Valentín Letelier, approximately 1933–1935.

Some summaries compress his Barros Arana attendance to 1929–1934. The estate chronology supports frequent changes rather than one continuous course. He did not complete a university degree and apparently chose not to enter university.

An accident during his youth left him immobilized for a period. His friend **Guillermo Tapia** reportedly encouraged him to write during the convalescence. This became Serrano’s conventional account of the beginning of his literary vocation.

By the early 1930s he was moving within a Santiago circle that included **Héctor Barreto**, **Guillermo Atías**, **Santiago del Campo**, and **Julio Molina Müller**. Later associates in the literary Generation of 1938 included Eduardo Anguita, Braulio Arenas, Teófilo Cid, Enrique Gómez Correa, Carlos Droguett, Juan Emar, and others. Serrano called this milieu the **“secret generation.”**

## Education and Formation

Serrano was essentially self-educated after secondary school. His formation combined:

- Chilean modernist and avant-garde literature.
- German Romanticism.
- Nietzsche, Wagner, Goethe, Novalis, and Hermann Hesse.
- Oswald Spengler’s cyclical theory of civilization.
- Jungian psychology, especially archetypes, synchronicity, individuation, and the Self.
- Hinduism, yoga, tantra, kundalini, Shaivism, and the doctrine of cyclical ages.
- Tibetan Buddhism and Himalayan sacred geography.
- Hermeticism, alchemy, Gnosticism, Grail legends, and the supposed “King of the World.”
- Twentieth-century occult racialism.
- National Socialist racial mythology and antisemitic conspiracy literature.
- Later Holocaust-denial literature.

No single teacher initiated him into all these fields. His intellectual development was cumulative and frequently transformed literary images into assertions about history or cosmology.

Among his formative personal contacts were:

- **Héctor Barreto**, friend and martyr figure in Serrano’s early political imagination.
- **Vicente Huidobro**, literary elder and political challenger.
- **Jorge González von Marées**, leader of Chile’s Movimiento Nacional-Socialista.
- A Chilean esoteric circle sometimes described as an occult order led by a figure whom Serrano called “F.K.” Its exact structure and membership remain poorly documented outside Serrano’s recollections.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru**, **Indira Gandhi**, the Dalai Lama, and Indian religious practitioners encountered during his diplomatic service.
- **Hermann Hesse** and **Carl Gustav Jung**, whose conversations and correspondence provided prestige and intellectual vocabulary, though neither endorsed Serrano’s later esoteric Hitlerism.

## Early Political and Literary Career, 1932–1946

### Left-wing phase

Beginning around 1932, Serrano contributed to left-wing newspapers or periodicals including:

- *Sobre la Marcha*, associated with the Uruguayan writer **Blanca Luz Brum**.
- *Frente Popular*.
- *La Hora*.

Following the violent death of his friend Héctor Barreto at the hands of Chilean National Socialists, Serrano briefly moved toward communism. This was a personal and emotional alignment rather than a long, disciplined communist career. According to the Biblioteca Nacional and Serrano’s recollections, Vicente Huidobro challenged him to act on his politics by going to fight in Spain; Serrano did not go, and the episode contributed to his break with communism.

### Generation of 1938

In 1938 he selected, introduced, and annotated *Antología del verdadero cuento en Chile*. It included his work and stories by writers such as Pedro Carrillo, Braulio Arenas, Adrián Jiménez, Juan Tejeda, Eduardo Anguita, Teófilo Cid, Juan Emar, Carlos Droguett, Anuar Atías, and Héctor Barreto.

The anthology was both a literary intervention and an attempt to create a generational identity. Serrano later described the group as united less by a formal program than by catastrophe, friendship, nocturnal Santiago, and a search for an undisclosed spiritual America.

### Conversion to Chilean National Socialism

On 5 September 1938, Chilean police killed approximately sixty young members and supporters of the Movimiento Nacional-Socialista after a failed coup attempt centered on the Seguro Obrero building. Serrano interpreted the killings as martyrdom. The massacre became the decisive event in his turn toward National Socialism.

He wrote for **Trabajo**, the movement’s newspaper, and accompanied its leader, **Jorge González von Marées**, on speaking tours. This association is documented political activity, not merely later self-identification.

His transition involved an unresolved personal contradiction: the movement whose members killed Barreto became the movement Serrano joined after the state killed its militants. In his mythic reconstruction he treated both Barreto and the Seguro Obrero dead as victims of the same degraded age.

### *La Nueva Edad*

From July 1941 through 1942 Serrano directed and edited the journal *La Nueva Edad*. At least thirty-five issues are listed in the estate bibliography. Its articles included:

- “El gran símbolo de Hess no ha sido comprendido.”
- “Chile es un hueso duro de roer.”
- “Más allá del nacismo.”
- “Los judíos invaden Chile.”
- “La India o el triunfo del espíritu.”
- “La democracia y el nacionalsocialismo.”
- “Nacionalsocialismo y comunismo.”
- “Nacionalsocialismo y cristianismo.”

These texts establish that his National Socialism and antisemitism were public and explicit during the Second World War. His later Hitlerism was therefore not simply a post-1970 invention, although its cosmic and occult system was developed much later.

During 1943–1946, his estate says that he appeared on wartime “blacklists.” This is plausible given his open Axis sympathy, but the exact Allied or Chilean documents, dates, and legal consequences were not located in the consulted sources. There is no evidence in those sources that he was imprisoned or formally prosecuted for wartime activity.

## Antarctica and Postwar Literary Development, 1947–1952

Serrano joined the **Second Chilean Antarctic Expedition of 1947–1948**, traveling aboard Chilean naval vessels into the Antarctic region. This was a real state expedition connected to Chile’s territorial program; it was not an occult expedition in origin.

His Antarctic experience produced:

- The lecture and pamphlet *La Antártica y otros mitos* (1948).
- Sections of *Ni por mar ni por tierra* (1950).
- *Quién llama en los hielos* (1957).
- A lifelong identification of Antarctica as a sacred, hidden, or polar landscape.

The expedition gave him documentary experience of the continent but not evidence for the later claims he attached to it. He increasingly interpreted ice fields, warm-water “oases,” unexplored interior regions, vanished civilizations, UFOs, and Hitler-survival stories as parts of a unified polar mystery.

In 1950, *Ni por mar ni por tierra… (historia de una generación)* reconstructed his literary generation, Barreto’s death, Chilean landscape, and Serrano’s movement from ordinary politics toward myth. It remains one of the most valuable biographical sources, though it is literary memoir rather than neutral history.

His 1951 European travels included postwar Germany and Switzerland. He wrote articles about Berlin, General Eisenhower’s headquarters, Berchtesgaden, Giovanni Papini, and Hermann Hesse.

## Diplomatic Career and Major Journeys

## India and South Asia, 1953–1962

Serrano entered Chile’s diplomatic service and went to India in 1953. Sources variously describe him as chargé d’affaires, minister, and ambassador across this period. Chile’s representation was elevated during his tenure, and he ultimately held ambassadorial rank.

His responsibilities or concurrent accreditations reportedly extended at different times to **India, Nepal, Burma/Myanmar, and Indonesia**, although exact dates for every accreditation require confirmation from Chilean foreign-ministry appointment records.

### Official and political contacts

He developed a documented relationship with Prime Minister **Jawaharlal Nehru** and Nehru’s daughter **Indira Gandhi**. The estate chronology says his intervention in arranging a visit by Chilean pianist **Claudio Arrau** helped begin his friendship with Indira Gandhi and her family.

Serrano supported Chilean cultural diplomacy, wrote in *The Hindustan Times*, and participated in discussions concerning the nonaligned world. Near the end of the posting, he prepared a memorandum on possible Chilean participation in the Conference of Non-Aligned Countries.

He met the **Fourteenth Dalai Lama** in Mussoorie shortly after the Dalai Lama’s 1959 flight from Tibet. Serrano published “Encuentro con el Dalai Lama” that year and “Mi despedida del dalái lama” in 1962. Later admirers enlarged this into a claim that Serrano was the first diplomat to extend official assistance or recognition. The meeting is documented; the stronger claim of uniquely decisive diplomatic intervention has not been established from Indian, Tibetan, or Chilean government records consulted here.

### Pilgrimages and religious inquiry

Serrano traveled extensively through India, including Himalayan pilgrimages associated with Amarnath and Badrinath. He sought yogis, temples, sacred rivers, and traditions concerning Mount Kailash. His published travel writing mixed observation, dream, allegory, and initiatory interpretation.

His Indian period produced:

- *Quién llama en los hielos* (1957).
- *Las visitas de la reina de Saba* (1960).
- *Los misterios* / *The Mysteries* (1960).
- *La serpiente del paraíso* (written by 1961; published in 1963).

These books created his international literary reputation. They were translated into English and several European and Asian languages.

### Hesse and Jung

Serrano first met **Hermann Hesse** at Montagnola in June 1951 and revisited him during his years abroad. Their friendship is supported by published letters, contemporary articles, photographs, Hesse-related publication records, and Serrano’s accounts.

He also met **Carl Gustav Jung** and corresponded with him from the late 1950s until Jung’s death in 1961. Jung contributed a preface or introductory text associated with *The Visits of the Queen of Sheba*. Serrano later reproduced Jung’s letters in *El círculo hermético*.

The evidentiary limits are important:

- The meetings and correspondence are documented.
- Jung and Hesse engaged with Serrano’s literary, mythological, psychological, and Indian interests.
- Neither man can be shown to have endorsed Serrano’s later Holocaust denial, racial cosmology, or deification of Hitler.
- Serrano’s later presentation of the relationships was filtered through his own system and should not be treated as a statement of Jung’s or Hesse’s politics.

Serrano visited both men for the last time in 1961. After Jung’s death, he wrote to Hesse about him; related material appeared in the *Neue Zürcher Zeitung*. Hesse died in 1962.

## Yugoslavia, Romania, and Bulgaria, 1962–1966

Serrano declined a proposed embassy in Cuba and accepted appointment to Yugoslavia. He served in Belgrade from approximately 1962 to 1964 and continued with responsibilities in Eastern Europe thereafter.

In Yugoslavia he encountered the political system of **Josip Broz Tito**, maintained Chilean diplomatic representation, and sought Tito’s involvement in Latin American and nonaligned affairs. Serrano later said that he wanted Chile to invite Tito partly to counter Bolivian diplomatic initiatives.

He became Chile’s first ambassador extraordinary and plenipotentiary to:

- The Socialist Republic of Romania, presenting credentials to **Chivu Stoica**.
- The People’s Republic of Bulgaria, presenting credentials to **Georgi Traikov** on 10 December 1965.

He continued to publish on Nehru, Indira Gandhi, Jung, Hesse, Rilke, Teilhard de Chardin, mythology, and UFOs.

## Austria and Europe, 1964/1967–1970

The Biblioteca Nacional gives 1964–1970 for Serrano’s Austrian ambassadorship; the estate divides 1967–1970 as the Vienna phase, reflecting overlapping Eastern European responsibilities and a gradual relocation.

In Vienna he represented Chile before the Austrian government and international organizations based there. His diplomatic work has been overshadowed by his literary and political activities, and no evidence of personal enrichment, corruption, or diplomatic criminality was found.

He cultivated literary and political contacts across Europe and visited Montagnola frequently. His publications during this period included:

- *El círculo hermético. De Hermann Hesse a C. G. Jung*.
- English and German editions of the same work.
- *La flor inexistente* / *The Ultimate Flower*.
- International editions of his Indian travel books.

## Dismissal, Swiss Residence, and Return to Hitlerism, 1970–1980

### End of diplomatic service

Following the election of **Salvador Allende** in 1970, Serrano was removed from diplomatic service. Sources agree on the political transition but differ slightly over whether the decisive administrative date was in late 1970 or 1971.

Serrano represented the dismissal as political exclusion. No source consulted indicates that it resulted from a criminal or financial investigation. Nor was evidence found that Allende and Serrano had a substantial personal relationship.

### Montagnola and Casa Camuzzi

Serrano formally established residence in **Casa Camuzzi**, Montagnola, on 9 November 1971. Hesse had formerly lived in part of the same historic building. Serrano remained in the Ticino region for much of the 1970s.

His finances in Switzerland are obscure. Diplomatic income had ended; he wrote, published, and relied on property or private means, but no reliable reconstruction of bank accounts, investments, tax status, or publishing revenue was found. Later descriptions of him as wealthy or aristocratic rest more on family background, properties, manner, and elite connections than on documented net worth.

### Intellectual transition

The dismissal and Swiss isolation intensified his return to Nazism. He read postwar occult-Nazi and revisionist literature and fused it with Hinduism, tantra, Jungian terminology, alchemy, Gnosticism, Nietzsche, Wagner, and polar mythology.

Important publications were:

- *El/Ella: Book of Magic Love* / *Elella, libro del amor mágico*.
- *Nietzsche y el eterno retorno*, incorporated in a 1974 trilogy.
- *El cordón dorado: hitlerismo esotérico* (1978).
- *Nietzsche y la danza de Siva* (written in 1977, published 1980).
- *Nos: libro de la resurrección* (1980).

This was not a simple return to conventional Third Reich doctrine. Serrano recast Hitler as a divine or transhistorical figure and the political defeat of 1945 as one episode in a cosmic war.

## Later Chilean Life, 1980–2009

Serrano returned permanently to Chile around 1980. He lived in Santiago, later maintained a substantial Valparaíso residence, and returned to Santiago in his last years.

### Relations with the Pinochet regime

After the 1973 coup, Serrano returned to Chile for visits and reportedly met members of the military junta. His own account says he gave them a presentation on international politics and advocated a “Prussian socialism” influenced by Spengler.

The meeting is a **reported fact/self-report** rather than evidence that Serrano shaped government policy. The Pinochet regime did not adopt his esoteric Hitlerism. Serrano later criticized its Catholic conservatism, neoliberal economics, concessions to foreign capital, and failure to create the racial-socialist order he wanted.

In 1982 he was reportedly invited to lecture at the Chilean Academy of National Security, where he expressed hope that the army might become a racial warrior order. This is reported in a contemporary Anti-Defamation League profile; the academy’s program or transcript was not located.

Serrano should therefore be distinguished from the regime’s official ideologues. He supported the overthrow of Allende and interacted with military figures, but he remained an independent, sometimes hostile fascist critic rather than a documented policymaker or security official.

### Walter Rauff funeral, 1984

On 14 May 1984 former SS officer **Walter Rauff** died in Santiago. West Germany had sought Rauff’s extradition for his role in the development and deployment of gas vans used to murder tens of thousands of people.

At Rauff’s burial on 15 May:

- Serrano appeared in a black leather coat.
- After the family had withdrawn, he and several others raised their arms in the Nazi salute.
- They shouted “Heil Hitler” and “Heil Walter Rauff.”
- Serrano told reporters that Rauff was a symbol and praised the Chilean government for resisting pressure for extradition.
- He denied that Rauff had created mobile gas chambers and called the Holocaust a Jewish fabrication.

These are **documented facts**, supported by photographs, UPI reporting, and contemporaneous Chilean press quotations.

There is no evidence that Serrano participated in Rauff’s wartime crimes, brought him to Chile, or belonged to a formal Rauff business structure. Their ideological association and Serrano’s public defense of him are documented; the precise depth and duration of their private friendship are less clear.

### Holocaust denial and organized activity

During the 1980s Serrano became one of Chile’s most conspicuous Holocaust deniers. He used major newspapers, book launches, pamphlets, and foreign revisionist networks to circulate claims that:

- No homicidal gas chambers existed.
- The six-million death toll was invented.
- Rauff had been falsely accused.
- Holocaust history served financial and territorial Jewish objectives.

These claims are contradicted by extensive German documentation, perpetrator records, physical and forensic evidence, demographic research, eyewitness testimony, and court findings.

Serrano cited Paul Rassinier, Robert Faurisson, Richard Harwood, Arthur Butz, David Irving, and the Institute for Historical Review. He helped publish or publicize the **Leuchter Report**, a discredited defense exhibit prepared for Ernst Zündel’s Canadian trial.

He was advertised as a speaker at an Institute for Historical Review convention in Los Angeles in 1986. He was also associated with or published through **CEDADE**, the Spanish neo-Nazi organization, and corresponded with international National Socialists including **Matt Koehl**.

A declassified or archived profile from the Reagan Presidential Library states that Chilean Jewish community leaders asked the justice minister to consider sanctions against racist agitation. The consulted material does not show that Serrano was indicted or convicted.

### Publications and activism

In 1984 he published *Adolf Hitler, el último avatara*, the second and most elaborate volume of his Hitler trilogy. At its Café Santos launch on 9 September, he publicly attacked the historical account of the Holocaust.

Other political publications included:

- *El nacionalsocialismo, única solución para los pueblos de América del Sur*.
- *La resurrección del héroe*.
- *El ciclo racial chileno*.
- His preface to *Los protocolos de los sabios de Sion y su aplicación en Chile*.
- *Contra la usura*, with material by Gottfried Feder and Serrano.
- *Estrategia sionista para apoderarse de la Patagonia argentina y chilena*.
- *El nuevo orden transnacional y la Patagonia*.
- His edition or promotion of the *Informe Leuchter*.

He organized annual Hitler-birthday observances and cultivated younger adherents. On 5 September 1993 he led a public rally in Santiago honoring Rudolf Hess and those killed after the 1938 Seguro Obrero coup attempt.

### Melimoyu project

During the later 1980s Serrano promoted a settlement or sacred refuge around **Melimoyu**, a volcanic mountain in Chilean Patagonia. He treated it as the antipode or southern counterpart of Mount Kailash.

The project combined:

- conservationist language;
- anti-globalization;
- national sovereignty;
- racial separatism;
- apocalyptic refuge;
- sacred geography.

Serrano’s own memoirs describe his attempt to colonize or secure Melimoyu as a failure. The available material does not establish a large functioning colony, durable commercial enterprise, or major investment structure. Claims that it was to become a secret Nazi redoubt belong partly to journalistic reconstruction and partly to Serrano’s own mythmaking.

### Valparaíso and final publications

From approximately 1990 Serrano lived principally in Valparaíso, comparing his return with Ezra Pound’s return to Venice. He published the final volume of his Hitler trilogy, *Manú, “por el hombre que vendrá”*, in 1991.

He self-published pamphlets on Patagonia, sovereignty, Chilean decline, globalization, and international politics. His four-volume *Memorias de él y yo* appeared from 1996 to 1999.

In 1998 he publicly defended **Paul Schäfer**, leader of Colonia Dignidad, in a letter titled “Yo defiendo a Paul Schafer.” Schäfer was later convicted of sexually abusing children, and evidence established extensive crimes and cooperation between Colonia Dignidad and Pinochet-era security organs. Serrano’s defense is documented; no evidence located here establishes that Serrano participated in the colony’s crimes or finances.

In 2001 he published *Se acabó Chile: la estrella de Israel reemplaza a la estrella de Chile*. He continued issuing letters, interviews, polemics, revised editions, and political-spiritual works until late life.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### From communism to National Socialism

Serrano’s development was not a conventional left-to-right progression based principally on economics. Personal death, generational solidarity, political martyrdom, and a search for transcendence repeatedly overrode doctrinal consistency.

His phases were approximately:

1. Literary rebellion and sympathy with the left.
2. Brief communist alignment after Héctor Barreto’s killing.
3. National Socialist commitment after the Seguro Obrero massacre.
4. Wartime racial and antisemitic journalism.
5. Postwar concentration on travel, India, psychology, and literary myth.
6. Post-1970 synthesis of Nazism, Hinduism, Gnosticism, alchemy, and polar mythology.
7. Public neo-Nazi activism, Holocaust denial, and apocalyptic Chilean nationalism.

### Esoteric Hitlerism

Serrano’s mature system held that:

- The material universe was the creation or prison of a lesser demiurgic power.
- Divine “hyperborean” beings existed outside or prior to ordinary material creation.
- A primordial Aryan race descended into the material world and became corrupted through racial mixing.
- Human history was a cosmic struggle between solar, Aryan, heroic forces and demiurgic, material, Jewish, Christian, Masonic, communist, capitalist, and globalizing forces.
- Hitler was an avatar or divine manifestation rather than merely a German politician.
- Germany’s defeat was outward and temporary.
- Initiates could recover a transcendent body through “magic love,” tantra, memory, will, and alchemical transformation.
- Chile, Patagonia, the Andes, Antarctica, Tibet, and Hyperborea formed a sacred geography.
- The swastika, Black Sun, Grail, kundalini serpent, vajra, and Chilean star were mutually interpretable signs.

This was a syncretic construction rather than a recognized form of Hinduism, Buddhism, Jungian psychology, or historical National Socialism.

### Christianity

Serrano retained Catholic family imagery and accepted aspects of an esoteric or “Aryan” Christ, but he attacked institutional Christianity and the Vatican. He contrasted what he called **Kristianismo**—a heroic, solar Christ principle—with conventional Christianity, which he associated with Judaism, egalitarianism, and the material world.

### Nietzsche, Wagner, Jung, and Hesse

Serrano appropriated:

- **Nietzsche’s** eternal return, Übermensch, will, and critique of Christianity.
- **Wagnerian** myth, music drama, heroic death, and Germanic symbolism.
- **Jung’s** archetypes, Self, individuation, alchemy, and synchronicity.
- **Hesse’s** initiatory fiction, inward journey, polarity, and Asian religious themes.

He did not merely summarize these figures; he placed them inside his own cosmology. Readers must distinguish their documented writings from Serrano’s interpretations.

### Ecology and anti-modernism

Serrano opposed industrial homogenization, urban mass society, liberal capitalism, communism, and neoliberal globalization. He praised wilderness, indigenous rootedness, sacred mountains, local ancestry, and “deep ecology.”

His occasional positive language about Mapuche culture did not eliminate his racial hierarchy. He could idealize an indigenous people as territorially rooted while condemning “mestizaje” elsewhere. His positions were selective, mythic, and often internally inconsistent.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Documented literary and intellectual relationships

- **Héctor Barreto:** close youthful friend; Barreto’s death became the founding trauma of Serrano’s generational memoir.
- **Guillermo Atías:** literary companion and communist activist in the 1930s.
- **Eduardo Anguita, Braulio Arenas, Teófilo Cid, Enrique Gómez Correa, Carlos Droguett, Juan Emar:** members or associates of the Generation of 1938 literary scene.
- **Vicente Huidobro:** uncle or close family elder; literary and political influence.
- **Hermann Hesse:** meetings, friendship, and correspondence from 1951 until Hesse’s death.
- **Carl Gustav Jung:** meetings and correspondence in the late 1950s and 1961.
- **Giovanni Papini:** met during European travel; Serrano published an account.
- **Ezra Pound:** Serrano wrote about and reportedly cultivated a relationship with him; the estate describes Pound among his international friendships.
- **Gonzalo Rojas:** literary acquaintance; Serrano participated in a 1998 writers’ congress honoring him.
- **Cristián Warnken:** later television interviewer; attended Serrano’s funeral.

- **Jawaharlal Nehru:** documented acquaintance and friendship during the Indian posting.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, Crimes, and Financial Questions

### Established political conduct

The following are documented:

- Membership or active association with Chilean National Socialism.
- Wartime antisemitic publication.
- Public veneration of Hitler.
- Holocaust denial.
- Public Nazi salute at Walter Rauff’s grave.
- Publication and distribution of antisemitic conspiracy texts.
- Organization of Nazi commemorations and a 1993 rally.
- International correspondence with neo-Nazis.
- Public defense of Paul Schäfer.
- Advocacy of racial hierarchy and separation.

### Criminal liability

No consulted source showed that Serrano was:

- convicted of a criminal offense;
- formally charged for political violence;
- imprisoned;
- found personally responsible for a murder, disappearance, torture, or terrorist attack;
- charged with fraud, corruption, money laundering, or tax offenses.

Jewish organizations urged governmental action against racist incitement. That lobbying and concern should not be converted into a nonexistent indictment or conviction.

### Intelligence connections

Because Serrano was a diplomat, met former Nazis, and moved through Cold War political networks, intelligence-related speculation is common. No consulted primary record establishes that he was an operative of the CIA, KGB, DINA, German intelligence, or another service.

Claims that he or Rauff directly advised the DINA require separate evidence. Rauff’s possible role in Chilean repression has been investigated by others; Serrano’s appearance at Rauff’s funeral does not by itself prove operational involvement.

### Violence

Serrano glorified political struggle, racial war, Hitler, SS symbolism, and heroic death. No evidence was found that he personally carried out a violent attack.

Possible violence by people influenced by him must be separately proved. Intellectual influence, ideological responsibility, conspiracy membership, and criminal participation are different evidentiary categories.

### Antisemitism and false documentary claims

Serrano republished or endorsed *The Protocols of the Elders of Zion*. The *Protocols* is a fabricated text derived partly from Maurice Joly’s nineteenth-century political satire and circulated as supposed evidence of a Jewish conspiracy. Serrano treated it as authentic or prophetically true despite its established fraudulent origin.

His Holocaust-denial claims were not unresolved scholarly questions. They contradicted surviving German records, material evidence, demographic studies, and judicial findings. Their historical importance lies in their role in Chilean and international denial networks, not in their evidentiary value as accounts of the Holocaust.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

## Hitler escaped to Antarctica

### Story

Serrano maintained that Hitler did not die in Berlin in April 1945. In his version, Hitler escaped through secret German technology, submarines, flying discs, or nonordinary means to an Antarctic or subterranean refuge.

### Appearance and circulation

Elements appeared in postwar South American Hitler-survival literature, including Ladislao Szabó’s *Hitler está vivo*, and were progressively incorporated into Serrano’s Antarctic writings and Hitler trilogy. Serrano’s official estate site continues to summarize the story.

### Evidence

No reliable evidence supports Hitler’s escape. Soviet autopsy evidence, dental identification, eyewitness testimony from the Berlin bunker, and later historical investigations establish his suicide in Berlin on 30 April 1945.

Claims concerning “120 missing submarines” are not supported by German naval loss and surrender records.

### Reputational effect

The story made Serrano influential in overlapping neo-Nazi, occult, UFO, conspiracy, and New Age subcultures. It also caused many literary readers to regard him as a fantasist.

## Hitler as an avatar

### Story

Hitler was not merely a political leader but an avatar of Vishnu, solar being, initiate, or manifestation of a transhistorical divine power.

### Origin

The doctrine was systematized in *El cordón dorado*, *Adolf Hitler, el último avatara*, and *Manú*.

### Evidence

This is a religious assertion, not a verifiable historical fact. It is not supported by mainstream Hindu traditions and was Serrano’s own racialized synthesis.

### Effect

It became the defining doctrine of esoteric Hitlerism and influenced later occult-fascist groups.

## Hyperborea and extraterrestrial Aryans

### Story

Divine Aryans originated outside ordinary terrestrial existence, descended at the pole or Hyperborea, and became trapped in material creation through racial mixing.

### Sources

Serrano combined classical Hyperborean lore, Theosophy, Nazi racial occultism, Hindu cyclical cosmology, Gnosticism, and modern UFO motifs.

### Evidence

No archaeological, genetic, or astronomical evidence supports an extraterrestrial Hyperborean master race.

### Effect

The story translated political racism into cosmic mythology and allowed political defeat to be interpreted as metaphysical victory.

## Secret Nazi flying discs and hidden technology

### Story

The Third Reich developed operational flying discs capable of reaching Antarctica or other dimensions.

### Evidence

Nazi Germany pursued experimental aircraft and weapons programs, but no reliable evidence establishes operational antigravity craft or vehicles capable of the feats Serrano described.

### Effect

The claim linked postwar Nazi-survival mythology to UFO culture.

## Serrano as first protector of the Dalai Lama

### Story

Admirers say Serrano was the first or decisive diplomat to assist the Dalai Lama after his escape from Tibet.

### Evidence

Serrano did visit the Dalai Lama in Mussoorie in 1959 and published an account. The broader claim is not established by Tibetan exile-government, Indian government, or Chilean diplomatic documentation consulted here.

### Effect

It enhanced Serrano’s image as a mediator between Chile, India, Tibet, and sacred polar geography.

## Antarctica’s warm oases and hidden inhabitants

### Story

Warm regions within Antarctica concealed ancient beings, German survivors, entrances to an inner world, or remnants of a primordial civilization.

### Evidence

Ice-free Antarctic oases and geothermal phenomena exist in ordinary geological senses. They do not substantiate inhabited secret civilizations or Nazi bases.

### Effect

Serrano transformed genuine exploration into initiatory legend and made Antarctica central to his literary identity.

## Melimoyu as antipode of Kailash

### Story

The Patagonian volcano Melimoyu was a southern sacred counterpart or antipode of Mount Kailash and a possible refuge for a future initiatic order.

### Evidence

This is Serrano’s symbolic geography. The mountains’ spiritual equivalence is not an empirical geographical finding.

### Effect

It motivated his failed settlement or refuge project and remains important among followers.

## The ancestral ring and “memory of the blood”

Serrano described an inherited sapphire ring and childhood experiences as signs of ancestral destiny. These stories appear in his memoirs and interviews. The object may have existed; its supernatural or initiatory significance is a matter of self-created mythology.

## Friendship inflation

Posthumous presentations sometimes list Hesse, Jung, Nehru, Indira Gandhi, the Dalai Lama, and Ezra Pound together as equally intimate friends. The underlying relationships varied:

- Hesse, Jung, Nehru, and Indira Gandhi are supported by substantial direct contact.
- The Dalai Lama meeting is documented, but lasting intimacy is less certain.
- Other claimed friendships require individual archival verification.
- Contact does not imply endorsement of Serrano’s later ideology.

## Health, Crises, and Death

A youthful accident caused a prolonged period of immobility and indirectly encouraged his writing.

The early deaths of both parents and the violent death of Héctor Barreto were persistent psychological and literary traumas. Serrano described solitude, doubled identity, visions, dreams, and encounters with a separate “I.” These should not be converted into retrospective psychiatric diagnoses.

No reliable evidence of alcoholism, narcotic addiction, long-term psychiatric hospitalization, or a diagnosed psychotic disorder was found.

In February 2009 he suffered a cerebral hemorrhage or stroke at his Santiago apartment near Cerro Santa Lucía. He died on **28 February 2009**.

His funeral took place on 3 March. Reporting records:

- A Catholic Mass.
- Attendance by relatives, writers, society figures, and National Socialist followers.
- Swastikas and Nazi salutes.
- “Heil Hitler” and “Heil Miguel Serrano.”
- Nordic and Mapuche invocations led by Vladimir Ferrada Grandón.
- Departure accompanied by the “Horst-Wessel-Lied.”
- Burial in the Cementerio General.

Among those reported present were Sabela Quintela, Sebastián Araya, Tamara Acosta, Cristián Warnken, Erwin Robertson, Carlos Cardoen, Julita Astaburuaga, Mary Rose Mc-Gill, Santiago Elordi, and Antonio Gil.

## Death, Posthumous Reputation, and Legacy

Serrano left several distinct reputations.

### Chilean literary reputation

He remains associated with the Generation of 1938, Antarctic writing, visionary travel literature, and the unusual literary fusion of Chilean landscape with Indian religion. *Ni por mar ni por tierra*, *La serpiente del paraíso*, *Las visitas de la reina de Saba*, and *El círculo hermético* continue to attract readers who do not necessarily share his politics.

### Diplomatic reputation

His Indian and European postings brought him into contact with Nehru, Indira Gandhi, the Dalai Lama, Hesse, Jung, and Eastern European governments. His work helped circulate Chilean writing and cultural diplomacy abroad. His later political notoriety largely eclipsed the routine institutional history of his diplomatic service.

### Neo-Nazi and occult reputation

Within the international radical right he became, with **Savitri Devi**, the principal founder of esoteric Hitlerism. His most durable ideological innovation was not ordinary electoral fascism but the conversion of Hitler into a cosmic, initiatory, and apocalyptic figure.

His work influenced or supplied imagery to:

- occult neo-Nazi groups;
- Black Sun and Hyperborean currents;
- racial-pagan organizations;
- UFO-Nazi conspiracy literature;
- some Satanist, New Age, Traditionalist, and countercultural fringes;
- later online radical-right subcultures.

Influence does not always imply faithful reading. Symbols and excerpts were frequently detached from his larger system.

### Critical reinterpretation

Scholars increasingly examine Serrano at the intersection of:

- Western esotericism;
- political religion;
- modern Gnosticism;
- postwar fascist networks;
- Holocaust denial;
- South American nationalism;
- environmental and indigenous appropriation;
- New Age circulation.

His documented friendships with Jung and Hesse create continuing controversy because later readers sometimes use those relationships to legitimate his politics. The chronology shows the problem: the relationships were real, but his fully developed esoteric Hitlerism was published mainly after both men were dead.

### Estate and continuing cult

Sabela Quintela and EB Libros preserved, republished, and systematized his corpus. The official site presents Serrano’s life as a unified initiatory mission. Followers commemorate him as a teacher; opponents remember the Rauff funeral and Holocaust denial.

His funeral itself became a final act of symbolic self-construction: Catholic family tradition, Chilean aristocratic society, Mapuche and Nordic invocation, swastikas, the “Horst-Wessel-Lied,” literary acquaintances, and neo-Nazi followers were assembled in one ceremony.

## Atlas Connections

Only connections supported by the consulted sources are included. Mere shared intellectual interests are excluded.

- **Augusto Pinochet Ugarte — reported fact/self-report.** After the September 1973 coup, Serrano reportedly met members of the junta, including Pinochet, and presented views on international politics and “Prussian socialism.” He later criticized the regime’s neoliberal model and its failure to adopt his racial-national program. No evidence shows that he became a policymaker or DINA operative.

- **Carl Gustav Jung — documented fact.** Serrano met Jung in Switzerland during his diplomatic years, corresponded with him between approximately 1957 and 1961, and reproduced letters in *El círculo hermético*. Jung contributed a text associated with *The Visits of the Queen of Sheba*. The record does not show Jung endorsing Serrano’s later esoteric Hitlerism.

- **Hermann Hesse — documented fact.** Serrano first met Hesse in Montagnola in June 1951, visited him repeatedly, and corresponded with him. He later lived in Casa Camuzzi, where Hesse had once resided, and published accounts of their friendship. Hesse died before Serrano’s mature Hitler trilogy appeared.

- **Savitri Devi — documented fact.** At least three letters from Savitri Devi to Serrano survive; one is dated New Delhi, 28 March 1980, and another 31 March. They addressed one another as comrades and discussed their independently developed forms of Hitler devotion. The surviving correspondence suggests parallel development rather than simple one-way borrowing.

- **Salvador Guillermo Allende Gossens — documented institutional connection.** Serrano’s diplomatic career ended after Allende’s election in 1970. Available accounts describe removal for political reasons. No significant personal relationship was established.

- **Friedrich Nietzsche — documented intellectual influence.** Serrano explicitly interpreted Nietzsche in *Nietzsche y el eterno retorno*, *Nietzsche y la danza de Siva*, lectures, and his later cosmology. This was textual influence, not a personal connection.

- **Wilhelm Richard Wagner — documented intellectual influence.** Serrano lectured on Nietzsche and Wagner and used Wagnerian myth, heroic aesthetics, and Germanic symbolism in his religious-political system.

- **Julius Evola — reported intellectual connection.** Serrano engaged with the Traditionalist and tantric milieu in which Evola was a major author. Later scholarship commonly compares or identifies Evolian influence on Serrano. The consulted sources did not establish a face-to-face meeting or surviving direct correspondence, so the connection should be treated as textual/intellectual rather than personal.

- **René Guénon — reported intellectual influence.** Guénonian themes concerning primordial tradition, sacred geography, cyclical decline, and the King of the World appear in the milieu from which Serrano drew. Direct personal contact was impossible and is not claimed; precise dependence varies by work.

- **Jiddu Krishnamurti — documented reported meeting.** Serrano published “Mi encuentro con Krishnamurti” in *La Nación* on 24 May 1953. No lasting collaboration was established.

- **Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi — documented intellectual and ceremonial connection, not a personal meeting.** Serrano published a 1956 diplomatic tribute, “Message of Love and Peace: Chilean Diplomat’s Tribute to Gandhiji,” and knew Gandhi’s son Devadas. Gandhi had died before Serrano’s Indian posting.

- **Adolf Hitler — ideological and mythic connection only.** Hitler is not separately listed in the supplied roster, although he is central to Serrano’s life. Serrano never met him. Claims of mystical contact, survival, or avataric presence are religious myths rather than documented relationships.

No supported direct connection was found with Aleksandr Dugin, Benito Mussolini, René Guénon beyond textual influence, Salvador Allende beyond the institutional dismissal, or other superficially relevant roster members. Absence of evidence has not been converted into a connection.

## Chronology

- **1917, 10 September:** Born at Santo Domingo 661, Santiago.
- **c. 1922–1925:** Mother and father die; raised by grandmother Fresia Manterola.
- **1927–1935:** Attends several Santiago secondary schools; does not proceed to university.
- **Early 1930s:** Begins writing; joins the literary circle around Héctor Barreto and Guillermo Atías.
- **1932 onward:** Writes for left-wing publications.
- **1936:** Héctor Barreto is killed; Serrano briefly aligns with communism.
- **1938:** Edits *Antología del verdadero cuento en Chile*.
- **1938, 5 September:** Seguro Obrero massacre; turns decisively toward Chilean National Socialism.
- **1939:** Publishes *Un discurso de América del Sur*; writes for *Trabajo*.
- **1941:** Publishes *La época más oscura*; launches *La Nueva Edad*.
- **1941–1942:** Directs at least thirty-five issues of *La Nueva Edad* and publishes National Socialist and antisemitic articles.
- **1943–1946:** Reportedly placed on wartime blacklists.
- **1947–1948:** Participates in the Second Chilean Antarctic Expedition.
- **1948:** Publishes *La Antártica y otros mitos*.
- **1950:** Publishes *Ni por mar ni por tierra*.
- **1951, June:** Meets Hermann Hesse at Montagnola.
- **1951:** Meets Irene Klatt Getta; travels in postwar Europe.
- **1953:** Begins diplomatic posting in India; publishes account of meeting Krishnamurti.
- **1953–1962:** Serves in India; travels through South Asia and the Himalayas; develops friendships with Nehru and Indira Gandhi.
- **1957:** Publishes *Quién llama en los hielos*.
- **1957–1961:** Corresponds with C. G. Jung.
- **1959:** Meets the Dalai Lama in Mussoorie.
- **1960:** Publishes *Las visitas de la reina de Saba* and *Los misterios*.
- **1961:** Last meetings with Jung and Hesse.
- **1962:** Leaves India; becomes ambassador in Yugoslavia.
- **1962:** Hermann Hesse dies.
- **1963:** Publishes *La serpiente del paraíso*.
- **1964–1966:** Eastern European diplomatic appointments; accredited to Romania and Bulgaria.
- **1964/1967–1970:** Serves as ambassador in Austria.
- **1968:** Publishes major editions of *El círculo hermético*.
- **1969:** Publishes *La flor inexistente*.
- **1970–1971:** Removed from diplomatic service following Allende’s election.
- **1971, 9 November:** Establishes residence in Casa Camuzzi, Montagnola.
- **1972:** Publishes *El/Ella* in English-language editions.
- **1973–1974:** Returns to Chile after the coup for meetings and publications; maintains Swiss residence.
- **1978:** Publishes *El cordón dorado*, inaugurating the esoteric-Hitlerist trilogy.
- **1980:** Publishes *Nos* and *Nietzsche y la danza de Siva*; returns permanently to Chile around this time.
- **1980, March:** Corresponds with Savitri Devi.
- **1982:** Reportedly lectures at the Chilean Academy of National Security.
- **1984, May:** Gives Nazi salute at Walter Rauff’s funeral and publicly denies the Holocaust.
- **1984, September:** Launches *Adolf Hitler, el último avatara* at Café Santos.
- **1984:** Visits Karl Wilhelm Krause and meets Arno Breker in Germany.
- **1985–1989:** Develops Melimoyu refuge project; publishes racial, antisemitic, and National Socialist pamphlets.
- **1986:** Advertised as a speaker at the Institute for Historical Review convention.
- **Late 1980s:** Promotes a Chilean edition of the *Leuchter Report*.
- **c. 1989:** First meets Sabela Quintela in Spain.
- **1990:** Establishes residence in Valparaíso.
- **1991:** Publishes *Manú* and *El nuevo orden transnacional y la Patagonia*.
- **1993, 5 September:** Leads Santiago rally honoring Rudolf Hess and the Seguro Obrero dead.
- **1994:** Sabela Quintela moves to Chile.
- **1996–1999:** Publishes four volumes of *Memorias de él y yo*.
- **1998:** Participates in congress honoring Gonzalo Rojas; publicly defends Paul Schäfer.
- **2000:** Marries Sabela Quintela.
- **2001:** Publishes *Se acabó Chile*.
- **2004:** Creates Einherier Books as an official distribution vehicle.
- **2008:** Receives a career-achievement award.
- **2009, 28 February:** Dies in Santiago after a cerebral hemorrhage.
- **2009, 3 March:** Funeral and burial at Cementerio General de Santiago.
- **2010 onward:** Einherier Books becomes EB Libros; estate continues archival publication and reissues.
- **2017:** Gonzalo León publishes the fictionalized novel *Serrano*.
- **Posthumously:** Serrano remains a subject of literary, historical, esoteric, and radical-right scholarship.
