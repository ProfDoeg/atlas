## Works by Paul Klee

### Pictorial obra

- **1883–1940:** Paul Klee’s catalogue-numbered pictorial oeuvre. This obra comprises approximately 9,600 drawings, prints, watercolours, and paintings, individually named and described in the nine-volume *Catalogue raisonné Paul Klee*: vol. 1, 1883–1912; vol. 2, 1913–1918; vol. 3, 1919–1922; vol. 4, 1923–1926; vol. 5, 1927–1930; vol. 6, 1931–1933; vol. 7, 1934–1938; vol. 8, 1939; and vol. 9, 1940. It cannot honestly be reduced to a selection of celebrated pictures and still be called “everything”; the individual catalogue entries, including untitled works, collectively constitute the title-level list. The catalogue is not absolutely closed: newly discovered or identified works continue to appear as addenda. ([Catalogue record and volume ranges](https://opac.kbr.be/BIBFEDERALE/detailstatic.aspx?RSC_BASE=SYRACUSE&RSC_DOCID=16310391&TITLE=catalogue-raisonne-paul-klee&_lg=fr-BE); [catalogue scope](https://search.worldcat.org/nl/title/paul-klee-catalogue-raisonne/oclc/41150970); [Zentrum Paul Klee research archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research))

- **1923:** *Burg 2 (Castle 2)*. Watercolour and pencil on paper, bordered in gouache and pen and mounted on cardboard; catalogued as 1923, 107 and CR 3201. Rediscovered in May 2017 and published as an addendum because it was absent from the printed nine-volume catalogue, making the nominal completeness of the pictorial obra demonstrably provisional. ([Addenda to the Catalogue raisonné](https://www.zwitscher-maschine.org/addenda-catalogue-raisonne-paul-klee))

- **c. 1940:** *Untitled (Child and Kite) / Untitled (Flower and Snake)*. Two fully worked compositions on opposite faces of one glue-paint-on-cardboard object; Klee neither entered them in his own catalogue nor designated a principal face. Both titles, as well as the decision to treat *Child and Kite* as the recto, were supplied posthumously by Felix Klee, so this object resists both a stable title and a simple count of “one work.” ([Zentrum Paul Klee object record](https://guide.zpk.org/fr/artwork/12406))

- **1883–1940:** Uncatalogued and newly recognized recto-verso compositions. Research has identified drawings, watercolours, or paintings on the reverse of roughly 600 of the 9,600 catalogue entries; some function as working stages, while others may be autonomous manifestations sharing one support. Their status shows that an inventory of physical objects and an inventory of compositions are not identical. ([Zentrum Paul Klee, “Klee’s versos”](https://www.zpk.org/en/ausstellung/klees-versos))

### Illustrated-book projects

- **1911–1912; published 1920:** *Candide, or Optimism* (*Kandide oder die beste Welt*). A sequence of twenty-six drawings made as illustrations to Voltaire’s narrative and subsequently issued with the German text; the obra is the pictorial cycle, not authorship of Voltaire’s book. The drawings fall within the catalogue raisonné’s pictorial corpus.

- **1920:** *Potsdamer Platz oder die Nächte des neuen Messias*. Ten lithographs accompanying Kurt Corinth’s text. This is a collaborative book manifestation: Corinth supplied the literary work, while Klee made the lithographic cycle.

- **1923:** *The Apprentices at Sais* (*Die Lehrlinge zu Sais*). A cycle of fifty-one drawings for Novalis’s text, published posthumously in book form in 1949; the later volume is an edition of Klee’s illustrations rather than a prose work written by him.

### Hand puppets and puppet theatre

- **1916–1925:** Approximately fifty hand puppets made for Felix Klee. This is a domestic theatrical obra as well as a group of sculptural objects: thirty puppets survive at the Zentrum Paul Klee, while approximately twenty are lost. Because several figures have descriptive or posthumous names and the lost group is not completely recoverable, a definitive one-bullet-per-puppet inventory cannot be reconstructed solely from the surviving ensemble. ([Zentrum Paul Klee exhibition guide](https://archive.zpk.org/admin/data/hosts/zpk/files/page_editorial_link/file_en/185/zpk_af_kosmos_klee_e_web.pdf?lm=1539353902); [Zentrum Paul Klee, *Hand Puppets* catalogue](https://hatjecantz.de/products/15527-paul-klee))

- **1916:** *Untitled (Mr Death)*. A hand puppet rather than a sculpture intended for autonomous exhibition; it belongs to the first group made for Felix.

- **1919:** *Untitled (Crowned Poet)*. A hand puppet whose surviving name describes its character rather than establishing a title demonstrably fixed by Klee.

- **1921:** *Untitled (Barber of Baghdad)*. A hand puppet combining modeled and repurposed materials; its theatrical function makes it both character and object.

- **1923:** *Untitled (Electrical Spook)*. A hand puppet incorporating a ceramic electrical socket into its head, an example of Klee converting a household object into a theatrical character.

- **1920s:** *Matchbox Spirit*. A hand puppet incorporating a matchbox; it belongs to a work group that its scholarly catalogue explicitly describes as not originally claiming the status of “great art.” ([Zentrum Paul Klee, *Hand Puppets* catalogue](https://hatjecantz.de/products/15527-paul-klee))

- **From 1916:** Felix Klee’s puppet theatre. Klee constructed a theatre in which his son could perform with the puppets; it is an enabling theatrical apparatus and collaborative project, not merely another puppet. ([Zentrum Paul Klee exhibition guide](https://archive.zpk.org/admin/data/hosts/zpk/files/page_editorial_link/file_en/185/zpk_af_kosmos_klee_e_web.pdf?lm=1539353902))

### Diaries, poems, and correspondence

- **1898–1918:** *Diaries*. Four private diaries combining autobiography, travel record, reflections on music and literature, and notes toward an artistic theory. The familiar title and organization derive from posthumous editions by Felix Klee and later textual scholarship; the manuscripts are Klee’s obra, while the published books are editorial manifestations. ([University of California Press, *The Diaries of Paul Klee, 1898–1918*](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/the-diaries-of-paul-klee-1898-1918/paper); [Zentrum Paul Klee archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research))

- **Various dates; collected posthumously from 1946:** *Poems* (*Gedichte*). A corpus of poems rather than one book conceived and published by Klee; the collected volumes are posthumous editorial manifestations, so their contents should not be mistaken for a single lifetime obra.

- **Various dates:** Family and professional correspondence. The surviving letters to family, friends, artists, dealers, and museums form an archival corpus rather than a deliberately bounded literary work. Its limits are determined by survival and archival collection, not by a project Klee declared complete. ([Zentrum Paul Klee archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research))

### Art-theoretical texts and manifestos

- **1912:** *The Exhibition of the Moderner Bund at the Kunsthaus Zürich*. An exhibition review: criticism rather than manifesto or protocol.

- **1913:** *Robert Delaunay: On Light*. Klee’s German translation of Delaunay’s text, published in *Der Sturm*. This is a translational manifestation, not an independently authored theory by Klee.

- **1918; published 1920:** *Creative Credo* (*Schöpferische Konfession*). An art-theoretical manifesto asserting a generative conception of image-making; composed during 1918 and first published in Kasimir Edschmid’s anthology in 1920. ([Bibliography of Klee’s writings](https://www.uni-weimar.de/uploads/tx_showcase/Immatrkulationsliste_Dessau_und_Berlin_und_Publikationsregister_Bauhausscans.pdf); [study of the theoretical texts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0039368108000654))

- **1920:** *Colour as Science* (*Die Farbe als Wissenschaft*). A short polemical contribution concerning colour theory, published in a special colour issue of *Das Werk*; it is an intervention in a debate rather than a systematic treatise. ([Bibliographical record](https://www.literapedia-bern.ch/images/d/d2/Literapedia_Bern_A-Z_20140219.pdf))

- **1921:** *Reply to a Survey of Artists: On the Value of Criticism*. A response to a questionnaire published in *Der Ararat*; its form is occasioned critical testimony, not a freestanding book. ([Bauhaus publication register](https://www.uni-weimar.de/uploads/tx_showcase/Immatrkulationsliste_Dessau_und_Berlin_und_Publikationsregister_Bauhausscans.pdf))

- **1923:** *Ways of Studying Nature* (*Wege des Naturstudiums*). A compact theoretical text published in *Staatliches Bauhaus in Weimar 1919–1923*; it describes an artistic relation to natural process rather than serving simply as a practical protocol. ([Bauhaus publication register](https://www.uni-weimar.de/uploads/tx_showcase/Immatrkulationsliste_Dessau_und_Berlin_und_Publikationsregister_Bauhausscans.pdf); [study of the theoretical texts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0039368108000654))

- **1924; published 1945:** *On Modern Art* (*Über die moderne Kunst*). The manuscript of Klee’s only public lecture, delivered at the Kunstverein Jena on 26 January 1924 and published only after his death. It is simultaneously an event, a lecture-text, and a posthumous publication; the 1945 book is not evidence that Klee made a new work in that year. ([Zentrum Paul Klee chronology](https://archive.zpk.org/admin/data/hosts/zpk/files/page_editorial_paragraph_file/file_en/329/press-release-klee-kandinsky.pdf?lm=1434630845); [bibliographical record](https://www.literapedia-bern.ch/images/d/d2/Literapedia_Bern_A-Z_20140219.pdf))

- **1925:** *Pedagogical Sketchbook* (*Pädagogisches Skizzenbuch*). Bauhaus Book 2, a deliberately selected and organized condensation of Klee’s much larger teaching archive. It is a book and a pedagogical protocol, but it is not the entirety of his theory of form. ([Metropolitan Museum of Art record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/818890); [Zentrum Paul Klee archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research))

- **1926:** *Wassily Kandinsky*. A commemorative or catalogue essay written for Kandinsky’s sixtieth-birthday exhibition; it is occasional criticism, not a monograph.

- **1927:** *Emil Nolde*. A contribution to a publication honoring Nolde on his sixtieth birthday; again, an occasional essay rather than a book-length project.

- **1928:** *Exact Experiments in the Realm of Art* (*exakte versuche im bereich der kunst*). A theoretical article published in the Bauhaus journal; its lower-case presentation belongs to the publication. It argues for exact investigation in art without reducing creation to a mechanically executable protocol. ([Study of the theoretical texts](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0039368108000654); [Bauhaus publication register](https://www.uni-weimar.de/uploads/tx_showcase/Immatrkulationsliste_Dessau_und_Berlin_und_Publikationsregister_Bauhausscans.pdf))

### Pedagogical projects and working documents

- **1921–1922:** *Contributions to a Pictorial Theory of Form* (*Beiträge zur bildnerischen Formlehre*). A handwritten lecture book of approximately 190 pages developed for Klee’s Bauhaus teaching. It is simultaneously manuscript, diagrammatic object, teaching protocol, and evolving project rather than a conventional authored book. ([Zentrum Paul Klee research archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research))

- **1921–1931:** *Pictorial Creation* (*Bildnerische Gestaltungslehre*). Approximately 3,900 pages of lecture notes, diagrams, revisions, and teaching preparations produced and repeatedly reorganized during Klee’s Bauhaus years; in 1928 he arranged much of the material into twenty-four chapters. This unfinished and internally changing pedagogical archive resists being called one work, yet its organization makes it more than an accidental accumulation of papers. ([Zentrum Paul Klee research archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research); [Zentrum Paul Klee Bauhaus exhibition guide](https://archive.zpk.org/admin/data/hosts/zpk/files/page_editorial_paragraph_file/file_en/115/af_meisterklee_heft_92s_72dpi.pdf?lm=1343313424))

- **1956 and later:** *The Thinking Eye* (*Das bildnerische Denken*) and *The Nature of Nature / Infinite Natural History* (*Unendliche Naturgeschichte*). These are Jürg Spiller’s posthumously edited selections from Klee’s pedagogical manuscripts. They are indispensable manifestations of the archive but should not be classified without qualification as books made and completed by Klee. ([Zentrum Paul Klee research archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research))

### Cataloguing as an œuvre-project

- **1911–1940:** Klee’s handwritten oeuvre catalogue. A continuously maintained record in which Klee numbered, dated, titled, described, and sometimes technically annotated his visual production. It is both an archival document and a lifelong project that actively shaped what counted as a work; omissions such as the two-sided compositions from around 1940 show that it is authoritative without being exhaustive. ([Zentrum Paul Klee research archive](https://www.zpk.org/en/research); [Addenda to the Catalogue raisonné](https://www.zwitscher-maschine.org/addenda-catalogue-raisonne-paul-klee); [Zentrum Paul Klee two-sided object record](https://guide.zpk.org/fr/artwork/12406))
