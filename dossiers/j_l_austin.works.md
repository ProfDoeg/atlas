## Works by John Langshaw Austin

### Philosophical papers and surviving manuscript projects

- **1930s–1940s / 1979:** *The Line and the Cave in Plato’s Republic*. A philosophical project on Plato surviving in lecture notes rather than a publication-ready text; J. O. Urmson reconstructed it posthumously, so the printed essay is partly an editorial manifestation and resists treatment as a wholly finished Austin work. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1939 / 1967:** *Agathon and Eudaimonia in the Ethics of Aristotle*. A manuscript study of Aristotle written in 1939 but first published posthumously in 1967; its delayed manifestation should not be mistaken for its date of composition. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1939:** *Are There A Priori Concepts?* A symposium paper published in the supplementary volume of the *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society*. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **circa 1940 / 1961:** *The Meaning of a Word*. A manuscript philosophical paper, not published by Austin; its first public manifestation was in the posthumous *Philosophical Papers*. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1946:** *Other Minds*. Austin’s contribution to an Aristotelian Society symposium, subsequently treated as a stand-alone paper; its original identity is an intervention within a multi-speaker philosophical event. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1950:** *Truth*. A symposium paper presenting Austin’s account of truth and correspondence, originally published alongside contributions by P. F. Strawson and D. R. Cousin. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1953:** *How to Talk—Some Simple Ways*. A journal paper developing a classificatory method for examining different uses of language. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **circa 1954 / 1961:** *Unfair to Facts*. An unpublished manuscript project on facts, perception, and truth, issued only after Austin’s death; it overlaps material used in the lectures behind *Sense and Sensibilia*. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1956:** *Ifs and Cans*. A British Academy lecture and published paper concerning conditional constructions, ability, and freedom. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1957:** *A Plea for Excuses*. Austin’s presidential address to the Aristotelian Society: simultaneously a performed address, a methodological proposal, and a published philosophical paper about the language of action and responsibility. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1958:** *Pretending*. A symposium paper distinguishing several activities ordinarily grouped under “pretending”; its original form was part of a philosophical exchange rather than an autonomous book chapter. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525/chapter-abstract/162271468)

- **1958 / 1966:** *Three Ways of Spilling Ink*. A lecture-derived philosophical project about action and responsibility, published posthumously under L. W. Forguson’s editorship; the surviving printed text is therefore an editorial reconstruction, not an author-supervised final paper. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

### Lectures, broadcasts, and posthumous lecture-books

- **1947–1959 / 1962:** *Sense and Sensibilia*. A recurring Oxford lecture project on perception, delivered in changing forms and last given in 1959; Geoffrey J. Warnock constructed the posthumous book from Austin’s heterogeneous manuscript notes. It resists being called a finished book by Austin because Warnock reported that scarcely any sentence in the resulting continuous text was a direct transcription of a complete Austin manuscript. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [bibliographical description quoting Warnock’s foreword](https://www.quaritch.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/PFStrawson.pdf)

- **1955 / 1962:** *How to Do Things with Words: The William James Lectures Delivered at Harvard University in 1955*. A twelve-lecture project performed at Harvard and published after Austin’s death from his lecture materials under J. O. Urmson’s editorship. The book is a posthumous manifestation of the lectures, not a monograph completed and published by Austin. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic bibliography](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525), [publication history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_to_Do_Things_with_Words)

- **1956 / 1961:** *Performative Utterances*. An unscripted BBC Third Programme radio talk; the surviving paper is a corrected transcript, so the original obra was spoken and broadcast, while the familiar text is its documentary manifestation. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525)

- **1958 / 1962:** *Performatif-Constatif*. A conference paper presented at the Anglo-French Royaumont colloquium and published posthumously in French with the ensuing discussion. No original English text is known; G. J. Warnock’s 1963 *Performative-Constative* is a back-translation, and the published discussion preserves Austin’s replies as well as the paper itself. [Oxford Academic foreword](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525/chapter-abstract/162267760), [Royaumont publication record](https://www.royaumont.com/projet-culturel/les-sciences-humaines/publications/), [study of the textual history](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/298257/1/Badir_Performatif.pdf)

### Reviews and problem reports

- **1950:** *Intelligent Behaviour: A Critical Review of The Concept of Mind*. A substantial review of Gilbert Ryle’s book, first published in the *Times Literary Supplement*; it is a critical intervention rather than a free-standing treatise. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/), [Cambridge bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/interpreting-j-l-austin/bibliography/E10411C7D478467EC03FA02583870946)

- **1952:** *Critical Notice: Aristotle’s Syllogistic, from the Standpoint of Modern Formal Logic, by Jan Łukasiewicz*. An extended critical notice in *Mind*, made in response to another scholar’s book and therefore neither an edition nor an independent monograph. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://seop.illc.uva.nl/entries////////austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/LXI.243.395)

- **1952:** *Report on Analysis “Problem” No. 1: “What Sort of ‘If’ Is the ‘If’ in ‘I Can If I Choose’?”* A two-page adjudicatory report on a problem set by the journal *Analysis*; it is a commissioned judgment on submitted solutions rather than a conventional philosophical paper. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://seop.illc.uva.nl/entries////////austin-jl/), [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/analysis/article-pdf/12/6/125/214360/12-6-125a.pdf)

- **1958:** *Report on Analysis “Problem” No. 12: “‘All Swans Are White or Black.’ Does This Refer to Possible Swans on Canals on Mars?”* Another brief problem report for *Analysis*, functioning as an assessment of proposed answers rather than a self-contained treatise. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/)

### Translation and editorial works

- **1949:** *Lectures on the Philosophy of Leibniz*. Austin edited H. W. B. Joseph’s lectures for posthumous publication. The intellectual lectures are Joseph’s; Austin’s obra here is the scholarly edition, whose authorship must not be reassigned to him. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/)

- **1950:** *The Foundations of Arithmetic: A Logico-Mathematical Enquiry into the Concept of Number*. Austin’s English translation of Gottlob Frege’s *Die Grundlagen der Arithmetik*, originally produced so the work could be used for an Oxford examination. It is a made English text but not an Austin-authored philosophical book: the underlying work and argument are Frege’s. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/)

### Collected manifestation

- **1961; expanded 1970 and 1979:** *Philosophical Papers*. A posthumous collected-works manifestation edited by J. O. Urmson and G. J. Warnock, not a new Austin-authored obra. Its third edition contains thirteen papers and was designed to gather Austin’s published philosophical articles together with reconstructed or previously unpublished material; it excludes *Performatif-Constatif* because the editors considered it too close to *Performative Utterances*. [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525), [editorial foreword](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525/chapter-abstract/162267760)

### Sources

- Guy Longworth, [“John Langshaw Austin,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2016/entries/austin-jl/). Consulted especially for the chronological primary bibliography and the status of manuscripts, broadcasts, books, translation, and edition.

- J. L. Austin, J. O. Urmson, and G. J. Warnock, eds., [*Philosophical Papers*, 3rd ed.](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525), Oxford University Press, 1979. Consulted as the standard collected-works edition and for its table of contents.

- J. O. Urmson and G. J. Warnock, [foreword to *Philosophical Papers*](https://academic.oup.com/book/12525/chapter-abstract/162267760). Consulted for the collection’s editorial history and the status of *Performatif-Constatif*.

- Cambridge University Press, [bibliography to *Interpreting J. L. Austin*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/interpreting-j-l-austin/bibliography/E10411C7D478467EC03FA02583870946). Used to check publication histories of the review and posthumous texts.

- Fondation Royaumont, [“Les publications Royaumont”](https://www.royaumont.com/projet-culturel/les-sciences-humaines/publications/). Consulted to verify the 1958 colloquium and its 1962 proceedings.

- Sémir Badir, [“Performatif et énonciation”](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/298257/1/Badir_Performatif.pdf). Consulted for the disputed linguistic and textual status of *Performatif-Constatif* and the absence of a known English original.

- University of California, Irvine Libraries, Eddie Yeghiayan, [*John L. Austin: A Bibliography*](https://www.lib.uci.edu/library/publications/philosophy/austin/index.html). Consulted as a dedicated scholarly bibliography and used to check the boundary between Austin’s writings and writings about him.
