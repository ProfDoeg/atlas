# Rudolf Ritsema: Research Dossier

**Subject:** Rudolf Ritsema (1918–2006), Dutch-born editor, bookseller, Eranos administrator, and lifelong student and translator of the *I Ching*  
**Prepared:** 31 August 2026  
**Scope:** Documentary reconstruction from publicly accessible biographical accounts, institutional histories and catalogues, archival guides, scholarly studies, contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous testimony, bibliographic databases, and Ritsema’s publications.  
**Source caution:** Ritsema left no published autobiography and has never received a full-length biography. The most detailed continuous life account available online derives from Shantena Augusto Sabbadini, his late collaborator, and is therefore both unusually valuable and personally close to its subject. The institutional crisis of 1988–1990 generated sharply opposed narratives. This dossier distinguishes official records, participants’ testimony, later interpretation, and unsupported assertions.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full name:** Rudolf Ritsema.
- **Variant spellings encountered:** Rudolph Ritsema occurs occasionally in secondary or bookselling records, but Rudolf is the form used in his publications and by the Eranos Foundation. The surname is sometimes garbled in commercial databases. No pseudonym is documented.
- **Birth:** 3 October 1918, Velp, municipality of Rheden, Gelderland, Netherlands.
- **Death:** 8 May 2006, aged 87. The publicly accessible sources consulted do not securely establish the place or medical cause of death. The date was the sixty-first anniversary of his marriage.
- **Nationality:** Dutch by birth. Long resident in Switzerland; no source consulted establishes naturalization.
- **Principal residences and institutional locations:** Velp, Netherlands; Heppenheim in the Odenwald, Germany; the Geheeb school community in Switzerland; Geneva; Leiden, Netherlands; and, for the greater part of his mature life, the Eranos estate at Moscia-Ascona on Lake Maggiore, Canton Ticino, Switzerland.
- **Occupations and functions:** Student of biology; antiquarian bookseller and head of the Oriental department in the antiquarian division of E. J. Brill, Leiden; conference organizer; editor of *Eranos-Jahrbuch*; sole executive or president/director of the Eranos Foundation; independent student of classical Chinese; *I Ching* translator, commentator, and workshop leader.
- **Common descriptions in secondary literature:** “Sinologist,” “orientalist,” “I Ching scholar,” “translator,” “psychologist,” and director of an East–West study center. These labels require qualification. He studied classical Chinese for the *I Ching* project but no completed university degree or academic appointment in Sinology is documented. He underwent Jungian analysis and used the oracle as a psychological instrument but was not documented as a licensed psychologist or analyst.
- **Languages:** Dutch by origin; educated in German- and French-speaking environments; published or supervised work in English, German, Italian, and French; studied classical Chinese. The precise level of his spoken and written command of each language is not documented.
- **Marriage:** Catherine Gris (1917–2007), also styled Catherine or Katherine Ritsema; married 8 May 1945. She was a Geneva music teacher, pianist, singer (one scholarly source calls her an opera singer), photographer, concert performer, and a central—often insufficiently credited—participant in the Eranos household and organization.
- **Children:** No reliable source consulted identifies children of Rudolf and Catherine Ritsema. This is not proof that they had none; it is an unresolved point.
- **Known honors or academic degrees:** None securely documented beyond completion of a baccalaureate. No honorary degree, state decoration, university chair, patent, or formal professional qualification was found.

## Evidentiary Profile and Unresolved Questions

The surviving public record is institution-heavy and private-life-light. It documents Ritsema’s work at Eranos and on the *I Ching* far better than his family life, income, health after the polio attack, or circumstances of death.

The following remain unestablished in the consulted sources:

- his full birth-registration data and any middle names;
- names and life histories of siblings;
- the exact dates of his moves among Germany, Switzerland, the Netherlands, and Ascona;
- the exact course, length, and examinations of his University of Geneva studies;
- the dates and terms of his employment at Brill;
- whether he ever acquired Swiss citizenship;
- whether he and Catherine had children;
- his personal assets, salary, royalties, ownership interests, or estate;
- the exact date on which he formally became sole executive/president of the Eranos Foundation and the exact date on which he formally relinquished it;
- his place and medical cause of death.

These silences are material. Later capsule biographies often turn a gradual transfer of practical work into a simple succession in 1962, although the legal and organizational record shows Adolf Portmann as the first president of the 1961 foundation and Ritsema as his assistant and eventual successor.

## Family and Ancestry

### Father: Ipo Christiaan Ritsema

Ritsema’s father is named in the detailed biographical tradition as **Ipo Christiaan Ritsema**. A Dutch civil-registration index records a man of that exact name born in Haarlem on 20 August 1879 to Anne Reinier Ritsema and Maria Gerardina Vermande. The identity is highly plausible but cannot be made conclusive without the subject’s birth or marriage record. Genealogical databases describe an Ipo Christiaan Ritsema of the same generation as an apothecary in 1941. Bibliographic records attribute to Ipo Christiaan Ritsema an *Index Phytochemicus*, a systematic survey of plant substances, consistent with the family account that Rudolf’s father was trained in chemistry and remained deeply interested in biology.

Sabbadini’s account presents the paternal family as Frisian in origin and Ipo Christiaan as rational, scientific, and temperamentally different from his religiously inclined wife. It is a characterization from family memory, not an independent psychological assessment.

### Mother: Frances Johanna van den Berg

Ritsema’s mother is identified as **Frances Johanna van den Berg**. The collaborator-derived biography describes her ancestry as partly Frisian, partly British, and Huguenot. No primary genealogical documents confirming that descent were located in this research. Rudolf reportedly associated his mother and maternal grandmother with a vivid religious assurance: a feeling of being guided by God or “carried in God’s arms.” That recollection became part of the later explanation of his combined religious and rational inheritance.

### Siblings and extended family

No siblings are identified in the reliable accessible literature. Because Ritsema belonged to a widespread Dutch family name and because several unrelated Ritsemas were prominent, surname resemblance is not sufficient evidence of kinship.

### Catherine Gris / Catherine Ritsema

Catherine Gris was born in 1917 and died in 2007, according to scholarly secondary literature. She came from Geneva, worked there as a young music teacher, and met Rudolf at the Geheeb school community in Switzerland near the end of his schooling. Later sources variously call her pianist, singer, opera singer, and photographer. These descriptions are compatible rather than necessarily contradictory: she taught music, performed piano recitals at Eranos, sang, and took photographs preserved or reproduced by the Foundation.

She and Rudolf married on 8 May 1945. Catherine’s work was structural to Eranos. She performed concerts, hosted participants, helped manage a demanding annual household, assisted Rudolf’s administrative work, and—according to Hans Thomas Hakl—exerted strong influence on invitations and institutional decisions. Her unfinished manuscript, *L’Œuvre d’Eranos et Vie d’Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn*, is an important unpublished source for Eranos history. Hakl consulted it; later researchers have sometimes been unable to locate or consult it.

Accounts of the 1988 conference describe a piano concert by Catherine in the Ritsemas’ large living room immediately after Rudolf announced that the traditional conferences would end. The concert’s placement became part of hostile recollections of the evening: critics believed the social schedule was used to prevent debate. This is interpretation by opponents, not a proven intention on Catherine’s part.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1918–1933

Rudolf Ritsema was born four weeks before the armistice ending the First World War. He grew up in the Netherlands in a household later depicted as joining two temperaments: his father’s scientific rationalism and his mother’s religious trust.

At approximately age eleven, around 1929–1930, he entered the **Odenwaldschule** at Heppenheim, Germany. The school, founded in 1910 by Paul and Edith Geheeb, was a prominent experiment in progressive education. Its ideal was a self-governing community across generations rather than a conventional authoritarian boarding school. Ritsema later regarded Paul and Edith Geheeb as formative figures.

Later revelations established extensive sexual abuse at the Odenwaldschule across much of its history. Nothing in the sources consulted alleges Ritsema’s participation in, knowledge of, or victimization by abuse, and the best-known documented waves postdate his attendance. The school’s later scandal belongs in the institutional background but must not be retrojected into his biography without evidence.

## Education and Formation, 1933–1945

### Flight of the Geheebs and transfer to Switzerland

After the Nazi seizure of power in 1933, Paul and Edith Geheeb left Germany and re-established their educational community in Switzerland, eventually known as the **École d’Humanité**. Ritsema continued within the Geheeb orbit. The specific school locations and dates in his case are not clearly given in the accessible record.

Near the end of his school years he met Catherine Gris, a young Geneva music teacher who later became his wife. He completed the baccalaureate and enrolled in the biology faculty of the **University of Geneva**. This choice followed his father’s scientific interests.

### Interrupted university study and wartime self-education

The Second World War cut off funds from the Netherlands. Ritsema’s formal studies were interrupted; he supported himself with miscellaneous jobs. No evidence was found that he completed a university degree. During this period he read intensively in French and German literature, Tibetan Buddhism, and Jungian psychology. His mature identity as a learned mediator among traditions was thus partly autodidactic rather than university-certified.

### Alwine von Keller and the *I Ching*, 1944

Ritsema had known **Alwine von Keller** (1878–1965 in the Eranos Foundation’s institutional chronology; some later sources incorrectly give 1975) as an English-literature teacher in the Geheeb-school environment. In 1944, during a period described as personal uncertainty, Edith Geheeb encouraged him to seek von Keller’s help. Rudolf and Catherine entered analysis with her.

Von Keller used Richard Wilhelm’s German *I Ging* in her analytical practice as an introspective complement to dream work. In Sabbadini’s account, Ritsema’s first question was **“Who am I?”** The cast produced Hexagram 16, usually translated as Enthusiasm, changing to Hexagram 46, Pushing Upward or Ascending. Ritsema felt an immediate resonance and understood the encounter as disclosing his vocation.

The wartime Wilhelm edition was difficult to obtain. Ritsema borrowed von Keller’s first volume for a week and typed out the entire volume. This story first appears in the close collaborator tradition and is repeated in scholarly accounts; it is plausible but no typescript provenance was independently established.

The episode became Ritsema’s foundation story. Its historical core—analysis with von Keller and first use of the *I Ching* in 1944—is well attested. Its vocational meaning and the exact first cast are reported recollection. Later retellings sometimes present the cast as an oracular commissioning; that is interpretation, not an independently testable fact.

### Marriage

Rudolf and Catherine married on 8 May 1945, the day of Germany’s unconditional surrender in Europe. No account located states that the date was deliberately chosen for its public symbolism. Rudolf died exactly sixty-one years later.

## Early Career: Leiden, Brill, Polio, and the Road to Eranos, 1946–1955

### Return to the Netherlands and E. J. Brill

In 1946, about a year after the marriage, the couple moved to the Netherlands. Ritsema became head of the Oriental department in the antiquarian division of **E. J. Brill** in Leiden. Brill was already one of Europe’s leading publishers and booksellers in Oriental languages and religious studies. The position trained him in evaluating, acquiring, and selling scholarly and rare-book libraries. It also placed him inside the commercial infrastructure of European Oriental studies without making him a university Sinologist.

He continued *I Ching* research on weekends. This dual life—professional bibliographic expertise and private oracular study—helps explain both the philological architecture of his later concordance and the absence of a conventional academic career.

### Poliomyelitis, 1947

In summer 1947 Ritsema contracted poliomyelitis. The infection paralyzed his diaphragm and brought him close to death. He survived but was left paralyzed from the waist down. Accounts describe continuing severe immobility. Details of rehabilitation, assistive devices, respiratory aftereffects, or later medical care are not publicly documented.

The illness permanently reorganized his life. A neurologist advised a warmer and drier winter climate than Leiden. During the following winter—usually dated 1947–1948—the Ritsemas spent several weeks or a month near Ascona on Lake Maggiore.

### Casa Shanti, Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn, and the first Eranos encounter, 1948

Alwine von Keller had taken up residence at **Casa Shanti**, beside **Casa Gabriella**, home of Eranos founder Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn. Through this proximity the Ritsemas met Fröbe-Kapteyn in 1948. At the end of the stay Catherine gave a piano concert. Olga invited the couple to return and housed them in guest accommodation above or near the Eranos lecture hall.

The Ritsemas thereafter spent approximately a month each year at Eranos. A close friendship developed around a shared interest in the *I Ching*. Fröbe-Kapteyn had known the oracle through Richard Wilhelm and Jung and used it in private and institutional decisions. Ritsema later claimed that the experiential use of the oracle had been one of her unfulfilled original intentions for Eranos.

This claim became decisive in the 1988 dispute. Supporting evidence exists: Fröbe-Kapteyn consulted the oracle, had discussed experiential work with Jung in 1934, and recognized Ritsema’s practice as congenial. The stronger claim—that she intended the scholarly lecture series eventually to be replaced by *I Ching* groups—was contested by traditional participants and is not dictated unambiguously by her will.

## Eranos Apprenticeship and Succession, 1956–1966

### Entry into organization, 1956

From 1956 Rudolf and Catherine became increasingly involved in Eranos. Rudolf worked with Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn and Basel zoologist **Adolf Portmann** to plan the annual conferences. Catherine assisted the household and musical program. The couple’s role was practical, editorial, and relational: coordinating speakers, correspondence, hospitality, texts, and publication.

### Fröbe-Kapteyn’s final arrangements, 1961–1962

On 25 August 1961 Fröbe-Kapteyn executed a will establishing a new Eranos Foundation. It defined the purpose as annual Eranos conferences conducted in the same spirit as since 1933 and publication of their lectures as yearbooks. It appointed Adolf Portmann as sole executive. The Foundation’s later history also states that she asked Portmann and the Ritsemas to continue her work.

Fröbe-Kapteyn died at Casa Gabriella on 25 April 1962. Portmann became the Foundation’s first president and intellectual head; Rudolf Ritsema handled a large share of the organization, assisted by Catherine. Later biographies that say Ritsema simply “succeeded Olga in 1962” compress this division of authority.

Swiss federal authorities formally placed the Foundation under federal supervision in 1970 because of its international character. Its legally recorded fields included religious studies, humanities, natural sciences, art history, philology, psychology, biology, musicology, physics, Gnosticism, Jewish mysticism, Zen, and Protestant and Catholic theology.

### The labor of continuity

From 1962 through Portmann’s death in 1982, the Ritsemas supplied much of the operational continuity that made annual conferences possible. Portmann remained president and senior intellectual figure, while Ritsema increasingly acted as organizer and editor. The distinction matters: he was simultaneously steward of an inherited institution and an intellectual actor with his own deferred project.

## Development of the Eranos *I Ching*, 1967–1988

### From glosses on Wilhelm to a new translation

Ritsema’s earliest published studies appeared in James Hillman’s *Spring*. They analyzed key terms and individual hexagrams. Around 1970–1971 he concluded that critical annotations to Richard Wilhelm’s translation were insufficient and conceived a new translation from the Chinese text.

His purpose was not a modern historical-critical translation in the emerging academic sense. He wanted an **oracular** rather than philosophical version: a text that minimized a priori interpretation and allowed the user to encounter a field of images. He studied classical Chinese specifically for this project.

The method developed around several principles:

- use one stable English “core word” for a Chinese graph wherever possible;
- attach a “field of meanings” to the core word;
- print a concordance allowing users to compare every occurrence;
- suppress explanatory commentary that would close interpretation;
- treat archaic Chinese as imaginal and minimally grammatical;
- understand consultation as resonance between a cast image and an emotionally significant present situation, comparable to dream work;
- refuse a mechanically predictive or prescriptive model of divination.

James Hillman and publisher Robert Hinshaw supported the long gestation. The sources do not establish whether their support was financial, editorial, intellectual, or all three in particular episodes.

### Stephen Karcher collaboration

In the late 1980s American poet and mythographer **Stephen L. Karcher** joined the project. Together they produced three provisional volumes, *Chou Yi: The Oracle of Encompassing Versatility* (1990–1991), and the 816-page commercial edition *I Ching: The Classic Chinese Oracle of Change: The First Complete Translation with Concordance* (1994).

Karcher later left Ritsema’s project. Public practitioner sources state simply that they “parted company”; the causes and terms of the separation were not documented in the sources consulted. Both subsequently issued revised descendants of the common 1994 work. It would be inaccurate to describe the later Ritsema–Sabbadini edition as merely a reprint, but equally inaccurate to treat it as wholly independent of the Ritsema–Karcher text.

## Sole Organizer and Institutional Concentration, 1979–1987

Portmann became ill during the late 1970s. In 1979 he could not complete his planned retrospective address. He was absent in 1980 and died in 1982. Ritsema thereafter became the sole official organizer and executive center of the traditional conferences. Erik Hornung took over Portmann’s customary concluding address but not the Foundation’s legal control.

The structure was unusually concentrated. The foundation council effectively consisted of one person, so the will of the executive could function as a Foundation resolution. This legal arrangement later made the 1988 change possible even when many speakers opposed it.

Ritsema’s admirers describe these decades as stewardship under severe physical and administrative burdens. Critics describe increasing formality, restricted access, and personal identification with the institution. Hakl reports personal communications accusing him of strictness toward younger people and suggesting that the Ritsemas were strained by professors lobbying for invitations and sometimes sending gifts. Hakl explicitly states that documentary evidence was insufficient to judge these accusations. No bribery, fraud, criminal charge, or finding of corrupt conduct is documented.

The Foundation’s finances were a genuine problem. A first mortgage loan of 250,000 Swiss francs had been placed on the property in 1962. Despite donations and attendance fees, debts accumulated. The exact balance in 1988 is not supplied in the sources consulted. The debts did not diminish after the traditional conferences stopped; according to Tilo Schabert’s history, they increased substantially.

## The 1987–1990 Eranos Crisis

### Ritsema’s announced rationale

At the 1987 conference Ritsema said Eranos was completing a span equivalent to a whole life cycle. In his 1988 address, **“Encompassing Versatility: Keystone of the Eranos Project,”** he developed the argument through an astrological seven-year schema attributed to Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn. The fifty-sixth conference completed eight seven-year cycles. The next phase, he said, belonged to the outer planets Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto.

He used a chrysalis metaphor: general lectures had completed their preparatory role; participants now had to integrate the images into their individual lives. A January 1989 circular, *Neues von ERANOS: zurück zu den Quellen*, called the shift a return to Eranos’s original East–West and psychological purpose. The *I Ching* would be the instrument of direct encounter.

Other explanations also appeared:

- the Foundation lacked money to continue the large conferences;
- Ritsema wanted time to find a successor and resolve the finances;
- the traditional lecture-holiday format no longer attracted younger participants;
- he regarded some lecture work as exhausted or insufficient;
- he believed Fröbe-Kapteyn’s original experiential intention had finally become timely.

These explanations are not mutually exclusive. Critics stressed that he offered different rationales at different times.

### The announcement, 24–25 August 1988

On the evening of 24 August, Ritsema assembled the speakers in the Casa Gabriella library. According to Tilo Schabert, he announced curtly that this had been the last traditional Eranos conference, supplied no evidence, and did not invite discussion. Gilbert Durand protested and called it a betrayal; Schabert argued that Eranos had acquired an existence beyond any individual. Ritsema ended the meeting and directed participants toward Catherine’s scheduled piano concert.

The next morning Erik Hornung concluded his lecture on Akhenaten with a criticism of one-sidedness and an assertion that Eranos would live wherever human plurality was realized. Audience protest followed. Schabert’s narrative says Ritsema ended the disturbance by announcing that the buses would depart in five minutes.

Hakl’s later reconstruction differs on sequence in some details but confirms the essential facts: Ritsema announced the end without prior consultation; Hornung, Durand, Schabert, David Miller, James Hillman, Jean Servier, and others supported continuation; a last-minute meeting failed to reverse the decision.

### Documentary status

- **Established:** Ritsema announced an end to the traditional series and redirected the Foundation toward the *I Ching*.
- **Established:** speakers and participants protested; a rival continuation was organized.
- **Established:** Foundation debt was serious and the legal structure concentrated authority in Ritsema.
- **Participant allegation:** he deliberately timed the announcement and Catherine’s concert to block deliberation.
- **Participant allegation:** his action had no legal basis. This is disputed. Critics invoked Fröbe-Kapteyn’s will and federal statutes requiring annual interdisciplinary conferences; supporters invoked Portmann’s appointment of Ritsema and the executive’s legal authority to alter an obsolete format.
- **Interpretive claim:** he sought to let Eranos die with him. Hakl reports this as an inference or accusation, not a documented declaration.

### Amici di Eranos and the divided succession

Opponents organized a network in late 1988. The **Associazione Amici di Eranos** was formally created in 1989 under Erik Hornung and Tilo Schabert with municipal and tourist-office support. Their first renewed conference in 1990 attracted nearly two hundred people.

Ritsema’s Foundation resumed smaller public conferences in October 1990, partly because the statutes required activity. There were thereafter two claims to continuity:

1. **Amici di Eranos:** continuity of the plural, interdisciplinary, public annual conference.
2. **The Eranos Foundation at Moscia:** continuity of the legal entity, property, founder’s experiential intention, and Ritsema’s *I Ching* transformation.

Christa Robinson defended Ritsema as Portmann’s sole legitimate successor and argued that altering the form was his right and duty. The Amici appealed to the Swiss federal supervisory authority and sought access to the property and library. No court judgment declaring Ritsema’s change illegal was located. The name “Eranos” was not legally protected, allowing both lines to use it.

## The Eranos Round Table and *I Ching* Project, 1989–2002

### Structure

Ritsema replaced the lecture/audience division with small three-day Round Table sessions. Typically eighteen people sat together: five or six presenters, nine or ten selected participants, and three hosts. Every participant worked with a personal question and an oracle cast. Meals and informal exchange were integral to the “banquet” meaning of Eranos.

The Foundation organized as many as three or four sessions annually between 1990 and 2002. Themes included dreams, ethics, healing, personal and collective identity, science and gender, politics and economy, psyche and matter, time, unknown gods, and emergence of change. Uehiro Foundation sponsorship supported several ethics-centered sessions.

The project was simultaneously:

- an experimental laboratory of Jungian and archetypal-psychological practice;
- a long-term test bed for the translation;
- a retreat and group process;
- an assertion of institutional continuity;
- a move away from academic public lectures.

Supporters such as Bruno Rhyner argued that purely academic lectures could stimulate intellect but not provide direct orientation. Critics feared a “life-enhancement group,” a privatization of Eranos, or the subordination of a broad institution to one text.

### English, Italian, German, and French projects

- **English:** Ritsema and Karcher tested provisional versions in Round Tables from 1990 to 1992; commercial edition in 1994.
- **Italian:** Publisher Maurizio Rosenberg attended the first 1990 Round Table and proposed an Italian version. Shantena Augusto Sabbadini began working with Ritsema in 1991. *Eranos I Ching: Il libro della versatilità* appeared in 1996 and an expanded/reissued edition through UTET in 1997.
- **German:** Swiss linguist Hansjakob Schneider joined the project in 1997; *Eranos Yi Jing: Das Buch der Wandlungen* appeared in 2000.
- **French:** Imelda and Pierre Gaudissart worked under Ritsema’s direction; *Le Yi Jing Eranos* appeared in 2003.
- **Revised English:** Sabbadini and Ritsema incorporated work from the Italian and German translations and ten years of sessions into *The Original I Ching Oracle* (2005).

### The textual base and method

The project used the 1715 “Palace Edition” commissioned under the Kangxi emperor. Ritsema owned a rare copy. This was a traditional received text, not an archaeological reconstruction from newly excavated manuscripts.

The central innovation was lexical transparency. Repeated Chinese graphs received repeated English core words, with a concordance and semantic fields. Readers could see patterns hidden by fluent literary translation. The cost was deliberately strange English and reduced syntax.

### Technical reception and criticism

Practitioners have praised the edition for freeing consultation from a translator’s moralizing commentary and making recurrent vocabulary visible. The 1994 book acquired the informal name “R&K.” It influenced later teachers and digital I Ching communities.

Critical observations include:

- it is better described as a halfway point between a translation and a dictionary/concordance than as fluent English;
- a one-word-per-graph rule can obscure Chinese syntax and context;
- Ritsema’s claim that archaic Chinese has “minimal grammar” is overstated;
- traditional graph etymologies based on the *Shuowen Jiezi* and later literary associations do not necessarily recover Zhou-period meanings;
- the revised introduction makes demonstrably incorrect claims about the first two “Wings” being the original *Zhouyi* and about the relative ages of trigram arrangements;
- historical persons and myths embedded in the text are sometimes erased by the drive for semantic purity;
- claims of a “pure and complete” text are programmatic, not neutral scholarly findings;
- the edition’s psychological-archetypal frame is itself an interpretation even while it claims to minimize interpretation.

These criticisms concern method and historical philology. They do not negate the edition’s practical value as a concordance or its distinct purpose as an oracular instrument.

## Later Years, Retirement, and Death, 1994–2006

The official Foundation history states that Christa Robinson served as president from 1994 to 2001, indicating that Ritsema had stepped down by 1994. Some capsule biographies say “early 1990s.” The precise transfer date and whether Ritsema retained another directorial role are not clear.

Round Table work continued under the Foundation through 2002. Ritsema devoted his final years to revision of the *I Ching* and to an unpublished manuscript, **The Periplus of the Eranos Archetype**. Later historiography lists it among major unprinted histories of Eranos. Hakl reports that the work advanced twenty-one theses about dense forms dissolving into a universal river; the final thesis envisaged dissolution of the Foundation’s legal entity into the esoteric archetype of Eranos. This text helps explain the metaphysical dimension of the 1988 decision but remains unpublished and not fully available for verification.

*The Original I Ching Oracle*, co-authored with Sabbadini, appeared in London in 2005. It was the final major work published during Ritsema’s life.

Rudolf Ritsema died on 8 May 2006, aged 87, the sixty-first anniversary of his marriage. The place and cause of death were not established in the sources consulted. Catherine died in 2007.

At a June 2006 Eranos gathering, Robert Temple acknowledged Ritsema’s achievements but accused him of failing to keep Eranos open, barring people who loved the institution from the premises, and concentrating for twenty years on the *I Ching*. This was a partisan statement at a moment of institutional “rebirth,” not an official adjudication. It is nevertheless direct evidence of the hostility that survived his death.

## Companies, Institutions, Projects, and Financial Interests

### E. J. Brill

- **Role:** head of Oriental department, antiquarian division, Leiden, beginning around 1946.
- **Functions:** buying and selling scholarly libraries and rare books.
- **Ownership:** no evidence of equity ownership or executive control of the company.
- **Importance:** trained him in bibliography, Orientalist publishing, textual comparison, and the international scholarly book trade.

### Eranos Foundation

- **Initial involvement:** annual guest from 1948; organizational collaborator from 1956.
- **Post-1962 role:** chief practical organizer under President Adolf Portmann; co-editor from 1967.
- **After Portmann:** sole executive/organizer, with exact formal appointment date uncertain.
- **Property:** Casa Gabriella, Casa Eranos, library, archives, and other estate buildings at Moscia-Ascona belonged to the Foundation, not shown to be Ritsema’s private property.
- **Finance:** accumulated mortgage debt; first documented loan of CHF 250,000 in 1962; continued donations, fees, publication income, and later sponsorship. Personal benefit to Ritsema is not documented.
- **Merger:** Eranos Foundation and Alwine von Keller Foundation merged in March 1980 as the Eranos and Alwine von Keller Foundation.
- **Succession:** Christa Robinson, 1994–2001, followed by Wanda Luban, Maria Danioth, and John van Praag.

### Eranos *I Ching* Project

- **Intellectual property:** collaborative translations published by commercial and institutional publishers. Contractual division of copyright and royalties among Ritsema, Karcher, Sabbadini, the Foundation, and publishers was not located.
- **Sponsors and partners:** Uehiro Foundation for Practical Ethics and Education; Spring Journal; Element Books; Red Edizioni; UTET; O. W. Barth; Encre; Watkins; Daimon Verlag.
- **Technology:** no patented invention. The concordance and one-core-word system were editorial methods.

### Archival holdings

- **Eranos Foundation archives:** correspondence, institutional documents, photographs, and unpublished manuscripts connected to Ritsema and Catherine.
- **Museo delle Culture, Lugano:** the Ticino library system lists a dedicated **Fondo Rudolf Ritsema** under collection code `WLC=LUMCritsema`.
- **Swiss National Sound Archives:** 216 magnetic tapes, roughly 200 hours, covering Eranos lectures from 1968 to 1998.
- **Eranos audiovisual archive:** includes the 1951 silent film *Eranos* and later interviews/documentaries.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### Politics

No party membership, elective office, political candidacy, military service, intelligence affiliation, or participation in political violence is documented. His life intersected with the Nazi era through the Geheebs’ departure from Germany, but no evidence places him in resistance, collaboration, imprisonment, or exile in the legal sense.

### Religion and spirituality

Ritsema’s early family religiosity was Christian or theistic in language, though no church membership is documented. His mature formation combined:

- his mother’s trust in divine guidance;
- Tibetan Buddhist reading;
- Jungian analysis and individuation;
- the *I Ching* as oracle;
- Henry Corbin’s imaginal world;
- James Hillman’s archetypal psychology;
- Buddhist imagery of non-attachment and dissolution in his late *Periplus*;
- the Eranos tradition of comparative religion.

He was neither a conventional Confucian commentator nor a purely historical Sinologist. His *I Ching* was a Western psychological-oracular project informed by Chinese text.

### Image, language, and interpretation

The mature Ritsema treated images as active events rather than signs with one fixed meaning. Like dreams, oracle images acquired meaning in relation to the user’s present question. He therefore sought to preserve ambiguity and lexical resonance. His “ethic of the image” was procedural: do not impose an external interpretation prematurely; attend to what the image provokes.

This method contained a paradox. To avoid interpretation he constructed strong rules—stable core words, semantic fields, selected traditional etymologies, archetypal psychology, and the received 1715 text. The edition displaced one kind of interpretation while installing another.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Paul and Edith Geheeb

Progressive educators and formative mentors. Edith reportedly directed Ritsema toward Alwine von Keller during his crisis. Their school community also brought Rudolf and Catherine together.

### Alwine von Keller

Former teacher, Jungian analyst, resident of Casa Shanti, and transmitter of the *I Ching* to Ritsema in 1944. She provided the bridge from the Geheeb educational world to Eranos.

### Catherine Gris Ritsema

Spouse, musician, host, organizer, photographer, and participant in institutional decisions. The administrative survival of Eranos from 1962 to 1988 was the work of the Ritsemas as a couple, even when publications named Rudolf alone.

### Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn

Founder, patron, host, friend, and source of institutional legitimacy. Shared *I Ching* practice formed the bond from 1948. Ritsema later represented his transformation as fulfillment of her deferred intention; opponents appealed to her will against him.

### Adolf Portmann

Basel zoologist, president, co-organizer, and co-editor. Portmann supplied scholarly stature and a biological philosophy of form; Ritsema supplied administration and editorial continuity. Portmann’s illness and death concentrated authority in Ritsema.

### C. G. Jung

Jung shaped the psychological framework of Eranos and the Western *I Ching* tradition, but no close personal relationship between Jung and Ritsema is documented in the consulted sources. Ritsema inherited a Jung-mediated institution and used Jung’s early refusal of experiential oracle work as evidence that a later historical moment had arrived.

### James Hillman

Archetypal psychologist, publisher of Ritsema’s early *I Ching* studies in *Spring*, supporter of the translation, Eranos speaker, and opponent of ending the traditional conference. Their relationship joined collaboration and later institutional disagreement.

### Robert Hinshaw

Publisher associated with Daimon Verlag and supporter of the translation’s development. Later remained active in preserving Eranos materials.

### Stephen L. Karcher

American poet, mythographer, translator, and co-director of the early project. His collaboration enabled the provisional English version and 1994 edition. The later separation is documented, but not its cause.

### Shantena Augusto Sabbadini

Italian theoretical physicist turned spiritual teacher. Joined in 1991, co-led Round Tables, co-translated the Italian version and revised English edition, and became the principal transmitter of Ritsema’s biography and method after his death.

### Hansjakob Schneider; Pierre and Imelda Gaudissart

Collaborators responsible respectively for German and French forms of the Eranos translation.

### Erik Hornung, Tilo Schabert, Gilbert Durand

Longstanding Eranos participants and the most visible opponents of Ritsema’s 1988 decision. Hornung and Schabert led the parallel conference continuation. Durand publicly described the end as betrayal.

### Christa Robinson

Analytical psychologist, defender of Ritsema’s legal and spiritual succession, and president of the Foundation from 1994 to 2001.

### Uehiro Foundation and Eiji Uehiro

Japanese partners and sponsors who helped connect the oracle project to applied ethics and funded international sessions.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, Crimes, and Financial Questions

### Ending the traditional Eranos conferences

This is the principal controversy of Ritsema’s life. The action itself is documented. Disputed issues are its legality, motives, process, and faithfulness to Fröbe-Kapteyn.

- **Critics’ case:** her will required annual interdisciplinary conferences and yearbooks “in the same spirit”; Ritsema acted without consultation; the oracle project narrowed a plural institution; he blocked communal deliberation and restricted access.
- **Supporters’ case:** Portmann legally appointed him; the sole executive possessed authority; the lecture format had become obsolete and financially strained; Fröbe-Kapteyn had long wanted experiential oracle work; the Round Table restored active participation and the literal “banquet.”
- **Outcome:** institutional schism rather than a judicial resolution. Both branches continued under the Eranos name.

### Debt and property

The Foundation was heavily indebted by 1988. The debt is documented; personal enrichment is not. Critics proposed that financial distress motivated the cancellation. Ritsema himself told federal supervisors in 1989 that he wanted a pause to find a successor and clarify finances. Debts reportedly increased after the cancellation.

No evidence was found of embezzlement, false accounting, theft, fraud, money laundering, tax evasion, or conversion of Foundation property to personal ownership.

### Gifts and invitation pressure

Hakl reports oral allegations that professors seeking repeat invitations exerted pressure and gave presents, straining Rudolf and Catherine. He explicitly says there was no documentary basis to determine the truth or extent. No source alleges that Ritsema sold invitations or accepted bribes. This material belongs under unverified participant recollection, not corruption.

### Restricted access and control

Robert Temple stated in 2006 that Ritsema kept the property closed to many who wished to visit the library and gather there. Other critics similarly described a locked institution. This is a documented accusation by a successor-group participant. The rationale—privacy, finances, disability, preservation, personal control, or policy—is not established.

### Professional status

Promotional texts often call Ritsema a Sinologist or psychologist. His real expertise was substantial, but the record does not show a degree or professional license in either field. This is best treated as inflation or looseness in later publicity, not misconduct by Ritsema unless evidence shows that he personally made false credential claims; none was found.

### Translation claims

Subtitles and advertising call the work the “first complete translation with concordance,” “pure and complete texts,” and, in some promotions, the most important advance since Wilhelm. These are publishing claims. Specialists dispute the historical and linguistic premises. There is no scholarly consensus that it is the definitive or uniquely complete *I Ching*.

### Criminality and intelligence connections

No investigation, arrest, indictment, conviction, prison term, intelligence-service connection, political violence, abuse allegation, addiction, or financial crime involving Rudolf Ritsema was found in the consulted record.

## Illness, Disability, and Psychological Crisis

- **Personal uncertainty, circa 1944:** close-source accounts say a period of existential uncertainty led Edith Geheeb to refer him to Alwine von Keller. It is not diagnosed as mental illness.
- **Jungian analysis:** Rudolf and Catherine began analysis with von Keller in 1944. No termination date is documented.
- **Poliomyelitis, 1947:** diaphragm paralysis, near death, permanent paralysis from the waist down. This is the best-documented medical event.
- **Later health:** no detailed record. The administrative burden of Eranos and the effects of severe disability are repeatedly inferred but not medically documented.
- **Cause of death:** unknown in the accessible sources.

## Myths, Legends, Symbolic Narratives, and Disputed Stories

### “Who am I?” and the answering hexagrams

1. **Story:** Ritsema asked the oracle “Who am I?” and received Enthusiasm (16) changing to Pushing Upward (46), recognizing his life’s work.
2. **Appearance:** transmitted by Sabbadini and repeated in a 2020 history-of-psychology study.
3. **Circulators:** Ritsema’s collaborator and later Eranos/I Ching writers.
4. **Evidence:** the 1944 initiation through von Keller is multiply attested; no contemporaneous consultation record was located.
5. **Reputational effect:** establishes an oracular call narrative, fusing biography and vocation.

### Typing Wilhelm in one week

1. **Story:** unable to obtain the rare wartime book, Ritsema borrowed it for a week and typed the entire first volume.
2. **Appearance:** collaborator biography and introductions derived from it.
3. **Evidence:** no surviving typescript publicly identified.
4. **Effect:** symbolizes disciplined devotion and anticipates the exhaustive concordance.

### Completing Fröbe-Kapteyn’s secret or deferred intention

1. **Story:** Olga always wanted Eranos to become an experiential *I Ching* laboratory, but Jung said the time was not ripe; Ritsema fulfilled the plan in 1988.
2. **Appearance:** Ritsema’s 1989 circular; Ritsema–Sabbadini’s 1997 “Images of the Unknown”; later Foundation histories.
3. **Circulators:** Ritsema, Sabbadini, Christa Robinson, the legal Foundation line.
4. **Evidence for:** Olga’s consultations, 1934 discussion with Jung, friendship with Ritsema around the oracle.
5. **Evidence against or limiting:** her will explicitly preserved annual interdisciplinary conferences; contemporaries rejected the claim that oracle groups could replace them.
6. **Effect:** supplied spiritual legitimacy for institutional rupture.

### Eight cycles of seven and the outer planets

1. **Story:** fifty-six conferences completed eight seven-year cycles; Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto governed the next stage.
2. **Appearance:** Ritsema’s 1988 address.
3. **Evidence:** the conference count is arithmetical; the planetary governance is an astrological interpretation attributed to Fröbe-Kapteyn.
4. **Effect:** admirers saw transformation; opponents saw unilateral esoteric rationalization.

### Chrysalis and butterfly

1. **Story:** the lecture institution had to enter a chrysalis and dissolve so individual transformation could emerge.
2. **Appearance:** 1988–1989 transition texts and later summaries.
3. **Effect:** converted closure into metamorphosis and remains central to sympathetic accounts.

### “He gave his life to the I Ching”

1. **Story:** Ritsema devoted sixty years or his whole adult life to producing the Eranos edition.
2. **Evidence:** study from 1944 to 2005 is documented, but much of his labor from 1956 to 1988 was Eranos administration and yearbook editing.
3. **Effect:** accurately captures duration while obscuring the broader institutional career and the work of Karcher, Sabbadini, Schneider, the Gaudissarts, Catherine, and Round Table participants.

### Custodian versus captor of Eranos

Two hostile symbolic portraits developed after 1988:

- **Custodian:** the severely disabled steward who preserved Olga’s place for decades, kept the yearbooks alive, and finally realized her deepest intent.
- **Captor:** the sole executive who identified himself with the institution, locked its gates, ended its plural tradition, and redirected its assets to his private obsession.

Both are memory formations rooted in real conduct; neither is an adequate substitute for the documentary chronology.

### Death on the wedding anniversary

The date is factual. Later biography gives it a completed-vocation resonance. No evidence establishes foreknowledge, choice, or supernatural significance.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### Institutional legacy

Ritsema’s record cannot be separated into preservation and rupture. He helped keep the annual Eranos conferences and yearbooks functioning for more than three decades, including through founder succession, publisher collapse, Portmann’s illness, debt, and the burdens of property. He then terminated the inherited format without communal consent and redirected the legal Foundation.

The split produced two legitimate but incomplete inheritances. The contemporary Eranos Foundation’s official history integrates both the traditional and Round Table phases into a seven-phase chronology. The Amici/Verein history preserves the memory of opposition and insists that plural scholarly conferences continued despite Ritsema.

### *I Ching* legacy

The Eranos translation’s durable contribution is its concordance-based exposure of repeated Chinese vocabulary and semantic fields. It is widely used by diviners who want less prescriptive commentary. Its influence is stronger in practitioner communities, archetypal psychology, and popular spirituality than in mainstream historical Sinology.

The work also models a specific cross-cultural operation: ancient Chinese graphs are brought into dialogue with Jungian dreams, Corbin’s imaginal, and group resonance. This is not culturally neutral translation. Its value and limitations arise from the same decision.

### Disability and labor

Public memory often mentions the 1947 polio attack but rarely integrates it into the scale of his achievement. Ritsema carried out decades of correspondence, editing, hosting, and textual work while permanently paralyzed. At the same time, disability should not be used to sentimentalize or excuse decisions that contemporaries contested.

### Catherine’s obscured contribution

Recent scholarship and archival captions increasingly make Catherine visible as musician, photographer, historian, host, and co-steward. The formula “Rudolf directed Eranos” compresses a household partnership. Her unfinished history may remain one of the most important sources not yet fully published.

### Continuing reinterpretation

Three trends shape his reputation:

1. **Hagiographic compression:** lifelong master of the *I Ching*, chosen by Olga, who completed his destiny.
2. **Institutional indictment:** authoritarian president who closed the gates and nearly killed Eranos.
3. **Historical reconstruction:** autodidact, bibliographic professional, disabled administrator, translator, and strong-willed successor operating inside debt, an unusual one-person legal structure, and competing conceptions of Eranos.

The third accommodates the evidence most fully without dissolving the real conflict.

## Chronology

- **3 October 1918:** Born in Velp, Gelderland, Netherlands.
- **c. 1929–1930:** Enters the Odenwaldschule, Heppenheim, at age eleven.
- **1933 onward:** Continues in the Geheeb educational orbit after Paul and Edith Geheeb leave Nazi Germany for Switzerland.
- **Late 1930s:** Meets Geneva music teacher Catherine Gris; completes baccalaureate.
- **c. 1939–1940:** Enrolls in biology at the University of Geneva; wartime loss of Dutch funds interrupts study.
- **Early 1940s:** Supports himself through miscellaneous work; reads French and German literature, Tibetan Buddhism, and Jungian psychology.
- **1944:** Rudolf and Catherine begin analysis with Alwine von Keller; he first consults the *I Ching* and reportedly types Wilhelm’s first volume.
- **8 May 1945:** Marries Catherine Gris.
- **1946:** Couple moves to the Netherlands; Ritsema joins E. J. Brill’s antiquarian division in Leiden and heads its Oriental department.
- **Summer 1947:** Contracts poliomyelitis; diaphragm paralysis and near death; permanent paralysis below the waist.
- **Winter 1947–1948:** Recuperative stay near Ascona; reconnects with von Keller at Casa Shanti and meets Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn.
- **1948 onward:** Ritsemas spend about one month annually at Eranos; friendship with Olga deepens around the *I Ching*.
- **1956:** Begins helping Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn and Adolf Portmann organize the annual Eranos conferences.
- **25 August 1961:** Fröbe-Kapteyn’s will establishes the second Eranos Foundation and appoints Portmann sole executive; the Ritsemas are entrusted with continuation in the broader succession account.
- **25 April 1962:** Fröbe-Kapteyn dies. Portmann becomes president; Rudolf and Catherine assume much of the organization.
- **1967:** First conference yearbook co-edited by Portmann and Ritsema.
- **1970:** Federal ordinance places the internationally oriented Foundation under Swiss federal supervision.
- **c. 1970–1971:** Ritsema decides to replace his Wilhelm commentaries with a new Chinese-to-Western-language oracular translation.
- **1970–1984:** Publishes a series of term and hexagram studies in *Spring*.
- **1972:** Rhein-Verlag liquidated; yearbook publication shifts to Brill and later Insel.
- **1979:** Publishes *Eranos and Its Meaning* with Portmann.
- **March 1980:** Eranos Foundation merges with the Alwine von Keller Foundation.
- **1980:** Portmann can no longer attend; Ritsema’s authority becomes effectively sole.
- **1982:** Portmann dies; Ritsema becomes sole organizer/editor of the traditional conference.
- **1987:** Announces that Eranos is completing a full life cycle; publishes “The Origins and Opus of Eranos.”
- **24–25 August 1988:** Announces end of the traditional conferences; Durand, Hornung, Schabert, and others protest.
- **Late 1988–1989:** Issues *zurück zu den Quellen* circular; centers the Foundation on the *I Ching*. Opponents organize the Amici di Eranos.
- **1989:** Provisional *Chou Yi* project begins; no traditional public conference is held by the Amici that year.
- **1990:** First *Chou Yi* volumes; Amici conference attracts nearly 200; Ritsema’s Foundation resumes smaller public Round Tables in October.
- **1991:** Sabbadini begins Italian translation work; third provisional English/concordance volume completed.
- **1992:** Uehiro-sponsored ethics conference; Ritsema publishes “The Ethic of the Image”; two-cassette project introduction appears.
- **1994:** Ritsema and Karcher publish the 816-page English edition. Christa Robinson’s documented presidency begins.

## Atlas Connections

### Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn

- **[T5] Documented fact:** Ritsema met Eranos founder Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn at Moscia-Ascona in 1948 through their mutual connection with Alwine von Keller. A friendship developed around their shared use of the *I Ching*. From 1956 Ritsema worked directly with Fröbe-Kapteyn and Adolf Portmann in organizing the annual Eranos conferences, continuing until her death at Casa Gabriella on 25 April 1962. The Eranos Foundation confirms that Portmann and Ritsema inherited responsibility for Eranos after her death. [Eranos Foundation history](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/page.php?page=4&pagename=history), [Sabbadini’s biographical account](https://www.shantena.com/en/eranos/rudolf/)

### Mircea Eliade

- **[T3] Documented fact:** Eliade corresponded directly with Ritsema about his participation in Eranos. A surviving letter dated **25 September 1964**, held by the Fondazione Eranos, records Eliade assuring Ritsema that his scheduling difficulties did not signify “a defection regarding Eranos.” On **21 December 1970**, Ritsema wrote to Henry and Stella Corbin that Eliade appeared to have lost interest in Eranos after repeated refusals. These records establish direct correspondence and Ritsema’s administrative engagement with Eliade, without by themselves proving friendship. [*Phanês*, vol. 5 (2022), archival citations on pp. 73–74](https://phanes.live/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/Phanes-5-2022.pdf)

### Carl Gustav Jung

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Writing decades after Jung’s death, Ritsema discussed Jung as a formative figure in Eranos history and recounted the earlier exchange in which Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn proposed experiential work with the *I Ching* and Jung reportedly judged that the time was not yet ripe. Ritsema used that episode in his 1987–1989 accounts of Eranos’s origins and transformation to justify the later Round Table project. The historical exchange is transmitted through Ritsema’s institutional reconstruction and should not be treated as proof that Jung endorsed Ritsema’s eventual 1988 program. [Eranos Foundation history](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/page.php?page=4&pagename=history), [Ritsema bibliography in the Eranos lecture catalogue](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/page.php?page=11&pagename=lecturers)
