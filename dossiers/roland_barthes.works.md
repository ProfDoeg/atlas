## Works by Roland Gérard Barthes

### Books, book-length essays, and image–text objects published in his lifetime

- **1953:** *Le Degré zéro de l’écriture*. A book-length critical essay assembled partly from articles published since 1947; it proposes “writing” as a historically and socially situated practice rather than a neutral vehicle. [1][2]
- **1954:** *Michelet par lui-même*. A critical portrait and montage of extracts made for the “Écrivains de toujours” series; its hybrid of biography, thematic lexicon, and self-projection already resists the ordinary scholarly monograph. [1][2]
- **1957:** *Mythologies*. An obra assembled from short contemporary “mythologies,” most previously published in *Les Lettres nouvelles*, followed by the theoretical essay “Le Mythe, aujourd’hui.” [1][2]
- **1963:** *Sur Racine*. A polemical critical book formed from three earlier studies; its publication precipitated the dispute with Raymond Picard answered by *Critique et vérité*. [1][2]
- **1964:** *Essais critiques*. An author-arranged collection of essays, reviews, and prefaces written between 1953 and 1963; it is a made book but not a single continuously composed argument. [1][2]
- **1964:** *La Tour Eiffel*. An image–text object pairing Barthes’s essay with André Martin’s photographs; authorship of the total book is consequently collaborative rather than Barthes’s alone. [1][2]
- **1964:** *Éléments de sémiologie*. A systematic theoretical essay first published in *Communications* and issued with *Le Degré zéro de l’écriture* in book form in 1965. [1][2]
- **1966:** *Critique et vérité*. A polemical essay responding to Raymond Picard and defending the new criticism; it functions partly as a manifesto for criticism as an explicit language about language. [1][2]
- **1967:** *Système de la mode*. A semiological study derived from Barthes’s research on written fashion; its analytical corpus consists not of garments but of fashion descriptions in magazines. [1][2]
- **1970:** *S/Z*. A book-length, lexia-by-lexia reading of Balzac’s “Sarrasine,” developed from the 1967–1969 EPHE seminar; it is simultaneously criticism, theoretical experiment, and performed protocol of reading. [1][4]
- **1970:** *L’Empire des signes*. An illustrated image–text composition generated by Barthes’s encounters with Japan; it explicitly constructs a fictive sign-system rather than claiming to describe Japan ethnographically. [1][2]
- **1971:** *Sade, Fourier, Loyola*. Three studies of “logothetes,” or founders of languages, assembled into a comparative critical triptych. Parts had appeared separately before the book. [1][2]
- **1972:** *Nouveaux essais critiques*. A small authorial collection issued after *Le Degré zéro de l’écriture* rather than as a wholly independent first-edition volume; its status is therefore both new obra and publishing composite. [1][2]
- **1973:** *Le Plaisir du texte*. A fragmentary theoretical-literary book about reading, pleasure, and jouissance; its alphabetized discontinuity resists classification as either treatise or conventional essay. [1][2]
- **1973:** *Erté: Romain de Tirtoff*. Barthes’s critical text within an illustrated art monograph that also contains material by Erté; the complete volume is collaborative, while Barthes made its principal essay. [2]
- **1975:** *Roland Barthes par Roland Barthes*. An illustrated, alphabetically fragmented self-portrait made for a biographical series and deliberately presented as the speech of a fictional character; it both is and resists autobiography. [1][2]
- **1975:** *Alors, la Chine?* A short polemical pamphlet expanding Barthes’s newspaper response to reactions provoked by his 1974 journey to China. [1][2]
- **1977:** *Fragments d’un discours amoureux*. A sequence of alphabetically ordered “figures” of amorous speech, transformed from the 1974–1976 EPHE seminar; neither novel nor psychological handbook, it stages a discourse rather than explaining it. [1][4]
- **1977–1978:** *Leçon*. The inaugural lecture delivered at the Collège de France on 7 January 1977 and published as a book in 1978; as an institutional utterance it is also a manifesto for Barthes’s chair of literary semiology. [1][2]
- **1978:** *Arcimboldo*. Barthes’s essay in an illustrated art book introduced by Achille Bonito Oliva; the publication is a collaborative image–text object, not solely a Barthes monograph. [2]
- **1979:** *Sollers écrivain*. A book assembled from previously published writing on Philippe Sollers with pages adapted from the Collège de France course on the Neutral. [1][2]
- **1980:** *La Chambre claire: note sur la photographie*. An illustrated essay, meditation on mourning, and theory of photographic experience; Barthes calls it a “note,” and its private affective inquiry prevents easy reduction to a technical treatise. [1][2]

### Individually published shorter texts, interventions, and voices

- **1942–1981:** *Articles, entretiens et comptes rendus*. This is an obra in the sense of a body of work, not one text: Thierry Leguay’s standard chronological bibliography separately records Barthes’s essays, reviews, prefaces, questionnaires, public interventions, and interviews, beginning with “Culture et tragédie” and continuing through posthumously printed pieces. The five-volume *Œuvres complètes* reproduces the published writings chronologically; treating every reprint or translation as a new work would confuse works with editions. [1][2]
- **1942:** *Culture et tragédie*. Barthes’s first known publication, a critical essay. Leguay assigned it to the sanatorium review *Existences*, but Philippe Roger subsequently identified its actual first venue as *Cahiers de l’étudiant*; the bibliographical correction should not be silently erased. [1][7]
- **1942:** *Notes sur André Gide et son Journal*. An early critical essay on Gide, originally published in a student or sanatorium-period review and later only partly reprinted. [1]
- **1943:** *Les Anges du péché*. A film review or critical note on Robert Bresson’s film, published in *Existences*. [1]
- **1943:** *À propos du numéro spécial de Confluences sur les problèmes du roman*. A periodical intervention concerning the novel. [1]
- **1943:** *Plaisir aux classiques*. An early literary-critical essay; some later references date it 1944, so its dating is not entirely stable across bibliographies. [1][7]
- **1944:** *En Grèce*. A travel or recollective essay published in *Existences*, not a book-length travel narrative. [1]
- **1944:** *Réflexions sur le style de L’Étranger*. A critical essay on Camus’s novel. [1][7]
- **1947:** *Gromaire, Lurçat et Calder*. An art-critical article published in *France-Asie*. [1]
- **1947:** *Prospectus pour une exposition de Dominique Marty*. A gallery prospectus: an occasional exhibition text whose practical setting makes it closer to a manifestation than to an autonomous essay. [1]
- **1967–1968:** *The Death of the Author / La mort de l’auteur*. A theoretical essay first manifested in English in the multimedia magazine *Aspen* in 1967, then in French in *Mantéia* in 1968. These are linguistic manifestations of one text, not two independently made works. [1]
- **1974:** *Carnets du voyage en Chine*. Working notebooks kept between 11 April and 4 May 1974; Barthes did not publish them, and the 2009 book is an editorially established archival object rather than an author-approved obra. [2]
- **1976:** *All except you*. An essay on Saul Steinberg’s drawings, conceived as an image–text artist’s book but published only in 1983. Because both author and artist reportedly withheld the projected publication during their lifetimes, its posthumous status resists being called an authorized finished work. [2][8]
- **1977–1979:** *Journal de deuil*. Individual mourning notes written after the death of Barthes’s mother and kept in a folder bearing this designation; the 2009 continuous codex is a posthumous editorial construction, and publication of these private cards has been disputed. [2][6]
- **1979:** *Soirées de Paris*. Worked diary entries recording Parisian evenings; their degree of preparation suggests possible publication, but Barthes left no conclusive authorization, so they remain between project and private writing. [5][6]
- **Various dates:** *Prefaces, catalogue essays, encyclopedia contributions, questionnaires, conference papers, and discussion interventions*. These are hundreds of separately situated textual acts fully itemized by Leguay and incorporated into the *Œuvres complètes*. They resist reduction either to “books” or to a single undifferentiated oeuvre, but duplicating their later collection titles would falsely multiply them. [1][2]
- **1962–1980:** *Recorded and transcribed interviews*. A large oral and dialogic body of work, subsequently collected in *Le Grain de la voix* and separately catalogued in the scholarly list of Barthes’s written, radio, and television interviews; because interlocutors shaped them and Barthes sometimes rewrote transcriptions, authorship is collaborative and variable. [1][2][9]
- **1960s–1980:** *Radio and television appearances, lectures, and readings*. These are vocal and audiovisual manifestations rather than books; some survive as recordings, some only as transcripts or bibliographical records, and Leguay’s “textes et voix” bibliography is the relevant work-level inventory. [1][2]

### Teaching works: seminars, courses, and performed research

- **1963:** *Inventaire des systèmes contemporains de signification: systèmes d’objets (vêtements, nourriture, logement)*. An EPHE research seminar: a collective, performed intellectual project rather than a fixed authored text. [4]
- **1964:** *Inventaire des systèmes de signification contemporains*. An EPHE seminar continuing the inventory of contemporary sign systems. [4]
- **1965–1966:** *Recherches sur la rhétorique*. Two successive EPHE seminar cycles; their identity lies in teaching and collective inquiry, not in a book subsequently bearing the same title. [4]
- **1967:** *Recherches sur le discours de l’Histoire*. An EPHE seminar project on historical discourse. [4]
- **1967–1969:** *Analyse structurale d’un texte narratif: “Sarrasine” de Balzac*. Two EPHE seminar years that fed into *S/Z* but were not identical with it; the seminar notes were separately reconstructed and published in 2011. [2][4]
- **1971:** *La notion d’idiolecte: premières questions, premières recherches*. An EPHE research seminar, inherently provisional in its announced “first questions.” [4]
- **1972:** *Dix ans de sémiologie (1961–1971); La théorie du texte*. An EPHE seminar or paired teaching project reviewing semiology and developing a theory of the text. [4]
- **1973:** *Les problèmes de la thèse et de la recherche; La notion de modernité; analyse d’un cas de paranoïa*. A multipart EPHE programme rather than a unitary work; the institutional listing joins distinct research exercises under one year. [4]
- **1973–1974:** *Étude des problèmes relatifs à la constitution d’un lexique d’auteur (idiolecte); travail collectif sur la biographie; la voix*. A collective EPHE seminar later edited as *Le Lexique de l’auteur*; the 2010 volume also adds rejected fragments from *Roland Barthes par Roland Barthes* and therefore is not simply the original seminar. [2][4]
- **1974–1976:** *Le Discours amoureux*. A two-year EPHE seminar and collective research process from which *Fragments d’un discours amoureux* emerged. Its notes and unpublished pages were editorially published in 2007, but seminar and book remain different works. [2][4]
- **1976–1977:** *Les problèmes d’interprétation dans l’opéra; le problème des ratures du texte écrit*. Barthes’s final EPHE programme, comprising two distinct research problems rather than a finished treatise. [4]
- **1976–1977:** *Comment vivre ensemble: simulations romanesques de quelques espaces quotidiens*. Barthes’s first Collège de France course, a performed inquiry into idiorrhythmic forms of communal life; its notes became an editor-established book only in 2002. [2][4]
- **1976–1977:** *Qu’est-ce que tenir un discours? Recherche sur la parole investie*. A Collège de France seminar accompanying *Comment vivre ensemble*; it is a collective oral investigation, not merely an appendix to the course. [4]
- **1977–1978:** *Le Neutre*. A Collège de France course organized in successive “figures” of the Neutral; the posthumous 2002 volume presents Barthes’s teaching notes, not a book he completed for publication. [2][3][4]
- **1978–1979:** *La Préparation du roman I: de la vie à l’œuvre*. A Collège de France course about the desire and practical conditions for writing a novel. It is a project about making a work, not evidence that the projected novel was completed. [2][3][4]
- **1978–1979:** *La métaphore du labyrinthe: recherches interdisciplinaires*. A collective Collège de France seminar accompanying the first *Préparation du roman* course. [4]
- **1979–1980:** *La Préparation du roman II: l’œuvre comme volonté*. The second course cycle, interrupted by Barthes’s accident and death; later edited with the first cycle in 2003. The Collège de France’s annual-summary page says no teaching took place in 1979–1980, while the course bibliography and published notes identify this second cycle, a documentary discrepancy that remains visible. [2][3][4]

### Archives, notebooks, and unfinished projects

- **1941:** *Évocations et Incantations dans la tragédie grecque*. A university research dissertation on Greek tragedy. It belongs to Barthes’s scholarly formation and survives as an academic work, but it was not part of the published oeuvre he later authorized. [7]
- **1945 onward:** *Fichier Michelet*. A systematic card index made while Barthes read Michelet; both research instrument and generative textual machine, it underlies the 1954 book without being reducible to it. [6]
- **Circa 1940–1980:** *Le Grand Fichier*. More than a thousand surviving manuscript cards organized by Barthes as a continuing apparatus of notation, classification, memory, and writing. It is an obra as working system or protocol, but resists the boundary of the finished work because it remained perpetually extensible. [6]
- **1977–1979:** *Agenda and mourning-card constellation*. Barthes’s dated agendas and the separately maintained mourning slips overlap biographically without constituting a single author-declared diary; later publication choices should not erase that material distinction. [6]
- **1978–1980:** *Vita Nova*. An unfinished novel-project surviving in eight successive plans and associated notes. The project—Barthes’s intention to pass from teaching and criticism toward “the Novel”—is the work that exists; no completed novel manuscript exists. [5][6]
- **1979–1980:** *Plans for a life/work after mourning*. Notes surrounding *Vita Nova*, *La Préparation du roman*, “Délibération,” and the proposed turn toward literary writing form a constellation rather than one securely titled work. Any claim that Barthes “left a novel” resolves too neatly what the archive preserves as intention, rehearsal, and abandonment. [5][6]
- **Undated and various dates:** *Personal notebooks and travel notebooks*. These include working, travel, address, vocabulary, and diary-like notebooks now held with the archive. They are made objects and writing instruments, but not all are autonomous texts or works intended for readers. [6]
- **Various dates:** *Correspondence*. Barthes made a large body of letters, postcards, and inscriptions, some published selectively after his death. It is a corpus of situated communications rather than a single work, and the published selections cannot claim completeness. [6]
- **Various dates:** *Drawings, doodles, and graphic notations*. The archive and exhibitions preserve graphic material made by Barthes, often continuous with handwriting and note-taking. It resists classification as a separate visual-art oeuvre because no catalogue raisonné or authorial corpus boundary establishes it as such. [6]

### Posthumous editorial manifestations—not new works made by Barthes

- **1981:** *Le Grain de la voix: entretiens, 1962–1980*. A posthumous collection of interviews; the component dialogues are Barthes’s works with interlocutors, while the collection as such is an editorial manifestation. [1][2]
- **1982:** *L’Obvie et l’Obtus: Essais critiques III*. A posthumously assembled collection of previously dispersed essays on image, music, and text, not a new book planned and completed by Barthes. [1][2]
- **1983:** *All except you*. The posthumous artist’s-book manifestation of Barthes’s 1976 Steinberg essay; its publication history is inseparable from doubts about lifetime authorization. [2][8]
- **1984:** *Le Bruissement de la langue: Essais critiques IV*. A posthumous editorial collection of essays on language, literature, and reading. [2]
- **1985:** *L’Aventure sémiologique*. A posthumous collection of semiological writings; its unity and title belong partly to editorial construction. [2]
- **1986:** *Sur la littérature*. A separately published 1980 dialogue with Maurice Nadeau; the conversation is the work, while the codex is posthumous. [2]
- **1987:** *Incidents*. A posthumous collection joining heterogeneous autobiographical pieces, including Moroccan notes and *Soirées de Paris*. Whether Barthes intended some of this private material for publication remains disputed. [2][5]
- **1993–1995; revised 2002:** *Œuvres complètes*. Éric Marty’s chronological collected-works edition—first in three volumes and then revised in five—makes Barthes’s published corpus accessible but is itself an editorial monument, not an additional work authored by Barthes. [2]
- **2001:** *La Plus Déchirante des fêtes*. A posthumously issued short text paired with drawings by Josef Nadj and commentary by Michel Archimbaud; the publication is a later collaborative object and should not be backdated as a lifetime Barthes book. [2]
- **2002:** *Écrits sur le théâtre*. Jean-Loup Rivière’s posthumous gathering of Barthes’s dispersed theatre criticism; Barthes had contemplated but never completed such a collection. [2]
- **2002:** *Comment vivre ensemble*. Claude Coste’s established and annotated edition of the 1976–1977 course and seminar notes; it manifests the teaching work but does not convert it retrospectively into an author-finished book. [2]
- **2002:** *Le Neutre*. Thomas Clerc’s established and annotated edition of the 1977–1978 course notes. [2]
- **2003:** *La Préparation du roman I et II*. Nathalie Léger’s edition of the 1978–1980 course materials; despite its title, it is neither the projected novel nor proof that such a novel existed. [2]
- **2007:** *Le Discours amoureux: séminaire à l’École pratique des hautes études (1974–1976)*. A posthumous reconstruction of the seminar, supplemented with unpublished pages omitted from *Fragments d’un discours amoureux*. [2]
- **2009:** *Carnets du voyage en Chine*. Anne Herschberg Pierrot’s edition of the 1974 travel notebooks; the editorial book and the notebooks themselves are distinct objects. [2]
- **2009:** *Journal de deuil*. Nathalie Léger’s edition of Barthes’s mourning cards. Its transformation of separate slips into a public book produced substantial disagreement about privacy, intention, and the limits of the oeuvre. [2][5]
- **2010:** *Le Lexique de l’auteur*. Anne Herschberg Pierrot’s edition of the 1973–1974 seminar, joined to unpublished fragments from *Roland Barthes par Roland Barthes*; it is deliberately composite. [2]
- **2011:** *“Sarrasine” de Balzac*. Claude Coste and Andy Stafford’s edition of the 1967–1969 EPHE seminars; it records a teaching process related to but not identical with *S/Z*. [2]
- **2015:** *Album: inédits, correspondances et varia*. Éric Marty’s selection of unpublished writings, letters, and miscellaneous documents. It is an archival anthology whose selection and arrangement are editorial, not a final book Barthes left behind. [6]
