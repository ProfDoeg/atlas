## Works by Jiddu Krishnamurti

### Authored books, journals, poems, and statements

- **1910:** *At the Feet of the Master*. A Theosophical instruction book issued under the name “Alcyone”; Krishnamurti’s authorship has long been disputed, and the work should therefore be treated as attributed rather than securely authored.
- **1912:** *Education as Service*. A second instructional book credited to Alcyone; as with *At the Feet of the Master*, biographers have questioned how much of it Krishnamurti actually composed.
- **1921:** *Lecture Delivered at Benares on December 28th, 1921*. A public address subsequently printed in the *Herald of the Star*; it is a performed talk preserved as text, not a book conceived as such.
- **1923–1931:** *Poems, prose pieces, and parables later collected as From Darkness to Light*. More than sixty poems appeared in Order of the Star periodicals and collections; the later anthology is editorial, while the individual writings are Krishnamurti’s early literary production.
- **1928:** *The Immortal Friend*. A collection of poems from Krishnamurti’s messianic and Theosophical period.
- **1928:** *The Pool of Wisdom*. A small composite publication containing addresses and poems; its heterogeneous contents resist classification as a single continuous book.
- **1928:** *Life in Freedom*. Campfire addresses delivered at Benares, Ojai, and Ommen and subsequently made into a book; primarily an oral work in printed form.
- **3 August 1929:** *The Dissolution of the Order of the Star: A Statement*. A manifesto in the strongest sense: the speech did not merely discuss the Order but performed its dissolution. It was also issued as a fourteen-page pamphlet.
- **1929:** *India’s Aspirations*. A political and philosophical article first printed in the *International Star Bulletin* and reprinted in *The Nation and Athenaeum* in 1930.
- **1931:** *The Song of Life*. Krishnamurti’s final published collection of poetry.
- **1947–1948:** *A New Approach to Living; The Way of Living; The Way of Peace*. Three radio addresses broadcast by All India Radio and issued together as a sixteen-page pamphlet; the made things were broadcasts before they became print.
- **1953:** *Education and the Significance of Life*. An expository book on education and Krishnamurti’s first book issued by a commercial publisher.
- **1956:** *Commentaries on Living, Series One*. A deliberately written hybrid of landscape description, reflection, and reconstructed encounters with visitors, prepared from Krishnamurti’s notebooks and edited by D. Rajagopal.
- **1958:** *Commentaries on Living, Series Two*. The second volume of the same written, cross-generic project.
- **1960:** *Commentaries on Living, Series Three*. The third and concluding volume of the project.
- **June 1961–March 1962:** *Krishnamurti’s Notebook*. A dated manuscript journal recording perceptions, states of consciousness, and the painful phenomenon Krishnamurti called “the process”; published in 1976, it is one of the few major books he wrote rather than spoke.
- **1973–1975:** *Krishnamurti’s Journal*. An intermittent handwritten journal combining observations of nature with reflection; published in 1982.
- **1978–1985:** *Letters to the Schools*. A sequence of letters addressed to Krishnamurti schools, written as an educational intervention rather than private correspondence; twenty-seven appeared in two volumes in 1981 and 1985, and seventeen additional letters were published posthumously in *The Whole Movement of Life Is Learning*.
- **21 October 1980:** *The Core of the Teachings*. A concise written statement prepared at Mary Lutyens’s request; it is a retrospective declaration of the continuity of Krishnamurti’s teaching and functions partly as a manifesto.
- **1984:** *Intent of the Krishnamurti Schools*. A distributed statement of educational intention. It refuses the form of a curriculum or protocol: Krishnamurti explicitly left no instructional blueprint or authorized interpretation.
- **February 1983–March 1984:** *Krishnamurti to Himself: His Last Journal*. Spoken reflections recorded by Krishnamurti at Pine Cottage in Ojai and edited from tape by Mary Lutyens after his death; it is an oral journal, not an autograph text.

### Talks and broadcasts made into books

- **1940:** *War Abolished: One Way to Permanent Peace*. Talks delivered in Ojai and Pennsylvania, rearranged by L. L. Woolacott into a chaptered pamphlet published in 1943; the resulting textual object is partly editorial.
- **1945–1946:** *Authentic Report of Sixteen Talks Given in 1945 & 1946*. Stenographic records of two Ojai series, issued in 1947 with a short preface by Krishnamurti.
- **Published 1954:** *The First and Last Freedom*. A thematic book assembled from talks and question-and-answer sessions, with a substantial foreword by Aldous Huxley; it is an edited textual manifestation of oral work.
- **Talks given 1948–1960; published 1964:** *This Matter of Culture*. Talks and discussions with young people, edited by D. Rajagopal; republished in 1970 as *Think on These Things*, which is another title and edition of substantially the same work.
- **Published 1969:** *Freedom from the Known*. Mary Lutyens’s continuous thematic arrangement of passages from numerous talks; the prose is Krishnamurti’s, but the book-form and sequence are editorial.
- **Published 1970:** *The Only Revolution*. An edited alternation of talks, dialogue, and landscape passages prepared by Mary Lutyens.
- **Published 1970:** *The Urgency of Change*. A book-length question-and-answer exchange in which Alain Naudé supplied the questions; the dialogic object is jointly occasioned even though Krishnamurti’s answers dominate it.
- **1968–1969:** *You Are the World*. Verbatim reports of talks and discussions at Brandeis, Berkeley, Santa Cruz, and Stanford, published in 1972.
- **Talks given 1970; published 1973:** *Beyond Violence*. Edited public talks and discussions from Santa Monica, San Diego, London, Brockwood Park, and Rome.
- **Published 1972:** *The Impossible Question*. Edited talks and discussions; the named book is a later container for distinct oral events.
- **Published 1973:** *The Awakening of Intelligence*. A large editorial constellation of talks and dialogues with Jacob Needleman, Alain Naudé, Swami Venkatesananda, David Bohm, and others, edited by Cornelia and George Wingfield Digby.
- **Published 1974:** *Krishnamurti on Education*. Talks and discussions with students and teachers at Rishi Valley and Rajghat; it records an educational practice rather than setting out a protocol.
- **Published 1975:** *Beginnings of Learning*. Edited discussions with Brockwood Park students and staff.
- **Published 1979:** *Meditations*. Evelyne Blau’s compilation of passages on meditation; this is a post-performance selection rather than a discrete composition by Krishnamurti.
- **1981:** *The Network of Thought*. Talks delivered at Saanen and Amsterdam and published in 1982.
- **Published 1984:** *The Flame of Attention*. A book made from public talks delivered in India, Europe, and the United States; the title and grouping belong to publication rather than to a single performance.
- **Published 1985:** *The Way of Intelligence*. A compilation of talks and discussions, including exchanges with scientists and Buddhist scholars.
- **1985:** *Last Talks at Saanen*. Krishnamurti’s final Saanen talks, published posthumously in 1987.
- **1985:** *Washington, D.C., Talks*. Two public addresses delivered in April 1985 and published posthumously in 1988.
- **1985–1986:** *The Future Is Now: Last Talks in India*. Edited versions of Krishnamurti’s final Indian talks and discussions, published posthumously in 1988; its component events, rather than the later book, are the works he directly made.

### Dialogues and collaborative inquiries

- **February 1974:** *A Wholly Different Way of Living*. Eighteen recorded conversations with religious-studies scholar Allan W. Anderson, conceived for audio and television and later published as a book; it is a multimedia dialogue rather than solely a text.
- **May 1975:** *Truth and Actuality*. Dialogues with David Bohm at Brockwood Park, first published in 1977; Krishnamurti and Bohm are joint makers of the conversational event.
- **1975:** *The Limits of Thought*. Seven discussions with Bohm, published as a collection only in 1999; the posthumous book-title should not be mistaken for an independently composed work.
- **1976:** *The Transformation of Man*. Two filmed conversations among Krishnamurti, David Bohm, and psychiatrist David Shainberg. An abridged textual manifestation appeared in *The Wholeness of Life* and was later republished under the film’s title.
- **Late 1960s–mid-1980s:** *Dialogues with Pupul Jayakar and associates*. Recurring informal inquiries in India, selections of which became *Exploration into Insight* and *Fire in the Mind*. The boundaries and titles are editorial; the underlying obra is an extended, collaborative practice of dialogue.
- **Late 1970s:** *Dialogues with Buddhists*. Five discussions with Walpola Rahula, Irmgard Schloegl, David Bohm, Phiroz Mehta, and others, published posthumously as *Can Humanity Change?*; authorship is intrinsically plural.
- **1980:** *The Ending of Time*. Thirteen conversations with David Bohm at Ojai and Brockwood Park; recordings and the edited 1985 book are different manifestations of the same collaborative inquiry.
- **June 1983:** *The Future of Humanity*. Two recorded dialogues with David Bohm, released on video and as a 1986 book.
- **March 1984:** *Krishnamurti at Los Alamos*. Participation in a symposium on creativity in science at Los Alamos National Laboratory; the work is an event preserved in proceedings and video, not a sole-authored treatise.
- **1984:** *Knowledge Is Conditioning*. The first of three filmed conversations with Mary Zimbalist at Brockwood Park.
- **1984:** *Thought and Time Are the Cause of Fear*. The second filmed conversation with Zimbalist.
- **1984:** *Religion Is an Inquiry*. The third filmed conversation, with Zimbalist and Ray McCoy.
- **1985:** *Why Don’t You Listen?*. The first of two filmed small-group discussions with Mary Zimbalist and Scott Forbes; the standard bibliography does not supply a stable title for the second discussion, so none is invented here.
- **Published 1996:** *Questioning Krishnamurti*. An editorial collection of encounters with Iris Murdoch, Jonas Salk, David Bohm, Walpola Rahula, Pupul Jayakar, Bernard Levin, Huston Smith, Chögyam Trungpa, and others; it is not a single work made under that title during Krishnamurti’s lifetime.

### Recorded performances

- **1928 and 1930:** *Film Clips: New York 1928, Ojai 1930*. Two short films from uncredited sources, one apparently a newsreel and the other a filmed restatement of the Order-dissolution position. Because Krishnamurti did not control the filming or surviving compilation, these are records of his performance rather than securely attributable films by him.
- **1966:** *The Real Revolution*. Krishnamurti’s first full-length talks recorded on video, edited into half-hour television programs for WNDT; the televised program is a collaborative production.
- **6 February 1969:** *True Revolution*. A Berkeley talk released in limited circulation as a Pacifica Radio spoken-word album.
- **1982:** *The Nature of Love*. A filmed Ojai talk, also circulated as *Krishnamurti: On the Nature of Love*.
- **1984:** *Third Discussion with Students at Rishi Valley*. A five-part filmed school discussion; its numerical title reveals that it belongs to a larger event-series rather than standing alone.
- **4 January 1986:** *Third Public Talk at Madras*. Krishnamurti’s last public talk, later edited into *The Future Is Now*.
- **Recorded talks and discussions, chiefly mid-1940s–1986:** *The audio corpus*. Hundreds of complete talks, dialogues, interviews, and school discussions survive on wire, tape, and later media. This corpus resists a finite work-list: each recording preserves an event, later extracts and commercial releases frequently retitle it, and archival cataloguing remains in progress.
- **Recorded talks and discussions, 1960s–1986:** *The film and video corpus*. The foundations preserve the complete set of Krishnamurti videos for which they hold copyright, including approximately thirty-five early film reels and extensive later videotape. These are audiovisual manifestations of live inquiry, not films authored by Krishnamurti in the ordinary directorial sense.
- **1930s–1950s:** *The stenographers’ typescripts*. More than 35,000 pages preserve talks delivered before comprehensive audio recording. They are indispensable manifestations of Krishnamurti’s oral work, but the stenographers’ mediation prevents them from being treated as autograph writings.

### Collected and editorial manifestations

- **1927–1933; collected 1969–1972:** *Early Writings Series*. Seven volumes gathering writings, talks, and pamphlets originally issued by the Star Publishing Trust; the series is a retrospective editorial construction.
- **1923–1931; collected 1980:** *From Darkness to Light*. Poetry, prose, and parables from the early period. Its subtitle, *The Collected Works of Krishnamurti, Volume One*, is unrelated to the later seventeen-volume *Collected Works*.
- **1933–1934; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 1: The Art of Listening*. Previously published talks and discussions; the volume-title is editorial.
- **1934–1935; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 2: What Is the Right Action?*. A chronological documentary volume, not a book composed under this title.
- **1936–1944; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 3: The Mirror of Relationship*. A chronological compilation spanning otherwise separate speaking occasions.
- **1945–1948; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 4: The Observer Is the Observed*. An edited archival container for talks and discussions.
- **1948–1949; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 5: Choiceless Awareness*. An edited archival container.
- **1949–1952; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 6: The Origin of Conflict*. An edited archival container.
- **1952–1953; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 7: Tradition and Creativity*. An edited archival container.
- **1953–1955; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 8: What Are You Seeking?*. An edited archival container.
- **1955–1956; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 9: The Answer Is in the Problem*. An edited archival container.
- **1956–1957; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 10: A Light to Yourself*. An edited archival container.
- **1958–1960; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 11: Crisis in Consciousness*. An edited archival container.
- **1961; collected 1991:** *The Collected Works, Volume 12: There Is No Thinker, Only Thought*. An edited archival container.
- **1962–1963; collected 1992:** *The Collected Works, Volume 13: A Psychological Revolution*. An edited archival container.
- **1963–1964; collected 1992:** *The Collected Works, Volume 14: The New Mind*. An edited archival container.
- **1964–1965; collected 1992:** *The Collected Works, Volume 15: The Dignity of Living*. An edited archival container.
- **1965–1966; collected 1992:** *The Collected Works, Volume 16: The Beauty of Death*. An edited archival container.
- **1966–1967; collected 1992:** *The Collected Works, Volume 17: Perennial Questions*. The last published volume of this chronological series.
- **Projected coverage 1910–1986:** *The Complete Works of J. Krishnamurti / Complete Teachings Project*. A foundation-led project intended to integrate the entire surviving corpus, once estimated at more than fifty print volumes. It remained unfinished in the cited bibliographic account, so its incompleteness is part of the work’s status.
- **Published 1991; expanded 1999:** *The Krishnamurti Text Collection and Index*. A searchable CD-ROM described as containing the published textual corpus from 1933 through 1986, with seventy additional transcripts in the later version; it is a digital scholarly apparatus and manifestation, not a new Krishnamurti composition.
- **Published 1995:** *The Book of Life*. An editorially arranged sequence of 365 passages from earlier talks and writings.
- **Published 1996:** *Total Freedom: The Essential Krishnamurti*. A posthumous anthology edited by Mary Cadogan, Alan Kishbaugh, Mark Lee, and Ray McCoy.
- **Published 1997:** *Krishnamurti: Reflections on the Self*. Raymond Martin’s posthumous thematic selection.
- **Published 2000:** *To Be Human*. David Skitt’s posthumous thematic selection.
- **Published 2004:** *The First Step Is the Last Step*. A re-edited manifestation of talks previously published as *Krishnamurti in India, 1970–71*.
- **Published 2005:** *Facing a World in Crisis*. David Skitt’s posthumous selection from talks and discussions.
- **Published 2007:** *The Whole Movement of Life Is Learning*. Ray McCoy’s edition of the school letters, combining the two lifetime volumes with seventeen previously unpublished letters.
- **Compiled from 1933–1986 material:** *The thematic “On…” books*. Foundation editors produced volumes on conflict, education, fear, freedom, God, living and dying, learning and knowledge, love and loneliness, mind and thought, nature and the environment, and relationship. These are useful manifestations of the archive but resist attribution as separately conceived works by Krishnamurti.

### Educational and institutional projects

- **1928:** *Rishi Valley School*. An educational project established by Krishnamurti and associates in Andhra Pradesh. It is an evolving institution rather than a finished object, and Krishnamurti supplied an intention rather than a fixed pedagogical protocol.
- **1934:** *Rajghat Besant School and the Rajghat educational centre*. A school and associated educational-social project at Varanasi, created with collaborators and later administered institutionally.
- **1968:** *Krishnamurti Foundation Trust*. The British foundation established to preserve and disseminate the work and to create a school; Krishnamurti emphasized that it was administrative rather than a body possessing spiritual authority.
- **1969:** *Brockwood Park School*. Krishnamurti’s English educational project and a continuing site for dialogue among students, teachers, and visitors.
- **1969:** *Krishnamurti Foundation of America*. One of four foundations established by Krishnamurti to preserve and disseminate the teachings and support educational work.
- **1970:** *Krishnamurti Foundation India*. The successor body charged with educational, archival, and publishing work in India.
- **Early 1970s:** *The Spanish/Latin American Krishnamurti foundation*. The fourth principal foundation, ultimately based in Spain; like the others, it was designed as a practical custodial instrument, not a religious organization.
- **1973:** *The School–KFI, Chennai*. A foundation school emerging from discussions about extending Krishnamurti’s educational work; its realization was collective.
- **1975:** *Oak Grove School*. Founded by Krishnamurti and the KFA trustees in Ojai as part of the international school project.
- **1978:** *The Valley School*. A Bengaluru educational project founded under Krishnamurti’s aegis. Its daily institutional form was made by teachers and trustees as well as by Krishnamurti.
- **Across his lifetime:** *The teachings*. Krishnamurti resisted treating these as a doctrine, system, method, or proprietary philosophical work. They are best classified as an obra dispersed across live encounters, handwritten records, broadcasts, collaborative dialogues, schools, and archives—a body of work that deliberately refuses separation into authoritative text and prescribed practice.
