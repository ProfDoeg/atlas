## Works by Alexandre Dumas

### Dramatic works

- **1822:** *Ivanhoé*. Unpublished stage play during Dumas’s lifetime; first printed in 1974.
- **1825:** *La Chasse et l’Amour*. Vaudeville, written with Adolphe de Leuven and Pierre-Joseph Rousseau.
- **1826:** *La Noce et l’Enterrement*. Vaudeville, written with Adolphe de Leuven and Lassagne.
- **1828:** *Fiesque de Lavagna*. Stage play left unpublished until 1976.
- **1829:** *Henri III et sa cour*. Historical drama and a foundational manifestation of French Romantic theatre.
- **1829:** *La Cour du roi Pétaud*. Theatrical parody of *Henri III et sa cour*.
- **1830:** *Christine, ou Stockholm, Fontainebleau et Rome*. Historical drama.
- **1831:** *Napoléon Bonaparte, ou Trente Ans de l’histoire de France*. Historical drama.
- **1831:** *Antony*. Contemporary prose drama.
- **1831:** *Charles VII chez ses grands vassaux*. Verse tragedy.
- **1831–1832:** *Richard Darlington*. Drama written with Dinaux, the collective pseudonym of Prosper Goubaux and Jacques-François Beudin.
- **1832:** *Térésa*. Drama whose collaborative authorship is associated with Anicet-Bourgeois.
- **1832:** *Le Mari de la veuve*. Comedy.
- **1832:** *La Tour de Nesle*. Historical drama substantially entangled with Frédéric Gaillardet’s prior scenario and with later litigation over authorship.
- **1832:** *Le Fils de l’émigré*. Play left unpublished until 1995.
- **1833–1834:** *Angèle*. Drama written with Anicet-Bourgeois.
- **1834:** *La Vénitienne*. Drama associated with Anicet-Bourgeois as collaborator.
- **1834:** *Catherine Howard*. Historical drama.
- **1834:** *La Tour de Babel*. Drama.
- **1835:** *Cromwell et Charles Ier*. Historical drama.
- **1836:** *Le Marquis de Brunoy*. Drama.
- **1836:** *Don Juan de Marana*. Theatrical “mystery.”
- **1836:** *Kean, ou Désordre et Génie*. Drama based on the actor Edmund Kean’s life.
- **1837–1838:** *Caligula*. Five-act verse tragedy.
- **1838:** *Paul Jones*. Drama later remade by Dumas as a novel.
- **1838:** *Le Bourgeois de Gand, ou le Secrétaire du duc d’Albe*. Historical drama.
- **1839:** *Bathilde*. Drama.
- **1839:** *Mademoiselle de Belle-Isle*. Comedy.
- **1839:** *L’Alchimiste*. Drama.
- **1839:** *Léo Burckart*. Drama whose authorship is collaborative, with Gérard de Nerval.
- **1840:** *Jarvis l’honnête homme, ou Le Marchand de Londres*. Drama.
- **1841:** *Un mariage sous Louis XV*. Comedy.
- **1841:** *Jeannic le Breton, ou le Gérant responsable*. Drama associated with Anicet-Bourgeois as collaborator.
- **1842:** *Lorenzino*. Historical drama later reworked as the novel *Une nuit à Florence sous Alexandre de Médicis*.
- **1842:** *Le Séducteur et le Mari*. Comedy.
- **1842:** *Halifax*. Comedy.
- **1842:** *Le Mariage au tambour*. Vaudeville.
- **1843:** *Les Demoiselles de Saint-Cyr*. Comedy.
- **1843:** *Louise Bernard*. Drama.
- **1843:** *L’École des princes*. Drama.
- **1844:** *Le Laird de Dumbiky*. Comedy.
- **1845:** *Le Garde-forestier*. Drama.
- **1845:** *Un conte de fées*. Vaudeville.
- **1846:** *Échec et mat*. Comedy.
- **1847:** *Intrigue et Amour*. Adaptation of Schiller’s drama rather than an autonomous original plot.
- **1848:** *Catilina*. Historical tragedy.
- **1848:** *Hamlet, prince de Danemark*. Stage adaptation made with Paul Meurice from Shakespeare.
- **1848:** *Le Cachemire vert*. Comedy-vaudeville.
- **1849:** *La Jeunesse des mousquetaires*. Dramatic reworking of the opening portion of the Musketeers cycle.
- **1850:** *Urbain Grandier*. Dramatic adaptation of Dumas’s historical narrative.
- **1850:** *Pauline*. Dramatic adaptation of the novel.
- **1850:** *Les Frères corses*. Dramatic adaptation of the novella.
- **1850:** *La Chasse au chastre*. Dramatic adaptation of Dumas’s story.
- **1852:** *Benvenuto Cellini*. Drama derived from *Ascanio*.
- **1853:** *Le Marbrier*. Comedy.
- **1854:** *La Conscience*. Drama.
- **1854:** *La Jeunesse de Louis XIV*. Comedy.
- **1854:** *Romulus*. Comedy.
- **1856:** *L’Orestie*. Tragic adaptation after Aeschylus.
- **1856:** *La Tour Saint-Jacques*. Drama.
- **1856:** *Le Fils de la nuit, ou le Pirate*. Drama.
- **1856:** *Le Verrou de la reine*. Comedy.
- **1857:** *L’Invitation à la valse*. Comedy.
- **1858:** *L’Honneur est satisfait*. Comedy.
- **1858:** *Les Forestiers*. Dramatic adaptation of *Catherine Blum*, also known as *Les Gardes forestiers*.
- **1860:** *L’Envers d’une conspiration*. Comedy.
- **1860:** *Le Gentilhomme de la montagne*. Dramatic adaptation of *El Salteador*.
- **1860:** *La Dame de Monsoreau*. Dramatic adaptation of the novel.
- **1861:** *Le Prisonnier de la Bastille, fin des mousquetaires*. Dramatic continuation and conclusion of Dumas’s stage Musketeers.
- **1863:** *La Veillée allemande*. Stage work.
- **1864:** *Roméo et Juliette*. Adaptation left unpublished until 1976.
- **1864:** *Les Voleurs d’or*. Play recovered in 2002.
- **1864:** *Les Mohicans de Paris*. Dramatic adaptation of the novel.
- **1865:** *Les Deux Diane*. Dramatic adaptation of Paul Meurice’s Dumas-signed novel.
- **1866:** *Gabriel Lambert, le bagnard de l’Opéra*. Dramatic adaptation of Dumas’s novel.
- **1868:** *Valentin-Valentine*. A play by Max de Bourdon substantially retouched by Dumas; he refused to sign it, so its resistance to inclusion as a Dumas work is explicit.
- **1869:** *Madame de Chamblay*. Dramatic adaptation of *Ainsi soit-il !*

### Opera, oratorio, and other musical texts

- **1837:** *Piquillo*. Opéra-comique libretto written with Gérard de Nerval, with music by Hippolyte Monpou.
- **1843:** *La Belle Isabeau*. Poetic tale made into a song by Hector Berlioz: a text intended for musical manifestation rather than simply a printed poem.
- **1846:** *Jeanne d’Arc au bûcher*. Dramatic verse scene set to music by Franz Liszt.
- **1855–1856:** *Samson*. Oratorio text, only partly published; the manuscript is lost, so the work survives incompletely.
- **1858:** *Thaïs, ou La Bacchante*. Opéra-comique with music by Eugène Gautier; the score and manuscript burned at the Salle Favart, though a copy of the libretto survives.
- **1860:** *Le Roman d’Elvire*. Opéra-comique libretto, with music by Ambroise Thomas.

### Translations, rewritings, and appropriated narratives

- **1839:** *Jacques Ortis*. French translation of Ugo Foscolo’s epistolary novel, probably assisted by Pier-Angelo Fiorentino.
- **1854:** *Saphir, pierre précieuse montée par Alex. Dumas*. Reworked collection drawn from Moïse Saphir; its title openly describes Dumas as the “setter,” not originator, of the material.
- **c. 1856–1864:** *Ivanhoé*. Translation or adaptation of Walter Scott, distinct from Dumas’s 1822 play of the same name.
- **1857:** *L’Homme aux contes*. Adapted children’s stories after Grimm, Andersen, and Alfred Henry Forrester.
- **1858:** *Mémoires d’un policeman*. Nine translated British detective stories credited to “Perceval” and “published by Alexandre Dumas”; Dumas may have rewritten them, so this resists classification as his authored work.
- **1858–1859:** *Marianna*. Adaptation after Pushkin.
- **1858–1859:** *Le Chasse-neige*. Adaptation after Pushkin.
- **1858–1859:** *Un coup de feu*. Adaptation after Pushkin.
- **1858–1859:** *Le Faiseur de cercueil*. Adaptation after Pushkin.
- **1858–1859:** *La Maison de glace*. Adaptation after Ivan Lazhechnikov.
- **1859–1863:** *La Frégate l’Espérance*, later *La Princesse Flora*. Adaptation after Alexandre Bestuzhev-Marlinsky.
- **1859:** *Jane*. Adaptation after Bestuzhev-Marlinsky.
- **1859–1862:** *Moullah-Nour*, later *La Boule de neige*. Adaptation after Bestuzhev-Marlinsky.
- **1859–1862:** *Ammalat-Beg*, later *Sultanetta*. Adaptation after Bestuzhev-Marlinsky.
- **1860:** *Le Père Gigogne, contes pour les enfants*. Collection of adapted children’s tales.
- **1860:** *La Vie au désert: Cinq Ans de chasse dans l’intérieur de l’Afrique méridionale*. Bénédict Révoil’s translation from Gordon Cumming, revised by Dumas; it is editorial collaboration rather than an original Dumas narrative.
- **1868–1872:** *Le Capitaine Rhino*. Adaptation of an English story, serialized in Dumas’s newspaper and collected posthumously.
- **1868–1872:** *Le Lion père de famille*. Adaptation of an English story, published with *Le Capitaine Rhino*.

### Travel books made from other people’s journeys

- **1838:** *Quinze jours au Sinaï*. Travel book made with Adrien Dauzats from a journey Dumas did not take; authorship and experience therefore do not coincide.
- **1852:** *Un Gil Blas en Californie*, also *Californie: un an sur les bords du San Joaquin et du Sacramento*. Travel narrative made with Bénédict-Henry Révoil.
- **1854–1856:** *Pèlerinage de Hadji-abd-el-Hamid-Bey*. Travel book made from Louis du Couret’s journeys.
- **1855:** *Marie Giovanni, journal d’une Parisienne en voyage*. Travel narrative made from Marie Callégari’s journey; her identity was not established publicly until 1979.
- **1857–1860:** *L’Arabie heureuse: souvenirs de voyages en Afrique et en Asie par Hajji ‘Abd al-Hamid Bey*. Continuation made from Louis du Couret’s material.
- **1858:** *Les Baleiniers: Voyage aux terres antipodiques*. Travel book made from Dr Félix Maynard’s journal.

- **1831:** *La Vendée après le 29 juillet*. Political-historical pamphlet.
- **1833:** *Gaule et France*. Historical essay.
- **1836:** *Guelfes et Gibelins*. Historical study.
- **1838:** *Murat*. Historical biography later incorporated into *Crimes célèbres*.
- **1839–1840:** *Napoléon*. Historical biography.

- **1841:** *Histoire du 23e régiment d’infanterie de ligne*. Regimental history in the commissioned *Armée française* project.
- **1842:** *Jehanne la pucelle (1429–1431)*. Historical study later incorporated into *Jeanne d’Arc*.
- **1843:** *Jeanne d’Arc*. Historical biography incorporating the earlier *Jehanne la pucelle*.
- **1843:** *Filles, Lorettes et Courtisanes*. Social-historical portraits.
- **1843:** *Histoire du 2e régiment d’infanterie légère*. Regimental history.
- **1844–1845:** *Louis XIV et son siècle*. First large unit of *Histoire de deux siècles*.
- **1845:** *Les Médicis*. Dynastic and art-historical work derived from *La Galerie de Florence*.

- **1849:** *Louis XV*. Historical volume in *Histoire de deux siècles*.
- **1850–1851:** *Louis XVI et la Révolution*, also *Histoire de Louis XVI et de Marie-Antoinette*. Historical volume.
- **1851–1852:** *Le Drame de quatre-vingt-treize: Scènes de la vie révolutionnaire*. Revolutionary history.
- **1852:** *Le Dernier Roi des Français, ou Histoire de la vie politique et privée de Louis-Philippe*. Political biography.
- **1855:** *Henri IV*. Historical portrait in *Les Grands Hommes en robe de chambre*.
- **1855:** *César*. Historical portrait in *Les Grands Hommes en robe de chambre*.
- **1855–1856:** *Louis XIII et Richelieu*. Historical portrait in *Les Grands Hommes en robe de chambre*.
- **1860–1861:** *Mémoires de Garibaldi*. Dumas’s edited French presentation of Garibaldi’s manuscript: translation, political intervention, and mediated autobiography rather than a wholly authored Dumas work.
- **1861–1863:** *Naples et ses provinces*. Historical-political serial whose Italian manifestation contains three chapters not present in French.
- **1862:** *Ali Pacha*. Unfinished Italian chronicle, first translated completely into French in 2009.
- **1862–1863:** *Les Bourbons de Naples*. Historical-political serial, chiefly published in Italian and translated fully into French only in 2012.
- **1862:** *De l’origine du brigandage, des causes de sa persistance et des moyens de le détruire*. Italian-language political brochure functioning partly as diagnosis and partly as proposed protocol.
- **1863:** *De Naples à Rome*. Italian-language political and travel chronicle, unpublished in French during the period covered by the consulted bibliography.

### Art, artists, and theatrical criticism

- **1839:** *Le Tasse*. Biographical-critical study.
- **1841–1844:** *La Galerie de Florence*. Italian-published art-historical project later redistributed among several French books; its shifting containers make “the work” difficult to identify with one edition.
- **1843:** *Un Alchimiste au dix-neuvième siècle*. Biography of Henri de Ruolz, first serving as a preface to *La Villa Palmieri*.
- **1845:** *Michel-Ange et Raphaël Sanzio*. Conclusion of *La Galerie de Florence*.
- **1849–1850:** *Mémoires de J.-F. Talma*. Talma’s papers collected and arranged by Dumas: an editorially made autobiography, not Dumas’s own memoir.
- **1854:** *Une vie d’artiste: aventures et tribulations d’un comédien*. Biography of Étienne Mélingue.
- **1855:** *La Dernière Année de Marie Dorval*. Biographical memorial.
- **1859:** *L’Art et les artistes contemporains au Salon de 1859*. Art criticism.
- **1861:** *Italiens et Flamands*. Reconfigured portions of *La Galerie de Florence*, with new material on Giulio Romano.
- **1861:** *Trois Maîtres*. Art-critical volume derived from the earlier Florence project.
- **1865:** *Causerie sur Eugène Delacroix et ses œuvres*. Memorial art criticism.
- **1867:** *À propos de l’art dramatique*. Theatrical essay.
- **1867–1868:** *Souvenirs dramatiques*. Collection of theatrical and literary criticism, much of it reassembled from Dumas’s journalism of 1836–1838 and later periodicals.

### Memoir, conversation, and miscellaneous prose

- **1833:** *La Vendée et Madame*. Political-historical recollection.
- **1833:** *Ma Jeunesse*. Autobiographical text.
- **1833:** *Comment je devins auteur dramatique*. Autobiographical account of artistic formation.
- **1852–1854:** *Mes Mémoires*. Expansive autobiography ending before Dumas’s later life and therefore incomplete as a life narrative.
- **1854–1855:** *Souvenirs de 1830 à 1842*. Final continuation of *Mes Mémoires*.
- **1854–1857:** *Causeries*. Periodical conversations and anecdotes collected in book form in 1857.
- **1858–1860:** *La Route de Varennes*. Historical journey and investigative chronicle.
- **1858–1867:** *Histoire de mes bêtes*. Animal memoir collection, incomplete in its Belgian manifestation.
- **1860:** *Les Fous du docteur Miraglia*. Recollection or anecdotal narrative.
- **1861:** *Les Morts vont vite*. Memorial and anecdotal collection.
- **1861:** *Bric-à-brac*. Two-volume assemblage of autobiographical notes, articles, tales, and conversations; its miscellaneity is constitutive rather than accidental.
- **1863:** *Lettres sur la cuisine à un prétendu gourmand napolitain*. Epistolary culinary essays.
- **1864:** *La Chasse*. Journal article or essay rather than an autonomous book.
- **1864:** *Le Pays natal*. Recollection.
- **1864:** *Les Serpents*. Journal article later absorbed into a posthumous collection; treating it as a separate work remains debatable.
- **1866:** *Madame Lafarge*. Serial account of a celebrated criminal case, not collected until 2005.
- **1866:** *Causeries familières*. Conversational prose collection.
- **1867–1868:** *Histoire d’un lézard: Souvenirs de Naples*. Neapolitan recollection.
- **1868:** *Causeries sur la mer*. Maritime conversational essays.

### Journalism and publishing projects

- **1848:** *Le Mois*. Political newspaper founded and largely written by Dumas; this is a serial publishing project and collective manifestation, not one stable text.
- **1853–1857:** *Le Mousquetaire*. Literary daily founded, directed, and extensively filled by Dumas; individual unsigned or collaborative contributions make its boundary as his “work” porous.
- **1857:** *Le Monte-Cristo*. Periodical founded by Dumas.
- **1857–1860:** *Le Monte-Cristo*. Dumas’s weekly literary journal and vehicle for fiction, memoir, and causeries; its contents overlap later books.
- **1860–1864:** *L’Indipendente*. Italian newspaper founded and directed by Dumas in Naples as an instrument of Garibaldian politics: both publishing enterprise and political action.
- **1868:** *Le d’Artagnan*. Newspaper founded by Dumas; a short-lived publishing project rather than a single authored text.

### Architecture and theatrical enterprises

- **1846–1847:** *Château de Monte-Cristo*. Dumas commissioned and closely shaped this residence and writing retreat from architect Hippolyte Durand; it is an architectural obra of patronage and self-mythology, not a text.
- **1847–1850:** *Théâtre-Historique*. Theatre founded by Dumas to manifest historical drama on stage; institution, building, repertory project, and near-Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single literary work.

### Culinary obra

- **1869–1873:** *Grand Dictionnaire de cuisine*. Monumental culinary encyclopedia drafted near the end of Dumas’s life and edited posthumously by Alphonse Lemerre and Anatole France; its posthumous shaping means that the surviving book is both Dumas’s project and an editorial manifestation.
- **1878:** *Petit Dictionnaire de cuisine*. Posthumous abridgment derived from the *Grand Dictionnaire*; it is not an independently made Dumas work, though bibliographies often list it under his name.
- **1877:** *Propos d’art et de cuisine*. Posthumous retitling of the second volume of *Bric-à-brac*, not a newly made work.

### Posthumous manifestations and disputed attributions

- **1846:** *Les Deux Diane*. Novel written by Paul Meurice and issued under Dumas’s name; Dumas publicly repudiated authorship in 1865, so it belongs to the history of his œuvre-signature but not securely to what he made.
- **1854:** *Vie et Aventures de la princesse de Monaco*. Novel probably written entirely by the Comtesse Dash.
- **1855–1857:** *Madame du Deffand*. Comtesse Dash novel serialized and issued under Dumas’s name, later divided as *Les Confessions de la marquise* and *Mémoires d’une aveugle*.
- **1855–1864:** *La Dame de volupté*. Comtesse Dash novel first called *La Comtesse de Verrue, ou La Dame des Luynes*; it is distinct in authorship from Dumas’s reuse of *Maison de Savoie* material despite overlapping subjects and titles.
- **1864:** *Les Deux Reines*. Sequel by the Comtesse Dash, not Dumas.
- **1865:** *Un pays inconnu*. Reissue of *Les Aztecs*, actually a translation by Bénédict Révoil; the Dumas attribution is not authorially sustainable.
- **1856:** *Un cadet de famille, ou Mémoires d’un jeune cadet*. Translation of Edward John Trelawny’s *Adventures of a Younger Son*, undertaken at Dumas’s direction rather than written by him.
- **1856:** *De Victor-Amédée III à Charles-Albert*. Fourth volume appended to *La Maison de Savoie* but not written by Dumas and subsequently excluded from his works.
- **1946:** *Le Sphinx rouge*. Posthumous book manifestation of *Le Comte de Moret*, not a separate composition.
- **2005:** *Le Chevalier de Sainte-Hermine*. Claude Schopp’s posthumous reconstruction of Dumas’s unfinished serial; the project is Dumas’s, while the completed book-object is partly editorial.
- **2006:** *Mémoires d’Horace*. First book manifestation of the 1860 serial, not a newly discovered composition.
- **2013:** *L’Assassinat de la rue Saint-Roch*. First French book manifestation of the Italian serial.
- **2016:** *Un cas de conscience*. First book manifestation of the 1866 serial.
- **2019:** *Le Comte de Mazzara*. First book manifestation of the 1866 serial.
