## Works by Johann Friedrich Overbeck

### Oil paintings and autonomous painted studies

- **1806–1807?:** *Jason Appears before His Uncle Pelias at Iolcus*. Overbeck’s first experiment in oils; Boetticher prints the impossible range “1807 bis 1806,” so its exact date remains unsettled and its location was already unknown in 1898.
- **1808:** *The Raising of Lazarus*. Early oil painting identified by August Kestner as Overbeck’s first; a distinct small repetition was completed in 1822.
- **1810:** *Self-Portrait*. Oil portrait made for Overbeck’s family in Lübeck; he also etched the composition.
- **1810:** *The Angel Appears to Joseph in a Dream and Commands the Flight into Egypt*. Small oil painting made for Overbeck’s sister, Frau Leithoff.
- **1810:** *Portrait of Franz Pforr*. Oil on panel portraying Overbeck’s fellow Lukasbund artist; an oil sketch of Pforr is separately recorded.
- **1811:** *Madonna with the Sleeping Child Holding Passion Flowers*. Oil painting acquired by the banker Simon Veit.
- **1812:** *Portrait of Herr Schuler*. Commissioned bust-length oil portrait; the sitter’s fuller identity is not established in the catalogue.
- **1811–1813:** *Adoration of the Magi*. Oil on panel commissioned by Queen Caroline of Bavaria; the catalogue allows either 1812 or 1813 as the completion year.
- **1813:** *Portrait of Giovanni Colombo*. Nearly life-size oil portrait, recorded as underpainted in November; its completion is not documented.
- **1813–1819?:** *The Blessed Virgin*. Madonna commissioned by Dall’Armi; Boetticher does not establish whether it is identical with the Madonna exhibited in 1819 or with the painting formerly owned by Thorvaldsen.
- **1814:** *Adoration of the Shepherds*. Oil on panel commissioned by Rudolf von Honegger.
- **1812–1816:** *Christ with Mary and Martha*. Oil painting commissioned by David Vogel.
- **1819:** *Rest on the Flight into Egypt*. Monogrammed oil painting exhibited at the Palazzo Caffarelli.
- **1815–1820:** *The Annunciation*. Oil painting commissioned by the Rostock jurist Fromm.
- **1821:** *Portrait of Vittoria Caldoni*. Nearly life-size oil portrait of the Albano vineyard worker; the catalogue also records another portrait and a repetition, whose precise relationships remain unclear.
- **1820–1824:** *The Finding of Moses*. Small oil-on-panel painting commissioned by J. H. Albers.
- **1809–1824:** *Christ’s Entry into Jerusalem*. Large oil painting begun in Vienna and completed in Rome; its unusually long genesis makes it both an executed painting and a sustained project.
- **1822:** *The Raising of Lazarus*. Small oil repetition, distinct from the 1808 painting.
- **1826:** *Saint Alphonsus Liguori*. Oil painting formerly in the Collegium Romanum.
- **1825:** *The Holy Family with the Lamb*. Large oil painting; a later sepia drawing was made expressly to mediate the composition to an engraver.
- **1824–1828:** *Elijah in the Fiery Chariot*. Oil on panel commissioned by Count B. Sant; Overbeck made a separate repetition for Crown Prince Friedrich Wilhelm IV in 1833.
- **1811–1828:** *Sulamith and Maria*, or *Italia and Germania*. Oil painting developed from an idea associated with Franz Pforr; its double title and unusually extended production make it a work of artistic friendship as well as an autonomous painting.
- **1824:** *The Rose Miracle (Indulgence) of Saint Francis of Assisi*. Oil color study for the later Portiuncula fresco, not merely a reduced copy: the monumental realization introduced changes.
- **1820–1830:** *Self-Portrait with Wife and Child*. Family portrait on panel; a repetition remained with Overbeck’s heirs.
- **1827–1833:** *Christ on the Mount of Olives*. Monumental oil painting acquired for Hamburg’s municipal hospital; Overbeck completed a smaller repetition in 1841.
- **by 1841:** *The Good Shepherd with the Lamb on His Shoulders*. Oil painting given to the religious confraternity of San Luca.
- **1829–1836:** *Lo Sposalizio (The Marriage of Mary and Joseph)*. Oil painting commissioned by Count Atanazy Raczyński.
- **1827–1838:** *The Visitation*. Oil on panel commissioned by Frau Nies, née du Fay.
- **1830–1840:** *The Triumph of Religion in the Arts*. Monumental allegorical oil painting for the Städel; its accumulation of sacred figures and historical artists approaches a pictorial manifesto for Overbeck’s theory of Christian art.
- **1839:** *Christ on the Mount of Olives with the Three Sleeping Disciples*. Oil sketch for a commission from the Prince-Bishop of Trent; the intended full painting was apparently never executed, so the project survives through its preparatory manifestation.
- **1839–1840:** *The Expulsion of Hagar and Ishmael*. Oil painting commissioned by Senator Jenisch.
- **1841:** *Madonna with the Sleeping Child in Her Lap*. Circular oil painting made for Johann von Bruchmann; a separate repetition on panel was begun in 1842 and sold in 1843.
- **1836:** *The Death of Saint Joseph*. Oil painting later given to Basel; Overbeck produced another version for Henry Woods in 1857.
- **1843–1844:** *Self-Portrait*. Head-and-shoulders oil portrait painted for the Uffizi collection of artists’ self-portraits.
- **1837–1845:** *The Entombment of Christ (Pietà)*. Large oil painting commissioned for Lübeck’s Marienkirche.
- **1846:** *Mary Magdalene Anoints Christ’s Feet*. Small oil painting intended for Overbeck’s brother Christian.
- **1847–1851:** *The Incredulity of Saint Thomas*. Large altarpiece commissioned by A. J. Beresford Hope.
- **1852:** *The Savior Seated upon the Clouds with the Cross*. Oil painting derived from an earlier drawing and made for the future Cardinal Viale-Prelà.
- **date not fixed:** *The Good Shepherd*. Small oil-on-panel painting showing Christ carrying one lamb while five follow; Boetticher supplies no secure production date.
- **1829–1854:** *The Assumption of the Virgin*. Monumental altarpiece commissioned for Cologne Cathedral; the cartoon was completed in 1847 and the painting in 1854.
- **1851–1856:** *The Holy Family with the Christ Child Dreaming of the Passion*. Oil painting made for Madame Boissonnet.
- **1859–1860:** *The Coronation of the Virgin*. Oil painting acquired by Emperor Maximilian of Mexico.
- **1862–1865:** *The Seven Sacraments*. Seven painted designs on canvas—six monochrome and *Penance* fully colored—executed with the participation of Overbeck’s pupils Karl Hoffmann and Ludwig Seitz. The uneven authorship and their status as designs rather than final monumental decorations resist treatment as seven straightforward easel paintings.
- **1863–1864:** *The Sacrament of Penance*. Monochrome canvas for Count Orlov-Davydov; a separate smaller colored version remained with the patron.
- **date not fixed, after 1859:** *Pentecost (Confirmation)*. Independent easel painting developed from the *Seven Sacraments* project.
- **1864–1866:** *Christ Escapes Those Who Would Cast Him from the Mountain at Nazareth*. Large oil version made for the Antwerp Academy after the composition created for the Quirinal ceiling.
- **1866–1869:** *The Visitation*. Monumental oil painting commissioned for Santa Maria Auxilium Christianorum near Spoleto; it was almost, but not completely, finished at Overbeck’s death.
- **1867–1868:** *The Visitation*. Smaller oil version acquired by H. Harms of Lübeck.
- **date not fixed:** *Madonna and Child*. Oil painting in Lübeck, accompanied there by Overbeck’s sepia study.
- **date not fixed:** *Portrait of Ernst Platner*. Oil portrait in profile; Boetticher records no date.
- **date not fixed:** *Angel’s Head*. Small oil study or autonomous fragment; its title is descriptive rather than demonstrably authorial.
- **date not fixed:** *Bust of an Italian Woman*. Oil study or portrait fragment; the sitter and date are unknown.
- **date not fixed:** *Madonna*. Oil painting formerly owned by Dr. F. J. Stiebel; the catalogue supplies no more specific title or date.

### Frescoes, ceilings, and collaborative environments

- **1816:** *The Seven Lean Years*. Fresco lunette for the collaborative Joseph cycle in the Casa Bartholdy, Rome; removed from the wall in 1886–87 and transferred to Berlin, so its present state is a displaced architectural fragment.
- **1817:** *The Sale of Joseph*. Fresco for the Casa Bartholdy cycle; likewise detached and transferred to Berlin.
- **1817–1829:** *Jerusalem Delivered*. Overbeck’s room-scale fresco project for the Casino Massimo, comprising *Godfrey of Bouillon Summoned by Gabriel*, *Preparations for the Assault and Peter the Hermit’s Call to Prayer*, *Armida’s Phantom Appearing to Rinaldo*, *The Death of Odoardo and Gildippe*, *Sofronia and Olindo Rescued by Clorinda*, *Erminia among the Shepherds*, *The Baptism of the Dying Clorinda*, the personification *Jerusalem Delivered*, and part of the grisaille frieze. It is a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single-authored picture: Joseph von Führich painted several further scenes and completed portions left unfinished by Overbeck.
- **1829:** *The Rose Miracle of Saint Francis*. Fresco on the façade of the Portiuncula chapel at Santa Maria degli Angeli, Assisi; damaged by the 1832 earthquake and restored by Domenico Carrattoli in 1837, so the surviving surface is not entirely Overbeck’s untouched work.
- **1857:** *Christ Miraculously Escapes His Persecutors at Nazareth*. Tempera ceiling painting commissioned by Pius IX for the Quirinal as a symbolic commemoration of the pope’s flight from Rome in 1848. Although catalogued with murals, it was painted on canvas and installed overhead.

### Graphic cycles and projects intended for translation into other media

- **1809:** *Diploma of the Brotherhood of Saint Luke*. Vignette showing Saint Luke drawing; both a corporate emblem and a protocol-like document image, subsequently etched by Overbeck.
- **1813, unrealized:** *The Seven Works of Mercy*. Proposed drawing cycle known from *Feeding the Hungry*; because the full series was apparently never made, the project—not a completed cycle—is the relevant work.
- **1815–1816:** *Alexander the Great’s Entry into Babylon*. Overbeck contributed drawings after Thorvaldsen’s relief for a twenty-two-plate engraved publication; this is mediated, collaborative graphic work rather than an independently authored book.
- **by 1816:** *Designs for the Tomb Reliefs of Augusta Böhmer, Baroness Schubart, and Philipp Bethmann-Hollweg*. Three sculptural projects designed by Overbeck and executed by Thorvaldsen.
- **1816:** *Ave Maria / Benedicta in Mulieribus*. Two-part watercolor combining the Annunciation and Visitation with ornamental marginal imagery.
- **1817–1829:** *Cartoons for Jerusalem Delivered*. Full-scale and smaller drawings governing the Casino Massimo frescoes; some survive separately, but their identity oscillates between working instruments and autonomous finished drawings.
- **1830:** *Design for Christian Schlosser’s Tomb*. Drawing for a relief of Christ appearing to Mary Magdalene, translated into marble by the sculptor Lotsch.
- **circa 1831–1834:** *The Old and the New Covenant*. Six allegorical drawings pairing Servitude with Adoption, Fear with Trust, and Captivity with Liberty.
- **1839:** *Illustrations for Heures Nouvelles*. Twelve small Passion and devotional drawings made for engraving in a French prayer book; the designs are Overbeck’s obras, while the printed plates are manifestations executed by Steifensand, Butavand, and Joseph Keller.
- **1835–1844:** *The Twelve Apostles and Four Evangelists*. Sixteen cartoons for frescoes executed by Max Seitz in the chapel of the Villa Torlonia at Castel Gandolfo; authorship is divided between Overbeck’s designs and Seitz’s painted realization.
- **1843–1852:** *The Gospels in Forty Pictures*. Forty finished drawings narrating the life of Christ, conceived as a unified picture-Bible and disseminated through engravings by several hands. It is both a graphic cycle and a book project, but the engraved publication should not be confused with Overbeck’s original drawings.
- **1838/39–1853:** *The Coronation of Mary*. Design and full-scale cartoon for a projected Cologne Cathedral painting; the cartoon was completed, but the intended painting was not executed in this form.
- **before 1856, unrealized:** *Saint John Resting on Christ’s Breast*. Cartoon for a painting intended for Princess Radziwiłł; the painting remained unmade, while Karl Hoffmann translated the design into sculpture.
- **1851–1854:** *The Lord’s Prayer*. Sepia design showing Christ teaching the disciples, made as the model for a monumental stained-glass window executed by Max Ainmiller for Hamburg’s Katharinenkirche.
- **1855:** *The Guidance of the Blind*. Memorial-tablet design for Countess Giulia Baldeschi, translated into terracotta by Karl Hoffmann.
- **date not fixed, before 1861:** *Design for the Exterior Frieze of the Casa Hoffmann-Overbeck at Rocca di Papa*. Architectural-decoration project; the catalogue does not establish whether it was executed.
- **before 1861:** *Allegory of the Arts*. Sepia design surrounded by a ribbon-like frame.
- **before 1861:** *The Wise and Foolish Virgins*. Four sepia scenes designed for the medallions surrounding *Allegory of the Arts*; together the sheets form one decorative project.
- **1861:** *Seal of the Arciconfraternità di San Pietro*. Institutional emblem designed for Prince Orsini, president of the confraternity.
- **1847–1862:** *The Seven Sacraments*. Seven large outline cartoons, surrounded by subsidiary friezes, side panels, and base scenes. Conceived for tapestries, fresco, or tempera decoration and accompanied by Overbeck’s explanatory text, the project is a Gesamtkunstwerk in intention even though no single final medium was fixed.
- **date not fixed:** *Designs for the Seven Sacraments*. At least ten preliminary drawings are recorded separately from the seven completed cartoons; the discrepancy means the project cannot be reduced to seven unique compositions without qualification.

### Devotional journeys in images

- **by 1857:** *The Way of the Cross*. Fourteen large watercolors commissioned by Carlo Baldeschi and the Marchese Campana and later owned by Pius IX; a pictorial devotional itinerary rather than a conventional book.
- **1861:** *Second, Smaller Way of the Cross*. Fourteen sepia drawings designed for Karl Hoffmann to translate into relief sculpture.
- **date not fixed:** *The Holy Way of the Cross*. Fourteen additional sepia station drawings acquired for Vienna’s Kunsthistorisches Hofmuseum. Boetticher catalogues these separately, but their precise relation to the other two *Via Crucis* cycles is not fully resolved.

### Writings that act within the visual projects

- **by 1862:** *Explanatory Text for The Seven Sacraments*. Prose commentary interpreting the principal scenes and surrounding imagery. It resists classification as an independent literary work: it is a verbal component of the unrealized multisensory church-decoration project.
- **date not fixed:** *Verses for The Christ Child in Joseph’s Workshop*. Short verses printed beneath an engraving after Overbeck’s drawing; they are a textual adjunct to the image rather than a securely documented autonomous poem.
- **1833:** *Account of the Opening of Raphael’s Tomb*. A letter to Philipp Veit describing the event; it is correspondence and testimony, not a work Overbeck presented as an independent publication.

### Corpus whose individual limits remain open

- **circa 1790s–1869:** *The body of drawings, studies, cartoons, watercolors, album leaves, portrait studies, and compositional exercises*. This cannot honestly be turned into a closed “everything” list at present: the current critical-catalogue project states that the extent of Overbeck’s drawings is still unquantifiable, with works dispersed through museums and private collections, while Lübeck alone holds about 250 objects. Many sheets are stages in other obras, collaborative instructions, repetitions, or later autonomous manifestations, and their resistance to a stable count is itself part of the record.
