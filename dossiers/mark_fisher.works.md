## Works by Mark Fisher

### Music and sonic fiction

- **1994:** *Entropy in the UK*. A four-track 12-inch EP made collectively by Fisher, Simon Biddell, and Lee Thompson as D-Generation; Fisher’s precise musical role is undocumented, although contemporary evidence identifies him as a member and co-ideologue. ([Simon Reynolds](https://reynoldsretro.blogspot.com/2018/11/d-generation-or-dawn-of-k-punk.html); [The Wire](https://www.thewire.co.uk/news/45139/ding-gold-at-the-end-of-the-vinyl-slush-pile-in-charity-shops))
- **c. 1994–95:** *Concrete Island*. A projected second D-Generation EP surviving as prerelease cassettes; it was not commercially issued under that name, so it is an unfinished or arrested project rather than an ordinary release. At least one piece migrated into the Lower Depths’ work. ([Simon Reynolds](https://reynoldsretro.blogspot.com/2022/07/d-generation-mark-fishers-first.html))
- **1995:** *Isle of the Dead*. A 12-inch EP by the Lower Depths, the group formed after D-Generation had to change its name; attribution to Fisher is collective and the members’ individual musical functions remain unclear. ([Simon Reynolds](https://reynoldsretro.blogspot.com/2022/07/d-generation-mark-fishers-first.html))
- **2005:** *londonunderlondon*. An audio essay or sonic fiction made with Justin Barton, combining voices, theory, narrative, and composed sound; first broadcast on Resonance FM. Its makers later regarded the transmitted ninety-minute form as longer than the intended work, making its boundaries unstable. ([Urbanomic](https://www.urbanomic.com/document/londonunderlondon/); [The Showroom](https://theshowroom.org/events/incursions-departures-londonunderlondon-and-on-vanishing-land))
- **2006–13:** *On Vanishing Land*. A forty-five-minute audio essay made with Barton from a 2006 walk between Felixstowe and Sutton Hoo and premiered as an installation work at the Showroom in 2013. Its later album release is a manifestation of the earlier work, not a separately authored Fisher composition. ([The Showroom](https://theshowroom.org/events/incursions-departures-londonunderlondon-and-on-vanishing-land); [The Wire](https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/book-extracts/read-an-extract-from-sonic-faction-audio-essay-as-medium-and-method))

### Collective theory-fiction and para-academic work

- **c. 1995–2003:** *Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (Ccru)*. A collective research project and Gesamtkunstwerk of seminars, performances, websites, zines, fictional personae, diagrams, music culture, and theory-fiction in which Fisher became a principal participant. Much of its writing was deliberately unsigned or collectively signed, so assigning every Ccru text to Fisher individually would falsify the project’s resistant authorship; the collected corpus is conventionally titled *Ccru: Writings 1997–2003*. ([Urbanomic](https://www.urbanomic.com/series/ccru/); [The Wire](https://www.thewire.co.uk/news/45139/ding-gold-at-the-end-of-the-vinyl-slush-pile-in-charity-shops))
- **1990s, date uncertain:** *White Magic*. A web essay published through CritCrim.org and associated with Fisher’s pre-*k-punk* period; surviving bibliographies name it, but readily accessible evidence does not establish a secure first-publication date. ([Mark Fisher overview](https://www.markfisher.theatrescotland.com/index.php/mark-fisher-journalist-cv/))
- **1999:** *Flatline Constructs: Gothic Materialism and Cybernetic Theory-Fiction*. Fisher’s doctoral thesis: an academic text that also behaves as theory-fiction, joining cybernetics, Gothic materialism, popular culture, and philosophy. Completed at Warwick in 1999, it circulated electronically before being made into a posthumous book in 2018 and a revised commercial edition later. ([Warwick Research Archive](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/110900/1/WRAP_Theses_Fisher_1999.pdf); [publisher description](https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/Flatline-Constructs/Mark-Fisher/9781803417851))
- **2001:** *Gothic Materialism*. A journal article derived from the thesis and published in *Pli*; it is a distinct, shorter manifestation of that research rather than another book. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2004–08:** *Hyperstition*. A collective weblog and theory-fiction laboratory involving Fisher, Nick Land, Anna Greenspan, Steve Goodman, Reza Negarestani, and others. Individual posts sometimes used personae, including “Linda Trent,” so its corpus resists stable personal attribution. ([archived project description](https://www.reddit.com/r/sorceryofthespectacle/comments/jov627); [*k-punk* collected writings](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))

### Networked projects and bodies of writing

- **2003–16:** *k-punk*. Fisher’s weblog under the name “k-punk”: simultaneously a publication, public notebook, critical persona, archive, and open-ended body of work on music, cinema, politics, fiction, and theory. It cannot adequately be reduced to a succession of essays, and the later collected edition is explicitly selective rather than complete. ([*k-punk: The Collected and Unpublished Writings*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2004 onward:** *Dissensus*. A discussion forum co-founded by Fisher and Matt Ingram (“Woebot”). This is an infrastructural and conversational creation rather than a singly authored text; Fisher supplied its name and an inaugural explanation, “Why Dissensus?” ([*k-punk* editor’s notes](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2004–16:** *Freelance criticism and political writing*. A dispersed corpus made for *The Wire*, *Fact*, *New Statesman*, *Sight & Sound*, *Frieze*, *The Guardian*, *Film Quarterly*, *New Humanist*, *The Quietus*, *e-flux*, and other venues. No authoritative bibliography claims completeness; Darren Ambrose’s posthumous *k-punk* collection samples this corpus while expressly excluding some posts, the early Ccru writing, and material already used in Fisher’s three books. ([Ambrose’s editorial account](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf); [Goldsmiths bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))

### Books

- **2009:** *Capitalist Realism: Is There No Alternative?* A short theoretical book diagnosing capitalism as an atmosphere governing imagination, work, education, and mental health. Parts developed from earlier *k-punk* writing, but Fisher refashioned them into a sustained book-length argument. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2014:** *Ghosts of My Life: Writings on Depression, Hauntology and Lost Futures*. A composed collection of revised and interconnected essays on music, film, politics, depression, and the “slow cancellation” of possible futures; it is more than a facsimile of the blog posts from which parts originated. ([Goldsmiths Research Online](https://research.gold.ac.uk/15798/))
- **2016–17:** *The Weird and the Eerie*. A book-length study distinguishing two aesthetic modes across fiction, film, and music. Bibliographies variously date it 2016, the year attached to the Goldsmiths record, or 2017, its public-release year; it appeared immediately before or shortly after Fisher’s death depending on territory and bibliographical convention. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))

### Edited works and publishing manifestos

- **2009:** *The Resistible Demise of Michael Jackson*. An edited multi-author collection conceived and assembled by Fisher; his made object is principally the editorial construction, not the individually authored chapters. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2009:** *Zer0 Books Statement*. A publishing manifesto written by Fisher for the imprint he co-founded with Tariq Goddard. Reprinted across Zer0’s list for years, it acts by defining a publishing programme and therefore fits “manifesto” more closely than “essay.” ([editor’s note in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2009–14:** *Zer0 Books*. A publishing project co-founded and editorially shaped by Fisher and Goddard. It is not Fisher’s individual work and its books belong to their respective authors, but the imprint’s programme, commissioning, and public identity form a collaborative cultural creation. ([editor’s note in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2014–17:** *Repeater Books*. A successor publishing project founded collectively by Fisher, Tariq Goddard, Etan Ilfeld, Phil Jourdan, Matteo Mandarini, Alex Niven, and Tamar Shlaim. It is best classified as an institution and continuing programme rather than a Fisher-authored obra. ([Repeater Books](https://repeaterbooks.com/about-copy/))
- **2016:** *Post-Punk Then and Now*. A multi-author collection co-edited with Gavin Butt and Kodwo Eshun, growing from a conference and treating post-punk as an unfinished cultural and political project. Editorial authorship is shared. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))

### Essays, chapters, and pamphlets separately recorded in the scholarly bibliography

- **2006:** *Gothic Oedipus: Subjectivity and Capitalism in Christopher Nolan’s Batman Begins*. A scholarly article reading the film through Gothic and psychoanalytic structures. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2007:** *“How Very Lacanian”: From Fantasy to Hyperreality in Basic Instinct 2*. A film-philosophy article rather than a book or review. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2007:** *“You Have Always Been the Caretaker”: The Spectral Spaces of the Overlook Hotel*. An article on *The Shining* and spectral space, published in *Perforations*. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2009:** *Materialismo gótico*. A Spanish-language selection from *Flatline Constructs*, edited into *Nosotros, los brujos*; it is a translated manifestation of the thesis rather than a wholly new Fisher work. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2010:** *Memorex for the Krakens: The Fall’s Pulp Modernism*. A commissioned book chapter on Mark E. Smith and the Fall. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2011:** *We’re Not All in This Together: Public Space and Antagonism in the Wake of Capitalist Realism*. A book chapter extending the political analysis of *Capitalist Realism*. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2011:** *The Privatisation of Stress*. A journal article arguing that apparently private distress has political and economic conditions. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2012:** *Postcapitalist Desire*. A contribution to the collective manifesto *What We Are Fighting For*; the title later named Fisher’s final lecture series, but the chapter and lectures are distinct manifestations. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2012:** *What Is Hauntology?* A journal article in *Film Quarterly* that defines and differentiates a critical tendency Fisher had already developed through the blog and *Ghosts of My Life*. ([editor’s notes in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2013:** *Indirect Action: Some Misgivings About Horizontalism*. A book chapter intervening in debates about organization and political strategy. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2013:** *“A Social and Psychic Revolution of Almost Inconceivable Magnitude”: Popular Culture’s Interrupted Accelerationist Dreams*. An *e-flux* essay treating postwar popular culture as a carrier of unrealized futurity. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2013:** *It’s Easier to Imagine the End of the World than the End of Capitalism*. A chapter in *The Visual Culture Reader*; it condenses material associated with *Capitalist Realism* and is therefore a separate publication but not an independent book project. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2013:** *Capitalist Realism and Neoliberal Hegemony: A Dialogue*. A dialogic essay made with Jeremy Gilbert; its authorship and argumentative form are jointly produced. ([New Formations bibliographical record](https://journals.lwbooks.co.uk/newformations/vol-2013-issue-80-81/abstract-8525/))
- **2013:** *Exiting the Vampire Castle*. A political intervention first published by *The North Star*. It acts polemically upon left organization and discourse and thus approaches a manifesto, although Fisher did not formally label it one. ([editor’s notes in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2014:** *“What Is It That You’d Say You Do Here?”: The Post-Fordist Comedy of Management*. A chapter written for a reader on Harun Farocki’s *A New Product*. ([Goldsmiths Research Online bibliography](https://research.gold.ac.uk/view/creator/Fisher%3D3AMark%3D3A%3D3A.date.html))
- **2014:** *Good for Nothing*. A short political-autobiographical essay on depression, class, and value; it is neither memoir nor clinical protocol, but deliberately breaks the boundary between psychic testimony and structural analysis. ([editor’s notes in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2015:** *Reclaim Modernity: Beyond Markets, Beyond Machines*. A political pamphlet written with Jeremy Gilbert. Its proposals and collective orientation make it closer to a manifesto than a neutral description. ([Compass bibliographical link recorded in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))

### Lectures and unfinished projects

- **2016:** *Postcapitalist Desire*. Fisher’s final Goldsmiths seminar series, delivered as five lectures before his death interrupted the course. The lectures were events and pedagogical encounters; *Postcapitalist Desire: The Final Lectures* (2020), edited from recordings by Matt Colquhoun, is their posthumous textual manifestation rather than a book Fisher completed. ([Repeater catalogue PDF](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5f1035bf13e5775b39293778/5f8c13b139466abe45c99f8e_Repeater__PostCapitalist.pdf))
- **2016–17:** *Acid Communism*. An unfinished book project intended to move from the diagnosis of capitalist realism toward collective desire, consciousness, and postcapitalist possibility. Fisher completed only an introduction; because the intention and conceptual programme survive without the projected book, it is properly a project, not a completed obra. ([*k-punk: The Collected and Unpublished Writings*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **15 November 2016:** *Mannequin Challenge*. Fisher’s final unfinished *k-punk* post, left unpublished at his death and subsequently printed by Darren Ambrose. It resists classification as a finished essay: what survives is a dated textual fragment and evidence of thought still in progress. ([editor’s note in *k-punk*](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))

### Posthumous manifestations, not new works made by Fisher

- **2018:** *Flatline Constructs: Gothic Materialism and Cybernetic Theory-Fiction*. The first book publication of Fisher’s 1999 thesis. The made text is Fisher’s; the book form, title page, foreword, and editorial presentation are posthumous. ([Warwick Research Archive](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/110900/1/WRAP_Theses_Fisher_1999.pdf); [publisher description](https://www.simonandschuster.ca/books/Flatline-Constructs/Mark-Fisher/9781803417851))
- **2018:** *k-punk: The Collected and Unpublished Writings of Mark Fisher (2004–2016)*. A large posthumous selection edited by Darren Ambrose. Its constituent texts are Fisher’s, but its selection, exclusions, organization, section titles, and identity as a single book are Ambrose’s editorial creation; it explicitly does not include all of Fisher’s writing. ([Repeater edition](https://library.josh8.com/philosophy/Mark%20Fisher,%20Darren%20Ambrose%20(editor)%20-%20K-punk_%20The%20Collected%20and%20Unpublished%20Writings%20of%20Mark%20Fisher-Repeater%20(2018).pdf))
- **2019:** *On Vanishing Land*. The Flatlines LP/digital edition is a posthumous manifestation of the Barton–Fisher audio essay, not evidence that Fisher made a new work in 2019. ([The Wire](https://www.thewire.co.uk/in-writing/book-extracts/read-an-extract-from-sonic-faction-audio-essay-as-medium-and-method))
- **2020:** *Postcapitalist Desire: The Final Lectures*. Matt Colquhoun’s edited transcription of Fisher’s incomplete 2016 course. It preserves Fisher’s speech and pedagogy while necessarily turning situated, dialogic classes into a posthumous book. ([Repeater catalogue PDF](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/5f1035bf13e5775b39293778/5f8c13b139466abe45c99f8e_Repeater__PostCapitalist.pdf))
