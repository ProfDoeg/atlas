## Works by John Pierpont Morgan

### Documentary works

- **1887–1913:** *Morgan Collections Correspondence*. A dispersed documentary corpus of nearly eight thousand surviving letters and invoices concerning Morgan’s purchases of books, manuscripts, furniture, decorative arts, and other objects. It is an archival remainder rather than a deliberately composed work, and many documents were made by dealers, agents, and staff rather than Morgan himself. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/archives))

- **18–19 December 1912:** *Testimony of J. P. Morgan before the Bank and Currency Committee*. Spoken testimony under examination by Samuel Untermyer during the Pujo Committee’s investigation of the alleged “money trust”; the printed text is a government-produced transcript, so Morgan made the answers but not the publication as a whole. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdc.13001206/?st=brief), [Securities and Exchange Commission Historical Society](https://sechistorical.org/digital-archive/testimony-of-j-p-morgan-on-the-money-trust-investigation-to-the-subcommittee-of-the-committee-on-banking-and-currency-u-s-house-of-representatives-pujo-committee/))

### Banking houses

- **1864–1871:** *Dabney, Morgan & Co.* A securities firm made through Morgan’s partnership with Charles H. Dabney. This was a collective commercial institution, not Morgan’s sole creation. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1871–1895:** *Drexel, Morgan & Co.* A banking partnership formed by Morgan and Anthony J. Drexel; it became the principal institutional vehicle for Morgan’s railroad finance and reorganization. Its obra was organizational and contractual rather than literary. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont), [History](https://www.history.com/articles/john-pierpont-morgan))

- **1895:** *J. P. Morgan & Co.* The banking house created by reorganizing and renaming Drexel, Morgan & Co. after Drexel’s death. Although identified with Morgan, it was the continuing work of a partnership and staff rather than an individual artifact. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont), [Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/business-leaders/jp-morgan))

### Railroad “Morganizations”

- **1869:** *Albany and Susquehanna Railroad settlement and reorganization*. Morgan helped wrest the railroad from the Gould–Fisk struggle and stabilize its control; this is an early example of an institutional arrangement rather than a newly built railway. Contemporary summaries differ over whether to call Morgan its reorganizer or chiefly its financial mediator. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont), [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/morgan-j-p))

- **1885:** *New York, West Shore and Buffalo Railroad reorganization*. Morgan reorganized the bankrupt line and arranged its lease to the New York Central. The made thing was a new financial and managerial order imposed on an existing physical railway. ([History of Rail Transportation in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transportation_in_the_United_States))

- **1886:** *Philadelphia and Reading Railroad reorganization*. A financial reconstruction conventionally included among Morgan’s “Morganizations”; it should not be mistaken for authorship of the railroad or its infrastructure. ([History of Rail Transportation in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transportation_in_the_United_States))

- **1888:** *Chesapeake and Ohio Railway reorganization*. Morgan and his associates refashioned the insolvent railroad’s capitalization and governance. The attribution is corporate and collaborative, notwithstanding its customary identification with Morgan. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1888:** *Cleveland, Cincinnati, Chicago and St. Louis Railway reorganization*. A reconstruction of the “Big Four” system through Morgan’s banking organization, again making a corporate structure rather than railway track. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1889–1890:** *Railroad presidents’ conferences*. Morgan convened competing railroad executives to devise agreements for stable, uniform rates and compliance with the Interstate Commerce Act. These agreements acted as a commercial protocol, although they depended on collective assent and did not constitute a durable single organization. ([History of Rail Transportation in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transportation_in_the_United_States))

- **1894:** *Southern Railway*. Morgan’s reorganization of the Richmond Terminal properties produced the Southern Railway system. It was widely treated as his creation, but its tracks, constituent companies, management, and capital came from many prior and contemporary makers. ([Men of Progress](https://www.cga.ct.gov/hco/books/Men_of_Progress_1898.pdf), [Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1895:** *Erie Railroad reorganization*. A restructuring executed by Morgan’s banking house after receivership; its status as Morgan’s personal work is therefore resistant and derivative. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1895:** *Philadelphia and Reading Railway reorganization*. A further reconstruction of the Reading properties, distinct from the 1886 intervention and sometimes compressed with it in summary accounts. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1896:** *New York and New England Railroad reorganization*. A corporate and financial reconstruction credited to Morgan’s house, not an independently authored object. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1897:** *Northern Pacific Railway reorganization*. Morgan’s syndicate supplied capital and a new financial structure to the bankrupt railway. The work was the reorganization itself, collaboratively made with managers, investors, lawyers, and other bankers. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1899:** *Baltimore and Ohio Railroad reorganization*. A Morgan-house restructuring of an existing railway, properly categorized as an institutional project rather than a new railroad authored by Morgan. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

### Industrial and transport combinations

- **1892:** *General Electric Company*. Morgan engineered the merger of Edison General Electric and Thomson-Houston to form GE. This was a jointly made corporate combination—not Morgan’s technological invention, despite later shorthand calling him a founder. ([GE](https://www.ge.com/news/reports/ge-alstom-continue-shared-history-acquisition))

- **1898:** *Federal Steel Company*. A steel combination whose formation Morgan financed, providing the industrial and financial nucleus later absorbed into U.S. Steel. Its attribution belongs jointly to Morgan, Elbert H. Gary, constituent companies, investors, and managers. ([Biography.com](https://www.biography.com/business-leaders/jp-morgan), [U.S. Steel](https://www.ussteel.com/about-us/history))

- **1901:** *United States Steel Corporation*. Morgan’s syndicate purchased Carnegie Steel and combined it with Federal Steel and other concerns to create the first billion-dollar industrial corporation. The corporation is a collective institutional obra, with Andrew Carnegie, Charles M. Schwab, Elbert H. Gary, and Morgan all essential to its making. ([U.S. Steel](https://www.ussteel.com/about-us/history), [Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont))

- **1901:** *Northern Securities Company*. A holding company designed to place the Northern Pacific, Great Northern, and Chicago, Burlington and Quincy interests under coordinated control. The project resisted permanence: the federal government challenged it under the Sherman Act, and the Supreme Court ordered its dissolution in 1904. ([U.S. Department of State, Outline of U.S. History](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-S20-PURL-gpo39912/pdf/GOVPUB-S20-PURL-gpo39912.pdf))

- **1902:** *International Mercantile Marine Company*. A Morgan-led holding company combining five major transatlantic passenger lines, including White Star; it was an attempted integrated shipping system rather than a ship made by Morgan. Business historians caution that the merger has often been oversimplified as Morgan’s unilateral Wall Street promotion. ([Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/business-history-review/article/abs/study-in-merger-formation-of-the-international-mercantile-marine-company/BD7C64308DBB4041B4041A8C3DF5A4BE), [The Growth and Dissolution of a Large-Scale Business Enterprise](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/growth-and-dissolution-of-a-largescale-business-enterprise/trouble-on-the-transatlantic-route-the-formation-of-the-imm-and-furness-response/BB04D3C9FC05CBA80E22E65C40EEAA07))

- **1902:** *International Harvester Company*. A Morgan-financed agricultural-machinery combination assembled from McCormick, Deering, and other manufacturers. Morgan made the financial and negotiating framework, not the constituent machines or businesses. ([History](https://www.history.com/articles/john-pierpont-morgan), [Christian Science Monitor review of Jean Strouse’s biography](https://www.csmonitor.com/1999/0722/p20s2.html))

- **1902:** *Piccadilly, City and North East London Railway*. An unrealized project for a Morgan-controlled London Underground line. It belongs here precisely as an unfinished intention: rival promoter Charles Tyson Yerkes defeated the effort to obtain parliamentary authority, so no railway corresponding to Morgan’s project was made. ([J. P. Morgan biographical account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._P._Morgan#Failed_London_Underground_line:_1902))

### Financial interventions

- **1895:** *Morgan–Belmont gold syndicate*. A contractual financial mechanism organized with August Belmont and European banking houses to supply the United States Treasury with gold in exchange for bonds. It functioned as an emergency protocol, was profitable to its makers, and was politically disputed rather than an uncontested act of public rescue. ([Encyclopædia Britannica, 1911](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Morgan,_John_Pierpont), [contemporary contract report preserved by the Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ndnp/scu/batch_scu_andersonpink_ver02/data/sn86063760/00294550793/1895022701/0237.pdf))

- **1907:** *Panic of 1907 rescue organization*. Morgan convened bankers, trust-company officials, and corporate executives, directed investigations of endangered institutions, and organized pools of liquidity and securities purchases. This was a temporary performative system assembled in a crisis—not a named institution, not Morgan’s act alone, and not identical with the later Federal Reserve System. ([Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/inside_adams/2021/03/united-copper-panic-of-1907/), [Morgan Library study guide](https://www.themorgan.org/sites/default/files/pdf/MorganStudyGuide.pdf))

### Collection as obra

- **before 1887–1913:** *Pierpont Morgan collection*. A deliberately assembled body of rare books, illuminated and literary manuscripts, old-master drawings and prints, antiquities, and decorative arts. It can be treated as Morgan’s largest Gesamtkunstwerk, but it resists individual authorship: Morgan selected and financed it while dealers, scholars, agents, and—especially after 1905—librarian Belle da Costa Greene materially shaped it. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/about/introduction), [Bookman](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/online/bookmans-paradise/the-bookman), [Morgan Archives](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/archives))

- **1902:** *Richard Bennett library acquisition*. Morgan’s purchase of Bennett’s library brought twenty-seven Caxton imprints into his collection. The acquisition made a new ensemble and altered Morgan’s library, but neither the books nor their texts were Morgan’s creations. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/collections-spotlight-spring-2022))

- **1909–1910:** *Charles Fairfax Murray drawings acquisition*. Morgan acquired roughly fifteen hundred old-master drawings, establishing the core of the Morgan’s drawings holdings. Sources variously place the transaction in 1909 or 1910, so the range is retained rather than silently choosing one date. ([Morgan Library & Museum research guide](https://www.themorgan.org/research/guides/drawings-and-prints), [Morgan Library & Museum press account](https://www.themorgan.org/press/2003/facets-morgan-tour))

### Commissioned architecture

- **1902–1906:** *Mr. Morgan’s Library*. A freestanding private library commissioned by Morgan to house his collection and designed by Charles Follen McKim of McKim, Mead & White. It is Morgan’s project and patronal Gesamtkunstwerk, but the building’s architectural authorship belongs to McKim and its construction and decoration to numerous designers, artists, and craftspeople. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/J-Pierpont-Morgans-Library), [About the Morgan](https://www.themorgan.org/about/introduction))

- **circa 1904–1906:** *East Room decorative program*. The library’s integrated ceiling, lunettes, architectural ornament, bookcases, and collection display formed a multimedia interior directed through Morgan’s commission, McKim’s design, and H. Siddons Mowbray’s painting. Morgan intervened in its iconography—for example, requesting that Caxton replace Gutenberg—but did not personally execute it. ([Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/collections-spotlight-spring-2022))
