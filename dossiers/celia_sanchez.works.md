## Works by Celia Esther Sánchez Manduley

### Monument and memorial projects

- **1953:** *Busto de José Martí en el Pico Turquino*. Commemorative monument comprising Jilma Madera’s bronze bust, pedestal, and plaques; Sánchez joined her father Manuel Sánchez Silveira and the Asociación de Antiguos Alumnos del Seminario Martiano in organizing and installing it, so this is a collective obra rather than her sculpture. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 135–138](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **From the 1960s:** *Preservation and restoration of the Sierra Maestra historical sites*. A continuing memorial project—especially the conservation of the Comandancia de La Plata—which resisted conventional monument-making by preserving places in their wartime form; Sánchez also promoted unobtrusive monumental ensembles at revolutionary sites. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 243–244](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Undated; partially realized after 1980:** *Small historical museums at the principal sites of the revolutionary war*. An unfinished-at-her-death museum network conceived by Sánchez; as a distributed project rather than a single object, it resists being called one completed work. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 244](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1972 onward:** *Historical reconstruction of the First Front*. A research-and-memorial project beginning with Sánchez’s personally directed reconstruction of the Battle of Guisa and continuing through studies by a team she formed; authorship is necessarily collective. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 243](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Undated:** *Reconstruction of the Castro family house at Birán*. A heritage reconstruction whose surviving evidence includes Sánchez reviewing its architectural model; the standard biography associates her with it but does not delimit her design authorship or give a completion date. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 253, photograph and caption](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Archive, research, and publishing institutions

- **1956–1980:** *Archive of the revolutionary struggle*. A deliberately assembled documentary body begun during the war and expanded afterward through nationwide recovery, identification, approximate dating, classification, preservation, and microfilming. It is both obra and ongoing archival project, made largely from texts created by others rather than an authored book. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 237–243](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1964:** *Oficina de Asuntos Históricos*. An archival and historical-research institution created and named by Sánchez to make her personal collecting project permanent; its holdings incorporated revolutionary records and rescued collections relating to Martí, Máximo Gómez, Francisco Vicente Aguilera, Carlos Manuel de Céspedes, and Manuel Sanguily. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 240–243](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Undated:** *Oficina de Publicaciones del Consejo de Estado*. A publishing institution credited by the standard biography itself as Sánchez’s creation; the source supplies neither an exact founding date nor a fully separated account of her role, so it should not be converted into a dated publication of her own. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 357](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Documentary texts and testimony

- **Before 1 January 1959:** *Active documentation in the Fondo Celia Sánchez Manduley*. A body of 1,595 surviving documents attributed to Sánchez, including correspondence, operational communications, notes, and administrative texts; the official biography calls this almost all her active documentation through the revolutionary victory, not a complete collected-works edition. Individual titles are generally unfixed, and it would be misleading to manufacture them. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 355–356](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1959–1980:** *Post-revolutionary correspondence, memoranda, directives, and administrative papers*. A dispersed body of functional texts—often protocols in the broad sense because they organized preservation, construction, supplies, hospitality, and government work—rather than a named literary oeuvre. The standard biography does not claim that this later corpus is complete or provide a work-by-work register. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 237–260, 355–356](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1959:** *Note to Camilo Cienfuegos of 16 March 1959 concerning the revolutionary archive*. A programmatic letter that acts as both manifesto and archival protocol: it defines the archive’s chronological scope, preservation method, working copy, and collective ownership. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 237–238](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates unspecified:** *Biographical records of rebel combatants*. Short historical-biographical texts written by Sánchez while she was also preserving documents and recording Fidel Castro’s mountain speeches; their number, titles, and completeness have not been established in the consulted scholarly account. ([Carmen Perilli, “La mujer que armó el archivo de la revolución,” pp. 25–44](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6548193.pdf))

- **Dates unspecified:** *Fifty-three recorded testimonies concerning episodes in her life*. An oral-history corpus made through Sánchez’s spoken recollection and institutional recording; these are documentary manifestations rather than authored books, and the official biography does not list individual titles. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 356](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Landscape and recreational Gesamtkunstwerke

- **Early 1960s:** *Cabañas en los árboles del Parque La Güira*. Experimental recreational architecture in Pinar del Río executed with Sánchez’s participation before Guamá; the biography supplies no individual cabin titles, architects, or exact dates. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 245](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **From 1959:** *Centro turístico de Guamá*. A collective Gesamtkunstwerk at Laguna del Tesoro combining over-water cabins, restaurants, museum, ceramic workshop, landscape, and a sculptural Taíno village commissioned by Sánchez from Rita Longa. Fidel Castro originated the development proposal, while Sánchez directed its realization and visual coherence; architectural and sculptural authorship therefore remains plural. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 244–245](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1966:** *Coppelia*. A thousand-seat ice-cream park and service system: Mario Girona was its architect and Fidel Castro proposed the giant heladería, while Sánchez directed the six-month realization and shaped staffing, uniforms, tableware, colors, and presentation. It is better classified as a collective architectural-service Gesamtkunstwerk than as a building authored by Sánchez alone. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 245–246](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Campamento 5 de Mayo*. An international student camp at Cayajabos in the Sierra del Rosario; Sánchez was not its formal director but helped determine the site and design, supervised construction, and devised its integration into the forest through extensive planting. This qualified attribution should not be silently enlarged into sole authorship. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 246–247](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1969–1972:** *Parque Lenin*. A 670-hectare landscape, architectural, and recreational Gesamtkunstwerk whose overall conception and execution the official biography assigns to Sánchez while also calling it Fidel Castro’s original idea; Antonio Quintana led the architectural project with Mario Girona and a large team. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 247–248](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf); [Coyula and Gómez, architectural history](https://www.uni-kassel.de/upress/online/OpenAccess/978-3-7376-5049-6.OpenAccess.pdf))

- **Around the 1970s:** *Southern Havana landscape complex*. Sánchez acted as the gestor of the linked Parque Lenin, Jardín Botánico Nacional, and Jardín Zoológico Nacional development. This is an urban-scale project rather than a single authored object, and the individual architectural and landscape designs belong to specialist teams. ([Coyula and Gómez, architectural history](https://www.uni-kassel.de/upress/online/OpenAccess/978-3-7376-5049-6.OpenAccess.pdf))

- **Begun by 1972:** *Parque Zoológico Nacional*. A zoological-landscape institution of which Sánchez was the principal promoter and at whose works she labored; the sources do not make her its architect. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 251, 259](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **From 1974:** *Ciudad de los Pioneros José Martí*. A children’s city and camp at Tarará whose scale and program Sánchez transformed, including facilities for asthmatic and diabetic children, pictorial wayfinding, restricted internal traffic, a theatrical entrance, and a small Che Guevara museum. Builders could no longer reliably distinguish her ideas from those transmitted from Fidel Castro, making divided authorship part of the work’s history. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 248–251](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Palacio Central de Pioneros Ernesto Che Guevara*. A large educational-recreational complex within Parque Lenin associated by the official biography with Sánchez’s constructive work; its architectural design was produced by Néstor Garmendía and collaborators, so hers is project stewardship rather than sole design authorship. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 254](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Campamento internacional de pioneros en Varadero*. A children’s international camp listed among the works associated with Sánchez’s project activity; the standard biography does not specify its designers, exact date, or the boundaries of her contribution. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 254](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Buildings, interiors, and hospitality systems

- **Dates not fixed:** *Mar Azul and the first post-revolutionary hotel program*. A group of tropical-hotel projects initiated under Sánchez’s supervision, beginning with Mar Azul at Santa María del Mar and followed by hotels at Pasacaballos, Guardalavaca, and elsewhere. The biography names some locations but leaves “others” unspecified, so the corpus resists a complete building-by-building catalogue. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 251–252](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Hotel Marea del Portillo*. A hotel at Pilón for which Sánchez participated from site selection through the curving access-road alignment, intended to preserve the surrounding coconut grove; architectural authorship is not assigned to her. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 252](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Casa de descanso para cosmonautas*. A boldly designed residence at Varadero whose construction Sánchez closely supervised; the consulted biography does not identify the architect or establish that she designed it herself. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 252](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Casas de visitas y protocolo*. A nationwide, incompletely enumerated body of guest houses constructed or adapted under Sánchez, together with the operational protocol she established for their decoration, gardens, staffing, service, and uniforms. The source says “almost all” such houses, which both asserts broad authorship and prevents a closed list. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 252–253](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Salón de protocolo de Cubanacán*. A protocol interior associated with Sánchez’s constructive and decorative work; neither exact date nor separate design authors are supplied in the standard biography. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 253–254](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Gran salón de recepciones del Palacio de la Revolución*. A ceremonial interior and environmental composition including Sánchez’s indoor grove of tree ferns and monumental Cuban stones. It combines architecture, landscape, furniture, hospitality, and found natural objects, making Gesamtkunstwerk more accurate than “room.” ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 253–260](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **1979:** *Palacio de Convenciones de La Habana*. Sánchez’s last major building project, created for the Sixth Summit of the Non-Aligned Movement; she participated from the project phase and supervised functional, decorative, and furnishing decisions, while the architectural design was collective rather than hers alone. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 254](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Schools in the countryside*. An unnamed and unenumerated body of educational construction associated by the standard biography with Sánchez; it cannot honestly be decomposed into individual works from the consulted evidence. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 254](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Escuela Vocacional Lenin and other vocational schools*. Educational building projects associated with Sánchez, but the biography’s phrase “and others” leaves both extent and individual attribution unresolved. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 254](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Productive, ecological, and social projects

- **Date not fixed:** *First crocodile-breeding station in the Ciénaga de Zapata*. A fauna-conservation and breeding project proposed by Fidel Castro and organized by Sánchez; it is a living system rather than a stable object. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 259](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Experimental bird breeding at El Laguito*. An experiment with parakeets, parrots, and other birds; the biography records the activity but gives no formal title, dates, or completed institutional form, so it resists classification as a finished work. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 259](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Flower-growing and distribution enterprise*. An agricultural and cultural infrastructure created by Sánchez from an initiative of Fidel Castro, including imported seed and acclimatized varieties; it is a productive system, not simply a garden. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 258](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Plan porcino de Bauta*. A circular-economy experiment using school-catering food waste for pig raising, begun with a trial herd at Bauta; the idea arose in Fidel Castro’s special-plans system and was materialized under Sánchez’s care. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 254–255](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Rural schoolchildren’s shoe program*. A multi-year census, procurement, and distribution system delivering approximately half a million pairs annually until domestic production could assume the need. It is a logistical obra without a conventional title or singular physical object. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 255](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **From 1969:** *Plastic-footwear production line*. An industrial project involving the purchase of an Italian factory, adaptation of a Cuban building, and introduction of plastic shoes and sandals; Sánchez supervised its realization but acted on an instruction from Fidel Castro and within a collective manufacturing apparatus. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 255–256](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Primor*. A line of fine women’s shoes that emerged from production initiatives promoted by Sánchez; the source does not identify her as the individual designer of particular models. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 256](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Orthopedic-shoe and prosthesis workshops*. A network or group of productive workshops promoted by Sánchez; their number, locations, dates, and individual names are not given. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 256](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Craft, design, dress, and protocol

- **Dates not fixed:** *Traditional-craft revival program*. A distributed project that located artisans, established workshops, supplied materials, secured incomes, and trained apprentices in coconut, gourd, yarey, leather, shell, metal, textile, tortoiseshell, ceramic, and seed work. The resulting objects were made by many hands; Sánchez made the sustaining system rather than every artifact. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 257–258](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Cuban furniture and interior-design lines*. Product families promoted by Sánchez for economical interiors with a consciously Cuban character; the standard biography does not provide model names or distinguish her concepts from the work of employed designers. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 258](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Stained-glass windows and leaded-glass lamps*. New craft-production lines created under Sánchez’s direction; these denote classes of collaboratively produced objects, not individually titled works by her hand. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 258](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Empresa de Producciones Varias (EMPROVA)*. An enterprise founded and directly supervised by Sánchez to integrate craft, furniture, glass, and interior-production lines. As an institution that generated many objects by other makers, it is an obra-organism rather than a single artifact. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 258](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Date not fixed:** *Safari campaign and ministerial fashion show*. A performative design intervention promoting the lightweight safari suit for Cuban official dress, launched through a model parade by ministers and political leaders. Sánchez introduced the garment’s public use but is not identified as its inventor or pattern designer. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 256–257](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Protocol-house guayabera uniforms*. A dress protocol instituted by Sánchez: short-sleeved, light-colored guayaberas for men and guayabera dresses for women serving in state guest houses. This is both clothing design direction and an instructional text embodied as uniform practice. ([Álvarez Tabío, p. 253](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

- **Dates not fixed:** *Cuban state-reception format*. A protocol and multisensory manifestation through which Sánchez introduced regional foods, vernacular serving vessels, an eastern Cuban organ, oyster carts, native flowers, and Cuban dress into official hospitality. Because it fused cuisine, music, craft, performance, interior, and service, it is best understood as a recurring Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a text or single event. ([Álvarez Tabío, pp. 257–258](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf))

### Sources

- Pedro Álvarez Tabío, *Celia: ensayo para una biografía*, 2nd ed. (Havana: Oficina de Publicaciones del Consejo de Estado, 2004). This is the standard official biography, based principally on the Fondo Celia Sánchez Manduley and more than one hundred interviews; its author explicitly calls it a first approximation rather than an exhaustive academic biography and acknowledges that many works, projects, and initiatives remain unnamed. [Full text](https://sos-de-fra-1.exo.io/patria-libros/books/23_Oficina_de_Asuntos_Historicos/Celia_ensayo_para_una_biografia.pdf); [bibliographic record](https://patrialibros.org/book/951).

- Carmen Perilli, “La mujer que armó el archivo de la revolución,” *Telar* 20 (January–June 2018), pp. 25–44. A scholarly critical reading that identifies Álvarez Tabío’s book as the official biography and emphasizes Sánchez’s archive, letters, recordings, and combatant biographies. [PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6548193.pdf).

- Mario Coyula and collaborators, history of post-revolutionary Havana architecture in *Havanna: Architekturführer / Architectural Guide* materials, especially “Parques hacia el sur.” Used to distinguish Sánchez’s role as gestor from the design authorship of Antonio Quintana, Mario Girona, and their architectural collaborators. [Open-access PDF](https://www.uni-kassel.de/upress/online/OpenAccess/978-3-7376-5049-6.OpenAccess.pdf).

- Interviews with Cuban architects published by Kassel University Press, including recollections of the Parque Lenin project team and Sánchez’s initiating and coordinating role. [Open-access PDF](https://www.uni-kassel.de/upress/online/OpenAccess/978-3-7376-5091-5.OpenAccess.pdf).
