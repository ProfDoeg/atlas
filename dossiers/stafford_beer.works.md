## Works by Anthony Stafford Beer

### Books, poetry books, and book-scale works

- **1959:** *Cybernetics and Management*. A monograph on applying cybernetics to management; Beer substantially altered it for the 1967 “new edition,” so that edition is a revised manifestation rather than a mere reprint. [B, p. 1]
- **1966:** *Decision and Control: The Meaning of Operational Research and Management Cybernetics*. A theoretical monograph, awarded the Lanchester Prize. [B, p. 1]
- **1968:** *Management Science: The Business Use of Operational Research*. An expository book on operational research in business. [B, p. 1]
- **1972:** *Brain of the Firm*. A book-form exposition of the Viable System Model; its greatly enlarged 1981 second edition is materially different but remains the same named obra. [B, p. 1]
- **1974:** *Designing Freedom*. A book derived from Beer’s CBC Massey Lectures; it is simultaneously public pedagogy and a political argument about institutional design. [B, p. 1]
- **1975:** *Platform for Change*. An experimental book and manifesto whose diagrams, marginal material, inserts, and multiple textual levels resist separation into ordinary chapters. [B, p. 1; A, pp. 40–41]
- **1977:** *Transit*. A privately circulated collection of poems. [B, p. 1]
- **1979:** *The Heart of Enterprise*. A book-length continuation of Beer’s account of viable organization. [B, p. 1]
- **1981:** *Brain of the Firm*, second edition. A “much extended” reconstruction of the 1972 book, not simply a reset edition. [B, p. 1]
- **1983:** *Transit*, second edition. A much-expanded poetry collection issued with the audio works *Transit—Selected Readings* and *One Person Metagame*, making the package a small Gesamtkunstwerk of page, voice, and participatory procedure. [B, p. 1]
- **1985:** *Diagnosing the System for Organizations*. A diagnostic handbook and protocol for applying the Viable System Model. [B, p. 1]
- **1986:** *Pebbles to Computers: The Thread*. A co-authored illustrated history of computing made with photographer Hans Blohm. [B, pp. 1, 3]
- **1989:** *Chronicles of Wizard Prang*. A literary book issued by Beer’s Cwarel Isaf Institute. [B, p. 1]
- **1994:** *Beyond Dispute: The Invention of Team Syntegrity*. A book that presents and operationalizes Team Syntegrity; it functions both as theoretical obra and protocol. [B, p. 1]
- **1994:** *How Many Grapes Went into the Wine: Stafford Beer on the Art and Science of Holistic Management*. A post-selection of Beer’s papers edited by Roger Harnden and Allenna Leonard; the individual writings are Beer’s, but the collected-book object and its title were editorially made. [B, pp. 1–2]
- **Date unknown:** *Panencleisis*. A surviving three-volume manuscript with a glossary; the archive does not establish publication, completion, or a secure date, so it resists classification as a finished book. [A, p. 52]
- **Early work; date uncertain:** *Pillow by Her Head*. An apparently unfinished novel surviving as manuscript, notes, and plans; it is a project rather than an established publication. [A, p. 46]
- **1945:** *Ion* by Plato, English translation. A handwritten translation made in Burma; it is Beer’s textual artefact but not an original authored work, and no publication is established. [A, p. 46]
- **Date unknown:** Untitled or collectively catalogued children’s stories. The archive records a box of such stories without supplying stable titles, dates, or evidence of publication. [A, p. 51]

### Cybernetic machines, experiments, and technical artefacts

- **1956:** *Stochastic Analogue Machine*. A physical probabilistic computing device; it is a machine rather than a text, and a surviving example is held with Beer’s artefacts. [M; P]
- **1950s–1960s:** *Algedonic computer*. A wood-and-brass learning-system apparatus using reward-and-punishment signals; surviving hardware makes it an artefact as well as a conceptual experiment. [M]
- **Late 1950s–early 1960s:** Biological-computing experiments. Beer attempted to use responsive biological systems—including colonies of *Daphnia*—as adaptive controllers; these experimental assemblages resist being called finished “works,” and some failed materially. [P]
- **Late 1950s–1960s:** Cybernetic-factory project. A long-running project to model or realize an adaptive, substantially self-organizing factory; its manifestations include machines, experiments, diagrams, and *Towards the Cybernetic Factory*, not one bounded object. [P]
- **Date unknown:** *DIA model*. A physical model catalogued among Beer’s consultancy and research materials; the provisional archive list does not establish its full name, makerly boundaries, or date. [A, p. 19]

### Organizational and governmental projects

- **1961–1966:** *SIGMA (Science in General Management)*. An operational-research consultancy co-founded by Beer and Roger Eddison; it is an organization and sustained consultancy project, not solely Beer’s individual obra. [A, pp. 2–3]
- **1966–1970:** IPC Development Division and management-science system. Beer created and directed a corporate research-and-development structure at International Publishing Corporation; its surviving manifestations include organization charts, memoranda, reports, and models rather than a single titled work. [A, pp. 3–6]
- **1971:** *CATA: Organisation and Development*. A consultancy report for Canada’s Committee on Atlantic Transportation and an organizational intervention, surviving in handwritten, typed, and incomplete copies. [A, p. 13]
- **1971–1973:** *Project Cyberstride*. Beer’s original Chilean proposal and the statistical-control portion of what became Cybersyn; its code was collaboratively produced by British and Chilean teams, so sole authorship would be misleading. [A, pp. 29–31; Y]
- **1971–1973:** *Project Cybersyn / Proyecto Synco*. A governmental cybernetic system made collaboratively under Salvador Allende, with Beer as principal architect rather than sole maker; the 1973 coup interrupted it before full realization. [A, pp. 28–33; Y]
- **1971–1973:** *Cybernet*. The telex communications network within Cybersyn and the component most consistently put into operation; it was collaborative infrastructure, not a Beer-authored text. [Y]
- **1971–1973:** *Cyberstride software suite*. Statistical monitoring and forecasting software within Cybersyn; Beer specified its cybernetic logic, while Arthur Andersen programmers and Chilean personnel developed and implemented it. [A, pp. 29–30; Y]
- **1971–1973:** *CHECO*. A Chilean economic-simulation project associated with Cybersyn; it remained incomplete and unreliable, and Beer commissioned or guided a team rather than making every component himself. [A, pp. 29–30; Y]
- **1972:** *Cybersyn Operations Room*. A total decision environment of people, chairs, controls, and wall displays; Beer specified it as a symbiotic control machine, while Chilean designers and engineers created its physical manifestation. [Y]
- **1972–1973:** *Cyberfolk*. A proposed participatory feedback system through which citizens could register reactions to policy; it remained unrealized, making the intention—the project—the work. [Y]
- **1974:** *Towards Practical Foresight Systems*. A completed draft arising from the CHECO work; the archive attributes the eventual report to the CHECO team rather than unambiguously to Beer alone. [A, p. 30]
- **1974–1979/80:** Warburtons cybernetic reorganization. A continuing industrial consultancy that Beer began as a “cybernetic overhaul”; its work survives diffusely in correspondence and organizational records. [A, p. 7]
- **1974:** Dominican Republic port-planning intervention. A consultancy from which Beer was expected to produce a plan, but the archive describes political conflict and does not establish a completed Beer design. [A, pp. 8–9]
- **1970s:** Canadian National Railway cybernetic-services work. A cluster of organizational reports, models, and presentations made with other participants; boundaries of individual authorship are not consistently recorded. [A, pp. 12–17]
- **1979:** City of London, Ontario integrated managerial support system. A municipal systems project proposed through Ernst & Ernst with Beer’s participation; it is a collaborative consultancy rather than a single authored object. [A, pp. 19–20]
- **1980s:** *URUCIB*. A Uruguayan governmental information-system project based on the Viable System Model; Víctor Ganón led it and Beer served as adviser, so it should not be attributed to Beer alone. [L]
- **Undated / unrealized:** Zimbabwe organizational project. The archive records correspondence and information sheets but states that the project never came to fruition and that Beer never visited; the plan, not a completed system, is the relevant work. [A, pp. 10–11]

### Painting and mixed-media art

- **Dates various:** Individual paintings. Beer made a substantial visual-art corpus and exhibited paintings, but the consulted scholarly bibliography does not catalogue each canvas, and the archive catalogue does not provide a complete catalogue raisonné; claiming an exhaustive title list would therefore be false. [C; M]
- **1981:** *Shakta: Self-Portrait*. A painted self-portrait, independently identified in the Stafford Beer painting archive. [V]
- **Date uncertain:** *Requiem* painting cycle. A sequence of paintings intended to be viewed in an order determined by lines laid out as an enneagram; it is an installation-like Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a set of autonomous canvases. Its date and complete constituent-title list are not established by the consulted bibliography. [M]
- **Dates unknown:** Untitled or incompletely catalogued paintings, drawings, charts, and models. The archive confirms their survival but does not supply a complete work-level inventory or secure attribution for every object, so they resist conversion into separate named entries. [A, pp. 4, 30, 53–54; C]
