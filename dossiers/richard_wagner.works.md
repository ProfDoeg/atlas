## Works by Wilhelm Richard Wagner

### Gesamtkunstwerke, stage works, and stage projects

- **1826–1828:** *Leubald* (WWV 1). Five-act tragedy; its intended music is lost, while the dramatic text survives.
- **c. 1830:** *Schäferoper* (WWV 6). Untitled pastoral-opera project, fragmentary and lost; it survives principally as evidence of an intention rather than as a performable work.
- **1832–1833:** *Die Hochzeit* (WWV 31). Unfinished opera: Wagner abandoned and reportedly destroyed much of it, but an introduction, chorus, septet, and dramatic material survive.
- **1833–1834:** *Die Feen* (WWV 32). Completed three-act *große romantische Oper*, revised in 1834 and first staged posthumously in 1888.
- **1835–1836:** *Das Liebesverbot oder Die Novize von Palermo* (WWV 38). Completed two-act *große komische Oper*, combining Wagner’s own libretto and music.
- **c. 1836–1842:** *Die hohe Braut* (WWV 40). Completed libretto for a five-act grand opera, but not a completed Wagner composition; Johann Friedrich Kittl later set the text as *Bianca und Giuseppe*.
- **c. 1838:** *Männerlist größer als Frauenlist oder Die glückliche Bärenfamilie* (WWV 48). Comic-opera project with a completed libretto but only three musical numbers in piano score; its identity lies between dramatic text and unfinished opera.
- **1837–1840; revisions 1843–1847:** *Rienzi, der Letzte der Tribunen* (WWV 49). Five-act *große tragische Oper* and an early Gesamtkunstwerk in the practical sense that Wagner supplied both poem and score.
- **1840–1841; revisions 1846–1860:** *Der fliegende Holländer* (WWV 63). Three-act romantic opera whose several authorized forms resist reduction to a single immutable score.
- **c. 1841–1843:** *Die Sarazenin* (WWV 66). Opera project surviving as dramatic drafts, without a completed musical setting.
- **1842:** *Die Bergwerke zu Falun* (WWV 67). Opera project represented by a drafted libretto but no completed music.
- **1842–1845; revisions through 1875:** *Tannhäuser und der Sängerkrieg auf Wartburg* (WWV 70). Three-act romantic opera existing in materially different Dresden, Paris, and later mixed states rather than one uncontested final version.
- **1845–1848:** *Lohengrin* (WWV 75). Three-act romantic opera for which Wagner wrote both dramatic poem and music.
- **1846–1849:** *Friedrich I* (WWV 76). Historical-opera project surviving only in sketches; calling it an opera describes the project rather than a finished stage work.
- **1849:** *Jesus von Nazareth* (WWV 80). Dramatic project surviving as a prose scenario and a single musical sketch.
- **1849–1850:** *Achilleus* (WWV 81). Project for a drama or opera, represented only by prose drafts.
- **1849–1850:** *Wieland der Schmied* (WWV 82). Project for a heroic opera, surviving in prose designs rather than a musical work.
- **1848–1874:** *Der Ring des Nibelungen* (WWV 86). A four-part *Bühnenfestspiel*—poem, music, staging conception, and festival project—whose refusal to separate media makes “Gesamtkunstwerk” more exact than “book” or “opera.”
- **1851–1854:** *Das Rheingold* (WWV 86A). “Preliminary evening” of the *Ring*, complete as a music drama but conceived as one member of the larger tetralogy.
- **1851–1856:** *Die Walküre* (WWV 86B). First day of the *Ring*.
- **1851–1871:** *Siegfried* (WWV 86C). Second day of the *Ring*, developed from the earlier project *Der junge Siegfried* and composed across a long interruption.
- **1848–1874:** *Götterdämmerung* (WWV 86D). Third day of the *Ring*, originating in the differently conceived drama *Siegfrieds Tod*.
- **1856:** *Die Sieger* (WWV 89). Buddhist stage project surviving only as a short prose sketch; it resists being called a musical work because no score was produced.
- **1854–1859:** *Tristan und Isolde* (WWV 90). Three-act *Handlung*, Wagner’s deliberately unusual genre-name for an integrated dramatic poem and score.
- **1845–1867:** *Die Meistersinger von Nürnberg* (WWV 96). Three-act music drama or opera, conceived intermittently over more than two decades.
- **1868:** *Luthers Hochzeit* (WWV 99). Opera project surviving in prose sketches, with no music composed.
- **1868:** *Ein Lustspiel in einem Akt* (WWV 100). Prose scenario for an unrealized one-act comedy.
- **1870:** *Eine Kapitulation: Lustspiel in antiker Manier* (WWV 102). Political theatrical satire; Wagner made the text, while music associated with it was by Hans Richter and is lost.
- **1865–1882:** *Parsifal* (WWV 111). Three-act *Bühnenweihfestspiel*—a “festival play for the consecration of the stage”—uniting poem, music, ritualized performance, and the Bayreuth project.

### Music for other theatrical works

- **1832:** *Ouvertüre und Bühnenmusik zu König Enzio* (WWV 24). Incidental music for Ernst Raupach’s drama; only the overture survives.
- **1834:** *Musik zu Beim Antritt des neuen Jahres 1835* (WWV 36). Overture, three interludes, and two choruses for Wilhelm Schmale’s allegorical play.
- **1834–1835:** *Ouvertüre und Bühnenmusik zu Columbus* (WWV 37). Music for Theodor Apel’s historical drama; the overture survives, while the remaining incidental music is lost.
- **c. 1837:** *Musik zu Die letzte Heidenverschwörung in Preußen* (WWV 41). Fragmentary, now-lost incidental music for a play attributed in the catalogue to J. Singer.
- **c. 1841:** *Descendons gaiement la courtille* (WWV 65). Wagner’s chorus-and-orchestra contribution to the collaborative vaudeville-ballet-pantomime *La descente de la courtille*.

### Orchestral and chamber works

- **1829:** *Streichquartett D-Dur* (WWV 4). Lost string quartet; it is sometimes confused with a supposed “Starnberg Quartet,” for whose existence no evidence survives.
- **1830:** *Paukenschlag-Ouvertüre B-Dur* (WWV 10). Lost orchestral overture, known by its “timpani-stroke” nickname.
- **c. 1830:** *Politische Ouvertüre* (WWV 11). Lost and probably unfinished orchestral project.
- **1830:** *Ouvertüre mit Schlusschor zu Die Braut von Messina* (WWV 12). Lost or doubtfully surviving overture and chorus inspired by Schiller’s drama.
- **c. 1830:** *Orchesterwerk e-Moll* (WWV 13). Fragmentary orchestral composition without a secure substantive title.
- **1830:** *Ouvertüre C-Dur* (WWV 14). Lost orchestral overture.
- **1831:** *Ouvertüre Es-Dur* (WWV 17). Lost and probably incomplete overture.
- **1831:** *Konzertouvertüre Nr. 1 d-Moll* (WWV 20). Completed concert overture.
- **c. 1832:** *Zwei tragische Entreakte* (WWV 25). Pair of orchestral entr’actes, the first in D major fragmentary and the second in C minor.
- **1832:** *Konzertouvertüre Nr. 2 C-Dur* (WWV 27). Concert overture.
- **1832:** *Symphonie C-Dur* (WWV 29). Wagner’s completed early symphony.
- **1834:** *Symphonie E-Dur* (WWV 35). Unfinished symphony: the first movement was completed, while the second remains fragmentary.
- **1836:** *Polonia-Ouvertüre* (WWV 39). Concert overture in C major.
- **1837:** *Rule-Britannia-Ouvertüre* (WWV 42). Overture in D major based on the British patriotic melody.
- **1839–1840; revised 1855:** *Eine Faust-Ouvertüre* (WWV 59). Orchestral overture first intended as the opening movement of an unrealized *Faust* symphony; the 1855 revision is a distinct authorized state.
- **1844:** *Trauermusik nach Motiven aus Webers Euryanthe* (WWV 73). Funeral music for large wind ensemble and drums, made from Weberian material.
- **1846–1847:** *Symphonien* (WWV 78). Plans and sketches for at least two symphonies; these are projects rather than completed works.
- **1857:** *Träume*, version for violin and chamber orchestra (WWV 91B). Wagner’s instrumental manifestation of one of the *Wesendonck-Lieder*.
- **1864; orchestral version 1871:** *Huldigungsmarsch* (WWV 97). Homage march for Ludwig II, first for military band and later recast for symphony orchestra.
- **1868:** *Romeo und Julie* (WWV 98). Fragmentary symphonic sketch, sometimes described only as a group of themes.
- **1870; revised orchestration 1878:** *Siegfried-Idyll* (WWV 103). Instrumental birthday gift for Cosima Wagner, originally a private household manifestation titled *Tribschen-Idyll* and later enlarged for concert use.
- **1871:** *Kaisermarsch* (WWV 104). Imperial march for orchestra, with an optional or concluding choral element.
- **1874–1883:** *Themen und Melodien zu Symphonien und Ouvertüren* (WWV 107). Catalogue grouping of late orchestral plans and sketches; it resists treatment as one finished composition.
- **1876:** *Großer Festmarsch zur Eröffnung der hundertjährigen Gedenkfeier der Unabhängigkeitserklärung der Vereinigten Staaten* (WWV 110). Large orchestral ceremonial march commissioned for the American centennial.

### Piano works

- **1829:** *Sonate d-Moll* (WWV 2). Lost piano sonata.
- **1829:** *Sonate f-Moll* (WWV 5). Lost piano sonata.
- **1830–1831:** *Klavierauszug von Beethovens Neunter Symphonie* (WWV 9). Solo-piano reduction: Wagner’s made object is an arrangement of Beethoven’s symphony, not an independently authored symphony.
- **1831:** *Sonate B-Dur für Klavier zu vier Händen* (WWV 16). Lost four-hand piano sonata.
- **1831:** *Sonate B-Dur, Op. 1* (WWV 21). Piano sonata and Wagner’s published Opus 1.
- **1831:** *Fantasie fis-Moll* (WWV 22). Single-movement piano fantasy.
- **1831–1832:** *Polonaise D-Dur* (WWV 23A). Polonaise for solo piano.
- **1831–1832:** *Polonaise D-Dur für Klavier zu vier Händen* (WWV 23B). Four-hand manifestation of the polonaise, catalogued separately.
- **1832:** *Große Sonate A-Dur* (WWV 26). Three-movement piano sonata.
- **c. 1840:** *Albumblatt für Ernst Benedikt Kietz: Lied ohne Worte* (WWV 64). Short piano album-leaf.
- **1853:** *Polka G-Dur* (WWV 84). Piano miniature.
- **1853:** *Eine Sonate für das Album von Frau Mathilde Wesendonck* (WWV 85). Piano sonata in A-flat major, simultaneously a substantial composition and a personalized album gift.
- **1854:** *Züricher Vielliebchen-Walzer* (WWV 88). Piano waltz.
- **1858; revised 1881–1882:** *Thema As-Dur*, also called *Elegie* or *Porazzi-Thema* (WWV 93). Brief piano or uninstrumented musical thought whose later revision and posthumous titles make its identity unusually unstable.
- **1861:** *In das Album der Fürstin M.* (WWV 94). Piano album-leaf for Princess Pauline von Metternich.
- **1861:** *Ankunft bei den schwarzen Schwänen* (WWV 95). Piano album-leaf in A-flat major.
- **1875:** *Albumblatt für Frau Betty Schott* (WWV 108). Piano album-leaf in E-flat major.

### Songs, vocal miniatures, and arias

- **1828–1830:** *Lieder* (WWV 7). Group of early song sketches and drafts rather than a finished, authorially fixed cycle.
- **1829:** *Arie* (WWV 3). Lost vocal piece; no secure descriptive title survives.
- **c. 1830:** *Arie für Sopran und Orchester* (WWV 8). Lost vocal-orchestral aria.
- **1831:** *Sieben Kompositionen zu Goethes Faust* (WWV 15). Seven vocal settings drawn from Goethe’s drama.
- **1832:** *Szene und Arie für Sopran und Orchester* (WWV 28). Lost concert or theatrical vocal scene.
- **1832:** *Glockentöne (Abendglocken)* (WWV 30). Lost song on a text by Theodor Apel.
- **1833:** *Aubrys Arie aus Der Vampyr* (WWV 33). Adaptation of an aria from Heinrich Marschner’s opera, including a new concluding allegro by Wagner; authorship is therefore composite.
- **1837:** *Sanfte Wehmut will sich regen* (WWV 43). Bass aria added to Carl Blum’s *Mary, Max und Michel*.
- **c. 1837:** *Gebet aus Die Schweizerfamilie* (WWV 45). Lost bass aria made for Joseph Weigl’s opera.
- **c. 1838:** *Der Tannenbaum* (WWV 50). Song for voice and piano on a text by Georg Scheurlin.
- **1839:** *Norma il predisse, o Druidi* (WWV 52). Bass aria with male chorus and orchestra added to Bellini’s *Norma*; it is a supplement to another composer’s opera.
- **1839:** *Dors, mon enfant* (WWV 53). French song for voice and piano, with an anonymous text.
- **1839:** *Extase* (WWV 54). Fragmentary song on a French text by Victor Hugo.
- **1839:** *Attente* (WWV 55). French song on a text by Victor Hugo.
- **1839:** *La tombe dit à la rose* (WWV 56). Fragmentary French song on a text by Victor Hugo.
- **1839:** *Mignonne* (WWV 57). French song after Pierre de Ronsard.
- **1839:** *Soupir (Tout n’est qu’images fugitives)* (WWV 58). Song for mezzo-soprano and piano on a poem by Jean Reboul.
- **1839–1840:** *Les deux grenadiers* (WWV 60). Song using a French translation of Heinrich Heine.
- **1840:** *Les Adieux de Marie Stuart* (WWV 61). Song for soprano and piano on a text by Pierre-Jean de Béranger.
- **1857–1858:** *Fünf Gedichte für eine Frauenstimme*, commonly *Wesendonck-Lieder* (WWV 91A). Five songs on poems by Mathilde Wesendonck; Wagner called two “studies” for *Tristan und Isolde*, and the familiar orchestral cycle largely reflects later orchestration by Felix Mottl rather than Wagner alone.
- **c. 1858:** *Es ist bestimmt in Gottes Rat* (WWV 92). Draft song on a text by Ernst von Feuchtersleben.
- **1871:** *Der Worte viele sind gemacht* (WWV 105). Humorous unaccompanied song or vocal epigram for Ludwig Kraft.

### Choruses, ceremonial pieces, and domestic manifestations

- **1831–1832:** *Dein ist das Reich* (WWV 19A). Study fugue for voices.
- **1831–1832:** *Doppelfuge* (WWV 19B). Contrapuntal study without fixed instrumentation or text, resisting classification as a finished vocal work.
- **1837:** *Nicolay* (WWV 44). Folk or imperial hymn for soloists, chorus, and orchestra on a text by Heinrich von Brackel.
- **1838–1839:** *Gesang am Grabe von Holtei* (WWV 51). Lost funeral chorus.
- **1843:** *Der Tag erscheint* (WWV 68A). Festive song for unaccompanied male chorus.
- **c. 1843:** *Der Tag erscheint* (WWV 68B). Alternate manifestation for male chorus and brass.
- **1843:** *Das Liebesmahl der Apostel* (WWV 69). Large biblical scene for male voices and orchestra, lying between concert cantata, sacred drama, and spatial performance work.
- **1844:** *Gruß seiner Treuen an Friedrich August II.* (WWV 71). Ceremonial greeting existing in versions for male chorus and winds and for voice and piano.
- **1844:** *An Webers Grabe (Hebt an den Sang)* (WWV 72). Funeral song for male chorus.
- **1869:** *Wahlspruch für die Deutsche Feuerwehr* (WWV 101). Motto-like male chorus: a small ceremonial text that acts publicly as much as it describes.
- **1873; revised 1874:** *Kinder-Katechismus zu Cosels Geburtstag* (WWV 106). Domestic choral jeu d’esprit for Cosima Wagner’s birthday, first with piano and later with orchestra.
- **1877:** *Willkommen in Wahnfried, du heil’ger Christ* (WWV 112). Christmas song for children’s voices, made for the Wahnfried household.
- **c. 1880:** *Ihr Kinder, geschwinde, geschwinde* (WWV 113). Three-part children’s chorus, another domestic manifestation rather than a public concert work.

### Arrangements, interventions, and composite authorship

- **1831:** *Klavierauszug von Haydns Symphonie Nr. 103* (WWV 18). Lost piano reduction of Haydn’s symphony.
- **1833:** *Instrumentation einer Kavatine aus Bellinis Il pirata* (WWV 34). Lost orchestration of music by Bellini.
- **1837–1839:** *Rigaer Opernbearbeitungen* (WWV 46). Catalogue complex encompassing Wagner’s orchestral interventions in existing stage works; some constituent identifications and dates remain approximate.
- **c. 1837:** *Retuschen zu Bellinis Norma* (WWV 46A). Alterations to Bellini’s orchestration.
- **c. 1838:** *Kavatine aus Meyerbeers Robert der Teufel, für Streicher* (WWV 46B). Reorchestration or transcription of music by Meyerbeer.
- **c. 1839:** *Jägerchor aus Webers Euryanthe* (WWV 46C). Reorchestration of Weber’s chorus.
- **date uncertain:** *Bearbeitung aus Halévys Le Guitarrero* (WWV 46D). Arrangement associated with Wagner’s theatrical employment; the catalogue does not warrant a more exact date.
- **date uncertain:** *Bearbeitungen aus Halévys La reine de Chypre* (WWV 46E). Arrangements of music by Halévy.
- **date uncertain:** *Suiten aus Aubers Zanetta ou Jouer avec le feu* (WWV 46F). Arrangements for flute and string trio.
- **c. 1838:** *Li marinari* from Rossini’s *Les soirées musicales* (WWV 47). Instrumentation of Rossini’s duet.
- **1840–1842:** *Pariser Arrangements* (WWV 62). Catalogue group of commercial arrangements made from other composers’ music; it is a body of work rather than a single composition.
- **c. 1840:** *Suiten für Cornets à pistons* (WWV 62A). Lost arrangements of music by various composers.
- **1840–1842:** *Bearbeitungen aus Donizettis La favorite* (WWV 62B). Commercial arrangements from Donizetti’s opera.
- **1840–1842:** *Grande Fantaisie sur La Romanesca, Op. 111, für Klavier zu vier Händen* (WWV 62C). Four-hand arrangement of Henri Herz’s fantasy.
- **1840–1842:** *Bearbeitungen aus Halévys Le Guitarrero* (WWV 62D). Arrangements for assorted instruments.
- **1840–1842:** *Bearbeitungen aus Halévys La reine de Chypre* (WWV 62E). Arrangements for assorted instruments.
- **1840–1842:** *Suiten aus Aubers Zanetta für Flötenquartett* (WWV 62F). Two arrangement suites.
- **c. 1844:** *Instrumentationszusätze zu Spontinis La vestale* (WWV 74). Orchestral supplements to Spontini’s triumphal march.
- **1846–1847:** *Iphigenia in Aulis* (WWV 77). Extensive German adaptation and reorchestration of Gluck’s *Iphigénie en Aulide*; it is neither simply Wagner’s opera nor merely a neutral edition.
- **c. 1848:** *Palestrinas Stabat mater* (WWV 79). Wagner’s performing arrangement of Palestrina.
- **1850:** *Bearbeitung von Mozarts Don Giovanni* (WWV 83). Partly lost adaptation prepared for performance at Zurich.
- **c. 1854:** *Konzertschluss zur Ouvertüre von Glucks Iphigenia in Aulis* (WWV 87). New concert ending appended to Gluck’s overture.
- **1875:** *Wein, Weib und Gesang* (WWV 109). Wagner’s arrangement and instrumentation directions for Johann Strauss II’s waltz.

### Manifestos, theoretical writings, criticism, and public interventions

- **1840:** *Eine Pilgerfahrt zu Beethoven*. Fictional critical tale in which imaginative narrative becomes music criticism.
- **1840–1841:** *Ein Ende in Paris*. Novella about a German musician’s failure and death in Paris.
- **1841:** *Ein glücklicher Abend*. Fictional dialogue or critical tale about musical experience.
- **1841:** *Der Virtuos und der Künstler*. Critical essay distinguishing display from artistic vocation.
- **1841:** *Der Künstler und die Öffentlichkeit*. Essay on the artist’s relation to public life.
- **1842:** *Autobiographische Skizze*. Commissioned autobiographical account; it is self-fashioning as well as documentation.
- **1846:** *Bericht über die Aufführung der neunten Symphonie von Beethoven*. Performance report and act of interpretation concerning Beethoven’s Ninth.
- **1848:** *Der Nibelungen-Mythus als Entwurf zu einem Drama*. Prose myth and dramatic project from which the *Ring* developed.
- **1848:** *Wie verhalten sich republikanische Bestrebungen dem Königtume gegenüber?* Political memorandum advocating a transformed monarchy.
- **1849:** *Die Wibelungen: Weltgeschichte aus der Sage*. Historical-mythic essay joining political genealogy, legend, and speculative history.
- **1849:** *Die Revolution*. Political manifesto published anonymously; it acts by calling revolutionary agency into being rather than merely describing events.
- **1849:** *Der Mensch und die bestehende Gesellschaft*. Political-social essay and manifesto of emancipation.
- **1849:** *Die Kunst und die Revolution*. Manifesto arguing for a revolutionary transformation of art and society.
- **1849:** *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft*. Theoretical manifesto formulating the integrated “artwork of the future” and the social conditions of its making.
- **1849–1850:** *Kunst und Klima*. Speculative essay on environment, human development, and art.
- **1850; substantially expanded 1869:** *Das Judenthum in der Musik*. Antisemitic polemic, first issued pseudonymously and later republished under Wagner’s name with additions; the two states should not be silently conflated, and its status as an exclusionary intervention is integral to what the text is.
- **1851:** *Über die Goethestiftung*. Open letter to Franz Liszt on cultural patronage and institutions.
- **1851:** *Ein Theater in Zürich*. Institutional proposal for theatrical organization.
- **1851:** *Oper und Drama*. Three-part theoretical treatise on poetry, music, drama, and their projected union.
- **1851:** *Eine Mitteilung an meine Freunde*. Autobiographical and programmatic preface that retrospectively constructs Wagner’s artistic development.
- **1852:** *Über musikalische Kritik*. Essay on the function and failures of music criticism.
- **1853:** *Die bisherige Sonderkunst und das Kunstwerk der Zukunft*. Programmatic essay or lecture closely related to Wagner’s Gesamtkunstwerk theory; its publication history includes collaborative circumstances noted by the digital scholarly bibliography.
- **1854:** *Das Kunstwerk der Zukunft und die Kritik der Gegenwart*. Polemical response concerning the reception of Wagner’s theory.
- **1860:** *Brief an einen französischen Freund als Vorwort zu einer Prosa-Übersetzung seiner Operndichtungen*. Public letter functioning as a preface and explanation of Wagner’s dramatic practice.
- **1864:** *Über Staat und Religion*. Political-philosophical memorandum written for Ludwig II; both counsel and theoretical intervention.
- **1865:** *Tagebuchaufzeichnungen für Ludwig II.* Private journal-like communications made for the king; they resist simple classification as either diary or treatise.
- **1867–1868:** *Deutsche Kunst und deutsche Politik*. Series of essays linking cultural production to German national politics.
- **1868:** *Censuren*. Collection of polemical critical judgments.
- **1869:** *Über das Dirigieren*. Practical-critical essay on conducting; partly a protocol for musical performance and partly a polemic.
- **1870:** *Beethoven*. Extended commemorative essay interpreting Beethoven through Wagner’s aesthetics and Schopenhauerian philosophy.
- **1871:** *Vorwort zur Gesamtausgabe*. Preface to Wagner’s collected writings, itself an act of retrospective selection and construction of an oeuvre.
- **1872:** *Über die Bestimmung der Oper*. Essay on opera’s artistic purpose.
- **1872:** *Richard Wagner über den angeblichen Brief Victor Hugos an ihn*. Public corrective or statement concerning a purported letter from Hugo.
- **1872:** *Bericht an den Deutschen Wagner-Verein über die Umstände und Schicksale, welche die Ausführung des Bühnenfestspieles Der Ring des Nibelungen begleiten*. Institutional report and appeal supporting the Bayreuth project.
- **1873:** *Das Bühnenfestspielhaus zu Bayreuth*. Exposition of the festival-theatre project: architectural, institutional, and artistic protocol rather than merely descriptive prose.
- **1873:** *Ein Rückblick auf die Bühnenfestspiele des Jahres 1876* [projected retrospective materials are associated with the Bayreuth writings]. Dating and title boundaries vary between collected editions, so these Bayreuth reports should be checked at item level in the critical digital bibliography.
- **1877:** *Über Schauspieler und Sänger*. Practical and theoretical essay on stage performance.
- **1878:** *Was ist deutsch?* Essay on German identity, first conceived earlier and published in substantially developed form.
- **1879:** *Wollen wir hoffen?* Cultural-political essay.
- **1879:** *Publikum und Popularität*. Essay on audiences and artistic popularity.
- **1880:** *Religion und Kunst*. Late theoretical essay proposing a renewed relation between religion and art.
- **1880:** *Was nützt diese Erkenntnis?* Supplement to *Religion und Kunst*.
- **1881:** *Erkenne dich selbst*. Late cultural and moral polemic.
- **1881:** *Heldentum und Christentum*. Essay connecting Wagner’s ideas of heroism, Christianity, race, and culture; its ideological claims are part of the object and should not be neutralized by calling it simply “aesthetic theory.”
- **1882:** *Das Bühnenweihfestspiel in Bayreuth*. Essay framing *Parsifal* and its Bayreuth performance as a consecratory institution.
- **1883:** *Über das Weibliche im Menschlichen*. Unfinished late essay; its fragmentary state is essential and no completed argument should be inferred.

### Autobiography, diaries, poems, and correspondence

- **1865–1880; privately printed 1870–1880:** *Mein Leben*. Dictated autobiography, written with Cosima’s assistance and issued privately in four parts; its collaborative production and deliberate self-mythologizing complicate attribution to Wagner alone.
- **1865–1882:** *Das braune Buch*. Notebook containing diary entries, drafts, poems, musical ideas, and memoranda; it is a heterogeneous working object rather than a single continuous literary work.
- **1848–1874:** *Der Ring des Nibelungen: Dichtung*. The dramatic poem was printed and circulated independently before completion of the score, but it is also inseparable from the larger Gesamtkunstwerk; treating it as merely a “book” obscures that double status.
- **various dates:** *Operndichtungen and dramatic poems*. Wagner repeatedly published the texts of his stage works separately and later incorporated them into his collected writings; these are textual manifestations of the music dramas, not additional operas.
- **various dates:** *Occasional poems, dedications, album verses, slogans, and verse greetings*. These form a dispersed textual corpus in the collected writings and correspondence; many resist elevation into independently titled “works,” and a library edition should not be mistaken for a new obra.
- **1830s–1883:** *Briefe*. Wagner’s correspondence comprises thousands of individually situated communications now edited in *Sämtliche Briefe*. It is a made documentary corpus, but not a single authorially designed work; individual letters become separate entries only when Wagner himself published or repurposed them as open letters, prefaces, manifestos, or essays.
- **1871–1883:** *Gesammelte Schriften und Dichtungen*. Wagner’s authorially supervised collected corpus, originally issued in nine volumes during his lifetime, with a tenth appearing in 1883; it is an obra of selection and revision as well as a container of earlier texts, and later “complete” editions should not be counted as new Wagner works.

This is the standard thematic catalogue underlying WWV 1–113; bibliographic identification is recorded in the [catalogue summary and references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_compositions_by_Richard_Wagner#Sources).
- Richard Wagner-Gesamtausgabe, *Richard Wagner: Sämtliche Werke*, series A and B (Mainz: Schott, 1970–), the critical collected-works edition; its scope and volume structure are described by the [Akademie der Wissenschaften und der Literatur, Mainz](https://www.adwmainz.de/projekte/richard-wagner-saemtliche-werke/informationen.html) and in the [edition overview](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagner-Gesamtausgabe).
- *Catalogue complet des œuvres musicales de Richard Wagner: Wagner-Werk-Verzeichnis (WWV)*, a work-by-work public concordance derived from Deathridge–Geck–Voss, including lost, fragmentary, arranged, and projected items: [complete WWV concordance (PDF)](https://richard-wagner-web-museum.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Catalogue-WWV-integral.pdf).
- Richard Wagner, *Gesammelte Schriften und Dichtungen*, ten volumes (Leipzig: E. W. Fritzsch, 1871–1883), Wagner’s authorially organized collected writings; a digitized volume and its cumulative contents are available through [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=WYJOAAAAMAAJ).
- *Richard Wagner Schriften – digital*, scholarly digital bibliography of Wagner’s writings, recording titles, textual forms, first publication, transmission, dates, and attribution: [project description](https://www.wagner-schriften.de/projektbeschreibung) and [bibliographic contents](https://www.wagner-schriften.de/inhalte).
- Richard Wagner, *Sämtliche Schriften und Dichtungen*, later expanded collected edition, digitized by the Warburg Institute: [volumes and contents](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/dbh460b2404852v-11-12.pdf).
- *Richard Wagner: Werke, Schriften und Briefe*, digital corpus based on *Sämtliche Schriften und Dichtungen*, *Mein Leben*, *Das braune Buch*, and *Sämtliche Briefe*: [database description and bibliographic basis](https://dbis.ur.de/CAE/resources/3685).
- Richard Wagner, *Sämtliche Briefe* (Leipzig/Wiesbaden: Deutscher Verlag für Musik/Breitkopf & Härtel, 1967–), the standard critical correspondence edition, used here to delimit correspondence as a corpus rather than manufacture separate “works” from every surviving letter.
