## Works by Sir Isaac Newton

### Mathematical and natural-philosophical obras

- **c. 1664–1666:** *Waste Book*. A working notebook in which Newton developed infinite series, the binomial theorem, fluxional methods, and investigations of motion; it is neither a finished treatise nor a single work, but an evolving mathematical workshop ([Newton Project, mathematical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mathematical); [Portsmouth catalogue](https://archive.org/details/catalogueofports00newt)).
- **October 1666:** *The October 1666 Tract on Fluxions*. An unpublished mathematical tract setting out an early form of Newton’s method of fluxions; its modern descriptive title comes from the manuscript’s date rather than from a title supplied for publication ([Newton Project, “Enlightening Science”](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/about-us/enlightening-science)).
- **1669:** *De analysi per aequationes numero terminorum infinitas*. A manuscript treatise on infinite series and quadrature, circulated through Isaac Barrow and John Collins but first printed in 1711 ([Gray, pp. 46–55](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400); [Newton Project, mathematical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mathematical)).
- **1669–1671:** *Lectiones opticae*. Newton’s Lucasian lectures on optics, revised in more than one manuscript form and published only after his death; they are a lecture-course project rather than a book originally prepared for print ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific); [Portsmouth catalogue](https://archive.org/details/catalogueofports00newt)).
- **1671:** *Methodus fluxionum et serierum infinitarum*. A systematic calculus treatise composed in Latin, left unpublished by Newton and issued in John Colson’s English translation in 1736; the posthumous printed title and form should not be mistaken for Newton’s own completed publication ([Newton Project, “History of the Method of Fluxions”](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00356); [Gray, pp. 46–55](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **c. 1665–1672:** *The Lawes of Motion*. A short manuscript investigation of collision and motion, surviving as an unfinished set of propositions rather than a polished treatise ([Newton Project, NATP00105](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/NATP00105)).
- **6 February 1671/2:** *A Theory concerning Light and Colours*. A paper communicated to the Royal Society and printed in the *Philosophical Transactions*; it announces and experimentally argues for Newton’s theory of colour ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific)).
- **8 July 1672:** *A Series of Queries Propounded … to be Determined by Experiments*. An experimental protocol in interrogative form: questions designed to direct tests of the new theory of light rather than merely describe it ([Royal Society journals archive guide](https://royalsociety.org/-/media/journals/librarians/archive/journals-archive-brochure.pdf); [Gray, pp. 35–45](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1672–1676:** *Replies concerning the theory of light and colours*. A polemical and experimental correspondence with Ignace-Gaston Pardies, Robert Hooke, Francis Linus, Anthony Lucas, Henry Oldenburg, and others. The constituent letters are separate interventions, not chapters of a planned book, and survive in manuscript and printed states ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific)).
- **1675:** *An Hypothesis Explaining the Properties of Light*. A speculative paper read to the Royal Society, combining an ether hypothesis with optical, chemical, physiological, and gravitational conjectures; its genre is explicitly hypothesis, not settled doctrine ([Newton Project, NATP00002](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/NATP00002)).
- **early 1675:** *Discourse Concerning Light and Colours*. A substantial manuscript draft associated with the 1675 optical work; it remains a draft and therefore resists treatment as an independently completed publication ([Newton Project, NATP00126](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/NATP00126)).
- **28 February 1678/9:** *Letter to Robert Boyle on the Aether*. A privately transmitted speculative account of an ethereal medium and its possible physical and chemical operations; it is a letter functioning as a miniature natural-philosophical treatise ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific)).
- **1684:** *De motu corporum in gyrum*. A short manuscript sent to Edmond Halley on orbital motion under centripetal force; it became the generative project from which the *Principia* grew, though the surviving versions differ in extent and wording ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific); [Portsmouth catalogue](https://archive.org/details/catalogueofports00newt)).
- **1685:** *De motu corporum liber primus*. An expanding draft of the first book of the *Principia*. It is best understood as an intermediate manifestation of that project, not an entirely separate published obra ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific)).
- **1687; revised 1713 and 1726:** *Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica*. A three-book mathematical-natural-philosophical obra establishing laws of motion and a system of gravitation. The second and third editions are materially revised manifestations—most conspicuously through the *General Scholium*—but not three unrelated works ([Newton Project, “Enlightening Science”](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/about-us/enlightening-science); [Gray, pp. 5–34](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1680s; revised after 1687:** *De mundi systemate* / *A Treatise of the System of the World*. A non-mathematical exposition related to the projected third book of the *Principia*, abandoned in that role and published posthumously in Latin and English in 1728; the relationship between its manuscript versions and those two printings is editorially complex ([Newton Project, THEM00291](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/THEM00291); [Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **c. 1691–1692; published 1704:** *Tractatus de quadratura curvarum*. A treatise on fluxions and quadrature, repeatedly revised before Newton appended it to the first *Opticks*; composition date and printed date therefore differ substantially ([Newton Project, mathematical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mathematical); [Gray, pp. 46–55](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1692–1693:** *Four Letters to Richard Bentley*. Four letters presenting arguments about cosmic order, matter, gravity, and divine agency, later printed as a set although originally made as separate pieces of correspondence ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious); [Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1701:** *Scala graduum caloris. Calorum descriptiones et signa*. An anonymously printed paper proposing a temperature scale and an experimental protocol for measuring high temperatures; attribution to Newton is accepted in the standard bibliography ([Gray, pp. 65–67](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400); [Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific)).
- **1704:** *Opticks: or, A Treatise of the Reflexions, Refractions, Inflexions and Colours of Light*. An experimental treatise whose propositions are joined to open-ended “Queries”; the volume also carried two mathematical treatises that Newton removed from later editions ([Newton Project, NATP00031](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/NATP00031); [Gray, pp. 35–45](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1704:** *Enumeratio linearum tertii ordinis*. A classification of cubic curves, printed as one of the two mathematical appendices to the first *Opticks* and omitted from later editions of that optical work ([Newton Project, mathematical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mathematical); [Gray, pp. 46–55](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1707; revised 1712:** *Arithmetica universalis, sive de compositione et resolutione arithmetica liber*. An algebraic lecture-course published by William Whiston from Newton’s Lucasian material, initially without Newton’s knowledge or consent; its status as a Newton-authorized book is therefore disputed, although Newton participated in revision of the second edition ([Newton Project, account of the publication](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/OTHE00089); [Gray, pp. 56–64](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1711:** *Methodus differentialis*. A short treatise on interpolation and finite differences, printed with Newton’s consent in the *Analysis per quantitatum series, fluxiones ac differentias* collection ([Newton Project, historical account](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/OTHE00075); [Gray, pp. 46–55](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1712:** *Commercium epistolicum D. Johannis Collins et aliorum de analysi promota*. An anonymously issued Royal Society dossier in the Newton–Leibniz priority dispute. Newton shaped, supplied, and secretly revised the supposedly institutional report, but sole authorship of the composite volume would be misleading ([Newton Project, mathematical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mathematical); [Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1713:** *General Scholium*. A theological-natural-philosophical manifesto appended to the second edition of the *Principia*, acting publicly to define the limits of legitimate explanation as well as describing Newton’s system ([Newton Project, 1713 Principia materials](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/about-us/enlightening-science)).
- **1714–1715:** *An Account of the Book Entitled Commercium Epistolicum*. Newton’s initially anonymous public defence of the Royal Society’s priority judgment, printed in the *Philosophical Transactions* in 1715 and revised again for publication in 1722 and 1725 ([Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400); [Newton Project, mathematical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mathematical)).
- **c. 1700–1727:** *History of the Method of Fluxions* and associated priority papers. A shifting collection of drafts, memoranda, historical annotations, and attacks concerning Leibniz; it never stabilized as one completed work and its boundaries remain archival rather than authorial ([Newton Project, NATP00356](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/diplomatic/NATP00356); [Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific)).

### Chronological, scriptural, and ecclesiastical projects

- **c. 1670s–1720s:** *Theologiae gentilis origines philosophicae*. A repeatedly rewritten Latin project connecting ancient religion, natural knowledge, idolatry, and political history. It survives through overlapping drafts and notes rather than as a text with one settled boundary ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious); [Newton Manuscripts Catalogue introduction](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/introduction-to-the-newton-manuscripts-catalogue)).
- **late 1670s–1690s:** *Treatises and drafts on the symbolism of biblical prophecy*. A large, multilingual manuscript complex concerning Revelation, prophetic language, Solomon’s Temple, and Church history. Some sections have modern editorial titles, but the whole resists being called one finished work ([Newton Project, Yahuda MS 2 / THEM00045](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/THEM00045)).
- **c. 1680s–1690s:** *Proœmium and first chapter of a treatise on Church history*. The opening of an unfinished Latin history of the Church; its incompleteness is part of its identity, not missing bibliographical information ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious)).
- **c. 1680s–1690s:** *An Historical Account of Two Notable Corruptions of Scripture*. A textual-critical dissertation composed as a letter to a friend, probably intended for mediated publication but suppressed during Newton’s life; it appeared posthumously in 1754, and the addressee and stages of revision complicate its status as a single letter ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious); [Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1680s–1720s:** *Treatise on Revelation*. A very large prophetic-exegetical project preserved in multiple drafts, especially Yahuda MS 1 and related manuscripts. Its sections were repeatedly rearranged and only some material was transformed into the posthumous *Observations* ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious); [Newton Manuscripts Catalogue introduction](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/introduction-to-the-newton-manuscripts-catalogue)).
- **early 1690s:** *Paradoxical Questions concerning the Morals & Actions of Athanasius & His Followers*. A sixteen-question historical-theological polemic against Athanasian authority, surviving in more than one draft and unpublished in Newton’s lifetime ([Newton Project, THEM00010](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/THEM00010)).
- **after 1693–1720s:** *Papers relating to chronology and Theologiae gentilis origines philosophicae*. A 275-folio working archive of chronology, mythology, and theology. It is an obra in the sense of a sustained project, but not a work whose many notes can honestly be assigned individual authorial titles ([Newton Project, THEM00098](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/THEM00098)).
- **c. 1700s:** *Twenty-three Queries about the Word ὁμοούσιος*. An interrogative theological investigation into the Nicene term “consubstantial”; like the optical queries, it operates as a research protocol as much as a declarative treatise ([Newton Project, THEM00011](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/THEM00011)).
- **post-1710:** *Irenicum, or Ecclesiastical Polyty Tending to Peace*. A projected settlement of fundamental and non-fundamental articles of faith, surviving as several complete and partial drafts, positions, and theses rather than one final text ([Newton Project, THEM00003](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/THEM00003)).
- **post-1710:** *A Short Schem of the True Religion*. A concise theological scheme surviving with variant drafts; spelling and title vary across the manuscripts, and Newton did not publish it ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious)).
- **c. 1710s–1720s:** *The Original of Religions*. A group of drafts arguing for an ancient true religion subsequently corrupted into idolatry; the title covers related but not fully unified manuscripts ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious)).
- **before 1727; published 1728:** *The Chronology of Ancient Kingdoms Amended*. Newton’s long chronological reconstruction of ancient history, left in a state judged publishable by his executors but edited after his death; the 1728 volume is consequently a posthumous manifestation of a still-revisable project ([Newton Manuscripts Catalogue introduction](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/introduction-to-the-newton-manuscripts-catalogue); [Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **before 1727; published 1733:** *Observations upon the Prophecies of Daniel, and the Apocalypse of St. John*. A posthumously assembled prophetic treatise derived from Newton’s extensive manuscripts; the printed unity owes something to editorial selection and should not be projected uncritically onto the archive ([Newton Project, religious writings](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/religious); [Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).

### Chymical projects, notebooks, and protocols

- **c. 1664–1696:** *Notebook containing alchemical notes and experimental reports* (Cambridge University Library, MS Add. 3975). A 283-page laboratory and reading notebook accumulated over three decades; it is simultaneously protocol, experiment log, commonplace book, and project, not a finished treatise ([Newton Project, ALCH00110](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00110)).
- **late 1660s–1690s:** *Alchemical transcripts, translations, extracts, recipes, and experimental records*. Roughly seventy catalogued manuscript groupings survive, many copying or transforming other writers and several partly written by amanuenses. They resist enumeration as seventy autonomous Newton “works”: authorship ranges from original composition through translation and annotation to mere possession ([Newton Project, alchemical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/alchemical)).
- **c. 1670–1675:** *Of Natures Obvious Laws & Processes in Vegetation*. An unfinished chymical-natural-philosophical essay exploring active principles in matter and vegetation; the manuscript breaks off and was never readied for publication ([Newton Project, alchemical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/alchemical)).
- **c. 1670:** *Verses at the End of B[asil] Valentine’s Mystery of the Microcosm*. A 569-line manuscript rendering or adaptation of alchemical verse. Because it derives from another source, it is better classified as translational or scribal making than as wholly original poetry ([Newton Project, ALCH00052](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00052)).
- **1670s–1690s:** *Ripley Expounded* and *Notes upon Ripley*. An extended exposition of George Ripley’s alchemy accompanied by a separate body of notes; its dependence on an earlier author makes it commentary rather than an independent system ([Newton Project, ALCH00043](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00043)).
- **1680s:** *Index Chemicus*. An elaborate, approximately 34,000-word alphabetical research index to more than 144 alchemical writings, supplemented by definitions and miniature essays. It is an unfinished knowledge apparatus—part index, part lexicon, part research project—rather than a conventional book ([Newton Project, ALCH00019](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00019)).
- **early 1690s:** *Notanda Chemica* and *Sententiae notabiles*. Organized alchemical reading notes in a notebook that begins an abandoned second *Index Chemicus*; the entries are scholarly apparatus and cannot all be treated as original works ([Newton Project, ALCH00027](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00027)).
- **early 1690s:** *Practica Mariae Prophetissae in Artem Alchemicam*. A short Latin alchemical composition or compilation attributed by the catalogue to Newton; its traditional materials make the degree of original authorship difficult to delimit ([Newton Project, alchemical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/alchemical)).
- **certainly after 1689, probably c. 1696:** *Praxis*. Newton’s most highly organized surviving alchemical treatise, joining theoretical chapters to practical operations. It remains unpublished and unfinished, but scholarship regards its conceptual organization as Newton’s own despite its dense dependence on earlier literature ([Newton Project, ALCH00094](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00094)).

### Administrative, monetary, and institutional works

- **1672:** *Geographia generalis* by Bernhardus Varenius, Newton edition. Newton did not author Varenius’s geography: he corrected, augmented, and edited a Cambridge edition for students. It belongs here as editorial making, not as an original Newtonian book ([Gray](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **1696–1727:** *Mint papers*. Reports, memoranda, assays, tables, warrants, examinations, prosecution papers, correspondence, and accounts produced or drafted during Newton’s service as Warden and Master of the Mint. The Newton Project catalogues well over a thousand Newton-attributed items, but many are collaborative, clerical, copied, or untitled institutional acts and resist conversion into a list of autonomous works ([Newton Project, Mint papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mint)).
- **1696–1699:** *Counterfeiting examinations and prosecution dossiers*. Newton personally conducted or directed extensive witness examinations and assembled evidence during the Great Recoinage. These are juridical protocols and institutional records—often written in formulaic or collaborative form—not literary works ([Newton Project, Mint papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mint)).
- **1696–1727:** *Reports on coinage*. A series of official analyses of fineness, weight, exchange, foreign coins, Mint procedure, and monetary policy. George John Gray bibliographs them collectively because their manuscript and printed states overlap and because some were issued anonymously or as government documents ([Gray, pp. 68–75](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400); [Newton Project, Mint papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mint)).
- **1701:** *On the Quantity of Coyn in the Nation*. A monetary-policy memorandum estimating England’s circulating coin; it survives as an official manuscript rather than an authorially published economic treatise ([Newton Project, MINT00265](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/MINT00265)).
- **21 September 1717:** *Report on the Value of the Guinea*. An official report recommending a reduced valuation of the gold guinea, an intervention that helped establish Britain’s de facto gold standard. It is an administrative act with major economic effects rather than a book ([Newton Project, Mint papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mint); [Gray, pp. 68–75](https://archive.org/details/cu31924001085400)).
- **c. 1705–1725:** *Tables and reports on assays, coin weights, and international monetary values*. These calculational objects were made for recurrent Mint decisions and sometimes survive only as drafts or appended tables; their “work” status lies in what they enabled the institution to do ([Newton Project, Mint papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/mint)).

### Instruments and other material constructions

- **1668:** *First reflecting telescope*. Newton constructed his first successful reflecting telescope using a concave primary mirror and diagonal secondary mirror. The instrument itself does not survive, so it is a documented lost object rather than the telescope now held by the Royal Society ([Royal Society object catalogue](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=MOB%2F005&src=CalmView.Catalog)).
- **1671:** *Second reflecting telescope*. A paper, cardboard, wood, and metal instrument made by Newton and demonstrated to the Royal Society; the object traditionally identified as this telescope survives in the Society’s collection, although its later mounting and inscribed plate are not themselves proof that every component remains exactly as Newton made it ([Royal Society object catalogue](https://catalogues.royalsociety.org/CalmView/Record.aspx?id=MOB%2F005&src=CalmView.Catalog); [Whipple Museum replica record](https://collections.whipplemuseum.cam.ac.uk/objects/9215/)).
- **1660s–1670s:** *Optical apparatus for prism, lens, and colour experiments*. Newton made or arranged darkened rooms, apertures, lenses, prisms, mirrors, screens, and measuring setups described in his optical papers. These were temporary experimental manifestations and protocols rather than individually titled, securely surviving objects ([Newton Project, scientific papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/scientific); [Newton Project, Opticks](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/NATP00031)).
- **1660s–1690s:** *Furnaces, crucibles, alloys, and other chymical experimental preparations*. Newton’s laboratory notebooks document repeated material constructions and trials, but the apparatus and products generally do not survive and often have no fixed title or discrete boundary. Calling each one a “work” would misrepresent an iterative experimental practice ([Newton Project, ALCH00110](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/catalogue/record/ALCH00110); [Newton Project, alchemical papers](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/alchemical)).

### Correspondence and notebooks that resist work-status

- **1661–1665:** *Trinity College notebook*. A student commonplace book containing notes on reading, languages, philosophy, mathematics, and the celebrated *Quaestiones quaedam philosophicae*. It is a made intellectual environment rather than one authored text ([Newton Project, notebooks](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/his-notebooks)).
- **1662:** *List of sins*. A private devotional memorandum conventionally known by an editorial description, not an authorial title; its existence does not make it a literary work ([Newton Project, notebooks](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/his-notebooks)).
- **1660s–1727:** *Scientific, mathematical, theological, personal, and official correspondence*. Hundreds of letters survive, ranging from short receipts to treatise-like interventions. The scholarly editions catalogue letters separately because correspondence is a relational corpus: authorship may be shared through copying, dictation, enclosure, and institutional transmission ([Newton Project, correspondence introduction](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/introduction); [Newton Project, all works](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/texts/newtons-works/all)).
- **1660s–1727:** *Untitled drafts, calculations, diagrams, reading notes, extracts, tables, and fragments*. The manuscript catalogue records thousands of such acts of making, many embedded inside composite physical manuscripts. They cannot responsibly be promoted into separately titled obras; their resistance to work-status is itself the accurate classification ([Newton Manuscripts Catalogue introduction](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/introduction-to-the-newton-manuscripts-catalogue); [Portsmouth catalogue](https://archive.org/details/catalogueofports00newt)).
