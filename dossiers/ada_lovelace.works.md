## Works by Augusta Ada King

### Invention project

- **1828:** *Flyology*. A projected illustrated treatise on artificial flight, accompanied by experiments with wings and plans for a steam-powered flying machine. Lovelace announced an intention to write the book, but no completed text or machine is known; its status is therefore project rather than finished obra. ([Hollings, Martin, and Rice](https://doi.org/10.1080/17498430.2017.1325297))

### Mathematical working papers

- **1840–1842:** *Mathematical correspondence with Augustus De Morgan*. A sequence of letters functioning simultaneously as correspondence, mathematical exercises, and a self-directed course in algebra, functions, series, differential and integral calculus, dynamics, and complex numbers; it resists classification as a single work because knowledge was produced dialogically between pupil and teacher. The surviving Lovelace letters are individually dated and indexed in Bodleian Library, Lovelace–Byron Papers, box 170. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/correspondence-with-de-morgan/))

- **Undated, circa 1840–1842:** *Notes and exercises on differential equations* [untitled]. Six manuscript pages explaining or working through differential equations; a study document rather than a publication, preserved as box 170, folios 168–170. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/correspondence-with-de-morgan/))

- **Undated, circa 1840–1842:** *Trigonometric calculations involving surds* [untitled]. A manuscript exercise or working sheet preserved as box 170, folios 171–172; the catalogue explicitly marks the handwriting, and consequently Lovelace’s authorship, as uncertain. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/correspondence-with-de-morgan/))

- **September 1841:** *Chain-rule exercise* [untitled]. A surviving mathematical working sheet, box 170, folio 174; it is an exercise rather than a formally composed treatise. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/correspondence-with-de-morgan/))

- **Undated, circa 1841:** *Definition of the complete derivative and reading instructions* [untitled]. A brief mathematical note preserved as box 170, folio 175; its manuscript form resists treatment as an independently published work. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/correspondence-with-de-morgan/))

- **Undated, circa 1841:** *Königsberg bridges sheet* [descriptive title]. A manuscript investigation preserved as box 170, folios 176–177 and identified by the editors as a particularly significant piece of mathematical work; it is a working paper, not a finished or author-titled obra. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/))

### Translation and computational obra

- **1842–1843:** *Sketch of the Analytical Engine Invented by Charles Babbage*. Lovelace’s English translation of Luigi Federico Menabrea’s French memoir, published under her initials in volume 3 of *Scientific Memoirs*. This is a translation—an authored transformation of Menabrea’s text—not an original treatise wholly made by Lovelace. ([Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp38764))

- **1843:** *Notes by the Translator, A–G*. Seven substantial explanatory essays appended to the Menabrea translation, collectively longer than the translated memoir and describing the Analytical Engine’s operations, symbolic reach, and possible applications. They constitute Lovelace’s principal published obra, but surviving correspondence shows close consultation with Charles Babbage, and scholars dispute how particular mathematical passages and examples should be apportioned between them. ([Füegi and Francis](https://www.ada-europe.org/archive/auj/auj-36-2.pdf); [Misa](https://tjmisa.com/papers/2015_Misa-Lovelace-Babbage-ch2.pdf))

- **1843:** *Diagram for the computation of Bernoulli numbers by the Analytical Engine*. A tabular protocol embedded in Note G: it specifies a sequence of machine operations and is often called the first published computer program. Its authorship is disputed rather than securely singular—Babbage and Lovelace exchanged versions, Babbage had devised earlier programs, and recent scholarship assigns important contributions to both—while the Analytical Engine itself remained unbuilt. ([History of Science Museum](https://www.hsm.ox.ac.uk/imagining-ai); [Misa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02919))

### Unfinished scientific projects

- **1844–1851:** *A calculus of the nervous system* [project; no fixed title]. Lovelace envisioned a mathematical account of nervous and mental processes and pursued related studies in electricity and physiology, but no completed calculus or publishable treatise survives. It is properly an ambitious research project, not a lost book whose contents can safely be reconstructed. ([Hollings, Martin, and Rice](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/distributed/A/bo28594392.html))

- **1848:** *Unspecified paper intended for publication*. In a surviving letter Lovelace promised Augustus De Morgan an unnamed paper that was “about to be published”; the indexed corpus supplies neither a title nor a securely identifiable surviving publication. Its resistance to being called a work is material evidence: it may have been a draft, an abandoned project, or a publication not yet identified. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/correspondence-with-de-morgan/))

### Epistolary body of work

- **Circa 1820s–1852:** *Correspondence* [no collective title]. Lovelace made a large, dispersed body of letters to family members, tutors, scientists, and writers, including Charles Babbage, Augustus De Morgan, Mary Somerville, Michael Faraday, and Edward Bulwer-Lytton. The letters are individual occasional texts rather than a work designed as a whole; modern selections and transcriptions are editorial creations, while the surviving manuscript corpus remains distributed among archives. ([Clay Mathematics Institute](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/); [Hertfordshire Archives material described by CMI](https://www.claymath.org/online-resources/ada-lovelaces-mathematical-papers/ada-and-the-lytton-family/))
