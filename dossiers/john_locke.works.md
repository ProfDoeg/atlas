## Works by John Locke

### Poems and occasional literary works

- **1654:** *On the Peace between England and Holland*. A Latin occasional poem celebrating the Treaty of Westminster; Locke’s first identified publication, printed anonymously in an Oxford anthology. ([Clarendon Edition overview](https://sites.psu.edu/johnlockeresources/archives/19); [Goldie, *Political Essays*, “Minor Essays”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/locke-political-essays/minor-essays/B6C443919BAB7C8F79F25C53D3E407F4))
- **1660:** *To the Memory of Oliver Cromwell*. An unsigned Latin elegy conventionally attributed to Locke and published in Oxford’s memorial volume; the attribution is probable rather than absolutely demonstrable. ([Clarendon Edition overview](https://sites.psu.edu/johnlockeresources/archives/19); [Clarendon Edition report](https://ojs.lib.uwo.ca/index.php/locke/article/download/7597/6217/13719))
- **1662:** *To the King*. A Latin congratulatory poem for Charles II’s marriage, printed anonymously in an Oxford collection and assigned to Locke by the scholarly editors. ([Clarendon Edition report](https://ojs.lib.uwo.ca/index.php/locke/article/download/7597/6217/13719))
- **c. 1677:** *Atlantis*. An unfinished satirical or utopian narrative preserved among Locke’s journal papers; it resists classification as a completed work and survives as a literary experiment. ([Attig, “Publications from Locke’s Papers”](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))

### Political and religious projects in manuscript

- **1660:** *First Tract on Government* (the English Tract). A disputation defending the civil magistrate’s power to regulate religiously “indifferent” practices; it remained unpublished in Locke’s lifetime. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1661–1662:** *Second Tract on Government* (the Latin Tract). A second, Latin treatment of the magistrate’s authority over worship, likewise unpublished and markedly less tolerant than Locke’s mature position. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1661–1662:** *An necesse sit dari in Ecclesia infallibilem Sacro Sanctae Scripturae interpretem?* A Latin disputation denying that the church requires an infallible interpreter of Scripture. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1663–1664:** *Questions Concerning the Law of Nature* (also *Essays* or *Disputations on the Law of Nature*). Eight Latin moral-philosophical disputations, probably composed for Locke’s Oxford teaching; the collective English title is editorial rather than Locke’s fixed title. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1667:** *An Essay Concerning Toleration*. A manuscript policy essay advocating a qualified toleration; it is a project toward, but not simply an early edition of, the later *Letter*. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1669:** *The Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina*. A colonial constitutional instrument produced for the Lords Proprietors. Locke participated as secretary and helped revise its successive versions, but scholarship disputes whether he can be called its author rather than its draftsman or administrative collaborator. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html); [Arneil, *John Locke and America*](https://academic.oup.com/book/5585))
- **c. 1673–1674:** *Obligation of Penal Laws*. A short political manuscript asking when subjects are morally obliged to obey penal statutes; its title is supplied from the manuscript’s subject rather than from a publication. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1679:** *Toleratio*. A very brief Latin memorandum on toleration, not a finished treatise. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1680–1681:** *Critical Notes on Stillingfleet* (also called *Defence of Nonconformity* or *The Nature of Churches*). Notes responding to Edward Stillingfleet’s attacks on Protestant separation; the competing modern titles signal that this is an unfinished manuscript complex, not a book Locke named and issued. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1681–1683:** *Selecting the Grand Jury*. A political tract on jury selection, surviving with related queries; it was not published by Locke and its present title is editorial. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1693:** *For a General Naturalization*. A memorandum or policy project advocating broader naturalization in England. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1695:** *Venditio*. A short ethical-economic manuscript on just pricing and market exchange; its journal heading functions as its conventional title. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1697:** *An Essay on the Poor Law* (also *Report on the Poor*). A reform proposal produced for the Board of Trade, combining administrative report, disciplinary scheme, and social-policy protocol; it was not enacted. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1696–1700:** *Board of Trade and Plantation Papers*. Roughly two hundred reports, representations, minutes, and other state documents co-signed by or closely associated with Locke as commissioner. They resist treatment as individual authored works because they were institutional and collaborative productions. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))

### Drafts and manifestations of the project on human understanding

- **1671:** *Draft A of the Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. The earliest surviving autograph form of Locke’s epistemological project, conventionally named by modern editors rather than issued by Locke as a work. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1671:** *Draft B of the Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. A second and substantially fuller formulation made in the same year; it is an autonomous manuscript stage, not an edition of the eventual book. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1685:** *Draft C of the Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. The third surviving major draft, materially closer to the printed *Essay* but still a distinct manuscript manifestation. ([John Locke Society](https://thejohnlockesociety.com/2021/06/03/new-volume-in-the-clarendon-edition-of-the-works-of-john-locke/); [Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1688:** *Abrégé d’un ouvrage intitulé Essai philosophique touchant l’entendement*. Locke’s French abridgment and advance manifestation of the still-unpublished *Essay*, prepared for Jean Le Clerc’s periodical rather than merely extracted by a later editor. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib))
- **1689–1690:** *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*. A four-book philosophical obra on the origin, extent, and limits of human knowledge, published in December 1689 with 1690 on its title page and repeatedly revised by Locke through the fourth edition of 1700. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [Attig bibliography](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/works-of-john-locke-9780313243592/))
- **1697:** *An Examination of P. Malebranche’s Opinion of Seeing All Things in God*. A critical philosophical essay left unpublished; its argumentative target and manuscript status make it a discrete work rather than an addition to the *Essay*. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1698:** *Remarks upon Some of Mr Norris’s Books*. Manuscript criticisms of John Norris’s philosophy, assembled as remarks rather than completed for publication. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1697–1704:** *Of the Conduct of the Understanding*. A protocol for directing inquiry and correcting intellectual habits, conceived as an addition to the *Essay* but left unpublished and incomplete at Locke’s death. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))

### Public political and toleration works

- **c. 1679–1683; published 1689–1690:** *Two Treatises of Government*. A composite political obra: the first treatise dismantles Robert Filmer’s patriarchal argument, while the second constructs Locke’s account of political authority, property, consent, resistance, and dissolution. Its exact composition chronology remains disputed, and the title page bears 1690 although publication began late in 1689. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [John Locke chronology](https://www.johnlocke.net/p/chronology.html))
- **1685–1686; published 1689:** *Epistola de Tolerantia*. A Latin public intervention written in the Netherlands and published anonymously at Gouda; it is both philosophical argument and manifesto for separating civil jurisdiction from the care of souls. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib))
- **1689:** *A Letter Concerning Toleration*. William Popple’s English translation of the *Epistola*, issued with Locke’s approval but not made by Locke himself; it is therefore a consequential manifestation of his work rather than a second independently authored work. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib))
- **1690:** *A Second Letter Concerning Toleration*. An anonymous polemical reply to Jonas Proast’s attack on the first *Letter*. ([Encyclopedia.com bibliography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/philosophy-and-religion/philosophy-biographies/john-locke))
- **1692:** *A Third Letter for Toleration*. A book-length continuation of Locke’s controversy with Proast and therefore a discrete polemical act, not merely an edition of the original *Letter*. ([Encyclopedia.com bibliography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/philosophy-and-religion/philosophy-biographies/john-locke))
- **1704, unfinished; published 1706:** *A Fourth Letter for Toleration*. Locke’s incomplete final response to Proast; it resists being called a finished work and was printed posthumously from the surviving manuscript. ([Encyclopedia.com bibliography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/philosophy-and-religion/philosophy-biographies/john-locke))

### Money, trade, and economic policy

- **1668–1674:** Manuscript papers on interest, money, property, labour, and trade. These are separate memoranda and exploratory projects—including the paper conventionally called *Labour*—rather than a single finished economic treatise. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html); [PhilPapers description of the Clarendon economic writings](https://philpapers.org/browse/locke-works))
- **1691:** *Some Considerations of the Consequences of the Lowering of Interest, and Raising the Value of Money*. A public economic-policy pamphlet developed from earlier memoranda and directed against statutory reduction of interest. ([John Locke major works](https://thegreatthinkers.org/locke/bibliography/major-works/); [John Locke chronology](https://www.johnlocke.net/p/chronology.html))
- **1695:** *Short Observations on a Printed Paper, Intituled, For Encouraging the Coining Silver Money in England*. A brief intervention in the recoinage controversy. ([PhilPapers description of the Clarendon economic writings](https://philpapers.org/browse/locke-works))
- **1695:** *Further Considerations Concerning Raising the Value of Money*. A second, more extensive recoinage pamphlet answering proposals to alter the monetary standard. ([PhilPapers description of the Clarendon economic writings](https://philpapers.org/browse/locke-works))

### Education and practical protocols

- **1684–1685:** *Directions Concerning Education*. The manuscript first draft of Locke’s educational project, written as advice for the Clarke family; its later editorial title marks it as a draft rather than the published book. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1693:** *Some Thoughts Concerning Education*. A practical sequence of recommendations on forming a gentleman’s body, habits, judgment, and studies; it grew out of private letters but became a deliberately ordered public work. ([Routledge list of works](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [John Locke chronology](https://www.johnlocke.net/p/chronology.html))
- **c. 1677:** *Essay on Recreation*. A short manuscript considering recreation as part of the management of time and education. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **undated:** *Of Study*. A concise instructional text on reading and intellectual work; its date is not securely fixed, so none should be supplied merely to regularize the chronology. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1686; published 1706:** *A New Method of Making Common-Place-Books*. A protocol for indexing commonplace books by means of a letter-key system, first communicated in French and later published in English. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))

### Theology and biblical scholarship

- **1695:** *The Reasonableness of Christianity, as Delivered in the Scriptures*. An anonymous theological argument identifying the minimal scriptural profession required of Christians. ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/46453); [John Locke chronology](https://www.johnlocke.net/p/chronology.html))
- **1695:** *A Vindication of the Reasonableness of Christianity*. A polemical defence against John Edwards’s charge that the earlier work promoted Socinianism. ([John Locke major works](https://thegreatthinkers.org/locke/bibliography/major-works/))
- **1697:** *A Second Vindication of the Reasonableness of Christianity*. A further and substantially longer answer to Edwards. ([Attig bibliography](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/works-of-john-locke-9780313243592/))
- **1701:** *A Discourse of Miracles*. A theological-philosophical essay on miracles as credentials of divine revelation; it remained unpublished during Locke’s lifetime. ([Boston Collaborative Encyclopedia of Western Theology](https://people.bu.edu/wwildman/bce/mwt_themes_440_locke.htm); [Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1702:** *Christianae Religionis Brevis Delineatio*. A concise Latin outline of Christian religion preserved in manuscript; it is closer to a doctrinal schema than a completed public treatise. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1703:** *An Essay for the Understanding of St Paul’s Epistles*. A hermeneutic protocol explaining how the Pauline letters should be read; it serves as the methodological introduction to Locke’s paraphrases. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1703–1704; published 1705–1707:** *A Paraphrase and Notes on the Epistle of St Paul to the Galatians*. A scriptural paraphrase with critical notes, prepared near the end of Locke’s life and published posthumously. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1703–1704; published 1706:** *A Paraphrase and Notes on the First Epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians*. One component of Locke’s unfinished Pauline project, but a separately organized commentary. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1703–1704; published 1706:** *A Paraphrase and Notes on the Second Epistle of St Paul to the Corinthians*. A second separately published component of the Pauline project. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1703–1704; published 1707:** *A Paraphrase and Notes on the Epistle of St Paul to the Romans*. A sustained biblical commentary published after Locke’s death. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1703–1704; published 1707:** *A Paraphrase and Notes on the Epistle of St Paul to the Ephesians*. The last separately issued part of the Pauline project; the larger plan remained incomplete at Locke’s death. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))

### Controversial letters on philosophy and theology

- **1697:** *A Letter to the Right Reverend Edward, Lord Bishop of Worcester*. Locke’s first public answer to Edward Stillingfleet’s criticism of the *Essay* and its supposed theological consequences. ([John Locke chronology](https://www.johnlocke.net/p/chronology.html); [Attig bibliography](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/works-of-john-locke-9780313243592/))
- **1697:** *Mr. Locke’s Reply to the Right Reverend the Lord Bishop of Worcester’s Answer to His Letter*. The second, separately published stage of the Stillingfleet controversy. ([Attig bibliography](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/works-of-john-locke-9780313243592/))
- **1699:** *Mr. Locke’s Reply to the Right Reverend the Lord Bishop of Worcester’s Answer to His Second Letter*. The third and final published instalment of the exchange. ([Attig bibliography](https://www.bloomsbury.com/us/works-of-john-locke-9780313243592/))

### Natural philosophy, medicine, and material records

- **c. 1666:** *Morbus*. A short Latin essay on disease; the conventional title comes from the manuscript, and its status is that of an early medical exercise. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1666–1667:** *Respirationis Usus*. A Latin paper on the function of respiration, preserved among Locke’s medical manuscripts. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1668:** *Anatomia*. A methodological medical essay associated with Thomas Sydenham. Locke wrote the surviving manuscript, but the extent to which it is Locke’s, Sydenham’s, or a collaborative production remains disputed. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1669:** *De Arte Medica*. An essay rejecting speculative medical systems in favour of observation; as with *Anatomia*, its relationship to Sydenham makes simple individual authorship uncertain. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1668:** *The Case of Anthony Ashley Cooper*. Clinical observations connected with the drainage of Shaftesbury’s liver abscess; this is a medical case record and intervention, not a book. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1660s–1704:** Medical case notes and recipes. Locke made numerous patient records, treatment notes, and pharmaceutical recipes, often in collaborative medical settings; they resist division into stable titled works. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1660s–1680s:** *Herbarium*. A material collection of pressed and labelled plant specimens assembled by Locke. It is an obra in botanical practice rather than a text, and portions survive as a dispersed or reconstructed collection. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1660s–1700s:** Weather charts and meteorological records. Systematic tabular observations made for natural history; they are data-bearing graphic objects rather than continuous prose works. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1692–1693:** Contributions to Robert Boyle’s *General History of the Air*. Locke edited, arranged, corrected, and supplied material for Boyle’s posthumous book; this is an editorial and collaborative making, not an independently authored Locke title. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1697–1704; published 1720:** *Elements of Natural Philosophy*. An introductory survey of matter, motion, astronomy, geography, and related subjects, apparently written for educational use and issued only after Locke’s death. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html); [Oxford Academic, Locke and natural philosophy](https://academic.oup.com/book/26023))

### Journals, notebooks, catalogues, and correspondence

- **1675–1704:** *Journals*. A continuing dated record of travel, reading, medicine, natural observations, politics, theology, and philosophical problems. It is a working archive rather than one finished literary work, and only portions have appeared in complete scholarly form. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1675–1679:** French travel journals. A geographically concentrated manifestation within the larger journal corpus, recording Locke’s travel, encounters, observations, and reading in France. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1650s–1704:** Commonplace books and adversaria. Numerous indexed notebooks in which Locke collected extracts, observations, arguments, and project fragments; they are tools of composition and thought and resist classification as completed works. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1650s–1704:** Reading lists and lists of prohibited or difficult-to-obtain books. Working bibliographical instruments made for personal, educational, or practical use, not public books. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **1674–1704:** *Library Catalogue*. Locke’s evolving catalogue of his own books, organized through his characteristic indexing practices; it is both a bibliographical protocol and a record of a changing material collection. ([Attig](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/bib/ch0n.html))
- **c. 1650–1704:** Correspondence. Thousands of letters to and from Locke survive, with Locke’s own letters ranging from private notes to medical advice, philosophical argument, political intelligence, and editorial direction. The standard Clarendon edition treats this as an eight-volume documentary corpus; individual letters are made things, but the “correspondence” itself is an archival creation rather than a single work authored by Locke. ([Routledge bibliography](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/locke-john-1632-1704/v-1/bibliography/locke-john-1632-1704-bib); [Bodleian manuscript example](https://openpublishing.psu.edu/locke/mss/mslocke-c8.html))
