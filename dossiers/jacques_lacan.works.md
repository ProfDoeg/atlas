## Works by Jacques Marie Émile Lacan

### Medical and psychiatric works

- **1926:** *Abasie chez une traumatisée de guerre*. Clinical report, co-authored with Théophile Alajouanine and Pierre Delafontaine; its shared authorship prevents treating it as Lacan’s work alone.
- **1928:** *Hallucinations intermittentes dans un cas de schizophrénie*. Psychiatric case report, co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1928:** *Délire hallucinatoire à thème de persécution avec idées hypocondriaques*. Clinical presentation, co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1928:** *Délire à deux dans un couple mère-fille*. Case report, co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1928:** *Les troubles du langage écrit chez une paranoïaque présentant des éléments délirants du type paranoïde*. Clinical-linguistic study, co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1928:** *Délire d’interprétation à type de délire de grandeur*. Psychiatric case presentation, co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1929:** *Syndrome d’automatisme mental et délire systématisé avec hallucinations*. Clinical report, co-authored with Henri Claude and Pierre Migault.
- **1930:** *Paralysie générale avec syndrome d’automatisme mental*. Clinical report, co-authored with Henri Claude and Pierre Migault.
- **1930:** *Structure des psychoses paranoïaques*. Psychiatric paper associated with Lacan’s early attempt to rethink paranoia; surviving bibliographies do not always treat its publication history identically.
- **1931:** *Psychoses à base d’automatisme et syndrome d’automatisme*. Psychiatric paper, co-authored with Henri Ey.
- **1931:** *Écrits “inspirés”: schizographie*. Study of psychotic writing, co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1931:** *Folies simultanées*. Clinical study, co-authored with Henri Ey and Pierre Migault.
- **1931:** *Troubles du langage écrit chez une paranoïaque*. Study of the writings of Lacan’s patient “Marcelle,” co-authored with J. Lévy-Valensi and Pierre Migault.
- **1932:** *Spasme de torsion et troubles mentaux post-encéphalitiques*. Medical case report, co-authored with Henri Claude and Pierre Migault.
- **1932:** *De la psychose paranoïaque dans ses rapports avec la personnalité*. Doctoral thesis and the only book-length work Lacan himself published as a conventional monograph; the later *Premiers écrits sur la paranoïa* attached to editions of it is editorial packaging, not a second 1932 book.
- **1933:** *Un cas de démence précocissime*. Clinical report, co-authored with Henri Claude and Georges Heuyer.
- **1933:** *Le problème du style et la conception psychiatrique des formes paranoïaques de l’expérience*. Psychiatric-aesthetic essay linking paranoiac experience, style, and artistic production.
- **1933:** *Un cas de perversion infantile par encéphalite épidémique précoce diagnostiqué sur un syndrome moteur fruste*. Clinical report, co-authored with Georges Heuyer.
- **1933:** *Sur le problème des hallucinations*. Psychiatric conference contribution rather than an autonomous book.
- **1933:** *Alcoolisme subaigu à pouls normal ou ralenti: coexistence du syndrome d’automatisme mental*. Clinical paper, co-authored with Georges Heuyer.
- **1933:** *Motifs du crime paranoïaque: le crime des sœurs Papin*. Medico-psychoanalytic interpretation of a criminal case.
- **1938:** *Les complexes familiaux dans la formation de l’individu: essai d’analyse d’une fonction en psychologie*. Commissioned encyclopedia article, subsequently treated as a substantial early work.
- **1946:** *Propos sur la causalité psychique*. Conference paper contesting organicist accounts of madness.
- **1947:** *La psychiatrie anglaise et la guerre*. Report on British wartime psychiatry and group organization.

### Psychoanalytic essays and conference texts

- **1936:** *Au-delà du “principe de réalité”*. Essay marking Lacan’s movement from psychiatry toward psychoanalysis.
- **1936:** *Le stade du miroir*. The Marienbad congress presentation was interrupted and its original text is not securely preserved; it resists treatment as a stable written work.
- **1945:** *Le temps logique et l’assertion de certitude anticipée: un nouveau sophisme*. Philosophical-psychoanalytic essay organized around a logical problem.
- **1948:** *L’agressivité en psychanalyse*. Conference report formulated as a series of theses.
- **1949:** *Le stade du miroir comme formateur de la fonction du Je telle qu’elle nous est révélée dans l’expérience psychanalytique*. Revised congress paper, not simply the recoverable text of the lost or interrupted 1936 presentation.
- **1950:** *Introduction théorique aux fonctions de la psychanalyse en criminologie*. Conference report co-authored with Michel Cénac.
- **1950–1951:** *Prémisses à tout développement possible de la criminologie*. Intervention later printed as Lacan’s response to discussion; its title is editorially connected to the occasion.
- **1951:** *Intervention sur le transfert*. Congress intervention centered on Freud’s Dora case.
- **1953:** *Le mythe individuel du névrosé, ou Poésie et vérité dans la névrose*. Lecture whose text circulated through notes and later editorial reconstruction; the title itself exists in variants.
- **1953:** *Le symbolique, l’imaginaire et le réel*. Lecture inaugurating Lacan’s three-register formulation.
- **1953:** *Fonction et champ de la parole et du langage en psychanalyse*. Written report and oral “Rome Discourse”; it is both essay and intervention in the politics of psychoanalytic training.
- **1953:** *Discours de Rome*. Oral delivery associated with *Fonction et champ* but not textually identical to it.
- **1953:** *Variantes de la cure-type*. Encyclopedic essay subsequently revised for *Écrits*.
- **1954:** *Introduction au commentaire de Jean Hyppolite sur la Verneinung de Freud*. Essay framing Hyppolite’s seminar intervention.
- **1955:** *Le Séminaire sur “La Lettre volée”*. Written reconstruction of work first developed orally; later placed at the opening of *Écrits* despite its date.
- **1955:** *La chose freudienne, ou Sens du retour à Freud en psychanalyse*. Vienna lecture recast as a polemical essay.
- **1956:** *Situation de la psychanalyse et formation du psychanalyste en 1956*. Satirical and institutional critique.
- **1957:** *La psychanalyse et son enseignement*. Conference paper defining what psychoanalysis teaches through the structure of language.
- **1957:** *L’instance de la lettre dans l’inconscient ou la raison depuis Freud*. Essay developed from a university lecture.
- **1957–1958:** *D’une question préliminaire à tout traitement possible de la psychose*. Essay derived from the psychosis seminar but made into a distinct written construction.
- **1958:** *Jeunesse de Gide ou la lettre et le désir*. Critical-psychoanalytic essay on André Gide.
- **1958:** *La signification du phallus*. Munich lecture, first delivered in German under the title *Die Bedeutung des Phallus* and subsequently established in French.
- **1958:** *La direction de la cure et les principes de son pouvoir*. Congress report and critique of prevailing analytic technique.
- **1958:** *Remarque sur le rapport de Daniel Lagache: “Psychanalyse et structure de la personnalité”*. Extended response to Lagache, written for a colloquium and later published.
- **1958:** *La psychanalyse vraie, et la fausse*. Unpublished-in-Lacan’s-lifetime polemical text, printed posthumously.
- **1959:** *À la mémoire d’Ernest Jones: sur sa théorie du symbolisme*. Memorial essay that is also a critique of Jones’s theory.
- **1959–1960:** *Hamlet*. A sequence within Seminar VI, later excerpted under several editorial titles; those excerpt titles do not constitute independently composed Lacanian works.
- **1960:** *L’éthique de la psychanalyse*. Two Brussels lectures related to, but not identical with, Seminar VII.
- **1960:** *La métaphore du sujet*. Written form of an intervention in a philosophical discussion.
- **1960:** *Propos directifs pour un congrès sur la sexualité féminine*. Congress paper.
- **1960:** *Subversion du sujet et dialectique du désir dans l’inconscient freudien*. Royaumont congress paper.
- **1960:** *Position de l’inconscient*. Bonneval congress intervention, revised before publication.
- **1961:** *Maurice Merleau-Ponty*. Memorial essay.
- **1962:** *De ce que j’enseigne*. Lecture on Lacan’s teaching, later editorially incorporated into collections.
- **1962:** *Kant avec Sade*. Essay initially intended as a preface or afterword for an edition of Sade but separately published after rejection from that position.
- **1964:** *Du “Trieb” de Freud et du désir du psychanalyste*. Rome colloquium contribution.
- **1965:** *Hommage fait à Marguerite Duras, du ravissement de Lol V. Stein*. Critical homage that explicitly resists reducing literature to a psychoanalytic case.
- **1965–1966:** *La science et la vérité*. Opening lesson of Seminar XIII revised as a written essay.
- **1966:** *Réponse à des étudiants en philosophie sur l’objet de la psychanalyse*. Written response to questions from the *Cahiers pour l’analyse*.
- **1966:** *Présentation de la traduction française des Mémoires d’un névropathe*. Presentation of Paul Duquenne’s translation of Daniel Paul Schreber.
- **1966:** *De la structure en tant qu’immixtion d’une altérité préalable à un sujet quelconque*. Johns Hopkins conference intervention; first publication was in English, so the precise relation between spoken French, translation, and later French text is editorially mediated.
- **1966:** *La place de la psychanalyse dans la médecine*. Intervention at the Collège de médecine.
- **1967:** *La méprise du sujet supposé savoir*. Naples lecture.
- **1967:** *De Rome 53 à Rome 67: la psychanalyse, raison d’un échec*. Rome discourse assessing the reception of Lacan’s earlier program.
- **1967:** *De la psychanalyse dans ses rapports avec la réalité*. Milan lecture.
- **1967:** *Discours de clôture des journées sur les psychoses chez l’enfant*. Closing conference address.
- **1968:** *Allocution sur les psychoses de l’enfant*. Published form associated with the 1967 closing address; editorial titles vary.
- **1968:** *Introduction de Scilicet au titre de la revue de l’École freudienne de Paris*. Programmatic introduction to an unusually anonymous journal.
- **1968:** *Note de lecture sur le numéro de L’Arc consacré à Freud*. Brief review.
- **1968–1969:** *D’un Autre à l’autre: résumé du séminaire*. Lacan’s own retrospective abstract, distinct from the seminar transcript.
- **1969:** *Préface à une thèse*. Preface to Anika Lemaire’s study of Lacan.
- **1969:** *L’acte psychanalytique: résumé du séminaire*. Lacan’s retrospective abstract of Seminar XV.
- **1969:** *La logique du fantasme: résumé du séminaire*. Lacan’s retrospective abstract of Seminar XIV.
- **1970:** *Radiophonie*. Answers written for questions broadcast by Belgian radio; a literary-theoretical text made for radio rather than a transcript of spontaneous speech.
- **1971:** *Lituraterre*. Written essay derived from a seminar lesson but substantially recast for publication.
- **1971:** *Un homme et une femme*. Lecture later published in a form dependent on transcription.
- **1972:** *Du discours psychanalytique*. Milan lecture.
- **1972:** *L’étourdit*. Dense written essay whose typography, homophony, formulas, and diagrams make it more than a record of speech.
- **1973:** *Postface au Séminaire XI*. Afterword to the established French edition of Seminar XI.
- **1973:** *Introduction à l’édition allemande d’un premier volume des Écrits*. Preface theorizing symptoms and analytic reading.
- **1973:** *Note italienne*. Institutional-theoretical note addressed to an Italian group; also known through the editorial title *Note aux Italiens*.
- **1974:** *Préface à L’Éveil du printemps*. Preface to Frank Wedekind’s play.
- **1974:** *La troisième*. Rome lecture using knots and diagrams; surviving printed forms depend on recording and transcription.
- **1974:** *Le triomphe de la religion*. Press conference or interview later made into a posthumous book; it was not composed as that book.
- **1975:** *Joyce le Symptôme I*. Opening address to the Joyce symposium.
- **1975:** *Joyce le Symptôme II*. Written version related to, but not identical with, the symposium address.
- **1975:** *Conférence à Genève sur le symptôme*. Lecture and discussion preserved through transcription.
- **1976:** *Préface à l’édition anglaise du Séminaire XI*. Preface notable as a late statement on the end and effects of analysis.
- **1976:** *Conférences et entretiens dans des universités nord-américaines*. A group of talks and discussions at Yale, Columbia, and MIT; the collective title is editorial, while the constituent events remain oral works.
- **1977:** *Ouverture de la Section clinique*. Institutional and teaching address.
- **1977:** *Propos sur l’hystérie*. Brussels lecture surviving as a transcript.
- **1977:** *Les mathèmes de la psychanalyse*. Congress intervention.
- **1978:** *Le rêve d’Aristote*. Short text first published in a multilingual volume.
- **1980:** *Monsieur A.*. Polemical public letter directed against Jacques-Alain Miller.
- **1980:** *Le malentendu*. Caracas address and late statement about Lacanian teaching.

### The annual seminars: oral teaching works

- **1951–1952:** *L’homme aux loups*. Private or semi-private seminar on Freud’s case; the surviving object is reconstructed from participants’ notes, not an authorially fixed text.
- **1952–1953:** *L’homme aux rats*. Seminar on Freud’s case; again a dispersed oral work rather than a stable book.
- **1953–1954:** *Les écrits techniques de Freud* (Seminar I). Annual oral seminar preserved by stenotypes and subsequently transformed into an edited book.
- **1954–1955:** *Le moi dans la théorie de Freud et dans la technique de la psychanalyse* (Seminar II). Oral teaching cycle; published titles and divisions were established editorially.
- **1955–1956:** *Les psychoses* (Seminar III). Annual seminar.
- **1956–1957:** *La relation d’objet* (Seminar IV). Annual seminar.
- **1957–1958:** *Les formations de l’inconscient* (Seminar V). Annual seminar; a contemporary summary approved by Lacan and the later edited transcript are different textual objects.
- **1958–1959:** *Le désir et son interprétation* (Seminar VI). Annual seminar.
- **1959–1960:** *L’éthique de la psychanalyse* (Seminar VII). Annual seminar.
- **1960–1961:** *Le transfert dans sa disparité subjective, sa prétendue situation, ses excursions techniques* (Seminar VIII). Annual seminar, commonly shortened editorially to *Le transfert*.
- **1961–1962:** *L’identification* (Seminar IX). Annual seminar.
- **1962–1963:** *L’angoisse* (Seminar X). Annual seminar.
- **1963:** *Les Noms-du-Père*. Seminar project terminated by Lacan after its single session of 20 November; its incompletion is constitutive of the work.
- **1964:** *Les fondements de la psychanalyse* / *Les quatre concepts fondamentaux de la psychanalyse* (Seminar XI). The first title belongs to the teaching event; the second became the title of the edited book.
- **1964–1965:** *Problèmes cruciaux pour la psychanalyse* (Seminar XII). Annual seminar.
- **1965–1966:** *L’objet de la psychanalyse* (Seminar XIII). Annual seminar.
- **1966–1967:** *La logique du fantasme* (Seminar XIV). Annual seminar.
- **1967–1968:** *L’acte psychanalytique* (Seminar XV). Annual seminar.
- **1968–1969:** *D’un Autre à l’autre* (Seminar XVI). Annual seminar.
- **1969–1970:** *L’envers de la psychanalyse* (Seminar XVII). Annual seminar.
- **1971:** *D’un discours qui ne serait pas du semblant* (Seminar XVIII). Annual seminar.
- **1971–1972:** *…ou pire* (Seminar XIX). Annual seminar; its ellipsis is part of the title.
- **1971–1972:** *Le savoir du psychanalyste*. Parallel Sainte-Anne talks, sometimes annexed to Seminar XIX but not identical with it.
- **1972–1973:** *Encore* (Seminar XX). Annual seminar.
- **1973–1974:** *Les non-dupes errent*. Annual seminar; the alternative designation *Les Noms du Père* records Lacan’s pun but can misleadingly imply a resumption of the abandoned 1963 seminar.
- **1974–1975:** *R.S.I.* (Seminar XXII). Annual seminar organized around the real, symbolic, and imaginary and their knotting.
- **1975–1976:** *Le sinthome* (Seminar XXIII). Annual seminar whose speech, writing, and knot diagrams resist separation into independent media.
- **1976–1977:** *L’insu que sait de l’une-bévue s’aile à mourre*. Annual seminar with a deliberately homophonic title; normalized spellings destroy part of the made object.
- **1977–1978:** *Le moment de conclure*. Annual seminar.
- **1978–1979:** *La topologie et le temps*. Annual seminar dominated by manipulations and drawings as well as speech.
- **1980:** *Dissolution*. A sequence of letters, declarations, and seminar-like sessions concerning the dissolution of the École freudienne; calling it Seminar XXVII is an editorial convention, not an uncontested authorial designation.

### Manifestos, protocols, and institutional acts

- **1953:** *Statuts de la Société française de psychanalyse*. Institutional document made collectively; Lacan participated in the project, but sole authorship should not be assigned to him.
- **1964:** *Acte de fondation de l’École freudienne de Paris*. Manifesto and performative institutional act: the writing creates the school it announces.
- **1964:** *Note adjointe à l’Acte de fondation*. Organizational supplement to the founding act.
- **1964:** *Préambule*. Programmatic supplement defining the school’s relation to psychoanalytic work.
- **1967:** *Proposition du 9 octobre 1967 sur le psychanalyste de l’École*—first version. Protocol proposing the institutional procedure known as the pass; rejected or contested within the school and published only later.
- **1967:** *Proposition du 9 octobre 1967 sur le psychanalyste de l’École*—second version. Revised protocol; it should not silently replace the materially different first version.
- **1967:** *Discours à l’École freudienne de Paris*. Institutional address defending the proposition on the pass.
- **1969:** *Adresse au jury d’accueil*. Institutional instruction concerning admission and training.
- **1971:** *Acte de fondation de l’École freudienne de Paris: note de 1971*. Later institutional clarification rather than a new founding act.
- **1973:** *Note sur le choix des passeurs*. Protocol governing the selection of participants in the pass.
- **1975:** *Note sur la désignation des passeurs*. Further protocol concerning the pass.
- **1980:** *Lettre de dissolution*. Manifesto-act dissolving the École freudienne de Paris rather than merely reporting its dissolution.
- **1980:** *Aux mille*. Circular intervention addressed to those supporting the dissolution process.
- **1980:** *Déclaration de l’École de la Cause freudienne*. Foundational institutional text; its precise collective and editorial status complicates attribution to Lacan alone.
- **1980:** *Acte de fondation de l’École de la Cause freudienne*. Performative founding document associated with Lacan’s final institutional project.

### Broadcast, film, and recorded manifestations

- **1966:** *Petit discours à l’ORTF*. Radio address, later printed; the broadcast and printed transcription are related but not identical objects.
- **1966:** *Entretien avec Jacques Lacan*. Press interview, jointly made with its interviewer and subsequently stabilized in print.
- **1966:** *Un psychanalyste s’explique*. Newspaper interview whose title was supplied by the publication rather than necessarily by Lacan.
- **1966:** *Sartre contre Lacan: bataille absurde*. Press interview or statement framed by an editorial headline.
- **1970:** *Radiophonie*. Radio work composed as written answers, broadcast in four installments and later printed; it belongs simultaneously to broadcast and writing.
- **1972:** *Entretien à la télévision belge*. Television interview surviving in transcription.
- **1973–1974:** *Télévision*. Filmed interview with questions by Jacques-Alain Miller and direction by Benoît Jacquot, broadcast by the ORTF in March 1974 and also published as a book; it is a collaborative audiovisual manifestation, not simply a written monograph.
- **1974:** *Conférence de presse à Rome*. Recorded press encounter connected with *La troisième*.
- **1976:** *Jacques Lacan parle*. Film by Françoise Wolff recording Lacan’s Louvain appearance; the film is Wolff’s work, while Lacan’s filmed lecture and exchanges are his performance within it.

### Diagrams, mathemes, and topological constructions

- **1936–1949:** *The mirror-stage schema*. A conceptual and visual construction developed across presentations and essays, not a single securely dated drawing.
- **1953 onward:** *The real–symbolic–imaginary triad*. A continuing conceptual project manifested in speech, diagrams, and later knot constructions rather than one autonomous text.
- **1955:** *Schema L*. Diagrammatic object formalizing relations among subject, ego, other, and Other.
- **1957–1958:** *Graph of desire*. Diagrammatic construction developed through Seminar V and related writings; several states of the graph exist, so there is no single uncontested final object.
- **1957–1958:** *Schema R*. Diagrammatic construction used in Lacan’s account of psychosis.
- **1958:** *Schema I*. Topological diagram of the psychotic structure.
- **1961:** *Optical schema*. Lacan’s adaptation of an optical experiment into an analytic diagram; it exists in several successive forms.
- **1961–1962:** *The unary trait and torus constructions*. A diagrammatic and topological project developed in Seminar IX, inseparable from Lacan’s oral demonstrations.
- **1962–1963:** *The cross-cap and object a constructions*. Topological models used in Seminar X; physical manipulation, drawing, and speech jointly constitute the work.
- **1969–1970:** *The four discourses*. A set of rotating mathemes—the master, university, hysteric, and analyst discourses—first systematically constructed in Seminar XVII.
- **1971–1972:** *Sexuation formulas*. Mathemes developed over several seminars and writings rather than created in one settled textual instant.
- **1972–1973:** *Borromean knot constructions*. An extended topological project that became central to Lacan’s late teaching; individual drawings cannot be reduced to illustrations of a prior prose text.
- **1975–1976:** *The sinthome knot*. Topological construction developed through Seminar XXIII, sometimes requiring strings or physical demonstrations; it approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk of speech, writing, drawing, and performed manipulation.

### Collections and editorially made books

- **1966:** *Écrits*. Author-arranged collection with a newly composed opening, transitions, postscripts, and a deliberate nonchronological architecture; it is both a collection of earlier things and a new obra.
- **1973:** *Le Séminaire, livre XI: Les quatre concepts fondamentaux de la psychanalyse*. First book made from an annual seminar, its text established by Jacques-Alain Miller; authorship of the speech is Lacan’s, while the book-object is necessarily an editorial construction.
- **1973:** *Télévision*. Published text made in conjunction with the filmed interview and Jacques-Alain Miller’s questions.
- **1975–present:** *Le Séminaire de Jacques Lacan*. Posthumously continuing series of books established by Jacques-Alain Miller from oral teaching and documentary witnesses. It is not a collected-works edition authored in book form by Lacan, and disputes over transcription, punctuation, titles, omissions, and editorial authority are part of each volume’s status.
- **1975:** *De la psychose paranoïaque dans ses rapports avec la personnalité, suivi de Premiers écrits sur la paranoïa*. Editorial republication combining the 1932 thesis with early psychiatric papers.
- **2001:** *Autres écrits*. Posthumous collection edited by Jacques-Alain Miller; the constituent works are Lacan’s, but the selection, title, order, and book-object are not Lacan’s own completed design.
- **2001–2012:** *Pas-tout Lacan*. Digital archival compilation by the École de la cause freudienne of writings, interventions, letters, interviews, and other documents omitted from the principal books. It is invaluable but explicitly “not-all,” and therefore cannot certify completeness.
- **2005:** *Des Noms-du-Père*. Posthumous editorial conjunction of *Le symbolique, l’imaginaire et le réel* and the sole session of *Les Noms-du-Père*.
- **2005:** *Le triomphe de la religion, précédé de Discours aux catholiques*. Posthumous book assembled from distinct oral occasions.
- **2005:** *Mon enseignement*. Posthumous collection of three talks, not a book Lacan designed under that title.
- **2007:** *Le mythe individuel du névrosé, ou Poésie et vérité dans la névrose*. Posthumous book presentation of a lecture whose text depends on documentary reconstruction.
- **2011:** *Je parle aux murs: entretiens de la chapelle de Sainte-Anne*. Posthumous editorial book made from the *Le savoir du psychanalyste* talks.
- **2012:** *Je dis: la parole dans tous ses états*. Posthumous thematic collection; its unity is editorial rather than an authorial project completed by Lacan.
- **2013:** *La logique du fantasme* and other subsequently issued seminar volumes. These official editions continue to turn oral events into books after Lacan’s death; their publication dates must not be substituted for the dates of the teaching itself.

### Objects that resist a works list

- **Throughout his career:** *Clinical case presentations at Sainte-Anne*. Repeated live interviews with patients formed part of Lacan’s teaching, but many survive only through unauthorized or anonymized notes. They are performances involving vulnerable third parties and resist both stable titling and straightforward attribution as authored texts.
- **Throughout his career:** *Letters, telegrams, postcards, dedications, and pneumatic notes*. Hundreds survive or have been printed in archival compilations. Some are consequential textual acts, but private communications, editorially titled letters, and mere documentary traces cannot responsibly be inflated into uniformly autonomous “works.”
- **Throughout his career:** *Interventions in discussions*. Bibliographies record numerous questions, replies, and remarks after other speakers’ papers. Their wording often depends on stenography or journal editing, and their existence as speech does not automatically make each an authorially fixed text.
- **1953–1980:** *Stenotypes, audience notes, recordings, and transcripts of the seminars*. These are witnesses to Lacan’s oral work, made partly by stenographers, auditors, editors, and technicians. No single witness is the seminar itself.
- **1950s–1970s:** *Patient writings reproduced or discussed by Lacan*. Their appearance in his clinical and theoretical work does not make them his creations.
- **1960s–1970s:** *Knots, strings, drawings, blackboard inscriptions, and paper models used in teaching*. Many were ephemeral performed objects. Dating and authorship can be uncertain because drawings were copied by auditors or redrawn for publication.
- **Post-1981:** *New titles assigned to excerpts, interviews, lectures, or seminar sequences*. Such titles may name useful editorial objects, but they should not be retroactively counted as books or independently titled works made by Lacan.
