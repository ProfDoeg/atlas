## Works by Anita Garibaldi

### Dictated correspondence

- **7 March 1848:** *Letter to Stefano Antonini*. An untitled political and personal letter reporting Anita’s arrival in Genoa and the reception given to Garibaldi’s family. Although older accounts called the manuscript entirely autograph, Silvia Cavicchioli’s scholarly biography identifies Anita as illiterate and concludes that she probably dictated it to her host, the Uruguayan consul Giuseppe Matteo Antonini; it is therefore her text but not securely her handwriting.

### Public political address

- **March 1848:** *Address to the Genoese*. A short, untitled statement thanking the people of Genoa for their welcome and accepting a tricolour to be delivered to Giuseppe Garibaldi. It functions as a manifesto—an act of public identification with the Italian national cause—but the surviving reports do not establish whether Anita composed, dictated, merely approved, or spoke the wording attributed to her, so its textual authorship remains uncertain.

### Disputed autograph correspondence

- **June 1849:** *Letter to “the citizen Mumina”*. A three-page political letter from Rome, addressed through Matteo Antonini in Genoa, reporting on Giuseppe Garibaldi and expressing confidence in the Roman Republic. Christie’s catalogued it as an autograph signed letter and inconsistently dated it “[Rome, 1848],” although its references and the catalogue’s own narrative place it in June 1849; because Cavicchioli describes Anita as illiterate and the document has not appeared in a critical edition of her writings, the extent of her physical authorship remains unresolved.

### Limits of the corpus

- **1821–1849:** *No established collected works*. Anita Garibaldi left no recognized literary, artistic, architectural, or musical oeuvre and has no catalogue raisonné or collected-works edition. The standard modern scholarly biography treats her principally as a revolutionary actor and reconstructs her life from fragmentary evidence; consequently, battles, journeys, childcare, horsemanship, and political collaboration are historically important actions but resist honest classification as separately titled obras made by her.
