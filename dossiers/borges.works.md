## Works by Jorge Luis Borges

### Poetry books and lyric cycles

- **1923:** *Fervor de Buenos Aires*. First poetry book; Borges repeatedly revised it, so the 1923 object and the later canonical text are materially different states of the obra. [1][2]
- **1925:** *Luna de enfrente*. Poetry book, substantially altered in later collected editions. [1][2]
- **1929:** *Cuaderno San Martín*. Poetry book concerned with Buenos Aires, history, and local myth. [1][2]
- **1943:** *Poemas (1922–1943)*. Retrospective poetry collection that revises the three early books and adds poems; it is therefore a new authorial arrangement, not merely a reprint. [1]
- **1954:** *Poemas (1923–1953)*. Revised and expanded reconfiguration of Borges’s poetic corpus. [1]
- **1965:** *Para las seis cuerdas*. Cycle of milongas and tango lyrics intended for musical realization; the words are poems, potential songs, and protocols for performance at once. [1][3]
- **1969:** *El otro, el mismo*. Poetry collection incorporating *Para las seis cuerdas* and work written across several decades. [1][2]
- **1969:** *Elogio de la sombra*. Poetry and brief prose; its mixed forms resist a strict poetry/prose division. [1][2]
- **1972:** *El oro de los tigres*. Poetry collection with some prose miniatures. [1][2]
- **1975:** *La rosa profunda*. Poetry book dictated after Borges’s blindness. [1][2]
- **1976:** *La moneda de hierro*. Poetry collection. [1][2]
- **1977:** *Historia de la noche*. Poetry collection. [1][2]
- **1981:** *La cifra*. Poetry and short prose; another deliberately mixed late book. [1][2]
- **1985:** *Los conjurados*. Borges’s final lifetime book, predominantly poetry with prose pieces; published the year before his death. [1][2]

### Essays, criticism, and composite prose obras

- **1925:** *Inquisiciones*. First essay collection; Borges later repudiated it and excluded it from his lifetime *Obras completas*, making that attempted erasure part of the work’s history. [1][2]
- **1926:** *El tamaño de mi esperanza*. Essays and cultural polemics; likewise repudiated and suppressed by Borges in later collections. [1][2]
- **1928:** *El idioma de los argentinos*. Essays on language and Argentine identity; another early book Borges declined to reissue during most of his life. [1][2]
- **1930:** *Evaristo Carriego*. A biographical-critical essay that becomes a broader imaginative archaeology of Buenos Aires; the expanded 1955 edition adds substantially different material. [1][2]
- **1932:** *Discusión*. Essays and literary criticism; revised and expanded in 1957. [1][2]
- **1936:** *Historia de la eternidad*. Essay collection that also first housed “El acercamiento a Almotásim,” a fiction disguised as a review of a nonexistent book. [1][2]
- **1947:** *Nueva refutación del tiempo*. Philosophical essay issued as a small independent book; its deliberately paradoxical “refutation” is closer to a speculative intervention than a systematic treatise. [2][3]
- **1950:** *Aspectos de la literatura gauchesca* (also catalogued as *Aspectos de la poesía gauchesca*). Critical lectures/essays; bibliographies vary in rendering the title, and later material was absorbed into other books. [1][2]
- **1952:** *Otras inquisiciones (1937–1952)*. Essay collection whose reviews, metaphysical arguments, literary histories, and invented conjectures continually test the boundary between criticism and fiction. [1][2]
- **1955:** *Leopoldo Lugones*. Critical-biographical monograph made with Betina Edelberg. [1][2]
- **1960:** *El hacedor*. Gesamtkunstwerk in miniature: poems, parables, narratives, dedications, dream texts, and essayistic fragments organized as one book. [1][2]
- **1961:** *Antología personal*. Borges’s author-selected arrangement of poetry, fiction, and essays; selection itself is the obra here. [1]
- **1963:** *El lenguaje de Buenos Aires*. Composite book pairing Borges’s writings with essays by José Edmundo Clemente; Clemente enlarged the 1968 edition. [1][2]
- **1968:** *Nueva antología personal*. A second self-anthology with little overlap with the first and several previously uncollected pieces; it is a new arrangement rather than a simple revision. [1]
- **1971:** *El congreso*. Independent book publication of a single long story later incorporated into *El libro de arena*. [1]
- **1977:** *Prólogos con un prólogo de prólogos*. Authorially assembled collection of prologues; these texts act on other books by installing ways of reading them. [1][2]
- **1982:** *Nueve ensayos dantescos*. Nine essays on Dante, assembled as a book late in Borges’s life from work written over many years. [1][2]

### Fiction, pseudo-scholarship, and literary forgery

- **1935:** *Historia universal de la infamia*. Hybrid of rewritten histories, fictional biographies, translations, and forgeries; the expanded 1954/1958 state is not identical to the first edition. [1][2]
- **1941:** *El jardín de senderos que se bifurcan*. First major fiction collection and the nucleus later incorporated into *Ficciones*. [1][2]
- **1944:** *Ficciones*. Composite story collection joining *El jardín de senderos que se bifurcan* to *Artificios*; the 1956 edition added three stories. Many components imitate reviews, bibliographies, or scholarly reports and resist being called conventional stories. [1][2]
- **1949:** *El Aleph*. Story collection; expanded in 1952/1957, so its canonical form is a revised manifestation rather than the exact 1949 object. [1][2]
- **1951:** *La muerte y la brújula*. Borges-selected collection of previously published stories; its status is authorial reconfiguration rather than a wholly new textual corpus. [1]
- **1970:** *El informe de Brodie*. Story collection presented in a deliberately plainer late style. [1][2]
- **1975:** *El libro de arena*. Story collection, including the previously independent *El congreso*. [1][2]
- **1977:** *Rosa y Azul: La rosa de Paracelso; Tigres azules*. Paired limited-edition stories; later bibliographies sometimes treat the two components separately. [1]
- **1983:** *Veinticinco de agosto de 1983 y otros cuentos*. Four-story collection later retitled *La memoria de Shakespeare*; the competing titles name substantially the same late fictional grouping. [1][2]

### Collaborative literary obras and invented authors

- **1935:** *La leche cuajada de La Martona*. Advertising brochure made with Adolfo Bioy Casares for a dairy company; commercial protocol, parody, and collaborative prose object rather than a literary “book” in the usual sense. [1][4]
- **1942:** *Seis problemas para don Isidro Parodi*. Comic detective stories written with Bioy Casares and attributed to their invented author H. Bustos Domecq; authorship is deliberately staged as a fiction. [1][4]
- **1946:** *Dos fantasías memorables*. Two stories made with Bioy Casares under the Bustos Domecq persona, privately printed in 300 copies and not commercially issued until 1970. [1][4]
- **1946:** *Un modelo para la muerte*. Detective novella made with Bioy Casares under the further pseudonym B. Suárez Lynch; privately printed in 300 copies, with a first commercial edition in 1970. [1][4]
- **1953:** *El “Martín Fierro”*. Collaborative critical book with Margarita Guerrero. [1][2]
- **1955:** *La hermana de Eloísa*. A five-story book shared with Luisa Mercedes Levinson; only the title story is jointly written, so the volume is a collaborative container rather than a uniformly coauthored work. [1]
- **1957:** *Manual de zoología fantástica*. Bestiary made with Margarita Guerrero from literary sources, translations, paraphrases, and invention. [1][2]
- **1960:** *Libro del cielo y del infierno*. Anthology-collage made with Bioy Casares; selection, translation, paraphrase, and arrangement turn inherited passages into a new conceptual obra. [1][4]
- **1966:** *Literaturas germánicas medievales*. Critical history made with María Esther Vázquez; it reworks rather than simply reprints the earlier *Antiguas literaturas germánicas*. [1][2]
- **1967:** *Crónicas de Bustos Domecq*. Mock cultural criticism made with Bioy Casares and voiced by their invented author; fiction, essay, and literary performance are inseparable. [1][4]
- **1967:** *El libro de los seres imaginarios*. Expanded transformation, with Margarita Guerrero, of *Manual de zoología fantástica*; later Spanish and English manifestations contain differing selections. [1][2]
- **1977:** *Nuevos cuentos de Bustos Domecq*. Final lifetime collection made with Bioy Casares; some bibliographies incorrectly date it 1972. [4]
- **1984:** *Atlas*. Collaborative book with María Kodama combining travel photographs with Borges’s poems and prose; a word-image atlas and shared travel obra, not simply an essay collection. [1][2]

### Literary histories and pedagogical protocols

- **1951:** *Antiguas literaturas germánicas*. Literary history made with Delia Ingenieros. [1][2]
- **1965:** *Introducción a la literatura inglesa*. Introductory literary history made with María Esther Vázquez; its form is pedagogical protocol as well as criticism. [1][2]
- **1967:** *Introducción a la literatura norteamericana*. Literary history made with Esther Zemborain de Torres. [1][2]
- **1976:** *Qué es el budismo*. Expository book made with Alicia Jurado from lectures and research; it presents a tradition rather than claiming original religious doctrine. [1][2]

### Anthologies as made arrangements

- **1937:** *Antología clásica de la literatura argentina*. Selection made with Pedro Henríquez Ureña; its ordering constructs a usable Argentine canon. [2][5]
- **1940:** *Antología de la literatura fantástica*. Anthology made with Adolfo Bioy Casares and Silvina Ocampo; revised in 1965 and 1976, with translations, editorial interventions, and occasional uncertain or fictive attributions. [4][5]
- **1941:** *Antología poética argentina*. Poetry anthology made with Bioy Casares and Silvina Ocampo. [5]
- **1943:** *Los mejores cuentos policiales*. Detective-fiction anthology made with Bioy Casares; it includes translations and Borges’s own fiction, so editing and authorship overlap. [4][5]
- **1945:** *El compadrito: su destino, sus barrios, su música*. Thematic anthology made with Silvina Bullrich, including Borges’s prologue and other contributions. [1][5]
- **1951:** *Los mejores cuentos policiales, segunda serie*. Second detective anthology made with Bioy Casares; later editions sometimes merge both series. [4][5]
- **1955:** *Cuentos breves y extraordinarios*. Anthology-collage made with Bioy Casares from translated, paraphrased, altered, and sometimes fabricated miniatures; the category “anthology” does not fully describe its creative rewriting. [4]
- **1970:** *El matrero*. Borges-edited anthology of Argentine writing containing signed pieces and three Borgesian literary forgeries, making the editorial apparatus part of the fiction. [1]
- **1976:** *Libro de sueños*. Anthology of dreams and dreamlike texts assembled through quotation, translation, paraphrase, and original writing. It is a constellation of found and made texts rather than a conventionally authored book. [1][2]

### Manifestos, magazines, and editorial projects

- **1921:** *Ultraísmo*. Manifesto signed by Borges, Guillermo Juan, Eduardo González Lanuza, and Guillermo de Torre; it acts by announcing and organizing an avant-garde position rather than merely describing one. [2][5]
- **1921–1922:** *Prisma*. Two-issue mural magazine co-founded and edited by Borges; pasted to Buenos Aires walls, it was publication, visual object, and public intervention. [2][5]
- **1922–1923; 1924–1926:** *Proa*. Literary magazine co-founded and co-edited by Borges in two distinct series; the collective editorial project cannot be reduced to his individually signed texts. [2][5]
- **1936–1937:** *Destiempo*. Three-issue magazine made with Bioy Casares; a collaborative editorial experiment whose brief existence was the project. [4]
- **1946–1948:** *Los Anales de Buenos Aires*. Magazine directed by Borges; it published fiction, criticism, translations, and works under pseudonyms, but institutional and collective production prevents treating every page as solely his work. [2][5]
- **1945–1956:** *El Séptimo Círculo*. Detective-fiction series founded and initially directed with Bioy Casares. The series—selection, sequence, visual identity, and canon-making—is an editorial project, not a Borges-authored set of books. [4]
- **1975–1985:** *La Biblioteca de Babel*. International fantastic-literature series selected and introduced by Borges; each prologue acts on another author’s text, while the ordered series forms a larger curatorial obra. [2][5]

### Translations as Borges-made texts

- **1932:** *El cementerio marino*. Spanish translation of Paul Valéry’s poem; Borges made a new Spanish verbal object but not the underlying poem. [5]
- **1936:** *Perséphone*. Translation of André Gide’s dramatic poem, made with María Luisa Bombal. [5]
- **1937:** *Orlando*. Spanish translation of Virginia Woolf’s novel; published under Borges’s name, though scholarship has discussed the possible uncredited involvement of his mother, Leonor Acevedo. [5][6]
- **1938:** *La metamorfosis*. Spanish Kafka collection long credited to Borges. Borges denied translating the title novella, and scholars attribute at least that translation elsewhere; the attribution should therefore remain disputed. [5][6]
- **1940:** *Las palmeras salvajes*. Spanish translation of William Faulkner’s novel; as with *Orlando*, possible assistance or authorship by Leonor Acevedo has been debated. [5][6]
- **1941:** *Un bárbaro en Asia*. Spanish translation of Henri Michaux’s travel prose. [5]
- **1943:** *Bartleby*. Spanish translation of Herman Melville’s novella. [5]
- **1944:** *La carta robada*. Spanish translation of Edgar Allan Poe’s story. [5]
- **1944:** *Tres cuentos*. Spanish translations from Franz Kafka; the exact distribution of Borges’s responsibility within Kafka translations remains bibliographically contested. [5][6]
- **1969:** *Hojas de hierba*. Borges’s selection, translation, and prologue for Walt Whitman; it is a deliberately partial Spanish Whitman shaped by Borges’s choices. [5]

### Screenplays and moving-image obras

- **1955:** *Los orilleros*. Screenplay made with Bioy Casares; published before its eventual 1975 filming, so the script is an autonomous text as well as a protocol for a possible film. [1][4]
- **1955:** *El paraíso de los creyentes*. Unfilmed screenplay made with Bioy Casares; the unrealized project is still the obra. [1][4]
- **1969:** *Invasión*. Film conceived and scripted by Borges, Bioy Casares, and director Hugo Santiago. The finished film is collaborative moving-image work, not solely a Borges text. [7]
- **1973–1974:** *Les autres / Los otros*. Film screenplay made by Borges and Bioy Casares for Hugo Santiago; dating varies between the script’s preparation, 1973 production, and 1974 release. [7]

### Songs, recordings, and performed voice

- **1965:** *El tango*. Record/sonic work combining Astor Piazzolla’s music, Borges’s milonga and tango texts, and performances by singers and speakers. Borges made the words and participated in the conception, but the recording is a collaborative manifestation rather than his sole obra. [3]
- **1967–1968:** *The Norton Lectures on Poetry*. Six English-language lectures delivered at Harvard and preserved on tape; an oral work later transcribed as *This Craft of Verse*, not a book Borges himself prepared. [1][8]
- **1966:** *Curso de literatura inglesa en la Universidad de Buenos Aires*. Twenty-five spoken classes; students’ notes and later editorial reconstruction preserve the course, but the posthumous book is a manifestation made by editors from Borges’s teaching. [1][8]
- **1977:** *Borges el memorioso*. Recorded conversations with Antonio Carrizo. Authorship is dialogic: Borges’s speech is the material, while Carrizo’s questions and the transcription shape the object. [1]
- **1979:** *Borges, oral*. Five lectures assembled as a book; the primary obra was performed speech, with print as its fixed manifestation. [1][2]
- **1980:** *Siete noches*. Seven lectures revised for publication; both oral event and authorially shaped book must be counted. [1][2]

### Dialogues and assisted verbal works

- **1968:** *Conversations with Jorge Luis Borges*. English-language conversations with Richard Burgin; neither pure Borges authorship nor mere documentation, but a dialogic work shaped by interviewer, speaker, transcription, and editing. [1]
- **1973:** *Borges on Writing*. Seminars and conversations edited by Norman Thomas di Giovanni, Daniel Halpern, and Frank MacShane; Borges made the spoken responses, not the later book alone. [1]
- **1976:** *Diálogos: Borges–Sabato*. Conversations with Ernesto Sabato recorded and arranged by Orlando Barone; a three-person textual construction rather than a singly authored work. [1]
- **1977:** *Diálogos*. Conversations with Osvaldo Ferrari began appearing in broadcast form later and were subsequently collected in several volumes; the exact book boundaries are editorial manifestations of an extended spoken project. [5]

### Collected-work projects and posthumous manifestations

- **1953–1985:** *Obras completas*. Borges repeatedly revised the projected corpus through successive volumes and the 1974 and 1985 collected editions. It is an authorial project of selection, rewriting, inclusion, and exclusion—not a neutral container and never literally complete. [1][2]
- **1981:** *Obras completas en colaboración*. Authorially recognized collection of collaborative books; its arrangement consolidates works previously dispersed under real and invented names. [1][2]
- **1986:** *Textos cautivos*. Posthumous editorial collection by Enrique Sacerio-Garí and Emir Rodríguez Monegal of Borges’s writings for *El Hogar*. The component texts are Borges’s; the book as a book is not his own finished construction. [1]
- **1997:** *Textos recobrados 1919–1929*. Posthumous recovery of poems, manifestos, essays, reviews, and other fugitive publications. It documents made texts but is an editorial manifestation Borges neither titled nor assembled. [1]
- **2000:** *Autobiografía*. Spanish translation of the autobiographical essay Borges composed orally in English with Norman Thomas di Giovanni for *The New Yorker*. Its individual authorship, language, and textual mediation are inseparable and have been disputed. [1]
- **2000:** *This Craft of Verse*. Posthumous transcription of the 1967–1968 Norton lectures from rediscovered recordings; the lectures are Borges’s work, while this book-object belongs partly to its editor, Călin-Andrei Mihăilescu. [1][8]
- **2002:** *Textos recobrados 1931–1955*. Posthumous editorial corpus of stories, essays, poems, journalism, and reviews; recovery, not a Borges-designed book. [1]
- **2002:** *Museo: textos inéditos*. Posthumous collection of fugitive collaborations with Bioy Casares, including reviews, parodies, translations, and advertising. The texts are collaborative obras; the collection is an editorial manifestation. [4]
- **2003:** *Textos recobrados 1956–1986*. Posthumous editorial corpus of essays, poems, articles, and reviews; it expands access to Borges’s production without becoming a lifetime-authored book. [1]
- **2005:** *Borges profesor: Curso de literatura inglesa en la Universidad de Buenos Aires*. Reconstruction of Borges’s 1966 course from student notes and recordings, edited by Martín Arias and Martín Hadis. It preserves a pedagogical project but cannot be treated as a manuscript Borges prepared. [1][8]
- **2025:** *Cuadernos y conferencias*. Posthumous edition of notebooks Borges used to prepare talks after losing his sight. The notebooks and lecture projects are his; the titled publication and its boundaries are editorial. [9]
