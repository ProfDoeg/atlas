## Works by Joseph Alois Schumpeter

### Monographs and book-length obra

- **1908:** *Das Wesen und der Hauptinhalt der theoretischen Nationalökonomie*. A monograph on the nature, scope, and methods of theoretical economics.
- **1911/1912:** *Theorie der wirtschaftlichen Entwicklung*. A theoretical monograph on innovation, entrepreneurship, credit, profit, and cycles. The title page bears 1912, while bibliographies also date its appearance to 1911; the substantially revised 1926 edition is effectively a remade work, not merely a reprint.
- **1914:** *Epochen der Dogmen- und Methodengeschichte*. A book-length history of economic doctrine and method, originally issued as a division of Max Weber’s *Grundriss der Sozialökonomik*.
- **1915:** *Vergangenheit und Zukunft der Sozialwissenschaften*. A separately published lecture or pamphlet on the past and future of the social sciences.
- **1918:** *Die Krise des Steuerstaates*. A long essay issued separately, diagnosing the fiscal state historically and sociologically.
- **1919:** *Zur Soziologie der Imperialismen*. A two-part sociological study of imperialism, first appearing in a journal and also issued as a pamphlet.
- **1926:** *Theorie der wirtschaftlichen Entwicklung*, second edition. A thorough reconstruction of the 1911/12 book: Schumpeter removed the long methodological chapter and materially altered the argument, so it is an important second obra rather than a routine edition.
- **1934:** *The Theory of Economic Development*. The English manifestation of the revised *Theorie*, translated by Redvers Opie with Schumpeter’s participation; it does not reproduce the substantially different first edition.
- **1939:** *Business Cycles: A Theoretical, Historical and Statistical Analysis of the Capitalist Process*. A two-volume theoretical and historical investigation intended as an integrated analysis of capitalist evolution.
- **1942:** *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*. A hybrid obra—history of thought, sociology, political theory, and economic diagnosis—rather than a conventional economics treatise.
- **1946:** *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*, second edition. A revised manifestation containing a new preface and the new chapter “The Consequences of the Second World War.”
- **1946:** *Rudimentary Mathematics for Economists and Statisticians*. A mathematical handbook jointly attributed to William L. Crum and Schumpeter; its shared authorship prevents treating it as Schumpeter’s unaided obra.
- **1949:** *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*, third edition. A further revised manifestation with “Comments on Further Postwar Development.”
- **1920s–1950; published 1954:** *History of Economic Analysis*. An unfinished book-project left in manuscript at Schumpeter’s death and constructed for publication by Elizabeth Boody Schumpeter. It resists classification as a completed Schumpeter book because its final organization and editorial transitions are partly posthumous.
- **Unfinished; published 1970:** *Das Wesen des Geldes*. An unfinished treatise on money edited posthumously by Fritz Karl Mann; it is a surviving project, not a book Schumpeter completed or authorized in published form.

- **1909:** *On the Concept of Social Value*. An English-language theoretical article on social valuation.
- **1909:** *Bemerkungen über das Zurechnungsproblem*. A substantial article on imputation in value and distribution theory.
- **1910:** *Die neue Wirtschaftstheorie in den Vereinigten Staaten*. A survey and interpretation of contemporary American economic theory.
- **1910:** *Über das Wesen der Wirtschaftskrisen*. A theoretical study of economic crises.

- **1911:** *Gründungsgewinn in Recht und Wirtschaft*. A lecture on promoters’ profits surviving through three distinct contemporary reports; the reports are manifestations, and none can automatically be treated as a verbatim authorial text.
- **1913:** *Eine “dynamische” Theorie des Kapitalzinses: Eine Entgegnung*. A polemical theoretical reply, published together with Böhm-Bawerk’s rejoinder.
- **1913:** *Meinungsäusserung zur Frage des Werturteils*. A privately printed intervention in the dispute over value judgments.
- **1913:** *Zinsfuss und Geldverfassung*. An article on interest and monetary organization.
- **1914–1915:** *Die Wellenbewegung des Wirtschaftslebens*. A study of cyclical movement.
- **1914:** *Die “positive” Methode in der Nationalökonomie*. A methodological article.
- **1916–1917:** *Das Grundprinzip der Verteilungstheorie*. A major theoretical article on distribution.
- **1917–1918:** *Das Bodenmonopol*. A reply to Franz Oppenheimer concerning land monopoly.
- **1917–1918:** *Das Sozialprodukt und die Rechenpfennige*. An extended contribution to monetary theory.
- **1917/1918:** *Grundsätzliche Bemerkungen zur Valutafrage*. An unpublished manuscript on currency policy; its date is approximate.
- **1920–1921:** *Sozialistische Möglichkeiten von heute*. A long analytical essay on the practical possibility of socialism.
- **1923:** *Angebot*. An encyclopedia article on supply.
- **1923:** *Zum heutigen Stand der Diskussion zum Kapital*. An encyclopedia supplement on the state of capital theory.
- **1924:** *Der Sozialismus in England und bei uns*. A comparative essay on British and Central European socialism.
- **1924:** *Geldwerttheorie und Währungspraxis*. An intervention occasioned by the second edition of Ludwig von Mises’s monetary treatise.
- **1925:** *Das “volkswirtschaftliche Einkommen” aus der Landwirtschaft*. A short statistical-economic study of agricultural income.
- **1925:** *Kreditkontrolle*. A substantial article on credit control.
- **1925:** *Oude en nieuwe bankpolitiek*. A Dutch-language intervention on old and new banking policy.
- **1925:** *The Currency Situation in Austria*. A policy study prepared for the American investigation of European currency and finance.
- **1926:** *Konjunkturforschung*. A two-part newspaper essay on business-cycle research.
- **1927:** *Zur Frage der Grenzproduktivität*. A theoretical reply concerning marginal productivity.
- **1927:** *Die goldene Bremse an der Kreditmaschine*. A published lecture on gold and credit.
- **1927:** *Die sozialen Klassen im ethnisch homogenen Milieu*. A sociological treatise on social classes.
- **1927:** *Die Wirtschaftstheorie der Gegenwart in Deutschland*. A survey of contemporary German economic theory.
- **1927:** *Unternehmerfunktion und Arbeiterinteresse*. A policy essay relating entrepreneurship to workers’ interests.
- **1927:** *Zur Einführung der folgenden Arbeit Knut Wicksells*. An introduction to a publication of Wicksell’s mathematical economics.
- **1927:** *The Explanation of the Business Cycle*. An English-language theoretical article.
- **1928:** *Lohngestaltung und Wirtschaftsentwicklung*. An article on wage formation and economic development.
- **1928:** *Unternehmer*. An encyclopedia article defining the entrepreneur.
- **1928:** *Der Unternehmer in der Volkswirtschaft von heute*. An essay on the entrepreneur’s place in the contemporary economy.
- **1928:** *International Cartels and Their Relation to World Trade*. A conference paper on cartels and trade.
- **1928:** *Staatsreferendar und Staatsassessor*. An essay on German professional and administrative education.
- **1928:** *The Instability of Capitalism*. A major article asking whether capitalism is structurally unstable.
- **1928–1929:** *Erbschaftssteuer*. A policy essay on inheritance taxation.
- **1930:** *Das Kapital im wirtschaftlichen Kreislauf und in der wirtschaftlichen Entwicklung*. An essay distinguishing capital in static circulation from capital in development.
- **1930:** *Mitchell’s Business Cycles*. A long critical assessment of Wesley Clair Mitchell’s work.
- **1931:** *The Present World Depression: A Tentative Diagnosis*. A conference intervention diagnosing the Depression.
- **1931:** *The “Crisis” in Economics—Fifty Years Ago*. Probably derived from a Tokyo lecture; its precise original occasion remains qualified in the bibliography.
- **1931:** *The World Depression with Special Reference to the United States of America*. A Tokyo lecture whose reported Japanese publication is now considered lost.
- **1931:** *The Theory of the Business Cycle*. A Tokyo lecture published in a Japanese economics journal.
- **1931:** *The Present State of Economics—or on Systems, Schools and Methods*. A Japanese lecture-publication on the condition of economics.
- **1931:** *Recent Developments of Political Economy*. A Kobe lecture surviving through manuscript and later publication.
- **1931:** *The Present State of International Commercial Policy*. A Japanese lecture-publication on trade policy.
- **1932:** *A German View of the Reparations Problem*. An article also circulated in German translation and excerpt as *Vor der Reparationskonferenz*.
- **1932:** *Entwicklung*. An essay prepared as a contribution to a Festschrift for Emil Lederer.
- **1932:** *Das Woher und Wohin unserer Wissenschaft*. A farewell lecture reconstructed in a typescript prepared by August Lösch and Kläre Tisch; the mediated text resists treatment as an untouched authorial manuscript.
- **1933:** *The Common Sense of Econometrics*. A methodological article in the first volume of *Econometrica*.
- **1933:** *Am Streit mehr Freude, als an der Zusammenarbeit …?* A contribution, accompanied by Schumpeter’s preface, to the Spiethoff Festschrift on business-cycle research.
- **1934:** *Depression—Can We Learn from Past Experience?*. A chapter on lessons from earlier depressions.
- **1934:** *The Nature and Necessity of a Price System*. A chapter in the Columbia University Commission’s report on economic reconstruction.
- **1935:** *A Theorist’s Comment on the Current Business Cycle*. A short theoretical intervention.
- **1935:** *The Analysis of Economic Change*. An article on the analysis of capitalist change.
- **1937:** *Suggestions for the Quantitative Study of the Business Cycle*. A research-conference paper proposing empirical work.
- **1940:** *Monetary Processes during War Booms and Postwar Slumps*. A conference contribution known through an *Econometrica* report; the report is a manifestation rather than necessarily Schumpeter’s own finished text.
- **1940:** *The Influence of Protective Tariffs on the Industrial Development of the United States*. A published conference paper.
- **1941; published in part 1984:** *The Meaning of Rationality in the Social Sciences*. A manuscript or lecture later published only in part; it remains textually incomplete as a public obra.
- **1941:** *An Economic Interpretation of Our Time*. The Lowell Lectures, a lecture-cycle later edited from surviving materials.
- **1942:** *Review of George J. Stigler, The Theory of Competitive Price*. A substantive review article.
- **1943:** *Capitalism in the Postwar World*. A chapter on capitalism after the war.
- **1944:** *Untitled contribution in the American Economic Review, 34(1), pp. 161–164*. The bibliography records the publication but supplies no title; no title should be invented for it.
- **1946:** *L’avenir de l’entreprise privée devant les tendances socialistes modernes*. A Montreal address on preserving private enterprise.
- **1946:** *The American Economy in the Interwar Period: The Decade of the Twenties*. A presidential address to the American Economic Association.
- **1946:** *Capitalism*. An *Encyclopaedia Britannica* article.
- **1946:** *Keynes and Statistics*. A short article on Keynes’s use of statistics.
- **1947:** *Comments on a Plan for the Study of Entrepreneurship*. A research-program memorandum: closer to a protocol for inquiry than a conventional essay.
- **1947:** *Theoretical Problems of Economic Growth*. A conference paper.
- **1947:** *The Creative Response in Economic History*. An article defining innovation as a creative historical response.
- **1948:** *Wage and Tax Policy in Transitional States of Society*. A cycle of five lectures delivered at the National University of Mexico.
- **1948:** *There Is Still Time to Stop Inflation*. A public-policy magazine article.
- **1948:** *Statement on the Choice of Textbooks*. A very short professional statement.
- **1949:** *Science and Ideology*. A presidential address examining ideological elements in economic analysis.
- **1949:** *The Communist Manifesto in Sociology and Economics*. A historical and sociological interpretation of Marx and Engels’s manifesto; it is criticism of a manifesto, not itself a manifesto.
- **1949:** *Economic Theory and Entrepreneurial History*. A programmatic essay joining economic theory to entrepreneurial history.
- **1949; published 1987:** *Some Questions of Principle*. A manuscript published posthumously; its late manifestation should not be mistaken for a work written in 1987.
- **1949; published 1951:** *The Historical Approach to the Analysis of Business Cycles*. A conference paper published after Schumpeter’s death.
- **1949:** *English Economists and the State-Managed Economy*. A historical article on English economic thought and managed economy.
- **1950:** *The March into Socialism*. Schumpeter’s final American Economic Association address: a performative public intervention as well as an analytical text.

### Political memoranda and ministerial acts

- **1916–1918:** *Political Memoranda I–VII*. Seven confidential political memoranda, several untitled and therefore conventionally identified by opening words; the seventh survives only as a fragment. They are policy projects and interventions rather than publications.
- **1916:** *Grundlinien einer neuen juristischen Studienordnung*. A protocol-like proposal for reforming legal education, made jointly with Otto von Dungern, Armin Ehrenzweig, Max Layer, and Adolf Lenz.
- **1917:** *Universität Graz: Volkswirtschaftliches Seminar*. An institutional report on Schumpeter’s economics seminar.
- **1919:** *Grundlinien der Finanzpolitik für jetzt und die nächsten drei Jahre*. An official financial plan: simultaneously a policy document, governmental protocol, and political project.
- **March–October 1919:** *Ministerial speeches, parliamentary interventions, statements, interviews, and memoranda as State Secretary of Finance*. This corpus includes his program on taking office; advocacy of a capital levy; parliamentary speeches of 2 and 4 April, 25 April, 6 May, 4 and 28 July; statements on Hungary, peace negotiations, reconstruction, socialization banking, currency, the budget, provincial finance, state employees, and the Alpine affair; and his confidential financial-plan presentations. The surviving objects are heterogeneous—stenographic records, official documents, interviews, and newspaper reports—so many are manifestations of Schumpeter’s acts rather than texts demonstrably authored word for word by him.
- **Autumn 1919:** *Letter from Schumpeter to Chancellor Karl Renner*. An undated ministerial letter conventionally assigned to autumn 1919; its date is not more precise.
- **1919:** *Reports and declarations concerning the Alpine affair*. Official explanations to the cabinet and an investigating committee; these resist classification as autonomous works but belong to Schumpeter’s made governmental record.

### Archival projects, correspondence, and resistant objects

- **1911–1915:** *Reports on the economics seminars at the University of Graz*. Administrative reports for four academic years; institutional documents that resist treatment as literary works.
- **1913–1914:** *Bericht über die Mission als Austauschprofessor an der Columbia-Universität in New York*. An official report on Schumpeter’s American exchange professorship.
- **1940s:** *Pieces of Manuscripts, Discarded Manuscripts, Notes and Other Materials for the Socialism Book*. A heterogeneous archival body for *Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy*: plans, discarded passages, and drafts whose very abandonment resists conversion into separate finished works.
- **Throughout his life; published 2000:** *Briefe/Letters*. A posthumously edited body of 245 letters. The individual letters are things Schumpeter made, but the selected collection and its title are editorial objects made by Ulrich Hedtke and Richard Swedberg, not a book conceived by Schumpeter.
- **Posthumous 1951:** *Ten Great Economists: From Marx to Keynes*. An editorial collection of previously made biographical essays, assembled after Schumpeter’s death by Elizabeth Boody Schumpeter; the collection is not a newly authored 1951 work.
- **Posthumous 1951:** *Essays of J. A. Schumpeter*. Richard V. Clemence’s editorial collection of previously published papers; it is a new manifestation, not a distinct Schumpeter obra.
- **Posthumous 1952–1954:** *Aufsätze zur ökonomischen Theorie; Aufsätze zur Soziologie; Dogmenhistorische und biographische Aufsätze*. Editorial collected-works volumes assembled by Erich Schneider and Arthur Spiethoff; useful witnesses to Schumpeter’s corpus, but not books he designed.
- **Posthumous 1985–1993:** *Politische Reden; Aufsätze zur Wirtschaftspolitik; Aufsätze zur Tagespolitik*. Scholarly editorial reconstructions by Christian Seidl and Wolfgang F. Stolper; they manifest dispersed speeches and journalism rather than add newly made Schumpeter works.

### Sources

- Elizabeth B. Schumpeter, “[Bibliography of the Writings of Joseph A. Schumpeter](https://doi.org/10.2307/1884556),” *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 64, no. 3 (1950), pp. 373–384. This contemporary scholarly bibliography divides the corpus into books and pamphlets, articles, and reviews.
- Massimo M. Augello, “[Works by Schumpeter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-75999-4_3),” in *Joseph Alois Schumpeter: A Reference Guide* (Springer, 1990), pp. 115–158. This is the later scholarly reference bibliography used to check the expanded corpus.
- Ulrich Hedtke, “[Digitale Textnachweise und Bibliografie des Gesamtwerkes sowie der Vorlesungsmitschriften](https://www.schumpeter.info/doks/bibliografie.html),” version of 28 May 2025. This research bibliography explicitly includes monographs, articles, minor pieces, lectures, ministerial statements, unauthorized lecture reports, manuscripts, and newly identified Schumpeteriana, while warning that gaps remain.
- Ulrich Hedtke, “[Online-Nachweis der Hauptwerke](https://www.schumpeter.info/doks/hauptwerke.html),” Schumpeter Archive. Used to distinguish principal works from editions, translations, and posthumous editorial collections.
- Richard Swedberg, ed., *The Economics and Sociology of Capitalism* (Princeton University Press, 1991). Used through Hedtke’s bibliographic cross-references for unpublished lectures, research memoranda, and posthumously edited manuscripts.
- Erich Schneider and Arthur Spiethoff, eds., *Aufsätze zur ökonomischen Theorie* (1952), *Aufsätze zur Soziologie* (1953), and *Dogmenhistorische und biographische Aufsätze* (1954). These collected-work volumes are witnesses for dispersed articles, not the basis for treating later reprints as new works.
- Christian Seidl and Wolfgang F. Stolper, eds., *Politische Reden* (1985), *Aufsätze zur Wirtschaftspolitik* (1985), and *Aufsätze zur Tagespolitik* (1993). These editions supply the documentary basis for Schumpeter’s political, ministerial, and journalistic corpus.
