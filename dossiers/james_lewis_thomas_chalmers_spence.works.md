## Works by James Lewis Thomas Chalmers Spence

### Mythography, folklore, and cultural history

- **1905:** *The Mysteries of Britain: Secret Rites and Traditions of Ancient Britain Restored*. A speculative reconstruction of ancient British religion and ritual; bibliographies disagree over whether to treat it as Spence’s first substantial book. [1]
- **1907:** *The Mythologies of Ancient Mexico and Peru*. A concise comparative mythography and the precursor—not merely another edition—of Spence’s later, larger treatment of the same traditions. [1]
- **1908:** *The Popol Vuh: The Mythic and Heroic Sagas of the Kichés of Central America*. An English retelling assembled through earlier European translations rather than a direct translation from K’iche’; it is therefore simultaneously adaptation, interpretation, and popularization. [1]
- **1910:** *A Dictionary of Mythology*. A reference work covering several ancient and traditional mythologies. [1]
- **1911:** *The Civilisation of Ancient Mexico*. A historical and ethnographic synthesis; the title also appears with the spelling *Civilization*. [1]
- **1913:** *The Myths of Mexico and Peru*. A substantially developed mythographic survey, not simply a reprint of the 1907 book. [1]
- **1913:** *A Dictionary of Medieval Romance and Romance Writers*. A reference work devoted to medieval narrative traditions, characters, cycles, and authors. [1]
- **1914:** *The Myths of the North American Indians*. A synthetic retelling and classification of Indigenous North American narratives; its cultural groupings and terminology reflect early-twentieth-century anthropology. [1]
- **1915:** *Hero Tales and Legends of the Rhine*. An illustrated collection and literary retelling of Rhine legends rather than field-collected folklore. [1]
- **1915:** *Myths and Legends of Ancient Egypt*. A popular mythographic survey, subsequently reissued under such titles as *Ancient Egyptian Myths and Legends*; those later titles are manifestations, not separate obras. [1]
- **1916:** *Myths and Legends of Babylonia and Assyria*. A mythographic and historical synthesis; some catalogues date the American manifestation to 1917, but the British first edition is dated 1916. [1]
- **1917:** *Mexico of the Mexicans*. A contemporary cultural, social, political, and economic survey of Mexico, distinct from Spence’s books on pre-Columbian mythology; an American manifestation is dated 1918. [1]
- **1917:** *Legends and Romances of Brittany*. A literary compilation and retelling of Breton legendary material. [1]
- **1920:** *The Legends and Romances of Spain*. A collection and retelling of Spanish heroic, legendary, and romantic narratives. [1]
- **1921:** *An Introduction to Mythology*. A methodological textbook proposing ways to compare and interpret myth. [1]
- **1923:** *The Gods of Mexico*. A sustained study of pre-Columbian Mexican religion and iconography rather than a narrative anthology. [1]
- **1928:** *The Mysteries of Britain: Secret Rites and Traditions of Ancient Britain Restored*. The bibliographical record also assigns this title to 1905; the 1928 book is variously treated as the first edition or as a later manifestation, and the available bibliographies do not resolve the discrepancy consistently. [1][4]
- **1929:** *The Mysteries of Egypt; or, The Secret Rites and Traditions of the Nile*. A speculative reconstruction of Egyptian initiation, ritual, and esoteric tradition. [4]
- **1930 or 1932:** *The Magic and Mysteries of Mexico*. A study of Mexican religion, magic, and ritual; bibliographies divide between 1930 and 1932, apparently reflecting different national manifestations. [1][4]
- **1937:** *Boadicea: Warrior Queen of the Britons*. A popular historical biography that also participates in Spence’s reconstruction of ancient British identity. [4]
- **1938 or 1949:** *The History and Origins of Druidism*. A historical and speculative study of Druidism. Its first-publication date is disputed in secondary bibliographies, which give 1938 or 1949; later retitlings such as *Druids: Their Origins and History* are manifestations. [4]
- **1944:** *The Outlines of Mythology*. A compact survey produced for the Thinker’s Library; it is a new concise synthesis, although much of its subject matter revisits Spence’s earlier mythographic books. [5]
- **1945:** *The Religion of Ancient Mexico*. A short synthetic study issued in the Thinker’s Library; its relationship to Spence’s earlier Mexican-religion books is close enough that it resists easy classification as wholly new rather than substantially recomposed. [5]
- **1945 or 1949:** *The Magic Arts in Celtic Britain*. A study of Celtic magic, divination, supernatural belief, and Arthurian and Grail traditions. Sources give both dates, probably because they conflate distinct British manifestations; the dating remains unresolved here. [4][6]
- **1946:** *British Fairy Origins: The Genesis and Development of Fairy Legends in British Tradition*. A historical theory of British fairy belief rather than a collection of tales. [4]
- **1947:** *Myth and Ritual in Dance, Game, and Rhyme*. A comparative interpretive study arguing that customary performances and children’s activities preserve ritual structures. [7]
- **1948:** *The Minor Traditions of British Mythology*. A study of less-canonical British mythic figures and cycles. [4]
- **1948 or 1951:** *The Fairy Tradition in Britain*. A large synthetic study of fairy belief. Bibliographies give both 1948 and 1951; the disagreement appears to concern publication history rather than two independently made texts. [4]
- **1951:** *Second Sight: Its History and Origins*. A historical and folkloric study of visionary experience, especially in Scottish tradition. [8]
- **1952:** *Scottish Ghosts and Goblins*. A classified presentation and interpretation of Scottish supernatural traditions. [4]

### Occult reference, biography, and wartime interpretation

- **1920:** *An Encyclopaedia of Occultism*. A large alphabetical reference work on magic, occult movements, psychical research, personalities, and terminology; later expansions titled *Encyclopedia of Occultism and Parapsychology* incorporate other editors’ work and are not solely Spence’s obra. [1][9]
- **1921:** *Cornelius Agrippa, Occult Philosopher*. A biographical and interpretive monograph on Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa. [10]
- **1940:** *The Occult Causes of the Present War*. A polemical occult interpretation of Nazism and the Second World War, not a neutral history; as a text intended to intervene in an ongoing conflict, it approaches manifesto. [4]
- **1943:** *The Occult Sciences in Atlantis*. A speculative synthesis joining Spence’s lost-continent theory to his history of magic and esotericism. [4]

### Atlantis and other lost-world projects

- **1924:** *The Problem of Atlantis*. The opening book of Spence’s extended project to argue for Atlantis as a historical civilization; the revised 1925 issue and the later title *Atlantis Discovered* are manifestations, not new works. [1][4]
- **1925:** *Atlantis in America*. A comparative-archaeological argument that purported Old World–New World correspondences preserve Atlantean influence. [1]
- **1926 or 1927:** *The History of Atlantis*. Spence’s synthetic narrative of Atlantean civilization. British evidence supports 1926, while American and later bibliographies often give 1927; this is an edition-date dispute, not evidence of two separate obras. [1][11]
- **1932:** *The Problem of Lemuria: The Sunken Continent of the Pacific*. A speculative lost-continent study parallel to the Atlantis project. [4]
- **1942:** *Will Europe Follow Atlantis?* A short wartime warning that uses Atlantis as a political and civilizational analogy; it functions partly as a manifesto rather than merely describing the legendary continent. [4]
- **Undated as independent texts:** *The Atlantis of Plato* and *The Evidence for Lemuria from Myth and Magic*. These names circulate as if they were books, but reliable bibliographical evidence for independent lifetime obras under these titles is lacking; they appear to be extracts or later repackagings and therefore resist inclusion as separate works. [4]

### Poetry

- **1910:** *Le Roi d’Ys and Other Poems*. A poetry collection drawing on legendary, historical, and supernatural subjects. [1]
- **1913:** *Songs Satanic and Celestial*. A poetry collection whose paired title frames profane and sacred modes rather than a doctrinal occult treatise. [1]
- **1923:** *The Phoenix and Other Poems*. A Scots and English poetry collection associated with the Scottish literary revival; some bibliographies give 1924, but the Scottish Poetry Library identifies the Porpoise Press edition as 1923. [1][2]
- **1926:** *Plumes of Time*. A poetry collection in English and literary Scots. [2][3]
- **1927:** *Weirds and Vanities*. A poetry collection; one bibliographical transcription corrupts the title as *Weirds and Inanities*, but Scottish literary bibliographies support *Vanities*. [1][2][3]
- **1953:** *Collected Poems of Lewis Spence*. An authorial retrospective collection and therefore both an obra of selection and a manifestation gathering earlier poems; it should not be counted as evidence that every poem in it was newly made in 1953. [2]

### Fiction

- **1932:** *The Archer in the Arras and Other Tales of Mystery*. A collection of supernatural and mystery fiction; abbreviated bibliographies often omit the subtitle, making the object appear to be a single story rather than a story collection. [1][12]

### Translation and mediated work

- **1921:** *Spanish Painting*. An English-language manifestation of Aureliano de Beruete y Moret’s study, with Spence credited as translator. The underlying scholarly text is Beruete’s; Spence’s made contribution is the translation, not original authorship of the whole book. [13]

### Political texts and institutional projects

- **1920s–1928:** *Scottish National Movement*. A political organization founded and led by Spence to pursue Scottish self-government, later absorbed into the National Party of Scotland. It is an institutional project rather than a text or book, and its collective nature prevents treating it as Spence’s sole-authored obra. [14]
- **1928:** *The National Party of Scotland*. An essay published in the *Edinburgh Review* during the creation of the new party; as advocacy addressed to an immediate political formation, it operates partly as manifesto. [15]
- **1934:** *The Essential Scot*. A political-cultural periodical essay rather than a book; it is included because its independent title and attribution survive, while Spence’s much larger body of unsigned or uncollected journalism has no complete scholarly enumeration. [16]

### Editorial and periodical projects

- **1904–1905:** *The Edinburgh Magazine*. A periodical edited by Spence. The magazine is a collective creation and editorial project, not a solely authored text; individual unsigned contributions cannot safely be attributed to him without further evidence. [17]
- **1932–1933:** *The Atlantis Quarterly*. A journal founded and jointly edited by Spence and Charles Richard Cammell; only five issues appeared. It is best classified as an unfinished serial project and collective manifestation rather than a single authored work. [18]
