## Works by Pantaleon of Nicomedia

### No securely attributable obra

- No text, medical treatise, building, artwork, or other made object can be securely attributed to Pantaleon. The standard Greek hagiographical repertory catalogues numerous *Passiones*, encomia, and liturgical texts about him, but assigns them to anonymous hagiographers or later authors—not to Pantaleon himself ([Pinakes, IRHT](https://pinakes.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/saint/704/)).

### Legendary manifestations, not works

- **Undated:** Restoration of a snake-bitten boy. The *Passio* says that Pantaleon restored a dead boy through prayer; this is a narrated miracle—a manifestation attributed to divine power, not an obra made by Pantaleon—and the late, interpolated textual tradition cannot establish it historically ([*Acta Sanctorum*, July VI, 27 July](https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/ActaSanctorum/27.Juli.html#pantaleon)).

- **Undated:** Restoration of a blind man’s sight. The hagiographical tradition attributes this healing to Pantaleon, but presents it as a miracle rather than a documented medical procedure or independently identifiable creation ([*Acta Sanctorum*, July VI, 27 July](https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/ActaSanctorum/27.Juli.html#pantaleon)).

- **Undated:** Healing of a paralytic before Maximian. This is another manifestation narrated by the *Passio*: Pantaleon invokes Christ and the paralytic rises. The Bollandist editors warn that the surviving Acts are not “sincere,” contain evident faults and interpolations, and therefore cannot securely document the episode as Pantaleon’s historical work ([*Acta Sanctorum*, July VI, 27 July](https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/ActaSanctorum/27.Juli.html#pantaleon)).

- **Undated:** Medical practice and unspecified healings. Later tradition characterizes Pantaleon as a physician and credits him collectively with further cures, but preserves neither case records nor a medical corpus made by him; even his biography survives principally as legend ([*Encyclopedia of Ancient Christianity*, “Pantaleon Megalomartyr”](https://www.thetbs.org/study-materials/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Encyclopedia-of-Ancient-Christianity-by-Angelo-Di-Berardino-Thomas-C.-Oden-Joel-C.-Elowsky-James-Hoover-z-lib.org_.pdf); [*Acta Sanctorum*, July VI](https://www.heiligenlexikon.de/ActaSanctorum/27.Juli.html#pantaleon)).
