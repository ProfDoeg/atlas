## Works by Shams al-Din Muhammad of Tabriz

### Spoken teaching preserved as an obra

- **c. 1244–1247 (spoken; compilation undated):** *Maqālāt-e Shams-e Tabrīzī* (*Discourses of Shams of Tabriz*; also transmitted as *Kalimāt-e Shams*, *Maʿārif-e Shams*, *Asrār-e Shams al-Dīn-e Tabrīzī*, and *Khirqa-ye Shams*). This is not a book Shams demonstrably wrote or authorized but a discontinuous Persian record of conversations, teachings, anecdotes, and autobiographical remarks, assembled from notes by members of Rumi’s circle; whether Rumi, Sultan Walad, or several listeners compiled it remains disputed. It therefore resists classification as a single authored text: Shams made the oral *sohbet* or teaching-event, while other hands made the surviving textual obra. The comprehensive critical edition is Mohammad-Ali Movahed’s two-volume edition of 1990; the manuscript tradition preserves differing compilations rather than a settled authorial text. ([TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/sems-i-tebrizi), [Islamisation of Anatolia manuscript project](https://www.islam-anatolia.ac.uk/data/documents/TK_Kon_Mev_2144))

### Rejected or unresolved attribution

- **Undated:** *Marghūb al-qulūb* (“Desired of Hearts”). A Persian didactic poem of 138 couplets in *masnavi* form has been attributed to Shams, but the attribution has not been accepted by specialist scholarship; manuscripts also associate the title with other authors. It should be recorded as an attributed obra, not silently admitted into Shams’s corpus. ([TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/sems-i-tebrizi))

### Namesake rather than work

- **After 1244:** *Dīvān-e Shams-e Tabrīzī* (also *Dīvān-e Shams* or *Dīvān-e Kabīr*). Despite the title and Shams’s name appearing as an invoked presence or poetic signature, this lyric corpus is Rumi’s creation, dedicated to and imaginatively voiced through Shams; it is not a divan made by Shams and therefore does not belong in his oeuvre except as a necessary attribution warning. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/iran-viii2-classical-persian-literature/))
