## Works by Peter Andreas Thiel

### Books and sustained arguments

- **1995:** *The Diversity Myth: Multiculturalism and Political Intolerance on Campus*. A polemical book co-authored with David O. Sacks; the collaboration means it is not exclusively Thiel’s obra. Thiel later apologized for passages concerning rape, so the original text and his subsequent qualification both belong to its history. ([Internet Archive record](https://archive.org/details/diversitymythmul00thie); [Stanford Daily](https://stanforddaily.com/2016/10/31/peter-thiel-apologizes-for-insensitive-statements-on-rape/))

- **2004; collected 2007:** *The Straussian Moment*. A long political-philosophical essay first published in *Politics and Apocalypse* and subsequently collected in *The Cambridge Companion to Leo Strauss*; sources consequently assign it both 2004 and 2007 dates. ([Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-companion-to-leo-strauss/straussian-moment/2EE9859BF0B01A526020F458FB5D3664); [Hoover Institution](https://www.hoover.org/research/peter-thiel-straussian-moment))

- **2014:** *Zero to One: Notes on Startups, or How to Build the Future*. A book co-authored with Blake Masters and derived from Thiel’s 2012 Stanford lectures and Masters’s notes; it is therefore simultaneously Thiel’s argument, Masters’s textual reconstruction, and a transformed lecture course rather than an uncomplicated single-author book. ([Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/234730/zero-to-one-by-peter-thiel-with-blake-masters/); [Stanford Engineering](https://engineering.stanford.edu/magazine/article/peter-thiel-stanfords-startup-culture))

### Essays, manifestos, and public interventions

- **2007:** *Spending the Future*. An essay in *First Things* presenting technological stagnation as a civilizational and intergenerational problem. ([First Things](https://www.firstthings.com/article/2007/11/spending-the-future))

- **2008:** *The Optimistic Thought Experiment*. A substantial essay in *Policy Review* on definite and indefinite conceptions of the future. ([Hoover Institution](https://www.hoover.org/research/optimistic-thought-experiment))

- **2009:** *The Education of a Libertarian*. An autobiographical political manifesto published by *Cato Unbound*: it does not merely describe libertarian politics but announces Thiel’s turn toward technological means of escaping political constraint. ([Cato Unbound](https://www.cato-unbound.org/2009/04/13/peter-thiel/education-libertarian/))

- **2011:** *Swift as a Blind Horseman?* A review-essay on René Girard’s *Battling to the End*, published in *National Review*; it is criticism but also a compact statement of Thiel’s own apocalyptic political theology. ([National Review](https://www.nationalreview.com/2011/09/swift-blind-horseman-peter-thiel/))

- **2011:** *The End of the Future*. An essay arguing that technological progress outside computing had stalled; it became one of the principal texts of Thiel’s “stagnation” thesis. ([National Review](https://www.nationalreview.com/2011/10/end-future-peter-thiel/))

- **2011:** *What Happened to the Future?* A Founders Fund manifesto presenting the firm’s investment program as a response to technological stagnation. It resists assignment as Thiel’s individual work: the institutional publication has no individual byline, although bibliographies routinely associate its argument with him. ([Founders Fund](https://foundersfund.com/the-future/))

- **2012:** *CS183: Startup*. A Stanford lecture course—an oral and pedagogical project, not originally a book. The surviving “Notes Essays” were made from the lectures by student Blake Masters, so their words cannot simply be attributed to Thiel even though the conceptual sequence is his and later generated *Zero to One*. ([Stanford Engineering](https://engineering.stanford.edu/magazine/article/peter-thiel-stanfords-startup-culture); [Blake Masters’s course archive](https://blakemasters.com/peter-thiels-cs183-startup))

- **2014:** *Competition Is for Losers*. A newspaper manifesto adapted from the startup lectures and the argument of *Zero to One*, rather than a wholly independent intellectual project. ([Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/articles/peter-thiel-competition-is-for-losers-1410535536))

- **2014:** *You Should Run Your Startup Like a Cult. Here’s How*. An adapted extract from *Zero to One* published as a *Wired* essay; it is a distinct periodical manifestation of the book, not a separately conceived work. ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/2014/09/run-startup-like-cult/))

- **2014:** *Thinking Too Highly of Higher Ed*. An op-ed criticizing the economics and social status of American higher education. ([Tampa Bay Times](https://www.tampabay.com/opinion/columns/thinking-too-highly-of-higher-ed/2206895/))

- **2015:** *Developing the Developed World*. An essay restating the technological-stagnation project for the Independent Institute’s *The Independent Review*. ([Independent Institute](https://www.independent.org/publications/tir/article.asp?id=1031))

- **2015:** *Against Edenism*. A theological essay rejecting the ideal of returning to an unspoiled natural past and joining Christian eschatology to technological creation. ([First Things](https://www.firstthings.com/article/2015/06/against-edenism))

- **2015:** *The New Atomic Age We Need*. A newspaper intervention advocating renewed nuclear innovation; as an op-ed it acts as technological policy advocacy rather than detached description. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/11/28/opinion/the-new-atomic-age-we-need.html))

- **2016:** *The Online Privacy Debate Won’t End With Gawker*. An op-ed defending Thiel’s financing of litigation against Gawker. It is also an intervention in a controversy in which the author was an actor, making the text part argument and part justification of an extratextual project. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2016/08/16/opinion/peter-thiel-the-online-privacy-debate-wont-end-with-gawker.html))

- **2019:** *Good for Google, Bad for America*. An op-ed alleging that Google’s work and relations with China posed national-security questions; it functions as a demand for investigation as well as an argument. ([New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/08/01/opinion/peter-thiel-google.html))

- **2020:** *Back to the Future*. A review-essay responding to Ross Douthat’s *The Decadent Society* and extending Thiel’s account of stagnation, technology, and Christian futurity. ([First Things](https://www.firstthings.com/article/2020/03/back-to-the-future))

- **2025:** *A Time for Truth and Reconciliation*. A political op-ed interpreting the second Trump administration as an opportunity to expose institutional wrongdoing; its programmatic demand gives it the character of a manifesto. ([Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/a46cb128-1f74-4621-ab0b-242a76583105))

### Publishing and institutional platforms

- **1987–present:** *The Stanford Review*. A conservative student newspaper co-founded by Thiel, who served as its first editor-in-chief. The continuing periodical is a collective institution rather than his personal text, and its later contents should not be treated as works by him. ([Stanford Politics](https://stanfordpolitics.org/2017/11/27/peter-thiel-cover-story/); [Stanford Review](https://stanfordreview.org/about/))

- **1996–present:** *Thiel Capital*. An investment-management organization founded by Thiel. Its made object is neither a single fund nor a text but a durable apparatus for directing capital and supporting later ventures. ([Thiel Capital](https://www.thielcapital.com/))

- **2005–present:** *The Thiel Foundation*. A philanthropic institution created and led by Thiel to support science, technology, and long-term thinking. It is best understood as an umbrella project whose manifestations include the Thiel Fellowship, Imitatio, and Breakout Labs. ([Thiel Foundation](https://www.thielfoundation.org/); [Stanford Graduate School of Business case study](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/thiel-foundation-contrarian-philanthropy))

- **Date not fixed in the consulted sources:** *Imitatio*. A Thiel Foundation project arising from conversations among Thiel, René Girard, and Robert Hamerton-Kelly and intended to extend research on mimetic theory. Its own institutional history describes a collaborative emergence rather than assigning a single founding date or sole authorship. ([Imitatio](https://www.imitatio.org/about-imitatio); [Thiel Foundation](https://www.thielfoundation.org/))

- **2011–present:** *The Thiel Fellowship*. A grant-and-mentorship protocol created by Thiel for young people pursuing projects outside or in place of college. It is a continuing social experiment rather than a text, and fellows—not Thiel—make the ventures it enables. ([Thiel Fellowship](https://thielfellowship.org/); [Stanford Graduate School of Business](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/case-studies/thiel-foundation-contrarian-philanthropy))

- **2011–c. 2017:** *Breakout Labs*. A Thiel Foundation program created to fund early-stage scientific work outside conventional institutional channels. It was a funding protocol and portfolio rather than Thiel’s own scientific research; it later became independent as Breakout Ventures. ([Stanford Humanities Center](https://shc.stanford.edu/stanford-humanities-center/news/peter-thiel-launches-early-stage-research-effort-zaps-cleantech-and); [Breakout Ventures](https://breakout.vc/about))

### Companies and financial architectures

- **1998–2000:** *Confinity*. A financial-technology company co-founded by Thiel, Max Levchin, and others, initially concerned with handheld-device security and payments. It merged with Elon Musk’s X.com in 2000, so treating the subsequent PayPal as the seamless product of one founder obscures the company’s contested, collective formation. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/PayPal); [Computer History Museum](https://computerhistory.org/profile/peter-thiel/))

- **1999/2000–2002 as an independent company:** *PayPal*. The online-payments system and company emerged from Confinity’s product and its merger with X.com; Thiel led the merged company but was one among several founders and formative executives. Sources differ over whether to date “PayPal” from Confinity’s 1998 founding, the product’s 1999 launch, or the merged company’s 2000 renaming, so no single origin date is forced here. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/money/PayPal); [PayPal corporate history](https://about.pypl.com/who-we-are/history-and-facts/default.aspx))

- **2002–present:** *Clarium Capital Management*. A global-macro investment firm founded by Thiel after PayPal’s sale. Its positions and returns are manifestations of an investment thesis, but individual trades resist classification as authored works. ([New Zealand Department of Internal Affairs release reproducing Thiel’s statement](https://img.scoop.co.nz/media/pdfs/1702/PeterThielrelease1February2017.pdf); [World Economic Forum](https://www.weforum.org/people/peter-a-thiel/))

- **2003–present:** *Palantir Technologies*. A data-analysis software company co-founded by Thiel with Alex Karp, Stephen Cohen, Joe Lonsdale, and Nathan Gettings. It is a collective technological and governmental-commercial project; its platforms and deployments cannot accurately be catalogued as objects personally made by Thiel. ([Palantir SEC registration statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1321655/000119312520230013/d904406ds1.htm); [Founders Fund biography](https://foundersfund.com/team/peter-thiel/))

- **2005–present:** *Founders Fund*. A venture-capital firm co-founded by Thiel and partners including Ken Howery and Luke Nosek. It is simultaneously an investment organization and a programmatic project for converting Thiel’s technological theses into company formation, but its portfolio companies remain their founders’ works, not his. ([Founders Fund](https://foundersfund.com/team/peter-thiel/); [Thiel’s 2017 statement](https://img.scoop.co.nz/media/pdfs/1702/PeterThielrelease1February2017.pdf))

- **2010–present:** *Valar Ventures*. A venture-capital firm co-founded by Thiel, Andrew McCormack, and James Fitzgerald to invest substantially beyond Silicon Valley and the United States. It is a collaborative financial architecture, not a body of companies authored by Thiel. ([Valar Ventures](https://www.valar.com/); [Thiel’s 2017 statement](https://img.scoop.co.nz/media/pdfs/1702/PeterThielrelease1February2017.pdf))

- **2012–present:** *Mithril Capital*. A long-term venture-capital firm founded by Ajay Royan with Thiel. The firm’s own account makes the co-authorship explicit; portfolio businesses should not be collapsed into the founders’ personal obra. ([Mithril Capital](https://www.mithril.com/))
