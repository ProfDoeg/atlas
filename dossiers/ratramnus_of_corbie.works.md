## Works by Ratramnus of Corbie

### Doctrinal treatises and theological interventions

- **843–844:** *De corpore et sanguine Domini* (also catalogued as *De corpore et sanguine Christi*). A theological opinion commissioned by Charles the Bald on the Eucharist and framed as a response to Paschasius Radbertus; medieval and early-modern transmission sometimes misattributed it to John Scotus Eriugena or named its author “Bertramus.” ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4092))

- **849:** *De praedestinatione Dei*. A two-book doctrinal treatise addressed to Charles the Bald after Gottschalk of Orbais’s condemnation at Quierzy, defending double predestination while denying that God predestines sin. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4096))

- **c. 853:** *De anima*. A short theological-philosophical treatise composed at Charles the Bald’s request, arguing that the soul is incorporeal and not spatially bounded. It survives in two known manuscripts. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4091))

- **c. 853:** *De eo quod Christus ex virgine natus est*, conventionally called *De nativitate Christi*. A treatise on Christ’s birth and Mary’s continuing virginity. Its status as a wholly independent obra by Ratramnus is uncertain: Jean-Paul Bouhot argues that Ratramnus redacted a letter originally written by Abbot Odo of Corbie to Warin of Corvey, while an older chronology placed it around 835. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4095))

- **863:** *Liber de anima ad Odonem Bellovacensem*. A treatise written at Bishop Odo of Beauvais’s request against the idea of a single universal soul shared by human beings; it survives in only one known manuscript. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4098))

- **867:** *Contra Graecorum opposita Romanam ecclesiam infamantium*, in four books. A polemical ecclesiological treatise occasioned by Pope Nicholas I: its first three books defend the procession of the Holy Spirit from the Father and the Son, while the fourth answers Greek objections to Latin doctrine, customs, and Roman primacy. Some older scholarship dates it to 868. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4090); [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/ratramno-di-corbie/))

### Letters, responsa, and practical judgments

- **852:** *De infantibus incaute oppressis*. A short pastoral-juridical responsum on responsibility and penance for the accidental smothering of children, referring to the Council of Mainz of 852. It survives in a single manuscript and was first identified and edited in modern scholarship in 1982. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4094))

- **c. 860; before 865:** *De cynocephalis* (also *Epistola de cynocephalis*). A letter-treatise to the missionary Rimbert deciding that the dog-headed peoples reported in ethnographic tradition should be regarded as human and therefore capable of conversion. It is both correspondence and an anthropological-theological judgment, preserved in a single manuscript. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4093))

- **868–c. 870:** *De propinquorum coniugiis*. A letter-responsum to Archbishop Rimbert and Abbot Adalgar concerning marriage between relatives, probably prompted by the Council of Worms of 868. It resists treatment as a complete work because only two leaves, fragments of the same manuscript, survive. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4097))

### Lost works and creations known only indirectly

- **Date unknown:** *[Letter known from Hincmar’s dossier, as summarized by Flodoard]*. The letter itself is lost, and the surviving notice does not securely preserve a fixed title; it therefore remains evidence of an act of correspondence rather than a recoverable textual obra. ([Pierre Courcelle’s review of Bouhot](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1977_num_135_2_461386_t1_0362_0000_000))

- **c. 860–861:** *[Compilation defending the expression trina deitas]*. A substantial, now-lost compilation addressed to Bishop Hildegar of Meaux and assembled from Hilary of Poitiers and Augustine in support of calling the deity “threefold and one.” It has sometimes been called *De trina deitate*, but no authorial title survives; its existence and Ratramnus’s authorship are known through the hostile testimony of Hincmar of Reims. ([Hincmar, *De una et non trina deitate*](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/De_una_et_non_trina_deitate_%28Hincmarus_Rhemensis%29); [Courcelle’s review of Bouhot](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1977_num_135_2_461386_t1_0362_0000_000))

### Disputed attribution

- **862:** *[Anonymous episcopal opinion for the Council of Aachen, text D]*. A canonical opinion concerning Lothar II’s marriage case; the transmitted text is anonymous, and Karl Ubl has proposed Ratramnus as its author. It should not be treated as securely his obra, but the attribution is substantial enough to record as disputed. ([Geschichtsquellen, Council of Aachen 862](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/1356); [Ratramnus author record](https://geschichtsquellen.de/autor/4532))
