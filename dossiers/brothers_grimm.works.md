## Works by Jacob Ludwig Carl Grimm and Wilhelm Carl Grimm

### Joint collections and editorial projects

- **1812–1857:** *Kinder- und Hausmärchen*. An evolving collection and editorial project rather than a set of stories authored outright: oral reports, manuscripts, and printed sources were repeatedly selected and rewritten, especially by Wilhelm, across seven large editions; the 1812–1815 first edition and the extensively revised 1857 text therefore constitute different states of the obra. The surviving annotated working copies document this continuing manufacture. [UNESCO nomination and scholarly bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf) [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1812:** *Die beiden ältesten deutschen Gedichte aus dem achten Jahrhundert: Das Lied von Hildebrand und Hadubrand und das Weißenbrunner Gebet*. A joint diplomatic and metrical edition of two inherited texts—not their composition—and thus an editorial-scholarly object. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1812:** *Herausgabe des alten Reinhart Fuchs*. A jointly prepared journal edition of a medieval text; its made element is the transcription, arrangement, and commentary rather than the underlying poem. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1813–1816:** *Altdeutsche Wälder*. A three-volume joint scholarly periodical or miscellany assembled by the brothers, mixing editions, essays, documentary material, and notes; it resists classification as a single authored book. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1815:** *Lieder der alten Edda*. A joint edition and explanation of Old Norse poems, announced as a larger undertaking but ending with volume 1; it is therefore an unfinished editorial project as well as a published obra. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1815:** *Der arme Heinrich von Hartmann von der Aue*. A joint critical edition made from the Strasbourg and Vatican manuscripts; Hartmann’s poem is not the brothers’ creation, but the constituted text and apparatus are. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1815:** *Irmenstraße und Irmensäule*. A joint antiquarian investigation, sometimes assigned inconsistently in later bibliographies; contemporary work-lists treat it as a shared publication. [Complete list of Jacob Grimm’s separately published works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1816–1818:** *Deutsche Sagen*. A two-volume documentary collection of legends assembled and edited from oral, manuscript, and printed witnesses; like the fairy tales, it is a jointly manufactured corpus rather than straightforward original fiction. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm) [UNESCO bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf)

- **1818:** *Der Sünder unter den Gerechten*. A short joint translation from an old manuscript, published in a periodical. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1818:** *Das Märchen vom Schneider, der in den Himmel kam*. A separately published tale manifestation credited jointly to the brothers; it belongs to their collecting and textual-redaction practice rather than being securely classifiable as wholly invented fiction. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1826:** *Irische Elfenmärchen*. A joint German translation and adaptation of Thomas Crofton Croker’s Irish fairy-legend collection, supplemented by Wilhelm’s substantial essay on elves; it is simultaneously translation, commentary, and folklore project. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

- **1846:** *Das von den Juden getödtete Mägdlein*. A jointly attributed legend printed in the *Badisches Sagen-Buch*; its survival as an individual “work” is inseparable from the brothers’ problematic transmission of an antisemitic narrative tradition. [Wikisource record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

### Joint lexicographic project

- **1838–1961:** *Deutsches Wörterbuch*. A monumental historical dictionary conceived and begun by the brothers after 1838, with the first fascicle issued in 1852 and volume 1 in 1854. Wilhelm reached approximately the letter D and Jacob worked into F; at their deaths the project was radically incomplete, and later generations finished its 32 volumes, so the completed dictionary cannot be attributed to them without qualification. [Digital dictionary and project record](https://woerterbuchnetz.de/?sigle=DWB) [Wikisource publication history](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)

### Jacob Grimm: linguistic and philological obra

- **1808:** *Gedanken: wie sich die Sagen zur Poesie und Geschichte verhalten*. An early programmatic essay on legend, poetry, and history. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Sagen von Glocken*. A short antiquarian and folkloric article. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1811:** *Über den altdeutschen Meistergesang*. Jacob’s first separately published monograph, a historical-philological study of medieval German master-song. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1813:** *Gedanken über Mythos, Epos und Geschichte*. A theoretical essay joining mythology, epic, and historiography. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1815:** *Irmenstraße und Irmensäule*. An antiquarian study also catalogued as a joint work; the attribution boundary between the brothers is not consistently represented and should not be silently resolved. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1815:** *Silva de romances viejos*. An edited collection of Old Spanish romances: an act of selection and textual presentation, not original composition of the poems. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1818:** *Eine dänische Sammlung von Volkssagen*. A review-essay on a Danish collection of folk legends. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1818:** *Volkslied (“Ich lieb, ich lieb und darfs nicht sagen”)*. A published folk-song text associated with Jacob’s collecting activity; its status resists “authored work,” since the made object is his textual transmission rather than the anonymous song itself. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1819–1837:** *Deutsche Grammatik*. A four-part comparative-historical grammar, radically reconstructed after its first 1819 volume and expanded through 1837; it is a developing research obra rather than a single stable text. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1824:** *Wuk Stephanowitsch’ Kleine serbische Grammatik, verdeutscht mit einer Vorrede*. A German translation of Vuk Karadžić’s Serbian grammar with Jacob’s preface; the translation and framing, not the underlying grammar, are Jacob’s manufacture. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1826:** *Zur Recension der deutschen Grammatik*. A polemical scholarly response defending and clarifying the *Deutsche Grammatik*. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1828; revised 1854:** *Deutsche Rechtsalterthümer*. A documentary reconstruction of early Germanic law and custom; the substantially enlarged second edition represents a renewed state of the project. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1830:** *Hymnorum veteris ecclesiae XXVI interpretatio theodisca*. An edition and philological interpretation of twenty-six early German church-hymn translations. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1831:** *Selbstbiographie*. An autobiographical account prepared for a Hessian history of scholars and writers; it is both self-description and deliberate construction of a scholarly life. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm) [UNESCO bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf)

- **1834:** *Reinhart Fuchs*. A large critical edition and historical study of the medieval beast-epic tradition. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1835; enlarged 1844 and 1854:** *Deutsche Mythologie*. A comparative reconstruction of Germanic mythology, folklore, and custom; its successive expansions make it a continuing scholarly project rather than one fixed text. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1835:** *Taciti Germania*. A philological edition of Tacitus’s *Germania*; its editorial text and apparatus are Jacob’s work, while the ancient treatise is not. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1838:** *Lateinische Gedichte des X. und XI. Jahrhunderts*. A co-edited collection of tenth- and eleventh-century Latin poems made with Johann Andreas Schmeller. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1840:** *Sendschreiben an Karl Lachmann über Reinhart Fuchs*. A public scholarly letter extending and defending Jacob’s work on the beast epic. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1840:** *Andreas und Elene*. An edition of two Old English poems; the medieval poems are inherited texts, while Jacob made their scholarly presentation. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1842:** *Frau Aventiure klopft an Beneckes Tür*. A playful occasional poem or verse-address, anomalous within Jacob’s predominantly scholarly corpus. [Jacob Grimm collected-writings contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/Kleinere_Schriften.html?id=7G4HAAAAQAAJ)

- **1848; revised 1853:** *Geschichte der deutschen Sprache*. A two-volume historical and comparative account of German, combining linguistic history with ethnological reconstruction. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1850:** *Das Wort des Besitzes*. A linguistic essay on possessive expression, separately issued after presentation to the Berlin Academy. [Complete separately published works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1856:** *Über den Personenwechsel in der Rede*. An academy treatise on shifts of grammatical person in discourse. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1858:** *Über den Ursprung der Sprache*. A speculative-historical academy discourse on the origin of language. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1859:** *Rede auf Schiller*. A commemorative academy speech, repeatedly printed as a separate text. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

### Jacob Grimm: legal, political, and documentary projects

- **1838:** *Jacob Grimm über seine Entlassung*. A public justification written after the dismissal of the Göttingen Seven; it functions as a manifesto of constitutional and academic conscience, not merely as memoir. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1840–1863:** *Weisthümer*, parts 1–4. Jacob’s documentary edition of customary-law records; he supervised four parts, the fourth appearing in the year of his death. Parts 5–7 (1866–1878) continued the project posthumously under other hands and are not wholly his made work. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1848:** Parliamentary speeches and motions in the Frankfurt National Assembly. These are performative political interventions—closer to manifesto and legislative act than to autonomous literary works—and survive dispersed in proceedings rather than as one fixed titled obra. [Jacob Grimm biography and political record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **1863:** *Rede auf Wilhelm Grimm und Rede über das Alter*. Two memorial and reflective speeches issued together: one constructs Wilhelm’s scholarly remembrance, the other considers old age. [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

### Wilhelm Grimm: translations and early studies

- **1808:** *Über die Entstehung der altdeutschen Poesie und ihr Verhältnis zu der nordischen*. Wilhelm’s first substantial scholarly essay, comparing German and Nordic poetic traditions. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Des Löwen und König Dieterichs Kampf mit dem Lindwurm*. A translated heroic narrative; the source story is inherited, while Wilhelm made its German textual manifestation. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Romanze*. A translation published in the *Zeitung für Einsiedler*. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Die Meerfrau*. A short translated ballad or folk-poetic text. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Das Lied von der Frau Grimhild*. A German translation of a Scandinavian heroic song. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Mimmering Tand*. A translated Scandinavian song. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1808:** *Des Riesen Langbein und Wittich Wielands Sohn Kampf*. A translated heroic narrative from the Nordic tradition. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1810:** *Christi Wiedererscheinen in der Natur*. A translation from Adam Oehlenschläger’s Danish. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1811:** *Altdänische Heldenlieder, Balladen und Märchen*. A collection of Wilhelm’s German translations from Old Danish narrative poetry and prose; it is a translation anthology rather than an authored storybook. [Wilhelm Grimm selected-works record](https://authorscalendar.info/wgrimm.htm)

- **1813:** *Über Otacher im Hildebrandslied*. A brief philological note published within *Altdeutsche Wälder*. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1813; supplemented 1816:** *Zeugnisse über die deutsche Heldensage*. A documentary assemblage of evidence for German heroic legend, followed by *Nachträge*; its method is accumulation and comparison rather than continuous narrative. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1818:** *Märchen von einem, der auszog, das Fürchten zu lernen*. A separately printed manifestation of a tale also belonging to the developing *Kinder- und Hausmärchen* corpus; it should not be counted as wholly invented fiction. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1820:** *Die altnordische Literatur in der gegenwärtigen Periode*. A survey essay on contemporary work in Old Norse literature. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1821:** *Über deutsche Runen*. A monograph on German runic inscriptions and their interpretation. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1829:** *Die deutsche Heldensage*. A synthetic study and documentary map of German heroic legend; Wilhelm continued correcting and enlarging it, but its second edition appeared only in 1867, after his death. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

### Wilhelm Grimm: editions of medieval texts

- **1830:** *Grave Ruodolf*. A scholarly edition of the fragmentary Middle High German poem; incompleteness belongs to the surviving medieval object, not to a failure by Wilhelm to finish his edition. [Contemporary bibliography of Wilhelm’s editions](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/21965570.pdf)

- **1836:** *Der Rosengarten*. An edition of the medieval heroic poem, making a comparative scholarly text from manuscript witnesses. [Contemporary bibliography of Wilhelm’s editions](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/21965570.pdf)

- **1838:** *Ruolandes Liet*. A critical edition of the Middle High German *Rolandslied*. [Contemporary bibliography of Wilhelm’s editions](https://www.e-rara.ch/download/pdf/21965570.pdf)

- **1840:** *Konrads von Würzburg Goldene Schmiede*. A critical edition of Konrad’s Marian poem with an extended study of its imagery. [Handschriftencensus work record](https://www.handschriftencensus.de/werke/207)

- **1844–1846:** *Athis und Prophilias*. An edition and extensive academy study of the medieval romance and its variant forms. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1846–1848:** *Exhortatio ad plebem christianam; Glossae Cassellanae*. A philological edition and interpretation of early German glosses and a clerical exhortation, later supplemented by a 1854 addendum. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1849–1852:** *Über Freidank* and its *Nachträge*. A major study preparatory to Wilhelm’s edition of the medieval gnomic poet, including the ultimately rejected hypothesis that Freidank and Walther von der Vogelweide were identical. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm) [Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Grimm,_Wilhelm)

- **1851:** *Altdeutsche Gespräche*. An edition of early German dialogue texts, supplemented in 1852; the resulting object is both source publication and linguistic study. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1852:** *Zur Geschichte des Reims*. A foundational, unusually extensive historical study of rhyme, arising partly from Wilhelm’s work on Freidank. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm) [Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Grimm,_Wilhelm)

- **1857:** *Die Sage von Polyphem*. A comparative mythographic study tracing the Polyphemus story through multiple traditions. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **Prepared before 1859; published 1860:** *Freidank*. Wilhelm’s critical edition was a lifetime project but appeared posthumously; its date is therefore the date of manifestation, not of completed authorial labor. [Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Grimm,_Wilhelm)

### Wilhelm Grimm: shorter folklore and philological studies

- **1841–1842:** *Freidanks Grabmal*; *Zu Wernher vom Niederrhein*; *Witege mit dem Slangen*; *Zu Silvester*; *Zur Goldenen Schmiede*; *Zu Walther von der Vogelweide*. Six individual philological notes and studies published in the *Zeitschrift für deutsches Alterthum*; they are discrete texts, though later collected bibliographies often subsume them into Wilhelm’s smaller writings. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1842–1844:** *Die Sage vom Ursprung der Christusbilder*. An academy treatise comparing legends concerning the origins of images of Christ. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1853:** *Zwei Thiermärchen*. A brief publication of two animal tales; its “work” is collection and textual presentation rather than secure original authorship. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1853:** *Albanesische Märchen*. A small presentation of Albanian tales, part of Wilhelm’s comparative folklore practice. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1853:** *Der Swinegel*. A dialect tale-text whose status lies between collected folklore, linguistic specimen, and edited literary manifestation. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1853:** *Erklärung*. A one-page scholarly declaration or correction in the *Zeitschrift für deutsches Alterthum*; it barely resists treatment as an autonomous work, but the chronological bibliography records it as a separate contribution. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1854:** *Nachtrag zu den Casseler Glossen*. A supplement to Wilhelm’s earlier edition and interpretation of the Kassel glosses. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1855:** *Die Himmelsstürmer*. A comparative mythological and folkloric essay. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1856:** *Thierfabeln bei den Meistersängern*. A study of animal fables in the German Meistersinger tradition. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1856:** *Über die runische Inschrift eines in der Walachei ausgegrabenen … goldenen Ringes*. A brief epigraphic report on the runic inscription of the Pietroasele gold ring. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **1859:** *Zum Freidank*; *Spanische Märchen*; *Nochmals über Freidank*; *Holzschnitt zu einer Fabel*. Four late notes comprising corrections, comparative tale material, renewed argument about Freidank, and interpretation of a woodcut. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

- **Written before 1859; published 1865:** *Die Sage von Athis und Prophilias*; *Die mythische Bedeutung des Wolfes*; *Über eine Thierfabel des Babrius*. Three posthumously printed studies from Wilhelm’s papers; the 1865 date records publication, while their precise dates of composition are not fixed in the consulted chronology. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm)

### Autobiographies, speeches, correspondence, and working archive

- **1830:** *Selbstbiographie* (Wilhelm Grimm). Wilhelm’s autobiographical account, written in September 1830 and later published; like Jacob’s July account, it is a constructed self-record rather than a neutral dossier. [UNESCO bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf)

- **Across their adult lives:** Letters, notebooks, diaries, tale transcripts, excerpt books, and annotated working copies. These constitute an archival body of work without a single stable title or date; much of it resists being called an individual obra because it records collaboration, revision, correspondence, and unfinished research processes. [Digitales Grimmarchiv](https://grimmarchiv.de/) [UNESCO archive bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf)

- **Posthumously collected 1864–1885:** *Kleinere Schriften* (Jacob Grimm), eight volumes in the second collected edition. This is an editorial corpus containing speeches, essays, reviews, mythological studies, grammatical research, and occasional texts—not a new work made by Jacob after death; it supplies the standard chronological control for his dispersed writings. [UNESCO bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf) [Jacob Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Jacob_Grimm)

- **Posthumously collected 1881–1887:** *Kleinere Schriften* (Wilhelm Grimm), four volumes. This collected-works edition preserves his dispersed articles, reviews, speeches, translations, and biographical writings; it is the basis of the chronological bibliography on pages 637–659 of volume 4, but its arrangement is the later editors’ creation. [Wilhelm Grimm works record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Grimm) [UNESCO bibliography](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_grimm_rev.pdf)

- **Published posthumously from 1868 onward:** The brothers’ correspondence. The letters are things they made, but the titled printed “Briefwechsel” volumes are later editorial manifestations assembled by others; the authoritative project is consequently a bibliography and archive rather than one authorially constituted book. [Digitales Grimmarchiv](https://grimmarchiv.de/) [Wikisource correspondence record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Br%C3%BCder_Grimm)
