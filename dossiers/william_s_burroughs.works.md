## Works by William Seward Burroughs II

### Long fiction and book-length prose

- **1945; published 2008:** *And the Hippos Were Boiled in Their Tanks*. A collaborative crime novel written in alternating chapters with Jack Kerouac; its publication date is posthumous and should not be mistaken for its date of composition. ([Schottlaender bibliography](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8mq028p5); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1951–1953; published 1985:** *Queer*. A novel drafted in Mexico but withheld for more than three decades; the 1985 book is therefore a late manifestation of an early work. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1953:** *Junkie* (later *Junky*). A first-person novel published as by “William Lee”; later editions restored material and altered the title, so the successive texts are not entirely interchangeable. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1959:** *The Naked Lunch* (later usually *Naked Lunch*). A discontinuous novel assembled from the larger “Word Hoard”; its unstable ordering and later authorial corrections make it less a single fixed text than a succession of authorized manifestations. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography); [University of Delaware exhibition](https://exhibitions.lib.udel.edu/beat-visions-and-the-counterculture/home/burroughs/))
- **1961; revised 1966:** *The Soft Machine*. A cut-up novel and first volume of the Nova trilogy; the substantially rewritten second edition constitutes a distinct authorial state, not merely a reprint. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1962; revised 1967:** *The Ticket That Exploded*. A cut-up novel and second Nova volume, again radically revised after first publication. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1963:** *Dead Fingers Talk*. A new narrative made by rearranging passages from *Naked Lunch*, *The Soft Machine*, and *The Ticket That Exploded*; although often called a compilation, its recomposition gives it the status of a separate obra. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1964:** *Nova Express*. A cut-up novel and concluding volume of the Nova trilogy. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1969–1970:** *The Last Words of Dutch Schultz*. A “film novel” or image-script organized around the gangster’s recorded deathbed speech; it is neither a conventional novel nor a produced film. Bibliographies differ between the 1969 appearance and the 1970 book manifestation. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1971:** *The Wild Boys: A Book of the Dead*. A novel made from episodic prose, fantasy, and cinematic montage. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1973:** *Port of Saints*. A short novel whose later editions contain revisions and additional material. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1979:** *Blade Runner (A Movie)*. A novella presented as a screenplay or film treatment, adapted from Alan E. Nourse’s novel *The Bladerunner*; it was never produced as the proposed film and is unrelated to the screenplay of Ridley Scott’s similarly titled film. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1981:** *Cities of the Red Night*. A novel and the first volume of the Red Night trilogy. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1983:** *The Place of Dead Roads*. A novel and the second Red Night volume. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1987:** *The Western Lands*. A novel and the final Red Night volume. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1995:** *My Education: A Book of Dreams*. A book-length assemblage of recorded dreams; its organizing principle is the dream archive rather than continuous fictional narrative. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))

### Cut-ups, manifestos, protocols, pamphlets, and short prose objects

- **1956:** *Letter from a Master Addict to Dangerous Drugs*. A medical-journal article combining autobiographical testimony and claims about addiction; it is an essay rather than fiction, and some of its pharmacological assertions are now disputed. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1964:** *Roosevelt After Inauguration*. A satirical routine first issued as a pamphlet and later collected with other “atrocities.” ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1965:** *Valentine’s Day Reading*. A separately issued prose reading or routine, closer to performance literature than to a conventional book. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1965:** *Time*. An artist’s magazine and parody-manifestation made from cut-up text, collage, and altered news imagery; its physical design is part of the obra. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [University of Delaware exhibition](https://exhibitions.lib.udel.edu/beat-visions-and-the-counterculture/home/burroughs/))
- **1965:** *APO-33: A Metabolic Regulator*. A pamphlet-sized cut-up and collage object; it resists separation into literary text and visual artwork. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1969:** *The Dead Star*. A newspaper-format cut-up combining columns of prose and reproduced news images; its typography and grid are constitutive, making it an artist’s publication as much as a text. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [University of Delaware exhibition](https://exhibitions.lib.udel.edu/beat-visions-and-the-counterculture/home/burroughs/))
- **1971:** *Ali’s Smile*. A short prose work issued separately and subsequently recombined with the Scientology texts. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1971:** *The Electronic Revolution*. A manifesto and proposed protocol for using tape recorders, playback, and cut-up speech as instruments of disruption; it acts by instructing rather than merely describing. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **circa 1970; complete publication 2018:** *The Revised Boy Scout Manual: An Electronic Revolution*. A long parody-manual and tactical protocol related to *The Electronic Revolution*; fragments circulated earlier, while the complete manuscript appeared only posthumously. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1973:** *White Subway*. A separately issued sequence of routines later absorbed into *The Burroughs File*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1973:** *Mayfair Academy Series More or Less*. A small-format prose publication whose title identifies a deliberately unstable series or anti-series. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1974:** *The Book of Breething*. An illustrated artist’s book combining Burroughs’s texts with Bob Gale’s drawings; “Breething” is the work’s intentional spelling. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1975:** *Snack…*. A short prose pamphlet or chapbook, later incorporated into collections. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1976:** *Cobble Stone Gardens*. A chapbook of short prose, later included in *The Burroughs File*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1976:** *The Retreat Diaries*. A diary of Burroughs’s Buddhist retreat, first issued separately and later collected; it records a practice while also forming part of that practice. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1979:** *Doctor Benway*. A separately published routine centered on the recurring Benway figure, extracted from the larger textual continuum around *Naked Lunch*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1979:** *Wouldn’t You Polish Pine Floors with a South African Book Review?*. A broadside: a single-sheet textual and graphic object rather than a book. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1981:** *The Streets of Chance*. A separately issued short prose work or broadside later gathered into larger collections. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1981:** *Early Routines*. A retrospective gathering of early performance-like prose pieces; it is an authorial body of routines rather than a continuous new narrative. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1982:** *Sinki’s Sauna*. A short illustrated narrative issued as a small independent publication. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1982:** *Mummies*. A short separately published text-object. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1984:** *Ruski*. A short prose publication, subsequently collected. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1984:** *The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse*. A short illustrated book, not the later portfolio *Seven Deadly Sins*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1986:** *The Cat Inside*. A memoir, animal book, and prose meditation originally made as a limited illustrated publication; later trade editions are new manifestations of the same obra. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **circa 1987–1989:** *Clause 27 Is Proposition 6 Is the Whole Tamale*. A political broadside opposing anti-gay legislation; its uncertain date is preserved rather than silently regularized. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1989:** *Tornado Alley*. A short illustrated prose work whose first form was a limited artist’s book. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1991:** *Ghost of Chance*. A novella about Madagascar, extinction, and lemurs; it also functions as an ecological warning. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1991:** *X-Ray Man*. A separately issued text-and-image publication. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1992:** *Paper Cloud; Thick Pages*. An artist’s book whose material construction is part of the work and cannot be reduced to its verbal content. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1995:** *Pantopon Rose*. A separately issued early routine associated with the *Naked Lunch* textual complex. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1996:** *Concrete and Buckshot*. A text-and-image artist’s publication connected to Burroughs’s gun-made visual work. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1998:** *A Spiritual Exercise*. A posthumously issued broadside or chapbook manifestation of a short Burroughs text. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **2001:** *Words of Advice for Young People*. A posthumous artist’s-book manifestation of a previously performed and recorded text; the publication, unlike the underlying composition, was not made by Burroughs alone. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **circa 2004 publication:** *The Heroin Drug Cure*. A posthumously issued short text; the bibliography itself marks the publication date as uncertain. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))

### Collaborative books and Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1960:** *Minutes to Go*. A cut-up anthology and manifesto made with Brion Gysin, Sinclair Beiles, and Gregory Corso; it documents and performs the cut-up method at once. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1960:** *The Exterminator*. A text-and-collage collaboration with Brion Gysin, distinct from Burroughs’s 1973 collection *Exterminator!*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1953–1963:** *The Yage Letters*. A constructed correspondence with Allen Ginsberg, based on letters but edited into a literary sequence; later expanded as *The Yage Letters Redux*, so neither title represents an entirely neutral transcript. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1967:** *So Who Owns Death TV?*. A multilingual collaborative pamphlet with Claude Pélieu and Carl Weissner, combining montage, translation, and political provocation. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **circa 1970; published 2010:** *Rules of Duel*. A project credited to Burroughs and Graham Masterton, although the bibliography describes it as primarily Masterton’s work; Burroughs’s degree of authorship is therefore qualified rather than assumed. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1973:** *Brion Gysin Let the Mice In*. A collaborative book with Gysin and Ian Sommerville containing texts, images, and materials connected with their multimedia experiments. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1975:** *Sidetripping*. A photographic-text collaboration with Charles Gatewood; Gatewood’s photographs and Burroughs’s prose form a single image-text itinerary. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1976:** *Colloque de Tangier*. A collaborative conversational and textual publication with Brion Gysin. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1977–1978:** *The Third Mind*. A collaborative collage-book with Brion Gysin; the published volume is only one manifestation of a larger, partly unrealized project involving manuscripts, image grids, scrapbooks, and cut-ups. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Irish Museum of Modern Art](https://imma.ie/whats-on/cut-outs-and-cut-ups-hans-christian-andersen-and-william-seward-burroughs/))
- **1979:** *Colloque de Tangier, Volume 2*. A further collaborative publication with Brion Gysin and Gérard-Georges Lemaire. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1970s; published 1979:** *Ah Pook Is Here*. An unfinished Gesamtkunstwerk planned with Malcolm McNeill as an extensive word-image narrative; the full project was abandoned for financial reasons, and the 1979 publication preserves only a reduced textual and illustrated manifestation. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1988:** *Apocalypse*. A collaborative image-text work with Keith Haring, issued both as a print portfolio and a book manifestation. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1989–1990:** *The Valley*. A collaborative portfolio with Keith Haring comprising Burroughs texts and Haring etchings; it is one integrated print work rather than an illustrated trade book. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [The Valley catalogue summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Valley_%28suite%29))
- **1990:** *Seven Deadly Sins*. A portfolio combining seven Burroughs texts with shotgun-produced woodblock images; its sculptural box, prints, and writing together constitute the obra. Sources vary between 1990 and the 1991 publication listing. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/); [catalogue description](https://aba.org.uk/assets/catalogues/carl%40carlrwilliams.com/catalogue-2-Still-kicking.pdf))
- **1992:** *Painting and Guns*. An artist’s publication joining Burroughs’s account of his method with examples of gun-made paintings. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1989; recording issued 1993:** *The Black Rider: The Casting of the Magic Bullets*. A stage Gesamtkunstwerk made with director/designer Robert Wilson and composer Tom Waits: Burroughs wrote the book and texts, Waits the music, and Wilson created the staging. The 1993 Waits album is a related sound manifestation, not the whole theatrical work. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))

### Essays, interviews, collections, and editorial manifestations

- **1969–1970:** *The Job: Interviews with William S. Burroughs*. A collaboration with interviewer Daniel Odier that Burroughs expanded with substantial independent texts; it is neither simply an interview transcript nor wholly single-authored. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1970:** *Jack Kerouac*. A short memorial publication made with Claude Pélieu. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1973:** *Exterminator!*. A collection of stories and routines assembled as a new book; it is distinct from the 1960 Burroughs–Gysin *Exterminator*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1978:** *Ali’s Smile/Naked Scientology*. A collection that joins the earlier story with Burroughs’s polemical writings about Scientology. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1982:** *A William Burroughs Reader*. An editorial selection assembled by John Calder; it is a manifestation of existing texts rather than a wholly new Burroughs obra. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1984:** *The Burroughs File*. A collection of previously dispersed essays, routines, and pamphlets, including *White Subway*, *Cobble Stone Gardens*, and *The Retreat Diaries*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1984:** *New York Inside Out*. A photographic book to which Burroughs supplied text; authorship of the total object is collaborative rather than exclusively his. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1985:** *The Adding Machine: Collected Essays*. An authorial essay collection addressing writing, technology, artists, control systems, and cultural figures. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **mid-1950s; published 1989:** *Interzone*. An editorially reconstructed collection of Tangier-period manuscripts that precede and feed into *Naked Lunch*; the posthumous-seeming title is actually a lifetime publication, but its shape owes much to editor James Grauerholz. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1989:** *Uncommon Quotes, Volume 1*. A selection of Burroughs aphorisms; the selection is an editorial object made from his words rather than a continuous text composed under that title. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1993:** *The Letters of William S. Burroughs, 1945–1959*. An edited archive of correspondence prepared by Oliver Harris; the letters are Burroughs’s, while the book’s boundaries and apparatus are editorial. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1994:** *Photos and Remembering Jack Kerouac*. A photographic-remembrance collaboration whose variants include the separately issued *Remembering Jack Kerouac*. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1998:** *Word Virus: The William S. Burroughs Reader*. A posthumous reader edited by James Grauerholz and Ira Silverberg; it is an important manifestation of the oeuvre, but not a new single work authored under that title. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1997 journals; published 2000:** *Last Words: The Final Journals of William S. Burroughs*. Burroughs made the journal entries, while James Grauerholz selected and edited their posthumous book form. ([Shoaf checklist](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **Latin American notebook, 1953; published 2007:** *Everything Lost*. A posthumous scholarly transcription and facsimile of a notebook associated with the yagé journey; its title and finished-book identity are editorial, and it overlaps the documentary ground behind *The Yage Letters* without being the same text. ([Ohio State University Press record](https://ohiostatepress.org/books/titles/9780814210802.html); [Shoaf bibliography discussion](https://realitystudio.org/bibliography/books-and-broadside-prints/))
- **1959–1974 letters; published 2012:** *Rub Out the Words*. A posthumous volume edited by Bill Morgan; the correspondence is Burroughs’s work, but the selection and title are not an independently completed lifetime obra. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))

### Tape works, spoken-word records, and musical collaborations

- **1959–1970s:** *Tape-recorder cut-ups and experiments*. A dispersed body of spliced speech, radio, street recording, playback, and room-tone experiments made partly with Brion Gysin and Ian Sommerville; many tapes lack stable individual titles, and later albums select from rather than exhaust this project. ([UbuWeb sound archive](https://ubu.com/sound/burroughs.html); [Irish Museum of Modern Art](https://imma.ie/whats-on/cut-outs-and-cut-ups-hans-christian-andersen-and-william-seward-burroughs/))
- **1965:** *Call Me Burroughs*. A spoken-word LP of Burroughs reading material from *Naked Lunch* and *The Soft Machine*; the recorded voice and pacing make it a distinct performance object. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1971:** *Obsolete*. An album by Dashiell Hedayat and Gong containing a very brief Burroughs vocal appearance; it is not properly a Burroughs work, and resists inclusion except as a documented contributed performance. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1979:** *The Nova Convention*. A multi-artist live-recording album documenting the 1978 convention; Burroughs is a principal performer but not sole maker of the record. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1981:** *Nothing Here Now but the Recordings*. An LP assembled by Genesis P-Orridge from Burroughs’s experimental tape archive, including collaborations with Brion Gysin; its contents are Burroughs’s tape obra, while the album sequence is post-production by another maker. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1981:** *You’re the Guy I Want to Share My Money With*. A three-sided collaborative album with Laurie Anderson and John Giorno, each artist occupying a side and sharing the fourth through groove-dependent playback. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1984:** *Mister Heartbreak*. Laurie Anderson’s album, on which Burroughs performs the spoken role in “Sharkey’s Night”; the album as a whole is Anderson’s, not his. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1985:** *The Elvis of Letters*. A spoken-word recording made with Gus Van Sant. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1986:** *UnCommon Quotes*. A cassette recording of a live Burroughs reading at Caravan of Dreams; it is a performance manifestation rather than the similarly titled printed selection. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1986:** *Break Through in Grey Room*. An album assembling readings, tape cut-ups, and sound experiments made across earlier decades. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1987:** *Smack My Crack*. A Giorno Poetry Systems compilation with a Burroughs contribution; the compilation itself is collective, not solely his obra. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1989:** *Like a Girl, I Want You to Keep Coming*. John Giorno’s multi-artist album containing a Burroughs performance; attribution belongs to the contribution, not the whole album. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1989:** *Seven Souls*. A collaboration with Material in which Burroughs’s recitation is integrated with the group’s music; it was later remixed and expanded as *The Road to the Western Lands*. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1990:** *Dead City Radio*. A studio album setting Burroughs readings against music and sound production by multiple collaborators; it is a spoken-word/music Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a print collection transferred unchanged to audio. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1990:** *Millions of Images*. A spoken-word and sound collaboration with Gus Van Sant. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1992:** *The “Priest” They Called Him*. A recorded prose-and-guitar collaboration: Burroughs performs the narration and Kurt Cobain supplies an independently recorded guitar part. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1992:** *Just One Fix*. A Ministry song and video incorporating Burroughs’s voice and imagery; Burroughs also supplied cover artwork for the single, but Ministry remains the principal musical author. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1993:** *Spare Ass Annie and Other Tales*. A collaborative album with the Disposable Heroes of Hiphoprisy, setting Burroughs’s performances within newly made musical tracks. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1994:** *Words of Advice*. A Burroughs spoken performance embedded in Material’s album *Hallucination Engine*; it is a contributed track rather than an album authored by him. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1996:** *10%: File Under Burroughs*. A two-CD assemblage of recordings produced by Frank Rynne and Joe Ambrose; it is an archival manifestation, not a single session or wholly new Burroughs composition. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1996:** *Star Me Kitten*. A vocal collaboration with R.E.M., issued on *Songs in the Key of X*; the composition is R.E.M.’s and Burroughs’s making lies in the recorded recitation. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **recorded before 1997; issued 2000:** *Is Everybody In?*. A posthumously released performance in which Burroughs reads Jim Morrison’s words over music by the Doors; he is performer, not author of the verbal text or music. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))

### Films and moving-image collaborations

- **1963:** *Towers Open Fire*. An experimental film made with director Antony Balch and Brion Gysin from Burroughs’s scenarios, performances, cut-ups, and dream imagery; authorship is collaborative. ([UbuWeb film archive](https://ubu.com/film/burroughs.html); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1966:** *The Cut-Ups*. An experimental montage film by Antony Balch, Burroughs, and Brion Gysin using mathematically cut and repeated image and speech; the Centre Pompidou catalogues it jointly. ([Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/c88XqeL))
- **1960s; completed/released later:** *Ghosts at No. 9 (Paris)*. An experimental film project with Antony Balch and Brion Gysin assembled from footage shot around the Beat Hotel; its long and irregular production history makes a single date misleading. ([UbuWeb film archive](https://ubu.com/film/burroughs.html); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1970:** *Bill and Tony*. A short film experiment with Antony Balch in which Burroughs and Balch’s faces and voices are interchanged; it is an audiovisual cut-up. ([UbuWeb film archive](https://ubu.com/film/burroughs.html))
- **1982:** *The Final Academy Documents*. A filmed record and later edited video/DVD manifestation of the Final Academy performances, including Burroughs, John Giorno, Brion Gysin, and others; it documents an event rather than constituting a wholly Burroughs-authored film. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1993:** *The Junky’s Christmas*. An animated short directed by Francis Ford Coppola, Nick Donkin, and Melodie McDaniel around Burroughs’s recorded narration and story; Burroughs performs and supplies the source text but did not singly make the film. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **filmed 1980s–1990s; released 2007:** *Words of Advice: William S. Burroughs on the Road*. A collaborative documentary and performance record released after his death; its delayed completion means it is better classed as a posthumous manifestation of a lifetime collaboration. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))

### Interactive and performed roles

- **1995:** *The Dark Eye*. A CD-ROM video game in which Burroughs created the voice performance of Uncle Edwin and narrated Edgar Allan Poe texts; the game is not his sole obra, but his only documented videogame performance. ([MobyGames](https://www.mobygames.com/person/84907/william-s-burroughs/); [Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))
- **1980s–1990s:** *Film appearances and performed personae*. Burroughs appeared as himself or as a character in works including *Home of the Brave* and *Drugstore Cowboy*. These performances are things he made within other people’s films, but the films themselves should not be reassigned to him. ([Burroughs bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Burroughs_bibliography))

### Visual obra and materially unstable works

- **1950s–1970s:** *Scrapbooks, photographic cut-ups, collages, and “third-mind” pages*. A dispersed visual-textual practice made alone and with Brion Gysin; many sheets have no stable title, and treating every page as either manuscript or autonomous artwork would falsely settle an ambiguity central to the work. ([Irish Museum of Modern Art](https://imma.ie/whats-on/cut-outs-and-cut-ups-hans-christian-andersen-and-william-seward-burroughs/); [Semiose](https://semiose.com/en/exhibition/william-s-burroughs-william-s-burroughs-2016/))
- **from the early 1980s:** *Shotgun paintings*. A body of works on plywood, paper, doors, and other supports made by firing shotguns into paint-bearing surfaces; this is an ongoing procedure and corpus, not one work with a single title or date. ([Western Front](https://westernfront.ca/archives/get/ca_occurrences/1460); [Semiose](https://semiose.com/en/exhibition/william-s-burroughs-william-s-burroughs-2016/))
- **1980s–1997:** *Paintings on paper and wood*. A large body of individually titled and untitled works using spray paint, ink, acrylic, stencils, transferred photographs, automatic marks, and found materials. No complete catalogue raisonné was located, so inventing a supposedly exhaustive item-by-item list would overstate the surviving documentation. ([Semiose](https://semiose.com/en/exhibition/william-s-burroughs-william-s-burroughs-2016/); [RealityStudio exhibition chronology](https://realitystudio.org/bibliographic-bunker/collecting-william-s-burroughs-and-the-arts/william-burroughs-art-exhibitions/))
- **1980s–1990s:** *File-folder paintings*. A series made on functional manila folders, sometimes retaining labels, writing, collage, and signs of use; each object hovers between office artifact, support, archive, and painting. ([NYU William S. Burroughs Papers](https://findingaids.library.nyu.edu/fales/mss_625/all/))
- **1980s–1990s:** *Target, gunshot, and buckshot works*. Works made by shooting printed matter, woodblocks, paint containers, or collaborative images; because destruction is their production protocol, they resist the usual separation between performance, process, print, and finished object. ([Western Front](https://westernfront.ca/archives/get/ca_occurrences/1460); [RealityStudio art exhibitions](https://realitystudio.org/bibliographic-bunker/collecting-william-s-burroughs-and-the-arts/william-burroughs-art-exhibitions/))
- **1980s–1990s:** *Collaborative visual works*. Burroughs painted, shot, overprinted, or otherwise altered works with artists including Keith Haring, George Condo, and others. These objects require joint attribution, and the absence of a complete catalogue raisonné prevents a responsible claim that every individual sheet or panel has been enumerated here. ([RealityStudio art exhibitions](https://realitystudio.org/bibliographic-bunker/collecting-william-s-burroughs-and-the-arts/william-burroughs-art-exhibitions/); [Semiose](https://semiose.com/en/exhibition/william-s-burroughs-william-s-burroughs-2016/))
