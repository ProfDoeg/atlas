## Works by Linus Benedict Torvalds

### Early programs and experiments

- **c. 1980–1981:** *Untitled submarine game*. A machine-code game for the Commodore VIC-20 in which a submarine avoided fish; Torvalds describes it chiefly as a vehicle for experimenting with fast sideways scrolling, and no fixed title or surviving release is documented. [Computer History Museum](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf)
- **1986:** *GMOVE*. A graphics-moving routine, or software blitter, for the Sinclair QL, published as a program listing in the November 1986 issue of the Finnish magazine *BITTI*. Its date conflicts with Torvalds’s later recollection that he acquired his QL in early 1987, so the chronology remains uncertain. [Sinclair QL Preservation Project](https://sinclairql.net/), [preserved disassembly](https://sintonen.fi/gmove/), [Computer History Museum](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf)
- **mid-to-late 1980s:** *FORTH compiler for the Sinclair QL*. An apparently private compiler recalled by Torvalds among his QL programs; the surviving account supplies neither a distinct title nor a release history. [Planet Sinclair](https://rk.nvg.ntnu.no/sinclair/computers/ql/ql.htm)
- **mid-to-late 1980s:** *Untitled editor-assembler system for the Sinclair QL*. A combined programming tool made for Torvalds’s own use. It resists treatment as a recoverable published work because the account records its function but not a stable title, version, or surviving artifact. [Planet Sinclair](https://rk.nvg.ntnu.no/sinclair/computers/ql/ql.htm)
- **mid-to-late 1980s:** *Cool Man*. A Pac-Man clone for the Sinclair QL; contemporary retrospective evidence identifies the program but does not establish a precise date or public release. [Planet Sinclair](https://rk.nvg.ntnu.no/sinclair/computers/ql/ql.htm), [Sinclair QL historical summary](https://www.jungsi.de/en/retro-sinclair-ql/)
- **mid-to-late 1980s:** *Untitled MS-DOS-compatible floppy-disk driver for the Sinclair QL*. A replacement driver written after Torvalds found the supplied controller software unsatisfactory. It was a functional protocol between operating system and hardware, but its title, date, and distribution status are undocumented. [Planet Sinclair](https://rk.nvg.ntnu.no/sinclair/computers/ql/ql.htm)
- **mid-to-late 1980s:** *Other Sinclair QL programs and games*. Torvalds’s own retrospective account says that he wrote several additional programs but does not name or enumerate them. This is therefore evidence of an incomplete body of work, not license to manufacture a catalogue of individual titles. [Planet Sinclair](https://rk.nvg.ntnu.no/sinclair/computers/ql/ql.htm)

### Software obras and continuing projects

- **1991–present:** *Linux kernel*. An operating-system kernel begun as Torvalds’s personal 386 project, publicly manifested as version 0.01 in September 1991, and subsequently remade by a worldwide contributor community under his leadership. “Linux” consequently names both Torvalds’s originating obra and an immense collective, continuously changing project; it should not be confused with every complete operating system or distribution built around the kernel. [University of Helsinki](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/news/mathematics-and-science/how-did-30-year-old-linux-conquer-world), [University of Helsinki Linux archive](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/linux/), [kernel.org historical archive](https://www.kernel.org/pub/linux/kernel/Historic/)
- **1991:** *Boot loader and terminal-emulation program that became Linux*. Torvalds first made stand-alone programs that booted his 386, entered protected mode, switched tasks, and connected through a modem; as filesystem and system-call facilities accumulated, the experiment became the Linux kernel. It therefore resists classification as a separate finished work: it is the developmental prehistory of the larger project. [Computer History Museum](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2012/10/102658325-05-01-acc.pdf)
- **2005:** *Git*. A distributed version-control system designed and initially implemented by Torvalds for Linux-kernel development; the first self-hosted commit dates to 7 April 2005. Torvalds relinquished day-to-day maintenance after several months, and explicitly credits Junio Hamano and later contributors for most of the mature project, so present-day Git is a collective obra rather than solely his program. [Git project history](https://git-scm.com/book/en/v2/Getting-Started-A-Short-History-of-Git), [GitHub’s twentieth-anniversary history](https://github.blog/open-source/git/git-turns-20-a-qa-with-linus-torvalds/)
- **2011–present:** *Subsurface*. An open-source dive-log and dive-planning application initiated by Torvalds during the autumn of 2011. It became a collaborative, cross-platform project, with Dirk Hohndel assuming maintenance in 2012; its continuing codebase cannot be attributed wholly to its initiator. [Official Subsurface repository and project history](https://github.com/subsurface/subsurface)
- **date not fixed; maintained to the present:** *Torvalds’s uEmacs/PK 4.0 variant*. A privately modified and minimally maintained version of MicroEMACS 3.9e, used by Torvalds for decades. It specifically resists being called an original Torvalds creation: Dave Conroy, Daniel Lawrence, and Petri Kutvonen made the inherited editor, while Torvalds made and preserves his personal adaptation. [Torvalds’s uEmacs repository](https://github.com/torvalds/uemacs)

### Manifestos, protocols, and technical texts

- **1991:** *“What would you like to see most in minix?”* Torvalds’s comp.os.minix announcement of his still-unnamed free operating-system project. More than a description, the message acted by soliciting participation and publicly constituting the project that became Linux, making it a small manifesto or founding project-text rather than merely correspondence. [University of Helsinki](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/news/mathematics-and-science/how-did-30-year-old-linux-conquer-world)
- **1997:** *Linux: A Portable Operating System*. Torvalds’s University of Helsinki Master of Science thesis, dated 31 January 1997. It is a technical and academic account of portability in the kernel he had already created, not another manifestation of Linux itself. [University of Helsinki-hosted thesis](https://www.cs.helsinki.fi/petri/index_files/linus.pdf)
- **1999:** *The Linux Edge*. An essay on Linux’s design decisions, portability, and collaborative development, first published in *Open Sources: Voices from the Open Source Revolution* and also recorded as an article in *Communications of the ACM* 42(4), pages 38–39. [O’Reilly open-book edition](https://www.oreilly.com/openbook/opensources/book/linus.html), [DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/Torvalds99)
- **2001:** *Linux: New Operating System for Personal Computers*. A technical contribution credited jointly to Patrik Floréen and Linus Torvalds in Lauri Kinnunen’s edited *High Technology in Finland 1995*. The University of Helsinki records it as a 2001 publication, while the volume title contains “1995”; the bibliographic dating is therefore potentially confusing and is not silently regularized here. [University of Helsinki Research Portal](https://researchportal.helsinki.fi/en/publications/linux-new-operating-system-for-personal-computers/)
- **2001:** *“What Makes Hackers Tick? a.k.a. Linus’s Law”*. Torvalds’s prologue to Pekka Himanen’s *The Hacker Ethic and the Spirit of the Information Age*. It is an essay proposing a hierarchy of hacker motivations, not a coauthored book despite catalogues that sometimes promote contributors to book-level authorship. [Contemporary review and contents description](https://www.linuxjournal.com/article/4690)

### Book-length narrative

- **2001:** *Just for Fun: The Story of an Accidental Revolutionary*. An autobiographical book made jointly with journalist David Diamond. Its dialogic and mediated authorship matters: it is Torvalds’s life narrative but not a solitary memoir written by him alone, and later translations and reprints are manifestations of this work rather than additional obras. [HarperCollins bibliographic record via Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Just_for_Fun.html?id=6zSWd8Ou8BAC)

### Bibliographic artifacts that resist work-status

- **2018:** *Keynotes*. A four-page IEEE *International Conference on Consumer Electronics* proceedings record listing Torvalds with six other keynote speakers. DBLP catalogues it as a jointly authored conference item, but the generic title and collective speaker-list give insufficient evidence that it is a single text composed by Torvalds; it is safest treated as a proceedings artifact rather than an independent obra. [DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/iccel/TorvaldsJDMBBN18)
