## Works by George Herbert Walker Bush

### Autobiographical obras

- **1987:** *Looking Forward: An Autobiography*. A campaign autobiography made collaboratively with journalist and former Bush speechwriter Victor Gold; contemporary reporting describes two years of collaboration drawing partly on Bush’s recorded recollections, so sole authorship should not be presumed. [Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1987-05-14-vw-8884-story.html)

- **1998:** *A World Transformed*. A jointly authored political memoir by Bush and his national-security adviser Brent Scowcroft, organized around the international events of the Bush presidency rather than as a comprehensive autobiography. [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/22029/a-world-transformed-by-george-hw--bush-and-brent-scowcroft/)

- **1999:** *All the Best, George Bush: My Life in Letters and Other Writings*. An autobiographical assemblage of Bush’s letters, diary entries, memoranda, and connective commentary: the book is Bush’s obra, but many constituent texts originally existed as private communications or records made for other purposes. [TIME](https://time.com/4750536/george-h-w-bush-letters-excerpt/)

### Diaries, letters, and private textual corpora

- **1942–2018:** *Letters and Other Personal Writings*. A dispersed lifelong corpus rather than one fixed work: Bush wrote letters, notes, memoranda, and diary entries to relatives, friends, officials, and successors. *All the Best* publishes a selection, not a complete collected-works edition, while the Bush Library’s holdings contain further personal and political records; completeness and even the boundary between Bush’s papers and presidential records therefore resist determination. [George H.W. Bush Presidential Library, Guide to Holdings](https://www.bush41library.gov/sites/default/files/documents/2025-12/guide_to_holdings.pdf)

- **1974–1975; published 2008:** *The China Diary of George H. W. Bush: The Making of a Global President*. Bush’s day-by-day diary from his leadership of the United States Liaison Office in Beijing, later made into a scholarly book by editor Jeffrey A. Engel, who supplied the introduction, contextual essay, and editorial apparatus; the diary is Bush’s text, but the 2008 book is a collaborative editorial manifestation. [JSTOR/Princeton University Press](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt7stg8)

- **approximately 1981–1993:** *Vice-Presidential and Presidential Dictated Diary*. An intermittently dictated record underlying portions of Bush’s later books and historical accounts; it is a private archival corpus without a single authorially fixed title or complete publication, and consequently resists treatment as a finished obra. The presidential library describes its holdings as including records created or received by Bush and distinguishes those records from the selectively published *Public Papers*. [George H.W. Bush Presidential Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/digital-research-room/about-textual-collections)

### Selected and remade speech collections

- **2001:** *Heartbeat: George Bush in His Own Words*. An anthology assembled and edited by Jim McGrath from Bush’s speeches and writings. Its constituent words derive from Bush’s public and private textual corpus, but the selection and book-form obra are McGrath’s editorial creation rather than a book authored by Bush. [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Heartbeat-George-Bush-in-His-Own-Words/Jim-McGrath/9780743229753)

- **2009:** *Speaking of Freedom: The Collected Speeches*. A retrospective selection of forty-two presidential speeches chosen and annotated by Bush. It is therefore both a manifestation of earlier, frequently collaborative White House speech texts and a new curatorial obra made by Bush; “collected” does not mean complete. [Simon & Schuster](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Speaking-of-Freedom/George-H-W-Bush/9781451659597)

### Presidential public-text corpus

- **1989–1993:** *Public Papers of the Presidents of the United States: George Bush*. The official collected corpus of Bush’s presidential addresses, remarks, statements, messages, interviews, news conferences, nominations, memoranda, letters, and other public documents, issued in eight chronological books. These texts are acts of the presidency and often function as manifestos, protocols, or legal-administrative instruments, but their verbal authorship was distributed among Bush, speechwriters, advisers, agencies, and editors; presidential signature or delivery does not establish sole composition. [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/publications/presidential-papers.html) [GovInfo example: 1991, Book I](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-1991-book1/pdf/PPP-1991-book1.pdf)

- **20 January 1989:** *Inaugural Address*. A ceremonial and programmatic manifesto delivered upon assuming the presidency; it is individually catalogued within the official presidential-paper corpus, although, like other presidential addresses, it was made through White House speechwriting collaboration rather than demonstrably composed by Bush alone. [American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/inaugural-address-11)

- **31 January 1990:** *Address Before a Joint Session of the Congress on the State of the Union*. A constitutionally situated presidential message and policy manifesto, preserved as an individual text within the official corpus; delivery and institutional authorship belong to Bush, while compositional authorship is collective. [American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-before-joint-session-the-congress-the-state-the-union-1)

- **29 January 1991:** *Address Before a Joint Session of the Congress on the State of the Union*. A wartime State of the Union address functioning simultaneously as report, policy program, and persuasive act; it survives in the itemized *Public Papers*. [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PPP-1991-book1/html/PPP-1991-book1-doc-pg74.htm)

- **28 January 1992:** *Address Before a Joint Session of the Congress on the State of the Union*. Bush’s final formal State of the Union address, a presidential manifesto made within a collaborative institutional speechwriting process. [American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/address-before-joint-session-the-congress-the-state-the-union-2)

- **1989–1993:** *Executive Orders of George Bush*. A numbered corpus of presidential protocols: legally operative instructions rather than descriptive writings. Bush issued and signed them, but their language was normally prepared and reviewed institutionally, making them presidential acts without secure personal literary authorship. [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/executive-orders/george-bush)

- **1989–1993:** *Proclamations of George Bush*. A corpus of formally issued presidential texts that declare observances, policies, or legal conditions and thus act rather than merely describe. They belong to Bush’s presidential output while resisting classification as personally composed works. [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/presidential-documents/proclamations/george-bush)

- **1989–1993:** *Presidential Signing Statements*. Statements issued when Bush approved legislation, combining legal interpretation, constitutional argument, and administrative direction. They are executive manifestations attached to statutes made by Congress, not the statutes themselves, and their drafting was institutional. [American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/app-categories/presidential/signing-statements)

### Strategy documents and governmental projects

- **March 1990:** *National Security Strategy of the United States*. A congressionally mandated strategic report transmitted under Bush’s authority: part manifesto and part governmental protocol. It is an executive-branch creation associated with his presidency, not securely a text personally written by him. [National Security Strategy Archive](https://nssarchive.us/national-security-strategy-1990/)

- **August 1991:** *National Security Strategy of the United States*. A revised administration-wide strategy responding to the Gulf War and the transformation of the Soviet bloc; its institutional production makes it a Bush presidential project rather than an individually authored Bush obra. [National Security Strategy Archive](https://nssarchive.us/national-security-strategy-1991/)

- **January 1993:** *National Security Strategy of the United States*. The administration’s final strategic report, issued at the end of Bush’s term. It is an official manifestation of collective executive policy and resists attribution to Bush as a personal author. [National Security Strategy Archive](https://nssarchive.us/national-security-strategy-1993/)

- **1991:** *America 2000: An Education Strategy*. An administration policy project and instructional program rather than a personal book: it set national educational goals and proposed mechanisms for states and communities while expressly leaving implementation dispersed. Archival descriptions identify it among Bush White House publications, but do not establish Bush as its verbal author. [National Archives presidential-records notice](https://www.archives.gov/foia/pra-notifications/pdf/bush41/rn-lpgb-2014-101.pdf)

### Organizations and civic projects

- **1951:** *Bush-Overbey Oil Development Company*. A business venture co-founded by Bush and John Overbey to acquire and trade mineral and oil leases. It is an organizational project rather than a text or individually authored object. [George H.W. Bush Presidential Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)

- **1953:** *Zapata Petroleum Corporation*. An oil-exploration company co-founded by Bush with Hugh and Bill Liedtke and others. Bush helped make the enterprise, but it is a collective corporate project whose later activities cannot all be treated as his works. [George H.W. Bush Presidential Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)

- **1954–1966:** *Zapata Off-Shore Company*. The offshore-drilling venture led by Bush after its separation from Zapata Petroleum. It is a sustained commercial project and organization, not a textual obra, and corporate products or drilling operations resist attribution to Bush individually. [George H.W. Bush Presidential Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/bushes/president-george-bush)

- **1989–1993:** *Points of Light*. A presidential civic-service project developed through speeches, White House action, and the creation of an enduring nonprofit infrastructure. Its distributed institutional form makes it closer to a social project than a single authored work. [Points of Light history](https://www.pointsoflight.org/history/)

- **1991:** *George Bush Presidential Library and Museum project*. Bush participated in establishing the presidential-library project at Texas A&M University, but the building, archive, museum, and foundation are collective institutional creations; they should not be attributed to him as architectural or curatorial works. [George H.W. Bush Presidential Library](https://www.bush41library.gov/about-us)
