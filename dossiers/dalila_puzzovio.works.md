## Works by Dalila Puzzovio

### Paintings and works on paper

- **1961:** *Untitled paintings* (two documented works). Informalist oils on canvas, respectively 150 × 100 cm and 140 × 97 cm; the 2025 Museo Moderno loan inventory records them separately but supplies no further titles. [S1]
- **1973:** *Untitled tapestry*. A tapestry mounted on wood; the available institutional inventory gives neither a series nor a more specific title. [S1]
- **1973:** *Corset* (three works from *Dibujos para recortar y armar*). Three individually catalogued mixed-media drawings in ink on acetate, all sharing the same title and dimensions; they are designs for objects to be cut out and assembled rather than conventional autonomous drawings. [S2]

### Objects, installations, and reconstructed obras

- **1962:** *La novia*. A human-scale assemblage of pipes, fabric scraps, and discarded orthopedic casts, made for *El hombre antes del hombre*. Its surviving manifestations include two untitled period photographs; the original object’s present status is not specified. [S2]
- **1963:** *Cáscaras*. An installation-environment at Galería Lirolay made from discarded orthopedic casts, carts, furniture, and fabric, with attendants dressed as nurses enforcing silence. Because it encompassed objects, exhibition design, and performed reception, it is better understood as a Gesamtkunstwerk than as a single sculpture; much of the original installation is known through documentation and later reconstructions. [S2]
- **1963 / 1998:** *Lustrabotas*. A mixed-media object from *Cáscaras*, combining an orthopedic cast, wood, and fabric with a shoeshine box. The double date signals a later reconstruction or remaking rather than uninterrupted survival of the 1963 object. [S1][S2]
- **1963 / 1998:** *Lleno de sonrisas serias*. A multipart assemblage of orthopedic casts, metal, wood, fabric, and found objects from *Cáscaras*. Its catalogue dimensions and 1963/1998 dating identify the extant piece as a reconstruction or materially renewed version. [S1][S2]
- **1963:** *Escape de gas*. A sculptural assemblage consisting of a metal cart, plaster, paint, and a wooden base. It belongs to Puzzovio’s early orthopedic-cast vocabulary, although the official inventory does not assign it to *Cáscaras*. [S1]
- **1964 / 1998:** *Corona para los habitantes no humanos*. An installation-series of mortuary wreath-like objects made from orthopedic plaster, vinyl, and fabric for *La muerte*. Sources catalogue several differently sized components under the same title; their compound date again indicates reconstruction or remaking. [S1][S2]
- **1965:** *La esfera del tiempo*. One of three monumental wire-and-plaster corset structures installed for the Premio Nacional Instituto Torcuato Di Tella; it incorporated casts and wreaths from the preceding year. It was simultaneously obra and exhibition apparatus, and the original does not appear to survive intact. [S2]
- **1965:** *Se dan clases de tejido a mano y a máquina*. A second giant corset-installation, furnished with cushions and soft textiles. Its status oscillates between sculpture, room, display architecture, and environment. [S2]
- **1965:** *Jean Shrimpton, la plus belle fille du monde*. The third giant corset-installation, dedicated to the fashion model and fitted with an aperture through which interior lights could be seen. The 2025 retrospective reconstructed the corset environments, so present displays should not be mistaken silently for the lost 1965 originals. [S2][S3]
- **1966 / 1997 / 2025:** *Dalila autorretrato* (also catalogued as *Autorretrato de Dalila*). A monumental illuminated poster-panel, painted by commercial cinema-sign artisans from a collage joining Puzzovio’s face to Veruschka’s body and accompanied by inflatable cushions. The 1966 original disappeared; Puzzovio commissioned a remake in 1997, while the Museo Moderno restored or reconstructed its original monumental presentation in 2025. [S2][S3]
- **1967:** *Dalila doble plataforma*. A work-in-progress joining a lit shop-window installation, fluorescent double-platform shoes, advertising, and their commercial sale through Grimoldi stores—simultaneously obra, commodity, fashion design, and distribution protocol. Sources disagree materially about its extent: Puzzovio’s gallery describes sixteen pairs, another historical account says twenty-five pairs, and the 2025 inventory lists forty-eight shoes; these may describe different displays or states, but the discrepancy is unresolved. [S1][S2][S4]
- **1967:** *Designs for Dalila doble plataforma*. At least three unique preparatory shoe designs survive, one securely dated 1967 and two catalogued circa 1967, together with an offset advertising poster. These are components and manifestations of the larger project but also separately catalogued objects. [S2]
- **undated:** *Teddy Bear*. A stuffed bear catalogued as an independent object in the 2025 exhibition inventory without a date. Its lack of date should be retained rather than inferred from its appearance in Puzzovio’s earlier public imagery. [S1]
- **c. 2000:** *Criollitas jewelry*. One bracelet, two pairs of clip earrings, and one brooch, all untitled and made from unidentified materials. The inventory treats them as four separate design objects; their precise dates and production context remain unspecified. [S1]

### Public manifestations and photo-performances

- **16 August 1965:** *¿Por qué son tan geniales?* Made with Edgardo Giménez and Charlie Squirru, this was a large urban advertising panel showing the three artists with attributes of their works, accompanied by distributed leaflets and press activity. It resists reduction to a poster: the billboard, publicity campaign, self-fashioning, and surviving untitled photographs collectively constituted the obra. [S2][S3]
- **1979:** *Mientras unos construyen, otros destruyen*. A photo-performance conceived for *Claudia* magazine: an elaborate wedding tableau staged amid demolitions for the widening of Avenida 9 de Julio, with Puzzovio responsible for the mise-en-scène, clothing, and hats. Its material afterlife consists of multiple untitled photographic groups—period photographs by Margarita Dionisi and later 2019 prints credited to Isidoro Rubini—so the action and its records are related but not identical works. [S1][S5]
- **2011:** *El Deslumbre*. A participatory installation-performance at arteBA that reactivated *Dalila doble plataforma* as a shoe-shop environment and catwalk on which visitors tried on and modeled the shoes. It is a later manifestation of the 1967 project rather than merely a replica of its display. [S6]

### Fashion, textile, and accessory projects

- **1966:** *Help Valentino*. Costumes—and, according to a contemporary account, scenographic participation with Edgardo Giménez—for the foundational Buenos Aires café-concert show featuring Antonio Gasalla, Edda Díaz, Norah Blay, and Carlos Perciavalle. This was applied theatrical design, not a book or a single autonomous art object. [S7][S8]
- **1967–1968:** *Psexoanálisis*. Costume design, made with Charlie Squirru, for Héctor Olivera’s film and particularly associated with Libertad Leblanc. Seven surviving untitled costume drawings are dated 1967 in the Museo Moderno inventory, while the released film dates from 1968; confusing design date and release date would erase the project’s production history. [S1][S5][S9]
- **1969:** *Pepsi*. Costumes for Malvina Pastorino in a production at Teatro Cómico, Buenos Aires. The standard biographical lists identify the commission but do not enumerate the individual garments. [S7]
- **1969:** *Cala*. A watercolor-and-ink scarf design intended for silk, extended by Puzzovio into a coordinated handbag and double-platform shoes. It formed part of her New York textile-design activity rather than an autonomous painting series. [S10]
- **1969:** *More than 200 textile patterns*. Commercial fabric designs conceived in New York with support from the National Cotton Council; some were Puzzovio’s alone and others collaborations with Charlie Squirru. Most original maquettes are held by Fundación IDA, but no consulted scholarly source supplies a complete title-by-title concordance, so inventing titles or pretending that every pattern is individually attributable would be misleading. [S9][S10]
- **c. 1970–1972:** *Tricot precolombino*. A multicolored wool dress with open sleeves and flared skirt, translating pre-Hispanic references and pop imagery into a jacquard-ready pattern. The 2025 inventory calls the surviving garment simply *Sin título* with the reference “Vestido Tricot”; *Tricot precolombino* is the descriptive title used by the MALBA design catalogue. [S1][S10]
- **c. 1970–1972:** *Dalila*. A prêt-à-porter knitwear collection designed with Charlie Squirru and sold by Madame Frou-Frou. It is a commercial collection and distributed body of design rather than one fixed obra. [S10]
- **c. 1970–1972:** *Forestal*. A second prêt-à-porter knitwear collection designed with Charlie Squirru and commercialized by Madame Frou-Frou. The consulted catalogue names the line but does not enumerate every garment. [S10]
- **c. 1970–1972:** *Homenaje a Urano en Escorpio*. A third collaborative prêt-à-porter knitwear collection sold by Madame Frou-Frou. Its title names a collection or project, not a single dress. [S10]
- **1971:** *La vera historia de Salomé*. Costume and set design for Miguel Ángel Rondano’s musical at Teatro General San Martín. Forty surviving untitled figurines—drawings and collages on paper, cardboard, and plastic, sometimes incorporating textile—are individually inventoried, but because they share no distinguishing titles they are named here as the design corpus rather than assigned invented ones. [S1][S5][S7]
- **1975:** *Hello Tato*. Costume design for Tato Bores’s musical comedy at Teatro Estrellas, Buenos Aires. Available biographical catalogues document the commission but not the number or individual titles of its costumes. [S7]
- **1976:** *La loca colección de Dalila Puzzovio*. A fashion collection documented in *Vosotras* magazine; the Museo Moderno inventory preserves the printed feature as an archival manifestation, but does not identify or title its constituent garments. [S1]
- **1977–1978:** *Con sabor a Pinky*. Television costumes designed with Charlie Squirru; sources differ between a start in 1977 and the range 1977–78. As broadcast wardrobe it was a continuing design project rather than a single stable object. [S7][S9]
- **1978:** *Tejidos de verano*. A textile/fashion project documented in *Revista La Nación*. The surviving inventory entry is the printed press page, not necessarily an extant garment, and gives no titles for individual designs. [S1]
- **1981:** *María Creuza show*. Costumes for the singer’s performance at Hotel Bauen, Buenos Aires. The standard biographical list records the commission without a garment-level catalogue. [S7]

### Photographic and digital collage bodies

- **from 1998:** *Híbridos*. A continuing body of manipulated personal photographs and collages, later transferred or enlarged on supports including Scotchprint. Because the sources describe a process and series rather than a closed numbered set, its completeness and individual titles cannot responsibly be supplied. [S9][S11]
- **2002:** *Teddy*. A work reproduced under this title and date in documentation for *Mitos manufacturados*; the consulted notice does not state its medium or dimensions. [S12]
- **2007:** *Híbridos*. The Galería Vasari manifestation of the series combined warm paper collages, landscapes, and corresponding Scotchprint interventions. This is both an exhibition project and a material phase of the longer body of work, not necessarily a newly closed series. [S11]
- **2012:** *Journey*. An exhibition-project at the Pabellón de Bellas Artes, Universidad Católica Argentina, surveying or recomposing Puzzovio’s visual journey. Sources identify the project but do not provide a complete checklist, so it cannot safely be decomposed into unnamed individual works. [S12]
- **2013:** *Mitos manufacturados*. An exhibition-project at Galería Rubbers bringing together works from across Puzzovio’s practice. It resists classification as one object, while the available announcement does not establish whether every component was newly made for it. [S12]

### Graphic, editorial, and advertising design

- **1962:** *Catalogue for El hombre antes del hombre*. An exhibition catalogue credited to Puzzovio in the artist’s archival chronology. It is a designed publication and document associated with *La novia*, not evidence for an otherwise unnamed sculpture. [S2]
- **1963:** *Cáscaras* poster. An offset exhibition advertisement made with Edgardo Giménez. It is both graphic design and a surviving manifestation of the larger *Cáscaras* environment. [S2]
- **1964:** *La muerte* poster. An offset exhibition advertisement made with Edgardo Giménez. Like the exhibition it announced, it participates in Puzzovio’s fusion of mortuary imagery, publicity, and installation. [S2]
- **1967:** *Dalila doble plataforma* poster. An offset advertisement for the double-platform project. It functioned as publicity within the work’s art–commerce circuit rather than merely describing an already self-contained sculpture. [S2]
- **dates not fully catalogued, through c. 1990:** *Editorial writing, illustration, and art direction*. Puzzovio produced articles, illustrations, fashion editorials, and magazine art direction, including cover graphics for *Cuisine & Vins*, sometimes with Charlie Squirru. The consulted institutional archive describes this as an extensive body but supplies no complete issue-level bibliography; it therefore resists honest enumeration as a closed list of works. [S2][S9]

### Architecture and interiors

- **1977:** *Puzzovio–Squirru house renovation*. A recycled-house/interior project awarded the Museo de la Ciudad’s prize for the best renovated house in Buenos Aires. The biographical record establishes the award but the consulted sources do not give a formal project title or precise address. [S12]
- **date not specified:** *Limbo shop*. A retail-interior project documented by Fundación IDA. Its date, location, collaborators, and surviving state are not supplied there, so no narrower attribution should be imposed. [S9]
- **date not specified:** *Adrogué shopping center*. An architectural/interior-design commission documented by Fundación IDA. The source does not identify the mall more precisely or give a date, making this a named project but not a securely bounded object. [S9]
- **c. 1999:** *NEC Buenos Aires offices*. A turnkey interior and corporate-space project commissioned by NEC Argentina as a central-city pied-à-terre for client meetings. Contemporary reporting attributes the project to Puzzovio, while Fundación IDA includes it among her principal interiors. [S9][S13]
