## Works by Francisco Salamone

### Built public architecture and urban works

- **1938:** *Cruz del cementerio de Carhué*. Monumental crucifix replacing the cross subsequently transferred to Arano.

### Built private and institutional architecture

- **1929 or earlier:** *Parque Hotel de La Falda*. Hotel building documented by a signed Salamone perspective published in 1929 and described there as constructed under his direction; the surviving evidence does not establish how much of the design was his.
- **1937:** *Casa Carlos Calegari, Alberti*. Private residence attributed to Salamone in later inventories but omitted from the 2023 confirmed-works list; the attribution should therefore remain qualified.
- **1950:** *Edificio de renta, Vicente López*. Rationalist income-producing apartment building at Zufriategui 637.
- **1951:** *Colegio Michael Ham, Vicente López*. Enlargement comprising the frontage and pavement on General Juan Lavalle 1076 rather than the entire school.
- **1954:** *Casa de Emilio Canzani, Mar del Plata*. Private rationalist residence at Buenos Aires 2715.

### Municipal infrastructure in Villa María

- **1934:** *Plaza Independencia, Villa María*. Landscaping and urban-furnishing intervention; this realized work must be distinguished from Salamone’s separate 1935 preliminary redesign.
- **1934:** *Plaza San Martín, Villa María*. Landscaping intervention.
- **1934–1935:** *Pavimentación de calles de Villa María*. Municipal roadworks directed by Salamone; contemporary controversy concerned their rapid deterioration, making this an engineering commission rather than a discrete building.
- **1930s:** *Matadero municipal de Villa María*. Municipal slaughterhouse left unfinished or defective when Salamone departed; sources disagree over how much of the failed construction should count as his completed obra.
- **1930s:** *Parque del cementerio de Villa María*. Municipal cemetery landscaping; surviving accounts identify the commission but do not supply a secure completion date.

### Unfixed or disputed bodies of work

- **1920s:** *Private houses and minor buildings in Córdoba*. The scholarly reconstruction recognizes this early body of work but cannot securely enumerate it: records are incomplete and some candidates may be demolished, radically remodeled or unidentified. It therefore resists treatment as a list of settled individual works.
- **1920s:** *Works in Valle Hermoso*. A short list survives through oral testimony without documentary corroboration; these manifestations cannot presently be promoted into securely attributed works.
- **1936–1940:** *Furniture, lighting, paving, fountains, pergolas and interior fittings for the Buenos Aires municipal commissions*. Salamone designed such elements as parts of larger civic ensembles, often without separate fixed titles; treating every bench or lamp as an autonomous work would falsely divide their Gesamtkunstwerk character.
- **1936–1940:** *Municipal paving, drainage and ancillary engineering works*. Contemporary contracts sometimes bundled these with named buildings, but the surviving catalogues do not consistently separate, title or date every intervention; completeness at object level is therefore impossible.
- **Date unknown:** *Cross near Estomba or Cabildo*. Local tradition attributes a memorial cross at an airplane-crash site to Salamone, allegedly because of his friendship with the Saldungaray family, but the 2023 scholarly account reports no documentary record; it remains an attribution, not a confirmed work.
- **Date unknown:** *House on Sarmiento Street, Alberti*. Locally attributed to Salamone but unconfirmed and absent from the latest confirmed inventory.
- **1936–1940:** *Projects for General Sarmiento, Caseros/Daireaux and Patagones*. A surviving presentation sheet records intended contracts that did not proceed; without a complete set of identifiable drawings, these are evidence of unrealized intentions rather than a secure list of individual works.
- **1937 onward:** *Works at Saavedra*. Construction was interrupted after the murder of a local political leader and completed years later from sketches left by Salamone’s firm; authorship of the final fabric is consequently divided and cannot be assigned to him without qualification.

### Rejected attributions

- **Date unknown:** *Reloj de los Italianos, Chascomús*. Sometimes associated with Salamone, but identified in the attribution literature as a work by Telésforo Sallaberry; it is not a Salamone work.
- **Date unknown:** *Municipalidad de Mar Chiquita, Coronel Vidal*. Attributed instead to the firm Ingeniero Civil Francisco Marseillán SRL.
- **Date unknown:** *Delegación municipal de Pirovano*. Attributed instead to Ingeniero Civil Francisco Marseillán SRL.
- **Date unknown:** *Municipalidad de Puan*. Attributed to César Fernández, not Salamone.
- **Date unknown:** *Municipalidad de Salto*. Attributed to Esteban Pérez, not Salamone.
