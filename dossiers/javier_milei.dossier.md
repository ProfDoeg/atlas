# Javier Gerardo Milei: Research Dossier

*Research cutoff: 3 September 2026. Javier Milei is living and remains president of Argentina. Judicial and political proceedings described below are ongoing unless otherwise stated.*

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full name:** Javier Gerardo Milei.
- **Born:** 22 October 1970, Palermo, City of Buenos Aires, Argentina.
- **Nationality:** Argentine; obtained Italian citizenship *jure sanguinis* in December 2024.
- **Principal residences:** raised chiefly in Villa Devoto, Buenos Aires; later lived in the Abasto neighborhood; spent part of the COVID-19 period with his parents in Vicente López; since assuming office has used the presidential residence at Quinta de Olivos.
- **Occupations:** economist, university lecturer, business economist and consultant, author, radio host, television commentator, national deputy (2021–2023), president of Argentina (since 10 December 2023).
- **Political organization:** founder and leader of La Libertad Avanza (LLA).
- **Public labels and self-descriptions:** “liberal-libertarian,” anarcho-capitalist “in theory” and minarchist “in practice”; “El León”; “El Peluca”; “El Loco,” originally a school nickname and later the title of an unauthorized biography; “General Ancap,” a superhero persona performed at a 2019 cosplay convention.
- **Campaign symbols:** lion, chainsaw, dollar bill, black-and-yellow color scheme, slogan “¡Viva la libertad, carajo!”
- **Marital status and descendants:** never married; no documented children.

## Family and Ancestry

Milei is the elder child of **Norberto Horacio Milei**, born about 1945, and **Alicia Luján Lucich**. His sister, **Karina Elizabeth Milei**, was born in 1972 or 1973.

Norberto worked as a bus driver and subsequently became involved in transport businesses. Alicia was principally described as a homemaker. Available genealogical reporting attributes Italian ancestry to the Milei line and Croatian or Adriatic ancestry to the Lucich line. Some secondary genealogies identify fingerprint-system pioneer Juan Vucetich as a distant Lucich-family relation, but a complete documented chain has not been located in the sources consulted.

Milei has repeatedly said that his father subjected him to physical and psychological violence and that his mother failed to protect him. These are **documented autobiographical allegations**, not independently adjudicated findings. He said that he eventually treated his parents as though they did not exist. During the COVID-19 lockdown he resumed contact and helped support them. His maternal grandmother **Elia**, herself reportedly descended from Italians, and Karina were described as his childhood refuges.

Karina became his organizer, closest confidante and political gatekeeper. Milei calls her **“El Jefe”**—deliberately using the masculine noun. She managed his emergence as a public speaker, his campaigns and the construction of LLA; after his presidential victory she became secretary-general of the Presidency. Journalistic descriptions of her as his “emotional monopoly,” surrogate parent or indispensable psychological support are **reported interpretations**, principally based on interviews with former associates and unauthorized biographical reporting, rather than clinical findings.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1970–1988

Milei grew up in Villa Devoto. He has described himself as isolated, bullied and nicknamed “El Loco” because of his temper and outbursts. Accounts of domestic violence and school bullying come primarily from Milei, Karina and people interviewed by journalists.

He attended the Catholic **Cardenal Copello School**. He was an enthusiastic football goalkeeper, playing for neighborhood teams Alvear and El Ideal and then in the youth system of **Club Atlético Chacarita Juniors**, approximately 1983–1989, reportedly reaching the fourth division.

He also developed a strong attachment to rock music, especially the Rolling Stones. At school he sang in **Everest**, a Stones cover band, and imitated Mick Jagger. Music, staged entrances and reworked rock songs later became part of his political presentation.

Milei says Argentina’s 1981 exchange-rate and debt crisis prompted his interest in economics. He has recalled wondering why economists could not control inflation and later abandoning serious football as hyperinflation approached.

## Education and Formation

Milei enrolled at the private **University of Belgrano**, receiving a licenciatura in economics, usually dated to 1993. He subsequently completed postgraduate study in economic theory at the **Instituto de Desarrollo Económico y Social (IDES)** and economics coursework at **Universidad Torcuato Di Tella**.

He does **not** hold an earned doctorate. He later received honorary doctorates, including one from **ESEADE** and, during his presidency, from Hungary’s University of Public Service/Ludovika. A Spanish edition of *El camino del libertario* incorrectly described him as a University of Buenos Aires graduate and holder of a doctorate. The publisher called the material an error and undertook to correct it. The inaccurate dust-jacket biography is documented; evidence that Milei personally composed it has not been established.

His early economics was mathematically and neoclassically oriented. He studied growth theory, monetary economics and the work of Robert Lucas, Milton Friedman and other Chicago-associated economists. Milei dates his decisive “conversion” to the Austrian school to **2014**, when he read Murray Rothbard’s treatment of monopoly in *Man, Economy, and State*. He subsequently read Ludwig von Mises’s *Human Action* repeatedly and absorbed Friedrich Hayek, Jesús Huerta de Soto, Walter Block, Hans-Hermann Hoppe and Israel Kirzner. He credits Argentine liberal scholar **Alberto Benegas Lynch (h)** as an important mentor.

## Early Professional Career

The surviving public CV and institutional biographies give a broad but not always precisely dated sequence:

- Economist or senior economist at **HSBC Argentina**.
- Chief economist at **Máxima AFJP**, a private pension-fund administrator.
- Chief economist or coordinator at **Estudio Broda**, the consultancy associated with Miguel Ángel Broda.
- Argentine-government adviser in matters before the World Bank’s **International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes (ICSID/CIADI)**.
- Member or participant in bodies associated with the **International Chamber of Commerce**, B20 and World Economic Forum.
- From approximately 2008 until entering elected politics, chief economist or senior economic adviser at **Corporación América**, the conglomerate led by **Eduardo Eurnekian**, with major airport, infrastructure, energy and agribusiness holdings.

A leaked or reported 1993 personnel assessment from an early banking application said his English and economics knowledge were inadequate at that time. This is a **reported employment assessment**, not a comprehensive appraisal of his later work.

Milei taught macroeconomics, monetary economics, growth, microeconomics, public finance and mathematics at several Argentine institutions, including the University of Belgrano and UADE. He claimed to have taught for more than twenty years and lectured widely. A former UADE student complained about aggressive and insulting treatment; the existence of the complaint is reported, but no criminal finding resulted.

His long professional partnership with economist **Diego Giacomini**, begun around 2005, produced books and media appearances. Their relationship later collapsed. Giacomini became a severe critic, accusing Milei of intellectual dishonesty, personal abuse and economic inconsistency. These remain assertions by a former collaborator, albeit one with close firsthand access.

## Media Career and Political Formation, 2014–2020

From the mid-2010s Milei became a frequent television and radio commentator. His shouting, insults, theatrical hairstyle and sweeping denunciations of the political “caste” distinguished him in an already combative media environment.

He hosted or participated in programs including **Cátedra libre** and **Demoliendo mitos**. Media monitoring placed him among Argentina’s economists receiving the most television airtime by 2018. He presented inflation as always monetary, taxation as coercive appropriation, the central bank as a mechanism of political theft and social justice as unjust.

His ideological trajectory moved from conventional market economics toward:

1. orthodox monetarism and Chicago-style analysis;
2. Austrian economics after 2014;
3. theoretical anarcho-capitalism;
4. practical minarchism—a minimal state restricted chiefly to security and justice.

He has defended private property, unrestricted contracting, free trade, currency competition, substantial deregulation and eventual closure of the Central Bank. Although “dollarization” became shorthand for his 2023 campaign, he often described the intended endpoint as free currency choice, expecting Argentines to choose the US dollar.

He entered organized libertarian politics alongside or in the same media-political milieu as José Luis Espert, Carlos Maslatón, Ramiro Marra, Victoria Villarruel and the Benegas Lynch family. These alliances later divided. Maslatón, once a promoter, became an adversary; Espert moved through rivalry, rapprochement and renewed controversy; Vice-President Villarruel’s relationship with the presidential circle deteriorated after taking office.

## Chronological Political Life

### 2020–2021: Building La Libertad Avanza

Karina Milei helped convert public lectures and open-air economics classes into a campaign organization. Milei ran in the City of Buenos Aires through the **La Libertad Avanza** coalition. His theatrical campaign attacked both Peronism and the center-right coalition Juntos por el Cambio.

On 14 November 2021 LLA won about **17%** of the Buenos Aires city vote. Milei entered the Chamber of Deputies on 10 December 2021.

### 2021–2023: National deputy

Milei served one two-year period. His signature practice was a monthly public drawing for his net legislative salary, which continued through the end of his mandate. He argued that he was returning money extracted through taxation. Critics called it data collection or campaign promotion; no finding established illegality.

His legislative record was comparatively thin, reflecting both the small size of his bloc and his presidential campaigning. He voted against budgets and taxes and used the chamber principally as a platform against the “caste.”

LLA expanded through provincial agreements that sometimes included conservative nationalists, former Peronists and relatives or defenders of officials associated with Argentina’s military dictatorship. Milei’s ticket with **Victoria Villarruel**, an activist on behalf of victims of guerrilla violence and a critic of the dominant human-rights narrative about the dictatorship, broadened his coalition beyond libertarian economics.

### 2023 presidential election

Milei won the August 2023 presidential primary with roughly **30%**, unexpectedly finishing first nationwide. His campaign promised:

- radical spending cuts symbolized by a chainsaw;
- reduction or elimination of ministries;
- abolition of the Central Bank;
- currency competition/dollarization;
- privatization and deregulation;
- tougher policing;
- alignment with the United States and Israel;
- rejection of compulsory abortion funding and reconsideration of the 2020 abortion law.

In the 22 October general election, economy minister **Sergio Massa** placed first and Milei second. Milei then obtained support from former president Mauricio Macri and defeated Massa in the 19 November runoff with approximately **55.65%**, the largest raw presidential vote total in Argentine history up to that point.

### 2023–2024: First presidential year

Milei took office on **10 December 2023**, describing the inherited situation as an approaching hyperinflationary catastrophe and warning that adjustment would initially worsen conditions.

Early measures included:

- reducing the number of ministries;
- a sharp peso devaluation;
- removal or reduction of subsidies and price controls;
- suspension of many public works;
- layoffs and non-renewal of contracts in the federal administration;
- the anti-blockade or “anti-picket” security protocol;
- **DNU 70/2023**, a sweeping decree deregulating labor, commerce, housing, health insurance and other sectors;
- an initial “omnibus” reform bill, withdrawn after congressional defeat;
- the narrower **Ley Bases 27,742**, approved in June 2024, including delegated emergency powers, labor and state reforms, privatization authorizations and the **RIGI** large-investment incentive system.

Courts suspended or invalidated portions of DNU 70, especially labor provisions; other sections remained operative. The decree’s constitutional treatment continued through Congress and litigation.

The government recorded monthly fiscal surpluses and Argentina’s first annual financial surplus in years. Monthly inflation, 25.5% in December 2023 after the devaluation, fell sharply during 2024. The adjustment also produced an initial recession, collapsing construction and consumption, reduced real pensions and public-sector wages, and a rapid rise in measured poverty. INDEC recorded poverty at 52.9% in the first half of 2024; it later fell as inflation slowed, wages partly recovered and activity rebounded.

Milei vetoed legislation increasing pension spending and university financing. Congress sustained those 2024 vetoes. Large university, labor and pension demonstrations followed. Security forces used gas, batons and arrests at several demonstrations. Injuries to protesters, journalists and police were documented; responsibility for individual acts remained contested.

Internationally Milei made unusually frequent ideological trips and appearances, including Davos, CPAC events, Israel, Italy, Spain, Germany and the United States. He cultivated ties with Benjamin Netanyahu, Jair Bolsonaro, Giorgia Meloni, Donald Trump and Elon Musk. Diplomatic clashes included a dispute with Spain after comments about Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’s wife, tension with Colombia and Mexico, and the dismissal of foreign minister **Diana Mondino** after Argentina voted at the UN against the US embargo on Cuba.

### 2025–September 2026

Disinflation and fiscal balance remained Milei’s central political assets. INDEC reported poverty at **28.2% in the second half of 2025**, down markedly from the 2024 peak, although methodology, household incomes, pension losses and the durability of the recovery remained subjects of dispute. The economy recovered after the 2024 contraction, but reserves, exchange-rate policy, employment quality and dependence on external financial backing remained vulnerabilities.

In October 2025 LLA and allied lists won approximately **41%** in the midterm legislative election, versus about 32% for the principal Peronist opposition. LLA expanded to approximately 95 deputies but still required alliances for ordinary and special majorities.

The administration experienced exceptionally high senior turnover. By July 2026 one count recorded **274 departures**, including three chiefs of cabinet and six ministers. Individual departures arose from policy disagreements, internal competition, scandals and reorganizations.

By 2026 Milei’s governing structure centered on Karina Milei, economic minister **Luis Caputo**, deregulation minister **Federico Sturzenegger**, security minister **Patricia Bullrich**, presidential strategist **Santiago Caputo**, and a changing cabinet. Relations between Milei and Vice-President Villarruel were openly hostile.

## Economic, Institutional and Financial Interests

### Employment-linked business networks

The most important pre-political corporate relationship was with **Eduardo Eurnekian and Corporación América**. Milei credited Eurnekian with professional opportunities; Eurnekian at times praised him but later criticized aspects of his temperament and administration.

No evidence located establishes that Milei held a controlling interest in Corporación América, HSBC, Máxima AFJP or Estudio Broda. His role was employee, economist or adviser.

### Personal assets

His initial presidential declaration reported approximately **ARS54 million** using the filing’s declared valuations: an apartment or house in Buenos Aires, a 2013 Peugeot RCZ, a 2015 Mercedes-Benz Sprinter, pesos and approximately US$20,000–23,000.

The year-end comparisons are complicated by inflation and changes in valuations:

- end-2023 adjusted/public total: about **ARS125.7 million**;
- end-2024: about **ARS206.0 million**, a 64.8% nominal rise;
- end-2025: about **ARS295.2 million**, a 43% nominal annual rise.

The 2025 filing reportedly included a roughly 100-square-meter Buenos Aires property, two vehicles, US$20,000 cash, about US$65,562 in Argentine savings accounts and peso deposits. Nominal peso increases do not by themselves demonstrate comparable real enrichment. No consulted court finding established illicit enrichment.

## Political, Religious and Intellectual Development

### Economics and state theory

Milei treats individual life, liberty and property as prior to the state. His recurrent intellectual lineage is:

- Adam Smith and classical liberalism;
- Carl Menger’s subjective value theory;
- Ludwig von Mises’s praxeology and anti-interventionism;
- Friedrich Hayek’s knowledge problem and critique of central planning;
- Murray Rothbard’s anarcho-capitalism and monopoly theory;
- Milton Friedman and Robert Lucas on money and expectations;
- Alberto Benegas Lynch (h) and Jesús Huerta de Soto as living teachers or mentors.

He has described state taxation as theft, politicians as a parasitic caste and socialism as coercion. In office he accepted state practices—including managed exchange rates, taxes and negotiated intervention—that pure anarcho-capitalism would reject, explaining the discrepancy as practical sequencing.

### Social and historical positions

Milei opposes abortion and has proposed a referendum on the 2020 legalization law. He opposes gender quotas, identity-based public policy and state-financed sex education. He has rejected the concept of human-caused climate change in some statements, while later government language was less categorical.

During the 2023 campaign he said private organ markets merited philosophical discussion and treated the sale of children as an extreme hypothetical illustrating limits on market exchange. Opponents presented these remarks as policy proposals; Milei later denied proposing child sales, and organ commercialization did not become government policy.

Milei and Villarruel questioned the symbolic figure of **30,000 disappeared** under the 1976–1983 dictatorship, citing documented case lists closer to 8,000–9,000. Human-rights organizations maintain that 30,000 is a historically grounded estimate and symbol reflecting incomplete clandestine records. Milei acknowledged state crimes but promoted the concept of a “complete memory” including guerrilla victims. Claims that he directly defended the dictatorship are therefore disputed; his administration’s revision of commemorative language is documented.

### Religion

Raised Roman Catholic, Milei publicly professed belief in God but attacked Pope Francis before the presidency, calling him politically sympathetic to communism and using insulting language. After meeting Francis in February 2024, Milei said he had reconsidered and established a positive relationship.

Beginning around 2021, Rabbi **Shimon Axel Wahnish** taught Milei Torah. Milei visited the grave of Chabad-Lubavitch leader Menachem Mendel Schneerson in New York and prayed at the Western Wall in Jerusalem. He considered conversion to Judaism but said presidential duties on the Sabbath complicated it. As of the research cutoff, credible reporting continued to describe him as interested in conversion, not conclusively converted. Wahnish became Argentina’s ambassador to Israel.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations and Unresolved Questions

### Plagiarism and attribution

**Documented textual overlap / disputed characterization:** Investigations identified extensive passages in newspaper columns and *Pandenomics* resembling or reproducing work by Murray Rothbard, Henry Hazlitt, Ludwig von Mises and Mexican scientists without conventional attribution. Mexican researcher **Guillermo J. Escalante** and colleagues were associated with a legal complaint concerning material originally published by Universidad Autónoma del Estado de México.

Further reporting identified passages in *Capitalismo, socialismo y la trampa neoclásica* closely matching work by Chilean economists and an Argentine CONICET researcher. Milei answered that newspaper columns were popularization, traditionally lacked academic footnotes, earned him no money, and that he opposed intellectual-property doctrine. That response disputes culpability but not every documented textual match. No final criminal conviction was located.

**2026 AI-paper controversy:** A mathematical growth paper promoted by Milei and Demian Reidel was alleged by critics to contain AI-generated prose, elementary or malformed constructions and unreliable citations. Reidel denied plagiarism and defended the results as novel. This remained an **academic controversy**, not a court finding.

### CoinX

In 2021–2022 Milei promoted **CoinX**, a digital-asset investment platform, after visiting its offices. Investors later alleged fraud when payments stopped; its operator was investigated and detained in a separate criminal case. Milei said he had been paid for promotional activity and denied participation in the business. Complaints were filed against him. The promotion is documented; knowing participation in investor fraud has not been judicially established in the consulted record.

### Alleged sale of candidacies

In 2023 former supporters alleged that LLA intermediaries demanded payments, sometimes reported as US$10,000–50,000, for campaign places or access. Federal electoral authorities opened a preliminary investigation. Milei and Karina denied selling candidacies and said requested funds covered campaign expenses. The claims remain **allegations/investigative matters**; no consulted final judgment established that Milei sold offices.

### $LIBRA cryptocurrency affair

This is the most serious direct investigation of Milei’s presidency.

**Documented facts:**

- At 19:01 on **14 February 2025**, Milei’s X account promoted the newly launched $LIBRA token and supplied its contract information, presenting it as a private initiative to finance Argentine businesses.
- The token’s price rose sharply and then collapsed as insiders or early wallets withdrew substantial value.
- Milei deleted the post and said he had not known the project’s details.
- Promoters including **Hayden Mark Davis**, **Mauricio Novelli**, **Manuel Terrones Godoy** and **Sergio Morales** had contacts or meetings with presidential personnel.
- Argentine federal prosecutors opened a criminal investigation into possible fraud, influence trafficking, bribery, abuse of authority and incompatible negotiations. Civil litigation was also brought abroad.
- Congress created an investigative commission. Its opposition-controlled majority concluded that Milei and Karina had misused presidential authority and forwarded material for further investigation.
- A presidential investigative unit was created and then dissolved after the executive said its work was complete. The Office of Anticorruption concluded that Milei’s post was personal activity and found no ethics violation; critics disputed the office’s independence and reasoning.

**Allegations and unresolved evidence:** Digital forensics reported numerous communications around the launch among Milei’s circle and Novelli. A file reportedly found on Novelli’s telephone described a possible US$5 million arrangement for presidential support. The existence and provenance of the file were reported; proof that Milei accepted, signed or received such payment had not been established by final judgment as of the cutoff.

By March 2026 congressional investigators accused prosecutor Eduardo Taiano of delay or obstruction. Government representatives emphasized that Milei had not been convicted and disputed assertions that he knowingly participated in a “rug pull.” The legally unresolved alternatives include knowing promotion, influence trading, negligent endorsement or deception of Milei by promoters.

### ANDIS disability-agency bribery investigation

In August 2025 leaked recordings attributed to former disability-agency director **Diego Spagnuolo** described alleged kickbacks on medicine purchases, naming Karina Milei and presidential associate Eduardo “Lule” Menem. Police raided ANDIS and supplier **Suizo Argentina** properties; prosecutors investigated procurement, bribery and related offenses.

Spagnuolo had frequent access to Milei and reportedly said he warned the president. The recordings’ content and investigation are documented; whether every recording was authentic, whether Milei received a warning, and whether Karina or Javier participated remained unresolved. No final conviction of either Milei sibling was located.

### Treatment of Congress, courts and press

Opponents and legal scholars accused Milei of governing excessively through emergency decrees and delegated powers. Courts blocked portions of his labor decree; congressional conflict over DNU 70 continued. These are constitutional disputes, not proof that the entire program was unlawful.

Milei routinely singled out journalists and outlets with insults and accusations of corruption. Press organizations documented online harassment and restrictions at official events. In August 2026 he used repeated scatological insults against journalists and opponents. The speech is documented; competing interpretations characterize it as either direct political expression or intimidation amplified by state power.

### Protest policing

Security Minister Bullrich’s protocol restricted roadway blockades and shifted policing costs toward organizers. During pension, university and labor protests, police used tear gas, rubber projectiles and physical force. Journalists and elderly demonstrators were injured. Government accounts emphasized projectiles, vandalism and unlawful blockades; civil-liberties organizations alleged disproportionate and indiscriminate force. Investigations of particular incidents should not be conflated with a final finding that Milei personally ordered unlawful assaults.

### Discriminatory or threatening speech complaints

After Milei’s 2025 Davos address attacked “woke” ideology and linked extreme versions of gender activism with abuse of children, lawyers and organizations filed criminal and discrimination complaints. Filing a complaint does not establish guilt. No final conviction was found.

### Cabinet turnover and internal power

The removal of Nicolás Posse, Diana Mondino and numerous other officials generated accounts of a closed decision structure dominated by Karina Milei and Santiago Caputo. High turnover is documented; claims of coercive psychological control or occult decision-making are interpretations or rumor.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda and Disputed Narratives

### Conan’s political mission

**Story:** Milei’s original English mastiff, Conan, supposedly communicated after death through a medium and conveyed a divine mission that Milei should become president.

**Appearance and circulation:** The account became prominent through Juan Luis González’s 2023 unauthorized biography *El loco*, interviews with former associates, and international campaign profiles.

**Evidence:** Conan existed and died of spinal cancer in October 2017. Milei consulted people associated with animal communication or mediumship, according to multiple attributed accounts. When asked directly whether he spoke telepathically with Conan, Milei gave teasing or evasive answers rather than an unequivocal factual denial. No independently verifiable evidence supports supernatural communication.

**Status:** **Reported belief/allegation and political mythology**, not empirically established fact. It reinforced both supporters’ image of an unconventional providential leader and opponents’ claims of instability.

### The five clones

**Documented core:** US cloning firms confirmed that cells from Conan were preserved and that five cloned puppies were produced in 2018. Names publicly associated with the dogs include **Murray, Milton, Robert and Lucas**, honoring economists Murray Rothbard, Milton Friedman and Robert Lucas; Milei has also treated one clone as Conan.

**Dispute:** Public images usually showed four dogs, while Milei spoke of five. Speculation that one died, never existed or is deliberately concealed remains **unverified rumor**. The laboratory record supports five births, not necessarily five surviving adult dogs.

### “The dogs are cabinet advisers”

Statements that each dog advises Milei on a policy specialty originated in biographical accounts and jokes that Milei sometimes encouraged. There is no documentary evidence that governmental decisions were literally delegated to animals. This is best classified as **reported personal mythology amplified by satire**.

### “El León” and prophetic destiny

Milei and supporters developed a lion identity through his hair, zodiac sign, campaign songs and biblical language. “The forces of heaven” became a movement label drawn from a phrase attributed to the Maccabees. Milei’s rise from commentator to president was narrated by supporters as providential victory; detractors treated it as a personality cult. These are competing political mythologies built around documented symbolism.

### “Madman,” “fascist,” “libertarian savior”

“Madman” began as a nickname and was institutionalized by hostile and critical biographies. “Fascist” is used polemically by opponents, while supporters call him the first genuinely libertarian head of state. His economics derive principally from anti-state Austrian and anarcho-capitalist literature, while his security, nationalist alliances and executive style differ from classical anti-authoritarian libertarianism. These labels describe interpretive frameworks, not self-proving factual categories.

## Present Status and Developing Legacy

As of 3 September 2026 Milei is alive, serving the third year of a constitutionally four-year presidential term ending in December 2027.

His administration has:

- produced rapid disinflation and fiscal balance;
- enacted extensive deregulation and investment incentives;
- initially deepened recession and poverty before later statistical improvement;
- weakened or restructured public agencies, subsidies, universities, science, media and cultural institutions;
- expanded the electoral strength of LLA in 2025;
- moved Argentine foreign policy sharply toward the United States and Israel;
- generated continuing constitutional, labor, human-rights and corruption disputes;
- made Argentina the first national test of an openly anarcho-capitalist-derived electoral movement.

Supporter mythology presents the recovery as “the Argentine miracle” and Milei as a world-historical enemy of socialism. Hostile mythology presents him as a psychologically controlled frontman, fascist dictator or foreign financial instrument. The documentary record supports neither complete mythology: he has exercised real personal authority within constitutional electoral institutions, while depending heavily on family, advisers, congressional allies, international finance and US political backing.

Final assessment of his economic transformation, institutional effects and unresolved investigations—including $LIBRA and ANDIS—cannot yet be made.
