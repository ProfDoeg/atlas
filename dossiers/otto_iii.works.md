## Works by Otto III

### Diplomas and governmental texts

- **984–1002:** *Ottonis III diplomata (The Diplomas of Otto III), nos. 1–431*. This is the complete critical corpus of grants, confirmations, judgments, privileges and mandates issued in Otto’s name, rather than 431 personally handwritten compositions; royal beneficiaries, petitioners, notaries and chancery scribes participated in making them. The edition includes lost documents, revised texts, doubtful pieces and outright forgeries, so its last number must not be mistaken for a count of authentic surviving originals. Individual status and dating are recorded in the [MGH critical edition](https://www.dmgh.de/mgh_dd_o_ii__dd_o_iii/) and its [continuously updated corrections](https://www.mgh-bibliothek.de/etc/dd/index.htm).

- **995:** *Meissen boundary charter* (MGH DO III 186). Ostensibly a diploma defining the diocese of Meissen, it belongs to Otto’s documentary corpus only as an attributed act: the transmitted text is a later fabrication or extensive falsification and cannot safely be treated as something his chancery made. Its disputed status is registered in the MGH edition and corresponding *Regesta Imperii* apparatus.

- **1001:** *Capitulare Ticinense* (MGH DO III 388). A legislative protocol issued at Pavia for recovering alienated ecclesiastical property and regulating its restitution; it acts upon institutions rather than merely describing policy. Scholarship connects it with Otto’s transformation of Pomposa into an imperial abbey, but its formulation remains the work of ruler, advisers and chancery collectively ([study of Pomposa and the capitulary](https://riviste.unimi.it/index.php/SSMD/en/article/view/23209)).

### Seals and political manifestations

- **983–996:** Otto III’s royal seal types. These wax-seal matrices present the child and then adolescent king through inherited ruler imagery; they are serial instruments of authentication, not autonomous portraits personally carved by Otto. Posse’s standard catalogue distinguishes successive types, while the MGH collection preserves casts and links them to the documents on which they occur ([MGH seal catalogue](https://data.mgh.de/databases/seals/bin/seal_list.html)).

- **996–997:** Otto III’s standing and enthroned imperial seal types. These replace the earlier bust with the emperor first standing and then seated *in majestate*; Posse identifies the enthroned design as the beginning of a durable imperial seal formula, but the matrices themselves were court products rather than Otto’s handiwork ([Posse catalogue discussion](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Die_Siegel_der_deutschen_Kaiser_und_K%C3%B6nige_Band_5/Siegelbild_und_Siegelform)).

- **998–1000:** *Renovatio imperii Romanorum* lead bulla. A seal and a miniature manifesto at once, bearing Otto’s imperial image and the programmatic legend “renewal of the empire of the Romans”; it first appears in April 998. Whether this slogan proves the existence of a coherent, comprehensive “Renovatio” project remains disputed, so the bulla is firmer evidence than the reconstructed political system often built around it ([Journal of Medieval History study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03044181.2017.1285246); [survey of the controversy](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304418198000165)).

- **1001–1002:** *Aurea Roma* lead bulla. Otto’s final bulla replaced the earlier renovatio legend with an image and invocation of “Golden Rome”; the transition is first securely attested in the documentary sequence around MGH DD O III 389–390. It is a political manifestation attached to diplomas, not a freestanding authored text ([MGH diplomatic corpus](https://www.dmgh.de/mgh_dd_o_ii__dd_o_iii/)).

### Coinage

- **983–1002:** Coin types issued in Otto III’s name. This is a distributed monetary obra—dies and repeated silver impressions produced at several mints—not a single object; attribution is especially difficult because many coins say only “Otto” and may belong to Otto I, II or III. Hermann Dannenberg’s catalogue remains the conventional typological reference, while museum catalogues preserve individual attributions such as the Berlin coin assigned to Otto III ([Berlin State Museums catalogue](https://search.smb.museum/object/obj-2362390)).

- **from 983/984, continuing after 1002:** *Otto-Adelheid-Pfennige*. A large family of pennies naming Otto and his grandmother Adelaide and probably initiated during their joint government; mint, chronology and even which “Otto” originated particular groups have long been contested. Modern scholarship generally restores the earliest stages to Otto III but recognizes that production continued beyond his and Adelaide’s deaths, so the whole series cannot simply be called his personal creation ([Berlin State Museums](https://search.smb.museum/object/obj-2362390); [modern numismatic study](https://en.numista.com/catalogue/catalogues/pdf/wiadomosci_numizmatyczne_20267_01_Suchodolski.pdf)).

### Manuscript commissions and gifts

- **c. 996:** *Liuthar Gospels* (Aachen Cathedral Treasury, inv. 25). An illuminated gospel book whose dedication image has the monk Liuthar presenting the codex to Otto; it is therefore a courtly gift or commission and a Gesamtkunstwerk of scripture, gold-ground painting, liturgy and imperial image rather than a text authored by the emperor. Its likely Reichenau origin and even the precise production centre remain scholarly questions ([UNESCO nomination dossier](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany_reichenau.pdf); [Aachen UNESCO account](https://www.unescoclub-aachen.de/seite/628219/weltdokumentenerbe-%E2%80%9Ememory-of-the-world%E2%80%9C.html)).

- **c. 998–1001:** *Gospel Book of Otto III* (Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Clm 4453). A lavishly illuminated gospel codex conventionally treated as an imperial commission and containing Otto’s ruler portrait; its makers were Reichenau-associated scribes and painters, not Otto. Its exact date, production site and route into Henry II’s possession are reconstructed rather than documented by a surviving commission ([Smarthistory object entry](https://smarthistory.org/gospel-book-of-otto-iii/); [UNESCO nomination dossier](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/germany-reichenau.pdf)).

- **c. 984–991:** *Prayerbook of Otto III* (Munich, Bayerische Staatsbibliothek, Clm 30111). This private devotional manuscript was made for the young king, but probably commissioned by Archbishop Willigis, Theophanu or Otto’s guardians; it therefore resists inclusion as Otto’s own work and belongs here only as a court creation addressed to and formative of him ([Library of Congress description](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021667997); [Journal of Medieval History study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304418101000112)).

- **c. 1000:** *Lothair Cross*. A jeweled processional cross associated with Otto’s Aachen patronage and sometimes said to have been commissioned or donated by Otto III, alternatively by Theophanu; neither attribution is demonstrable. It should consequently be treated as a disputed court commission, not securely as Otto’s made thing ([review of Eliza Garrison’s catalogue of Otto III’s patronage](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/17748/0)).

### Buildings and monastic foundations

- **c. 998–1001:** Otto III’s imperial palace in Rome. A built political project intended to establish a permanent imperial presence inside the city; contemporary evidence is sparse, the building has not been securely identified archaeologically, and proposed locations include the Palatine, Aventine and Lateran. Recent study dates its realization no earlier than April 998 and no later than January 1001, while leaving its site unresolved ([architectural-historical study](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/1011989); [survey of the location dispute](https://www.persee.fr/doc/mefr_1123-9883_2004_num_116_2_9335)).

- **998–1001:** *Church of Saint Adalbert*, later *San Bartolomeo all’Isola*, Rome. Otto founded a church on Tiber Island to receive relics associated with Adalbert of Prague and other saints; subsequent rebuilding means that the present basilica is not simply Otto’s surviving building. It is simultaneously architecture, reliquary and political-memorial foundation ([study identifying it as Otto’s Roman church foundation](https://czasopisma.bg.ug.edu.pl/index.php/gdanskie_studia_prawnicze/article/download/12691/11348)).

- **1001:** *Monastery of Saint Adalbert at Pereo*. A monastic and missionary foundation near Ravenna, consecrated in Otto’s presence and intended for a community connected with Adalbert and Slavic mission. The project had taken physical and institutional form before Otto’s death, although its subsequent history and exact remains do not survive as a unitary work ([study of the Pomposa–Pereo project](https://riviste.unimi.it/index.php/SSMD/en/article/view/23209); [Pomposa research project](https://www.cantierepomposa.it/index.php/it/3-ottone-iii-e-il-diploma-del-1001-la-trasformazione-in-monastero-imperiale)).

### Institutional creations and projects

- **998–1002:** *Renovatio imperii Romanorum*. A political project inferred from a seal legend, Otto’s Roman residence, titles, ceremonies and ecclesiastical policy rather than a single surviving manifesto. Scholars dispute its coherence and even whether Otto possessed the systematic programme traditionally attributed to him; it is therefore an unfinished and partly historiographical “work,” terminated by his death ([“Ideals and action in the reign of Otto III”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304418198000165); [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz74084.html)).

- **1000:** *Ecclesiastical province of Gniezno*. An institutional creation made jointly with Pope Sylvester II, Bolesław Chrobry and assembled churchmen: the new archbishopric was accompanied by suffragan sees at Kraków, Wrocław and Kołobrzeg. Otto’s exact powers, Bolesław’s resulting status and claims that the meeting amounted to a coronation remain disputed, so it cannot be represented as Otto’s unilateral design ([Roman Michałowski, *The Gniezno Summit*](https://monografie.fnp.org.pl/book/239/the-gniezno-summit); [study of the alleged coronation](https://journals.pan.pl/Content/136285/04-06_Jasinski_EN.pdf)).

- **1001:** *Imperial abbey of Santa Maria di Pomposa*. Otto transformed an existing monastery rather than creating its buildings from nothing; the obra is consequently a new legal and political status enacted through diploma and property policy. Scholarship treats it as one part of the linked Pomposa–Pereo programme, whose further development Otto’s death interrupted ([“From monasterium to imperial abbey”](https://riviste.unimi.it/index.php/SSMD/en/article/view/23209)).
