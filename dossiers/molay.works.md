## Works by Jacques de Molay

### Consultative memoranda

- **July–August 1306:** *Memorandum on the recovery of the Holy Land* (*Consilium super negotio Terrae sanctae*). A strategic project submitted to Pope Clement V, distinguishing a preliminary expedition from a general crusade; it is a consultative protocol rather than a literary treatise. Older editions wrongly dated it 1311 and joined it to the following memorandum as one text; modern scholarship separates the two. [Petit; Molinier, no. 3079; Demurger]

- **1306–1307:** *Memorandum against the union of the Temple and the Hospital* (*Consilium super unione Templariorum et Hospitalariorum*). A policy argument prepared for Clement V opposing the proposed merger of the military orders. Its exact date remains uncertain, and its transmission attached to the crusade memorandum long obscured its status as a distinct work. [Molinier, no. 3079; Lizerand; Demurger]

### Official correspondence and acts

- **1292–1307:** *Surviving letters and administrative acts issued as grand master of the Temple*. This is an institutional epistolary obra rather than a single authored “book”: slightly more than two dozen documents survive—twenty in the Archive of the Crown of Aragon, three in the Vatican archives, two in the former Public Record Office in London, and one in Spain’s Archivo Histórico Nacional—among them letters to James II of Aragon and commands to Templar officials. They were issued under Molay’s authority but normally drafted by secretaries, so “authorship” here means juridical responsibility, not autograph composition; Finke’s *Acta Aragonensia* is the principal scholarly edition for the Aragonese material. [Archive of the Crown of Aragon; Finke]

- **21 January 1295 or 1296:** *Letter to Pere de Sant Just, commander of Granyena*. An administrative letter ordering assistance for Molay’s projected passage to Cyprus and announcing a general chapter beforehand. Finke tentatively assigned it to 1295, whereas Alain Demurger dates it to 1296; the disagreement should not be concealed. [Finke, III, no. 18; Archive of the Crown of Aragon]

- **21 January 1296:** *Letter to Ramon de Bell-lloc, commander at Torres de Segre*. A sealed command concerning preparations and material assistance for Molay’s return to Cyprus, discovered in the Archive of the Crown of Aragon after Finke’s edition; its wording closely parallels the letter to Pere de Sant Just. The surviving original is not autograph: an unidentified secretary wrote it, while Molay authorized and sealed it. [Archive of the Crown of Aragon]

- **8 September 1307:** *Appointment of Ximeno de Lenda as provincial master of Aragon and Catalonia*. An executive act addressed collectively to the Templars of the two provinces, commanding obedience to the new provincial master and authorizing him to discipline offenders. It functions as a protocol—language that creates an office and directs conduct—rather than as descriptive prose. [Forey, no. XLVI; Hamburg Templar Sources]

### Statements preserved by judicial record

- **24 October 1307:** *Deposition before Guillaume de Paris*. A sworn oral confession recorded by inquisitorial notaries. It is a manifestation attributable to Molay, but it resists classification as his “work”: the surviving text is a hostile institution’s documentary construction of answers elicited under imprisonment, not a manuscript he composed. [Lizerand]

- **17–20 August 1308:** *Statements at Chinon*. Molay’s answers, confession, request for pardon, and reconciliation with the Church survive in the notarial record commonly called the Chinon Parchment. This is likewise recorded testimony rather than an authored text, and the instrument incorporates the speech of several Templar dignitaries as well as the actions of the papal commissioners. [Frale]

- **26 November 1309:** *Appearance before the papal commission in Paris*. A recorded declaration in which Molay declined to conduct a personal defence without appropriate counsel and sought an audience with the pope. The wording belongs to the commissioners’ procès-verbal; what Molay made was an oral forensic intervention, not the document that preserves it. [Michelet; Josserand]

- **18 March 1314:** *Revocation of the sentence and profession of the Temple’s innocence*. Chroniclers report that Molay publicly repudiated his earlier confession immediately before his execution. No contemporary text authored or dictated by him survives, and accounts differ in wording and detail; the famous curse summoning Clement V and Philip IV to divine judgment is a later legendary manifestation, not an authenticated work by Molay. [William of Nangis continuation; Josserand; Demurger]

### Material and institutional marks

- **By 1296:** *Grand-master’s seal*. The partially preserved black-wax impression attached to the letter to Ramon de Bell-lloc shows parts of a horse, a Greek cross, and the legend of the “knights of Christ.” It is an official manifestation of Molay’s authority, but not securely a personal design or an object physically made by him; the evidence warrants calling it his employed seal, not his artwork. [Archive of the Crown of Aragon]
