## Works by Francisco Pizarro González

### Epistolary works

Pizarro did not write these letters in his own hand: surviving documents were composed by secretaries and validated with his marks or rubric. They are nevertheless his works as dictated or authorized acts of government; the traditional autograph attribution has been explicitly disputed.

- **8 June 1533:** *Letter to Juan de Sámano requesting support for royal grants and an enlargement of New Castile*. A petitionary letter sent from Cajamarca and the first item in Levillier’s documentary corpus; its “signature” does not establish holograph authorship because Pizarro employed scribes and apparently could not write his name unaided. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...), [discussion of the signatures](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/vidas-de-los-espanoles-celebres--0/html/fee59b24-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_58.htm)

- **1 January 1535:** *Letter to Charles V concerning Pedro de Alvarado’s intervention in Quito*. A governmental report from Pachacámac describing the accommodation reached with Alvarado and Diego de Almagro; it is an authorized secretarial text rather than a literary chronicle or autograph manuscript. [Levillier edition](https://kuprienko.info/cartas-del-marques-don-francisco-pizarro-1533-1541/), [Archivo General de Indias record](https://pares.cultura.gob.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/contiene/123326)

- **29 June 1535:** *Letter to Juan Vázquez de Molina*. A patronage petition written from Cuzco, asking Molina to intercede with the emperor and help perpetuate Pizarro’s estate and memory; the manuscript is rubricated but not securely holograph. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...), [Archivo General de Indias description](https://pares.cultura.gob.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/description/125516)

- **28 August 1537:** *Letter to Fray Tomás de Berlanga concerning the conflict with Diego de Almagro*. A long political and military report sent from the valley of Añasco, defending Pizarro’s conduct during the civil conflict. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...), [Archivo General de Indias record](https://pares.cultura.gob.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/find?idDescContiene=123326&idFI=16)

- **9 April 1538:** *Letter to Pedro Mato*. A private-administrative assurance that Mato’s encomienda and property would be protected during his journey to Spain; its classificatory difficulty is precisely that it is simultaneously correspondence, patronage, and an act affecting colonial possession. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...), [Archivo General de Indias record](https://pares.cultura.gob.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/contiene/123326)

- **28 February 1539:** *Letter to Charles V accompanying six emeralds*. A letter-object or diplomatic gift from Cuzco: the text announces and interprets the stones sent with it, so the complete work was originally both writing and material offering. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...)

- **28 February 1539:** *Letter to Charles V recommending Cristóbal de Burgos*. A testimonial and petition for royal reward, distinct from the emerald letter despite sharing its date, place, addressee, and secretarial mode of production. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...)

- **13 February 1541:** *Letter to Juan Pérez de Guevara*. A patronage letter praising Guevara’s diligence and recommending Juan Martínez for support in forthcoming distributions. It survives as a rubricated gubernatorial communication, not an autograph composition. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...)

### Protocol

- **4 June 1540:** *Instruction to Diego Verdejo for the visitation from Chicama to Túcume*. A protocol prescribing how Verdejo was to enumerate Indigenous households, territories, production, mobility, and tribute. It was issued jointly by Pizarro as governor and Vicente de Valverde as bishop and protector, drafted and authenticated by their secretary; sole authorship should therefore not be claimed. [Levillier edition](https://studylib.es/doc/4999869/cartas-del-marqu%C3%A9s-don-francisco-pizarro.-francisco-a.-de...)

### Legal and testamentary obra

- **10 March 1526:** *Company agreement of Francisco Pizarro, Diego de Almagro, and Hernando de Luque*. A collective legal instrument defining the partners’ projected expedition and division of risks and gains. Pizarro did not sign it himself: the instrument says that witnesses signed for Pizarro and Almagro because they could not sign, making it an enacted agreement rather than an autograph text. [Documentary discussion](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/vidas-de-los-espanoles-celebres--0/html/fee59b24-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_58.htm)

- **26 July 1529:** *Capitulación de Toledo*. A royal contract between the Crown and Pizarro authorizing conquest, settlement, and government in New Castile. It is a jointly constituted juridical project rather than Pizarro’s sole composition; the surviving instrument notes that he used his personal mark because he could not sign. [Documentary discussion](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/vidas-de-los-espanoles-celebres--0/html/fee59b24-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_58.htm), [Lohmann Villena collected edition record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Testimonio.html?id=S3QaAAAAYAAJ)

- **5 June 1537:** *Testament of Francisco Pizarro*. A fifty-clause dispositive text dictated in Lima, creating inheritances, chaplaincies, pious obligations, and projects for objects and buildings. Later proceedings refer to this testament rather than to a new deathbed will, so an independently completed “testament of 1541” should not be silently invented. [Raúl Porras Barrenechea’s documentary study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/descargaPdf/el-testamento-de-francisco-pizarro-conquistador-del-peru/), [Lohmann Villena collected edition record](https://www.mcu.es/ccbae/ca/consulta/registro.do?control=BAB20060055478)

- **1537:** *Project for an Adoration-of-the-Magi altarpiece in Castile*. A testamentary commission for a retablo to be executed by excellent craftsmen, not a surviving object made by Pizarro’s hands. Its status is therefore project rather than completed obra, and the available testamentary evidence does not establish that it was executed. [Porras Barrenechea’s study of the testament](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/descargaPdf/el-testamento-de-francisco-pizarro-conquistador-del-peru/)

### Urban foundations and colonial spatial works

These were juridical and ceremonial remakings of inhabited Indigenous landscapes, not creations from empty ground. Their attribution is often collective: surveyors, notaries, clergy, soldiers, Indigenous laborers, existing settlements, and sometimes Pizarro’s lieutenants materially produced what tradition calls his “foundations.”

- **Between May and August 1532:** *San Miguel*. A colonial municipality established by Pizarro at Tangarará and subsequently relocated several times. The year and founder are secure, but the exact day remains disputed; 15 July is a later commemorative convention and should not be presented as an uncontested creation date. [INEI history](https://proyectos.inei.gob.pe/web/biblioineipub/bancopub/Est/Lib0260/cap-01.htm), [Congressional notice of the dating dispute](https://www2.congreso.gob.pe/Sicr/Congresistas/2006/20060264.nsf/vf02web/363C8C2E249073F405257656006C4B13/%24FILE/np_050527.PDF)

- **23 March 1534:** *The Spanish city of Cuzco*. A legal and ceremonial refoundation imposed upon the existing Inca capital, including an act of foundation, public proclamation, municipal organization, and projected redistribution of urban space. Calling Pizarro the maker without naming the pre-existing city would falsify the object: his obra was a colonial reclassification and reorganization of Cuzco, not Cuzco itself. [Municipality of Cuzco study](https://centrohistorico.cusco.gob.pe/media/ambientes_urbanos/Informe_Plaza_de_Armas.pdf), [Peruvian cultural-history account](https://repositorio.cultura.gob.pe/bitstream/handle/CULTURA/1461/RUNA%2002.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

- **25 April 1534:** *Santa Fe de Hatun Xauxa (Jauja)*. A municipal foundation and first Spanish capital of New Castile, enacted within an already inhabited Andean landscape. Its life as capital was brief because Pizarro transferred the governmental center to Lima. [Municipal history of Jauja](https://munijauja.gob.pe/historia_jauja.php)

- **Late 1534–5 March 1535:** *Trujillo*. A delegated and subsequently formalized municipal work: Diego de Almagro initiated the settlement and layout, while Pizarro completed or ratified its juridical foundation and cabildo. Attribution solely to Pizarro suppresses this divided making. [Peruvian Academy of History study](https://revistahistorica.academiahistoria.org.pe/index.php/revista-historica/article/view/3967), [survey of early Spanish-American urbanism](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=9392122)

- **18 January 1535:** *Ciudad de los Reyes (Lima)*. A foundation act, municipal institution, plaza, street-and-block scheme, and capital project laid over the Indigenous settlement and hydraulic landscape governed by Taulichusco. Pizarro made the Spanish juridical city and chose its site; he did not make the pre-existing settlement, roads, canals, or valley that enabled it. [*El damero de Pizarro*](https://publicacioneslima.pe/munilibro-8-el-damero-de-pizarro-el-trazo-y-la-forja-de-lima/), [surviving foundation-act description](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acta_de_Fundaci%C3%B3n_de_Lima)

- **19 January 1539:** *San Juan de la Frontera de Huamanga*. A delegated municipal project founded at Pizarro’s command in present-day Huamanguilla and later transferred to another site. Because the ceremony was executed under his mandate rather than simply by his hand, it is more accurately a gubernatorial project than an individual object. [Municipality of Huamanga](https://www.munihuamanga.gob.pe/huamanga-celebra-482-de-fundacion/)

- **1539–1540:** *Huánuco, La Plata/Chuquisaca, and Arequipa foundation projects*. Settlements ordered under Pizarro’s government but executed by lieutenants; Arequipa, for example, was founded by Garcí Manuel de Carbajal on 15 August 1540. These resist treatment as three personal “works”: they are delegated manifestations of his colonial program and collective acts with distinct local makers. [Archivo General de la Nación study](https://revista.agn.gob.pe/ojs/index.php/ragn/article/download/72/84?inline=1), [Arequipa municipal history](https://municharacato.gob.pe/origenes-del-distrito/)

### Buildings and architectural projects

- **1535–1541:** *Pizarro’s house or palace in Lima*. A residence and seat of government built on an Indigenous sacred or elite site beside the Rímac. The sixteenth-century complex continued to be altered after Pizarro’s death and the present Government Palace is a much later replacement, so neither the complete historic palace nor the modern building is straightforwardly his architectural work. [PUCP study, *El palacio de Pizarro del siglo XVI al XX*](https://repositorio.pucp.edu.pe/items/f9bc158d-ff54-4af2-819b-05a5e4d024cd), [official history of the Government Palace](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/presidencia/informes-publicaciones/19921-historia-de-palacio)

- **1535–1540:** *First church of Lima*. A collaborative sacred building for which Pizarro allocated a site, laid the first stone, and ceremonially carried the first timber. It became Lima’s first cathedral, but later cathedral campaigns replaced it; the present cathedral must not be catalogued as a building designed or wholly made by Pizarro. [Peruvian cultural-history account](https://repositorio.cultura.gob.pe/bitstream/handle/CULTURA/490/LIMA%20LA%20CIUDAD%20DE%20LOS%20REYES.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

### Expeditions as collective projects

- **1524–1525:** *First expedition south from Panama*. An exploratory and commercial project organized with Diego de Almagro and Hernando de Luque. It produced routes, observations, claims, violence, and failure rather than a single stable object; it therefore resists being called an individual “work.”

- **1526–1528:** *Second expedition and reconnaissance of the Pacific coast*. A collective maritime and overland project associated with Bartolomé Ruiz’s navigation and the continuation at Isla del Gallo. The later *Relación Sámano-Xerez* describes this undertaking but is not therefore a text authored by Pizarro. [Cusco Virtual Library edition of the early chronicles](https://bvirtual.culturacusco.gob.pe/items/show/33), [Lohmann Villena collected documentary edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/Testimonio.html?id=S3QaAAAAYAAJ)

- **1530–1541:** *Conquest, settlement, and government of New Castile*. A military, extractive, juridical, evangelical, and urban project pursued under the *Capitulación de Toledo*. It cannot responsibly be reduced to Pizarro’s personal obra: Spanish forces, African and Indigenous auxiliaries, rival conquistadors, royal officials, scribes, clergy, Indigenous polities, and coerced labor all made—and resisted—the resulting colonial order. [*Historia mínima del Perú*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctt14jxnbv), [Lohmann Villena collected documentary edition](https://www.mcu.es/ccbae/ca/consulta/registro.do?control=BAB20060055478)

### Bodily manifestation

- **c. 1478–1541; reidentified and reinstalled 1984–1985:** *Mortal remains attributed to Francisco Pizarro*. A wounded human body and later assemblage of skull and postcranial bones, not an intentional work by Pizarro; its resistance to the category *obra* is essential. The remains discovered in the Lima Cathedral crypt in 1977 were osteologically associated in 1984 and transferred to the public tomb in January 1985, but the identification was inferential—based on biological profile, historical provenance, articulation, and trauma rather than DNA—and later researchers have questioned its certainty. [Maples et al., “The death and mortal remains of Francisco Pizarro”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2668443/), [Benfer and McKern, bone-histomorphometry study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3511176/), [contemporary account of the 1985 reinstallation](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1985-02-10-mn-3334-story.html)
