## Works by Milton Friedman

### Edited and co-edited books

- **1935:** *The Ethics of Competition*. A collection of Frank H. Knight’s essays co-edited with Homer Jones, George Stigler, and W. Allen Wallis; Friedman made the editorial object, not its constituent essays. [Cole, A1](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1948:** *Sampling Inspection: Principles, Procedures, and Tables for Single, Double, and Sequential Sampling in Acceptance Inspection and Quality Control Based on Per Cent Defective*. A technical handbook co-edited with H. A. Freeman, Frederick Mosteller, and W. Allen Wallis. [Cole, A5](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1956:** *Studies in the Quantity Theory of Money*. An edited research collection containing Friedman’s programmatic restatement of quantity theory alongside studies by other economists. [Cole, A7](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)

### Authored and co-authored books, monographs, and collected essays

- **1939:** *Consumer Expenditures in the United States: Estimates for 1935–36*. A statistical research report co-authored with Hildegarde Kneeland and others. [Cole, A2](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1943:** *Taxing to Prevent Inflation: Techniques for Estimating Revenue Requirements*. A wartime fiscal-policy monograph made with Carl Shoup and Ruth P. Mack. [Cole, A3](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1945:** *Income from Independent Professional Practice*. An empirical economic study co-authored with Simon Kuznets. [Cole, A4](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1953:** *Essays in Positive Economics*. A collection of Friedman’s economic and methodological essays; several components had appeared earlier and therefore have lives as independent articles as well as chapters. [Cole, A6](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1957:** *A Theory of the Consumption Function*. A research monograph presenting and testing the permanent-income hypothesis. [Cole, A8](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1960:** *A Program for Monetary Stability*. A policy monograph developed from Friedman’s Fordham University lectures and functioning partly as a protocol for monetary institutions. [Cole, A9](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1962:** *Capitalism and Freedom*. A political-economic manifesto as well as an argumentative book, written with the acknowledged assistance of Rose D. Friedman. [Cole, A10](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1962:** *Price Theory: A Provisional Text*. A textbook manifestation of Friedman’s University of Chicago teaching; its provisional status matters because it was later expanded and revised. [Cole, A11](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1963:** *A Monetary History of the United States, 1867–1960*. A large-scale historical and statistical study co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz; portions later appeared separately, but those extracts are manifestations of this larger obra rather than wholly new compositions. [Cole, A12](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1963:** *Inflation: Causes and Consequences*. A short policy monograph subsequently incorporated into *Dollars and Deficits*. [Cole, A13](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1967:** *The Balance of Payments: Free versus Fixed Exchange Rates*. A paired debate-book with Robert V. Roosa rather than a single-author treatise. [Cole, A14](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1968:** *Dollars and Deficits: Inflation, Monetary Policy and the Balance of Payments*. A collection of previously made and newly gathered monetary-policy essays. [Cole, A15](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1969:** *The Optimum Quantity of Money and Other Essays*. A collection whose individual essays also remain separately identifiable works. [Cole, A16](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1969:** *Monetary vs. Fiscal Policy*. A structured debate made with Walter W. Heller; the disagreement, not sole authorship, is the organizing form. [Cole, A17](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1970:** *Monetary Statistics of the United States: Estimates, Sources, Methods*. A statistical reference work and methodological protocol co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz. [Cole, A18](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1972:** *An Economist’s Protest: Columns on Political Economy*. A book-form selection from Friedman’s periodical commentary, so the volume is a later manifestation of texts first made as columns. [Cole, A19](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1972:** *Social Security: Universal or Selective?* A debate-book made with Wilbur J. Cohen. [Cole, A20](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1973:** *Money and Economic Development: The Horowitz Lectures*. A lecture cycle subsequently made into a book. [Cole, A21](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1974:** *Milton Friedman’s Monetary Framework: A Debate with His Critics*. A composite debate volume edited by Robert J. Gordon: Friedman made the framework and replies, but not the critical contributions or the volume’s entire editorial form. [Cole, A22](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1975:** *There’s No Such Thing as a Free Lunch*. A collection of popular-economic writings, many previously published elsewhere. [Cole, A23](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1976:** *Price Theory*. The expanded and revised realization of the provisional 1962 textbook, not merely a reprint. [Cole, A24](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1978:** *Tax Limitation, Inflation and the Role of Government*. A policy tract assembled from Friedman’s arguments for limiting government revenue and expenditure. [Cole, A25](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1980:** *Free to Choose: A Personal Statement*. A political-economic manifesto co-authored with Rose D. Friedman and developed alongside the television project of the same name; book and series are related but distinct manifestations. [Cole, A26](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1982:** *Monetary Trends in the United States and the United Kingdom: Their Relation to Income, Prices and Interest Rates, 1867–1975*. A comparative monetary-history and statistical study co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz. [Cole, A27](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1983:** *Bright Promises, Dismal Performance: An Economist’s Protest*. A William R. Allen-edited collection of Friedman’s earlier writings; Friedman made the component texts, while the editor made this selection and arrangement. [Cole, A28](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1984:** *Tyranny of the Status Quo*. A public-policy book co-authored with Rose D. Friedman and connected to a television series; it operates as both analysis and political intervention. [Cole, A29](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1985:** *Five Essays*. A privately issued collection whose unity is editorial rather than that of a newly composed treatise. [Cole, A30](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1987:** *The Essence of Friedman*. A retrospective selection edited by Kurt R. Leube; the collected texts are Friedman’s, but the anthology as a shaped object is Leube’s editorial manifestation. [Cole, A31](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1991:** *Monetarist Economics*. A collection of writings that presents Friedman’s monetary economics as a coherent body of work. [Cole, A32](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1992:** *Money Mischief: Episodes in Monetary History*. A book of historical monetary case studies written for a broad readership. [Cole, A33](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1998:** *Two Lucky People: Memoirs*. A joint autobiography made with Rose D. Friedman; its plural voice resists attribution to Milton alone. [Cole, A34](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **2007:** *Milton Friedman on Economics: Selected Papers*. A posthumous collection: Friedman made its papers, but the book as selection and arrangement appeared after his death and should not be treated as an independently composed 2007 work. [Cole, A35](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)

### Separately issued extracts and altered manifestations

- **1965:** *The Great Contraction*. A separately issued manifestation of chapter 7 of *A Monetary History of the United States, 1867–1960*, co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz; it is an extract, not an independently originated study. [Cole, note to A12](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1980:** *From New Deal Banking Reform to World War II Inflation*. A separately issued manifestation of chapters 8–10 of *A Monetary History of the United States, 1867–1960*, again co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz. [Cole, note to A12](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)

### Articles, chapters, reviews, forewords, replies, and other short texts

- **1935–2009:** *Other Publications by Milton Friedman*. This is not a single text and resists being called one work: Cole’s scholarly bibliography enumerates 312 separately titled articles, chapters, reviews, discussions, replies, encyclopedia entries, introductions, forewords, and posthumously published pieces, beginning with “Professor Pigou’s Method for Measuring Elasticities of Demand from Budgetary Data” and ending with “Final Word.” Treating the corpus as an obra preserves its completeness without pretending that reprints are new creations; Cole records first publication, collaborators, and reprint relations item by item. [Cole, section B](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1935:** *Professor Pigou’s Method for Measuring Elasticities of Demand from Budgetary Data*. Friedman’s first listed scholarly article, an economic-methodological paper. [Cole, B1](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1937:** *The Use of Ranks to Avoid the Assumption of Normality Implicit in the Analysis of Variance*. A statistical article, with a supplementary appearance in 1939; the two appearances belong to one developing text. [Cole, B7](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1946:** *Roofs or Ceilings? The Current Housing Problem*. A policy pamphlet co-authored with George J. Stigler and directed against rent control. [Cole, B23](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1948:** *A Monetary and Fiscal Framework for Economic Stability*. A programmatic journal article proposing rule-governed stabilization institutions. [Cole, B30](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1948:** *The Utility Analysis of Choices Involving Risk*. A foundational scholarly article co-authored with L. J. Savage. [Cole, B31](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1949:** *The Marshallian Demand Curve*. A major price-theory article subsequently collected in *Essays in Positive Economics*. [Cole, B36](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1953:** *The Methodology of Positive Economics*. A methodological manifesto first presented within *Essays in Positive Economics* and later repeatedly anthologized. [Cole, B56](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1953:** *The Case for Flexible Exchange Rates*. A policy and theory essay first collected in *Essays in Positive Economics*. [Cole, B57](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1955:** *The Role of Government in Education*. A policy chapter introducing Friedman’s school-voucher proposal; because it recommends an institutional mechanism, it also functions as a protocol. [Cole, B67](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1956:** *The Quantity Theory of Money—A Restatement*. A programmatic chapter in *Studies in the Quantity Theory of Money* that redefined the modern monetarist research project. [Cole, B69](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1957:** *A Statistical Illusion in Judging Keynesian Models*. A methodological article co-authored with Gary S. Becker. [Cole, B71](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1958:** *Foreign Economic Aid: Means and Objectives*. A policy essay later collected in *The Essence of Friedman*. [Cole, B75](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1961:** *The Lag in Effect of Monetary Policy*. An empirical monetary-policy article. [Cole, B96](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1962:** *Should There Be an Independent Monetary Authority?* A chapter questioning discretionary central-bank independence and proposing rule-bound alternatives. [Cole, B102](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1963:** *Money and Business Cycles*. A major empirical article co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz. [Cole, B104](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1963:** *The Relative Stability of Monetary Velocity and the Investment Multiplier in the United States, 1897–1958*. A substantial empirical study co-authored with David Meiselman. [Cole, B107](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1966:** *The Minimum Wage: Who Pays?* A policy pamphlet co-authored with Yale Brozen. [Cole, B123](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1967:** *Why Not a Voluntary Army?* A policy chapter arguing for the abolition of conscription; it helped define the volunteer-army project but was not by itself the institutional change. [Cole, B130](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1968:** *The Role of Monetary Policy*. Friedman’s presidential address to the American Economic Association, appearing as a journal article and advancing the natural-rate argument. [Cole, B133](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1969:** *The Definition of Money: Net Wealth and Neutrality as Criteria*. A monetary-theory article co-authored with Anna J. Schwartz. [Cole, B139](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1970:** *A Theoretical Framework for Monetary Analysis*. A major theoretical article followed in the same volume by a correction or reply; a related monograph manifestation appeared later in 1970. [Cole, B146 and B160](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1970:** *The Social Responsibility of Business Is to Increase Its Profits*. A public argument published in *The New York Times Magazine*; more manifesto than neutral description, it was later reprinted in Friedman’s collections. [Cole, B151](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1970:** *The Counter-Revolution in Monetary Theory*. A Wincott Lecture issued as an Institute of Economic Affairs occasional paper and later collected elsewhere. [Cole, B155](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1977:** *Nobel Lecture: Inflation and Unemployment*. The published manifestation of Friedman’s 1976 Nobel memorial lecture, distinct from the live address. [Cole, section B](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1993:** *The “Plucking Model” of Business Fluctuations Revisited*. A late scholarly article returning to Friedman’s asymmetric model of economic fluctuations. [Cole, B283](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **1997:** *John Maynard Keynes*. An intellectual-biographical essay published by the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond. [Cole, B288](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **2001:** *How to Cure Health Care*. A public-policy essay proposing market-oriented changes to American health care. [Cole, B294](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **2005:** *A Natural Experiment in Monetary Policy Covering Three Episodes of Growth and Decline in the Economy and the Stock Market*. Friedman’s last listed sole-authored scholarly article published during his lifetime. [Cole, B305](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)
- **2009:** *Final Word*. A one-page posthumous contribution in an edited study of his methodology; its 2009 appearance is a manifestation, not evidence that Friedman composed it in that year. [Cole, B312](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf)

### Recorded commentary and lecture projects

- **1968–1977:** *Economics Cassette Series*. An audio periodical—206 recorded installments of Friedman’s commentary on current economic events—not a book and not reducible to its later transcripts. Hoover’s catalogue identifies the complete 206-part larger work. [Hoover sound-recording catalogue](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/groups/formats/category/Sound%20Recording)
- **1977–1978:** *Milton Friedman Speaks*. A recorded lecture series in which public addresses, question periods, sound recordings, and later video manifestations form a multimedia teaching project rather than a single fixed text. [Hoover, *Collected Works of Milton Friedman*](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/)

### Unbounded or incompletely catalogued bodies of work

- **1966–1984:** *Newsweek columns*. A recurring body of signed public commentary rather than one work; Cole deliberately excludes newspaper and news-magazine writing, while Hoover preserves hundreds of columns and op-eds. No claim of article-level completeness is possible from the scholarly bibliography alone. [Cole, scope note](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf); [Hoover overview](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/)
- **dates not fixed:** *Newspaper op-eds and public letters*. These are individual interventions scattered across newspapers, especially *The Wall Street Journal*; they resist treatment as a closed oeuvre because the standard scholarly bibliography excludes them and Hoover describes its holdings as extensive rather than exhaustive. [Cole, scope note](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf); [Hoover overview](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/)
- **dates not fixed:** *Speeches, lectures, and addresses*. These survive variously as performances, notes, transcripts, sound recordings, and moving images. Cole excludes speeches from his bibliography, while Hoover’s archive preserves only a catalogued body of manifestations; therefore neither authorship nor completeness can always be assigned at the title level. [Cole, scope note](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf); [Hoover collections description](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/browse/collection)
- **dates not fixed:** *Congressional testimony*. Statements and answers before congressional committees are public acts as much as texts, often jointly shaped by questioning; Cole excludes them, and Hoover catalogues a separate testimony category without establishing that every appearance survives. [Cole, scope note](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf); [Hoover overview](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/)
- **1931–2006:** *Milton Friedman papers*. Correspondence, drafts, notes, unpublished writings, statistics, photographs, sound recordings, and videotapes form an archival remainder, not a single authored work. The archive’s 227 manuscript boxes and later additions make “everything Friedman made” materially larger than any publications list, while unfinished drafts and routine correspondence resist being called works at all. [Hoover research guide](https://guides.hoover.org/future/intro); [Hoover acquisition notice](https://www.hoover.org/news/major-addition-milton-friedman-papers)

### Sources

- Julio H. Cole, [*Milton Friedman: A Bibliography*](https://fce2.ufm.edu/jhcole/FriedmanBiblio.pdf), revised September 22, 2010. Cole calls it the first complete listing of Friedman’s scholarly output; it is based on earlier bibliographies by Niels Thygesen, Gloria Valentine in Kurt Leube’s *The Essence of Friedman*, and Gilles Dostaler in *Milton Friedman et son oeuvre*. - Hoover Institution Library & Archives, [*The Collected Works of Milton Friedman*](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/). The collection contains more than 1,400 digital items by and about Friedman, including texts, sound recordings, moving images, bibliographic records, the complete *Free to Choose* series, and the 206-part *Economics Cassette Series*; because it also contains works about Friedman, its item count is not an authorship count.
- Hoover Institution Library & Archives, [Collected Works project-record catalogue](https://digitalcollections.hoover.org/objects/list?filter=collectionTitle%3ACollected+Works+of+Milton+Friedman+Project+records). The catalogue identifies 1,355 project records, of which 1,252 name Friedman as creator or contributor; these are archival or catalogue records, not 1,252 necessarily distinct authored works.
- Hoover Institution Library & Archives, [Milton Friedman papers and related collections](https://miltonfriedman.hoover.org/browse/collection). Used to identify the archival, audiovisual, and unpublished remainder that the scholarly bibliography intentionally omits.
- Hoover Institution Library & Archives, [“Milton and Rose Friedman: An Uncommon Couple”](https://www.hoover.org/news/milton-and-rose-friedman-uncommon-couple). Describes the publication of complete writing lists and identifies Friedman’s last self-compiled bibliography as a foundation of the digital project.
