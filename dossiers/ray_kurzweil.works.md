## Works by Ray Kurzweil

### Books: futurist syntheses, health protocols, and fiction

- **1990:** *The Age of Intelligent Machines*. An illustrated, multi-author survey and futurist argument edited and substantially written by Kurzweil; it grew from the museum-film project of the same name, so it is partly an authored book and partly a curated Gesamtkunstwerk of expert testimony, images, and prediction. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/classic-film-the-age-of-intelligent-machines))

- **1993:** *The 10% Solution for a Healthy Life: How to Eliminate Virtually All Risk of Heart Disease and Cancer*. A popular-health protocol advocating a low-fat regimen; the categorical promise belongs to the historical title, not to a present endorsement of its medical claims. ([Kurzweil Library bibliography](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/essays-celebrating-15-year-anniversary-of-the-book-the-singularity-is-near), [Penguin bibliography](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9781847928290/9781847928290-sample.pdf))

- **1999:** *The Age of Spiritual Machines: When Computers Exceed Human Intelligence*. A futurist treatise combining technological history, forecasts, philosophical argument, and imagined future dialogues—an obra whose speculative scenarios resist classification as either conventional scholarship or fiction. ([Kurzweil Library bibliography](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/essays-celebrating-15-year-anniversary-of-the-book-the-singularity-is-near))

- **2004:** *Fantastic Voyage: Live Long Enough to Live Forever*. Co-authored with physician Terry Grossman, this is a life-extension argument and practical health protocol intended to carry readers toward anticipated biotechnology; some publisher records give 2005, while Kurzweil’s official bibliography dates it 2004. ([Kurzweil Library bibliography](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/essays-celebrating-15-year-anniversary-of-the-book-the-singularity-is-near), [Penguin Random House](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/296471/fantastic-voyage-by-ray-kurzweil/))

- **2005:** *The Singularity Is Near: When Humans Transcend Biology*. A large-scale futurist synthesis of technological data, prediction, speculative narrative, and philosophical argument; it is less a neutral forecast than the principal manifesto of Kurzweil’s technological-singularity project. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/essays-celebrating-15-year-anniversary-of-the-book-the-singularity-is-near))

- **2009:** *Transcend: Nine Steps to Living Well Forever*. Co-authored with Terry Grossman, this sequel to *Fantastic Voyage* is explicitly organized as a nine-step health and longevity protocol. ([Kurzweil Library bibliography](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/essays-celebrating-15-year-anniversary-of-the-book-the-singularity-is-near), [Transcend](https://transcend.me/pages/books-and-publications))

- **2012:** *How to Create a Mind: The Secret of Human Thought Revealed*. A theory of neocortical pattern recognition presented simultaneously as neuroscience interpretation, artificial-intelligence design proposal, and manifesto for reproducing intelligence in machines. ([Kurzweil Library bibliography](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/essays-celebrating-15-year-anniversary-of-the-book-the-singularity-is-near))

- **2019:** *Danielle: Chronicles of a Superheroine*. Kurzweil’s first novel, an illustrated young-adult alternative history about a technologically adept problem-solver; Amy Kurzweil supplied its twenty-four illustrations, making the published object collaborative rather than Ray Kurzweil’s work alone. ([Danielle World](https://danielleworld.com/books/), [WordFire Press](https://www.baen.com/danielle.html))

- **2019:** *How You Can Be a Danielle*. A nonfiction action guide issued both digitally and as part of the printed *Danielle* volume; its status is therefore that of a companion protocol as well as a separately named text. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.booksbyraykurzweil.com/danielle-chronicles-of-a-superheroine), [Danielle World](https://danielleworld.com/how-to-be-a-danielle/))

- **2019:** *A Chronicle of Ideas: A Guide for Superheroines (and Superheroes)*. A digital nonfiction companion glossing hundreds of scientific, political, historical, and philosophical concepts tagged in *Danielle*; sources disagree on whether it covers 282, roughly 300, or 482 concepts, so no total is imposed here. ([Danielle World](https://danielleworld.com/a-chronicle-of-ideas-ray-kurzweil-novel/), [Society for Science](https://www.societyforscience.org/blog/conversations-with-maya-ray-kurzweil/), [WordFire Press](https://www.baen.com/danielle.html))

- **2024:** *The Singularity Is Nearer: When We Merge with AI*. A sequel and reassessment of *The Singularity Is Near*, updating Kurzweil’s manifesto of human–machine convergence after developments in deep learning, generative AI, biotechnology, and brain–computer interfaces. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/book-the-singularity-is-nearer), [Penguin bibliography](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9781847928290/9781847928290-sample.pdf))

### Collected and programmatic writings

- **2000:** *Promise and Peril*. An essay responding to arguments that genetics, nanotechnology, and robotics should be relinquished; because it argues for continuing technological development while constructing safeguards, it functions as a manifesto rather than merely a description. The contemporaneous bibliographical record dates its original *Interactive Week* publication to October 23, 2000. ([Washington National Cathedral briefing paper](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/are-we-becoming-an-endangered-species-technology-and-ethics-in-the-21st-century))

- **2001:** *The Law of Accelerating Returns*. An essay and mathematical-historical thesis asserting exponential technological change; it became the programmatic model underlying much of Kurzweil’s later work. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/the-intelligent-universe))

- **2003:** *The Ray Kurzweil Reader*. A digital collected-works manifestation containing thirty previously published essays from 2001–03; it is a compilation rather than thirty newly made works, and includes interviews and occasional pieces as well as essays. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/the-ray-kurzweil-reader))

- **2010:** *How My Predictions Are Faring*. A long self-audit of Kurzweil’s earlier forecasts, including all 147 predictions for 2009 from *The Age of Spiritual Machines*; it is simultaneously an essay, data report, and defense of his forecasting project. A later library page bears a 2011 date, but the original release page dates it October 1, 2010. ([Kurzweil Library release](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/how-my-predictions-are-faring-an-update-by-ray-kurzweil), [report PDF](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/images/How-My-Predictions-Are-Faring.pdf))

### Machines and software systems

- **Childhood, date unfixed:** *Mechanical puppet theatre*. Kurzweil recalls constructing a miniature “virtual reality world” whose sun, stars, and characters he controlled by mechanical linkages; this untitled childhood construction survives as testimony rather than a catalogued artifact. ([Computer History Museum oral history](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2013/05/102702108-05-01-acc.pdf))

- **1965:** *Computer music-composition program*. A program that analyzed patterns in classical composers and generated music in related styles, entered in the Westinghouse Science Talent Search and International Science Fair; it is an early generative system, not a fixed musical composition. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/in-print-scientific-american-futurist-ray-kurzweil-is-still-inventing), [Society for Science](https://www.societyforscience.org/blog/conversations-with-maya-ray-kurzweil/))

- **Late 1960s:** *College-matching system*. A commercial information-processing system devised while Kurzweil was an MIT undergraduate to match high-school students with colleges; it processed tens of thousands of cases and was sold to Harcourt, Brace & World, but the oral-history source supplies no stable product title or exact year. ([American Foundation for the Blind oral history](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil/part-4-4))

- **1974:** *Omnifont optical character-recognition system*. Software able to recognize proportionally spaced printed characters across typefaces rather than a single prescribed font. The standard institutional accounts credit Kurzweil as inventor or principal developer, while also describing implementation by the team he led; it should not be treated as a solitary-authored object. ([National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/inductees/raymond-kurzweil), [American Foundation for the Blind](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil/part-4-4))

- **Mid-1970s:** *CCD flatbed scanner*. The imaging component built by Kurzweil’s team to present whole printed pages to the OCR system; institutional histories credit Kurzweil with its invention, but it was developed within Kurzweil Computer Products and its individual engineering authorship was collective. ([National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/inductees/raymond-kurzweil), [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/theymadeamerica/whomade/kurzweil_lo.html))

- **Mid-1970s:** *Full text-to-speech synthesizer*. A speech system able to vocalize unrestricted textual input, developed as one component of the reading machine. “First” claims vary in their wording, so the defensible attribution is to Kurzweil as principal developer and to his company team as makers. ([Lemelson–MIT account](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/500000-lemelson-mit-prize-awarded-to-futurist-who-makes-a-career-of-helping-others), [American Foundation for the Blind](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil))

- **1976–1978:** *Kurzweil Reading Machine*. A print-to-speech apparatus integrating the flatbed scanner, omnifont OCR, and text-to-speech synthesis for blind readers—a technological Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single-component invention. Sources variously date its public unveiling or introduction to 1976, 1977, and 1978; these appear to mark different prototype, demonstration, and commercial milestones rather than a securely singular creation date. ([National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/inductees/raymond-kurzweil), [PBS](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/theymadeamerica/whomade/kurzweil_lo.html), [American Foundation for the Blind](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil))

- **1978:** *Kurzweil Data Entry Machine*. A more capable commercial application of Kurzweil Computer Products’ OCR technology for converting printed material into machine-readable data. It was a company product and therefore resists attribution to Kurzweil alone. ([American Foundation for the Blind oral history](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil/part-4-4))

- **1983–1984:** *Kurzweil 250*. A digital sampling keyboard designed to reproduce a grand piano and other acoustic instruments with then-unusual fidelity; a prototype appeared in 1983 and commercial shipments began in 1984. Kurzweil originated the project with musical advice from Stevie Wonder and engineering by the Kurzweil Music Systems team, so “Kurzweil’s invention” abbreviates a collaborative instrument-development process. ([Kurzweil Music Systems](https://kurzweil.com/about/), [National Inventors Hall of Fame](https://www.invent.org/inductees/raymond-kurzweil))

- **1987:** *Kurzweil Voice Report*. A commercially marketed large-vocabulary speech-to-text system, initially aimed at medical dictation and described as having a 100,000-word vocabulary. It was created by Kurzweil Applied Intelligence, not by Kurzweil working alone. ([Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library), [Lemelson–MIT account](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/500000-lemelson-mit-prize-awarded-to-futurist-who-makes-a-career-of-helping-others))

- **1990s, exact initial dates unresolved:** *Kurzweil 1000* and *Kurzweil 3000*. Personal-computer reading systems respectively associated with blind users and learners with reading disabilities; the institutional oral history identifies both as Kurzweil software advances, while Kurzweil states that the company producing the 3000 was founded in 1996. Their many later versions are product iterations, not separate Kurzweil obras. ([American Foundation for the Blind](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil), [Kurzweil prediction audit](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/images/How-My-Predictions-Are-Faring.pdf))

- **Late 1990s–2001:** *Ray Kurzweil’s Cybernetic Poet*. Generative and assistive writing software that learned statistical “poet personalities” from source texts, generated poems, and suggested language to human writers. Patent documentation names John Keklak alongside Kurzweil, so sole authorship would be inaccurate; moreover, its generated outputs are manifestations of the program, not individually authored Kurzweil poems. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/the-human-machine-merger-why-we-will-spend-most-of-our-time-in-virtual-reality-in-the-twenty-first-century), [patent citation in *The Singularity Is Near* notes](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/images/SingularityisNear_Notes.pdf))

- **1999–2006:** *FatKat*. An adaptive pattern-recognition system and investment fund—its name expands to Financial Accelerating Transactions from Kurzweil Adaptive Technologies—created in 1999 and reported as beginning live trading in 2006. It resists being called a finished work: it was a continuing financial-software research project embedded in a selective investment vehicle. ([Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **2001:** *Ramona*. A conversational chatbot, photorealistic digital avatar, virtual host, and motion-controlled stage persona. Because Kurzweil also performed through the avatar using body tracking and voice transformation, Ramona is a Gesamtkunstwerk joining software, character, performance, image, speech, and music. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/listen-to-pop-songs-by-ramona), [Lemelson–MIT account](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/500000-lemelson-mit-prize-awarded-to-futurist-who-makes-a-career-of-helping-others))

- **2005 onward:** *K-NFB Reader*, later *OneStep Reader*. A portable camera-based print-to-speech reader developed jointly by Kurzweil’s organization and the National Federation of the Blind, later evolving into smartphone software and, in 2022, a Sensotec product. The changing hardware, names, and institutional owners make it a continuing project or product lineage rather than one stable object. ([Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **2010:** *Blio*. Full-color e-reader software and an associated electronic-book platform preserving complex layouts and adding synchronized text-to-speech and multimedia. It was Kurzweil’s “brainchild,” but the released object was made institutionally by K-NFB Reading Technology with Baker & Taylor and other partners. ([K-NFB launch account](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/k-nfb-launches-free-e-reader-software-that-revolutionizes-digital-reading-experience), [Kurzweil Library project catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/kurzweil-projects-and-companies/knfb-reading-technology))

### Films, exhibitions, performances, and songs

- **1987:** *The Age of Intelligent Machines*. A two-part short documentary made for the traveling museum exhibition *Robots and Beyond: The Age of Intelligent Machines*; Kurzweil wrote, hosted, partnered in production, and served as executive producer, while Robert Richter is also credited as producer, director, and writer. Kurzweil’s archive calls it the basis of the 1990 book, making the film–exhibition–book complex a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than three wholly independent conceptions. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/classic-film-the-age-of-intelligent-machines), [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262111263/the-age-of-intelligent-machines-the-video/), [D-Word credits](https://www.d-word.com/documentary/89-Age-of-Intelligent-Machines-The))

- **2001:** *Come Out and Play*. An original pop song with lyrics by Kurzweil, music credited to Zoux, and vocals performed by Kurzweil as Ramona using pitch-alteration software; it is therefore a collaborative song and avatar performance, not solely a Kurzweil musical composition. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/listen-to-pop-songs-by-ramona))

- **2001:** *White Rabbit* as performed by Ramona. A technologically mediated cover performance by Kurzweil as his avatar, with music production credited to Zoux; the underlying song was made by others, so only this performance manifestation belongs in Kurzweil’s oeuvre. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/listen-to-pop-songs-by-ramona))

- **2010:** *The Singularity Is Near: A True Story About the Future*. A hybrid feature intertwining documentary interviews and graphics with a fictional narrative in which Ramona seeks legal personhood. Kurzweil wrote and co-produced it and is credited as co-director; the multiplicity of cast, crew, animation, and documentary participants makes it a collaborative film, not a solitary-authored text. ([Official film site](https://www.singularity.com/themovie/about.php), [Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/the-singularity-is-near-movie-available))

### Institutional and continuing projects

- **1973–1975 onward:** *Kurzweil Computer Products*. The company through which the OCR, scanning, speech, reading-machine, and data-entry projects were organized. Sources give 1973, 1974, and 1975 for its beginning; rather than silently choosing one, the range records the probable distinction between conception, organization, and incorporation. ([American Foundation for the Blind oral history](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil/part-4-4), [Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **1982 onward:** *Kurzweil Music Systems*. A company and continuing instrument-design project founded by Kurzweil with Stevie Wonder as musical adviser and Bruce Cichowlas as software engineer; its later keyboards are the work of changing teams and should not all be reassigned to Kurzweil personally. ([Kurzweil Music Systems](https://kurzweil.com/about/), [Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **1982 onward:** *Kurzweil Applied Intelligence*. A speech-recognition company founded to turn Kurzweil’s pattern-recognition research into commercial dictation systems, notably *Kurzweil Voice Report*. It is a project and organization, not itself a bounded authored work. ([Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **1985 onward:** *Kurzweil Foundation*. A philanthropic project founded and funded by Kurzweil to support blind and low-vision students through scholarships and accessibility initiatives. Its continuing activities are institutional actions rather than a single obra. ([Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **1996 onward:** *Kurzweil Educational Systems*. A company organized around print-to-speech and learning-support software, especially *Kurzweil 3000*; Kurzweil founded it but sold it in 2004, so subsequent products belong to successor teams. ([Kurzweil prediction audit](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/images/How-My-Predictions-Are-Faring.pdf))

- **Circa 1999–2000 onward:** *Medical Learning Company / FamilyPractice.com*. A joint venture with the American Board of Family Practice that made an online professional-education resource and a virtual-patient simulator. It resists individual attribution because Kurzweil’s standard company record identifies the venture but not his exact authorship of its instructional content or software. ([Lemelson–MIT account](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/500000-lemelson-mit-prize-awarded-to-futurist-who-makes-a-career-of-helping-others), [Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **2000–2001 onward:** *KurzweilAI.net*, now *The Kurzweil Library*. A web publication, archive, discussion platform, and curated digest founded and published by Kurzweil but made continuously by an editorial team and outside contributors. It is a continuing collected-works manifestation and curatorial project, not one text authored entirely by him. ([Kurzweil Library history](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library), [American Foundation for the Blind oral history](https://afb.org/blindness-and-low-vision/using-technology/interviews-technology-pioneers/ray-kurzweil/part-2-4))

- **2008 onward:** *Singularity University*, now *Singularity Group*. Co-founded by Kurzweil and Peter Diamandis as an educational organization centered on exponentially advancing technologies. It is an institutional project with many makers, curricula, and later transformations, rather than a personal Kurzweil work in the ordinary sense. ([Kurzweil Library](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/singularity-university-welcomes-2010-graduate-studies-program-participants), [Kurzweil Library company catalogue](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-the-kurzweil-library))

- **2012 onward:** *Natural-language understanding research at Google*. Kurzweil’s continuing team project in machine learning and human-language processing has no single stable public title or completed artifact in the consulted record. It belongs here as an unfinished institutional project, not as a product that can defensibly be credited to him alone. ([Kurzweil Library biography](https://www.thekurzweillibrary.com/about-ray-kurzweil))
