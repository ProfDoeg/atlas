## Works by Agha Hasan Abedi

### Banks as institutional obras

- **1959:** *United Bank Limited (UBL)*. A commercial-bank obra conceived and founded by Abedi with capital from the Saigol family; authorship is necessarily collective, but both the scholarly reference account and the surviving institutional history identify Abedi as its founder and organizing force. ([Historical Dictionary of Pakistan](https://sjbipp.org/publications/BK/pdf/BK-02-17.pdf), pp. 34–35; [BCCI Insights](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/agha-hasan-abedi))

- **1972–1991:** *Bank of Credit and Commerce International (BCCI)*. Abedi’s principal Gesamtkunstwerk: simultaneously a transnational bank, management culture, development project, patronage network and intended “Third World” institution. Its attribution to him is undisputed, but its status cannot be separated from its collapse, criminal convictions and the US Senate investigation’s finding that the corporate structure he created facilitated fraud and regulatory evasion; sympathetic former employees dispute aspects of that interpretation. ([US Senate, *The BCCI Affair: Executive Summary*](https://irp.fas.org/congress/1992_rpt/bcci/01exec.htm); [US Senate, “Origin and Early Years”](https://irp.fas.org/congress/1992_rpt/bcci/03hist.htm); [Historical Dictionary of Pakistan](https://sjbipp.org/publications/BK/pdf/BK-02-17.pdf), pp. 34–35)

### Foundations, prizes and educational institutions

- **1978–1979:** *Third World Foundation for Social and Economic Studies*. A research, publishing and convening institution initiated by Abedi and financed by BCCI: registered as a British charity in 1978 and formally launched in London on 29 October 1979, hence the two dates sometimes assigned to its creation. It is a collective institutional obra rather than Abedi’s personal text and ceased operating after BCCI’s 1991 closure. ([BCCI Insights chronology](https://bccibank-insights.com/key-events/1978-1979-third-world-foundation-global-south-vision); [foundation history](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/third-world-global-south/third-world-foundation))

- **1979:** *North–South Monitor*. A research-and-publication initiative launched by Abedi with the University of London’s Institute of Commonwealth Studies to examine the North–South dialogue. It resists attribution as a personal work: Abedi created and financed the platform, while its studies were produced by scholars and partner institutions. ([Centre for Development of Social Services](https://www.cdss.org.pk/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/CDSS-Newsletter-Fifth-Summer-Edition-2016.compressed.pdf); [Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/4402028/agha-hassan-abedi---a-legend-2016051445785))

- **1979–1980s:** *Third World Prize*. An annual US$100,000 award instituted with the Third World Foundation and announced at its October 1979 launch; the surviving retrospective lists prize activity from 1980 onward. It is an operative cultural instrument—a recurring act of recognition—rather than a conventional text or object. ([Third World Foundation history](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/third-world-global-south/third-world-foundation); [prize record, 1980–1989](https://bccibank-insights.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/Third%20World%20Prize%201980%20-%201989.pdf); [UN Law of the Sea Bulletin](https://www.un.org/depts/los/doalos_publications/LOSBulletins/bulletinpdf/bulE3.pdf))

- **1979 onward:** *Third World Quarterly*. A scholarly periodical associated with, financed by and published through the Third World Foundation. It belongs to Abedi’s institutional obra only in that qualified sense: Altaf Gauhar, not Abedi, supplied its editorial leadership, and individual articles belong to their named authors. ([BCCI Insights](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/people-and-associations/development-and-philanthropy); [Gauhar profile](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/people-and-associations/banking-business-education-and-public-life))

- **c. 1980s:** *South*. A magazine supported by the Third World Foundation and intended to articulate perspectives from the developing world. As with *Third World Quarterly*, this is an enabled collective manifestation, not a periodical personally written or edited by Abedi; Altaf Gauhar was editor-in-chief. ([BCCI Insights](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/people-and-associations/banking-business-education-and-public-life))

- **1980:** *Foundation for Advancement of Science and Technology (FAST), subsequently the FAST Institute and National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences*. An educational foundation and computer-science project established through Abedi’s patronage and a BCCI donation. Later university forms are institutional descendants, not works completed by Abedi alone. ([FAST-NU](https://www.nu.edu.pk/News/Details/348); [INFAQ Foundation](https://cdss.org.pk/infaq-foundation/))

- **1981:** *BCCI Foundation Pakistan, now INFAQ Foundation*. A nonprofit funding mechanism incorporated on 9 September 1981 at Abedi’s behest and financed by assigning BCCI’s Pakistani profits to charitable purposes. Its dual character is material: it funded education, health and social welfare, while contemporary critics also described it as a tax shelter, so it resists classification as uncomplicated philanthropy. ([INFAQ Foundation](https://cdss.org.pk/infaq-foundation/); [Los Angeles Times investigation](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1991-08-09-mn-201-story.html))

- **1980s, exact dates not securely reconstructed:** *BCCI national charitable foundations*. A distributed series of foundations through which portions of local BCCI profits supported health and education programs in several countries. These resist item-by-item cataloguing because the surviving institutional archive says that records are incomplete or remain with liquidators; consequently their exact number, names and dates should not be invented. ([BCCI Insights, “Philanthropy”](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/philanthropy); [Abedi profile](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/agha-hasan-abedi))

- **1985–1993:** *Ghulam Ishaq Khan Institute of Engineering Sciences and Technology*. An educational project substantially enabled by BCCI and INFAQ funding: the preparatory initiative dates to the mid-1980s, BCCI made a major commitment in 1988, and the institute opened after Abedi’s active career. It should not be called his sole creation; Ghulam Ishaq Khan, A. Q. Khan, public authorities and several founding donors shared its making. ([Historical Dictionary of Pakistan](https://sjbipp.org/publications/BK/pdf/BK-02-17.pdf), p. 265; [INFAQ Foundation](https://cdss.org.pk/infaq-foundation/))

### Unrealized institutional projects

- **Before 1985; no fixed founding date:** *Third World Bank*. A proposal for a financial institution through which developing countries could obtain short-term liquidity without dependence on the IMF or World Bank. It remained a project—an intention partly rehearsed through BCCI lending—not a separately constituted bank. ([BCCI Insights, “The Third World”](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/third-world-global-south); [Abedi profile](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/agha-hasan-abedi))

- **1985:** *Centre for Comprehensive Security and Common Survival*. A proposed global foundation and organizing centre introduced in Abedi’s New York address of 26 April 1985. The surviving evidence documents the proposal, not a completed institution, so the project rather than an organization is the obra. ([Abedi, *Survival in the Nuclear Age*](https://bccibank-insights.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/Speach%20by%20Agha%20Hassan%20Abedi%20-%20New%20York%20-%2026%20April%201985.pdf))

### Addresses and operative management texts

- **6 July 1983:** *Speech by the President of Bank of Credit and Commerce International at the Ljubljana Conference*. A conference address preserved as a transcript; its archive-supplied descriptive title should not be mistaken for a formally published essay title. ([BCCI archive record and transcript](https://bccibank-insights.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/Spech%20by%20Mr%20Agha%20Hassan%20Abedi%20at%20Ljubljana%20Coference%20-%206%20July%201983.pdf))

- **1984:** *Real Management*. An inaugural address to the Management Association of Pakistan that formulates Abedi’s spiritual-organizational conception of management. It functions partly as a manifesto for institutional conduct, although the surviving object is an address rather than a book. ([BCCI Abedi archive](https://bccibank-insights.com/library/agha-hasan-abedi))

- **26 April 1985:** *Survival in the Nuclear Age*. A New York address combining diagnosis with an operative proposal for a new international centre; in that respect it becomes a manifesto and project document rather than merely commentary. ([surviving transcript](https://bccibank-insights.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/Speach%20by%20Agha%20Hassan%20Abedi%20-%20New%20York%20-%2026%20April%201985.pdf))

- **12 November 1985:** *Toast in Honour of Robert Mugabe at the South–South Cooperation Banquet*. A ceremonial and diplomatic speech delivered in Harare in Abedi’s capacity as chairman of the Third World Foundation’s trustees. “Toast” describes the act it performed; the archive’s long descriptive wording is not necessarily an authorial title. ([BCCI Abedi archive](https://bccibank-insights.com/library/agha-hasan-abedi); [Third World Foundation context](https://bccibank-insights.com/explore/founder/third-world-global-south))

- **2 December 1985:** *BCC Presidents Meeting (UK and Far East)*. A transcribed, tape-derived management discourse addressed to BCCI regional leaders. It is best understood as an oral protocol intended to shape the bank’s next year, not as a polished literary text; the archive does not establish that Abedi prepared or corrected the transcript. ([complete archival transcript](https://bccibank-insights.com/sites/default/files/uploads/files/BCC%20-%20Presidents%20Meeting%20%28UK%20and%20Far%20East%29%202%20December%201985.pdf))
