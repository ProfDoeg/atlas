## Works by Christian Rosenkreuz

### Status of the maker

- **1378–1484 (legendary dates):** *Christian Rosenkreuz*. Rosenkreuz himself resists classification as a historical maker: the critical literature treats him as the allegorical founder constructed by the early-seventeenth-century Rosicrucian texts, with no contemporary evidence that he or his original fraternity existed. The inventory below therefore records things made **inside that fiction**, not surviving medieval artifacts or securely attributable writings ([Dickson](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Tessera_of_Antilia.html?id=teV5DwAAQBAJ); [Gilly index](https://www.theatrum-paracelsicum.com/Indexes/Gilly%2C_Cimelia_Rhodostaurotica%2C_2nd_ed._%281995%29); [*Fama* and its context](https://rosicrucianism.net/histoire/fama-fraternitatis.html)).

### Translations and personal records

- **Undated, during the legendary eastern journey:** *Latin translation of the Book M*. The *Fama Fraternitatis* says that Rosenkreuz translated at Damcar an Arabic book called only *M* into Latin; the object is lost or imaginary, its title and contents unfixed, and it may represent the “book of the world” rather than an ordinary codex ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fame_and_Confession_of_the_Fraternity_of_R._C./Fama_Fraternitatis); [Brill, *Reformation, Revolution, Renovation*](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004249394/9789004249394_webready_content_text.pdf)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Itinerarium*. A travel record attributed to Rosenkreuz and reportedly found in his vault; the *Fama* says its account was drawn largely from this and his *Vita*. No independent manuscript survives, so it is an embedded documentary fiction rather than an extant work ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fame_and_Confession_of_the_Fraternity_of_R._C./Fama_Fraternitatis)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Vita*. A life-record attributed to Rosenkreuz and found beside the *Itinerarium* in the vault. Whether the two names denote separate books, two sections of one record, or generic descriptions is not settled by the *Fama* ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fame_and_Confession_of_the_Fraternity_of_R._C./Fama_Fraternitatis)).

### Reform project and institution

- **Undated, after the legendary return to Europe:** *A new foundation for the learned arts*. Rosenkreuz is said to have offered Spanish scholars a comprehensive reform of their allegedly defective arts and sciences; they rejected it. This is a **project**, not a securely titled text, and the narrative never says that it reached a finished documentary form ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fame_and_Confession_of_the_Fraternity_of_R._C./Fama_Fraternitatis)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Fraternity of the Rose Cross*. An institutional and spiritual **project** founded by Rosenkreuz with three initial collaborators and later enlarged to eight members. Scholarship treats the fraternity described in the manifestos as a literary model for religious and intellectual reform, not a documented medieval organization ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fame_and_Confession_of_the_Fraternity_of_R._C./Fama_Fraternitatis); [Dickson](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Tessera_of_Antilia.html?id=teV5DwAAQBAJ)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Six articles of the Fraternity*. A collectively adopted **protocol** requiring free healing, ordinary local dress, annual assembly or written excuse, appointment of successors, use of “R.C.” as seal and character, and one hundred years of secrecy. The *Fama* associates the rules with Rosenkreuz’s founding initiative but does not assign him sole authorship ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://www.crcsite.org/rosicrucian-library/fama-fraternitatis/)).

### Architecture and Gesamtkunstwerk

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Sancti Spiritus* (“House of the Holy Spirit”). The fraternity’s completed meeting-house, described as Rosenkreuz’s “new building”; it is simultaneously architecture, institutional headquarters, and a concealed site. No historical structure has been identified, and even within the narrative its location remains secret ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://www.crcsite.org/rosicrucian-library/fama-fraternitatis/)).

- **Undated, completed before 1484:** *Compendium of the Universe / Rosenkreuz’s sepulchral vault*. A seven-sided, symbolically inscribed chamber containing an artificial light, altar, body, books, instruments, mirrors, bells, lamps, and elaborate songs: effectively a **Gesamtkunstwerk** joining architecture, writing, mechanism, library, body, and cosmological model. Its inscription claims that Rosenkreuz made it during his lifetime as his tomb, although the narrative elsewhere leaves room for the brethren’s participation in constructing or completing his burial place ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Fame_and_Confession_of_the_Fraternity_of_R._C./Fama_Fraternitatis); [Prinke, “The Great Work in the Theatre of the World”](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/g_work.html)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Mundus minutus* (“little world”). Rosenkreuz’s epitaph says that he constructed a microcosm whose motions corresponded to the macrocosm before drawing up the sepulchral compendium. It may be a mechanism, cosmological model, the vault itself, or a symbolic description rather than a separable object; it therefore resists treatment as an independent work ([*Fama Fraternitatis*, text and notes](https://www.tarrdaniel.com/documents/Thelemagick/gd/publication/english/Fama_Fraternitatis.html)).

### Collective scholarly and linguistic works

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Magical language and writing*. A constructed language and script made collectively by Rosenkreuz and the first brethren. The *Fama* reports its continuing ritual and scholarly use but preserves neither a complete grammar nor enough material to establish that such a language existed outside the narrative ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://www.gnosis.org/library/fama.html)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Large dictionary of the magical language*. A collective reference work accompanying the fraternity’s invented language and script. It is reported but neither quoted substantially nor independently attested ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://www.gnosis.org/library/fama.html)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *First part of the Book M*. A collectively made continuation or redaction by Rosenkreuz and the first brethren. Its relationship to the Arabic *Book M* that Rosenkreuz supposedly translated is unexplained, so translation, adaptation, and new composition cannot be cleanly separated ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://www.gnosis.org/library/fama.html)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Volume of all that humanity can desire, wish, or hope for*. A compendious book assembled by the enlarged group of eight brethren. It is a collective encyclopedic **project**, without a fixed title, and should not be silently identified with *Book M* ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://www.gnosis.org/library/fama.html)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Axiomata*. A work or body of axioms preserved in the fraternity’s philosophical library and regarded by later fictional brethren as its foremost possession. The *Fama* assigns it collectively to Rosenkreuz and his first companions rather than to Rosenkreuz alone; no independent text survives ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://anthroblog.anthroweb.info/quellen-der-esoterik/fama-fraternitatis/)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Rotae Mundi* (“Wheels of the World”). A work in the fraternity’s philosophical library, described as the most artful or technically accomplished of its holdings. Its medium and contents remain unspecified, so it could be a text, diagrammatic system, instrument, or combination of these ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://anthroblog.anthroweb.info/quellen-der-esoterik/fama-fraternitatis/)).

- **Undated, before 1484:** *Proteus* (also printed *Protus*). A work regarded by the later brethren as the most useful item in their philosophical library. Its unstable name, unknown medium, missing contents, and collective attribution make even its identity as a discrete text uncertain ([*Fama Fraternitatis*](https://anthroblog.anthroweb.info/quellen-der-esoterik/fama-fraternitatis/)).

### Pseudepigrapha and works not attributable to him

- **1459 within the fiction; composed about 1605 and printed 1616:** *Chymische Hochzeit Christiani Rosencreutz anno 1459* (*Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosenkreuz in the Year 1459*). A first-person alchemical romance or initiatory fiction in which Rosenkreuz is narrator and protagonist, not a securely identified historical author; Johann Valentin Andreae later claimed it as his own youthful *ludibrium*. Its fictional date belongs to the persona, while its actual making belongs to the early seventeenth century ([Deutsches Textarchiv record cited here](https://hermetism.org/encyclopedia/christian-rosenkreutz/); [Tilton](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Quest_for_the_Phoenix.html?id=bpxuNjTQv44C); [foundational bibliography](https://rosicrucianism.net/bibliographie/)).

- **Circulating by about 1610; printed 1614:** *Fama Fraternitatis*. This manifesto creates and narrates Rosenkreuz rather than constituting a work made by him; its voice is that of later brethren, and scholarship locates its production in the Tübingen reforming circle associated with Tobias Hess and Johann Valentin Andreae ([*Fama* historical record](https://rosicrucianism.net/histoire/fama-fraternitatis.html); [Gilly index](https://www.theatrum-paracelsicum.com/Indexes/Gilly%2C_Cimelia_Rhodostaurotica%2C_2nd_ed._%281995%29)).

- **Printed 1615:** *Confessio Fraternitatis*. A Rosicrucian **manifesto** speaking for the fraternity and elaborating its programme, but neither internally nor historically a work by Rosenkreuz. It belongs to the corpus that manufactured his posthumous authority ([foundational bibliography](https://rosicrucianism.net/bibliographie/); [Brill, *Reformation, Revolution, Renovation*](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004249394/9789004249394_webready_content_text.pdf)).
