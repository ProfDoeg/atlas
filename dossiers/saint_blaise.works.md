## Works by Saint Blaise of Sebastea

### No securely attested works

- **No date:** No surviving text, project, material object, or other *obra* can be securely attributed to Blaise. The standard hagiographic repertories instead catalogue anonymous *Passiones* about him—Greek BHG 276–276f and 971, and Latin BHL 1370–1380—not works made by him; even the speeches, prayers, cures, and blessings placed in his mouth or enacted through him resist classification as his works because they survive only inside later legendary narratives.

### Excluded later manifestations

- **Undated; transmitted in later recensions:** *Passion of Saint Blaise* (BHG 276–276f). This is an anonymous hagiographic narrative about Blaise, explicitly catalogued as *Hagiographica*, not a text by him; its reported acts and utterances therefore cannot safely be extracted as independent works of Blaise.
- **Undated; later recension:** *Passion of Saint Blaise* (BHG 971). This is another Greek hagiographic recension about the saint, likewise not an authored work attributable to him.
- **Medieval transmission:** *Latin lives and passions of Saint Blaise* (BHL 1370–1380). These constitute a dossier of later manifestations of his legend rather than Blaise’s own corpus; individual versions have anonymous or identifiable medieval makers, not Blaise.
- **Ninth century and later:** *Martyrological and synaxarial notices of Saint Blaise*. These notices depend upon the legendary Acts and are witnesses to his cult, not creations by him. The underlying historical record supports, at most, a cautiously stated early-fourth-century bishop and martyr; it does not establish an author or maker with recoverable works.
