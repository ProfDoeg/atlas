## Works by Fernão de Magalhães

### Counsel, petitions, and correspondence

- **October 1510:** *Opinion in the council of war at Cochin concerning the proposed attack on Goa*. A spoken piece of military counsel preserved in the council’s written protocol and signed by Magalhães; it is therefore partly utterance, partly documentary act, rather than a separately composed treatise. [S1]

- **1514–1516:** *Petitions to Manuel I for advancement and increased court stipend*. At least two career petitions are recorded, both unsuccessful; their exact texts, dates, and titles are not securely recoverable from the cited scholarship, so they are best treated as a small, incompletely surviving petitionary project. [S2]

- **Before 1517:** *Letters to Francisco Serrão in the Moluccas*. João de Barros reports that Portuguese officials found letters in which Magalhães promised to reach Serrão by Portugal or, failing that, by Castile. The originals are lost, their number and dates unknown, and the surviving report does not permit reconstruction of them as individual works. [S1][S3]

- **24 October 1518:** *Memorial to Carlos I concerning the Armada of the Spice Islands*. A petitionary memorandum requesting money, merchandise, appointments, and administrative assistance for the fleet; the archival catalogue also describes it as a letter complaining of the obstruction Magalhães encountered from Seville’s municipal authorities. [S4][S5]

- **Probably 1518:** *Letter to Manuel I proposing a return to Portuguese service*. Damião de Góis claimed to have seen such a letter, reportedly written after Portuguese diplomatic pressure on Magalhães; the document is lost, and both its circumstances and the sincerity of the proposal remain uncertain. This is an attested but irrecoverable text, not a securely extant work. [S6]

### Contracts and notarial acts

- **23 February 1518:** *Agreement among Fernão de Magalhães, Rui Faleiro, and Juan de Aranda*. A jointly made contractual instrument through which Aranda was promised a share of the prospective rewards from the projected voyage. Its legality generated an official inquiry later in 1518; authorship is collective and mediated by notarial drafting. [S2][S7]

- **22 March 1518:** *Capitulations of Valladolid*. A negotiated charter or protocol between the Crown and Magalhães and Faleiro, defining the projected discoveries, privileges, profits, ships, and territorial limits. It is not solely “by” Magalhães—the royal chancery produced its wording—but it is one of the principal made objects of his collaborative voyage project. [S1][S2][S8]

- **19 March 1519:** *Donation of a vineyard at Gaia to Isabel de Magalhães*. A notarized dispositive act transferring inherited property to his sister; its “work” consists in changing a legal relationship rather than describing one. [S9]

- **4 June 1519:** *First receipt for the dowry of Beatriz Barbosa*. A notarized acknowledgment by Magalhães that he had received 300,000 maravedís toward his wife’s dowry. It should not be confused with the adjacent dowry deed, which was made by Diego de Barbosa. [S9]

- **4 June 1519:** *Marriage-settlement deed in favor of Beatriz Barbosa*. A carta de arras by which Magalhães assigned his wife 2,000 ducats: a performative legal text, jointly dependent upon notary and witnesses. [S9]

- **4 June 1519:** *Second donation of property at Gaia to Isabel de Magalhães*. A separate notarial donation of a quinta containing vineyards and chestnut groves to his sister. [S9]

- **23 August 1519:** *Second receipt for the dowry of Beatriz Barbosa*. A notarized acknowledgment of the remaining 300,000 maravedís due from Diego de Barbosa. [S9]

- **24 August 1519:** *Testament of Fernão de Magalhães*. A legal and devotional protocol ordering burial, charitable gifts, inheritance, and the emancipation of his enslaved interpreter Enrique after Magalhães’s death. Only a damaged fragment of the notarial register survives in Seville, but a family copy in the Archivo General de Indias preserves the testament’s contents; an alleged Lisbon testament dated 1504 was exposed in the twentieth century as a forgery and is not included as his work. [S9][S10]

### Cartographic and navigational project

- **c. 1517–1518:** *Western-route project for reaching the Moluccas*. A collaborative geographical project developed with the cosmographer Rui Faleiro and supported by information from Francisco Serrão and the cartographers Pedro and Jorge Reinel. It resists reduction to a single text: it comprised calculations, maps, oral demonstrations, petitions, and the intended voyage itself, while its central territorial premise—that the Moluccas lay within Castile’s hemisphere—was mistaken. [S2][S3]

- **February–March 1518:** *Painted globe or chart presented to Carlos I*. Contemporary testimony says that Magalhães and Faleiro demonstrated their proposed western route with cartographic material while deliberately leaving the still-undiscovered passage unmarked. The object has not survived, and the respective contributions of Magalhães, Faleiro, and the Reinel cartographers cannot be securely separated; it is therefore a collaborative manifestation of the project rather than an attributable Magalhães map. [S2][S3]

### Expedition as obra and unfinished project

- **1518–1521:** *Armada of the Spice Islands*. Magalhães conceived, organized, and commanded a five-ship expedition intended to reach the Moluccas westward without entering Portugal’s allotted sphere. As his obra it was institutional, material, navigational, and political—a kind of early modern Gesamtkunstwerk of ships, contracts, maps, finance, command, diplomacy, and movement—yet it remained personally unfinished when he died at Mactan on 27 April 1521; the surviving *Victoria* completed the circumnavigation under Juan Sebastián Elcano in 1522, so the completed first circuit of the globe cannot accurately be assigned to Magalhães alone. [S2][S11]

- **21 October–27 November 1520:** *Passage through the Channel of All Saints*. A navigational accomplishment and geographical manifestation produced by the expedition under Magalhães’s command, not a freestanding authored text. Magalhães’s own name for the passage was the Channel or Strait of All Saints; “Strait of Magellan” is a later commemorative name. [S1][S2]

- **November 1520–March 1521:** *First recorded European crossing of the Pacific*. A route made through navigation rather than writing; Magalhães and his company named the ocean “Pacific” in response to the conditions they experienced. It belongs to the collective expedition and should not be mistaken for sole authorship of either the ocean or its indigenous geographies. [S2][S11]

### Command protocols

- **2 April 1520:** *Letter or summons sent to Luis de Mendoza during the mutiny at Port Saint Julian*. A tactical written message used to gain access to Mendoza’s ship immediately before his killing by Magalhães’s men. Its wording and physical original do not survive independently, so it resists treatment as a stable literary work. [S1]

- **21 November 1520:** *Order of the day in the Channel of All Saints*. A command protocol requiring Duarte Barbosa and the fleet’s pilots, masters, and officers to submit written opinions on whether the expedition should advance or turn back. The text survives only through João de Barros’s later transcription from papers seized by the Portuguese in the Moluccas, not as Magalhães’s original document. [S1]

- **Late November 1520:** *Decision and reasons for continuing through the strait*. Barros reports that Magalhães issued a lengthy written or formally declared response after receiving the officers’ opinions, and that his decision was notified throughout the fleet. Its text is lost; consequently it is an attested command act rather than a recoverable work. [S1]
