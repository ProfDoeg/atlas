## Works by Roberto Calvi

### Banking projects and institutional obra

- **1971–1982:** *The Banco Ambrosiano offshore system*. Calvi’s principal obra was not a single corporation but a deliberately dispersed financial architecture linking Milan with banks, holding companies, and nominally independent borrowers in Luxembourg, Switzerland, the Bahamas, Nicaragua, Peru, Panama, and Liechtenstein. Because several institutions predated his control and other people or the IOR formally owned parts of the network, it resists classification as Calvi’s work alone; contemporary investigations nevertheless identify him as its organizer and operator. ([Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell); [Banca d’Italia](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/relazione-annuale/1982/en_rel82_abridged_annualreport.pdf?language_id=1); [Italian parliamentary record](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/271362.pdf))

- **1971–1976:** *Cisalpine Overseas Bank Ltd.*, later *Banco Ambrosiano Overseas Ltd.* A Nassau bank developed into a central component of Calvi’s offshore system; Archbishop Paul Marcinkus was already a director in August 1971. Its authorship is collective—Calvi, Banco Ambrosiano officials, Michele Sindona’s circle, and the IOR all participated—so “Calvi’s creation” describes operational design rather than uncontested legal founding. ([Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell); [Italian parliamentary record](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/271362.pdf))

- **1976–1982:** *Banco Ambrosiano Holding S.A.* This Luxembourg holding company was the renamed continuation of Compendium, founded in 1963 under Carlo Canesi rather than by Calvi; Calvi made it the organizing hinge of his international group. It is therefore better understood as an inherited structure radically repurposed than as an institution he created from nothing. ([Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell); [Banca d’Italia](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/relazione-annuale/1982/en_rel82_abridged_annualreport.pdf?language_id=1))

- **1970s–1982:** *Ambrosiano Group Banco Comercial, Managua*. This Nicaraguan bank was one of the foreign lending engines assembled under Calvi’s direction; it advanced funds to offshore borrowers beyond effective Italian consolidated supervision. The surviving record treats it as part of a group mechanism, not as a wholly independent personal creation. ([Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell); [Banca d’Italia](https://www.bancaditalia.it/pubblicazioni/relazione-annuale/1982/en_rel82_abridged_annualreport.pdf?language_id=1))

- **1979–1982:** *Banco Ambrosiano Andino S.A.* Calvi authorized the incorporation of this multinational bank in Lima on 27 June 1979; it subsequently became the largest lender to the offshore debtor companies. The Peruvian parliamentary inquiry documents the act of constitution, while later accounts disagree about how much genuine banking purpose the institution retained once incorporated into Calvi’s financing system. ([Peruvian Chamber of Deputies inquiry](https://es.scribd.com/document/333642078/Informe-Caso-Ambrosiano-Andino-1984); [Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell))

- **1970s–1981:** The eight-company offshore borrower constellation: *Manic S.A.*, *Astolfine S.A.*, *Nordeurop Establishment*, *United Trading Corporation*, *Erin S.A.* (rendered *Erm S.A.* in one judicial account), *Bellatrix S.A.*, *Belrosa S.A.*, and *Starfield S.A.* These Luxembourg, Liechtenstein, and Panamanian entities collectively functioned as receptacles for loans and holders of pledged shares; their nominal control was attributed to the IOR, while Calvi’s practical authorship lay in connecting and financing them. The variant *Erin/Erm* and disputed beneficial ownership should not be silently regularized. ([Italian criminal-court reconstruction](https://archive.is/20140102110216/www.philipwillan.com/images/calvi%5B1%5D.pdf); [Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell))

- **21 April 1981:** *The Rizzoli–Corriere della Sera acquisition*. Through Centrale Finanziaria, Calvi arranged the purchase of 40 percent of Rizzoli, publisher of *Corriere della Sera*, as part of a recapitalization financed by Banco Ambrosiano. This was an acquisition project and instrument of control rather than a text or newly created company; its intended beneficiaries and Calvi’s relationship to P2 remain part of the historical controversy surrounding it. ([Camera dei deputati](https://documenti.camera.it/_dati/leg16/lavori/schedela/apriTelecomando_wai.asp?codice=16PDL0037300); [Law and Economics Yearly Review](https://www.laweconomicsyearlyreview.org.uk/Law_and_Economics_Yearly_Review_LEYR_Journal_vol_9_part_2_2020.pdf))

### Financial instruments and operative texts

- **27 August 1981:** *Letter of indemnity to the IOR* (conventional description; no securely established authorial title). Signed by Calvi five days before the IOR’s letters of patronage, this counter-letter purported to relieve the Vatican bank of responsibility for the offshore companies. It is a performative financial text—closer to an instrument or anti-guarantee than a descriptive letter—and its concealed relationship to the patronage letters was central to later allegations of deception. ([Italian criminal-court reconstruction](https://archive.is/20140102110216/www.philipwillan.com/images/calvi%5B1%5D.pdf); [TIME investigation](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C951806-6%2C00.html))

- **September 1981–June 1982:** The patronage-and-financing arrangement with the IOR. Calvi solicited and used two IOR patronage letters addressed to the Lima and Managua banks to keep lending to the offshore borrowers alive; the letters themselves were issued by IOR officials and therefore are not works authored by Calvi. His made thing was the operative arrangement joining those letters, his indemnity, inflated collateral, and continued loans—a protocol in practice, although never published as one. ([Italian criminal-court reconstruction](https://archive.is/20140102110216/www.philipwillan.com/images/calvi%5B1%5D.pdf); [Cornwell](https://www.scribd.com/document/152477077/God-s-Banker-Rupert-Cornwell))

### Depositions and final correspondence

- **1981:** Voluntary deposition made while imprisoned at Lodi (no fixed published title). This is a testimonial record rather than a literary work: Calvi reportedly acknowledged a large payment routed through Umberto Ortolani to the Italian Socialist Party. The parliamentary source establishes the deposition’s existence but not a complete, authoritative edition, so it cannot safely be subdivided into separately titled works. ([Italian Senate, bill no. 588](https://www.parlamento.it/service/PDF/PDFServer/DF/271362.pdf))

- **30 May 1982:** Letter to Cardinal Pietro Palazzini (untitled). Calvi sought intervention against the refusal of Marcinkus and Luigi Mennini to meet him and invoked what he knew about Vatican affairs. The letter survives as an evidentiary object associated with the papers carried during his flight, rather than as a work prepared for publication. ([Italian criminal-court reconstruction](https://archive.is/20140102110216/www.philipwillan.com/images/calvi%5B1%5D.pdf); [case chronology](https://www.cinquantamila.it/storyTellerArticolo.php?storyId=0000000117598))

- **Late May or early June 1982:** Letter or letters to Monsignor Hilary Franco (untitled). Evidence from the Ambrosiano proceedings records Calvi’s attempt to use Franco as an intermediary in a proposed rescue, but the exact number and dates of the letters are not consistently reported. This correspondence therefore resists entry as a securely bounded single work. ([Radio Radicale trial record, 2 July 1991](https://www.radioradicale.it/scheda/40280/processo-per-il-crack-del-banco-ambrosiano?i=2575215&qt-blocco_interventi=1); [Radio Radicale trial record, 14 November 1991](https://www.radioradicale.it/scheda/42870/crack-del-banco-ambrosiano))

- **5 June 1982:** Letter to Pope John Paul II (untitled). A strategic appeal warning that Ambrosiano’s collapse would gravely damage the Church, it behaves partly as petition and partly as threatened disclosure rather than as a manifesto. Its authenticity is disputed: the text was published from material attributed to Calvi, but the Vatican rejected it as inauthentic, and reports indicate that it may never have been sent. ([Italian criminal-court reconstruction](https://archive.is/20140102110216/www.philipwillan.com/images/calvi%5B1%5D.pdf); [contemporary discussion in *l’Unità*](https://archivio.unita.news/assets/derived/1992/04/22/issue_full.pdf))
