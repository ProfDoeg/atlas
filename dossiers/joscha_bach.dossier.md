## Family and Ancestry

The public record is sparse and rests mainly on Bach's recollections in interviews.

- **Parents.** Both were architecture students when he was born. Bach has described his father as an impulsive architect and artist and his parents as “East German hippies.” Dissatisfied with standardized East Bloc architecture, they acquired an old mill in the countryside southeast of Weimar and developed it into a home with sculpture gardens and spaces for concerts. His father, in Bach's account, valued the material security of socialism but disliked the state and its restrictions.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1973–1990

Bach grew up first in and around **Weimar**, a center of German literary history that was then part of the GDR. His parents' mill property placed at least part of his childhood in rural Thuringia. He has portrayed it as an artistically unconventional setting but also as socially isolated.

In later interviews he described himself as lonely, an early and largely self-directed reader, and poorly integrated with peers. He recalled reading philosophy, science fiction, the Bible, Gandhi, mathematics, and a biography of Einstein. These are retrospective self-reports rather than contemporaneous school records.

His formative machine was a **Commodore 64**. Because he often lacked playmates, he said, he programmed versions of games such as Parcheesi and *Missile Command*. He also wrote a text editor and graphics software. These recollections help explain the later fusion in his work of introspective philosophy and constructive computer modeling; no surviving childhood code or independent witness was located.

Bach finished secondary school around the collapse of the East German regime and the opening of the Berlin Wall in **1989–1990**. No reliable source consulted specifies the school, examination date, grades, political youth membership, military service, or conflict with GDR authorities. There is no documented exile, imprisonment, or political prosecution.

## Education and Formation

### North American bicycle journey

At the end of school or shortly afterward, Bach built a recumbent bicycle and rode more than **3,000 miles through the United States and Canada**, according to the 2018 profile in *Psychology Today*. The exact year, route, border entries, and duration were not published. It was travel, not an established migration.

### Berlin and New Zealand

Bach studied **computer science and philosophy at Humboldt University of Berlin**. The degree is variously rendered in English-language biographies as an M.A. or the German university diploma; it was completed around **2000**. He also spent approximately a year studying computer science at the **University of Waikato in New Zealand**. He later singled out a course on text compression as formative: compression suggested that intelligence could be understood through compact generative models rather than lists of explicit facts.

The consulted sources do not provide a full transcript, undergraduate thesis title, or exact dates for the Waikato stay. A 2001 paper on using multiple entropy models for textual input is consistent with this early interest but does not by itself prove the course chronology.

### Osnabrück doctorate and intellectual mentors

At the **Institute of Cognitive Science, University of Osnabrück**, Bach developed MicroPsi and completed the dissertation **“Principles of Synthetic Intelligence: Building Blocks for an Architecture of Motivated Cognition.”** Sources give **2006** as the doctoral year, while Bach's older publications page dates the thesis document **March 2007**. The safest reconstruction is that the doctorate was completed in the 2006–2007 academic period and the public draft followed in March 2007. The dissertation became the substantially developed 2009 Oxford University Press book *Principles of Synthetic Intelligence: PSI: An Architecture of Motivated Cognition*.

Biographical sources name **Dietrich Dörner**, **Kai-Uwe Kühnberger**, and **Hans-Dieter Burkhard** as doctoral advisers. Dörner was the decisive theoretical influence: his **Psi theory** treated cognition as the interaction of perception, memory, action, needs, motives, and emotion in a situated agent. Bach translated, modified, and computationally implemented those ideas in MicroPsi. Burkhard's multi-agent and robotics work and the RoboCup environment shaped Bach's early applied research. **Ronnie Vuine** was a recurring collaborator on the AEP toolkit and early MicroPsi software.

Other formative influences named by Bach in later talks include cybernetics, functionalism, phenomenology, artificial-life research, Marvin Minsky, Douglas Hofstadter, Daniel Dennett, Noam Chomsky, and science fiction. This list describes intellectual uptake, not necessarily personal mentorship.

## Early Career, 2000–2012

From 2000 onward Bach published on robot localization, case-based behavior prediction, agent control languages, text compression, and cognitive agents. Work with the Humboldt/Osnabrück groups included **RoboCup** and the **AEP (Agent Environment Platform) Toolkit**. The publications show an evolution from specialized robotics and agent engineering toward a general architecture of motivated cognition.

MicroPsi's early components included:

- a spreading-activation or “node net” representation;
- perceptual and episodic structures grounded in a simulated environment;
- needs and motives that determine goals;
- modulators that alter attention, resolution, activation, and behavior and thereby implement a functional model of emotion;
- planning and action selection under bounded knowledge;
- a constructivist account in which the agent's experienced world is an internal model, not a direct copy of reality.

In **2005–2008**, conference and journal papers presented MicroPsi to cognitive-science and AGI audiences. The **2009 Oxford book** was Bach's major early success: a full monograph connecting Dörner's psychological theory with implementable AI. It remains his only located sole-authored scholarly book.

By **2011–2012**, he published on motivational systems, emergent emotion, and the relation between mind and machine. He co-edited the proceedings of **KI 2011** with Stefan Edelkamp and **AGI 2012** with Ben Goertzel and Matthew Iklé. In 2012 he was associated with the **Berlin School of Mind and Brain** as a research fellow. The record establishes a productive research career before his move to the United States, though not a conventional tenured professorship.

## Chronological Life History

### 2013–2019: Boston/Cambridge, MIT, Harvard, and Epstein financing

In **2013**, AI researcher **Ben Goertzel** introduced Bach to financier and convicted sex offender **Jeffrey Epstein**. Bach says computer scientists **Roger Schank** and **Stephen Kosslyn** evaluated or discussed his work in that network. Epstein introduced Bach to MIT Media Lab director **Joi Ito**. A released September 2013 email shows Harvard mathematician **Martin Nowak** seeking a three-year visiting-researcher arrangement for Bach with space at Harvard's Program for Evolutionary Dynamics (PED), while Ito discussed how MIT could configure an appointment.

MIT's independent 2020 report found that the Media Lab hired Bach “in large part” because Epstein subsidized the cost. Epstein made three post-conviction gifts in November 2013 and July and September 2014 totaling **$300,000** to support Bach; those gifts represented 40 percent of Epstein's post-conviction donations to MIT identified by the report. Bach worked at the **MIT Media Lab**, generally described as a research fellow, during a period sources variously delimit as 2013–2016 or 2013–2017.

The **Harvard University review** found that Nowak gave Bach intermittent PED access from **2014 through 2019** and that Bach was listed as a research scientist, but Harvard could not locate the normal visitor participation agreement. Harvard reported that it neither paid Bach nor received funds for him, and could not determine whether Epstein supported him personally. At least two post-MIT papers acknowledged Epstein and/or PED support. Thus “MIT and Harvard researcher” is broadly true, but it compresses different and irregular statuses: he was not documented as a tenured professor at either institution.

Bach later disclosed that Epstein's support extended beyond the $300,000 MIT gifts. Released records and Bach's statements describe relocation, rent, salary gaps, health costs, school/daycare fees, travel, equipment, and household shortfalls for his family. A 2015 accounting reportedly projected **$89,000** in needs, including about $29,000 for school/daycare, $5,000 travel, $5,000 equipment and appliances, a $2,700 car repair, and a monthly deficit near $3,900. In 2019 correspondence, school amounts of $5,100 and $6,616 appear. Investigative reporting estimates total Epstein support exceeded **$1 million**, but no audited total or complete payment ledger has been published. The $300,000 MIT total and the over-$1-million estimate measure different categories and must not be conflated.

Bach corresponded extensively with Epstein about intelligence, sex differences, race, heredity, eugenics, population, climate change, politics, and AI. One released chain contained claims and speculation about slower cognitive development among Black American children, extended neuroplasticity and genetic switches, differences in women's interest in abstract systems, and population policy. These statements became a major public controversy when more correspondence was released in 2025. Their evidentiary status is discussed below.

Bach has acknowledged visiting Epstein's private island, **Little St. James**, for one evening, reportedly in **2015**, with a science-foundation director and family. He said he observed no illicit conduct and described a library and piano in the island's temple-like building. The visit and financial relationship are documented; no consulted source alleges that Bach participated in Epstein's sexual offenses.

Professionally, this period produced work on **MicroPsi 2**, motivation, request-confirmation networks, perceptual binding, and conversational agents. In **2014**, Bach co-founded **Micropsi Industries GmbH** in Berlin with Ronnie Vuine, Ulrik Deichsel, Dominik Welland, and Priska Herger. The company applied adaptive control and machine learning to industrial robotics, later marketing the **MIRAI** robot-control system. Bach's exact operational role, equity, dilution, departure date, and proceeds are not public.

### 2019–2022: San Francisco, AI Foundation, patent, and Intel

In early **2019**, Bach obtained an **O-1 visa** and joined the San Francisco-based **AI Foundation** as **Vice President of Research**. He has described this as the point at which he became financially independent of Epstein. AI Foundation worked on synthetic media, digital persons, and human-like AI interfaces.

On **7 June 2021**, AI Foundation filed a United States patent application naming Bach as sole inventor for **“Virtual representation with dynamic and realistic behavioral and emotional responses.”** It was published as **US 2022/0284649** and issued on **7 January 2025** as **US Patent 12,190,425**. The assignee is Artificial Intelligence Foundation, Inc.; inventorship does not establish that Bach personally owns the patent or receives continuing royalties.

By 2022, Bach was a **principal AI engineer/principal researcher at Intel Labs**. Public biographies place his Intel period between AI Foundation and his later Liquid AI/CIMC work, but exact hiring and departure dates were not found. His public work increasingly shifted from peer-reviewed architecture papers toward long-form lectures and interviews about consciousness, agency, digital physics, and AGI.

### 2023–2026: Liquid AI, honorary doctorate, and CIMC

Bach became an **AI strategist/adviser at Liquid AI**, a company founded by researchers including Ramin Hasani and associated with “liquid neural networks.” In a 2025 interview Bach stressed that he was helping with strategy and long-horizon questions rather than writing the company's production code. Public records do not establish equity or compensation.

In **2024**, the **University of Oulu** conferred an **honorary doctorate** on Bach at its twelfth conferment ceremony. This honor is distinct from his earned doctorate at Osnabrück. No other major state order, learned-society fellowship, or formal prize was located.

On **30 May 2025**, Bach announced the **California Institute for Machine Consciousness (CIMC)**, an independent institute intended to develop testable theories and engineering approaches to machine consciousness. Its public materials describe Bach as founder and executive director. The institute's stated program includes architectures for agency and selfhood, empirical markers of consciousness, and cross-disciplinary exchange. Its full legal structure, donors, assets, staff payroll, and audited finances were not located in the consulted public record.

In November 2025, congressional releases of Epstein-related correspondence triggered renewed reporting about Bach's funding and messages. Bach published a response on 28 November. By early 2026 he said he had received death threats, lost an AI-company advisory role, and had two talks canceled. *Der Standard* identified the company as **Liquid AI**, which confirmed that the collaboration had ended.

As of August 2026, Bach remains publicly active through CIMC, lectures, interviews, and online writing. He lives in California. There is no documented retirement.

## Political, Religious, Intellectual, and Artistic Development

### Political and religious position

Bach's childhood straddled East German socialism and reunification. He describes a maternal Communist family tradition and a father hostile to the GDR regime, but no record was found of Bach holding party office, standing for election, lobbying for a state, or belonging to a political organization. His public political comments are eclectic and do not support a clean partisan classification.

He treats religions principally as evolved cultural “software”: systems that coordinate values, identity, behavior, and meaning. He discusses Christianity, Buddhism, animism, and mysticism as cognitive or cultural technologies, sometimes sympathetically, but no reliable source establishes formal religious membership or devotional practice. Claims that he is straightforwardly atheist, Buddhist, occultist, or a religious leader therefore go beyond the record.

### Computational functionalism and the self

Bach's central position is computational-functionalist: mental states are constituted by information-processing organization, not by a special noncomputational substance. The brain constructs a **phenomenal world model**, a model of attention, and a model of an agent or “self” acting within that world. Conscious experience is the system's access to this virtual model. In this account, the self is real as an implemented model and control interface, not as an indivisible metaphysical object.

He distinguishes:

- **intelligence**: model-building in the service of control;
- **agency**: selection of actions under needs and goals;
- **sentience**: evaluative or valenced experience, often linked to needs;
- **consciousness**: access to, and organization of, a model of attention and experience;
- **self-awareness/metacognition**: modeling the system's own processes and identity.

These are Bach's theoretical proposals, not settled neuroscientific definitions.

### Motivation and emotion

MicroPsi models needs—physiological, social, and cognitive—as variables that create motives. Emotion is not a separate irrational substance but a pattern of cognitive modulation: arousal, attention, resolution, activation, and action-readiness change as the system estimates needs and prospects. This view developed from Dörner's Psi theory and was extended in MicroPsi 2 and conversational-agent papers.

### Reality, simulation, and “cyber animism”

Bach often says the world of experience is a simulation generated by the brain. He does **not** simply assert that the external universe must be somebody else's computer simulation. In interviews he has treated that cosmological hypothesis as possible but less likely than an unsimulated physical substrate. The popular label “simulation theorist” thus obscures a distinction between **phenomenological simulation** and **cosmological simulation**.

His later “cyber animist” vocabulary proposes that nature can be described as layers of self-organizing computational agents—“spirits” understood as software-like control systems rather than necessarily supernatural beings. This is a philosophical metaphor and research program, not an empirically established discovery.

### AGI, risk, and values

Bach argues that advanced AI could be existentially dangerous but also that civilization may need greater intelligence to manage ecological, political, and technological crises. He is skeptical that permanent human values can be written as a short axiom set. His preferred direction is to ground values in the continued viability, complexity, and agency of future systems. He has often been more optimistic about building AGI and machine consciousness than AI-safety advocates who favor moratoria, but he does not deny risk.

### Artistic dimension

No professional visual art, music, film, or literary fiction by Bach was located. His artistic associations are familial—his father and wife are described as artists—and rhetorical: lectures use elaborate diagrams, metaphors, and philosophical storytelling.

### Institutional investigations

The 2020 **Goodwin Procter report for MIT** examined MIT's post-conviction acceptance of Epstein donations. It is the strongest institutional record for Bach's appointment and the $300,000 total. Bach declined to be interviewed. The report's focus was MIT governance, not a criminal investigation of Bach.

The **Harvard review** examined Epstein's ties to Harvard. It found Bach's intermittent access, research-scientist listing, missing visitor agreement, lack of Harvard salary or earmarked Harvard funds, and uncertainty about private support. Bach did not respond to Harvard's interview requests. Declining those interviews is documented but is not an admission.

### Released correspondence and disputed interpretation

The 2025 releases made private exchanges public. In them Bach entertained or stated propositions about race-linked developmental timing, possible genetic mechanisms, sex differences in interest, eugenic interventions, and managing population through child-rearing costs. The messages themselves are primary evidence that he wrote those words; they are not evidence that the empirical claims are correct.

In his November 2025 response, Bach said he was exploring a **“human scaling hypothesis”** inspired by developmental documentaries and preschool studies; he denied that race was a causal explanation, rejected judging individuals by ancestry or sex, emphasized that he lacked expertise in heritability, and described parts of the exchange as an effort to understand Epstein's worldview. He called Epstein a sociopath and said he had underestimated the ethical cost of dependence on him.

Competing interpretations should remain separate:

1. **Bach's account:** exploratory, often provocative private theorizing; attempts to probe a patron; no program of racial superiority; later text severed observational claims from racial causation.
2. **Critical account:** repeated, willing discussion of racial hierarchy, sex stereotypes, eugenics, and population engineering with a convicted offender cannot be reduced to neutral probing; Bach's later explanation does not erase the content or power relationship.
3. **Documentary minimum:** the patronage, messages, acknowledged knowledge of the conviction, and island visit are real. Intent, stable ideology, and the reliability of the proposed hypotheses cannot be read off the existence of an email alone.

No court has adjudicated those interpretations. No consulted source reports retraction of Bach's papers because of the funding, although at least two acknowledged Epstein/PED support.

### Other controversies and failures

- **Speculative scope.** Critics argue that Bach moves too freely among cognitive science, metaphysics, neuroscience, and civilizational prediction. A 2026 paper by Dejan Grba used a Bach Chaos Communication Congress talk as an example in a critique of “science-populist” narratives. This is one critic's analysis, not a disciplinary verdict.
- **Institutional status inflation.** Public biographies sometimes say simply that Bach “worked at MIT and Harvard.” The affiliations are real, but the Harvard review's missing agreement and the patron-funded nature of the appointments make the simplified formulation incomplete.
- **Incomplete implementation.** MicroPsi is influential as a cognitive architecture and theoretical synthesis but did not become a dominant industrial or neuroscientific model, and no demonstrated human-level system resulted. That is a historical outcome, not proof the research program is valueless.
- **No located lawsuits or criminal cases.** Searches did not identify a lawsuit, professional-license proceeding, plagiarism finding, patent dispute, bankruptcy, substance-abuse scandal, or criminal case against Bach.

## Reputation and Legacy

Because Bach is living, this is a current-reputation assessment rather than a posthumous legacy.

- **Technical legacy.** MicroPsi is a distinctive bridge from German action-regulation psychology to implementable cognitive architecture. Its importance lies in integrating needs, motivation, emotion, perception, planning, and world modeling, rather than in a single benchmark victory.
- **Philosophy of consciousness.** Bach is a prominent public exponent of computational functionalism, virtual self-models, and the possibility of synthetic consciousness. Much of his influence is through recorded talks and podcasts rather than conventional citation alone.
- **Institution-building.** Micropsi Industries translated the name and some adaptive-agent ambitions into industrial robotics; CIMC formalized his later machine-consciousness program.
- **Public following.** Long-form appearances created a substantial audience that treats him as a synthesizer or sage. Promotional “wizard” and “guru” labels coexist with criticism that his arguments can outrun empirical support.
- **Epstein shadow.** The patronage record permanently complicates narratives of independent ascent. It supplied real resources and elite institutional access; it did not originate his pre-2013 scholarship. The email releases shifted public attention from his theories to his judgment, private speculation, and financial dependence.
- **Unresolved evaluation.** It is too early to know whether CIMC will yield testable, adopted methods for machine consciousness or whether Bach's later work will remain primarily conceptual. Claims that his consciousness theory has been proven, refuted, or implemented at human level all exceed the current record.
