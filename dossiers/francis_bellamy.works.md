## Works by Francis Julius Bellamy

### Sermons, lectures, and social-reform texts

- **1889–1891:** *Articles in The Dawn: A Magazine of Christian Socialism and Record of Social Progress*. A dispersed body of Christian-socialist journalism rather than a single obra; the surviving scholarly account says Bellamy wrote “several articles,” but neither it nor the digitized run supplies a complete, secure author-title list, and some periodical matter was unsigned. ([Kubal, pp. 15–16](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/18838/1/14.pdf); [digitized issues](https://archive.org/details/TheDawn.VolIi1-Vol.Ii7Nov.1890))

- **1889–1891:** *Looking Backward*. A public-education lecture or class on Edward Bellamy’s novel, documented as part of Francis Bellamy’s Christian-socialist teaching program; no independent text or exact delivery date has been identified, so it resists treatment as a recoverable written work. ([Kubal, pp. 15–16](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/18838/1/14.pdf))

- **1889–1891:** *Jesus the Socialist*. A lecture or sermon—not a book—known from contemporary programmatic activity and later scholarship; no complete manuscript is known, and accounts disagree over whether it should be called a sermon, speech, lecture, or course topic. ([Kubal, pp. 15–16](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/18838/1/14.pdf); [Jones and Meyer, chap. 6](https://erenow.org/modern/pledge-history-pledge-of-allegiance/6.php))

- **1889–1891:** *What Is Christian Socialism?* A public lecture or class title associated with Bellamy’s educational work. It should not be confused with the Society of Christian Socialists’ surviving manifesto of the same name, whose authorship is not securely assigned to Bellamy. ([Kubal, pp. 15–16](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/18838/1/14.pdf); [Society history and manifesto](https://archive.org/details/WhatIsChristianSocialism))

- **1889–1891:** *The Subject of Labor in Light of the Bible*. A Christian-socialist lecture or class; no surviving fixed text or more exact date has been established in the consulted scholarship. ([Kubal, pp. 15–16](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/18838/1/14.pdf))

- **1890:** *Municipal Government*. A conference paper delivered at the Baptist Congress in New Haven and printed in its proceedings; it argues for extending democratic control as the remedy for municipal corruption. ([Baptist Congress archive inventory](https://sbhla.org/wp-content/uploads/40.pdf); [printed proceedings](https://baptiststudiesonline.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/Baptist-Congress-9-1890.pdf))

- **1891:** *The Tyranny of All the People*. A political essay in the July 1891 *Arena*, developed from Bellamy’s lecture topic *Socialism versus Anarchy*; the lecture and published essay are related manifestations, not safely interchangeable titles. ([The Arena, July 1891](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/19603/19603-h/19603-h.htm); [Kubal, pp. 15–16](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/18838/1/14.pdf))

### Patriotic protocol, manifesto, and ceremony

- **1892:** *National Public-School Celebration of Columbus Day*. A nationally distributed ceremonial protocol devised and promoted through *The Youth’s Companion*, combining procession, flag raising, salute, prayer, song, declamation, and address. Bellamy organized and largely wrote the program, but it was institutionally produced with James B. Upham, the magazine, educators, Congress, and the Harrison administration, so sole authorship of the whole Gesamtkunstwerk should not be claimed. ([Rochester Review](https://www.lib.rochester.edu/IN/RBSCP/Databases/Attachments/Reviews/1988/50-3/1988_Spring.pdf); [Curren and Dorn excerpt](https://www.rochester.edu/newscenter/parsing-the-pledge-of-allegiance-supreme-court-anniversary-324462/))

- **1892:** *Pledge of Allegiance* (first published as the “Salute to the Flag”). A 23-word performative manifesto embedded in the Columbus Day protocol: it asks speakers to enact national allegiance rather than merely describe it. Francis Bellamy’s authorship was long accepted and supported by his later narrative and papers, but a nearly identical pledge printed before his claimed August composition has prompted a recent rival attribution to the schoolboy Frank E. Bellamy; the question is therefore disputed, not closed. ([University of Rochester](https://www.rochester.edu/newscenter/parsing-the-pledge-of-allegiance-supreme-court-anniversary-324462/); [*Rochester Alumni Review*, 1927](https://www.lib.rochester.edu/IN/RBSCP/Databases/Attachments/Reviews/1927/5-3/1927_February.pdf); [discussion of the competing attribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pledge_of_Allegiance#Contested_authorship_of_the_pledge))

- **1892:** *The Meaning of the Four Centuries*. The approximately thousand-word principal address written for the National Public-School Celebration and delivered by pupils or designated speakers across the country. It is a ceremonial script and national-origin manifesto, not simply an essay for private reading. ([Rochester Review](https://www.lib.rochester.edu/IN/RBSCP/Databases/Attachments/Reviews/1988/50-3/1988_Spring.pdf))

### Editorial and publishing obra

- **1891–1895:** *Work for The Youth’s Companion*. A body of staff writing, patriotic publicity, and editorial labor rather than one securely bounded work; its central recoverable products are the 1892 celebration program, address, and pledge. Much of the magazine’s institutional or unsigned copy cannot responsibly be converted into individually titled Bellamy works. ([Jones and Meyer, chaps. 6–7](https://erenow.org/modern/pledge-history-pledge-of-allegiance/7.php); [*The Youth’s Companion* publication history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Youth%27s_Companion))

- **1897–1898:** *Editorial work for The Illustrated American*. An editorial body of work whose boundaries resist a catalogue of signed texts: Bellamy served as editor, and much of the magazine’s editorial voice was unsigned. One securely associated 1897 editorial advances racial hierarchy and immigration restriction, but the consulted sources identify it by volume, number, and page rather than a stable title, so no title is invented here. ([Jones and Meyer, chap. 6](https://erenow.org/modern/pledge-history-pledge-of-allegiance/6.php); [Oregon Humanities](https://www.oregonhumanities.org/rll/magazine/encore-fall-winter-2011/under-god/); [periodical history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Illustrated_American))

- **1900:** *The Duke of Stockbridge: A Romance of Shays’ Rebellion*. Francis Bellamy’s posthumous edition of Edward Bellamy’s serial novel: an editorial reconstruction and publication project, not an authored novel by Francis. Later textual scholarship found that he had substantially edited the serial; Joseph Schiffman’s 1962 edition restored the original text, making the 1900 book a disputed manifestation of Edward’s work and a distinct editorial obra by Francis. ([Mount Holyoke study, p. 25 n.10](https://ida.mtholyoke.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/995a10eb-b503-4877-bbe8-396df0286eaa/content); [Jones and Meyer, chap. 6](https://erenow.org/modern/pledge-history-pledge-of-allegiance/6.php))

### Books and compilations

- **1905:** *Presidents of the United States in the Century: From Jefferson to Fillmore*. A historical survey in the Nineteenth Century Series, covering the presidencies from Thomas Jefferson through Millard Fillmore; it is Bellamy’s principal conventionally authored book. ([digitized 1905 edition](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Francis_Bellamy_Presidents_of_the_United_States_in?id=9TIsAAAAYAAJ); [contemporary series list](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e0/Political_progress_of_the_nineteenth_century_%28IA_politicalprogres00mack%29.pdf))

- **1909:** *Effective Magazine Advertising: 508 Essays about 111 Advertisements*. An edited compilation and advertising-analysis project assembled from readers’ essays rather than 508 essays written by Bellamy himself. Bellamy’s obra here is selection, organization, and editorial framing, while the individual responses retain distributed authorship. ([Ephemera Society bibliography](https://www.ephemerasociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Ephemera-Journal-Vol17-Issue-1.pdf); [Cambridge History of American Literature](https://www.cur.ac.rw/mis/main/library/documents/book_file/digital-64523271a5a609.46157689.pdf))

### Retrospective account

- **1927:** *Story of the Origin of Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag*. Bellamy’s first-person retrospective in the *Rochester Alumni Review*, explaining his claimed composition of the pledge and the surrounding campaign. It is testimony about an earlier work as well as a separate autobiographical essay; because it was written decades later amid an authorship controversy, it is evidence, not neutral verification. ([*Rochester Alumni Review*, February 1927](https://www.lib.rochester.edu/IN/RBSCP/Databases/Attachments/Reviews/1927/5-3/1927_February.pdf); [Jones and Meyer bibliography](https://erenow.org/modern/pledge-history-pledge-of-allegiance/13.php))
