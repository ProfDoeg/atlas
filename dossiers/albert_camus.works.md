## Works by Albert Camus

### Narratives: novels, récits, and short fiction

- **1936–1938; published 1971:** *La Mort heureuse*. Camus’s first completed novel, abandoned rather than published and later edited from his papers; its status is therefore that of a superseded but substantial project, not a lifetime publication. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1940–1942:** *L’Étranger*. A novel—or, in Camus’s preferred generic vocabulary, a récit—whose stark first-person narration forms the narrative part of his “cycle of the absurd.” ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1941–1947:** *La Peste*. A novel presented as a chronicle; the form deliberately blurs fiction, collective testimony, allegory, and documentary report. ([Œuvres complètes, II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1956:** *La Chute*. A book-length dramatic monologue conventionally classified as a récit rather than a novel. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *L’Exil et le Royaume*. A deliberately ordered collection of six nouvelles and thus both a book-level obra and a constellation of separate narratives. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1954:** *La Femme adultère*. A nouvelle, first published separately before becoming the opening narrative of *L’Exil et le Royaume*. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *Le Renégat ou Un esprit confus*. A nouvelle cast as a violent interior monologue and incorporated into *L’Exil et le Royaume*. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *Les Muets*. A nouvelle about a failed strike and the silence separating workers from their employer, incorporated into *L’Exil et le Royaume*. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *L’Hôte*. A nouvelle whose political and ethical situation resists reduction to an allegory with a single meaning; it forms part of *L’Exil et le Royaume*. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *Jonas ou L’Artiste au travail*. A nouvelle and artist-parable incorporated into *L’Exil et le Royaume*. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *La Pierre qui pousse*. A nouvelle set in Brazil and the concluding component of *L’Exil et le Royaume*. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1953–1960; published 1994:** *Le Premier Homme*. An unfinished autobiographical novel found in manuscript after Camus’s death; it is an open project whose incompletion is intrinsic to the surviving object. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))

### Lyrical and philosophical essays

- **1932:** *La Philosophie du siècle*. Camus’s early essay on Henri Bergson, published in the student review *Sud*. ([Camus au jour le jour](https://camusaujourlejour.com/camus-en-1932/))
- **1932:** *Intuitions*. A youthful sequence of five prose meditations—*Incertitudes*, *Délires*, *La Volonté de mensonge*, *Souhait*, and *Retour sur moi-même*—preserved among the early writings; whether the sequence should be treated as a finished work is uncertain. ([Camus au jour le jour](https://camusaujourlejour.com/camus-en-1932/))
- **1936:** *Métaphysique chrétienne et néoplatonisme: Plotin et saint Augustin*. Camus’s diplôme d’études supérieures thesis: an academic dissertation rather than a philosophical book composed for publication. ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1937:** *L’Envers et l’Endroit*. Camus’s first book, a suite of five lyrical-autobiographical essays; the collection is an obra distinct from its component texts. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *L’Ironie*. A lyrical essay collected in *L’Envers et l’Endroit*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *Entre oui et non*. A lyrical-autobiographical essay collected in *L’Envers et l’Endroit*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *La Mort dans l’âme*. A travel and autobiographical essay collected in *L’Envers et l’Endroit*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *Amour de vivre*. A lyrical essay collected in *L’Envers et l’Endroit*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *L’Envers et l’Endroit*. The title essay within the collection of the same name; text and book are related but non-identical objects. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1936–1939:** *Noces*. A four-part collection of Mediterranean lyrical essays, published as a book in 1939. ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1936:** *Noces à Tipasa*. A lyrical essay and bodily meditation on the landscape, later the first component of *Noces*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1936:** *Le Vent à Djémila*. A lyrical essay later collected in *Noces*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *L’Été à Alger*. A lyrical and social essay later collected in *Noces*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1937:** *Le Désert*. A lyrical essay on Italian art and mortality, later collected in *Noces*. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1942:** *Le Mythe de Sisyphe: Essai sur l’absurde*. A book-length philosophical-literary essay; it argues through images, readings, and examples rather than operating as a systematic philosophical treatise. ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1939–1953; collected 1954:** *L’Été*. A constructed book of eight essays written over fourteen years, not a contemporaneous diary or single continuous treatise. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1939–1940; published 1946:** *Le Minotaure ou La Halte d’Oran*. A long city-portrait and lyrical essay, later revised for *L’Été*. ([Carnets chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnets_%28Albert_Camus%29))
- **1940:** *Les Amandiers*. A short lyrical and ethical essay later collected in *L’Été*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1946:** *Prométhée aux Enfers*. A mythic-political essay later collected in *L’Été*. ([Carnets chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnets_%28Albert_Camus%29))
- **1947:** *Petit Guide pour des villes sans passé*. A compact essay on Algerian cities, later collected in *L’Été*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1948:** *L’Exil d’Hélène*. A cultural and political essay opposing measure to modern excess, later collected in *L’Été*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1950:** *L’Énigme*. An essay on the public image of the “writer of the absurd,” later collected in *L’Été*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1952:** *Retour à Tipasa*. A lyrical essay of return and recovery, later collected in *L’Été*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1953:** *La Mer au plus près: Journal de bord*. A sea-voyage meditation written in the form of a log but fashioned as a lyrical essay; it closes *L’Été*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1951:** *L’Homme révolté*. A book-length philosophical and historical essay on revolt; its hybrid of intellectual history, polemic, and ethics resists the category of systematic philosophy. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *Réflexions sur la guillotine*. An abolitionist essay first issued with Arthur Koestler’s contribution in *Réflexions sur la peine capitale*; it is argument, testimony, and political intervention at once. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))

### Original theatre

- **1936:** *Révolte dans les Asturies*. An agitational collective play created by the Théâtre du Travail, published without an author’s name after its performance was prohibited; Camus participated substantially, but sole authorship cannot be assigned to him. ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1938–1958:** *Caligula*. An original play repeatedly rewritten across several materially different versions; the familiar 1944 publication is not a transparent substitute for the earlier and later states. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1943–1944:** *Le Malentendu*. An original tragedy in three acts, first staged and published in 1944. ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1947:** *L’Impromptu des philosophes*. A one-act satirical play signed “Antoine Bailly,” published only posthumously; its pseudonymous presentation long made it marginal to the recognized œuvre. ([Œuvres complètes, II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1948:** *L’État de siège*. A “spectacle” devised with Jean-Louis Barrault rather than a conventional authored play; Camus supplied the text, while the originating theatrical conception and first production were collaborative. ([Société d’Histoire du Théâtre](https://sht.asso.fr/avant-propos-posterite-du-theatre-du-siecle-dor-espagnol-chez-albert-camus-et-ses-contemporains/))
- **1949:** *Les Justes*. An original play in five acts based on the 1905 assassination of Grand Duke Sergei; it belongs to Camus’s cycle of revolt. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))

### Translations, adaptations, and theatrical manifestations

- **1935–1939:** *Théâtre du Travail / Théâtre de l’Équipe*. A collective theatrical project founded and animated by Camus: troupe, political experiment, repertory, acting practice, and production protocol rather than a single text. Its staged repertory included adaptations of Malraux’s *Le Temps du mépris*, Gorky’s *Les Bas-fonds*, Gide’s *Le Retour de l’enfant prodigue*, and Copeau’s version of *Les Frères Karamazov*; Camus’s exact authorial share varies by production. ([Théâtre en acte chronology](https://www.reseau-canope.fr/edutheque-theatre-en-acte/fileadmin/user_upload/pdm/2007/pdm_20_les_justes.pdf))
- **1952:** *La Dernière Fleur*. Camus’s French translation of James Thurber’s illustrated antiwar fable; authorship of the originating text and images remains Thurber’s, while Camus made its French verbal manifestation. ([Bibliography by category](https://classiques.uqam.ca/contemporains/melancon_marcel_j/albert_camus_1983_english/albert_camus_1983_biblio.html))
- **1953:** *Les Esprits*. Camus’s three-act adaptation of Pierre de Larivey’s Renaissance comedy, also realized as a stage production. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1953:** *La Dévotion à la croix*. A French version and stage adaptation of Calderón de la Barca’s drama; it is neither wholly Camus’s play nor a merely mechanical translation. ([Association de la Régie Théâtrale](https://regietheatrale.com/cpt_publications/albert-camus/))
- **1955:** *Un cas intéressant*. Camus’s stage adaptation of Dino Buzzati’s play, produced by Georges Vitaly; its source plot and Camus’s French theatrical object must be distinguished. ([Association de la Régie Théâtrale](https://regietheatrale.com/cpt_publications/albert-camus/))
- **1956:** *Requiem pour une nonne*. A two-part, seven-tableau dramatic adaptation of William Faulkner’s novel, translated and staged by Camus; text, mise-en-scène, and surviving director’s notebook make it a composite theatrical obra. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *Le Chevalier d’Olmedo*. Camus’s French translation, adaptation, and production of Lope de Vega’s play. ([Camus et le théâtre](https://www.livre-provencealpescotedazur.fr/ressources/dossiers-thematiques/une-annee-un-auteur-albert-camus-73/camus-et-le-theatre-207))
- **1959:** *Les Possédés*. A three-part adaptation of Dostoevsky’s novel, written and staged by Camus after a long gestation; it is his most fully self-initiated adaptation project. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))

### Manifestos, journalism, and public intervention

- **1938:** *Rivage: Revue de culture méditerranéenne*. A short-lived review founded and edited by Camus and colleagues; the made thing is an editorial-cultural platform rather than an individually authored book. ([Camus bibliography overview](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/bibliographies/))
- **1938–1940:** *Articles for Alger républicain and Le Soir républicain*. A large journalistic corpus comprising reportage, editorials, court reports, cultural criticism, and unsigned or collaboratively produced newspaper matter; it resists treatment as one work, while uncertain bylines prevent a wholly uncontested item-by-item canon. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1938–1939:** *Le Salon de lecture d’Alger républicain*. A corpus of literary notices and reviews, some unsigned, assembled as a separate editorial unit by the Pléiade; attribution is not equally secure for every item. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1939:** *Misère de la Kabylie*. A series of eleven investigative reports for *Alger républicain*, later reworked into *Actuelles III*; reportage here acts as political accusation rather than neutral description. ([Société des Études camusiennes](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/albert-camus/))
- **1939–1940:** *Le Soir républicain*. A daily newspaper made collectively by Pascal Pia, Camus, and their colleagues; Camus served as editor and editorialist, so the newspaper is partly his manifestation but cannot be assigned to him as sole author. ([Œuvres complètes, I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1943–1944:** *Lettres à un ami allemand*. Four polemical letters, three first circulated under clandestine conditions and all collected in 1945; their address is fictionalized, making the sequence essay, resistance act, and manifesto. ([Œuvres complètes, II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1944–1947:** *Combat editorials and articles*. A corpus of roughly 165 signed and unsigned newspaper pieces written while Camus was principal editorial voice and later editorial director; collective newspaper production makes some individual attributions less secure than the corpus as a whole. ([Paris Libraries bibliography](https://bibliotheques.paris.fr/userfiles/file/nouveautes/rainermariarilke/bibliocamus.pdf))
- **1944–1948; collected 1950:** *Actuelles I: Chroniques 1944–1948*. A retrospective political collection fashioned from journalism, speeches, interviews, and polemics; it is a new book-level obra rather than a simple facsimile archive. ([Pléiade contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1948–1953; collected 1953:** *Actuelles II: Chroniques 1948–1953*. A second constructed volume of public interventions on violence, revolt, communism, artistic freedom, and contemporary politics. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1955–1956:** *Articles for L’Express*. A weekly political-journalistic corpus, much of it addressing Algeria; Camus selected several of these interventions for *Actuelles III*. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1939–1958; collected 1958:** *Actuelles III: Chroniques algériennes*. A deliberately retrospective assemblage of reportage, open letters, speeches, and proposals concerning colonial Algeria; its discontinuous dates and genres are part of the work’s political form. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1956:** *Appel pour une trêve civile en Algérie*. A speech and public appeal intended to produce an immediate political effect: properly a manifesto-act, not merely a description of Camus’s position. It was later incorporated into *Actuelles III*. ([La Revue d’Histoire Militaire](https://larevuedhistoiremilitaire.fr/2022/08/03/la-guerre-dalgerie-a-travers-les-ecrits-dalbert-camus/))
- **1957–1959; unfinished:** *Actuelles IV*. A projected fourth collection of political interventions, including anti-totalitarian speeches, journalism, and appeals for condemned Algerians; Camus did not complete the book, so the project—not any later editorial reconstruction—is the work he made. ([Le Monde](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2025/03/02/albert-camus-une-presence-quand-le-monde-s-abandonne-a-l-oubli_6573157_3246.html))

### Prefaces, criticism, lectures, and ceremonial speech

- **1946:** *Preface to L’Espagne libre*. A contribution to a collective anti-Franco volume; the preface is an intervention attached to another, collaboratively authored book. ([Carnets chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnets_%28Albert_Camus%29))
- **1947:** *Preface to Poésies posthumes by René Leynaud*. A memorial-critical preface to the work of Camus’s Resistance friend. ([Carnets chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnets_%28Albert_Camus%29))
- **1947:** *Preface to Laissez passer mon peuple by Jacques Méry*. A political-literary preface attached to another author’s book. ([Carnets chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnets_%28Albert_Camus%29))
- **1944–1959:** *Articles, préfaces, conférences*. The Pléiade establishes four chronological corpora—1931–1944, 1944–1948, 1949–1956, and 1957–1959—containing numerous occasional texts, interviews, lectures, tributes, and prefaces. These are many texts rather than one work, but unstable titles, collaborative interviews, and unsigned periodical publication make the scholarly corpus a more honest unit than an invented definitive list. ([Pléiade, volumes I–IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1944:** *Introduction aux Maximes de Chamfort*. A critical introduction later retained among Camus’s principal critical essays. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1948:** *Avant-propos à La Maison du peuple de Louis Guilloux*. A critical-prefatory text attached to Guilloux’s work. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1951:** *Rencontres avec André Gide*. A memorial-critical essay rather than a biography or systematic study. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1952:** *L’Artiste en prison*. An essay on Oscar Wilde and the relation between artistic freedom, suffering, and judgment. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1955:** *Roger Martin du Gard*. A substantial critical preface written for Martin du Gard’s *Œuvres complètes* in the Bibliothèque de la Pléiade. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1958:** *Sur Les Îles de Jean Grenier*. A preface for the new edition of Grenier’s book and an act of intellectual homage to Camus’s former teacher. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1958:** *René Char*. A critical-homage essay on the poet and friend. ([Pléiade essays contents](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957:** *Discours de Suède*. A book-level pairing of Camus’s Nobel banquet speech and his Uppsala lecture; it is a publication constructed from two ceremonial oral manifestations. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **10 December 1957:** *Discours de réception du prix Nobel de littérature*. A ceremonial speech delivered at the Nobel banquet and subsequently printed as part of *Discours de Suède*. ([BnF](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/albert-camus-1913-1960-bibliographie))
- **14 December 1957:** *L’Artiste et son temps*. Camus’s Uppsala lecture, subsequently paired with his Nobel acceptance speech in *Discours de Suède*. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))

### Radio, image-text, and other composite objects

- **1949:** *Les Silences de Paris*. A radio play: an acoustic work made for broadcast, not simply a dramatic text awaiting the stage. The script survives among Camus’s posthumously collected writings. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1952–1954:** *Désert vivant*. Camus supplied the French text for an illustrated Walt Disney nature album; it is an image-text collaboration rather than an autonomous Camus book. ([French bibliography listing](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Camus))
- **1952–1959; published 1965:** *La Postérité du soleil*. A photographic-textual collaboration with photographer Henriette Grindat, initiated with René Char; because its prose, photographs, sequence, and delayed realization are mutually constitutive, it approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk more closely than a conventional essay collection. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1956:** *Director’s notebook for Requiem pour une nonne*. A working theatrical object containing scenic requirements and performance directions; it resists being called a literary work, but records Camus’s making of the production. ([BnF archive report](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/culture/article/2026/07/08/albert-camus-archives-including-manuscripts-forged-documents-and-letters-join-national-collection_6755257_30.html))

### Notebooks, fragments, and unrealized projects

- **1935–1959; published 1962–1989:** *Carnets*. Seven private working notebooks containing observations, drafts, plans, quotations, travel notes, and compositional protocols; “Carnets” is the posthumous editorial title, since Camus called them cahiers and did not finish them as a public work. ([Carnets history](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnets_%28Albert_Camus%29))
- **1935–1942:** *Carnets I*. The first posthumously established notebook volume; it is an editorial manifestation of several working cahiers, not a book Camus prepared for publication. ([Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/camus/))
- **1942–1951:** *Carnets II*. The second editorially established notebook volume, preserving the workshop behind several published works. ([Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/camus/))
- **1951–1959:** *Carnets III*. The third editorially established notebook volume, ending shortly before Camus’s death. ([Pléiade, volume IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1949–1956; posthumously collected:** *Textes épars*. A Pléiade editorial corpus of fragments and occasional writings rather than a titled work created by Camus; its resistance to the category “obra” is precisely its archival status. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1950s; posthumously published:** *Orgueil*. A surviving prose fragment placed among the posthumous writings; its brevity and unfinished state make it a project-fragment, not a completed narrative. ([Œuvres complètes, III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **1957–1959; posthumously collected:** *Textes épars*. A second Pléiade corpus of late fragments and occasional texts; the collective title belongs to the editors, not to a completed Camus obra. ([Œuvres complètes, IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Camus.cG.htm))
- **late 1950s; unfinished:** *Némésis*. A projected philosophical-literary essay associated with the contemplated “cycle of love” and inspired partly by Camus’s dialogue with René Char; only notes and fragments were made. ([Le Monde](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2025/03/02/albert-camus-une-presence-quand-le-monde-s-abandonne-a-l-oubli_6573157_3246.html))
- **undated project:** *Don Juan*. A contemplated play that was never realized; it exists as an intention in Camus’s theatrical planning and therefore as a project, not a completed text. ([Cambridge Companion to Camus](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-camus/camus-and-the-theatre/B4E8306050B7546A6D97E3C717419C97))
- **late 1950s; unrealized:** revisions of *Le Malentendu* and *L’État de siège*. Camus considered substantially reworking both plays but did not complete definitive new versions; these are projects rather than additional works. ([Cambridge Companion to Camus](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-camus/camus-and-the-theatre/B4E8306050B7546A6D97E3C717419C97))

### Correspondence and epistolary bodies

- **1932–1960:** *Correspondence with Jean Grenier*. A sustained exchange with Camus’s teacher and friend; the individual letters were acts of communication, while the named “correspondence” is a posthumous editorial body rather than a book Camus made.
- **1939–1947:** *Correspondence with Pascal Pia*. An epistolary record of friendship, journalism, publishing, and disagreement; authorship is necessarily divided between the two correspondents. ([Société des Études camusiennes bibliography](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/bibliographies/nouvelle-bibliographie/))
- **1944–1959:** *Correspondence with Maria Casarès*. A large reciprocal archive of love letters, published only in 2017; it is jointly made private communication, not a lifetime literary work. ([Société des Études camusiennes bibliography](https://www.etudes-camusiennes.fr/bibliographies/nouvelle-bibliographie/))
- **1944–1958:** *Correspondence with Roger Martin du Gard*. A reciprocal literary and personal correspondence; its later publication creates a book from letters that originally existed as separate communications.
- **1945–1959:** *Correspondence with Nicola Chiaromonte*. A reciprocal intellectual correspondence concerned with politics, freedom, and literature; it is a documentary body rather than a unitary authored obra.
- **1945–1959:** *Correspondence with Louis Guilloux*. A reciprocal friendship correspondence whose book form is posthumous and editorial.
- **1946–1959:** *Correspondence with René Char*. A jointly made exchange between two writers; the letters remain separate acts even when published as a collected correspondence. ([BnF archive report](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/culture/article/2026/07/08/albert-camus-archives-including-manuscripts-forged-documents-and-letters-join-national-collection_6755257_30.html))
- **1951–1959:** *Letters to Michel and Janine Gallimard and to Francine de Fréminville*. Published selections disclose additional epistolary bodies, but no edition can yet be treated as a complete inventory of every letter Camus made.
- **1930s–1959:** *Uncollected and unpublished correspondence*. The surviving archive occupies dozens of boxes and includes exchanges with writers, artists, publishers, politicians, family, and readers. It cannot honestly be named as a closed work or claimed complete: the letters are individual objects, and the archive remains an open documentary corpus. ([BnF archive report](https://www.lemonde.fr/en/culture/article/2026/07/08/albert-camus-archives-including-manuscripts-forged-documents-and-letters-join-national-collection_6755257_30.html))
