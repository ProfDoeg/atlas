## Works by Lucía Sánchez Saornil

### Poetic corpora and book-projects

- **1914–1931:** *Early modernist and ultraist poetry*. A dispersed body of poems and poetic prose published in periodicals, sometimes under the masculine pseudonym Luciano de San-Saor; later editors have reconstructed it differently, so neither the 1996 *Poesía* nor the 2020 *Corcel de fuego* should be mistaken for a book assembled by Sánchez Saornil herself. The corpus is not closed: eleven previously uncollected poems from 1915–1916 were reported in 2025. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/); [Navas Ocaña and Plaza-Agudo](https://ojs.unito.it/index.php/artifara/article/download/11592/10008/41003))
- **1925:** *Estuario*. An announced poetry-book project that apparently never appeared; its intention and title survive, but no securely established finished book does. ([Bibliographic summary](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1936–1938:** *Romancero de Mujeres Libres*. Her only poetry collection published during her lifetime: seven political romances previously circulating in a magazine, song sheets, or posters. Sources date the booklet variously to 1937 and July 1938; the surviving edition identifies Barcelona, Publicaciones de Mujeres Libres, July 1938. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1); [digital facsimile record](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/))
- **c. 1960s:** *Siempre puede volver la esperanza*. A late, unpublished manuscript folder rather than a lifetime publication; the posthumous critical edition treats it as a poetic sequence, but reports that Sánchez Saornil may have sent other poems to South America without retaining copies make completeness uncertain. ([2022-edition record](https://datos.bne.es/resource/a7167899); [bibliographic discussion](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **c. 1960s:** *Sonetos de la desesperanza*. A late manuscript sonnet-cycle preserved within the material subsequently edited as *Siempre puede volver la esperanza*; it is a sequence, not an independently published lifetime book. ([Critical-edition discussion](https://premisrecerca.uvic.cat/sites/default/files/webform/4aa6b64f54e9a45ad9906b46b391f3fb82bdcf56_TR.pdf))

### Individually identifiable poems and poetic prose

- **1914:** *Nieve*. A late-modernist poem and the earliest publication long recognized by scholarship, printed in *Avante* on 31 January 1914; the later discovery of other early material changes the known corpus but not this publication date. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/); [bibliographical transcription](https://jesuscarmena.wordpress.com/2023/04/29/lucia-sanchez-saornil-poesia/))
- **1915–1916:** *La balada del otoño* and ten other poems in *Avante*. These eleven early poems were absent from the principal collected editions until their recent recovery; the critical study explicitly presents them as newly found, so the earlier “complete” corpus is no longer complete. ([Navas Ocaña and Plaza-Agudo](https://ojs.unito.it/index.php/artifara/article/download/11592/10008/41003))
- **before 1919:** *Armonías de ocaso* / *Madrigal de los besos*. Two publication titles for versions of the same poem: the first signed with her name and the second, published a month later, as Luciano de San-Saor. They should not be silently counted as two wholly independent compositions. ([Sobrino](https://www.letra15.es/L15-15/L15-15-14-Angel.Luis.Sobrino-Lucia.Sanchez.Saornil.en.elespejo.de.su.obra.html))
- **1918:** *Las canciones ingenuas*. A short critical prose piece—not a poem—signed Luciano de San-Saor and published in *El Progreso* of Lugo on 26 May 1918. ([Sobrino](https://www.letra15.es/L15-15/L15-15-14-Angel.Luis.Sobrino-Lucia.Sanchez.Saornil.en.elespejo.de.su.obra.html))
- **1919:** *El madrigal de tus sortijas*. A transitional poetic prose or madrigal published in *Grecia* on 1 January 1919; its hybrid form resists a clean division between poem and short prose text. ([Navas Ocaña](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/descargaPdf/la-reescritura-del-yo-en-la-poesia-de-lucia-sanchez-saornil-textualizar-el-cuerpo-corporeizar-el-texto-1204118/))
- **1919–1920:** *Poema de la vida*. A transitional poem concerned with marriage and female constraint, published in *Grecia*. ([Navas Ocaña](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/descargaPdf/la-reescritura-del-yo-en-la-poesia-de-lucia-sanchez-saornil-textualizar-el-cuerpo-corporeizar-el-texto-1204118/))
- **1919–1920:** *Estaba enamorada de mayo*. A prose poem published in *Grecia*; the generic label matters because it is neither conventional article nor lineated verse. ([Navas Ocaña](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/descargaPdf/la-reescritura-del-yo-en-la-poesia-de-lucia-sanchez-saornil-textualizar-el-cuerpo-corporeizar-el-texto-1204118/))
- **1920:** *Nocturno de cristal*. An ultraist poem published in *Cervantes* in April and again in *Cosmópolis* in November; the signatures differ slightly, illustrating the unstable relation between her civil name and Luciano de San-Saor. ([Cervantes Virtual anthology](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/descargaPdf/antologia-poetica-1197108/))
- **by 1920:** *Madrigal de ausencia*. A modernist love poem preserved in the reconstructed poetic corpus; it is an individual periodical text, not a separate book. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/))
- **by 1920:** *Crepúsculo sensual*. A modernist erotic poem known through the reconstructed corpus. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/))
- **by 1920:** *Para adormir en sueños*. A modernist poem whose Juan Ramón Jiménez reference helps locate its early aesthetic context. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/))
- **1920s:** *El pueblo con luna*. A rural-setting poem published under Luciano de San-Saor; because the pseudonym also appears on non-erotic writing, its use cannot securely be reduced to disguise of lesbian address. ([Sobrino](https://www.letra15.es/L15-15/L15-15-14-Angel.Luis.Sobrino-Lucia.Sanchez.Saornil.en.elespejo.de.su.obra.html))
- **1920s:** *Hora*. An ultraist poem notable as the first poem she signed with her civil name in that phase and for its suppression of punctuation. ([Biographical-bibliographic account](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1920s:** *Deseo* / *Ofrenda*. Two substantially related versions of one composition; scholarship warns against treating the variant titles as straightforwardly separate works. ([Critical-edition discussion](https://es.scribd.com/document/637044784/2-SPVE-Ruptura-estereotipos-tradicionales))
- **1936:** *Romance de «La Libertaria»*. A political romance about María Silva Cruz, first published in no. 5 of *Mujeres Libres* and later placed first in the *Romancero*; it also circulated as a CNT/*Mujeres Libres* poster or song sheet. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1); [bibliographic summary](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1936:** *¡Madrid, Madrid, mi Madrid!*. A romance of Madrid’s antifascist resistance, broadcast on radio, printed in *Mujeres Libres*, issued separately as a song sheet, and later incorporated into the *Romancero*: one text with several material manifestations. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/); [Directa](https://directa.cat/tres-voltes-oblidada/))
- **1936–1937:** *¡Ay, rinconcito de Asturias!*. A political romance issued separately as a song sheet and included in the *Romancero*, although apparently not first printed in *Mujeres Libres*. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1); [bibliographic summary](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1936–1937:** *Romance de la vida, pasión y muerte de la lavandera de Guadalmedina*. A narrative political poem commemorating Encarnación Giménez and, through her, civilian victims of fascist violence; later included in the *Romancero*. The river-name is sometimes corrupted in secondary sources, but *Guadalmedina* is the established title. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1))
- **1936–1937:** *El 19 de julio*. A political romance commemorating the outbreak of social revolution, included in the *Romancero*. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1))
- **1936–1937:** *Romance de Durruti*. An elegiac political romance for Buenaventura Durruti, circulated independently as a propaganda poster before or alongside its inclusion in the *Romancero*; some sources also call the separate issue *Versos sobre Durruti*. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1); [bibliographic summary](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1936–1937:** *Pasión de Asturias*. A political romance included in the *Romancero* but apparently not previously printed in *Mujeres Libres*. ([García-Manso](https://dspace.library.uu.nl/bitstream/handle/1874/425273/v1304409.pdf?sequence=1))
- **1937:** *Himno de Mujeres Libres*. A lyric for collective singing, with music by E. Sanginés: therefore a collaborative song rather than merely a printed poem. It appeared as sheet music but was not one of the seven romances in the *Romancero*. ([Mujeres Libres no. 12](https://files.libcom.org/files/Mujeres%20Libres%2012.pdf); [critical discussion](https://premisrecerca.uvic.cat/sites/default/files/webform/4aa6b64f54e9a45ad9906b46b391f3fb82bdcf56_TR.pdf))

### Essays, manifestos, and assembled prose

- **1913:** *Untitled or presently unidentified article on women’s social condition*. The earliest prose text attributed to Sánchez Saornil in the reconstructed bibliography; because the consulted scholarly summaries do not establish a secure title, none should be invented. ([Biographical-bibliographic account](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1924:** *Untitled or presently unidentified article on women’s social condition*. A second early intervention recorded by the reconstructed bibliography, again without a securely supplied title in the consulted sources. ([Biographical-bibliographic account](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1929:** *La verdadera y la falsa juventud*. An essay defending artistic renewal and the avant-garde; its position makes it an important counterpoint to her later repudiation of literary vanguardism. ([Sobrino](https://www.letra15.es/L15-15/L15-15-14-Angel.Luis.Sobrino-Lucia.Sanchez.Saornil.en.elespejo.de.su.obra.html))
- **1933:** *Literatura, nada más*. A polemical essay in the newspaper *CNT* reassessing her ultraist past and arguing for socially committed writing. It acts as an aesthetic manifesto rather than merely describing a literary movement. ([Plaza-Agudo](https://uvadoc.uva.es/bitstream/handle/10324/58207/revistas_uva_es__ogigia_article_view_6664_5129.pdf?sequence=3))
- **1933:** *¡No más palabras! Actuemos contra la guerra*. An antiwar newspaper intervention in *CNT*; one modern transcription instead assigns it to *Solidaridad Obrera*, no. 1088, 11 October 1935, so its original date and venue require verification against the newspaper copies. ([La Campana author archive](https://www.revistalacampana.info/autor/lucia-sanchez-saornil/))
- **1935:** *La cuestión femenina en nuestros medios*. A five-part polemical series published in *Solidaridad Obrera* on 26 September and 2, 9, 15, and 30 October. It functions collectively as an anarchofeminist manifesto, although its first material form was five newspaper articles rather than a book. ([La Campana](https://www.revistalacampana.info/autor/lucia-sanchez-saornil/); [series documentation](https://libertamen.wordpress.com/2022/08/01/la-cuestion-de-las-mujeres-en-nuestros-circulos-1935-lucia-sanchez-saornil/))
- **1937:** *Horas de revolución*. An authorial pamphlet assembling previously published articles from *CNT* and *Mujeres Libres*. Bibliographies disagree between 1937 and 1938, probably reflecting distinct issues or imprints; the scholarly portal dates the initial Publicaciones Mujeres Libres volume to 1937. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/); [Sobrino](https://www.letra15.es/L15-15/L15-15-14-Angel.Luis.Sobrino-Lucia.Sanchez.Saornil.en.elespejo.de.su.obra.html))
- **1937:** *Es sólo un minuto*. A revolutionary newspaper article subsequently gathered in *Horas de revolución*; it remains an individual text as well as a component of the pamphlet. ([Bibliographic reference](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))
- **1937:** *La maternidad bajo el signo de la revolución*. A photo-reportage made with photographs by Kati Horna for *Umbral*; authorship is divided across media, so it is better understood as a collaborative journalistic manifestation than as prose alone. ([CNT study](https://www.cnt.es/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/cnt-417-marzo15.pdf))
- **1936–1938:** *Unsigned editorials and articles in Mujeres Libres*. Sánchez Saornil belonged to the founding editorial group, but unsigned collective texts cannot automatically be assigned to her; their resistance to individual authorship is part of their status. ([Revista de Literatura study](https://revistadeliteratura.revistas.csic.es/index.php/revistadeliteratura/article/download/1586/926?inline=1))
- **1946:** *Manifesto associated with an attempted reorganization of Mujeres Libres*. Testimony attributes an underground manifesto to a postwar attempt involving Sánchez Saornil, but the activity and authorship are incompletely documented; it should be retained as a disputed collective manifesto, not silently converted into a securely authored personal essay. ([Biographical-bibliographic account](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc%C3%ADa_S%C3%A1nchez_Saornil))

### Editorial, organizational, and social projects

- **1933–1934:** *CNT*. Sánchez Saornil helped make the newspaper as secretary of its editorial staff; this is editorial labor and a collective periodical project, not authorship of every unsigned item it printed. ([Sobrino](https://www.letra15.es/L15-15/L15-15-14-Angel.Luis.Sobrino-Lucia.Sanchez_Saornil.en.elespejo.de.su.obra.html))
- **1936–1938:** *Mujeres Libres* (magazine). Conceived with Mercedes Comaposada and Amparo Poch as a cultural and emancipatory periodical for working women; its thirteen published numbers and an additional unissued final number constitute a collective project rather than a sole-authored work. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/); [history of the project](https://revistadeliteratura.revistas.csic.es/index.php/revistadeliteratura/article/download/1586/926?inline=1))
- **1936–1939:** *Mujeres Libres* (organization/federation). A social and educational project co-founded by Sánchez Saornil, Comaposada, and Poch: not a text, but an intentionally made institution for women’s “captación” and “capacitación.” Its collective authorship must not be reduced to any one founder. ([Cervantes Virtual](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/lucia_sanchez_saornil/semblanza/); [Revista de Literatura study](https://revistadeliteratura.revistas.csic.es/index.php/revistadeliteratura/article/download/1586/926?inline=1))
- **1937–1939:** *Umbral*. A graphic weekly co-created editorially by Sánchez Saornil as editor-in-chief; its fusion of reporting and photographic documentation makes it a collective multimedia publication rather than a personal book. ([CNT study](https://www.cnt.es/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/cnt-417-marzo15.pdf))
- **1937–1939:** *Solidaridad Internacional Antifascista, Spanish section*. A humanitarian and propaganda organization that Sánchez Saornil helped establish and served first through press and propaganda work and later as secretary-general. It is a collective social project—made through coordination, relief, publications, and institutions—not an individually authored obra. ([Biographical study](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=8789298); [CNT study](https://www.cnt.es/wp-content/uploads/2015/03/cnt-417-marzo15.pdf))

### Visual and applied work

- **1940s–1960s:** *Paintings and photographic-design work*. Biographical evidence says Sánchez Saornil painted and undertook photographic design after returning clandestinely to Spain, and a family-held folder reportedly preserved some paintings. No consulted catalogue raisonné identifies a complete set of titles, dates, or present locations; this therefore remains a fragmentary body of practice that resists conversion into a list of discrete, securely titled works. ([Aganipe biographical account](https://jesuscarmena.wordpress.com/2023/04/29/lucia-sanchez-saornil-poesia/))
