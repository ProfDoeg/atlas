## Works by Jean-Jacques Rousseau

### Music, opera, ballet, melodrama, and musical arrangements

- **July–October 1730:** *Menuet*. An early musical composition known from the scholarly chronology but apparently unpublished and perhaps lost.
- **1731:** *[Fragments d’une cantate]*. Surviving fragments of a cantata; the bracketed title is editorial rather than authorial.
- **c. 1735:** *Un papillon caressait une rose*. A song setting published in 1737.
- **c. 1740:** *La Découverte du Nouveau Monde*. An unfinished opera; its prologue and other surviving material do not constitute a complete performable work.
- **May 1741?:** *Iphis*. An operatic project, also known as *Iphis et Anaxarète*; the dating is approximate and the work survives incompletely.
- **June 1743–July 1745:** *Les Muses galantes*. An opéra-ballet whose text and only part of whose music survive; completeness therefore resists determination from the surviving object.
- **1744:** *Canzoni da batello*. A collection of Venetian boat songs that Rousseau edited rather than wholly composed; its place among his works is consequently editorial and musical, not simply authorial.
- **October–December 1745:** *Les Fêtes de Ramire*. A court divertissement made by reworking Voltaire and Rameau’s *La Princesse de Navarre*; authorship is collaborative and derivative rather than exclusive.
- **Before May 1751:** *[Symphonie à cors de chasse]*. An orchestral composition performed at the Concert Spirituel; the bracketed descriptive title and the absence of a surviving score make it a documented but materially elusive work.
- **1752:** *[Salve Regina]*. A sacred motet for solo voice and orchestra; the score is not known to survive.
- **Spring–summer 1752:** *Le Devin du village*. A one-act intermède for which Rousseau made both words and music—a genuine composite theatrical obra rather than merely a text.
- **July–August 1752:** *Les Saturnales*. A musical-stage project surviving only in fragmentary form.
- **August 1757:** *[Ecce sedes hic tonantis]*. A motet known from documentary evidence but apparently lost.
- **Autumn 1762; revised for performance by 1770:** *Pygmalion*. A scène lyrique or mélodrame joining spoken text, gesture, and instrumental music; Horace Coignet supplied most of the surviving performance music, while only two musical numbers are attributed to Rousseau, making it a collaborative proto-Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1769:** *[Motet à deux voix]*. A two-voice motet listed without a fixed authorial title and apparently no longer extant.
- **c. 1770:** *[Sur la musique militaire]*. A musical project or manuscript recorded in the scholarly chronology but not published and not securely recoverable as a finished work.
- **1772:** *[Leçon des ténèbres]*. A sacred vocal composition documented without a surviving complete score.
- **c. 1774–1776:** *Daphnis et Chloé*. An unfinished opera composed with Corancez; its collaborative and fragmentary state makes the project, rather than a completed score, the obra.
- **1775:** *Le Printemps de Vivaldi*. An arrangement of material from Vivaldi’s concerto; it is an act of recomposition and copying rather than an autonomous composition in the usual sense.
- **1776:** *Six nouveaux airs du Devin du village*. Six supplementary airs made for Rousseau’s earlier intermède.
- **Various dates, chiefly the 1770s; published 1781:** *Les Consolations des misères de ma vie*. A posthumously assembled collection of songs and airs, some original and some arrangements; the collection as such is editorial, so individual attribution and status are not uniform.
- **Chiefly 1750s–1770s:** *Music-copying manuscripts*. Rousseau produced numerous manuscript copies for patrons, including the prince de Conti. These are material artifacts he made, but normally not compositions of which he was the author.

### Theatre, tales, fiction, and literary experiments

- **c. 1732–1746:** *Narcisse, ou L’Amant de lui-même*. A prose comedy developed over many years and staged in 1752; Rousseau subsequently attached a polemical preface to its publication.
- **1742:** *Le Nouveau Dédale*. A literary or theatrical project surviving as an incomplete text.
- **1743–May 1744:** *Les Prisonniers de guerre*. A comedy, preserved but not published during Rousseau’s lifetime.
- **Autumn 1747:** *L’Engagement téméraire*. A comedy.
- **Autumn 1747:** *Arlequin amoureux malgré lui*. A brief comedy or commedia-inspired theatrical fragment.
- **September 1747:** *Le Persifleur*. An unfinished periodical-literary project cast in satirical and conversational forms.
- **1754:** *La Mort de Lucrèce*. A prose tragedy left unpublished in Rousseau’s lifetime.
- **1755–1756:** *La Reine fantasque*. A satirical fairy tale; it circulated and appeared in more than one eighteenth-century printing.
- **June 1756:** *Les Amours de Claire et de Marcellin*. A short narrative or pastoral tale.
- **June 1756:** *Le Petit Savoyard, ou La Vie de Claude Noyer*. A narrative fragment or projected life.
- **1756–1758:** *Julie, ou La Nouvelle Héloïse*. An epistolary novel, but also a carefully organized complex of letters, editorial personae, notes, music, and proposed images.
- **Late 1758–October 1759:** *Les Amours de Milord Édouard Bomston*. An unfinished narrative offshoot or supplement to *Julie*.
- **Early 1759:** *Préface dialoguée de La Nouvelle Héloïse*. A dialogue about the truth, fictionality, and moral operation of the novel; omitted from its first edition and published posthumously.
- **October 1760:** *Sujets d’estampes pour La Nouvelle Héloïse*. A protocol for twelve illustrations: Rousseau specifies what images should be made, making it part visual program and part instructional text rather than an illustrated book itself.
- **June 1762; resumed in 1768:** *Émile et Sophie, ou Les Solitaires*. An unfinished epistolary sequel to *Émile*; its status as project is essential because only two letters were completed.
- **June 1762; revised in summer 1768:** *Le Lévite d’Éphraïm*. A prose “poem” or biblical imitation whose genre deliberately lies between narrative, scripture, and poetic prose.

### Poetry, songs, inscriptions, and occasional writing

- **1730–1731:** *[Fragments d’une épître]*. Fragments of an early verse epistle; the title is editorial.
- **August 1731:** *[Épître à Gaudard]*. An early verse epistle recorded but not extant as a published work.
- **c. 1735:** *Sur Dieu*. A short religious poem.
- **c. 1736–1737:** *Vers à la louange des religieux de la Grande Chartreuse*. Occasional devotional verse.
- **1737:** *Virelai à Madame la baronne de Warens*. A fixed-form lyric addressed to Madame de Warens.
- **Summer 1738?:** *Le Verger de Mme la baronne de Warens*. A verse epistle or descriptive poem; the date remains approximate.
- **c. 1741:** *Épître à M. Borde*. A verse epistle.
- **c. 1741:** *À Mademoiselle Th. qui ne parlait jamais à l’auteur que de musique*. An occasional poem.
- **c. 1741:** *Énigme*. A verse riddle.
- **c. 1741:** *[Fragments amoureux]*. Editorially grouped fragments of love poetry, not a securely titled work.
- **c. 1741:** *Pour Madame de Fleurieu*. Occasional verse.
- **1741–1742:** *Épître à Monsieur Parisot*. A verse epistle.
- **14 March 1742:** *À Fanie*. A poem.
- **June 1742:** *Vers sur le commandement de Bohême*. Occasional verse.
- **April 1744:** *Fragment d’une épître à M. Borde*. A surviving fragment rather than a complete poem.
- **Summer 1744:** *Pour la Zulietta*. Occasional verse associated with Rousseau’s Venetian period.
- **1746?:** *Imitation libre d’une chanson italienne de Métastase*. A free poetic imitation, not a literal translation; its date is uncertain.
- **Autumn 1746:** *L’Allée de Silvie*. A pastoral poem.
- **c. 1750:** *Épître à M. de l’Étang*. A verse epistle.
- **1758–1759?:** *[Inscription mise au bas d’un portrait de Frédéric II]*. An epigrammatic inscription; its title is descriptive and its dating uncertain.
- **1766?:** *Quatrain pour un de ses portraits*. An occasional quatrain, uncertainly dated.
- **22 January–24 November 1770:** *« Pauvres aveugles… »*. A quatrain known by its opening words rather than a fixed title.
- **May 1770:** *Épitaphe de deux amants*. A verse epitaph.
- **1770:** *« Quiconque sans urgente nécessité… »*. A short moral verse known by its opening words rather than an authorial title.

### Political philosophy, political projects, and interventions

- **October 1749–March 1750:** *Discours sur les sciences et les arts*. A prize discourse arguing that cultivated arts and sciences have corrupted morals; rhetorically it operates as a public intervention as well as an essay.
- **1750?:** *[Fragments sur la liberté]*. Editorially titled philosophical fragments; neither date nor completeness is secure.
- **May 1751:** *Lettre à Monsieur l’abbé Raynal*. A public defense connected with the controversy over the first *Discours*.
- **1751:** *Discours sur cette question : « Quelle est la vertu la plus nécessaire au héros ? »*. A competition discourse on heroic virtue.
- **September–October 1751:** *Observations de Jean-Jacques Rousseau de Genève sur la réponse qui a été faite à son Discours*. Usually called the *Réponse à Stanislas*, this is a polemical reply within the first-*Discours* controversy.
- **1 November 1751:** *Lettre de J.-J. Rousseau de Genève à M. Grimm, sur la réfutation de son Discours par M. Gautier*. A public polemical letter.
- **December 1751–March 1752:** *Dernière Réponse à M. Bordes*. A final published intervention in the first-*Discours* controversy.
- **1752:** *Parallèle entre les deux républiques de Sparte et de Rome*. A fragmentary exercise in comparative political history.
- **1752:** *Histoire de Lacédémone*. An unfinished history of Sparta.
- **1752?:** *Sur les richesses*. A short political-moral fragment of uncertain date.
- **Spring 1752?:** *[Parallèle de Socrate et de Caton]*. An editorially titled fragment comparing exemplary ancient figures.
- **Late 1753–12 June 1754:** *Discours sur l’origine et les fondements de l’inégalité parmi les hommes*. A competition discourse and conjectural history of social inequality, published with a dedication to Geneva.
- **Late 1754:** *Économie politique* / *Discours sur l’économie politique*. An *Encyclopédie* article that became an independent political treatise; the double title reflects its two material lives.
- **1754–1759:** *Écrits sur l’abbé de Saint-Pierre*. An editorial grouping of extracts, judgments, drafts, and fragments rather than one authorially constituted book.
- **1754–1759:** *Extrait du projet de paix perpétuelle de M. l’abbé de Saint-Pierre*. An analytical reconstruction and condensation of Saint-Pierre’s peace project.
- **1754–1759:** *Jugement sur la paix perpétuelle*. Rousseau’s critical assessment of that project.
- **1754–1759:** *Polysynodie de l’abbé de Saint-Pierre*. An extract and recasting of Saint-Pierre’s proposal for government by councils.
- **1754–1759:** *Jugement sur la polysynodie*. Rousseau’s critical evaluation of the proposal.
- **c. 1756–1758:** *Que l’état de guerre naît de l’état social*. A fragmentary political argument; its relation to the other writings on war is editorially reconstructed.
- **1758–1759:** *De l’état de guerre*. An unfinished political treatise assembled from manuscripts; it resists being treated as a completed authorial book.
- **c. 1758–December 1760:** *Du contrat social, ou Essai sur la forme de la république*. The large unfinished “Geneva manuscript,” an earlier project distinct from the subsequently published *Contrat social*.
- **1759–1761:** *Du contrat social, ou Principes du droit politique*. A political treatise designed to act as a compact statement of legitimate sovereignty and law; it is also the completed remnant of a much larger projected *Institutions politiques*.
- **April–September 1762:** *[Projet de réponse à la question de la Société économique de Berne : « Quel peuple a été jamais le plus heureux ? »]*. An unfinished competition response, preserved under an editorial descriptive title.
- **October 1763–May 1764:** *Lettres écrites de la montagne*. Nine public letters defending Rousseau’s religious and political writings and attacking Geneva’s government; their polemical intervention gives them a manifesto-like force.
- **December 1763–July 1764?:** *Histoire du gouvernement de Genève*. An unfinished historical-political project of uncertain terminal date.
- **Autumn 1764–October 1765:** *Projet de constitution pour la Corse*. A commissioned constitutional project; it is a design for institutions rather than a constitution ever enacted.
- **October 1770–June 1771:** *Considérations sur le gouvernement de Pologne*. A commissioned program of political reform whose prescriptions make it partly a protocol for national and civic formation.

### Education, morality, religion, and natural philosophy

- **c. 1735–1738:** *Traité de la sphère*. A pedagogical treatise on astronomy and cosmography.
- **c. 1735–1738:** *Cours de géographie*. Teaching materials or a pedagogical manual rather than a conventionally finished book.
- **c. 1736:** *Prières*. A small group of devotional texts rather than a single securely constituted work.
- **c. 1736–1738:** *Chronologie universelle*. A historical-chronological teaching project, preserved in manuscript.
- **20 September 1738:** *Réponse au mémoire anonyme intitulé « Si le monde que nous habitons est une sphère »*. A scientific or cosmographical response.
- **Late 1740:** *Mémoire présenté à Monsieur de Mably sur l’éducation de M. son fils*. A practical educational memorandum.
- **c. 1740:** *Projet pour l’éducation de Monsieur de Sainte-Marie*. A protocol for educating an individual pupil; it remains a project rather than a general treatise.
- **1745:** *Idée de la méthode dans la composition d’un livre*. A methodological fragment associated with Rousseau’s research for Madame Dupin.
- **1745–1749?:** *Institutions chimiques*. A substantial manuscript course or treatise on chemistry; its date and degree of completion remain uncertain.
- **1752:** *Oraison funèbre de S.A.S. Monseigneur le duc d’Orléans*. A funeral oration.
- **March 1752?:** *Conseils à un curé*. A brief prescriptive religious text whose title describes its advisory function.
- **1756–1759:** *Morale sensitive, ou le matérialisme du sage*. A projected treatise on controlling moral life through physical surroundings; it is known principally as an unrealized project and should not be mistaken for a completed book.
- **October 1756:** *Réponse à un naturaliste*. A short philosophical-religious response, published only in the twentieth century.
- **c. 1756:** *Fiction, ou Morceau allégorique sur la révélation*. A philosophical-religious allegory; its title and textual boundaries have an editorial history.
- **c. 1756–1758:** *De l’art de jouir*. An unfinished moral essay.
- **Spring 1757:** *[Lettre sur la vertu]*. An editorially titled philosophical letter or fragment.
- **November 1757–spring 1758:** *Lettres morales* / *Lettres à Sophie*. A series of moral-philosophical letters; the alternate title reflects their addressee and manuscript history.
- **Late 1757; revised c. 1762–1765:** *Lettres à Sara*. Moral and religious letters revised over several years.
- **October 1758–1759?:** *Notes sur De l’esprit d’Helvétius*. Marginal and reading notes; they are an intellectual artifact but resist classification as an independently composed work.
- **Late 1758–May 1759:** *Émile (manuscrit Favre)*. An early manuscript state of the educational project; it is a distinct textual object but not a separate completed book.
- **Late 1758–October 1759:** *Émile, ou De l’éducation*. A hybrid educational treatise, philosophical fiction, novel of formation, and religious intervention; its refusal of a single genre is intrinsic to the obra.
- **Late 1762:** *[Sur le péché d’Adam et le salut universel]*. An editorially titled theological fragment.
- **July 1762?:** *[Fragment sur la condamnation de l’Émile]*. An unfinished response to the suppression of *Émile*; both date and title are editorial.
- **1763?:** *[Notes tirées des Œuvres morales de Plutarque]*. Reading notes, not a freestanding authorial work.
- **Summer 1764?:** *[Maximes extraites de l’Émile]*. Extracted maxims whose status is closer to a derivative compilation than a new work.
- **March 1765:** *Oraison funèbre d’Isabelle Guyenet*. A funeral oration.
- **1766?:** *Pensées d’un esprit droit et d’un cœur vertueux*. An attributed compilation of maxims whose authorial status is problematic: it should not be treated uncritically as a book deliberately constituted by Rousseau.

### Music theory, language, and theatrical criticism

- **August 1741–August 1742:** *Projet concernant de nouveaux signes pour la musique*. A proposed notation system submitted to the Académie des sciences; both project and protocol, since it designs and instructs the use of a new symbolic practice.
- **August–September 1742:** *[Mémoire adressé à Dortous de Mairan, Hellot et Fouchy]*. A memorandum defending the new musical notation; the title is supplied from its function and recipients.
- **Before November 1742:** *Dissertation sur la musique moderne*. A published explanation and demonstration of Rousseau’s numerical notation.
- **1745:** *[Lettre sur l’opéra italien et français]*. An unfinished or unsent comparative essay in letter form; its title is editorial.
- **December 1748–June 1751:** *Articles sur la musique pour l’Encyclopédie*. A corpus of encyclopedia articles rather than one work; Rousseau later reworked many entries for his *Dictionnaire de musique*.
- **March–April 1752:** *Lettre à M. Grimm au sujet des remarques ajoutées à sa lettre sur Omphale*. A music-critical letter.
- **Summer 1752–1753:** *Lettre sur la musique française*. A public polemic in the Querelle des Bouffons, acting almost as a manifesto for Italianate melody against French musical practice.
- **September–October 1753:** *Lettre d’un symphoniste de l’Académie royale de musique à ses camarades de l’orchestre*. A satirical intervention written under a musical persona.
- **1753–20 December 1764:** *Dictionnaire de musique*. A reference work developed from the *Encyclopédie* articles but extensively recast and enlarged.
- **1754–1763:** *Essai sur l’origine des langues*. A speculative essay joining language, music, climate, society, and history; its dating and intended relation to the second *Discours* have long been debated.
- **1755:** *Examen de deux principes avancés par M. Rameau*. A technical critique of Rameau’s harmonic theory.
- **Autumn 1755:** *L’Origine de la mélodie*. An unfinished music-theoretical manuscript whose independent boundaries and intended title are editorial questions.
- **February–20 March 1758:** *Lettre à d’Alembert sur les spectacles*. A book-length public letter on theatre, morals, and Geneva.
- **Early 1758:** *[Fragment sur les effets théâtraux]*. A theatrical fragment formerly misidentified as the *Fragment sur la puissance infinie de Dieu*; the changed title records a substantive scholarly reattribution of its subject.
- **1758 and 1763:** *De l’imitation théâtrale*. A fragmentary essay on dramatic imitation.
- **1761:** *Prononciation — Remarque lexicologique*. A brief linguistic note.
- **August 1774:** *Extrait d’une réponse du petit faiseur à son prête-nom, sur un morceau de l’Orphée de Gluck*. A satirical music-critical response written through fictionalized voices.
- **Winter 1774–March 1775:** *Fragment d’observations sur l’Alceste italien de M. le chevalier Gluck*. An unfinished critical essay on Gluck’s opera.

### Autobiography, self-portraiture, and self-defense

- **c. 1756–1762:** *Mon portrait*. A collection of autobiographical and self-analytical notes rather than a continuous finished narrative.
- **4, 12, 26, and 28 January 1762:** *Lettres à Malesherbes*. Four autobiographical letters that collectively form a crafted account of Rousseau’s character, vocation, and retreat.
- **1763?–1765:** *Les Confessions, Books I–IV*. The first stage of the autobiography; the uncertain starting date reflects manuscript evidence.
- **1764:** *Préambule du manuscrit de Neuchâtel*. A discarded or alternative opening to the *Confessions*, materially distinct from the final version.
- **1766–1768:** *Les Confessions, Books V–VI*. The second stage of composition.
- **August 1768:** *Sentiments du public sur mon compte dans les divers états qui le composent*. A tabular or analytical self-defense cataloguing supposed public attitudes toward Rousseau.
- **October 1769–late 1770:** *Les Confessions, Books VII–XII*. The concluding stage of the autobiography; publication was posthumous.
- **December 1770:** *Discours pour introduire la lecture des Confessions*. A performative preface composed for Rousseau’s private readings: it frames and authorizes the act of public self-disclosure.
- **1772–February 1776:** *Rousseau juge de Jean-Jacques: Dialogues*. Three dialogues staged as an inquiry and trial of “Jean-Jacques”; simultaneously autobiography, forensic self-defense, and dramatic philosophical fiction.
- **Late 1775:** *Du sujet et de la forme de cet écrit*. A framing text for the *Dialogues*.
- **April 1776:** *À tout Français aimant encore la justice et la vérité*. A manifesto-like appeal intended to recruit a reader and defender for the *Dialogues*.
- **Summer 1776:** *Histoire du précédent écrit*. An account of Rousseau’s failed attempt to deposit the *Dialogues* at Notre-Dame; part narrative, part documentary self-defense.
- **September 1776–12 April 1778:** *Les Rêveries du promeneur solitaire*. Ten meditative “walks”; the tenth was left unfinished at Rousseau’s death, so the work’s incompleteness is material rather than merely editorial.

### History, geography, research, and translations

- **April 1731:** *Lettre de Corchut à l’empereur Sélim*. A translation or adaptation of an attributed historical letter.
- **Summer 1737?:** *[Traduction de l’Ode de Jean Puthod]*. A translation of an ode; both date and supplied title remain qualified.
- **1745:** *Essai sur les événements importants dont les femmes ont été la cause secrète*. Historical research written for Madame Dupin’s projected work on women; its authorship is Rousseau’s, but its intended incorporation into another person’s book complicates its status.
- **1745:** *Un ménage de la rue Saint-Denis*. A historical or social anecdote prepared in the same collaborative research context.
- **1745:** *Sur les femmes*. Notes or an essay for Madame Dupin’s unfinished project, not originally an independent book.
- **1745–1751:** *Notes sur divers ouvrages pour trois livres de Madame Dupin*. A research corpus concerning women, friendship, and Montesquieu’s *Esprit des lois*; these are made texts but resist treatment as autonomous works.
- **1745–1751:** *[Extrait du onzième livre de la Géographie de Strabon]*. An extract or translation prepared from Strabo; the title is descriptive.
- **1752, March–April:** *[Essai de traduction d’une épître d’Horace]*. An incomplete translation exercise.
- **1753–late 1754:** *Traduction du premier livre de l’Histoire de Tacite*. A translation of Book I of Tacitus’s *Histories*.
- **September 1754:** *Pèlerinage autour du Léman*. A travel-related narrative or projected description of the Lake Geneva region.
- **1754 and 1756:** *[Fragments d’une histoire du Valais]*. Fragments from an unrealized regional history; the dates and textual grouping reflect more than one stage.
- **March 1758?:** *Traduction de l’Apocolocyntose de Sénèque*. A translation of Seneca’s satire; the chronology’s spelling varies, and the exact date is uncertain.
- **Late 1756–early 1757:** *Notes de lecture des Lettres sur les Anglais et les Français de Béat de Muralt*. Reading notes rather than a freestanding work.
- **Autumn 1768:** *Olinde et Sophronie tiré du Tasse*. A prose adaptation or translation from Tasso.
- **1768–1769:** *Essai de traduction du chant I de la Jérusalem délivrée du Tasse*. An incomplete translation of the first canto.
- **Undated:** *Traduction de fragments de Platon*. A group of translation fragments rather than a single securely dated work.

### Botany and living-paper objects

- **Spring–summer 1765:** *Plantes herborisées avec M. Neuhaus*. A documented group of collected plant specimens; it is a collaborative material practice, not simply a text.
- **September–October 1765:** *Flora Petrinsularis*. A projected flora of the Île Saint-Pierre, apparently unfinished or lost; what survives most securely is the project.
- **October 1769–December 1771?:** *Herbier du Musée des Arts décoratifs de Paris*. A mounted botanical collection; its objecthood, arrangement, labels, and specimens make it an obra distributed between nature and manufacture.
- **June 1770–April 1772:** *Herbier de la Bibliothèque centrale de Zurich*. A botanical collection assembled as a physical sequence of specimens and annotations.
- **1770–1778:** *Herbiers*. Numerous botanical collections assembled, annotated, retained, sold, or given away. They resist treatment as one work: surviving sets were dispersed and sometimes rearranged, and some traditional attributions include specimens supplied by other botanists.
- **22 August 1771–April 1774?:** *Lettres sur la botanique*. Eight pedagogical letters to Madeleine-Catherine Delessert; collectively they are both correspondence and a protocol for learning to observe and classify plants.
- **April 1772:** *Herbier pour Julie de La Tour*. A small presentation herbarium made as a botanical gift.
- **c. 1773–1778:** *Herbier Jean-Jacques Rousseau, Neuchâtel*. A large surviving assemblage now containing 1,242 specimens and more than 4,000 annotations; contributions by Jean-Baptiste-Christophe Fusée-Aublet and later rearrangements mean it cannot be treated as an exclusively or immutably authored object.
- **1770s, probably c. 1777:** *Dictionnaire des termes d’usage en botanique*. An unfinished botanical dictionary comprising roughly 184 terms; it is a working lexicographical project, not a completed publication.
- **1770s:** *Cartes à jouer botaniques*. Plant specimens mounted on playing cards and used for study, exchange, and teaching; these hybrid objects join collection, notation, image, and protocol.
- **1770s:** *Petits herbiers offerts à des correspondantes*. A series of presentation objects rather than one fixed work; surviving examples must be treated individually because their contents and later provenance differ.

### Legal, diplomatic, administrative, and cryptographic artifacts

- **27 June 1737:** *Testament*. A legal instrument: a text intended to alter the disposition of property rather than describe it.
- **3 March 1739:** *Mémoire pour répéter l’héritage de son frère*. A legal memorandum asserting an inheritance claim.
- **March 1739:** *Mémoire à Son Excellence Monseigneur le gouverneur de Savoie*. An administrative petition or memorandum.
- **March 1739:** *[Mémoire sur un projet de diligence par le Mont-Cenis]*. A practical transport proposal known under a descriptive title and apparently unpublished.
- **19 April 1742:** *Mémoire remis à M. Boudet Antonin*. A personal or administrative memorandum identified by its recipient and date rather than a literary title.
- **September 1743–July 1744:** *Dépêches de Venise*. Diplomatic dispatches written while Rousseau served the French ambassador; they are official state documents as well as authored prose.
- **8 March 1758:** *Reconnaissance et obligation*. A legal acknowledgment and obligation, included in the collected works as a biographical document but resistant to classification as a literary work.
- **20 and 28 January 1763:** *Deux lettres au maréchal duc de Luxembourg contenant une description du Val-de-Travers*. Two descriptive private letters that later acquired collective work-status through publication.
- **29 January 1763:** *Testament et note sur sa maladie*. A legal instrument paired with a personal medical memorandum.
- **January 1765:** *Notes sur le Sentiment des citoyens*. Defensive annotations responding to an anonymous attack.
- **March–April 1765:** *Déclaration relative à M. le pasteur Vernes*. A public declaration intended to establish a position in a personal and religious dispute.
- **25–30 August 1765:** *La Vision de Pierre de la Montagne, dit le Voyant*. A satirical-prophetic pamphlet fashioned as a vision.
- **10 July 1766:** *Lettre à David Hume*. A long accusatory letter crafted for possible public circulation, occupying the border between private correspondence and polemical publication.
- **November 1766:** *Déclaration destinée à un journal*. A public statement or manifesto-like intervention designed for periodical publication.
- **Late 1766–early 1767:** *Chiffres à chiffrer et à déchiffrer, I*. A cryptographic key or protocol for encoding and decoding messages.
- **March–April 1767:** *Chiffres à chiffrer et à déchiffrer, II*. A second cryptographic protocol.
- **9 April 1768?:** *Note mémorative sur la maladie et la mort de M. Deschamps*. A commemorative factual memorandum of uncertain precise date.
- **26 February 1770:** *Lettre à Saint-Germain*. A petitioning or justificatory letter later treated as a public document.
- **23 January 1774:** *Déclaration relative à différentes réimpressions de ses ouvrages*. A public declaration concerning unauthorized or misleading reprints; it acts to regulate attribution and publication rather than merely report them.

### Correspondence as a dispersed body of work

- **1732–1778:** *Correspondance*. At least 2,412 surviving private letters by Rousseau are included in the modern tricentennial edition, while R. A. Leigh’s 52-volume *Correspondance complète* also prints incoming letters, third-party correspondence, documents, doubtful items, and “phantom” letters. The corpus is therefore a dispersed body of writing rather than one authorially closed work, and its boundaries depend on whether public letters, drafts, lost letters, and received letters are counted.

(Geneva: Slatkine; Paris: Champion, 2012), especially the edition’s [chronologie détaillée des œuvres](https://rousseau.slatkine.com/chronology.php), [complete thematic contents](https://rousseau.slatkine.com/worksIdx.php), and [editorial presentation](https://rousseau.slatkine.com/intro.php). This scholarly chronology supplies the titles, composition ranges, publication dates, lost works, fragments, and projects used above.
- Société Jean-Jacques Rousseau, [“Les œuvres”](https://www.sjjr.ch/en/les-oeuvres), overview of the five-volume Bibliothèque de la Pléiade *Œuvres complètes* and the newer chronological critical edition.
- Bernard Gagnebin and Marcel Raymond et al., eds., *Jean-Jacques Rousseau: Œuvres complètes*, 5 vols., Bibliothèque de la Pléiade (Paris: Gallimard, 1959–1995), bibliographical structure summarized by the [Société Jean-Jacques Rousseau](https://www.sjjr.ch/en/les-oeuvres).
- R. A. Leigh, ed., *Correspondance complète de Jean-Jacques Rousseau*, 52 vols. (Geneva/Oxford, 1965–1998), described by the [Voltaire Foundation](https://www.voltaire.ox.ac.uk/publication/correspondance-compl%C3%A8te-de-rousseau-49/); compared with the Tricentennial edition’s 2,412-letter private-correspondence corpus in its [editorial presentation](https://rousseau.slatkine.com/intro.php).
- Enrico Natale et al., [RousseauOnline](https://www.rousseauonline.ch/about.php), digital edition of the 1780–1789 *Collection complète des œuvres de J. J. Rousseau*, used to distinguish Rousseau’s posthumous collected edition from later editions and reprints.
- [Les Herbiers de Rousseau](https://www.lesherbiersderousseau.org/), scholarly digital catalogue of surviving botanical collections, especially the record for the [Neuchâtel herbarium](https://www.lesherbiersderousseau.org/herbarium/106) and its mixed authorship, physical extent, annotations, and dating.
- Muséum national d’Histoire naturelle, [“Herbier dit ‘de Jean-Jacques Rousseau’”](https://www.mnhn.fr/fr/herbier-dit-de-jean-jacques-rousseau), consulted for the material and attributional status of Rousseau-associated herbaria.
