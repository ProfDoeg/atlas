## Works by Ian Lancaster Fleming

### James Bond novels

- **1953:** *Casino Royale*. A spy novel introducing James Bond; the first manifestation of Fleming’s sustained Bond project. [1][2]
- **1954:** *Live and Let Die*. A James Bond novel. [1][2]
- **1955:** *Moonraker*. A James Bond novel, issued in one American paperback as *Too Hot to Handle*; that is an alternative edition-title, not another obra. [1][2]
- **1956:** *Diamonds Are Forever*. A James Bond novel. [1][2]
- **1957:** *From Russia, with Love*. A James Bond novel. [1][2]
- **1958:** *Dr. No*. A James Bond novel. [1][2]
- **1959:** *Goldfinger*. A James Bond novel. [1][2]
- **1961:** *Thunderball*. A James Bond novel produced from a contested collaborative film project; Kevin McClory and Jack Whittingham’s contributions generated prolonged litigation, so unqualified sole authorship of the underlying story is disputed even though the published novel bears Fleming’s name. [1][3]
- **1962:** *The Spy Who Loved Me*. An experimental James Bond novel narrated principally by Vivienne Michel rather than Bond; Fleming became dissatisfied with it and resisted its adaptation and reprinting. [1][3]
- **1963:** *On Her Majesty’s Secret Service*. A James Bond novel and the middle part of the informal “Blofeld trilogy.” [1][2]
- **1964:** *You Only Live Twice*. A James Bond novel and the concluding part of the informal “Blofeld trilogy.” [1][2]
- **1965:** *The Man with the Golden Gun*. A posthumously published James Bond novel left in a less fully revised state than Fleming’s earlier books; the extent to which others edited the text has been debated, although no persuasive evidence establishes another author. [1][3]

### James Bond short fiction

- **1959:** *Quantum of Solace*. A James Bond short story first published in *Cosmopolitan*; it is largely a framed tale told to Bond rather than a conventional espionage adventure. [1][2]
- **1959:** *From a View to a Kill*. A James Bond short story, later collected in *For Your Eyes Only*. [1][2]
- **1960:** *For Your Eyes Only*. A James Bond short story, distinct from the collection bearing the same title. [1][2]
- **1960:** *Risico*. A James Bond short story. [1][2]
- **1960:** *The Hildebrand Rarity*. A James Bond short story whose ostensible object is a rare fish; its ending deliberately resists complete resolution. [1][2]
- **1962:** *The Living Daylights*. A James Bond short story first published in *The Sunday Times Magazine*. [1]
- **1963:** *The Property of a Lady*. A James Bond short story first printed in Sotheby’s annual *The Ivory Hammer*. [1]
- **1963:** *007 in New York*. A hybrid miniature—part Bond story, part city sketch and culinary itinerary—first published in the *New York Herald Tribune* under the title *Agent 007 in New York*. [1]
- **1965:** *Octopussy*. A James Bond short story serialized after Fleming’s death in the *Daily Express*. [1]

### Author-shaped collections

- **1960:** *For Your Eyes Only*. A book-object gathering five Bond stories: *From a View to a Kill*, *For Your Eyes Only*, *Quantum of Solace*, *Risico*, and *The Hildebrand Rarity*. It is a collection Fleming made, not a sixth story. [1]
- **1966–2002:** *Octopussy and The Living Daylights*. A posthumous, unstable collection: the 1966 book contained two stories, *The Property of a Lady* entered the 1967 paperback, and *007 in New York* entered editions in 2002. Its changing contents make it a succession of manifestations rather than a single fixed authorial object. [1]

### Children’s fiction

- **1964–1965:** *Chitty-Chitty-Bang-Bang: The Magical Car*. A children’s adventure written for Fleming’s son and first manifested as three small volumes; later one-volume editions do not constitute additional works. [1][2]

### Investigative and travel books

- **1957:** *The Diamond Smugglers*. A nonfiction book developed from Fleming’s interviews with John Collard of the International Diamond Security Organisation and from associated journalism; its form lies between reportage, interview narrative, and crime exposé. [1][2]
- **1963:** *Thrilling Cities*. A travel book assembled from Fleming’s *Sunday Times* journey around major cities; it is reportage filtered through an emphatically personal itinerary rather than a neutral guidebook. [1][2]
- **1960; published 2017:** *State of Excitement: Impressions of Kuwait*. A commissioned travel-book project suppressed before publication after the Kuwaiti authorities objected to it. The surviving typescript is an unfinished public manifestation but a completed—and rejected—project. [11]

### Early and non-Bond fiction

- **1925:** *The Ordeal of Caryl St. George*. A short story printed anonymously in *The Wyvern*, a school magazine Fleming helped produce at Eton; it is the earliest known manifestation of his fiction in print. [5]
- **c. 1927:** *Death, on Two Occasions*. An early, unpublished story written while Fleming studied in Austria. Its manuscript status and relationship to the differently titled *A Poor Man Escapes* are not consistently explained by secondary accounts, so they should not silently be treated as the same text. [12]
- **c. 1927:** *A Poor Man Escapes*. One of Fleming’s earliest surviving stories, set in snowy Vienna and left unpublished during his lifetime; the manuscript is held in the Michael L. VanBlaricum collection at the University of Illinois. [4]
- **c. 1951 or 1961:** *The Shameful Dream*. An unpublished-in-Fleming’s-lifetime story about journalist Caffery Bone. Nicholas Shakespeare dates it to the early 1950s, Henry Chancellor proposes 1961, and Mike VanBlaricum argues for about 1951; its date is therefore genuinely disputed. [4][13]

### Wartime protocols, reports, and institutional projects

- **1940–1941:** *Operation Goldeneye*. An intelligence contingency project devised to maintain communications with Gibraltar should Spain enter the war or Germany occupy the territory. It is an operational project rather than a literary work, and its surviving files are institutional documents rather than a single stable text. [14]
- **1940:** *Operation Ruthless*. A proposed deception operation for capturing a German naval Enigma machine and code material. Fleming drafted the plan, but it was not executed; its non-realization is part of the project’s status. [14]
- **1941:** *Memorandum to Colonel Donovan*. A protocol-like memorandum advising William J. Donovan on the organization of an American intelligence service. Claims that Fleming “wrote the charter” of the later OSS overstate a document created within a larger institutional process. [5][6]
- **1942:** *The Dieppe Raid*. A wartime account or memorandum, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- **1940s:** *Intrepid*. A wartime piece concerning William Stephenson, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- **1942–1945:** *30 Assault Unit*. Fleming helped conceive and direct the intelligence-gathering commando formation through orders and target intelligence. The unit itself is a collaborative military project, not Fleming’s sole obra and not reducible to one text. [14]
- **1944–1945:** *T-Force*. Fleming participated in developing the intelligence-targeting concept that became T-Force. Like 30 Assault Unit, it resists treatment as an individually authored “work.” [14]
- **Undated wartime manifestation:** *Letter to The Times*. A wartime letter collected under this title in *Talk of the Devil*; the collection’s contents establish the object but do not supply a date in their title list. [6]

### Crime and espionage journalism

- *The Secrets of Interpol*. Investigative journalism later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Airline Detective*. Crime journalism later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *The Great Policeman*. A profile or crime-related essay later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Soviet Espionage Inc.* An essay on organized Soviet intelligence activity, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Gary Powers and the Big Lie*. Cold War journalism about the U-2 affair, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]

### Writing, publishing, and literary criticism

- **1952–1964:** *The Book Collector*. A bibliographical quarterly conceived and founded by Fleming and later sustained by a wider editorial institution. It is properly an editorial project and serial manifestation, not a book authored by Fleming. [7]
- *Foreign News*. Fleming’s contribution to *The Kemsley Manual of Journalism*, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*; it is a professional protocol explaining the organization or practice of foreign reporting. [6][15]
- *Mudscape with Figures*. An essay on journalism or writing, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Bang Bang, Kiss Kiss*. An essay about thriller writing and the Bond formula, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *The Heart of the Mata*. A literary or journalistic essay later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Trouble in Havana*. A piece on writing or publishing, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- **1959:** *Raymond Chandler*. A memorial-critical essay on Fleming’s friend and fellow novelist, first published in *The London Magazine* and later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [4][6]
- *The Guns of James Bond*. A composite textual object built around Fleming’s correspondence with firearms expert Geoffrey Boothroyd. Because Boothroyd’s letters are integral to it, it is not straightforwardly Fleming’s sole-authored work. [4][6]
- **1962:** *The Seven Deadly Sins*. Fleming’s introductory or framing essay for the anthology he conceived and edited under the same title. [6][8]
- **1962:** *The Seven Deadly Sins*. An anthology project commissioned and edited by Fleming, with essays by seven other writers. The book is an editorial obra but not a sole-authored text. [8]
- *The Art, or Craft, of Writing Thrillers*. An instructional essay—effectively a writer’s protocol—later collected in *Talk of the Devil* and sometimes cited under the shortened title *How to Write a Thriller*. [6]
- *Some Caen, Some Cain’t*. A literary essay, its punning title referring to James M. Cain, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]

### Travel, marine life, and treasure journalism

- *Where Shall John Go?* A travel essay later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- **1953:** *Diving Through Twenty-two Centuries*. Underwater-archaeology journalism about Jacques Cousteau’s excavation of an ancient Mediterranean wreck, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6][9]
- *Eldollarado*. Treasure or travel journalism later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- **1953:** *Treasure Hunt at Creake Abbey*. Reportage on an unsuccessful search with mine detectors for buried treasure in Norfolk; the absence of treasure is part of the made narrative, not a failed classification. [6][9]
- *The Caves of Adventure*. Travel-adventure journalism later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Full Fathom Five*. Marine or underwater journalism later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- **1956:** *Adventure in the Sun*. A sequence of Jamaican nature and travel journalism, later collected as a composite section in *Talk of the Devil*. Its constituent newspaper manifestations are not consistently distinguished in the collected table of contents. [6][9]
- **1957:** *More Adventures in the Sun*. A further sequence of Jamaican journalism rather than a single homogeneous essay. [6][9]
- **1957:** *My Friend the Octopus*. A *Sunday Times* natural-history memoir about an octopus Fleming observed at Goldeneye; in collected form it falls within the broader adventure-in-the-sun material. [9]
- **1957:** *He Sells Sea-Shells…*. Travel and marine-life journalism from Fleming’s Indian Ocean journey. [9]
- **1958:** *Treasure Hunt in Eden I: Pirate Gold*. The first article in Fleming’s three-part Seychelles reportage. It is journalism, despite sometimes being called a “short story.” [6][9]
- **1958:** *Treasure Hunt in Eden II: Butterflies and Beachcombers*. The second article in the Seychelles sequence. [9]
- **1958:** *Treasure Hunt in Eden III: Gold or No Gold?*. The concluding article in the Seychelles sequence. [9]
- **1965:** *Introducing Jamaica*. Fleming’s introductory essay for the posthumous composite volume *Ian Fleming Introduces Jamaica*. The volume combines Fleming’s framing presence with writing and images by others and is therefore an editorial, multimedia manifestation rather than his sole-authored book. [6][10]
- *The Perplexing Date Line*. Travel journalism later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]

### Essays, prefaces, addresses, and occasional texts

- *The Organisation of a Foreign News Service*. A professional protocol describing how an international news operation should be structured, later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Partner! You Have Triumphed My Ace*. A short occasional text later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Dangerous Know-how*. An essay later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Preface to The Education of a Poker Player*. Fleming’s prefatory contribution to Herbert O. Yardley’s book; it is part of another author’s book-object, not a Fleming book. [6]
- *Nightmare Among the Mighty* / *Nightmare Among the Enemy*. An essay whose title appears as *Nightmare Among the Mighty* in the publisher’s contents and as *Nightmare Among the Enemy* in at least one catalogue record; the title variance should remain visible. [6]
- *When Did You Stop Eating Your Wife?* An essay or occasional piece later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Death Is So Permanent*. An essay later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Automobilia*. An essay on motoring later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *If I Were Prime Minister*. A speculative political essay later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Six Questions*. A short interview-like or questionnaire text later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]
- *Envoi*. A concluding occasional text later collected in *Talk of the Devil*. [6]

### Screen projects and story premises

- **1958–1961:** *James Bond television-series project*. Fleming prepared episode premises for a proposed television series that was never produced; several premises were subsequently reworked into the stories collected in *For Your Eyes Only*. The unrealized series is a project rather than a completed screen work. [3]
- **1959–1961:** *Thunderball screenplay project*. A collaborative film treatment and screenplay developed by Fleming, Kevin McClory, Jack Whittingham, Ivar Bryce, and Ernest Cuneo in changing combinations. No single draft can stand without qualification as Fleming’s sole work; the project later fed both Fleming’s novel and McClory’s film rights. [3]
- **1966:** *The Poppy Is Also a Flower*. A film developed from an anti-narcotics story premise attributed to Fleming and released after his death. Fleming did not write the finished screenplay, so it is best classified as a posthumous manifestation “based on an idea by Ian Fleming,” not as a Fleming-authored film. [3]

### Posthumous archival constructions

- **2008; expanded 2025:** *Talk of the Devil: The Collected Writings of Ian Fleming*. An editorial construction by Fergus Fleming and Kate Grimond gathering fiction, wartime papers, journalism, essays, letters, and occasional writing. Its editors explicitly said that they did not attempt to collect every overlooked scrap or make a definitive journalism corpus, so the volume is evidence for works but cannot by itself prove absolute completeness. [4][5][6]
- **2015:** *The Man with the Golden Typewriter: Ian Fleming’s James Bond Letters*. A posthumous selection edited by Fergus Fleming from correspondence Fleming made. The individual letters are Fleming’s texts, but the titled book and its arrangement are the editor’s later obra. [7]
