### Events: choreographic manifestations and protocols

- **1964–2009:** *Events*. A corpus of more than 700—and in some Trust accounts nearly 800—site-responsive performances assembled from existing and new choreographic material, beginning with *Museum Event No. 1*. An Event is not a single repeatable work: each manifestation recombined movement, music, performers, duration, and site, so the corpus resists both a definitive title list and conventional work-counting. [MCT-BIO]
- **1964:** *Museum Event No. 1*. The first named Event, made for Vienna’s Museum des 20. Jahrhunderts with Cage; its variable sixty-to-ninety-minute structure established the Event as a protocol for recombination rather than a fixed repertory dance. [MCT]
- **later career:** *MinEvents*. Shorter authorized arrangements of excerpts from Cunningham dances. They are derivative manifestations assembled for particular dancers and occasions, not stable new choreographies by Cunningham in the same sense as the repertory works above. [MCT-LIC]

### Books, conversations, notebooks, and textual objects

- **1968:** *Changes: Notes on Choreography*. Experimental artist’s book edited by Frances Starr, combining notes, diagrams, photographs, scores, and reflections. It is simultaneously an obra about process and a material manifestation of Cunningham’s choreographic thinking, not a conventional continuous treatise. [CHANGES]
- **1980/1985:** *The Dancer and the Dance: Merce Cunningham in Conversation with Jacqueline Lesschaeve*. Book-length conversation first published in French and later in English; its dialogic authorship belongs to Cunningham and Lesschaeve rather than to Cunningham alone. [EAI-B]
- **1982:** *Going to the Dance*. Collection of Cunningham’s essays and statements. It is a textual obra assembled from pieces written on different occasions rather than a single sustained argument. [BIB]
- **1984:** *A Pictures Book for John Cage Xmas 1984*. Spiral-bound notebook of choreographic notation given privately to Cage; the 2012 publication is a posthumous facsimile edition, not the date of the original object. [NYPL-PB]
- **2002:** *Other Animals: Drawings and Journals*. Artist’s book collecting Cunningham’s animal drawings with selected journal entries, edited with David Vaughan; it joins visual and written observation without treating either as illustration of the other. [OA]

### Drawings, journals, and choreographic notation

- **dates not comprehensively catalogued:** *Animal drawings*. A prolific corpus of individual drawings, selections of which were gathered in *Other Animals*. No consulted catalogue raisonné supplies a defensible item-by-item list, so this corpus resists being falsely converted into individually titled “works.” [OA]
- **dates not comprehensively catalogued:** *Personal journals*. Manuscript journals containing observations, travel records, sketches, and working thoughts. Published selections are manifestations of a larger archival corpus, not the whole textual obra. [OA]
- **throughout his career:** *Choreographic notes, charts, diagrams, and chance-operation materials*. These are working texts and protocols through which dances were generated and transmitted. Some are autonomous visual objects, but many resist being called works at all because their primary status is operational evidence of another work’s making. [MCT-ARC]
- **1984:** *Notation drawings for Pictures*. Arrows, lines, and figures plotting movement and stillness, preserved in *A Pictures Book for John Cage Xmas 1984*. They function at once as drawings, mnemonic protocol, and gift. [NYPL-PB]

### Techniques, systems, and digital projects

- **developed from the 1940s onward:** *Cunningham Technique*. A teachable system for training coordination, rhythmic accuracy, spatial clarity, and the independent articulation of torso and legs. It is a transmissible protocol and body of practice rather than a single fixed text or choreography. [TECH]
- **1989 onward:** *LifeForms/DanceForms choreographic practice*. Cunningham used computer animation software to devise and test movement that could then be translated into dancers’ bodies. He did not make the software itself; his obra here is the sustained choreographic project conducted through it. [MCT-BIO]
- **1998:** *Hand-drawn Spaces*. Three-screen motion-capture animation developed with Paul Kaiser and Shelley Eshkar. Authorship is collaborative, and its moving digital bodies make it neither simply a dance nor simply a film. [MCT-BIO]
- **2000/2008:** *Loops* motion-capture data. Digital capture of Cunningham’s hand choreography, later released under a Creative Commons licence. It is a reusable dataset and protocol derived from the 1971 dance, not an unproblematic second “original” work. [MCT-BIO]
- **2009:** *Mondays with Merce*. Webcast series presenting Cunningham’s teaching, repertory, rehearsal processes, and conversations. It is a serial media project made with a production team rather than a sole-authored film. [MCT-MEDIA]

### Institutional obra

- **1953–2011:** *Merce Cunningham Dance Company*. Cunningham founded and artistically directed the company as the principal vehicle for making, performing, and transmitting his choreography. The company is an institutional obra and long-duration project, but not his creation alone: its dancers, musicians, designers, administrators, and boards continually remade it. [MCT-BIO]
- **1964–2012:** *Cunningham Dance Foundation*. Administrative and educational structure formed to sustain the company, school, touring, and archive. It belongs to Cunningham’s institutional project while remaining a collective legal entity rather than a personal artwork. [LEGACY]
- **planned before 2009; executed 2010–2011:** *Legacy Plan*. Protocol for documenting dances, undertaking a final world tour, closing the company, and transferring its repertory and archives after Cunningham’s death. Its force is performative and administrative: the text caused an institution to end while enabling the obra to survive. [LEGACY]
