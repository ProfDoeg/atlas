## Works by Wilhelm Reich

### Psychoanalytic and sex-economic texts

- **1919:** *Die Spezialisierung des Ärztestandes: Eine Erwiderung*. Reich’s first published article, a medical-policy intervention rather than a psychoanalytic work.
- **1919:** *Zur „Aufklärung“ im Kampf gegen die Geschlechtskrankheiten*. An article on sexual education and venereal-disease prevention.
- **1919–1922:** *Meine Kindheit* and *Die Katastrophe*. Autobiographical manuscripts later incorporated into the editorially constructed volume *Passion of Youth*; they were not books published by Reich.
- **1920:** *Über einen Fall von Durchbruch der Inzestschranke in der Pubertät*. A clinical article derived partly from Reich’s autobiographical material.
- **1920:** *Libido-Konflikte und Wahngebilde in Ibsens „Peer Gynt“*. A psychoanalytic study first presented as Reich’s inaugural paper to the Vienna Psychoanalytic Association and published only posthumously.
- **1921:** *Kindliche Tagträume einer späteren Zwangsneurose*. A clinical case study of childhood fantasy and later obsessional neurosis.
- **1922:** *Über Spezifität der Onanieformen*. A psychoanalytic article on forms of masturbation.
- **1922:** *Zwei narzißtische Typen*. A short characterological study.
- **1922:** *Der Koitus und die Geschlechter*. A sexological essay, later translated as “Coition and the Sexes.”
- **1922:** *Trieb- und Libidobegriffe von Forel bis Jung*. A historical and theoretical survey of concepts of drive and libido.
- **1923:** *Zur Triebenergetik*. A theoretical article on the energetics of instinct.
- **1924:** *Über Genitalität vom Standpunkt der psychoanalytischen Prognose und Therapie*. An extended clinical essay on genitality, prognosis, and therapy.
- **1925:** *Weitere Bemerkungen über die therapeutische Bedeutung der Genitallibido*. A continuation of Reich’s argument that genital functioning has diagnostic and therapeutic significance.
- **1925:** *Der triebhafte Charakter: Eine psychoanalytische Studie zur Pathologie des Ich*. A monograph on the “impulsive character,” subsequently absorbed into the posthumous collection *Early Writings*.
- **1926:** *Die Quellen der neurotischen Angst*. A psychoanalytic essay revising the relation among libido, sexual stasis, and anxiety.
- **1927:** *Die Funktion des Orgasmus: Zur Psychopathologie und zur Soziologie des Geschlechtslebens*. Reich’s first book-length formulation of orgasm theory. It is not the same work as the substantially rewritten 1942 English book bearing the translated main title.
- **1929:** *Dialektischer Materialismus und Psychoanalyse*. A theoretical manifesto joining psychoanalysis to Marxist dialectical materialism; first published as an article and revised as a booklet in 1934.
- **1929:** *Der genitale und der neurotische Charakter*. A study of character as a libido-economic formation, later incorporated into *Charakteranalyse*.
- **1929:** *Die Stellung der Psychoanalyse in der Sowjetunion*. A report and political assessment arising from Reich’s study journey to the Soviet Union.
- **1930:** *Geschlechtsreife, Enthaltsamkeit, Ehemoral: Eine Kritik der bürgerlichen Sexualreform*. A polemical book about adolescent sexuality, abstinence, marriage, and social reform; later English editions appeared under the materially altered title *The Sexual Revolution*.
- **1931:** *Liebe verboten*. An anonymously issued mass-circulation propaganda pamphlet of the organization Reich directed. Individual authorship is uncertain, so it resists attribution as a conventional Reich work.
- **1931:** *Sexualpolitische Plattform des Deutschen Reichsverbandes für proletarische Sexualpolitik*. An organizational platform: a manifesto intended to constitute a political program, not merely describe one.
- **1932:** *Der Einbruch der Sexualmoral: Zur Geschichte der sexuellen Ökonomie*. An anthropological and political monograph, revised in 1935 and again for the posthumous English *The Invasion of Compulsory Sex-Morality*.
- **1932:** *Der sexuelle Kampf der Jugend*. A political-educational booklet addressed to young people and organized around practical sexual demands.
- **1933:** *Charakteranalyse: Technik und Grundlagen für studierende und praktizierende Analytiker*. A clinical treatise assembled partly from earlier papers. Its 1945 and 1949 editions add major new strata, making “the work” an evolving construction rather than a stable text.
- **1933:** *Massenpsychologie des Faschismus*. A political-psychological book explaining fascism through authoritarian character formation and sexual repression. Reich extensively revised and enlarged it in 1934 and 1946, and the editions should not be treated as interchangeable.
- **1934:** *Was ist Klassenbewußtsein?* A political manifesto published under Reich’s pseudonym Ernst Parell and intended to redirect socialist organization toward everyday needs and character structure.
- **1934:** *Der Orgasmus als elektrophysiologische Entladung*. A scientific-theoretical booklet proposing the orgasm as a tension-charge-discharge-relaxation process.
- **1935:** *Masse und Staat*. A restricted-circulation political manuscript—about one hundred copies according to the bibliography—later incorporated into *The Mass Psychology of Fascism*.
- **1935:** *Der Urgegensatz des vegetativen Lebens*. A biological-psychological essay on expansion and contraction, sexuality and anxiety.
- **1935:** *Psychischer Kontakt und vegetative Strömung*. A clinical monograph developed from Reich’s 1934 Lucerne congress lecture and later incorporated into *Character Analysis*.
- **1936:** *Die Sexualität im Kulturkampf: Zur sozialistischen Umstrukturierung des Menschen*. A comprehensive reworking of *Geschlechtsreife, Enthaltsamkeit, Ehemoral*; its revised English manifestation is *The Sexual Revolution*.
- **1937:** *Experimentelle Ergebnisse über die elektrische Funktion von Sexualität und Angst*. A report on Reich’s bioelectrical experiments with human subjects; later joined with related texts as *The Bioelectrical Investigation of Sexuality and Anxiety*.
- **1937:** *Menschen im Staat*. An unpublished political-autobiographical manuscript. It survives only indirectly and in revised material incorporated into *People in Trouble*, so its boundaries as an independent work are uncertain.

### Experimental reports, scientific cosmology, and later books

- **1938:** *Die Bione: Zur Entstehung des vegetativen Lebens*. A laboratory report with photographic plates presenting Reich’s bion experiments. The observations and Reich’s interpretation of them are historically inseparable but scientifically disputed.
- **1939:** *Die Entdeckung des Radiums durch Madame Curie und die Entdeckung der Sapa-Bione*. A comparison between Reich’s SAPA-bion work and the discovery of radium, issued within his institute’s report series.
- **1942:** *The Discovery of the Orgone, Volume I: The Function of the Orgasm*. A new synthetic book written in 1937–1940 under the manuscript title *Das Lebendige*. Despite sharing a title with the 1927 book, it is a substantially different obra recounting the development from psychoanalysis to Reich’s claimed biological-energy research.
- **1944:** *The Children of the Future*. An unpublished memorandum proposing a long-term study of healthy child development; it became the conceptual basis of the later Orgonomic Infant Research Center and should be classed as a project-text.
- **1945:** *Character Analysis*, second edition. An English translation and substantial revision of the 1933 book, including Reich’s later somatic approach; it is a transformed manifestation, not simply a reprint.
- **1945:** *The Emotional Plague*. An essay naming socially organized destructive behavior; first circulated through Reich’s periodical work and later incorporated into *Character Analysis*.
- **1945:** *Listen, Little Man!*. A direct-address polemic written as a manifesto against submission and authoritarian character, published with William Steig’s illustrations in 1948.
- **1946:** *The Mass Psychology of Fascism*, third edition. A greatly enlarged English reconstruction that adds work democracy and criticism of authoritarian socialism; its political position differs materially from the 1933 book.
- **1948:** *The Discovery of the Orgone, Volume II: The Cancer Biopathy*. A laboratory and clinical research report joining bion experiments, cancer observations, and Reich’s orgone hypothesis. Its therapeutic and physical claims are disputed and were central to later regulatory litigation.
- **1949:** *Ether, God and Devil*. A methodological and cosmological essay presenting “orgonomic functionalism.”
- **1949:** *Character Analysis*, third edition. A further reconstruction adding “The Expressive Language of the Living” and “The Schizophrenic Split,” thereby extending the book from psychoanalytic technique into somatic therapy and orgone theory.
- **1951:** *Cosmic Superimposition: Man’s Orgonotic Roots in Nature*. A cosmological treatise developed from a 1950 lecture and illustrated with photographs and diagrams.
- **1951:** *The Orgone Energy Accumulator: Its Scientific and Medical Use*. A manual and theoretical account of Reich’s accumulator. It is partly a protocol because it tells readers how the device was constructed and used; its physical and medical claims were rejected by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration and remain unsupported by accepted science.
- **1951:** *The ORANUR Experiment: First Report (1947–1951)*. A report on the “orgone against nuclear radiation” experiment at Orgonon. The text, apparatus, procedures, and site together approach a Gesamtkunstwerk of speculative physics, medicine, architecture, and environmental action.
- **1953:** *The Emotional Plague of Mankind, Volume I: The Murder of Christ*. A philosophical and autobiographical allegory using the Christ story to discuss the destruction of living expression.
- **1953:** *The Emotional Plague of Mankind, Volume II: People in Trouble*. A political autobiography reconstructed from earlier manuscripts, including material from *Menschen im Staat*; its layered composition makes its date and textual identity broader than the publication year.
- **1953:** *The Einstein Affair*. A documentary booklet presenting Reich’s correspondence and dispute with Albert Einstein over an accumulator temperature experiment. It is an advocacy dossier as much as a scientific report.
- **1954:** *OROP Desert, Part I: Spaceships, DOR and Drought*. A report compiled from 1952–1953 records concerning cloudbusting, drought, “deadly orgone radiation,” and alleged spacecraft. It combines expedition record, cosmology, technical protocol, and autobiographical narrative.
- **1955:** *Response to Ignorance*. A documentary supplement assembling Reich’s responses to the FDA proceedings and an operational protocol compiled by Eva Reich under his direction.
- **1956:** *Briefs for Appellants and Appendix*. Legal writings prepared in Reich’s appeal from his criminal-contempt conviction. Authorship is partly collective and forensic, so they are actions within a court proceeding rather than autonomous literary works.
- **1957:** *Record Appendix to Briefs for Appellants, Volume III: Suppressed Documentary Evidence*. A printed legal-documentary compilation issued by CORE Pilot Press.
- **1957:** *Contact with Space: ORANUR Second Report, 1951–1956*. Reich’s final book, written from the records of the Arizona expedition and published by colleagues in a small edition. It records the ORUR apparatus, the “spacegun,” weather operations, and Reich’s interpretation of unidentified aerial phenomena.

### Periodicals and serial editorial works

- **1934–1938:** *Zeitschrift für Politische Psychologie und Sexualökonomie*. A journal founded and edited by Reich, initially under the name Ernst Parell. Its collective authorship means the periodical is his organizational obra, but not every contribution in it is his text.
- **1937–1939:** *Klinische und experimentelle Berichte*. A numbered research-report series edited by Reich for the Institute for Sex-Economic Life Research.
- **1940:** *Seksualøkonomiske Meddelelser*. A Danish sex-economic bulletin associated with Reich’s Scandinavian network; the bibliography does not support attributing every item, or necessarily the entire serial, to Reich personally.
- **1942–1945:** *International Journal of Sex-Economy and Orgone Research*. An English-language research journal established under Reich’s direction and edited by Theodore P. Wolfe.
- **1947–1948:** *Annals of the Orgone Institute*. An irregular institutional journal directed by Reich rather than a conventionally scheduled periodical.
- **1949–1953:** *Orgone Energy Bulletin*. A periodical edited by the teaching and research staff under Reich’s direction, combining his writings with clinical and experimental reports by collaborators.
- **1954–1955:** *CORE: Cosmic Orgone Engineering*. A serial record of cloudbusting, atmospheric work, and the OROP Desert expedition.
- **1955–1956:** *Orgonomic Medicine*. A professional periodical created by Reich’s medical circle under his direction; its contents have multiple authors and should not be assigned wholesale to him.

### Clinical, political, and educational projects

- **1922–1930:** *Seminar für Psychoanalytische Therapie*. A teaching and clinical project directed by Reich in Vienna; its made form was a continuing seminar, case-discussion practice, and training method rather than a single text.
- **1928–1930:** *Sozialistische Gesellschaft für Sexualberatung und Sexualforschung*. A network of free sexual-advice clinics organized in Vienna by Reich and collaborators. It is a collective social project, not Reich’s sole-authored possession.
- **1931–1933:** *Deutscher Reichsverband für proletarische Sexualpolitik (Sexpol)*. A mass political and educational organization that Reich helped organize and direct. Its programs, clinics, lectures, pamphlets, and platform collectively form the work.
- **1934–1939:** *Institut für sexualökonomische Lebensforschung*. Reich’s Scandinavian research and training institute, encompassing vegetotherapy, bioelectrical work, bion experiments, lectures, and publishing.
- **From the mid-1930s:** *Vegetotherapy*. A clinical method joining character analysis to breathing, posture, muscular tension, and bodily expression. It is a repeatable therapeutic practice rather than a bounded object and therefore resists treatment as a single “work.”
- **1942 onward:** *Orgone Institute*. A research, training, clinical, and publishing organization developed around Reich’s work in the United States.
- **1949–1952:** *Orgonomic Infant Research Center*. A collaborative research and educational project concerned with pregnancy, birth, infancy, and prevention of character armoring. Reich initiated it, but its actual observations and care were collective.
- **1949–1957:** *Children of the Future*. The larger preventive project surrounding infant research, education, and self-regulation. Its posthumous book of that title is an editorial collection, not a book assembled by Reich himself.
- **1950–1957:** *Orgonon*. Reich’s Maine research estate, including laboratories, residences, workshops, observatory, archives, and experimental grounds. As an integrated environment for living, research, publication, and apparatus, it is the strongest candidate in his oeuvre for a Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1950–1951:** *The Einstein experiment*. A test of temperature differences in and around an orgone accumulator undertaken with Einstein’s initial participation. Reich and Einstein disputed the interpretation: Einstein attributed the effect to ordinary thermal convection, while Reich rejected that explanation.
- **1951:** *ORANUR*. A research project exposing small quantities of radioactive material to concentrated “orgone energy.” Its procedures and Reich’s interpretation remain scientifically disputed, and its boundaries extend beyond the published *First Report*.
- **1952–1954:** *Cloudbusting operations at Orgonon*. A sequence of atmospheric interventions using the cloudbuster, including the 1953 Maine drought operation. Reports of rain following operations establish the historical event, not Reich’s claimed causal mechanism.
- **1954–1955:** *Expedition OROP Desert Ea*. A field expedition to Arizona combining weather modification, radiation experiments, observations of aerial lights, and environmental remediation. The project, rather than any single report, is the work.
- **1954–1956:** *ORANUR II / ORUR*. A continuation of ORANUR using excited radioactive material at greater distance and in atmospheric operations; it culminated textually in *Contact with Space*.
- **1957:** *Wilhelm Reich Infant Trust*. A testamentary project created by Reich’s will to preserve his archives, property, and future work. Its later activities belong to the trustees, not to Reich’s lifetime oeuvre.

### Instruments, apparatus, and constructed environments

- **1935–1936:** *Bioelectrical experimental apparatus*. An arrangement of electrodes, amplifiers, and recording equipment used to measure skin potentials during pleasure and anxiety. It was an experimental system assembled with collaborators, not a uniquely fixed object.
- **1936–1939:** *Bion cultures and experimental preparations*. Laboratory-made cultures produced by heating, swelling, and sterilizing organic and inorganic materials. Whether they demonstrated transitional living structures, as Reich claimed, is disputed; as preparations, they are manifestations whose status as authored “works” is unstable.
- **1939:** *SAPA-bion apparatus and radiation observations*. A laboratory arrangement built around cultures Reich called SAPA bions. The apparatus existed, while the claimed novel radiation is not accepted by mainstream science.
- **circa 1940 onward:** *Orgone energy accumulator*. A chamber made from alternating organic and metallic layers. Reich developed and standardized the device, although individual examples were fabricated by staff and licensees; its claimed energy and medical effects are disputed.
- **1940s onward:** *Orgone accumulator blanket*. A portable layered textile-and-metal manifestation of the accumulator principle, produced in several sizes rather than as one unique artifact.
- **1940s onward:** *Shooter, funnel, and accumulator accessories*. Smaller devices intended to concentrate or direct the alleged energy. Designs and surviving examples vary, so this is a family of apparatus rather than one fixed work.
- **1940s:** *Orgone energy field meter*. An experimental electrical instrument intended to register variations Reich interpreted as orgone-energy fields; the archival record preserves apparatus and charts, but not a scientifically validated measurement principle.
- **1947–1948:** *Orgone motor project*. An attempt to obtain mechanical force from concentrated atmospheric orgone energy. The archive preserves a motor and experimental records, but Reich never supplied a reproducible public demonstration; it remains an unfinished project rather than a completed invention.
- **1950–1951:** *Orgone Energy Observatory*. A stone laboratory and observatory at Orgonon designed for atmospheric and astronomical observation. Architectural drawings bear Reich’s annotations, but professional designers and builders participated, so sole authorship should not be presumed.
- **1952:** *Cloudbuster*. An array of hollow metal pipes connected by flexible conduits to running water and used in attempts to alter atmospheric conditions. Reich devised the operative system, while particular machines were collectively fabricated.
- **1953–1954:** *DOR-buster*. A development of the cloudbuster intended to remove what Reich called deadly orgone radiation; surviving archival material includes a metal plate used on accumulator systems.
- **1954–1955:** *ORUR apparatus*. A cloudbuster-related system incorporating small quantities of radioactive material that had been treated in an accumulator. It was repeatedly modified, so the project resists identification as one stable machine.
- **1955–1956:** *Spacegun*. Reich’s name for the mobile ORUR-equipped atmospheric apparatus used in Arizona and Maine. It was not a conventional weapon, despite the name, but a technical-cosmological assemblage intended to act on atmosphere and alleged spacecraft.

### Images, recordings, correspondence, and archival manifestations

- **1951:** *Christ*. A painting by Reich reproduced in *The Murder of Christ*. The published reproduction confirms the object’s existence, but the present location and a complete catalogue of Reich’s visual art are not established by the standard bibliography.
- **Dates not comprehensively established:** *Paintings and drawings*. The archive records artwork, hand-drawn apparatus plans, diagrams, graphs, and annotated architectural drawings. This material is demonstrably made by or worked on by Reich, but no catalogue raisonné supplies secure individual titles, dates, and attributions for every sheet.
- **1936–1957:** *Correspondence with A. S. Neill*. A sustained epistolary work later edited as *Record of a Friendship*. The posthumous book is an editorial manifestation jointly constituted by both correspondents, not a title Reich made.
- **1919–1957:** *Diaries, journals, and autobiographical notebooks*. A discontinuous manuscript corpus later divided by editors into *Passion of Youth*, *Beyond Psychology*, *American Odyssey*, and *Where’s the Truth?*. Those titles and divisions are posthumous creations and should not be projected backward as four books authored by Reich.
- **1952:** *Interview with Kurt Eissler*. A recorded interview about Freud and Reich’s own development, later edited as *Reich Speaks of Freud*. The interview is Reich’s spoken manifestation; the 1967 book is a posthumous editorial construction.
- **1919–1957:** *Articles, notes, reviews, prefaces, addresses, interviews, minutes, leaflets, legal papers, letters, and unpublished manuscripts*. The Wilhelm Reich Bibliography identifies hundreds of such items and explicitly separates originals from later collections and translations. This corpus resists honest compression into invented “books”; the chronological bibliography and archive index remain the item-level authority where no stable standalone title or work-boundary exists.
