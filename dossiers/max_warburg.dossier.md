# Max Warburg: Research Dossier

**Subject:** Max Moritz Warburg (1867–1946)  
**Prepared:** 30 August 2026  
**Scope:** Documentary reconstruction of his life, family, banking career, political work, Jewish communal activity, Versailles role, forced emigration, controversies, myths, writings, and legacy.  

## Research note and evidentiary conventions

This dossier distinguishes among: **documented fact** (supported by institutional, archival, contemporaneous, or scholarly sources); **reported recollection** (especially Warburg's posthumously printed memoir); **allegation** (a claim made by an identified actor without adequate proof); **judicial finding**; and **myth/propaganda**. The principal modern biographical anchors are Christopher Kopper's entry in the *Neue Deutsche Biographie*, Frank Bajohr's entry in *Das Jüdische Hamburg*, Gabriele Hoffmann's full biography, Eduard Rosenbaum and Ari J. Sherman's bank history, Ron Chernow's family history, and surviving primary documents. Exact personal wealth figures are not securely reconstructable from the public sources consulted.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full name:** Max Moritz Warburg.
- **Common forms:** Max M. Warburg; Max Warburg. The German National Biography also indexes “Warburg, Moritz,” but this is not evidence of a separate working name.
- **Birth:** 5 June 1867, Hamburg, Free and Hanseatic City of Hamburg, North German Confederation.
- **Death:** 26 December 1946, New York City, United States, aged 79.
- **Religion/communal identity:** Jewish; born into the conservative, acculturated Hamburg Jewish banking family usually called the Mittelweg branch of the Warburgs. His public identity combined German/Hamburg patriotism, participation in Jewish communal institutions, and—more strongly from the late 1920s—engagement with Zionist and Palestine-oriented institutions.
- **Citizenship:** German by birth; emigrated to the United States in 1938; naturalized as a U.S. citizen in 1944, reportedly at age 77.
- **Primary profession:** Merchant banker and personally liable partner of M. M. Warburg & Co.; corporate director; economic and political adviser; Hamburg legislator; Jewish communal organizer and philanthropist.
- **Contemporary labels:** Called one of the “Kaiserjuden,” Jewish financiers who had access to Wilhelm II; in 1920s Hamburg he was sometimes styled the “König von Hamburg” (“King of Hamburg”), a description of informal influence, not an office or title.
- **Education/honors:** Abitur, 1886; no completed university degree. Honorary doctorate from the University of Hamburg, 1921; Gold Medal of the Hamburg Chamber of Commerce, 1927.
- **Principal residences:** Grindelhof 1a, Hamburg-Rotherbaum, in early childhood; Mittelweg 17 at Johnsallee from 1871; after marriage, the family's Kösterberg property in Blankenese; from 1907, a villa by Hamburg's Außenalster/Dammtor area; New York from 1938 until death.
- **Burial:** Public genealogical databases conflict, listing either Sleepy Hollow Cemetery, New York, or a later Hamburg burial. No sufficiently strong cemetery or civil-record source was located to settle this point; it should remain unverified.

## Family and Ancestry

### Banking dynasty

The family traced its name to Warburg in Westphalia and established itself in the Hamburg-Altona commercial world. Brothers Moses Marcus Warburg and Gerson Warburg founded M. M. Warburg & Co. in Altona in 1798. The house specialized in bills of exchange, foreign currency, overseas trade finance, securities, and later industrial and sovereign issues. It moved into Ferdinandstraße 75 in Hamburg in 1867, the year Max was born.

Max belonged to the **Mittelweg-Warburg** line, named for the family residence. The related **Alsterufer-Warburg** line descended from another branch. These family labels mattered socially and genealogically but were not separate legal corporate entities.

### Parents

- **Father:** Moritz Moses Warburg (1838–1910), personally liable partner and head of M. M. Warburg & Co. He represented the fourth banking generation and oversaw the house's expansion before Max succeeded him.
- **Mother:** Charlotte Esther Warburg, née Oppenheim (1842–1921), of another German Jewish mercantile/banking family.

### Siblings

The usual count is seven children, though some popular summaries miscount the daughters or omit Mary. The siblings were:

- **Mary Anna Warburg** (born 1865).
- **Abraham Moritz “Aby” Warburg** (1866–1929), art historian and founder of the Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg, later the Warburg Institute in London.
- **Max Moritz Warburg** (1867–1946).
- **Paul Moritz Warburg** (1868–1932), banker at Kuhn, Loeb & Co., naturalized American, influential advocate of the U.S. Federal Reserve, member and vice governor of the first Federal Reserve Board.
- **Felix Moritz Warburg** (1871–1937), partner of Kuhn, Loeb & Co.; husband of Frieda Schiff, daughter of Jacob Schiff; major American Jewish philanthropist.
- **Olga Charlotte Warburg Kohn-Speyer** (1873–1904).
- **Louisa Martha Warburg Derenberg** (1879–1973).
- **Fritz Moritz Warburg** (1879–1964), Hamburg banker, later emigrant in Sweden. Some lists effectively count eight children by including both Louisa and Fritz; the exact sibling count in secondary summaries is therefore inconsistent, but these individuals are documented family members.

### The “birthright for books” family story

A famous family recollection says that Aby, determined to become a scholar rather than a banker, transferred his firstborn right to lead the bank to the younger Max in exchange for Max's promise to buy every book Aby ever required. Max later called it the largest blank cheque of his life. The exchange is repeated in family and Warburg Institute histories. It captures the brothers' complementary careers and Max's long financial support of Aby's library. It should be classified as a **family anecdote based on later recollection**, not a surviving legal contract.

### Marriage and children

In 1899 Warburg married **Alice Magnus** (1873–1960), daughter of Hamburg/Altona merchant Hermann Levin Magnus (1825–1877) and Charlotte “Lola” Schorstein. Their affectionate joint nickname was reportedly **“Malice”** (Max + Alice). They had five children:

1. **Erich Hermann Max Warburg / Eric M. Warburg** (1900–1990), banker; M. M. Warburg partner from 1929; founder in New York of E. M. Warburg & Co., a predecessor of Warburg Pincus; U.S. Army Air Forces intelligence officer in the Second World War; postwar co-founder of Atlantik-Brücke and the American Council on Germany. Married Dorothea Thorsch; father of Max Marcus Alfons Warburg and daughters Marie and Erica.
2. **Lola Nina Hahn-Warburg** (1901–1989), married industrialist Rudolf Hahn; emigrated to Britain. She worked with British Jewish organizations and is associated with the initiative and advocacy that led to the Kindertransport, although the program had many organizers and no single founder.
3. **Renate Olga Warburg** (1904–1984), successively associated in the genealogical record with Richard Samson, Walter Strauss, and classical scholar Sir William M. Calder; emigrated in the 1930s.
4. **Anita Franziska Sophie Warburg** (1908–2008), emigrated to London in 1935, worked with refugee organizations and the British Red Cross, later lived in New York, and became an artist and arts/education patron; briefly married journalist Max Wolff.
5. **Gisela Warburg Wyzanski** (1912–1991), emigrated to the United States in 1939 and became active in Zionist and refugee work.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1867–1886

Max was born as the family's bank was moving into a more imposing Hamburg position. His early home at Grindelhof stood in the heavily Jewish Grindel district, sometimes called “Little Jerusalem.” In 1871 the household—parents, children, nurses, cook, and domestic staff—moved to a villa at Mittelweg 17, near the Außenalster. The move mirrored the family's rise from a specifically Jewish commercial quarter into Hamburg's affluent bourgeois elite without abandoning communal ties.

Accounts based on family papers portray Max as lively, sociable, energetic, and less bookish than Aby. He first received private instruction, spent three years in the preparatory section of the Gelehrtenschule des Johanneums, then attended its Realgymnasium. He completed the Abitur in 1886. There is no evidence of a university degree or formal academic training in economics. His education was instead the multilingual, itinerant apprenticeship customary in international merchant banking.

## Education and Formation, 1886–1893

### Continental and London apprenticeship

- **1886–1888:** Apprenticeship at J. Dreyfus & Co. in Frankfurt am Main.
- **1888:** Trainee/volunteer experience at Wertheim & Gompertz in Amsterdam.
- **October 1888–1889/90:** One-year volunteer service in the III Royal Bavarian Chevaulegers Regiment in Munich. He became a *Vizefeldwebel* and officer candidate. Biographical accounts state that a superior blocked his commission, probably for antisemitic reasons. The motive is a well-supported biographical interpretation rather than a surviving written admission by the officer.
- **1890:** Work at the Banque Impériale Ottomane in Paris; attended lectures at the Sorbonne without enrolling for a degree.
- **1891–1892:** Placement at N. M. Rothschild & Sons in London.

These posts gave Warburg practical command of foreign exchange, acceptance credit, sovereign issues, trading networks, and the different financial cultures of Frankfurt, Amsterdam, Paris, and London. They also helped establish the personal network on which his later influence depended.

## Early Career and Rise at M. M. Warburg & Co., 1893–1914

### Partnership and business expansion

Warburg returned to Hamburg in 1893 and entered M. M. Warburg & Co. as a personally liable partner. Paul joined the partnership in 1895 before ultimately moving to New York. After Moritz Warburg's death in 1910, Max became the dominant head of the Hamburg bank.

The bank's main businesses were:

- foreign exchange and bills of exchange;
- acceptance and short-term trade credit;
- financing Hamburg importers, exporters, shipping, and shipbuilding;
- securities trading and underwriting;
- sovereign and railway bonds;
- foreign and colonial projects.

The *Neue Deutsche Biographie* specifically identifies participation in bond issues for the **Baghdad Railway** and sovereign loans to **Japan** and **Liberia**. These were commercial operations with foreign-policy implications. The bank also supplied significant finance to German colonial ventures. Warburg therefore belonged to the Hamburg liberal-imperial commercial world: outward-looking and internationalist in method, but supportive of German overseas economic power.

The bank had 23 employees and a balance sheet of about 35 million marks in 1889. Biographical accounts state that under Max its balance sheet multiplied roughly fivefold and staffing doubled in the prewar expansion, though the exact comparison years vary. It entered the Reich government loan consortium in 1905, a major mark of status.

### Travel

Warburg made extended business trips to the United States, Russia, South Africa, Italy, and Scandinavia. On a voyage to Cape Town he contracted severe dysentery and reportedly lost about twenty kilograms. After recovering sufficiently, he traveled with an engineer into Swaziland to inspect a tin mine. This is the only serious illness found in the consulted biographical record before old age.

### Shipping and industry

His closest business friendship was with **Albert Ballin**, director-general of the Hamburg-Amerikanische Packetfahrt-Actien-Gesellschaft (HAPAG). Warburg financing assisted HAPAG's rise into the first rank of world shipping. Warburg joined HAPAG's supervisory board in 1910 and, through Ballin's influence, the board of **Blohm & Voss**, the Hamburg shipyard. The two also founded the **Cuxhavener Hochseefischerei AG** in 1908; contemporary-oriented biographies describe it as becoming Germany's leading fish supplier by turnover.

Warburg eventually held a dense portfolio of supervisory positions. By the mid-1920s, later accounts count roughly 27 boards, including at various times HAPAG, Blohm & Voss, Norddeutscher Lloyd, Beiersdorf, Deutsche Atlantische Telegraphengesellschaft, and I. G. Farben. A board seat signified monitoring, finance, and network power; it did not mean operational management of every company.

### Public and political offices before the war

- **1897–1902:** Commercial judge appointed by the Hamburg Senate.
- **1902:** Board member of the Central Association of the German Banking and Bankers' Industry.
- **1903–1919:** Member of the Hamburg Bürgerschaft. Sources differ by one year in beginning the term (1903 or 1904); the modern NDB gives 1903. He represented the National Liberal Party.
- **1903–1933:** Member of the Hamburg Chamber of Commerce.
- **1903:** Joined the governing body of the Hamburg securities exchange.
- **1908:** Member of the Berlin Stock Exchange Committee in the Imperial Interior Office, after campaigning to revise restrictive exchange law.
- **From 1908:** Adviser to the Hamburg World Economic Archive and board member of the German Colonial Society.
- **Occasionally from 1903:** Financial adviser with direct access to Kaiser Wilhelm II.

### University-building and culture

Warburg worked with Hamburg senator Werner von Melle to create the **Hamburgische Wissenschaftliche Stiftung** in 1907. He supported the **Hamburg Colonial Institute**, opened in 1908, and the long campaign that produced the **University of Hamburg** in 1919. The Colonial Institute was both a scholarly institution and an instrument of imperial administration; both functions belong in the record. Max also financed Aby's library and scholarly circle, helping make possible a distinctive Hamburg school involving Aby Warburg, Fritz Saxl, Ernst Cassirer, and others.

### The July Crisis encounter with Wilhelm II

Warburg's later notes report a lunch with Wilhelm II during the Kiel Week/Hamburg visit shortly before or around the Sarajevo assassination in June 1914. Wilhelm contemplated whether Germany should strike before Russian armament and railway building made a later war more dangerous. Warburg said he urged waiting, on the ground that German strength would improve. The exchange comes from Warburg's recollection; it is useful evidence of access and his stated advice, not proof that he influenced the subsequent imperial decision process.

## First World War, 1914–1918

### German patriotism and divided family loyalties

Warburg supported the German war effort financially and administratively and expressed “warlike feelings” in private correspondence at the outbreak of war. His brothers Paul and Felix were established in the United States; Paul became a member of the first Federal Reserve Board in August 1914 and U.S. citizen Paul was consequently placed in a position of severe divided family loyalty. Correspondence between Max and Jacob Schiff shows that the transatlantic family network did not erase national allegiance: Max worked for German interests; Schiff sought to observe American neutrality and later American war policy.

Max declined an offer to become German ambassador in Washington. There is **no reliable evidence in the sources consulted that he was an intelligence officer or ran espionage operations**. He advised government, diplomats, and military authorities on finance. That is political-economic access, not by itself proof of secret-service membership.

### Wartime finance and food supply

With Ballin, Warburg promoted the **Reichseinkauf**, later the state-controlled **Zentral-Einkaufsgesellschaft** (Central Purchasing Company), to buy food abroad for blockaded Germany. Warburg's bank organized currency, credit, and advance payments. In 1915 he joined the advisory body of the War Food Office. The purchasing organization was attacked during and after the war both for its performance and through antisemitic claims about Jewish profiteering. The documentary record supports Warburg's administrative and financing role; it does not establish personal criminal profiteering.

He and Felix also coordinated relief for Jews in German-occupied Eastern Europe. These actions generated conflict with some American Zionists and relief leaders who regarded German Jewish elite patriotism as misplaced or incompatible with Jewish solidarity.

### Jewish military service and the 1916 census

The Prussian War Ministry's 1916 **Judenzählung** counted Jewish soldiers amid accusations that Jews were avoiding service. Warburg, himself a former cavalry volunteer and officer candidate, joined protests by Jewish organizations. The ministry stated that Jewish conduct had not caused the measure, but did not publish results during the war. Warburg's intervention forms part of his movement from private assimilation toward more explicit defense of equal Jewish citizenship.

### Jewish organizational proposal

In 1918 he published **“Viod”**, proposing or discussing a *Vereinigung jüdischer Organisationen Deutschlands* (Union of Jewish Organizations in Germany) in *Neue Jüdische Monatshefte*. The essay reflected the need for coordinated representation across ideological divisions.

### Final imperial appointment

In October 1918 Chancellor Prince Max von Baden appointed Warburg a financial adviser. Warburg declined an offer to become Reich finance minister in autumn 1918. Modern biography connects the refusal to his judgment that antisemitism made appointment politically dangerous or counterproductive.

## Versailles and the Peace Settlement, 1919

### Exact status

On 12 March 1919 Foreign Minister Ulrich Graf von Brockdorff-Rantzau asked Warburg to become one of six principal German delegates. Warburg declined that senior role and recommended his partner **Carl Melchior**, who became a principal economic/financial delegate. Warburg nevertheless went to Versailles as an **economic and financial expert/adviser** attached to the German delegation.

The victorious powers did not conduct an ordinary negotiation with Germany. The German delegation arrived only at the end of April; the draft was delivered on 7 May; German responses were mainly written. Max's role was therefore analysis, drafting, consultation, and assessment, not bargaining as an equal across a conference table.

Warburg criticized Brockdorff-Rantzau's combative seated speech at the 7 May handover as an uncoordinated *faux pas*. He and Melchior judged the economic and reparations conditions unpayable or unacceptable. They withdrew from or ceased active service with the delegation during the crisis over acceptance. On 18–19 June, Warburg, Wilhelm Cuno, and other financial and economic experts signed or joined an expert recommendation warning the cabinet unanimously to reject the treaty. The National Assembly and the new Bauer government ultimately accepted under Allied threat of resumed hostilities; Germany signed on 28 June.

### Was Paul Warburg on the American side at Versailles?

**No documentary support was found for the literal claim.** Paul Warburg had been a U.S. Federal Reserve Board member and vice governor, but his term ended on 9 August 1918. In 1919 he founded and chaired the American Acceptance Council in New York. The U.S. State Department's official, detailed roster, **“Composition and Functions of the American Commission to Negotiate Peace, May 1, 1919,”** names the commissioners, personal staffs, secretariat, geographic divisions, finance personnel (Norman H. Davis, Thomas W. Lamont, Treasury aides), food, labor, shipping, legal, and other sections. Paul Warburg is absent.

Thus:

- **Documented:** Max served the German side as an expert.
- **Documented:** Paul was an influential American banker and former Federal Reserve vice governor.
- **Not documented:** Paul served as an American Versailles delegate, financial expert, or negotiator.
- **Not documented:** Max and Paul faced each other across the negotiating table.

The line “one brother on each side at Versailles” is best understood as a symbolic compression of a real transatlantic division during the war, later turned into a literal story.

## Weimar Republic, 1919–1933

### Political and monetary offices

- **1919–1933:** Member of Hamburg's Finance Deputation, advising the Senate.
- **1919:** Appointed to the Reichsbank Central Committee.
- **1924–autumn 1933:** Member of the eight-member General Council (*Generalrat*) of the Reichsbank, which shared responsibility for monetary-policy oversight.
- Joined the **German People's Party (DVP)** after the empire; left in autumn 1931 over its anti-republican move to the right.
- Declined another offer of the Reich Finance Ministry in 1922. One reported formulation was that two Jewish ministers—Warburg and Foreign Minister Walther Rathenau—would be more than Germany would tolerate. The sentiment is consistent with his recorded political judgment, though exact wording derives from recollection.

Warburg knew Rathenau and the two moved within overlapping financial-political networks. After Rathenau's assassination on 24 June 1922, authorities found or reported plans by right-wing extremists to murder Warburg as well. He received repeated threats during the period of right-wing political terrorism.

### Reparations, stabilization, and international credit

Carl Melchior, rather than Max, became the bank's most visible reparations diplomat at Brussels (1920), Paris and London (1921), and Genoa (1922). Max remained a supporting figure in Germany's international credit network. American family connections, especially Kuhn, Loeb & Co., became important sources of funds during the early 1930s crisis.

In 1920 Warburg and Paul von Mendelssohn-Bartholdy initiated the **Deutsche Warentreuhand AG**. It used fiduciary transfer of title to goods or inventory as collateral, enabling foreign loans to German businesses. The institution became a predecessor within the later German auditing/consulting lineage associated with BDO.

### Bank scale and risk policy

M. M. Warburg's balance sheet reached approximately **382 million Reichsmarks in 1929**, with **289 employees**. The bank survived the 1931 banking crisis without the direct Reich rescue on which several universal banks depended. The NDB attributes this to conservative lending, diversified risk, and millions in support from Kuhn, Loeb & Co. At the beginning of 1933 it was among Germany's five largest private banks and a major foreign-trade financier.

These figures document corporate scale, not Max's personal net worth. As a personally liable partner, his wealth was bound to partnership capital, securities, residences, family trusts/holdings, and board income. No reliable annual personal valuation was located.

### Major corporate positions

Warburg's positions included, with dates varying by company:

- M. M. Warburg & Co.—personally liable partner from 1893; principal leader after 1910 to 1938.
- HAPAG—supervisory board from 1910 to forced departure in 1933.
- Blohm & Voss—supervisory board.
- Norddeutscher Lloyd—supervisory board.
- Beiersdorf—supervisory role.
- Deutsche Atlantische Telegraphengesellschaft—supervisory role until Nazi-era exclusion.
- I. G. Farben—supervisory board, generally recorded as 1926–1931 or resignation by 1932.
- Cuxhavener Hochseefischerei AG—co-founder, 1908.
- Deutsche Warentreuhand AG—co-initiator, 1920.

His I. G. Farben seat predated the Nazi seizure of power and ended before the company's central wartime crimes. It documents his integration into Weimar industrial finance; it is not evidence that he directed Auschwitz-era slave labor or wartime production.

### Jewish life, philanthropy, and Zionism

Warburg was active in the Hamburg Jewish congregation from 1910 to 1938; chaired the German-Israelite orphanage; served the Talmud Torah school; joined the Centralverein deutscher Staatsbürger jüdischen Glaubens leadership in 1927; chaired the **Hilfsverein der deutschen Juden** from 1928 (some local sources specify 1935–1938 for its later phase); and joined the advisory body of the Jewish Agency for Germany in 1929.

His development was not a simple conversion from assimilation to Zionism. He remained a German patriot and communal integrationist while increasingly supporting Palestine as an avenue of Jewish institution-building and, after 1933, escape.

## Nazi Rule, Jewish Self-Help, and Forced Transfer of the Bank, 1933–1938

### Loss of offices

After 30 January 1933 Warburg was removed or forced out of the Hamburg Finance Deputation, Chamber of Commerce, securities exchange leadership, and, in summer, HAPAG's board. By 1936 he had lost most corporate and cultural posts, including connections to the Philharmonic Society and Kiel Institute for the World Economy. His Reichsbank General Council membership ended when that body was dissolved in autumn 1933; describing him as a continuing director of Hitler's Reichsbank is inaccurate.

At HAPAG no colleague offered a farewell. Warburg delivered his own. The often quoted recollection states that great German shipping was chiefly the work of two Jews, Albert Ballin and “the man who has the honor to stand before you”; he then ironically assumed the voice of the board in accepting blame for dismissing its proven colleague. The exact text is preserved through memoir/biographical transmission.

In May 1933 he and several non-Jewish business leaders appealed to the Reich government not to destroy Jewish equality in private business. The appeal received no answer.

### Haavara, Paltreu, and Alltreu

Warburg and Melchior helped arrange the 1933 transfer structure between German authorities and the Jewish Agency that became associated with **Haavara**. M. M. Warburg participated in **Paltreu** (*Palästina-Treuhandstelle der Juden in Deutschland GmbH*) and **Alltreu** (*Allgemeine Treuhandstelle für die jüdische Auswanderung GmbH*). The mechanisms allowed emigrants to deposit assets in Germany; funds financed German exports, and proceeds or corresponding value became available abroad, especially in Mandatory Palestine, after deductions and exchange losses.

Documented consequences and disputes:

- It enabled tens of thousands of German Jews to emigrate and preserve some assets that exchange controls would otherwise trap.
- It supported German exports and undercut aspects of the international anti-Nazi boycott.
- It privileged, especially in early forms, people with capital sufficient for British “capitalist” immigration certificates.
- Zionists, non-Zionist Jewish organizations, Revisionist Zionists, boycott advocates, Nazi factions, and German businesses fought over it for opposed reasons.

Warburg's role was therefore **documented negotiation and banking administration under coercive conditions**, not a rumor. Calling it simply rescue omits its costs and exclusions; calling it proof of ideological Nazi-Zionist unity erases the coercion, institutional conflict, and incompatible aims of the parties.

### Aid and limits

Warburg remained in Germany in part to protect Jewish employees and clients, finance emigration, and maintain institutional capacity. Modern family and biographical claims sometimes say he and associates enabled **75,000** people to flee. Institutional summaries more cautiously say “tens of thousands.” Because outcomes resulted from multiple bodies and transfer channels, assigning an exact number personally to Max is not presently verifiable.

A surviving December 1942 exchange in the Leo Baeck Institute shows the tragic limits of such networks: Helmuth Halberstadt requested help obtaining Cuban visas for relatives; Warburg replied that he was overwhelmed by similar requests. The relatives were deported. The document neither proves indifference nor successful rescue; it records scarcity and failure.

### Saving the Warburg Library

After Aby's death, Max backed Fritz Saxl and others in moving the Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg—books, photographs, staff, furnishings, and papers—from Hamburg to London in 1933. The Warburg Institute's history records that Max accepted the grave necessity and fully supported transfer. This preserved one of the twentieth century's major humanities collections.

### Jewish Community House

Warburg financed and advocated the Jewish Community House at Hartungstraße 9–11 in Rotherbaum, opened 9 January 1938. It gave the Jewish Cultural Association a venue after exclusion from other halls. His 6.5-page opening address survives as a typescript in the Leo Baeck Institute and is available in digital edition. It is a primary source for Jewish communal self-assertion under escalating persecution.

### “Aryanization” of M. M. Warburg & Co.

In September 1937 Reichsbank president/economics minister Hjalmar Schacht informed Warburg that the bank could no longer remain in the Reich loan consortium. Warburg answered that this would force liquidation. In spring–summer 1938 he transferred the partnership to trusted non-Jewish insiders, general representative **Rudolf Brinckmann** and businessman **Paul Wirtz**, with other non-Jewish limited partners.

The transaction was formally negotiated with friendly successors and aimed to preserve the bank, staff, and residual family interests. It was nevertheless an **Aryanization under state coercion**. The Reich Economics Ministry prevented payment of a fair market price. The family's retained silent participation was confiscated after war began in 1939. On 27 October 1941 authorities forced the name change to **Brinckmann, Wirtz & Co.**

Warburg's farewell to about 200 remaining employees in May 1938 ended, according to the transmitted text, with wishes for their work “for the blessing of the Hanseatic City of Hamburg and for the blessing of Germany.” It demonstrates the persistence of his German patriotism even while the state expelled him.

## Exile, 1938–1946

Warburg traveled to the United States in late August 1938 with Alice and a daughter. Some accounts say he initially contemplated return. The November pogrom and arrest of his brother Fritz made return impossible. Eric's residence/citizenship status helped secure his parents' admission in an era when roughly 300,000 Germans sought places under a U.S. German quota of about 27,000 annually.

Max lived in New York within the extensive American Warburg network. His son founded E. M. Warburg & Co. in 1938 and employed some European refugees. Max did not resume control of a comparable bank and was in his seventies. He continued correspondence and refugee assistance but faced vastly more requests than resources.

He became a U.S. citizen in 1944. During the war, Eric—not Max—served in U.S. military intelligence, became an Anglo-American intelligence liaison/interrogator, participated in the European campaign, and developed later relationships with Allen Dulles and John J. McCloy. Claims that transfer Eric's OSS/USAAF or postwar intelligence connections backward onto Max are genealogical conflations.

Max lived to see Nazi Germany defeated. Eric visited Hamburg in 1945 and found the bank operating amid the ruins. Max died in New York on 26 December 1946. The consulted obituary notices do not state a medically precise cause; therefore no cause of death should be asserted.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

Warburg's political trajectory can be described without forcing it into a single doctrine:

1. **Hamburg commercial liberalism:** support for free overseas trade, banking internationalism, municipal autonomy, and elite civic service.
2. **German national and imperial patriotism:** National Liberal politics; colonial investment and institutions; access to Wilhelm II; financial support for Germany during the First World War.
3. **Pragmatic republican participation:** DVP membership, Reichsbank and municipal service, international credit reconstruction, rejection of the Versailles economic terms.
4. **Break with the nationalist right:** left the DVP in 1931 when it moved against the republic; personally targeted by right-wing terrorism and antisemitic propaganda.
5. **German Jewish equal-citizenship activism:** Centralverein, congregation, schools, orphanage, protests over military discrimination.
6. **Increasing Palestine/Zionist engagement:** Jewish Agency advisory work and transfer mechanisms, especially as emigration became necessary. He never ceased to identify emotionally with Germany.

No evidence was found of Freemasonry, occult affiliations, addiction, psychiatric crisis, imprisonment, criminal conviction, or personal participation in political violence.

## Important Relationships and Networks

- **Aby Warburg:** brother, recipient of lifelong financial support; contrasting intellectual vocation; Max preserved his library.
- **Paul and Felix Warburg:** brothers in American banking; channels to Kuhn, Loeb, U.S. finance, and Jewish philanthropy; also sources of wartime loyalty conflict.
- **Fritz Warburg:** brother and Hamburg banking associate; arrested after the November 1938 pogrom and later escaped to Sweden.
- **Alice Warburg:** wife and household partner through wealth, persecution, and exile.
- **Eric Warburg:** son, banking successor, immigration sponsor, U.S. intelligence officer, and postwar restorer of family participation.
- **Albert Ballin:** closest business friend; HAPAG leader; partner in shipping, food, and fishery projects; fellow “Kaiserjude.” Ballin died in November 1918, by overdose in circumstances usually interpreted as suicide amid Germany's defeat and revolution.
- **Carl Melchior:** Jewish partner at M. M. Warburg; chief economic diplomat at Versailles and reparations conferences; collaborator in transfer arrangements; co-plaintiff in the Fritsch libel case.
- **Wilhelm II:** occasional financial advisee; access did not imply control of imperial policy.
- **Prince Max von Baden:** appointed him financial adviser in the last imperial government.
- **Werner von Melle:** partner in creating Hamburg scientific and university institutions.
- **Walther Rathenau:** friend/associate and minister who sought Warburg for cabinet service; his assassination heightened threats to Warburg.
- **Hjalmar Schacht:** Reichsbank president/economics minister; institutional counterpart who in 1937 communicated the bank's exclusion from the state-loan consortium. Cooperation on technical transfer arrangements did not prevent expropriation.
- **Jacob Schiff and Kuhn, Loeb & Co.:** American in-law and banking network; both wartime tension and later financial support.
- **Fritz Saxl:** scholarly organizer who helped move Aby's library to London, with Max's authorization and support.

## Controversies, Allegations, Litigation, and Financial Questions

### 1. Theodor Fritsch libel case

Antisemitic publisher **Theodor Fritsch**, editor of *Der Hammer*, alleged that Warburg and Melchior, as Versailles representatives, had betrayed Germany for American or Allied interests—the “international Jewish banker” charge. Warburg and Melchior sued.

- **Proceedings:** Hamburg *Schöffengericht*, 4–5 December 1924.
- **Initial result:** Fritsch was convicted of libel and sentenced to three months' imprisonment.
- **Appeal:** After further proceedings, the prison term was replaced or reduced to a fine of 1,000 marks, according to the contemporaneous JTA report.
- **Primary hostile publication:** Fritsch published the case record and his argument as *Mein Streit mit dem Hause Warburg* (Leipzig, 1925), incorporating the *Hammer* article.
- **Evidentiary meaning:** A German court rejected the betrayal claim sufficiently to convict for libel. The judgment does not immunize every Warburg action from criticism; it directly contradicts the specific claim that his Versailles service proved treason. His documented recommendation was that Germany reject the treaty.

### 2. Reparations and “international finance”

Warburg's global family ties made him an ideal target for claims that borders were theatrical and the family secretly coordinated both sides. The documentary record instead shows genuine conflicts of citizenship, wartime censorship and tension, different institutional obligations, and Max's opposition to Allied economic terms. Family access and commercial benefit across borders were real; centralized family control of governments is unproved.

### 3. German imperialism and colonial finance

Warburg financed colonial and overseas enterprises, sat on the German Colonial Society board, helped establish the Colonial Institute, and participated in Baghdad Railway and sovereign lending. These are documented components of German economic imperialism. No evidence was found that he personally administered colonial violence, but his finance and institution-building supported the imperial system.

### 4. I. G. Farben

Warburg sat on I. G. Farben's large supervisory board during the Weimar period, approximately 1926–1931/32, and departed before Hitler's chancellorship. The post is sometimes used to imply responsibility for Farben's later Nazi collaboration and Auschwitz complex. Chronology does not support that implication. The legitimate financial-history question is his participation in the interwar concentration of German chemical industry, not later acts committed after his departure.

### 5. Haavara and the anti-Nazi boycott

The transfer agreement simultaneously facilitated escape, salvaged part of emigrants' assets, promoted German exports, and weakened boycott unity. These are not mutually exclusive descriptions. Warburg was involved. The controversy was contemporary and intense. Evidence does not support reducing the arrangement either to altruistic rescue alone or to ideological alliance with Nazism.

### 6. Remaining until 1938

Warburg believed for too long that German legal, commercial, and personal networks might preserve space for Jewish life. Remaining allowed him to help clients, employees, communal bodies, and emigrants; it also required negotiation with Nazi officials and delayed his own flight. Sources document both the miscalculation and the assistance. There is no evidence that he joined the Nazi Party or endorsed its racial program.

### 7. Criminality, fraud, corruption, or money laundering

No documented indictment, criminal charge, conviction, money-laundering finding, or personal fraud by Max Warburg was located. Later criminal and tax controversies involving the modern M. M. Warburg bank—especially twenty-first-century cum-ex transactions—occurred decades after his death and should not be attributed to him.

### 8. Intelligence connections

Max advised imperial and republican officials on finance and possessed international political information. No documented intelligence-service office was located. His son Eric's U.S. wartime intelligence service and postwar relationships with Dulles and McCloy are real but belong to Eric's biography.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### Myth A: “Max represented Germany at Versailles while Paul represented America”

1. **Story:** Two Warburg brothers supposedly sat on opposite sides of the Versailles negotiating table, sometimes presented as proof that a banking family controlled both belligerents.
2. **Appearance/circulation:** The motif circulates in modern internet videos, posts, and conspiracy narratives; it builds upon older “international Jewish finance” propaganda directed at Max and Melchior in the early 1920s.
3. **Evidence:** Max's German expert role is documented. The official U.S. commission roster does not include Paul. Paul's official Federal Reserve service ended in August 1918, and his documented 1919 work centered on the American Acceptance Council in New York.
4. **Assessment:** **False as a literal delegation claim; symbolically derived from a real transatlantic family split.** No evidence was found that the brothers negotiated against each other at Versailles.
5. **Effect:** The story transformed the tragedy of divided loyalties into a theory of borderless secret control and remains one of the most repeated Warburg legends.

### Myth B: The Warburgs “wrote” or imposed Versailles

1. **Story:** Warburg bankers allegedly designed the treaty or reparations to profit from German ruin.
2. **Circulators:** Weimar antisemitic writers including Fritsch; later conspiracy literature.
3. **Evidence against:** Max and Melchior opposed the economic conditions; Max joined the unanimous expert recommendation to reject the treaty. Germany did not write the Allied draft. Paul was not on the listed American delegation.
4. **Assessment:** **Propaganda contradicted by primary documentation.** This does not mean bankers lacked influence on postwar credit; it means the alleged act is the reverse of Max's recorded recommendation.
5. **Effect:** Helped make Warburg a target of threats and libel and fed the wider stab-in-the-back mythology.

### Myth C: “Sidney Warburg” and Warburg financing of Hitler

1. **Story:** A supposed Warburg emissary named Sidney described transferring millions of dollars from American bankers to Hitler in meetings from 1929 to 1933.
2. **Appearance:** Dutch book *De geldbronnen van het nationaal-socialisme: drie gesprekken met Hitler* (1933), attributed to “Sidney Warburg,” later republished as *Hitler's Secret Backers* and in German versions.
3. **Evidence:** The publisher withdrew the original after learning that no such member of the New York Warburg family existed. Later editions contain accretions; James P. Warburg denied the story by sworn affidavit in 1949. No corroborating bank records or authenticated identity for “Sidney” have emerged.
4. **Assessment:** **Pseudonymous fabrication/forgery, not evidence against Max.** Max is not “Sidney.”
5. **Effect:** It became a durable source for claims that Jewish or Wall Street bankers secretly financed Nazism, often detached from the book's defective provenance.

### Myth D: Haavara proves a unified Nazi-Zionist project

1. **Story:** The transfer agreement is presented either as proof that Zionists and Nazis shared one political aim, or, on the opposite side, as an uncomplicated rescue compact.
2. **Appearance:** Contemporary boycott disputes and Zionist factional conflict from 1933 onward; later polemical histories from opposed political camps.
3. **Evidence:** Negotiation and economic cooperation occurred; the parties' goals differed; German Jews were under rapidly escalating coercion; the agreement aided emigration and German exports and harmed boycott cohesion.
4. **Assessment:** **The agreement is fact; the claim of ideological identity is an interpretive overreach.** A one-word moral label cannot describe all of its mechanisms and effects.
5. **Effect:** Warburg's practical role is alternately celebrated as mass rescue and denounced as collaboration.

### Legend E: Aby's birthright for unlimited books

1. **Story:** Teenage Aby surrendered leadership of the family bank to Max for a promise of unlimited books.
2. **Appearance:** Family memoir and Warburg institutional tradition.
3. **Evidence:** Repeated family recollection; consistent with Max's later financing of the library; no legal document is known.
4. **Assessment:** **Plausible family legend/recollected oral compact.**
5. **Effect:** Became the founding parable of the Warburg Institute—banking capital exchanged for a library of cultural memory.

### Reputation claim: “rescued 75,000 Jews”

1. **Story:** Max personally saved 75,000 people.
2. **Appearance:** Modern publisher descriptions and commemorative accounts.
3. **Evidence:** Warburg and his institutions unquestionably facilitated large-scale emigration; stronger institutional language says “tens of thousands.” Individual attribution and the exact total are methodologically difficult.
4. **Assessment:** **Broad rescue activity documented; exact personal total unverified.**
5. **Effect:** Central to posthumous commemoration and sometimes obscures collective institutional work and failed cases.

## Works by Max Warburg

Max was not a prolific public author. His principal known output consists of memoranda, speeches, letters, economic advice, and memoir notes rather than books.

### Authored/published during his lifetime

- **“Viod” (Vereinigung jüdischer Organisationen Deutschlands).** *Neue Jüdische Monatshefte*, year 2, no. 14, 25 April 1918, pp. 315–318. Genuine signed article.
- **Versailles economic memoranda and expert recommendations** (1919), including participation in the 18–19 June expert warning against acceptance. Collective governmental documents; not sole authorship.
- **Speeches and addresses**, many unpublished or preserved as typescripts, including the opening greeting for the Jewish Community House, Hamburg, 9 January 1938. Genuine speech; modern digital edition and English translation available.
- **Correspondence**, including extensive family, banking, relief, and political letters. Individual letters survive across the Max M. Warburg Collection, Warburg Institute family correspondence, Schiff/Warburg documentation, and other institutional collections. These are archival documents, not a single author-edited publication.

### Posthumous memoir

- ** *Aus meinen Aufzeichnungen* ** (New York, 1952, privately printed). Memoir/notes by Max M. Warburg, selected or edited and issued posthumously by Eric M. Warburg. Some reference works incorrectly give the title as *Aus meinen Erinnerungen*. It is a primary recollective source but not a contemporaneously published autobiography; wording and selection passed through posthumous family editing.

### Materials not found

No patents, novels, technical inventions, paintings, musical compositions, films, or commercial recordings under Max's name were found. Claims should not confuse his works with those of Aby Warburg, Paul Warburg, or later Max M. Warburg Jr.

## Death and Posthumous Restitution

Max died in New York on 26 December 1946. A contemporary JTA obituary called him a banker, philanthropist, and Jewish communal leader and emphasized his 1938 departure from Germany and 1944 naturalization. No dependable source consulted supplied a specific cause of death.

In 1949 Eric M. Warburg represented the family in a restitution settlement through which the Warburgs again became partners in the Hamburg bank. The Warburg name gradually returned; in 1969 the firm became M. M. Warburg-Brinckmann, Wirtz & Co., and later M. M. Warburg & CO. These events belong to the posthumous corporate legacy and do not mean Max personally recovered the firm.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

- **Hamburg commemoration:** Warburgstraße in Rotherbaum was named for him in 1947. His honorary role in Hamburg history has increasingly been restored after institutions expelled him under racial law.
- **Banking:** He transformed a respected merchant bank into one of Germany's leading private banks, particularly in overseas and trade finance, and brought it through the 1931 crisis. The bank survived Aryanization under trusted successors and ultimately restored family participation.
- **University and scholarship:** His financing contributed to Hamburg's scientific foundation, Colonial Institute, and university. His support preserved Aby's library, now the Warburg Institute. This legacy includes both major scholarship and the colonial purposes of part of Hamburg's academic infrastructure.
- **Jewish self-help:** He became a central organizer of German Jewish emigration finance and communal institutions under Nazi rule. The scale was large; exact personal rescue totals remain uncertain.
- **German-American networks:** Through Paul, Felix, and Eric, the family embodied transatlantic finance. Max himself remained the German/Hamburg pole until expulsion. Eric later made reconciliation and Atlantic institutions a family legacy.
- **Historiographical reinterpretation:** Early postwar accounts stressed the patriotic banker and benefactor; later work analyzed elite finance, imperialism, Haavara, Aryanization, and the limits of accommodation. Conspiracy culture continues to recast the same network as secret supranational rule.
- **Name confusion:** Modern honors sometimes go to his grandson Max M. Warburg Jr. (born 1948). The 2017 Leo Baeck Medal cited the family's historical self-help work but was awarded to the grandson, not posthumously to Max Sr.

## Atlas Connections

### Aby Moritz Warburg

- **[T3] Documented fact:** Max and his elder brother Aby maintained sustained personal and institutional correspondence. The Warburg Institute catalogue records, among other holdings, Max’s letters to Aby dated 29 November and 3 December 1927 concerning the Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg’s finances and governance. [Warburg Institute Archive catalogue](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/sites/default/files/WIA%20Section%20I.pdf)

- **[T5] Documented fact:** The brothers knew one another throughout their lives, and Max became Aby’s principal financial supporter after their youthful agreement that Aby would relinquish the expected banking role in return for books. The bargain’s precise wording is a **reported family recollection**, but their sibling relationship, direct dealings, and Max’s continuing support are documented.

- **[T9-] Documented fact:** Aby originated the Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg; after Aby’s death in 1929, Max chaired its Hamburg governing committee and supported its transfer—books, photographs, papers, furnishings, and staff—to London in 1933. Max is the later party in this shared-object connection. [Warburg Institute history](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/about-us/history-warburg-institute/transfer-warburg-institute)

### Paul Moritz Warburg

- **[T3] Documented fact:** Max and his younger brother Paul maintained extensive transatlantic family, banking, and political correspondence, including exchanges about wartime finance, German-American relations, and Jewish relief during the First World War. Their letters document conflicting national obligations rather than coordinated control of both governments. Relevant correspondence survives in the [Paul Moritz Warburg Papers at Yale](https://archives.yale.edu/repositories/12/resources/2983).

- **[T5] Documented fact:** Max and Paul were brothers, banking colleagues, and recurring personal interlocutors. Paul joined M. M. Warburg & Co. in 1895 before settling in New York, while their later family and financial contacts linked the Hamburg bank with American banking networks.

### Albert Einstein

- **[T3] Documented fact:** Max and Einstein corresponded in 1930 about a proposed agricultural-colonization project for Eastern European Jews in Peru. Max wrote Einstein on 24 April after obtaining expert assessments and concluded that the proposal was impracticable; Einstein wrote to Max from Berlin on 7 July offering his name and assistance to promote or clarify the project. [Chronology of the Franz Oppenheimer papers](https://wiki.studiumdigitale.uni-frankfurt.de/SOZFRA/images/a/a3/FO_Chronik.pdf); [Einstein’s letter to Max Warburg](https://www.shapell.org/manuscript/einstein-1930-jewish-refugees-in-peru/)

### Chaim Weizmann

- **[T5] Documented fact:** Weizmann made a one-day personal visit to Max in Hamburg on 25 December 1929. In a confidential letter to Felix Warburg dated 26 December, Max described their extended discussion of Palestine, the Jewish Agency, political conditions, and relations among Zionists and non-Zionists. [American Jewish Archives publication of the letter](https://sites.americanjewisharchives.org/publications/journal/PDF/1999_51_01_02_doc_wentling.pdf)

### Martin Buber

- **[T3] Documented fact:** Max addressed a letter to Buber on 1 October 1933. The document is cited from the Max M. Warburg file at the Weizmann Institute of Science in scholarship on Hamburg Jewry; the surviving citation establishes one-sided written engagement, although the accessible source excerpt does not reproduce the letter’s full contents. [Published archival citation](https://igdj-hh.de/upload/freimark_preussen_hamburg.pdf)

### David Ben-Gurion

- **[T4] Documented fact:** At the Jewish Agency Council meeting in Lucerne in September 1935, Max participated in the proceedings and presented nominations for an Executive whose listed members included Ben-Gurion. This supports presence in the same specifically documented institutional session, but not by itself friendship or a separate personal meeting. [Contemporary meeting report](https://www.infocenters.co.il/jabo/jabo_multimedia/p316/10992.pdf)
