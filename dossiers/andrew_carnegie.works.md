## Works by Andrew Carnegie

### Travel books and memoir

- **1879:** *Notes of a Trip Round the World*. Privately printed travel notes from Carnegie’s 1878–79 journey; repeated private impressions preceded the substantially public manifestation *Round the World*, so the two titles are related states of one evolving obra rather than wholly independent books. [Carnegie’s account](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17976/17976-h/17976-h.htm) and the contemporary *Encyclopedia Americana* give 1879 for the private printing. [Source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Encyclopedia_Americana_%281920%29/Carnegie,_Andrew)

- **1882:** *Our Coaching Trip, Brighton to Inverness*. A privately printed record of the Carnegie party’s 1881 coaching journey; Carnegie’s autobiography identifies it as the precursor to *An American Four-in-Hand in Britain*. [Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17976/17976-h/17976-h.htm)

- **1883:** *An American Four-in-Hand in Britain*. The public, revised manifestation of *Our Coaching Trip*: a travel narrative whose identity therefore resides partly in revision and republication rather than in an entirely new journey. [Source](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Encyclopedia_Americana_%281920%29/Carnegie,_Andrew)

- **1884:** *Round the World*. The commercial manifestation of Carnegie’s privately circulated travel notes; Carnegie recalled that Scribner’s requested it after extracts and notices of the private book reached newspapers. [Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17976/17976-h/17976-h.htm)

- **Written principally 1914; published 1920:** *Autobiography of Andrew Carnegie*. An unfinished autobiographical manuscript published posthumously under John C. Van Dyke’s editorship. Because the editor rearranged and revised material selected after Carnegie’s death, the published book is not an entirely unmediated authorial object. [Text and publication record](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17976/17976-h/17976-h.htm); [Library of Congress papers](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm79015107/)

### Political, economic, and biographical books

- **1886; revised 1893:** *Triumphant Democracy; or, Fifty Years’ March of the Republic*. A comparative political-economic argument for American republican institutions; the extensively revised edition is a later manifestation of the same obra, not a separate title. [Contemporary bibliography](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/A_Carnegie_anthology_%28IA_cu31924029807942%29.pdf)

- **1900–1901:** *The Gospel of Wealth and Other Timely Essays*. A constructed collection of previously published essays rather than a single newly written treatise; British and American manifestations bear 1900 and 1901 dates, respectively. Its customary shortened title, *The Gospel of Wealth*, can misleadingly identify either the collection or its foundational 1889 essay. [Carnegie Corporation history](https://carnegie.org/publication/the-gospel-of-wealth/); [contemporary bibliography](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/A_Carnegie_anthology_%28IA_cu31924029807942%29.pdf)

- **1902:** *The Empire of Business*. A collection assembled from Carnegie’s earlier essays, addresses, and journalistic interventions on business, labor, education, wealth, and public affairs; it is thus a composite obra whose components had prior lives. [Google Books record for Carnegie’s contemporary anthology and bibliography](https://books.google.com/books?id=uJ2bebSSxPMC)

- **1903:** *The Secret of Business Is the Management of Men*. A short business text or separately issued extract rather than a book of the scale of Carnegie’s major collections; bibliographies do not consistently distinguish its first separate manifestation from the underlying essay or address. [Bibliographic listing](https://www.carnegielibrariesiowa.org/andrew-carnegie-resources/)

- **1905:** *James Watt*. A compact biography in the “Famous Scots” series. It is a commissioned historical book and act of Scottish commemoration, distinct from Carnegie’s collections of occasional writing. [Contemporary bibliography](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/A_Carnegie_anthology_%28IA_cu31924029807942%29.pdf)

- **1907–1908:** *Problems of To-day: Wealth—Labor—Socialism*. A collection of revised essays and addresses; sources variously date the American publication 1907 or 1908, and the title page of the particular manifestation should govern any edition-level date. [Contemporary bibliography](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/A_Carnegie_anthology_%28IA_cu31924029807942%29.pdf); [contemporary biographical notice](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Cyclop%C3%A6dia_of_American_Biography/Carnegie,_Andrew)

### Manifestos and programmatic essays

- **1886:** *An Employer’s View of the Labor Question*. A magazine essay also issued separately as a pamphlet. It intervenes in industrial relations from the employer’s position and therefore functions partly as a manifesto, although Carnegie’s later conduct at Homestead made its conciliatory authorship politically contested. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **February 1889:** *The Bugaboo of Trusts*. A *North American Review* essay subsequently reprinted as a pamphlet and absorbed into later collections; those are manifestations of the essay, not additional obras. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **June 1889:** *Wealth*. The first of the two *North American Review* essays later jointly called “The Gospel of Wealth.” It is a manifesto in the strong sense: it prescribes the rich person’s social duty and supplied the protocol Carnegie claimed for his own philanthropy. [Carnegie Corporation text and publication history](https://carnegie.org/publication/the-gospel-of-wealth/)

- **December 1889:** *The Best Fields for Philanthropy*. The companion essay to “Wealth,” specifying preferred forms of giving; together the two essays became the textual project called “The Gospel of Wealth.” [Carnegie Corporation publication history](https://carnegie.org/publication/the-gospel-of-wealth/)

- **1890:** *Some Facts about the American Republic*. An address delivered at Dundee and subsequently circulated in print; it is an occasional political text rather than a conventional book. [Source recorded in the contemporary anthology](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/A_Carnegie_anthology_%28IA_cu31924029807942%29.pdf)

- **1904:** *Industrial Peace*. An address delivered at the National Civic Federation’s annual dinner on December 15 and issued as a pamphlet. It acts as an industrial-relations intervention rather than merely documenting the occasion. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **1905:** *A League of Peace*. Carnegie’s rectorial address at the University of St Andrews on October 17, later issued in several pamphlet and periodical manifestations. It is a peace manifesto proposing organized international power rather than a retrospective description. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Andrew_Carnegie)

- **1906:** *Edwin M. Stanton*. A commemorative address delivered for Stanton Memorial Day at Kenyon College and separately published; it occupies the boundary between speech, historical portrait, and memorial object. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **1906:** *An Address Delivered on the Opening of the New Engineering Buildings of the University of Edinburgh*. A ceremonial speech made into a pamphlet; the event and printed text are distinct manifestations of the same intervention. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **1907:** *Ezra Cornell*. An address to Cornell University students on the founder’s centennial, separately printed afterward. Its made object is simultaneously an oration and an institutional act of memory. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **1907:** *The Negro in America*. An address delivered before Edinburgh’s Philosophical Institution on October 16 and printed as a pamphlet. The historical title is Carnegie’s; its racial terminology and paternalistic argument belong to the period and should not be silently normalized. [Library of Congress collection description](https://www.loc.gov/item/mm79015107/)

- **1910:** *Speech at the Annual Meeting of the Peace Society, Guildhall, London, May 24, 1910*. A separately printed peace address whose descriptive title varies slightly among manifestations; no more definitive literary title should be invented. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **1911:** *Britain and Her Offspring*. A political pamphlet advocating closer relations among English-speaking peoples; it is an occasional programmatic text, not a full-length book. [Bibliographic record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Carnegie%2C%20Andrew%2C%201835-1919)

- **1854–1920:** *Speeches, articles, memoranda, letters, and book files*. This is an archival body of work rather than one titled obra. The Library of Congress finding aid documents a “Speeches and Writings File” but does not establish a complete critical canon of every newspaper interview, letter, draft, or speech, so forcing each surviving document into an allegedly exhaustive list would confuse archival remnants with settled works. [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms009340/ms009340.pdf)

### Compilations and mediated textual objects

- **1915:** *A Carnegie Anthology*. Margaret Barclay Wilson arranged this privately printed calendar of extracts from Carnegie’s writings and speeches, accompanied by portraits and a bibliography. Carnegie supplied the underlying words but Wilson made the selection and arrangement; consequently it resists classification as a solely authored Carnegie work. [Digitized volume](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/db/A_Carnegie_anthology_%28IA_cu31924029807942%29.pdf)

### Industrial projects

- **1872–1901:** *Carnegie’s integrated iron-and-steel enterprise*. An evolving industrial project encompassing partnerships, mills, coke supplies, transport interests, and sales organizations, ultimately consolidated as Carnegie Steel Company in 1892. It is a corporate and infrastructural obra made collectively by workers, engineers, managers, and partners; calling it Carnegie’s individual “work” would erase that distributed authorship. [Carnegie Corporation biographical history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1892–1901:** *Carnegie Steel Company*. The corporate manifestation of Carnegie’s consolidated steel holdings, sold to J. P. Morgan in 1901 and absorbed into United States Steel. Carnegie created and controlled the organization, but neither its mills nor their output can responsibly be attributed to him alone. [Carnegie Corporation history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

### Cultural and educational Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1881–1917:** *The Carnegie library program*. A distributed philanthropic project that Carnegie and, later, his corporation financed through conditional grants: 2,509 public-library buildings worldwide received approximately $56 million. The buildings are not individually Carnegie-authored—local authorities selected sites and architects and undertook maintenance—so the obra is best understood as the grant protocol and international network, not as 2,509 personally designed works. [Program history](https://carnegie.org/article/andrew-carnegies-library-legacy/)

- **1889:** *Braddock Carnegie Library*. The first Carnegie-funded library to open in the United States. It is a funded civic institution and building rather than Carnegie’s architectural work. [Library-program chronology](https://carnegie.org/article/andrew-carnegies-library-legacy/)

- **1890:** *Carnegie Free Library of Allegheny*. The first American Carnegie library offered a grant, although Braddock opened earlier; this distinction explains conflicting claims about the “first” Carnegie library. [Library-program chronology](https://carnegie.org/article/andrew-carnegies-library-legacy/)

- **1891:** *Carnegie Hall*. A three-auditorium performing-arts complex opened as the Music Hall and designed by William B. Tuthill. Carnegie initiated and financed it, but architectural authorship belongs to Tuthill; the institution, building, and continuing performance culture together make it a qualified Gesamtkunstwerk. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1895:** *Carnegie Institute of Pittsburgh*. Conceived as a combined library, art gallery, music hall, and natural-history museum, it deliberately joins media and forms of knowledge and therefore fits the category Gesamtkunstwerk unusually well. Its present descendants include the Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh and Carnegie Museums of Pittsburgh. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1900:** *Carnegie Technical Schools*. An educational institution established in Pittsburgh and later transformed through mergers and changes of status into Carnegie Mellon University. Carnegie made the founding project and endowment, not the present university as a finished personal work. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1901:** *New York City branch-library project*. A $5.2 million grant intended to construct 65 branches and bind neighborhood libraries into a genuinely public system. It is an urban knowledge-network project rather than a single building. [Library-program chronology](https://carnegie.org/article/andrew-carnegies-library-legacy/)

### Philanthropic protocols and institutions

- **1901:** *Carnegie Trust for the Universities of Scotland*. A trust created by deed on June 7, 1901, and incorporated by royal charter in 1902 to support research, university development, and students of limited means. Its operative object is a continuing funding protocol rather than a static monument. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1902:** *Carnegie Institution of Washington*. A scientific-research institution endowed with $22 million, now called the Carnegie Institution for Science. Carnegie established its conditions and resources, while its scientific discoveries belong to its researchers rather than to him. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1903:** *Carnegie Foundation and the Peace Palace project*. A Dutch foundation endowed with $1.5 million to erect and maintain a courthouse and international-law library at The Hague; the resulting Peace Palace opened in 1913. Carnegie made the institutional and financial project, while Louis M. Cordonnier and others made the architecture. [Project history](https://carnegie.org/article/built-to-last/)

- **1903:** *Carnegie Dunfermline Trust*. A civic trust created for Carnegie’s birthplace, supporting Pittencrieff Park, recreation grounds, clinics, cultural facilities, and educational activity. It is a deliberately open-ended urban and social project rather than one object. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1904:** *Carnegie Hero Fund Commission*. A $5 million fund established by deed after the Harwick mine disaster to recognize civilian rescue and support injured rescuers or the dependants of those killed. Its deed is a protocol that converts acts of rescue into continuing public recognition and material aid. [Institutional history](https://carnegie.org/article/the-carnegie-hero-fund-commission/)

- **1905:** *Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching*. Initially a $10 million pension system for college teachers, later an institution concerned with educational standards and research. Carnegie created the institutional mechanism; its later programs are manifestations made by successors. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1908–1911:** *The international Carnegie Hero Funds*. Carnegie replicated the American hero-fund protocol in Britain and nine continental European countries. These related legal bodies are better understood as national manifestations of one philanthropic project than as a single centrally governed institution. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1910:** *Carnegie Endowment for International Peace*. A $10 million institution intended to advance the abolition of international war. It is the organizational manifestation of Carnegie’s peace project, not evidence that the project achieved its intended end. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1911:** *Carnegie Corporation of New York*. Carnegie’s final and broadest grantmaking foundation, endowed with most of his remaining fortune and instructed to advance and diffuse knowledge while allowing trustees to adapt to changing conditions. Renamed the Andrew Carnegie Foundation in 2026, it is a deliberately self-revising philanthropic protocol rather than a fixed work. [Foundation history](https://carnegie.org/about/history/)

- **1913:** *Carnegie United Kingdom Trust*. A grantmaking trust for social and educational improvement in Britain and Ireland. Its programs change over time, so the continuing trust—not every later grant—is Carnegie’s made object. [Carnegie institutional overview](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)

- **1914:** *Church Peace Union*. A $2 million organization intended to mobilize religious and moral institutions against war, later renamed the Carnegie Council for Ethics in International Affairs. Its transformation after Carnegie makes it an evolving institutional project rather than an invariant work. [Carnegie institutional history](https://carnegie.org/about/andrew-carnegie-story/)
