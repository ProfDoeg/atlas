## Works by Johannes Trithemius

### Monastic rules, exhortations, and spiritual exercises

- **1484–1486:** *Summa dictorum patrum de vitiis et virtutibus*. A two-book florilegium of patristic sayings on virtues and vices, Trithemius’s earliest named composition; he reports that it remained unpublished, and its survival is uncertain.
- **c. 1486:** *Sermones sive exhortationes ad monachos*. Two books of exhortations addressed to monks; a monastic teaching collection rather than a record of sermons delivered on particular occasions.
- **c. 1486–1487:** *Commentarius in Regulam sancti Benedicti*. Commentary on the Benedictine Rule. Trithemius describes two completed books and two still to be written, so the planned four-book project was never completed.
- **c. 1486–1487:** *De tentationibus religiosorum*. Two-book treatise on the temptations encountered in religious life.
- **c. 1486:** *De institutione vitae sacerdotalis*. A protocol-like manual for priestly conduct, written for a priest named Nicolaus; an edition appeared by 1494.
- **c. 1487:** *De proprietate monachorum* / *De vitio proprietatis monachorum*. Reform tract against monks’ private ownership of property.
- **c. 1490:** *De modo et forma visitationis monachorum*. An instructional protocol for conducting visitations of monasteries.
- **before 1494:** *De vanitate et miseria humanae vitae*. A contemplative treatise designed to turn its reader from worldly attachments.
- **1493:** *Liber penthicus sive lugubris de statu et ruina ordinis monastici*. A lament on the decline of Benedictine observance, composed for Abbot Blasius of Hirsau and intended to act upon its monastic audience through communal reading.
- **1496:** *De cura pastorali*. An oration and practical admonition on pastoral responsibility.
- **1496:** *De duodecim excidiis observantiae regularis*. An oration identifying twelve causes of the destruction of regular observance.
- **1497:** *De triplici regione claustralium*. A three-book scheme of monastic formation, distinguishing beginners, the progressing, and the perfect.
- **1497:** *De spirituali exercitio monachorum*. A practical program of spiritual exercise appended to *De triplici regione claustralium*.
- **1497:** *Compendium spiritualis exercitii*. An abridged protocol derived from *De spirituali exercitio monachorum* at the request of the abbot of Bursfelde.
- **1497:** *De operatione divini amoris*. An oration on divine love delivered at the Bursfelde Congregation’s Erfurt chapter on 27 August 1497.
- **c. 1500:** *De vera conversione mentis ad Deum*. A contemplative oration on the mind’s conversion to God.
- **date uncertain, before 1507:** *De institutione virginum*. A treatise for women religious on consecrated virginity; Trithemius listed it among his works, but its textual survival is uncertain.
- **date uncertain, before 1507:** *De continentia viduarum*. A treatise on widowhood known from Trithemius’s own catalogue; he could no longer remember its opening words, and its survival is uncertain.
- **date uncertain, before 1507:** *De institutione vitae coniugalis*. A treatise on married life, likewise self-recorded but apparently lost.
- **date uncertain, before 1507:** *De simonia et proprietate monialium*. A responsum to the physician Burkhard von Horneck concerning simony and property-holding by nuns.
- **date uncertain:** *De modo et forma celebrandi capitulum provinciale*. A protocol for holding a provincial chapter.
- **date uncertain:** *De modo celebrandi capitulum annale*. A related protocol for the annual chapter, written for the abbot of Bursfelde.
- **date uncertain:** *Constitutiones capitulorum provincialium*. Trithemius’s edited and abridged collection of provincial-chapter statutes.
- **date uncertain:** *Constitutiones annales capituli*. A further collection of annual statutes prepared at Cologne and Sponheim; Trithemius says it had not been published.

### Learned catalogues and praises of writing

- **1491–1492; printed 1494:** *De scriptoribus ecclesiasticis*. A chronological bio-bibliographical catalogue of nearly a thousand Christian writers. It is both reference work and an intervention in the recovery of neglected texts; early manuscript states differ from the printed recension.
- **1491–1495; printed 1495:** *Catalogus illustrium virorum Germaniae*. A bio-bibliographical catalogue asserting a specifically German learned tradition; its entries include authors absent from the larger *De scriptoribus ecclesiasticis*.
- **1492:** *De viris illustribus ordinis sancti Benedicti*. A four-book Benedictine bio-bibliography covering the order’s history, saints, prelates, and writers.
- **1492:** *De laude scriptorum manualium*. A manifesto for manuscript copying after the arrival of print: it argues that monastic scribal labor remains spiritually and materially valuable.
- **1492:** *De origine, progressu et laudibus ordinis fratrum Carmelitarum*. A two-book institutional history and encomium of the Carmelites, incorporating the order’s contested claim of descent from Elijah.
- **1492:** *In laudem et commendationem Ruperti abbatis Tuitiensis*. An encomium of Rupert of Deutz. Its extent and independent transmission are less secure than those of Trithemius’s major catalogues.
- **date uncertain:** *De computatione paschali*. A one-book protocol for calculating Easter, composed for the Franciscan Albertus Latro; Trithemius says it was unpublished, and it is apparently lost.

### Chronicles, annals, genealogy, and saints’ lives

- **c. 1495–1503, continued to 1509:** *Chronicon Sponheimense*. Chronicle of Sponheim Abbey from its foundation through Trithemius’s own abbacy. Most of the work is his, but later continuations complicate the boundary of the obra; its early history also participates in his disputed use or invention of sources.
- **1495–1503:** *Chronicon Hirsaugiense*. The first Hirsau chronicle commissioned by Abbot Blasius. Trithemius described the project as substantially written but initially unfinished; it is distinct from his later, much larger *Annales Hirsaugienses*.
- **c. 1500–1506:** *Chronicon successionis ducum Bavariae et comitum Palatinorum*. A dynastic history of the dukes of Bavaria and counts palatine, made for the Palatine court. Its genealogical constructions must be treated cautiously because Trithemius sometimes supplied fabulous or fictitious ancient authorities.
- **1507–1514:** *Chronicon monasterii sancti Iacobi Herbipolensis* / *Compendium breve fundationis et reformationis monasterii sancti Iacobi*. Chronicle of St James’s Abbey at Würzburg. The work is partly institutional record, partly the new abbot’s construction of his monastery’s past.
- **1509–1514:** *Annales Hirsaugienses*. A vast two-volume history extending far beyond Hirsau into Frankish and German history. Its scale makes it a historiographical Gesamtkunstwerk of annal, institutional memory, biography, genealogy, and imperial history; several alleged early sources are judged fictitious, and its reliability is consequently disputed.
- **c. 1514:** *Compendium de origine gentis Francorum* / *Compendium sive breviarium primi voluminis annalium*. An abridgment of the opening portion of the larger Frankish-German annals.
- **c. 1514–1515:** *De origine regum et gentis Francorum*. A compact genealogy and origin-history of the Franks; its relationship to the preceding compendium is close enough that catalogues do not always delimit the two consistently.
- **c. 1510–1514:** *De ducibus et episcopis Franciae orientalis*. A history of the dukes and bishops of East Francia made for Bishop Lorenz von Bibra; known through early work lists, with uncertain survival as an independent text.
- **c. 1510–1514:** *Vita beati Rabani Mauri*. A three-book life of Rabanus Maurus dedicated to Archbishop Albrecht of Mainz and Magdeburg.
- **c. 1510–1514:** *De translatione sancti Rabani Mauri*. Account of the translation of Rabanus’s relics from Mainz toward Saxony; conceived as a companion to the *Vita*.
- **c. 1510–1514:** *Vita beati Maximi episcopi Moguntini*. A saint’s life of the reputed Mainz bishop Maximus. The historicity and identity of its subject are problematic, making the text as much a production of cultic memory as a recoverable biography.
- **c. 1510–1514:** *De laudibus et miraculis beatae Mariae in Dittelsbach factis*. A two-book collection of Marian miracles associated with Dettelbach.
- **c. 1510–1514:** *De miraculis beatae Mariae in Heilbrunn*. A six-book Marian miracle collection known from early inventories; its precise title, place-name, and completeness vary in later references.

### Cryptography, constructed languages, and occult philosophy

- **1499–c. 1500, subsequently revised:** *Steganographia*. A project for eight books of concealed communication, of which Trithemius completed two and began a third. Its angelic and planetary language made it appear necromantic, but the first two books and the long-unread third contain cryptographic systems; the unfinished eight-book intention is part of the work and should not be silently collapsed into the surviving three-book text.
- **before 1507:** *Glossēuphoria* / *Glottēuphoria*. A lost or unlocated system said to enable someone ignorant of Latin to generate grammatical Latin through an occult writing method. Its extraordinary claims survive chiefly in Trithemius’s own work list, so it resists treatment as a securely recoverable text.
- **1507–1508; revised through at least 1514:** *Polygraphia*. A six-book cryptographic handbook constructed from alphabets, substitution tables, coded vocabularies, and combinatorial devices; completed in manuscript and printed posthumously in 1518.
- **c. 1508–1514:** *Clavis Polygraphiae*. A key and explanatory supplement to the *Polygraphia*. It circulates with that work but is separately titled and functions as its decoding protocol.
- **1508:** *De septem secundeis, sive Chronologia mystica*. A compact astrological world chronology organized by seven planetary intelligences. Trithemius presents it as historical explanation while intermittently distancing himself from literal belief.
- **1508:** *Antipalus maleficiorum*. A four-book polemical and bibliographical manual against illicit magic, witchcraft, and harmful superstition. It catalogues forbidden magical books while prescribing ways to recognize and oppose them.
- **1507–unfinished at death:** *De daemonibus*. A projected twelve-book demonological refutation of magic and superstition. Only part was completed; its detailed plan survives, but calling it a finished treatise would erase its status as an unfinished project.
- **c. 1508:** *Hieraticum*. A work in thirty-four sections said to concern remedies for illnesses, made for Elector Joachim of Brandenburg. It is reported in early testimony but is lost or unidentified, and later occult traditions may have distorted what kind of object it was.
- **c. 1508:** *De causis et remediis morborum fugacium et maleficiorum*. A three-book medical-demonological work on the causes and remedies of elusive illnesses and harmful magic, known from an early list but not securely extant.
- **c. 1508:** *De licitis et illicitis cogitationibus Christianorum*. Two books distinguishing ideas or practices permissible and impermissible to Christians; known from early testimony and apparently lost.

### Theology, controversy, and court commissions

- **1508:** *Liber octo quaestionum ad Maximilianum Caesarem*. Responses to eight theological questions posed by Emperor Maximilian I, addressing faith and understanding, miracles, Scripture, evil, providence, and divine permission; later print dates should not be mistaken for dates of composition.
- **c. 1505–1508:** *Panaletheia*. A fourteen-book courtly miscellany of questions arising from Trithemius’s discussions with Elector Joachim of Brandenburg. Trithemius says he completed it, but the work is lost or unlocated.
- **c. 1510–1515:** *Apologia adversus Carolum Bovillum*. A defense against Charles de Bovelles’s charge that the *Steganographia* was magical or demonic. Early witnesses list it as a separate work, but its survival and final form are uncertain.
- **date uncertain:** *Contra simoniam et proprietatem quarundam monialium*. A juridical-theological response concerning simony and nuns’ property; possibly the same work as *De simonia et proprietate monialium*, so it should not be counted twice without reservation.

### Marian and saintly devotion

- **1494:** *De laudibus sanctissimae matris Annae*. A devotional and hagiographical treatise promoting the cult of St Anne.
- **before 1507:** *Cursus sive officium quotidianum sanctae Annae*. A daily liturgical office for St Anne.
- **before 1507:** *Rosarium sanctae Annae*. A devotional rosary for St Anne.
- **before 1507:** *Oratio supplicatoria ad sanctam Annam*. Supplicatory prayer or small group of prayers to St Anne; transmission does not always preserve a stable boundary between these pieces and the larger Anne dossier.
- **1507:** *De laudibus sancti Ioseph*. A twenty-chapter devotional treatise on Joseph as foster father of Christ.
- **1507:** *Rosarium sancti Ioseph*. A rosary appended to the Joseph treatise.
- **1507:** *Tres orationes supplicatoriae ad sanctum Ioseph*. Three devotional prayers accompanying the Joseph dossier.
- **1507:** *Officium missae de sancto Ioseph*. A liturgical office and Mass for St Joseph.
- **1507:** *Sequentia de sancto Ioseph*. A sequence with a melody intended to be sung by boys. This is a musical-liturgical composition, not merely a prose “book”; the independently recoverable status of its melody is uncertain.
- **various dates:** *Collectae de sanctis*. A body of collects written for particular saints and their custodians. Trithemius names the activity but not every prayer, title, or date, so this resists conversion into a fictitiously precise list of individual works.

### Autobiography, correspondence, and institutional creations

- **1483–1505:** *The reformed library of Sponheim*. An institutional obra rather than a text: Trithemius assembled roughly two thousand manuscript and printed volumes in multiple languages. The collection was dispersed, and the surviving descriptions are partly his own self-representation, but the library itself was one of his principal made things.
- **c. 1502–1507:** *Nepiachus*. An autobiographical and self-bibliographical work presenting his life as an intellectual and spiritual pilgrimage. Trithemius did not intend publication during his lifetime, and its work list explicitly includes unfinished and unpublished projects.
- **1505–1516:** *Epistolae familiares*. A deliberately assembled correspondence rather than every letter he happened to send. The first compilations covered his departure from Sponheim and first year at Würzburg; four Würzburg books were eventually reported, while modern scholarship counts about 296 surviving letters.
- **1484–1516:** *Uncollected letters, occasional orations, prayers, maxims, and minor works*. Trithemius himself acknowledges numerous untitled minor compositions and letters for which he retained no copies. They are genuine making but resist itemization: supplying titles or dates would manufacture works that the evidence does not name.
- **before 1507:** *Early devotional compilations destroyed by Trithemius*. He says he burned his first rough compositions as insufficiently formed. These are manifestations he deliberately unmade; they cannot responsibly be promoted into separately titled works.
