## Works by Martín Miguel de Güemes

### Correspondence

- **1810–1821:** *Epistolario*. A surviving body of private and official letters rather than a deliberately composed book; its boundaries remain open because documents were dispersed or lost. Luis Güemes’s documentary edition prints letters both by and to Güemes, while Susana Martorell de Laconi separates his letters from parts, official communications, and proclamations; consequently, not every item conventionally called his “correspondence” is his own composition.[^1][^2]

- **1813–1821:** *Oficios, partes y escritos de gobierno*. A heterogeneous administrative corpus—requests, military reports, orders, financial instructions, diplomatic communications, and dispatches—preserved chiefly in *Güemes documentado*. These resist treatment as individually titled works: their editorial headings are generally supplied descriptions, and many were drafted or copied within a governmental secretariat before receiving Güemes’s signature.[^2][^3]

- **20 January 1813:** *Solicitud al Gobierno Central para que se le comuniquen las causas de su confinación*. A petition asserting a procedural claim rather than a literary work; Martorell de Laconi catalogues it from *Güemes documentado*, vol. 2, pp. 12–13.[^2]

- **6 December 1813:** *Oficio al director supremo Gervasio Antonio de Posadas*. An official request for reinstatement in the army under José de San Martín, catalogued in *Güemes documentado*, vol. 2, pp. 25–26.[^2]

- **4 January 1816:** *Oficio al gobernador de Córdoba*. A requisition and policy communication requesting horses for the northern defence and proposing voluntary donations or a forced loan; it is not a freestanding treatise, although the document records Güemes’s fiscal and military project.[^2]

- **11 January 1816:** *Oficio al director supremo*. A governmental and military situation report on the aftermath of Sipe-Sipe, the resources already committed, and the obstacles to further assistance; its conventional title merely identifies document type and recipient.[^2]

- **19 September 1816:** *Comunicación a Guillermo Marquiegui*. A diplomatic refusal of a royalist approach, transmitted as correspondence rather than issued as a public work. Its frequently excerpted patriotic declaration should not be mistaken for an independently titled manifesto.[^4]

- **11 October 1817:** *Oficio a Manuel Belgrano sobre la moneda falsa*. An administrative diagnosis of counterfeit coin circulating after the loss of the Potosí mint; subsequent governmental action does not make the entire monetary policy Güemes’s sole-authored “work.”[^5]

- **1816–1819:** *Correspondencia con Manuel Belgrano*. A sustained operational exchange whose surviving record is radically asymmetrical: Martorell de Laconi counts 125 letters from Belgrano but only fourteen letters from Güemes, explaining that much of Güemes’s side took the form of official reports and dispatches. It is therefore a collaborative documentary relationship, not a single authored obra.[^6]

### Proclamations and manifestos

- **23 February 1815:** *Proclama que el ciudadano Martín Miguel Güemes… dirige a sus vecinos y habitantes*. A printed or circulated public proclamation addressed to Salta’s inhabitants: manifesto in the strict sense, because it seeks to mobilize patriots, condemn neutrality, and constitute the gauchos as a public military body. Some modern references date it 22 February, but the reproduced document closes “Salta y febrero 23 de 1815”; the date should therefore be reported as disputed in secondary description.[^7]

- **6 August 1816:** *Proclama en Jujuy sobre la monarquía incaica*. A political manifesto supporting the proposal to crown a member of the Inca dynasty; contemporary circulation in *El Censor* made it a public intervention rather than merely an army address.[^8]

- **19 September 1816:** *Proclama desde Jujuy*. A wartime announcement promising the defeat or retreat of the royalist force. Its title is editorial and descriptive; the text is known through the documentary sequence leading to the proclamation of 27 September.[^3]

- **27 September 1816:** *Proclama a los ciudadanos y compatriotas de Salta*. A public notice announcing the enemy’s retreat and calling displaced inhabitants back to their homes, trades, and commerce. It acts simultaneously as military bulletin, reassurance, and instruction, so “manifesto” and “governmental proclamation” are both apt.[^3][^9]

- **1819:** *Proclama de Martín Miguel de Güemes a los habitantes de Jujuy*. A surviving public manifesto preserved under a descriptive archival title. The consulted catalogue supplies only the year, so a more exact date should not be inferred.[^10]

### Protocols, orders, and governmental instruments

- **12 September 1815:** *Oficio al director supremo sobre la creación de la División Infernal de Gauchos de Línea*. Both a report and a constitutive protocol: it specifies a cavalry division of two squadrons, each with two companies, and orders training for service on foot as well as horseback. Because Buenos Aires did not authorize or finance the requested formation, the document records Güemes’s effective provincial act as well as an incompletely recognized national project.[^11][^12]

- **1815–1821:** *Órdenes, bandos y disposiciones del gobierno de Salta*. A dispersed performative corpus that levied resources, organized defence, regulated troops, issued payments, and administered the province. It resists attribution as a body of personally composed “works”: some instruments survive only as copies, extracts, or secretarial documents bearing Güemes’s authority.[^1][^2]

- **24 May 1818:** *Bando para retirar de circulación la moneda falsa*. A regulatory protocol ordering the withdrawal of counterfeit or irregular currency after the central government rejected Güemes’s earlier handling of it. The measure was issued under his authority, but it belongs to an administrative controversy rather than to a stable authored oeuvre.[^5]

- **2 October 1819:** *Orden de pago expedida por Güemes*. A transactional governmental instrument, preserved in *Güemes documentado*, rather than a titled composition; its status as an obra lies in what the writing authorizes, not in literary authorship.[^13]

### Military and political creations

- **1814–1821:** *Sistema de defensa de la frontera norte*, later called the *Guerra Gaucha*. An evolving military-political creation combining mobile militia, intelligence, scorched resources, local command, and popular mobilization. It was neither a single text nor Güemes’s unaided creation—the gauchos, local commanders, civilians, Belgrano, and San Martín were indispensable makers—so “collective operational system” is more accurate than personal work.[^14][^15]

- **12 September 1815–1821:** *División Infernal de Gauchos de Línea* (“Los Infernales”). A military institution created and organized by Güemes’s office, not merely the subject of his constitutive oficio. The formation was collectively embodied by its soldiers and officers; calling it solely “his work” would obscure their agency and Buenos Aires’s refusal to fund the proposed line unit.[^11][^12]

- **1815–1821:** *Gobierno militar y popular de la provincia de Salta*. A political construction arising from Güemes’s local election as governor and sustained through provincial institutions, militia service, requisition, negotiation, and coercion. It resists classification as an individual obra because the cabildos, commanders, rural population, opponents, and neighbouring governments continually made and contested it.[^16][^17]

- **1815–1821:** *Fuero gaucho*. A legal-military arrangement shielding mobilized gauchos from ordinary civil jurisdiction while they served in provincial defence. It was a practice and contested jurisdictional regime rather than a single surviving “regulation”; *Güemes documentado* treats it as a special documentary subject, and agreements with Jujuy attempted to restrict its reach.[^1][^18]

- **1820–1821:** *Ejército de Observación sobre el Perú*. An unfinished military project that Güemes was appointed to complete and command as part of the continental campaign. He assembled forces, supplies, finance, and an advance connection under José Miguel Lanza, but the intended march into Upper Peru was not realized before political conflict and Güemes’s death; its incompletion is intrinsic to the work.[^19][^20]

- **1820–1821:** *Expedición al Alto Perú en combinación con San Martín*. A strategic project—related to, but not identical with, the institution called the Army of Observation—intended to open a landward front while San Martín operated from the Pacific. Documentary evidence records preparations and financing, yet no completed expedition; attribution must also be shared with San Martín, Lanza, provincial contributors, and the assembled forces.[^19][^21][^22]
