## Works by Alfred Jarry

### Juvenile theatre: the *Ontogénie* corpus

- **1885–1890:** *Les Brigands de la Calabre*. Juvenile play preserved in the manuscript corpus that Michel Arrivé named *Ontogénie*; it was not published as an independent work during Jarry’s lifetime. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *La Clochette, ou Shadow’s Home et Death-Castle*. Three-act juvenile play belonging to *Ontogénie*. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Le Parapluie-seringue du docteur Thanaton*. One-act juvenile play; the extravagant title already makes the dramatic machine as important as conventional plot. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Roupias Tête-de-Seiche*. Two-act juvenile play in the *Ontogénie* manuscripts. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Le Procès*. One-act adaptation of Roderich Benedix’s *Der Prozess*; it is therefore both translation and theatrical remaking rather than an entirely autonomous invention. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Les Antliaclastes*. Juvenile play surviving in one-act and later three-act versions; the versions should not be silently collapsed into a single fixed text. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Kroeflich, ou l’Héritage*. One-act juvenile comedy preserved in *Ontogénie*. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Un cours de Bidasse*. One-act juvenile stage text. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Bidasse et compagnie*. Juvenile play related to *Un cours de Bidasse* but catalogued separately by the collected edition. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *L’Ouverture de la pêche*. Comedy in five scenes from the juvenile manuscript corpus. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Le Futur malgré lui*. One-act juvenile play. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1885–1890:** *Les Alcoolisés*. A one-act “opéra-chimique”: an intentionally hybrid dramatic object, closer to a miniature Gesamtkunstwerk in conception than to a conventional book. [OCG I; OCP I]

### Ubu: collective invention, plays, reductions, and manifestations

- **c. 1888:** *Les Polonais*. Schoolboy puppet play developed around the Rennes physics teacher Félix Hébert by the Morin brothers and their companions; Jarry’s exact share in the earliest state is disputed, so it resists attribution as his work alone. It became the principal source of *Ubu roi*. [OCG II; OCP I]
- **c. 1888 and later:** *Onésime, ou les Tribulations de Priou*. Early dramatic manuscript absorbed into the shifting *Ubu cocu* dossier; its independent status and relation to other versions are editorial rather than securely authorial facts. [OCG II; OCP I]
- **c. 1888–1890s:** *Ubu intime, ou les Polyèdres*. One-act Ubu manuscript published only posthumously; it belongs to the unstable workshop surrounding *Ubu cocu*. [OCG II]
- **c. 1888–1897:** *Ubu cocu, ou l’Archéoptéryx*. Five-act play surviving through several manuscript states and related fragments, including the *Paralipomènes d’Ubu*; no single lifetime edition fixed it. [OCG II; OCP I]
- **1894–1896:** *Ubu roi, ou les Polonais*. Five-act prose drama remade by Jarry from the collective school play; issued in 1896 and manifested onstage at the Théâtre de l’Œuvre on 10 December 1896. Its authorship is therefore layered even though the published rewriting is Jarry’s. [BC; OCG II]
- **1896:** *Les Paralipomènes d’Ubu*. Supplementary Ubu text published in *La Revue blanche* and subsequently incorporated into the editorial dossier of *Ubu cocu*. [BC; OCG II]
- **1896:** *Ubu Roi*. Presentation brochure and performative address prepared for the December production; this is not the play itself but a text intended to act on its audience and frame the theatrical event. [BC]
- **1896:** *Discours d’Alfred Jarry à la première d’Ubu roi*. Spoken manifesto of theatrical intention, later preserved through a published account; its first form was an event rather than a book. [BC]
- **1896–1897:** *Ubu roi avec la musique de Claude Terrasse*. Autographic score-text object joining Jarry’s manuscript with Terrasse’s music; a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk and distinct material realization of the play. [BC]
- **1899–1900:** *Ubu enchaîné*. Five-act counter-play in which Ubu seeks enslavement; published in 1900 with *Ubu roi*. [OCG IV; OCP I]
- **1901:** *Ubu sur la Butte*. Two-act reduction of *Ubu roi* made for the marionette theatre at the Cabaret des Quat’z’Arts; the reduction is a new performance object, not merely an edition. [BC; EA]
- **1896–1907:** *Autour d’Ubu*. The collected edition’s corpus of presentations, notices, speeches, fragments, songs, and other satellite texts. It resists treatment as one authored “work,” but it records Jarry’s continuing manufacture of Ubu as public persona and theatrical apparatus. [OCG II]

### Poetry, prose constructions, and mixed books

- **1891–1893:** *La Revanche de la nuit*. Early poetic sequence preserved by the collected editions; its boundaries and publication status are editorially reconstructed. [OCP I]
- **1893:** *La Ballade du vieux marin*. Jarry’s translation of Samuel Taylor Coleridge’s *The Rime of the Ancient Mariner*, apparently made in November 1893 and published posthumously; it is a translation-work, not an original poem. [BC; OCG I]
- **1893–1897:** *Textes poétiques de jeunesse*. Editorial corpus of poems not all gathered by Jarry into an authorial book; calling the whole a work would overstate its unity. [OCG I]
- **1894:** *Les Minutes de sable mémorial*. Deliberately heterogeneous book of poems, prose, drama, typography, and authorial images; it is best treated as a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a simple poetry collection. [BC; OCG II]
- **1896:** *L’Autre Alceste*. “Drama in five recitals,” first published in *La Revue blanche*; its narrative form deliberately resists the ordinary distinction between play and prose tale. [BC; OCG II]
- **1896–1898:** *L’Amour en visites*. Composite book made from separately written and published pieces. The chapter “La Peur chez l’amour” was reported as having been written by Rachilde, leaving a specific authorship dispute inside the volume. [BC; OCG III]
- **1898:** *Par la taille*. One-act “moral and comic” work in prose and verse, dated by Jarry 5 December 1898 but not issued as a book until 1906. [BC; OCG VI]
- **1899:** *L’Amour absolu*. Autographic novel issued in only fifty copies; its facsimile handwriting makes the manuscript’s visual form part of the obra. [BC; OCG III]
- **1890s–1907:** *Poèmes* / *Derniers poèmes*. Editorial corpora of separately made poems, including occasional and unpublished pieces; neither heading names a book planned by Jarry, so these groupings resist being called individual works. [OCP II; OCG V]

### Novels, speculative fictions, and unfinished projects

- **1896–1897:** *Les Jours et les nuits: roman d’un déserteur*. Novel published in 1897, transforming military experience into a discontinuous account of waking, dream, and desertion. [BC; OCG II]
- **1897–1903:** *Gestes et opinions du docteur Faustroll, pataphysicien*. Pataphysical novel assembled and partially published in installments but not issued complete until 1911; during Jarry’s life it remained both obra and unfinished publication project. [BC; OCG III]
- **1899:** *Commentaire pour servir à la construction pratique de la machine à explorer le temps*. Pseudoscientific protocol published under Doctor Faustroll’s signature: simultaneously an instruction-text, diagrammatic speculation, and fictional manifestation. [BC; OCG III]
- **1900–1901:** *Messaline: roman de l’ancienne Rome*. Historical-erotic novel published in 1901. [BC; OCG V]
- **1901–1902:** *Le Surmâle: roman moderne*. Novel published in 1902; its bicycle race, perpetual-motion food, and “love machine” join technological fantasy to satire. [BC; OCG V]
- **1902–1906:** *La Dragonne*. Large novel-project surviving as contradictory drafts, plans, clippings, dictated passages, and fragments; only “La Bataille de Morsang” appeared during Jarry’s lifetime. Charlotte Jarry contributed or transmitted parts, and the 1943 reconstruction cannot securely be equated with a finished work by Alfred Jarry: its resistance to completeness and singular authorship is essential information. [OCG VI; LD]
- **c. 1903:** *L’Objet aimé*. One-act pastoral, unpublished and unperformed during Jarry’s life; a completed-looking stage text whose public existence was wholly posthumous. [OCP II]
- **c. 1903–1904:** *Pieter de Delft*. Opéra-comique project preserved posthumously; its intended musical realization was not completed in Jarry’s lifetime. [OCP III]
- **c. 1903–1904:** *Jef*. Dramatic work preserved and published posthumously; its performance history should not be confused with its date of composition. [OCP III]

### Operettas and collaborative musical theatre

- **Date uncertain, before 1900:** *Léda*. Operetta written with Berthe Blocq, also known as Berthe Danville or Karl Rosenval; the chronological bibliography explicitly treats its date as uncertain. [BC; OCG III]
- **c. 1900–1902:** *Les Silènes*. Jarry’s adaptation of Christian Dietrich Grabbe’s *Scherz, Satire, Ironie und tiefere Bedeutung*, with music by Claude Terrasse; performed in Brussels in 1902 but published only posthumously. It is adaptation, libretto, and performance-object at once. [OCP II; EA]
- **1904?:** *Le Bon Roi Dagobert*. Operetta project made with Eugène Demolder and Claude Terrasse; the bibliography dates the collaboration only conjecturally to November 1904. [BC; OCG V]
- **1904–1905:** *Le Manoir enchanté* / *Le Manoir de Cagliostro*. Collaborative operetta by Jarry, Demolder, and Terrasse; *Le Manoir de Cagliostro* was the title used for its January 1905 stage manifestation, not necessarily a separate work. [BC; OCG V]
- **1905?:** *L’Amour maladroit*. Collaborative operetta by Jarry, Demolder, and Terrasse; January–February 1905 is conjectural rather than certain. [BC; OCG V]
- **1905–1907:** *Le Moutardier du pape*. Three-act opérette-bouffe published in 1907 with Paul Ranson’s vignettes; a libretto awaiting or implying musical and scenic realization. [BC; OCG VI]
- **before November 1907:** *Pantagruel*. Five-act, six-tableau opérette-bouffe made with Eugène Demolder and composer Claude Terrasse; the vocal score appeared posthumously in 1910, so the surviving object is collaborative and its lifetime completeness is not straightforward. [BC; OCG VI]

### Almanacs, journals, and editorial Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1894–1895:** *L’Ymagier*. Art-and-literature review founded and edited with Remy de Gourmont, combining essays, old popular images, newly cut blocks, typography, and bibliographic archaeology. Individual contributions are not always signed, and the attribution of some anonymous matter to Jarry remains editorially disputed. [OCG I; OCP I]
- **1896:** *Perhinderion*. Two-number illustrated review created after Jarry’s break with Gourmont; its title, selection of images, texts, and physical design constitute an editorial Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single conventional text. [BC; OCG I]
- **1897–1898:** *Répertoire des Pantins*. Collaborative repertoire and performance apparatus associated with Jarry, Franc-Nohain, Claude Terrasse, Pierre Bonnard, Paul Ranson, and others at the Théâtre des Pantins. Its songs, scores, puppets, images, and playlets resist assignment to one author or one stable work. [EA; OCP I]
- **1899:** *Almanach du Père Ubu illustré*. Almanac made with drawings by Pierre Bonnard; simultaneously calendar, satire, illustrated book, and extension of the Ubu performance-world. [BC; OCG III]
- **1901:** *Almanach illustré du Père Ubu*. Second collaborative almanac with Bonnard, materially and textually distinct from the 1899 object. [BC; OCG IV]

### Criticism, journalism, manifestos, and acts in print

- **1891–1897:** *Textes critiques de jeunesse*. Collected corpus of reviews, art criticism, theatre notices, and polemics, beginning before Jarry formed them into any single book. It is an editorial obra—a body of work—not an authorially unified title. [OCG I]
- **1894:** *Être et Vivre*. Philosophical-critical essay published in *L’Art littéraire*; it functions partly as an aesthetic manifesto. [BC; OCG I]
- **1894:** *Minutes d’art*. Three-part art-critical sequence published across two reviews; its serial form is the work’s historical form. [BC; OCG I]
- **1894:** *Visions actuelles et futures*. Art-critical and speculative essay. [BC; OCG I]
- **1894:** *Gerhart Hauptmann*. Critical portrait contributed to *Portraits du prochain siècle*. [BC]
- **1894:** *Filiger*. Essay on the painter Charles Filiger, later partially reused in *L’Ymagier*. [BC]
- **1894–1895:** *La Passion* / *La Passion des clous du Seigneur*. Illustrated two-part text published in separate numbers of *L’Ymagier*; its division and changed title belong to its serial existence. [BC]
- **1894–1895?:** *Vallotton*. Critical portrait intended for an unrealized second volume of *Portraits du prochain siècle* and published only posthumously; it is an unfulfilled publication project as well as a surviving text. [BC]
- **1895:** *Les Monstres*. Essay published in *L’Ymagier*. [BC]
- **1895:** *Gauguin, Filiger*. Anonymous *Ymagier* notice attributed to Jarry rather than securely signed by him; the attribution must remain qualified. [BC]
- **1896:** *De l’inutilité du théâtre au théâtre*. Theatrical manifesto arguing through paradox and stage mechanics rather than merely describing theatre. [BC; OCG II]
- **1897:** *Questions de théâtre*. Programmatic theatre essay. [BC; OCG II]
- **1897:** *Manifeste*. Public declaration breaking with Lugné-Poe; drafted by Pierre Quillard and signed by Jarry and others, so Jarry participated in its performative act but did not solely author its wording. [BC]
- **1897:** *Opinion de M. Ubu sur le 14 Juillet*. Satirical public intervention spoken through the Ubu persona. [BC]
- **1900–1903:** *Spéculations*. Large serial corpus of reviews and topical pieces, chiefly for *La Revue blanche*; Jarry did not publish it as one lifetime book, and later collections impose their own boundaries. [BC; OCP II]
- **1902–1903:** *Gestes*. Serial group of reports and speculative journalism in *La Revue blanche*, treating circus, sport, machinery, violence, and public spectacle as exemplary “gestures.” It is a recurring mode and editorial corpus rather than one fixed text. [BC; OCP II]
- **1902–1907:** *La Chandelle verte: lumières sur les choses de ce temps*. Posthumously constructed collection of Jarry’s journalism and “speculations”; the title names a genuine body of work but not a book completed by Jarry. [OCP II; OCG IV]
- **1903–1904:** *Le Périple de la littérature et de l’art*. Continuing critical column for *La Plume*, comprising theatre, art, and literary reports; it is best classified as a serial critical project. [BC]
- **1904?:** *Le Poids des mots*. Text intended for *Le Figaro* but apparently unpublished there; its dating and finished status remain uncertain. [BC]
- **1904?:** *Docteur Macbeth*. Another proposed *Figaro* text known through the posthumous corpus; it remains closer to an unrealized publication project than a securely fixed work. [BC]
- **1904?:** *Architectures protocolaires*. Proposed *Figaro* text whose title makes built form and instruction overlap; surviving evidence does not justify pretending to a firm date or lifetime publication. [BC]
- **1905:** *Le Rabelais en français moderne*. Critical notice connected with Jarry’s sustained remaking of Rabelaisian language. [BC]
- **1907:** *Albert Samain (Souvenirs)*. Memorial booklet: personal recollection, critical portrait, and act of commemoration. [BC; OCG VI]

### Translation and adaptation projects

- **1893:** *La Ballade du vieux marin*. Translation of Coleridge, also listed above because it belongs equally to Jarry’s poetic production. [BC; OCG I]
- **c. 1901–1904:** *Olalla*. Translation or adaptation of Robert Louis Stevenson’s story; the collected edition treats it as a distinct Jarry text, but its exact compositional circumstances are less firmly documented than those of his published novels. [OCP II; OCG V]
- **c. 1901–1902:** *Les Silènes*. Adaptation of Grabbe, also listed among musical works because Claude Terrasse’s music and its puppet performance are constitutive manifestations. [OCP II; EA]
- **1905–1907:** *La Papesse Jeanne*. French translation of Emmanuel Rhoïdès’s Greek novel made with Jean Saltas and published posthumously in 1908. It is collaborative translation; neither sole authorship nor completion by Jarry alone should be implied. [BC; OCG VI]
- **1905–1907:** *Pantagruel*. Rabelaisian adaptation made with Demolder and Terrasse, also listed among operettas because its textual and musical identities cannot usefully be separated. [BC; OCG VI]

### Graphic works, book objects, and theatrical apparatus

- **1894:** *Les Palotins*. Author-cut woodblock design used in *Les Minutes de sable mémorial*; it is both an independent image and a component of the book’s larger visual-verbal construction. [PGD; OCG II]
- **1894:** *Le Crocodile aux phantasmes*. Oil on cardboard catalogued among Jarry’s paintings; unlike his textual images, it is a singular painted object. [PGD]
- **1894:** *Tête de martyr*. Image made to accompany a block sketch by Remy de Gourmont in *L’Ymagier*; authorship is collaborative at the level of design and cutting. [BC; PGD]
- **1895:** *César-Antechrist*. Pen lithograph published in *L’Ymagier*, distinct from the book and play bearing the same title. [BC; PGD]
- **1895:** *Sainte Gertrude*. Woodcut signed with Jarry’s pseudonym Alain Jans and published in *L’Ymagier*. [BC; PGD]
- **1895:** *Pains d’épices*. Suite of five popular-image designs—three Saint-Nicolas subjects, a child in a cradle, and a bird—signed Alain Jans in *L’Ymagier*; the suite, rather than any later reproduction, is the relevant obra. [BC; PGD]
- **1895:** *Monsieur Ubu à cheval*. Image printed with the “Acte terrestre” of *César-Antechrist* and subsequently bound into the Ubu visual mythology. [BC; PGD]
- **1896:** *Véritable portrait de Monsieur Ubu*. Woodblock portrait of Ubu with the spiral “gidouille”; a visual definition of the character rather than an illustration subordinate to one particular edition. [PGD]
- **1898:** *Répertoire des Pantins: Ouverture d’Ubu roi*. Lithographic sheet associated with the puppet theatre and Terrasse’s music; it is poster, score-cover, and theatrical artifact. [PGD]
- **1890s–1907:** *Paintings, engravings, drawings, manuscript designs, and book illustrations catalogued by Michel Arrivé*. Arrivé’s 1968 catalogue raisonné records a much larger graphic corpus than the few conventionally titled images above. Many items are untitled, embedded in manuscripts, collaborative, known only through reproduction, or difficult to separate from typography and performance; forcing invented titles upon each sheet would falsify the catalogue. [PGD]

### Correspondence and documentary self-construction

- **1893–1907:** *Correspondance*. Hundreds of letters, notes, dedications, petitions, and practical messages preserved across the collected editions. They are made texts and often continue the Ubu persona, but Jarry did not constitute them as a single work; the corpus resists that classification. [OCP I–III; OCG I–VI]
- **1893:** *Une protestation*. Petition signed by Jarry and published in *Le Journal*; a collective performative text whose action consists in the signature, not a solely authored essay. [BC]
- **1897:** Responses to the enquiries on academicians and on Alsace-Lorraine. Short commissioned interventions rather than independent books; the collected bibliography preserves them as separate acts in print. [BC]
- **1899:** Response to the enquiry *Opinions singulières et curieuses touchant le sieur Hamlet*. A commissioned critical response and public position, not a freestanding authored volume. [BC]
- **1906:** Detailed plan and dictated materials for *La Dragonne*. A project-document intended to permit continuation by Rachilde or Charlotte Jarry; it is not identical with either the surviving fragments or the posthumous 1943 book. [LD]
