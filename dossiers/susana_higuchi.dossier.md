# Susana Shizuko Higuchi Miyagawa: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary conventions

Susana Higuchi’s public record is unusually uneven. Her congressional service, electoral exclusion, divorce litigation, allegations about Japanese donations, and testimony concerning detention and torture are documented in official records. Her childhood, private business affairs, medical history, and later family reconciliation are known mainly through interviews, profiles, and books about the Fujimori family.

Labels used below:

- **Documented fact:** established by an official record, judgment, legislative proceeding, or contemporaneous document.
- **Reported fact:** consistently reported by identifiable sources but not independently established here by a primary record.
- **Allegation:** an accusation attributed to its maker; not treated as proven.
- **Investigative finding:** conclusion of an official inquiry, distinguishable from a criminal conviction.
- **Court finding:** conclusion in a judgment.
- **Disputed claim:** a claim for which materially different accounts exist.
- **Rumor or myth:** a circulating story lacking sufficient documentary support.

This research was completed through September 3, 2026. Searches included Spanish- and English-language variants of Higuchi’s full name and a dedicated adversarial pass using terms including *controversia, escándalo, fraude, acusaciones, demanda, tortura, mentira, conspiracy,* and *criticism*, including material published or recirculated in 2024–2026.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full name:** Susana Shizuko Higuchi Miyagawa.
- **Common public name:** Susana Higuchi.
- **Married forms in official records:** Susana Higuchi Miyagawa de Fujimori; Susana Higuchi de Fujimori.
- **Japanese given name:** Shizuko. A biographical account says it was understood within the family as meaning “tranquility.”
- **Birth:** April 26, 1950, La Victoria district, Lima, Peru.
- **Death:** December 8, 2021, Lima, Peru, aged 71.
- **Nationality:** Peruvian.
- **Ancestry:** Japanese-Peruvian, reportedly the daughter of immigrants from Fukuoka Prefecture.
- **Professions:** civil engineer, construction entrepreneur, educational entrepreneur, politician.
- **Principal public offices:**
  - First lady of Peru, July 28, 1990–August 1994.
  - Member of the unicameral Congress, July 26, 2000–July 26, 2001.
  - Member of Congress for Lima, July 26, 2001–July 26, 2006.
- **Principal political affiliations:** Movimiento Armonía Siglo XXI–FREMPOL; Frente Independiente Moralizador (FIM); subsequently an independent parliamentarian.
- **Religion:** No detailed personal theological record was located. Her 2001 congressional oath invoked God, and her family described her final hours in Catholic language, entrusting her to the Virgin of the Immaculate Conception. Her wake was held at a Catholic parish.
- **Burial:** Campo Fe cemetery, Huachipa, Lima, December 9, 2021.

No documentary evidence was found of a pseudonym, formal noble title, military rank, patents, or personally authored book.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and siblings

**Reported fact:** Higuchi was the youngest of four children of Tomás Higuchi and Seki Miyagawa. A detailed family reconstruction quoted from Luis Jochamowitz’s *Ciudadano Fujimori* gives her father’s Japanese name as **Koshio Higuchi**, saying he later used Tomás in Peru. Her mother was reportedly a nurse.

The family reportedly emigrated from **Fukuoka Prefecture**, on Kyushu, and became commercially successful in Lima. Jochamowitz describes Tomás/Koshio as belonging to a merchant background and says the Higuchi and Fujimori parents knew one another by the 1950s because they operated in the same line of business. The names and life histories of Susana’s three siblings are not adequately reconstructed in the accessible sources.

### Marriage

Higuchi married Alberto Kenya Fujimori Fujimori in 1974. Two dates appear in the record:

- **July 17, 1974:** the civil marriage date stated in the published divorce judgment.
- **July 25, 1974:** the Catholic ceremony at the Capilla de la Virgen de la O, adjoining Lima’s Church of San Pedro, described by Jochamowitz.

These are compatible rather than contradictory: the first was the civil registration, the second the religious wedding.

### Children

The marriage produced four children:

1. **Keiko Sofía Fujimori Higuchi**, born May 25, 1975.
2. **Hiro Alberto Fujimori Higuchi**, born in December 1976.
3. **Sachi Marcela—or, in the divorce judgment, Sachi Mariela—Fujimori Higuchi**, born in March 1979.
4. **Kenji Gerardo Fujimori Higuchi**, born May 19, 1980.

The spelling variation concerning Sachi’s second name appears in otherwise serious sources and should not be silently resolved.

### Family rupture

From 1992 onward Higuchi’s accusations against her husband’s siblings and administration produced an escalating family rupture. By August 1994 she and Alberto Fujimori were publicly separated. Keiko, then nineteen, replaced her as presidential hostess or first lady.

During the divorce case, the children’s residence and custody became contested. The published judgment records that Keiko and Hiro were already adults, while Sachi and Kenji were minors. The minors were heard by the court. Contemporary reporting said they were living principally with their father in Government Palace while maintaining contact with their mother.

The children publicly supported their father during the conflict. Materials later submitted in opposition to Fujimori’s extradition attributed psychological instability or unreliable memory to Higuchi. These characterizations became a lasting controversy because they were used to contest her torture allegations.

### Reconciliation

**Reported fact:** Higuchi later rebuilt relations with her children, particularly Keiko. By 2011 mother and daughter appeared together publicly and described themselves as having remained close despite “bitter” periods. Higuchi discreetly assisted Keiko’s 2016 campaign, appeared in campaign activities, and defended her after Keiko’s 2018 detention. In 2021 she again appeared with Kenji in support of Keiko’s presidential candidacy.

No comparably clear documentary account establishes a reconciliation between Higuchi and Alberto Fujimori. A judicial filing in 2020 identified a rural property at Sayán belonging to Higuchi as a proposed residence for the imprisoned former president, but that fact alone does not prove personal reconciliation.

## Childhood and Early Life

Higuchi was born in La Victoria, a dense commercial district of Lima. Public profiles portray the Higuchi household as disciplined, economically comfortable, and strongly marked by Japanese immigrant customs.

There is a conflict over her school:

- One secondary account says she completed school at fifteen at the state school **Señora de la Asunción**.
- A biographical bulletin published through the Peruvian Congress and an extract from *Ciudadano Fujimori* identify the **Gran Unidad Escolar Mercedes Cabello de Carbonera** as her primary and secondary school.

The latter is better supported and is therefore the strongest available identification, though the inconsistency remains unresolved.

Profiles describe her as academically gifted, reserved, and especially proficient in mathematics. “La niña tranquila”—“the quiet girl”—became a retrospective journalistic characterization connected both to her name Shizuko and to her reserved public manner.

No primary school registers, childhood correspondence, or detailed oral history from her parents was located.

---

## Education and Formation

After finishing secondary school at approximately fifteen, Higuchi entered the **Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería (UNI)** in Lima.

**Documented/officially reported facts:**

- She studied civil engineering.
- She graduated with the professional title of civil engineer.
- She later specialized in hydraulics.
- In a 2005 congressional debate she described herself as a licensed and dues-current civil engineer, while candidly saying that six years in politics had left her professionally out of date.

No reliable dates for matriculation or graduation were found. No thesis title, academic transcript, named university mentor, or postgraduate credential was located.

Her formation combined engineering and practical entrepreneurship. Accounts of her early career emphasize construction-site management, real-estate development, and mathematics education rather than salaried engineering employment.

## Meeting Alberto Fujimori and Family Enterprises

Several romanticized versions circulated about the couple’s meeting. One said Fujimori suffered a flat tire and entered a Higuchi family establishment on Avenida Grau; another said the encounter was accidental because they lived opposite one another.

Jochamowitz’s reconstruction gives a less accidental background: their parents already knew one another commercially. Alberto reportedly began bringing Susana mathematical problems. Higuchi later recalled that she had not initially known he was a professor and that “between numbers” they became acquainted.

The relationship was frequently described afterward as “mathematical”: each valued the other’s intelligence and practical abilities. They married when Higuchi was twenty-four and Fujimori was thirty-five or thirty-six.

### Construcciones Fuyi/Fuji

Sources vary between **Construcciones FUYI**, **Fuyi**, and **Construcciones Fuji**. The business reportedly built and sold residential properties. Higuchi managed sites directly, rising early, wearing jeans and work boots, and supervising laborers. Fujimori also became involved in construction; Jochamowitz describes it as the couple’s principal profitable activity for roughly a decade.

The company name’s exact legal spelling, incorporation date, ownership percentages, tax filings, and eventual dissolution were not recovered.

### Grupo Matemático Wisconsin

Higuchi also established or co-established **Grupo Matemático Wisconsin**, a pre-university mathematics academy in San Borja. Its name, corporate form, enrollment, finances, and dates of operation require further archival reconstruction.

### Wealth and property

The Higuchi family was reportedly wealthier than the Fujimori family when the marriage occurred. The couple accumulated construction and educational enterprises, but no complete audited history of their net worth is publicly available.

The divorce proceeding referred to liquidation of marital community property and to an earlier change in matrimonial property regime. The accessible judgment does not provide a complete asset schedule.

A 2020 habeas-corpus filing by Fujimori’s children described the **Pampa Bonita estate in the Santa Rosa irrigation area, Sayán Province**, as Higuchi’s property. This is documentary evidence of at least one later-life rural real-estate holding, not a comprehensive estate inventory.

---

## Early Political Involvement and the 1990 Campaign

Higuchi had no known independent political career before Alberto Fujimori’s 1990 presidential candidacy. Accounts differ on how early she knew of his plans; later authors say he disclosed them to her only at a late stage.

She nevertheless became operationally important to the campaign:

- family businesses and resources reportedly supported campaign activity;
- she appeared beside Fujimori and helped project an image of an industrious, technically trained immigrant-descended family;
- the couple’s modest-business narrative contrasted with the better-financed campaign of Mario Vargas Llosa.

No complete campaign-finance accounting permits a precise valuation of Higuchi’s contribution. Claims that Fuyi directly financed parts of the campaign appear in secondary accounts but were not verified against company ledgers.

Fujimori defeated Vargas Llosa in the June 1990 runoff and took office on July 28.

## First Lady of Peru, 1990–1994

As first lady, Higuchi assumed the presidency of the **Fundación por los Niños del Perú**, the customary social-welfare role attached to the presidential spouse. She participated in charitable distribution and received or supervised foreign donations.

Her public role developed during severe economic crisis, internal armed conflict, and Fujimori’s consolidation of executive power. She eventually criticized what she regarded as the administration’s lack of concern for the poor.

### Japanese donations scandal

#### Initial accusation

**Documented allegation:** On March 24, 1992, Higuchi publicly accused relatives of Alberto Fujimori—especially his sister **Rosa Fujimori de Aritomi** and sister-in-law **Clorinda Ebisue de Fujimori**, with other siblings later implicated—of diverting, selling, or improperly using clothing donated from Japan for poor Peruvian children.

A contemporaneous report records Higuchi saying that Ebisue trafficked in donated clothing. Higuchi’s later formulation was that the best goods were selected or sold and that inferior remnants reached intended beneficiaries.

#### Initial legal result

**Documented prosecutorial disposition:** In 1994 prosecutor Julia Eguía Dávalos archived Higuchi’s complaints, reporting that she found insufficient evidence or indication of the alleged crimes. This was not a judicial finding that diversion was impossible; it was a decision not to proceed on the evidence then accepted.

#### Later congressional investigation

After the Fujimori regime collapsed, a congressional investigation revisited Japanese cooperation and APENKAI, the **Asociación Perú no Shien no Kai**.

**Investigative finding:** The congressional report concluded that donations were managed personally and directly through members of the presidential family, with APENKAI facilities inside customs and Government Palace, and that goods were used politically under the cover of social assistance. It identified possible appropriation or irregular handling of clothing, instruments, vehicles, and money.

The report therefore provided institutional corroboration for the broader problem Higuchi had raised, although it did not convert every specific accusation she made in 1992 into a criminal conviction.

#### Bank-account accusation

In July 2001 Higuchi alleged that Alberto Fujimori held approximately **US$12.499 million** in a Tokyo account derived from donations for poor children. The claim was widely reported. It should be classified as an **allegation**: the sources consulted do not establish a final court judgment that identified such an account, balance, and provenance exactly as Higuchi described them.

### The April 1992 self-coup

On April 5, 1992, Fujimori dissolved Congress, intervened in the judiciary, and governed under emergency arrangements backed by the armed forces. Higuchi’s donations accusation had preceded the self-coup by less than two weeks.

Her later testimony connected the intensified restrictions and violence against her to the period immediately following both the donations complaint and the self-coup.

## Detention, Abuse, and Torture Allegations

This is the most contested part of Higuchi’s biography.

### Higuchi’s account

In sworn or formal testimony before congressional investigators, Higuchi said:

- she was held for approximately four months at the Army headquarters known as the **Pentagonito**, within facilities of the Army Intelligence Service (SIE);
- personnel prevented her from leaving;
- masked or military-appearing men beat, pushed, harassed, and physically assaulted her;
- she was taken to a dark basement;
- she received electroshocks;
- she was injected or drugged after regaining consciousness;
- Fujimori once threatened or attempted to attack her with a machete;
- adulterated medicines were sent to her;
- she experienced electroshock torture on two occasions, one in 1992 and another in 2000;
- in 2002 reporting, she characterized the cumulative abuse as more than 500 torture episodes or sessions.

The “500” figure is difficult to reconcile with the more specific statement identifying two electroshock episodes. It may have referred to repeated acts of mistreatment rather than 500 distinct electroshock sessions. The underlying testimony should be read in full before treating the figures as interchangeable.

### Corroborating evidence

**Reported corroboration:** Former Army intelligence agent **Leonor La Rosa Bustamante** said Higuchi had been held in an SIE basement in 1992.

**Investigative finding:** A Peruvian congressional commission found evidence that SIE basements were used as unauthorized detention, interrogation, and torture sites. Its report treated Higuchi’s case as a presumptive attempted uxorcide, kidnapping, and torture matter requiring accountability. Congress approved a report indicating that Fujimori may have violated her human rights and may have been implicated in an attempt on her life.

The report also stated that Higuchi informed Fujimori of her kidnapping and injuries and that he replied that she had been dreaming rather than ordering an investigation.

### Denial and moxibustion explanation

**Disputed claim:** Alberto Fujimori denied that Higuchi was tortured. After she displayed scars on her neck and back in 2002, he said they resulted from **moxibustion**, an East Asian heat treatment, undertaken to address back pain and help her stop smoking.

The existence of moxibustion as a therapy is not disputed; whether it caused the particular marks does not resolve the separate allegations of detention, beating, drugging, or electroshock.

### Judicial status

The congressional findings were not themselves criminal convictions. A 2007 Chilean extradition ruling considered Higuchi’s declaration among the evidence concerning alleged kidnapping and torture but did not produce a final Peruvian conviction of Fujimori specifically for torturing her.

Later procedural reporting indicated disagreement over whether the criminal investigation had been finally archived or remained legally capable of continuation. The materials consulted do not establish a final conviction of Fujimori, Montesinos, or named SIE personnel for the acts Higuchi alleged.

The correct evidentiary description is therefore:

- Higuchi’s detention and torture narrative: **formal allegation/testimony**.
- Use of SIE basements for illegal detention and torture under the regime: **officially investigated and documented institutional practice**.
- La Rosa’s statement about Higuchi’s presence: **reported corroborating testimony**.
- Parliamentary attribution of responsibility: **investigative finding**.
- Individual criminal responsibility for the alleged torture of Higuchi: **not established by a final conviction located in this research**.

## Break with Fujimori and Removal as First Lady

By 1994 the marital and political conflict was public. Higuchi called her husband a tyrant and accused his government of corruption and abuse. She also complained that Government Palace personnel restricted her movement and access to food.

A journalist later described an unreleased 1994 videotaped interview in which Higuchi allegedly said Palace authorities would not feed her or permit her to leave to purchase food. Because the recording has not been publicly examined, this remains a **reported account of an unreleased source**.

On August 23–24, 1994, Fujimori formally removed or ceased recognizing Higuchi as first lady. Keiko assumed the ceremonial role. Since “first lady” was not a constitutional office with a statutory tenure, contemporary sources vary by one day in dating the transition.

Higuchi created **Movimiento Armonía Siglo XXI**, subsequently allied with FREMPOL, and announced her intention to oppose her husband electorally.

---

## The “Susana Law,” Electoral Exclusion, and Hunger Strike

Congress adopted a prohibition on close relatives of a sitting president becoming candidates. The measure was widely nicknamed the **“Ley Susana”** because it obstructed Higuchi’s prospective candidacy.

### Presidential attempt

In October 1994 electoral authorities rejected Higuchi’s effort to mount a presidential candidacy. Contemporary reporting cited failure to satisfy signature or registration requirements; Higuchi alleged that Fujimori controlled the exclusion.

### Congressional list

Armonía-FREMPOL then attempted to register a list for the April 1995 congressional election. The JNE rejected it on procedural grounds, including duplication of seven candidates and failure to present a complete 120-person list within the required time.

Higuchi began a hunger strike. She was hospitalized on January 19, 1995 with tachycardia and ended the strike shortly afterward.

### Inter-American Commission ruling

On February 2, 1995, Higuchi petitioned the **Inter-American Commission on Human Rights (IACHR/CIDH)**.

The Commission requested provisional registration of her list and, in Report 119/99 of October 6, 1999, concluded that Peru had violated her political rights under Article 23 of the American Convention. It found that the JNE had arbitrarily changed or inconsistently applied its interpretive criteria, leaving Higuchi without an effective opportunity to correct the list.

This is a **formal international human-rights finding**, not merely Higuchi’s allegation. The Commission recommended reparation and legal changes to prevent repetition.

## Divorce Proceedings

On December 16, 1994, Higuchi publicly announced that reconciliation was impossible and that she intended to divorce, saying in one widely reported formulation that she did not want to remain “married to corruption.”

Fujimori filed a civil action for divorce on the ground of **injuria grave**, relying on Higuchi’s televised and published accusations as attacks on his honor.

The published judgment records:

- separation in fact from August 1994;
- Fujimori’s demands concerning divorce, custody, maintenance, property liquidation, and suspension of cohabitation;
- Higuchi’s agreement to dissolution of the marriage and property community but insistence that the children’s wishes be heard;
- partial conciliation regarding maintenance and separate residence;
- judicial treatment of her public accusations as serious marital injury.

The divorce was approved on July 25, 1995, the twenty-first anniversary of the religious wedding. Some later documents and biographies give October 1996 as the final legal date, probably reflecting an appellate or registration stage. The safest reconstruction is: **judgment in 1995; some official or retrospective records treat final dissolution as occurring in 1996**.

The divorce judgment did not adjudicate whether Higuchi’s political corruption accusations were substantively true; it treated their public expression as injurious within marital law.

## Return to Business, 1996–1999

A congressional biographical bulletin says Higuchi devoted herself “entirely” to her companies from 1996 through 1999 while continuing to assist people who came to her home seeking social help.

Little independently audited information survives about:

- Construcciones Fuyi/Fuji’s projects or revenues;
- Grupo Matemático Wisconsin’s corporate status;
- employees, partners, or creditors;
- taxes and liabilities;
- post-divorce division of assets.

### Montesinos and an alleged tax/property matter

Secondary accounts say Vladimiro Montesinos had earlier provided legal assistance to Higuchi or the family in connection with construction paperwork or an alleged tax-evasion issue, and that relevant documents disappeared or accusations were withdrawn.

This is potentially important because it would place Montesinos inside the family’s network before or during Fujimori’s rise. However, no underlying case file or final judgment was located in this research. It must therefore remain a **reported and incompletely documented claim**, not a finding that Higuchi committed tax fraud.

No conviction of Higuchi for fraud, corruption, money laundering, or tax evasion was found.

## Congressional Career, 2000–2006

### Election in 2000

In 1999 the FIM, led by **Fernando Olivera**, invited Higuchi to stand for Congress. She was elected in the April 2000 election.

On September 14, 2000, Higuchi appeared with Olivera and **Luis Iberico** at the Hotel Bolívar press conference where the Kouri–Montesinos video was shown publicly. The recording depicted presidential adviser Vladimiro Montesinos paying opposition congressman Alberto Kouri to support the government. The disclosure precipitated the collapse of Fujimori’s third-term government.

Higuchi was present as a member of the opposition group releasing the evidence; available accounts do not identify her as the person who obtained or technically authenticated the tape.

The 2000 Congress ended early after the regime’s fall and the transition government’s call for new elections.

### Reelection in 2001

Higuchi was reelected with the FIM for the 2001–2006 term. At her swearing-in she declared:

> “Por Dios, por la Patria, por un Perú nuevo con democracia, principios y valores, por la defensa de la verdad, por mi familia, por los niños, por los ancianos y por todos los peruanos en general, sí, juro.”

### Committees and work

Her recorded assignments included work on:

- Economy and Financial Intelligence;
- National Defense, Internal Order, Intelligence, Alternative Development, and Anti-Drug matters;
- Constitution and Regulations;
- Budget and General Account;
- Education, Science and Technology;
- Women and Social Development;
- Consumer Protection.

She served as president of the commission concerned with alternative development, anti-drug policy, and money laundering during part of the term.

Records show her involvement in or support for proposals concerning:

- creation or implementation of Peru’s Financial Intelligence Unit;
- nutritional and consumer information;
- regulation of professional engineering practice;
- investigation of the Mesa Redonda fireworks disaster;
- investigation of privatization proceeds;
- women’s quotas in municipal, regional, and congressional lists;
- police health-service personnel;
- technical education and conversion pressures affecting institutes;
- oversight of drug-control and alternative-development agencies.

The available official record does not support describing her as a major legislative theorist or unusually prolific lawmaker. Her work was practical, committee-oriented, and frequently connected with engineering, education, financial intelligence, consumer regulation, and investigations.

### Departure from the FIM

On July 25, 2002, Higuchi resigned from the FIM. Contemporary accounts attribute the break to the party’s failure to support a meeting she organized concerning political freedom in Cuba. She continued as an independent parliamentarian and later appeared in parliamentary groupings listed as independent or GPDI.

### Health during the final term

By 2005–2006 Higuchi was often seen using a cane and obtained several medical leaves. The sources consulted do not establish a definitive diagnosis for that period. Retrospective speculation that these problems were necessarily the direct consequence of alleged torture is not medically proven in the available record.

She left Congress in July 2006 and did not regain elected office.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

Higuchi did not leave a systematic body of political theory. Her public positions can be reconstructed from actions and statements:

- early participation in a pragmatic family-business and technocratic milieu;
- support for Alberto Fujimori’s outsider campaign in 1990;
- increasing emphasis on charitable distribution and poverty;
- denunciation of family control over public or charitable resources;
- opposition to authoritarian control and electoral exclusion;
- alignment with the anticorruption FIM;
- support for financial-intelligence and anti-money-laundering institutions;
- advocacy of freedom in Cuba, contributing to her rupture with the FIM;
- later support for Keiko Fujimori, despite the latter’s leadership of the political movement built around Alberto Fujimori’s legacy.

Her later family loyalty did not amount to a public retraction of every earlier accusation. It did, however, produce an enduring interpretive dispute: critics called her appearances an electoral rehabilitation strategy, while Higuchi and her children presented them as family reconciliation.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Alberto Fujimori

Husband, business partner, political beneficiary, president during her first-lady period, later adversary and divorce litigant. Their association moved from mathematical courtship and joint enterprise to political estrangement, allegations of corruption and abuse, and litigation.

### Keiko Fujimori

Eldest daughter and successor as first lady in 1994. During the family rupture Keiko sided publicly with her father. Later she reconciled with Higuchi, who supported her campaigns in 2011, 2016, and 2021.

### Hiro, Sachi, and Kenji Fujimori

Children whose custody and loyalties formed part of the marital conflict. Kenji later appeared publicly with Higuchi in political and family contexts. Sachi and Kenji attended her burial; Hiro was reportedly absent because he was traveling.

### Fujimori siblings and in-laws

Rosa Fujimori de Aritomi, Santiago Fujimori, Juana Fujimori, Pedro Fujimori, and Clorinda Ebisue were among relatives associated in varying degrees with the Japanese-donations controversy. Higuchi’s 1992 accusations transformed these relationships into political antagonisms.

### Vladimiro Montesinos

Presidential adviser and intelligence power broker. Secondary accounts place him in contact with the family’s business/legal affairs before his political ascendancy. Higuchi later accused the intelligence apparatus under Fujimori and Montesinos of detaining and torturing her. She was part of the FIM delegation publicly releasing the Kouri–Montesinos video.

### Fernando Olivera and Luis Iberico

FIM allies who helped return Higuchi to elected politics. Together they participated in the September 2000 disclosure of the first publicly broadcast “vladivideo.” Higuchi later broke with Olivera’s party.

### Leonor La Rosa Bustamante

Former SIE agent and torture survivor who reportedly corroborated Higuchi’s presence in SIE detention facilities.

### Oscar Pedraza Sierra

Representative of Armonía-FREMPOL and co-petitioner or representative in the IACHR electoral-rights proceedings.

### Álex Gonzales

Spokesman for Armonía-FREMPOL during the 1995 contest; later active in other Peruvian parties and elected mayor of San Juan de Lurigancho.

### Mario Vargas Llosa

No direct personal meeting or collaboration with Higuchi was established. He was Alberto Fujimori’s 1990 electoral opponent, making him part of the surrounding political history rather than a documented member of Higuchi’s personal network.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, and Financial Questions

### 1. Donations accusations

- **Claim:** Fujimori relatives diverted or sold Japanese charitable donations.
- **Initial outcome:** archived by a prosecutor for insufficient evidence.
- **Later evidence:** congressional investigation documented opaque, personalized family management and possible appropriation or political use.
- **Final characterization:** core concerns received substantial investigative support, but individual allegations and dollar amounts were not all established by final convictions.

### 2. Alleged US$12.499 million Tokyo account

- **Maker:** Higuchi, 2001.
- **Status:** allegation.
- **Evidence located:** contemporary report of her statement.
- **Not located:** definitive bank record or final judgment confirming the exact balance and source.

### 3. Torture and attempted killing

- **Maker:** Higuchi in testimony.
- **Support:** physical marks displayed publicly; testimony of Leonor La Rosa; documented SIE torture facilities; parliamentary investigative findings.
- **Denial:** Fujimori denied torture and attributed scars to moxibustion.
- **Legal result:** no final conviction specifically for torturing Higuchi was located.
- **Status:** serious, officially investigated allegation with corroborating evidence, but unresolved at the level of individual criminal conviction.

### 4. Mental-health characterizations

Fujimori-aligned accounts and statements attributed to Higuchi’s children described her as psychologically unstable or suffering memory problems. These claims were used to oppose extradition and dispute her testimony.

No independent clinical record sufficient to establish a psychiatric diagnosis was found. The descriptions must be treated as **partisan or forensic claims**, not medical findings.

### 5. Alleged business or tax irregularities

Secondary accounts connect Montesinos with legal assistance in an alleged tax or property-file problem involving the family construction business. No indictment, judgment, or complete case file against Higuchi was found. It would be improper to state that she was proven to have evaded taxes.

### 6. Political reversal toward Keiko

Critics argued that Higuchi’s later campaign appearances helped sanitize the Fujimori family history and obscured her earlier allegations. Supporters and the family described the appearances as reconciliation and maternal support.

Both the earlier denunciations and later campaign support are documented. Claims that she was coerced, manipulated, or had knowingly fabricated the earlier story remain unproven unless tied to specific evidence.

### 7. No personal corruption conviction found

Despite extensive adversarial searching, no credible record was found that Higuchi herself was convicted of corruption, fraud, money laundering, violent crime, or misuse of congressional office.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### The flat-tire romance

- **Story:** Alberto Fujimori met Higuchi after a flat tire led him to her family establishment.
- **Circulation:** popular press and neighborhood retellings.
- **Evidence against simplification:** their parents already knew one another commercially; the couple’s mathematical exchanges appear more central.
- **Effect:** converted a socially and commercially connected courtship into a chance-romance origin story.
- **Label:** rumor/romanticized family mythology.

### “The marriage was only mathematics”

- **Story:** their union was a purely practical alliance of two calculating personalities.
- **Evidence:** mathematics genuinely played a role in their courtship and business partnership. Higuchi also described Fujimori in affectionate terms during happier years.
- **Effect:** became a metaphor for both their enterprises and later emotional distance.
- **Label:** symbolic narrative grounded in reported fact but reductive as literal biography.

### “She was mad”

- **Story:** Higuchi’s corruption and torture allegations resulted from mental illness, dreams, or unreliable memory.
- **Circulation:** Fujimori’s responses, family statements, and pro-Fujimori commentary.
- **Evidence:** no independent diagnostic documentation located; her electoral-rights claim was upheld internationally, and later official inquiries supported important portions of her broader account of irregular donations and SIE abuse.
- **Effect:** undermined her credibility and enabled her public isolation.
- **Label:** hostile political narrative/unverified medical claim.

### Moxibustion as a complete explanation

- **Story:** all marks presented as torture evidence came from voluntary traditional treatment.
- **Circulation:** Alberto Fujimori’s 2002 denial and sympathetic repetition.
- **Evidence:** possible as an explanation for some burn-like marks, but it does not independently explain detention, beatings, drugging, or witness testimony.
- **Effect:** became the principal counter-narrative to her torture allegations.
- **Label:** disputed explanation.

### “More than 500 electroshocks”

- **Story:** Higuchi was electroshocked more than 500 times.
- **Evidence problem:** reporting sometimes collapses “more than 500 acts/sessions of torture” into “500 electroshocks,” while Higuchi elsewhere specified electroshock on two occasions.
- **Effect:** used both to dramatize the accusation and to attack it as implausible.
- **Label:** disputed numerical formulation; should not be repeated without qualification.

### The first and pure source of antifujimorismo

- **Story:** Higuchi single-handedly founded or embodied all later opposition to Fujimori.
- **Evidence:** she was an unusually visible insider who denounced corruption early, but opposition also came from displaced legislators, judges, journalists, civil-society groups, human-rights organizations, and political parties.
- **Effect:** turned her into an origin symbol for later resistance.
- **Label:** commemorative political mythology built around documented opposition.

### Reconciliation as retraction

- **Story:** because Higuchi later supported Keiko, she implicitly admitted that her earlier allegations were false.
- **Evidence:** no comprehensive retraction was located. Maternal reconciliation and political support do not logically decide the truth of past claims.
- **Effect:** used in partisan argument both to rehabilitate the Fujimori family and to accuse it of exploiting her.
- **Label:** unsupported inference.

### Recent online afterlife, 2024–2026

Recent social-media discussions continue to invoke Higuchi mainly in disputes about Keiko Fujimori. Common claims include that Keiko “permitted” or personally supported her mother’s torture, that Higuchi definitively recanted, or that every allegation was judicially proven.

The record supports none of those categorical formulations. Keiko sided publicly with her father and disputed or minimized the allegations; Higuchi later reconciled with her. No evidence located establishes Keiko’s personal participation in torture, a blanket retraction by Higuchi, or a final conviction proving every detail of Higuchi’s account.

## Illness, Later Life, and Death

After leaving Congress in 2006, Higuchi largely withdrew from sustained public office. She appeared episodically in family and electoral contexts.

In October 2018 she spoke publicly after Keiko’s detention, saying it had hurt her profoundly. Her intervention demonstrated continued personal solidarity while not resolving the historical disputes surrounding the family.

In November 2021 her health deteriorated. Reports said she had been hospitalized from approximately November 9 and transferred to Oncosalud on November 23 for specialized treatment. Keiko asked supporters to join a virtual Mass.

Higuchi died in Lima on December 8, 2021, after what her family called a difficult struggle with cancer. Some news reports additionally referred to a pulmonary complication. The specific cancer type, stage, treatment history, and immediate medical cause of death were not publicly established in the sources consulted.

Her wake took place at the Parroquia Santa María Madre de la Iglesia in Jesús María. She was buried at Campo Fe, Huachipa, on December 9. Keiko, Kenji, and Sachi attended; Hiro was reportedly unable to attend because he was traveling.

No major independent national decoration or professional engineering prize was found.

Documented distinctions and offices include:

- first lady of Peru, 1990–1994;
- president of the Fundación por los Niños del Perú;
- elected member of Congress in 2000 and 2001;
- congressional committee leadership, including work in alternative development, drugs, and money laundering;

She is frequently identified as the first—or only, through 2021—former Peruvian first lady subsequently elected to public office by popular vote.

## Works by Susana Higuchi

Higuchi did not publish a known book, memoir, diary, technical monograph, patent, or sustained collection of essays.

Her reconstructable corpus consists principally of institutional and audiovisual records:

1. **Public denunciation concerning Japanese clothing donations**, radio and press interviews, March 1992. Survives through contemporaneous reporting and later congressional quotation.
2. **Statements concerning Government Palace confinement and corruption**, press and television interviews, 1994–1995.
3. **Petition in Susana Higuchi Miyagawa de Fujimori v. Peru**, filed with the IACHR on February 2, 1995. Legally prepared advocacy under her name rather than sole literary authorship.
4. **Congressional oath**, July 2001.
5. **Testimony concerning SIE detention, torture, and alleged attempted killing**, congressional proceedings, 2001–2002.
6. **Statements regarding Japanese donations and alleged Tokyo account**, 2001.
7. **Congressional speeches and interventions**, 2000–2006, preserved in the *Diario de los Debates* and committee minutes.
8. **Legislative proposals and co-sponsored bills**, 2000–2006, including financial intelligence, professional regulation, consumer/nutritional information, electoral quotas, education, and investigations. These are institutional, often collaboratively drafted documents.
9. **Interview with Jaime Bayly**, January 24, 2001, later republished online under the title “¿Dejarías entrar a Fujimori a tu casa?”
10. **Television interview concerning Keiko Fujimori’s detention**, *Panorama*, October 14, 2018.
11. **Campaign statements and appearances supporting Keiko Fujimori**, 2011, 2016, and 2021.

No evidence was found that works were ghostwritten and commercially published under Higuchi’s name. Her legal petitions, speeches, and bills likely involved lawyers or congressional staff, as is normal, and should not be treated as individually composed literary works without manuscript evidence.

## Books and Major Works About Susana Higuchi

There is no full, source-critical standalone biography located. The most useful works embed her life in studies of Alberto Fujimori, the regime, or presidential families:

1. **Luis Jochamowitz, *Ciudadano Fujimori: la construcción de un político***. Planeta, 1993; later editions. Important for family background, courtship, early enterprises, and the couple before power.
2. **Sally Bowen, *El expediente Fujimori: Perú y su presidente, 1990–2000***. Peru Monitor, 2000. Investigative political history with significant family and regime context.
3. **Carlos Iván Degregori, *La década de la antipolítica: auge y huida de Alberto Fujimori y Vladimiro Montesinos***. Instituto de Estudios Peruanos, 2000. Political and institutional context.
4. **Yusuke Murakami, *Perú en la era del Chino: la política no institucionalizada y el pueblo en busca de un salvador***. Instituto de Estudios Peruanos/Center for Integrated Area Studies, Kyoto University, 2007. Study of Fujimorismo and political support.
5. **Juan Gasparini, *Mujeres de dictadores***. Península, 2002. Contains a substantial treatment of Higuchi among spouses of authoritarian rulers. Investigative but interpretive and sometimes polemical.
6. **José Alejandro Godoy, *El último dictador: vida y gobierno de Alberto Fujimori***. Debate, 2021. Includes a chapter on “Susana y la familia,” business formation, marital deterioration, allegations, and later political significance.
7. **Fernando Rospigliosi and other investigators’ works on the Fujimori–Montesinos system.** Useful for SIE and regime context, although not biographies of Higuchi.
8. **Peruvian congressional investigative reports on Vladimiro Montesinos, human-rights cases, Japanese donations, and APENKAI**, 2001–2003. These are indispensable primary institutional sources rather than narrative books.
9. **IACHR Report No. 119/99, Case 11.428, *Susana Higuchi Miyagawa de Fujimori v. Peru***, October 6, 1999. The controlling documentary source on her electoral exclusion.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

Higuchi’s posthumous image contains several distinct, sometimes incompatible roles:

- successful Japanese-Peruvian engineer and construction entrepreneur;
- spouse who materially helped build the Fujimori family’s pre-presidential economic base;
- first lady and charity administrator;
- early internal whistleblower against the presidential family;
- alleged victim of state and domestic abuse;
- excluded political candidate vindicated by the IACHR;
- anticorruption opposition congresswoman;
- mother who later reconciled with and campaigned for Keiko Fujimori.

Her death renewed attention to the 1992 donation allegations and torture testimony. Opponents of Fujimorismo cite her as an insider witness to corruption and intelligence abuse. Fujimori supporters emphasize her later reconciliation, dispute torture claims, or present family conflict as private tragedy. Neither camp’s compressed version accounts for the whole record.

The strongest durable institutional legacy is the IACHR’s finding that Peru violated her political rights through arbitrary electoral administration. Her donation accusations also became an early warning of the informal family networks and lack of transparency later documented in the regime.

The absence of a full personal archive or memoir leaves central questions unresolved: what she knew about Fujimori’s pre-1990 political plans, the complete history of their businesses, exact circumstances of her alleged detention, medical corroboration of torture, and why she later chose public political support for Keiko without a comprehensive reckoning with the earlier conflict.

## Atlas Connections

Only connections supported by the supplied atlas roster are included. Mere contemporaneity has been excluded.

### Alberto Kenya Fujimori Inomoto

- **Nature:** husband; father of her four children; business partner; beneficiary of her 1990 campaign support; president during her tenure as first lady; later political adversary and divorce litigant.
- **Dates and places:** acquainted in Lima before 1974; civil marriage July 17, 1974 and Catholic wedding July 25, 1974; joint family and business life principally in Lima; Government Palace relationship 1990–1994; separation from August 1994; divorce judgment in 1995, with some sources giving final dissolution in 1996.
- **Evidentiary label:** **documented fact**.
- **Contested dimension:** Higuchi alleged that Fujimori tolerated, knew of, or ordered abuse and torture against her; Fujimori denied this. Parliamentary inquiries found grounds for responsibility, but no final conviction specifically for her torture was located.

### Keiko Sofía Fujimori Higuchi

- **Nature:** eldest daughter; succeeded Higuchi as first lady; sided with Alberto Fujimori during the family rupture; later reconciled with her mother and received her public campaign support.
- **Dates and places:** born in Lima in 1975; assumed first-lady functions in August 1994; public mother-daughter reconciliation visible by 2011; campaign association in Peru during 2011, 2016, and 2021; present at Higuchi’s death rites in Lima in December 2021.
- **Evidentiary label:** **documented and reported fact**.
- **Contested dimension:** claims that Keiko personally participated in or ordered her mother’s abuse are unsupported by the materials reviewed. Her public alignment with her father and later statements disputing or minimizing Higuchi’s account are documented political facts, not proof of operational participation.

### Susana Shizuko Higuchi Miyagawa

The subject herself appears in the supplied roster. This is an identity match, not an interpersonal atlas connection.

### Absence finding

No adequately documented meeting, correspondence, collaboration, kinship, patronage, rivalry, or acknowledged intellectual influence was found between Higuchi and any other roster member. Mario Vargas Llosa was a central opponent in Alberto Fujimori’s 1990 campaign but does not appear in the supplied roster under his name. Broader political contemporaneity has therefore not been converted into a connection.

## Chronology

- **April 26, 1950:** Born in La Victoria, Lima.
- **c. 1965:** Completes secondary education at approximately fifteen; strongest sources identify Mercedes Cabello de Carbonera.
- **Mid-to-late 1960s:** Enters the Universidad Nacional de Ingeniería.
- **Before 1974:** Studies civil engineering; develops relationship with Alberto Fujimori through family acquaintance and shared interest in mathematics.
- **July 17, 1974:** Civil marriage to Fujimori in Santiago de Surco.
- **July 25, 1974:** Catholic wedding at the Capilla de la Virgen de la O in Lima.
- **May 25, 1975:** Birth of Keiko.
- **December 1976:** Birth of Hiro.
- **March 1979:** Birth of Sachi.
- **May 19, 1980:** Birth of Kenji.
- **1970s–1980s:** Operates Construcciones Fuyi/Fuji and Grupo Matemático Wisconsin; specializes in hydraulics.
- **1989–1990:** Assists Fujimori’s presidential campaign.
- **June 1990:** Fujimori wins the presidential runoff.
- **July 28, 1990:** Higuchi becomes first lady and leads the Fundación por los Niños del Perú.
- **March 24, 1992:** Publicly accuses Fujimori relatives of diverting Japanese clothing donations.
- **April 5, 1992:** Fujimori’s self-coup.
- **1992:** According to Higuchi’s later testimony, confined and tortured at SIE/Pentagonito facilities.
- **1994:** Prosecutor archives the original donations complaints for insufficient evidence.
- **August 1994:** Public marital rupture; removed or displaced as first lady; Keiko succeeds her.
- **September 1994:** Announces/organizes Movimiento Armonía Siglo XXI.
- **October 1994:** Presidential candidacy attempt rejected.
- **December 16, 1994:** Announces intention to divorce.
- **January 1995:** Armonía-FREMPOL congressional list rejected; Higuchi begins hunger strike and is hospitalized with tachycardia.
- **February 2, 1995:** Petitions the IACHR.
- **July 25, 1995:** Divorce judgment approved; some later sources give 1996 as final dissolution.
- **1996–1999:** Returns principally to business and private social assistance.
- **October 6, 1999:** IACHR Report 119/99 finds Peru violated her political rights.
- **April 2000:** Elected to Congress with the FIM.
- **September 14, 2000:** Appears with Fernando Olivera and Luis Iberico at public release of the Kouri–Montesinos video.
- **2000:** Higuchi later alleged a second electroshock episode during this year.
- **2001:** Reelected to Congress for 2001–2006.
- **January 24, 2001:** Interviewed by Jaime Bayly.
- **July 2001:** Alleges existence of a US$12.499 million Tokyo account derived from donations.
- **2001–2002:** Gives detailed testimony about confinement, torture, and attempted killing.
- **February 2002:** Displays scars and says she suffered extensive torture; Fujimori issues moxibustion denial.
- **July 10, 2002:** Congress approves an investigative report concerning alleged abuses against her.
- **July 25, 2002:** Resigns from the FIM.
- **2002–2006:** Continues as independent congresswoman; active in financial intelligence, education, engineering, drugs, consumer affairs, and oversight.
- **2005–2006:** Uses a cane and takes medical leave.
- **July 26, 2006:** Leaves Congress.
- **2011:** Appears publicly with Keiko during the presidential campaign.
- **March 2016:** Campaigns discreetly for Keiko.
- **October 14, 2018:** Speaks publicly about the pain caused by Keiko’s detention.
- **2020:** Sayán property identified in a judicial filing as belonging to Higuchi.
- **2021:** Appears in support of Keiko’s third presidential campaign.
- **November 2021:** Hospitalized and transferred to specialized cancer care.
- **December 8, 2021:** Dies in Lima.
- **December 9, 2021:** Buried at Campo Fe cemetery, Huachipa.
- **2024–2026:** Her torture allegations and reconciliation continue to circulate in polarized political narratives surrounding Keiko Fujimori.
