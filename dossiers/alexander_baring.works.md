## Works by Alexander Baring

### Political and economic pamphlets

- **1808:** *An Inquiry into the Causes and Consequences of the Orders in Council; and an Examination of the Conduct of Great Britain towards the Neutral Commerce of America*. A political-economic pamphlet opposing British restrictions on neutral and American trade. Contemporary second editions and an American reprint are manifestations of the same obra, not additional works; the title is sometimes modernized as *Enquiry*, but the bibliographical record uses *Inquiry*. ([Goldsmiths’-Kress bibliography](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/1005000A.pdf); [Dictionary of National Biography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Baring,_Alexander))

- **1847:** *The Financial and Commercial Crisis Considered*. A forty-page intervention in the currency controversy, written against the restrictive effects Baring associated with the Bank Charter Act of 1844. Four impressions or editions appeared in 1847, followed by an 1848 German translation; these are manifestations of one pamphlet, not separate obras. ([Goldsmiths’-Kress bibliography](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/1005000A.pdf); [digitized first edition](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_financial_and_commercial_crisis_considered_(IA_financialcommerc00ashbuoft).pdf); [Dictionary of National Biography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography,_1885-1900/Baring,_Alexander))

### Speeches issued as independent texts

- **1823:** *Mr. Alexander Baring’s Speech in the House of Commons, on the 15th Day of May, 1823, on Mr. Buxton’s Motion for a Resolution Declaratory of Slavery in the British Colonies Being Contrary to the English Constitution and to Christianity*. A separately printed parliamentary speech opposing Buxton’s antislavery motion and immediate emancipation. It is simultaneously an oration, a printed pamphlet, and a political act; modern scholarship emphasizes that its argument treated enslaved women instrumentally, so it cannot be described neutrally as merely a contribution to an abstract policy debate. ([bibliographical record](https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL2606728A/Ashburton_Alexander_Baring_1st_baron); [Thomas, “Inheriting Empire, Wealth, and Art”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14780038.2024.2426251))

- **1838:** *Speech of the Right Honourable Lord Ashburton, (in the House of Lords) on the Second Reading of the Canada Government Bill: Friday, February 2, 1838*. A separately published parliamentary oration about governing Lower Canada after the rebellions of 1837. The pamphlet’s printed date is 2 February; a further intervention by Ashburton in the same debate on 8 February belongs to the parliamentary record and should not be silently conflated with this independently issued text. ([bibliographical record](https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL2606728A/Ashburton_Alexander_Baring_1st_baron); [Hansard, 2 February 1838](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Lords/1838-02-02/debates/1a3717bb-8152-47fd-9bc6-87c18e4775b9/LordsChamber); [Hansard, 8 February 1838](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/lords/1838/feb/08/affairs-of-canada))

### Parliamentary speech as a body of work

- **1806–1848:** *Parliamentary speeches and interventions*. Baring produced a large, dispersed body of spoken political work in the Commons and Lords, particularly on trade, currency, banking, colonial government, and slavery; only some speeches acquired the more bounded status of separately printed works. The body resists enumeration as individual obras because Hansard records interventions, replies, and reported speech rather than an authorially constituted collected works, and no scholarly complete edition of Baring’s speeches has been identified. ([History of Parliament](https://www.historyofparliamentonline.org/volume/1820-1832/member/baring-alexander-1773-1848); [Historic Hansard person index](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/people/hon-alexander-baring/1847))

### Diplomatic instruments and operative texts

- **28 July 1842:** *[Note from Lord Ashburton to Daniel Webster concerning the Caroline affair]*. An officially exchanged diplomatic note without a separate authorial title, recording Britain’s explanation of the cross-border destruction of the *Caroline* and helping close the dispute. It is best classified as an operative diplomatic manifestation rather than a literary work: it altered relations between governments, and the documentary editor reports that Baring revised its initial form, preserving a genuine textual-variant problem. ([Avalon Project documentary edition](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/br-1842d.asp))

- **9 August 1842:** *A Treaty to Settle and Define the Boundaries between the Territories of the United States and the Possessions of Her Britannic Majesty in North America: For the Final Suppression of the African Slave Trade: and For the Giving Up of Criminals Fugitive from Justice, in Certain Cases* (the Webster–Ashburton Treaty). A jointly negotiated and signed diplomatic instrument made by Baring as British plenipotentiary with Daniel Webster, not a solely authored book. It is a manifesto in the strictly performative sense—a text that established boundaries and obligations—and a protocol in the broader diplomatic sense, although not an instructional manual; attribution to Baring alone would therefore be misleading. ([Avalon Project treaty edition](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/br-1842.asp); [U.S. Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1830-1860/webster-treaty))

- **9 August 1842:** *[Two notes from Lord Ashburton to Daniel Webster accompanying the Webster–Ashburton Treaty]*. Two untitled diplomatic notes printed with the treaty and explicitly identified by its documentary editor as Ashburton’s communications. They are ancillary operative texts rather than independent books, and their bibliographical identity remains attached to the treaty settlement rather than to stable authorial titles. ([Avalon Project treaty edition](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/br-1842.asp))

### Correspondence and business papers

- **c. 1790s–1848:** *Letters, memoranda, and business papers*. A substantial manuscript body survives in the Baring Archive and in scattered institutional collections, including family, professional, and diplomatic correspondence. This material resists treatment as a finite list of titled works: it is an archival residue of banking, political, familial, and diplomatic activity, not an authorially completed oeuvre, and the available finding aids do not establish that it is complete. ([Royal Economic Society, *Economists’ Papers*](https://res.org.uk/economists-papers/); [Baring Archive, Series HC1](https://baringarchive.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/the_baring_archive_hc1.pdf); [National Library of Scotland manuscript description](https://manuscripts.nls.uk/repositories/2/resources/2190/collection_organization); [Dartmouth Libraries archival records](https://archives-manuscripts.dartmouth.edu/agents/people/10203))
