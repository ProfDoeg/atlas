## Works by Renée Jeanne Falconetti

### Embodied theatrical creations

- **1913:** *Le Mariage de Victorine*. Conservatoire competition performance: Falconetti presented a scene from George Sand’s play and received a second prize. This was an examination piece rather than a public production, so it resists classification as an independent work. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1914:** *Esther*. Conservatoire competition performance of a scene from Racine’s tragedy, again awarded a second prize; an ephemeral student creation rather than authorship of the text. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1914–1915:** *Swiss wartime tour*. Performances given with Conservatoire students as wartime cultural propaganda; surviving accounts do not supply a dependable complete repertory, so this project cannot responsibly be divided into titled works. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1915:** *La Demande en mariage*. Falconetti’s documented professional stage debut, in Chekhov’s one-act comedy at the Odéon on 29 May. The made thing attributable to her is the performance, not Chekhov’s play. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *Charles II et Buckingham*. Odéon performance as Sarah Duncan. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *Gretchen*. Odéon performance; the surviving career catalogues do not consistently identify her role. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *Le Barbier de Séville*. Odéon performance as Rosine, distinct from her later Comédie-Française production of the same play. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *La Bonne Mère*. Odéon performance; an embodied interpretation whose exact role is not securely stated in the consulted repertories. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *Les Grandes Mademoiselles*. Odéon performance; role not securely identified. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *L’Arlésienne*. Odéon performance as Vivette. Some concise reference works call this her theatrical debut, whereas the fuller chronology places her professional debut in *La Demande en mariage* in 1915. [Treccani; Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1916:** *Le Carnaval des enfants*. Odéon performance as Hélène in Saint-Georges de Bouhélier’s play. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1917:** *Les Deux Orphelines*. Odéon performance as Louise, the blind orphan. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1917:** *Les Bouffons*. Odéon performance as Solange. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1917:** *La Chercheuse d’esprit*. Odéon performance; role not securely identified. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1917:** *Le Joli Rôle*. Odéon performance; role not securely identified. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1917:** *La Souris*. Odéon performance; role not securely identified. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1917:** *L’Affaire des poisons*. Odéon performance in Victorien Sardou’s drama; the consulted sources do not agree sufficiently to name her role. [Foucart; BnF]
- **1918:** *Le Mariage de Victorine*. Odéon performance, distinct from Falconetti’s 1913 Conservatoire examination scene. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1918:** *Annette et Lubin*. Odéon performance; role not securely identified. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1919:** *La Vie d’une femme*. Odéon performance in Saint-Georges de Bouhélier’s play. Sources that call this her Odéon “debut” conflict with the documented 1915–18 Odéon appearances and appear to mean a breakthrough or return rather than a literal first appearance. [Treccani; Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1919:** *Aux jardins de Murcie*. Performance at the Théâtre Antoine, first presented there on 15 October; Falconetti revived it with her own company in 1930. [BnF]
- **1920:** *La Captive*. Performance at the Théâtre Antoine in Firmin Gémier’s production; omitted from several shorter Falconetti lists but retained in the biographical repertory. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1920:** *Une faible femme*. Performance in Jacques Deval’s comedy at the Théâtre Fémina, first presented on 12 May. [BnF]
- **1920:** *Le Simoun*. Performance in Henri-René Lenormand’s play, staged by Gaston Baty at the Comédie Montaigne. [Treccani; Foucart]
- **1921:** *Le Feu qui reprend mal*. Performance at the Théâtre Antoine in Alexandre Arquillière’s production. [Foucart]
- **1921:** *Le Comédien*. Performance in Sacha Guitry’s play at the Théâtre Édouard-VII; contemporary reception reportedly treated her part as slight. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1922:** *La Chair humaine*. Performance as Jeanne Boulard in Henry Bataille’s play at the Théâtre du Vaudeville, first presented on 10 February. [BnF]
- **1922:** *L’Avocat*. Performance in Eugène Brieux’s comedy at the Théâtre du Vaudeville, staged by Victor Silvestre and first presented on 22 September. [BnF]
- **1923:** *Charly*. Title-role performance in the play by André Jager-Schmidt and Valentine Thomson-Jager-Schmidt, initially at the Théâtre Michel and later revived at the Théâtre de l’Étoile. [Hélène Falconetti; Foucart]
- **1923:** *La Fille perdue*. Stage performance recorded in the fuller biographical repertory but absent from many shorter chronologies; venue and role remain uncertain in the sources consulted. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1924:** *Le Bien-Aimé*. Performance in Jacques Deval’s play at the Théâtre de la Renaissance. [Foucart]
- **1924:** *La Féerie amoureuse*. Performance in Saint-Georges de Bouhélier’s work at the Théâtre du Nouvel-Ambigu, opening on 7 April; the title also appears as *La Féérie amoureuse*. [BnF; Foucart]
- **1924:** *Le Barbier de Séville*. Comédie-Française performance as Rosine, and therefore a new embodied creation rather than a repetition of the 1916 Odéon event. [Comédie-Française repertory; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1924:** *Le Cocu imaginaire*. Comédie-Française performance in Molière’s one-act comedy; role not securely established in the consulted summary repertories. [Comédie-Française repertory; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1924:** *Amoureuse*. Comédie-Française performance in Georges de Porto-Riche’s play. [Comédie-Française repertory; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1925:** *Bettine*. Comédie-Française performance in Alfred de Musset’s comedy. [Treccani; Comédie-Française repertory]
- **1925:** *Charly*. Revival of Falconetti’s title-role performance at the Théâtre de l’Étoile. It is a new manifestation of the 1923 theatrical creation rather than a newly authored work. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1925:** *Simili*. Performance in Claude Roger-Marx’s play, staged by Edmond Roze at the Théâtre du Vieux-Colombier. [Foucart]
- **1926:** *La Garçonne*. Performance as Monique Lerbier in the theatrical adaptation of Victor Margueritte’s novel at the Théâtre de Paris, opening on 12 July. This was the performance through which Dreyer encountered Falconetti. [Hélène Falconetti; *L’Officiel de la couture*, no. 61]
- **1927:** *Lorenzaccio*. Performance as Lorenzo at Monte Carlo and the Théâtre de la Madeleine. The frequently printed date 1928 or 1932 describes later manifestations, not necessarily this first Falconetti interpretation. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1927:** *Le Miroir qui fait rire*. Performance with Harry Baur at the Théâtre des Capucines, documented on 1 April. [Foucart]
- **1927:** *Miche*. Stage performance listed in the fuller biographical chronology, but its venue, role and precise production status remain uncertain. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1928:** *La Dame aux camélias*. Performance as Marguerite Gautier at the Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt; revived in 1931. [Treccani; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1928:** *L’Enfant prodigue*. Pantomime performance in Michel Carré fils’s wordless work at the Théâtre Fémina. As a bodily and gestural creation, it particularly resists being treated as a “text” in the bibliographical sense. [Foucart]
- **1929:** *Ces dames aux chapeaux verts*. Performance at the Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt in the adaptation of Germaine Acremant’s novel. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1929:** *La Rouille*. Performance as Nina Verganska in the French adaptation of Vladimir Kirchon and Andrei Ouspenski’s Soviet drama, opening at Falconetti’s Théâtre de l’Avenue on 22 November. The BnF describes the production as a three-act, ten-tableau play; some accounts characterize Falconetti’s part as secondary despite her management of the theatre. [BnF]
- **1930:** *Phèdre*. Title-role performance in Racine’s tragedy at the Théâtre de l’Avenue. It belonged to Falconetti’s repertory experiment rather than constituting her authorship of the play. [Treccani; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1930:** *Les Caprices de Marianne*. Performance in Musset’s comedy at the Théâtre de l’Avenue; the BnF dates the production only to February. [BnF]
- **1930:** *Juliette ou la Clé des songes*. Falconetti created the title role in Georges Neveux’s first play at the Théâtre de l’Avenue, staged by Alberto Cavalcanti in March. Some repertories place it in 1929, apparently reflecting preparation or the theatrical season rather than the documented première. [Artcena; BnF]
- **1930:** *Aux jardins de Murcie*. Revival at the Théâtre de l’Avenue of Falconetti’s 1919 performance. This is a manifestation of an earlier interpretation and part of her company’s final season. [BnF; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1931:** *La Dame aux camélias*. Revival of Falconetti’s Marguerite Gautier at the Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1931:** *La Vie d’une femme*. Odéon revival of the work she had performed in 1919. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1932:** *Lorenzaccio*. Odéon manifestation of Falconetti’s cross-gender Lorenzo, developed from her earlier interpretation of the role. [Treccani; Foucart]
- **1934:** *Jeanne d’Arc*. Title-role performance at the Odéon in Saint-Georges de Bouhélier’s dramatic work. Though it repeats the historical identity of her film role, it is a separate theatrical creation. [Foucart]
- **1935:** *La Créature*. Performance in Ferdinand Bruckner’s play, staged by Georges Pitoëff at the Théâtre des Mathurins and opening on 30 April. [BnF]
- **1935:** *La guerre de Troie n’aura pas lieu*. Falconetti created Andromaque in Jean Giraudoux’s play, staged by Louis Jouvet at the Théâtre de l’Athénée. Some derivative repertories incorrectly place the production at the Mathurins. [Treccani; Hélène Falconetti]
- **1942–1946:** *L’Échange*. Buenos Aires production of Paul Claudel’s play, both staged and performed by Falconetti. The sources consulted do not establish a secure première date, venue, role or complete production record; it therefore survives more as an attested project than a fully reconstructable work. [Hélène Falconetti; Foucart]
- **1942–1946:** *Les Monstres sacrés*. Buenos Aires production of Jean Cocteau’s play, staged and performed by Falconetti. Its exact date, venue and cast are likewise not securely recoverable from the standard summary sources. [Hélène Falconetti; Foucart]

### Varieté and composite performances

- **1929:** *Chanson des bois, des rues et d’ailleurs*. A self-created Théâtre Fémina varieté in which Falconetti sang, danced and acted. Neither simply play nor concert, it was a small Gesamtkunstwerk organized around her performing body; sources differ between 1928 and spring 1929, so the date remains unsettled. [Hélène Falconetti; Ford]
- **1930:** *Divertissement / Avenue 30*. Composite Théâtre de l’Avenue programme of sketches, music and Falconetti’s performance, presented on 31 May, with music by Maurice Jaubert and material by Paul Colline, Simon Gantillon and Marcel Espiau. The variant titles may name the programme and Jaubert’s musical contribution respectively rather than two separate works. [Jaubert catalogue; Hélène Falconetti]

### Theatre as project and obra

- **1929–1930:** *Falconetti’s Théâtre de l’Avenue*. Company, venue and socially educational theatrical project directed by Falconetti, with André Bloch-Desmorget and Pierre Lazareff among its administrators. Its productions included *La Rouille*, *Phèdre*, *Les Caprices de Marianne*, *Juliette ou la Clé des songes*, *Aux jardins de Murcie* and *Divertissement*; financially disastrous and short-lived, the undertaking is nevertheless an obra larger than any single performance. [BnF; Hélène Falconetti; Treccani]
- **1942–1946:** *Buenos Aires theatre company*. Falconetti’s final theatrical project, through which she mounted at least *L’Échange* and *Les Monstres sacrés*. The extant published record is too incomplete to establish whether these were its only productions, so the project must not be mistaken for a closed catalogue. [Hélène Falconetti; Foucart]

### Screen performances

- **1917:** *Le Clown*. A short silent film generally attributed to director-performer Maurice de Féraudy and sometimes said to include Falconetti as Alice Barnave. Its inclusion is disputed: several reference works accept it, while filmographies describing Falconetti as appearing in only two films omit it or treat the identification as unverified. [Treccani; Richard; Criterion press book]
- **1917:** *La Comtesse de Somerive*. Silent feature directed by Georges Denola and Jean Kemm, with Falconetti as Alix Valory. Some later accounts call *La Passion de Jeanne d’Arc* her first film and reduce this appearance to a test or confuse it with *Le Clown*, but contemporary film references and the Pathé tradition support the credit. [Richard; Fondation Jérôme Seydoux-Pathé; Treccani]
- **1928:** *La Passion de Jeanne d’Arc*. Falconetti’s screen creation of Jeanne in Carl Theodor Dreyer’s silent film, shot in 1927 and premiered in Copenhagen on 21 April 1928. The film is Dreyer’s obra, but Falconetti’s radically close-up-dependent performance is a distinct embodied creation within it and her final undisputed screen role. [Danish Film Institute; Criterion; Treccani]

### Posthumous manifestations of the film performance

- **1928:** *Original Danish première version of La Passion de Jeanne d’Arc*. The first public manifestation of Falconetti’s performance; French ecclesiastical and governmental censorship subsequently produced altered circulation versions. [Danish Film Institute; Janus Films]
- **1928–1929:** *Dreyer’s second version*. Reconstruction made by Dreyer from alternate takes after fire destroyed the original negative. Because different takes contain different acts by Falconetti, this is not merely another edition: it is an alternative surviving manifestation of her performance. [Danish Film Institute; Tybjerg]
- **1952:** *Lo Duca version*. Joseph-Marie Lo Duca’s shortened, rescored and retitled construction from material associated with the second negative. Dreyer rejected it; Falconetti did not make this version, although for decades it mediated what audiences believed her performance to be. [Tybjerg; Offscreen]
- **1981–1984:** *Dikemark/Oslo print and its restoration*. A 1928 print of Dreyer’s original version was discovered in 1981 at Dikemark Hospital in Norway and identified after reaching the Norwegian Film Institute; it returned to circulation in 1984. This “resurrection” is not a new Falconetti work but the recovery of the earliest manifestation of one, extending the historical journey of her made performance beyond her death. [Janus Films; Norwegian Film Institute; Tybjerg]
