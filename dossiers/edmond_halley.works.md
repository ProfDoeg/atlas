## Works by Edmond Halley

### Astronomical catalogues, tables, and observing programmes

- **1676:** *Methodus directa & geometrica, cujus ope investigantur aphelia, eccentricitates, proportionesque orbium planetarum primariorum*. A mathematical paper giving a geometrical method for determining planetary aphelia, eccentricities, and orbital proportions.
- **1677–1678:** *Observations of the Southern Stars*. The observational project undertaken on St Helena; bad weather limited it, but it produced measurements of 341 stars and the first telescopically observed catalogue of the southern sky.
- **1678:** *Catalogus stellarum australium, sive supplementum catalogi Tychonici*. A printed star catalogue derived from the St Helena project. Sources variously date its appearance to late 1678 or 1679.
- **1678:** *Planisphere of the Southern Hemisphere*. A celestial map presented, with the catalogue, to Charles II and the Royal Society; it is a visual and computational obra rather than merely an illustration to the book.
- **1678:** *Observations on the Transit of Mercury of 7 November 1677*. An astronomical report based on Halley’s St Helena observation, subsequently printed in the *Philosophical Transactions*.
- **1680–1681:** *Observations of the Great Comet of 1680*. A body of observations made during Halley’s continental journey, including work with Giovanni Domenico Cassini in Paris. It resists treatment as a single fixed text: it survives through reports, calculations, and correspondence.
- **1682:** *Observations of the Comet of 1682*. An observational and computational dossier concerning the comet later named for Halley; it became evidence for the periodicity argument published in 1705.
- **1686:** *Ephemeris ad annum a nativitate Domini 1686*. An annual computational almanac for the longitude of London.
- **1687:** *Ephemeris ad annum a nativitate Domini 1687*. An annual computational almanac.
- **1688:** *Ephemeris ad annum a nativitate Domini 1688*. An annual computational almanac.
- **1705:** *Astronomiae cometicae synopsis*. A Latin synoptic catalogue and theory of cometary orbits, separately issued and also printed in the *Philosophical Transactions*. It identified the comets of 1531, 1607, and 1682 as successive appearances of one body and made the projective claim that it would return.
- **1705–1706:** *A Synopsis of the Astronomy of Comets*. The English manifestation of the cometary synopsis; later appearances in collections are editions, not new works.
- **1715:** *A Short History of the Several New-Stars That Have Appear’d within These 150 Years*. A historical catalogue and interpretive paper on novae and variable stars.
- **1715:** *An Account of Several Nebulae or Lucid Spots Like Clouds, Lately Discovered among the Fixt Stars by Help of the Telescope*. A descriptive catalogue of six nebulae, combining earlier reports with Halley’s own observations.
- **1716:** *Methodus singularis qua Solis parallaxis sive distantia a Terra, ope Veneris intra Solem conspiciendae, tuto determinari poterit*. An observing protocol for determining the solar parallax by timing a transit of Venus from widely separated terrestrial stations; it was designed for astronomers living after Halley’s death.
- **1717–1718:** *Considerations on the Change of the Latitudes of Some of the Principal Fixt Stars*. A comparative study of ancient and modern stellar positions. It is often credited with announcing stellar proper motion, although later scholarship disputes whether Halley’s evidence actually established that phenomenon.
- **1720:** *Of the Infinity of the Sphere of Fix’d Stars*. A short cosmological paper arguing that the stellar system has no demonstrable outer limit.
- **1720:** *Of the Number, Order, and Light of the Fix’d Stars*. A companion paper estimating the distribution and visibility of stars.
- **1720–1739:** *Greenwich lunar-observation programme*. An eighteen-year project intended to follow a complete saros and improve lunar tables for finding longitude. It is not one authored text but a sustained made body of observations; Halley completed the programme, though the precision and usefulness of the surviving observations have been criticized.
- **1731:** *A Proposal of a Method for Finding the Longitude at Sea within a Degree, or Twenty Leagues*. A navigational protocol using measured lunar positions and predicted lunar places.
- **before 1742; published 1749:** *Edmundi Halleii astronomi dum viveret regii tabulae astronomicae*. Halley’s astronomical tables, with Latin and English precepts for computing the places of the Sun, Moon, planets, and comets. John Bevis edited the incomplete papers for posthumous publication, so the printed book is a collaborative manifestation rather than an unmediated authorial original.

### Terrestrial physics, meteorology, and hydrography

- **1683:** *A Theory of the Variation of the Magnetical Compass*. A geophysical paper proposing four magnetic poles to account for the observed declination of the compass.
- **1684:** *A Theory of the Tides at the Bar of Tonkin*. A paper interpreting information about the unusually complex tides near the Red River; the Royal Society preserves Halley’s manuscript.
- **1686:** *An Historical Account of the Trade Winds and Monsoons Observable in the Seas between and near the Tropicks*. A meteorological paper attributing the winds principally to differential solar heating.
- **1686:** *Chart of the Trade Winds and Monsoons*. A data-map printed with the historical account. It encodes wind direction graphically and therefore constitutes a cartographic obra, not simply prose illustration.
- **1686:** *A Discourse of the Rule of the Decrease of the Height of the Mercury in the Barometer, According as Places Are Elevated above the Surface of the Earth*. A mathematical and physical paper proposing a relation between barometric pressure and altitude.
- **1692:** *An Account of the Cause of the Change of the Variation of the Magnetical Needle; with an Hypothesis of the Structure of the Internal Parts of the Earth*. A second terrestrial-magnetism paper that turned the earlier four-pole model into a speculative physical architecture of concentric terrestrial shells.
- **1693:** *An Estimate of the Quantity of Vapours Raised out of the Sea by the Warmth of the Sun*. An experimental calculation of evaporation and its place in the terrestrial water cycle.
- **1694:** *A Discourse concerning an Hypothesis of the Manner of the General Deluge*. A speculative paper presented to the Royal Society, explaining the biblical Flood through the close approach of a comet. It was withheld from contemporary publication and appeared only in 1724.
- **1694:** *Some Further Thoughts upon the General Deluge*. A continuation of the preceding discourse, likewise presented in 1694 but first printed in 1724.
- **1698–1700:** *Atlantic magnetic survey*. A naval-scientific project conducted in the *Paramore* to measure magnetic declination over a large part of the Atlantic. Mutiny and the premature end of the first voyage interrupted, but did not cancel, the project.
- **1700–1703:** *A New and Correct Sea Chart of the Whole World, Shewing the Variations of the Compass*. A world magnetic chart derived substantially from the *Paramore* surveys and the first printed chart to use isogonic lines extensively. Several dated states and editions survive; those are manifestations of the chart rather than wholly separate works.
- **1701:** *Channel survey and tidal investigation*. A government voyage along the English Channel and adjacent coasts to observe tides, soundings, and navigational hazards. Its made result was distributed across charts, sailing directions, and official reports, rather than fixed in a single autonomous text.
- **1701:** *An Advertisement Necessary to Be Observed in the Navigation Up and Down the Channel of England*. A practical navigational protocol issued from the Channel survey.
- **1702:** *A New and Correct Chart of the Channel between England and France*. A hydrographic chart incorporating Halley’s observations of coasts, depths, currents, and tides.
- **1702:** *A New and Correct Chart Shewing the Variations of the Compass in the Western and Southern Oceans*. A regional magnetic chart related to the world chart; surviving impressions differ in imprint and date.
- **1714:** *A Short Account of the Cause of the Saltness of the Ocean, and of the Several Lakes That Emit No Rivers; with a Proposal, by Help Thereof, to Discover the Age of the World*. A geophysical paper and research project proposing that the increase of oceanic salt could serve as a geological clock.
- **1716:** *An Account of the Late Surprizing Appearance of the Lights Seen in the Air, on the Sixth of March Last; with an Attempt to Explain the Principal Phaenomena Thereof*. A report and physical theory of the aurora, connecting it with terrestrial magnetism and a luminous effluvium.
- **1719:** *An Account of the Phaenomena of a Very Extraordinary Aurora Borealis, Seen at London on November 10, 1719*. A brief observational report.
- **1722:** *Paper regarding the solar eclipse of 27 November 1722*. An observational manuscript preserved by the Royal Society; its archival description supplies the title, so it should not be mistaken for a separately titled book.
- **1723:** *An Account of the Appearance of Mercury Peeking over the Sun’s Disk on 29 October 1723*. An observational paper preserved in the Royal Society archives.

### Mathematics, mechanics, optics, and statistics

- **1686:** *A Discourse concerning Gravity, and Its Properties*. A mathematical-mechanical paper on falling bodies, projectile motion, and a problem in gunnery; part of it functions as a computational protocol for artillery.
- **1687–1688:** *A Geometrical Method of Finding the Roots of Affected Equations*. A sequence of mathematical papers on locating and constructing the real roots of polynomial equations.
- **1688:** *A Short Account of the Cause of the Saltiness of the Mediterranean Sea*. MacPike lists an early notice associated with Halley’s evaporation reasoning; its relation to the fuller 1714 paper is not entirely clear, so it is best treated as an earlier manifestation of the project rather than silently merged with it.
- **1688:** *A Ready and Easy Method for Constructing the Logarithms of the Natural Numbers*. A computational paper on generating logarithms.
- **1689:** *A New, Exact, and Easy Method of Finding the Roots of Any Equations Generally, and That without Any Previous Reduction*. An iterative numerical protocol now commonly called Halley’s method.
- **1691–1692:** *An Instance of the Excellence of the Modern Algebra in the Resolution of the Problem of Finding the Foci of Optick Glasses Universally*. A mathematical-optical paper applying algebra to thick and compound lenses.
- **1693:** *An Estimate of the Degrees of the Mortality of Mankind, Drawn from Curious Tables of the Births and Funerals at the City of Breslaw; with an Attempt to Ascertain the Price of Annuities upon Lives*. A demographic table, statistical argument, and pricing protocol for life annuities.
- **1693:** *Some Further Considerations on the Breslaw Bills of Mortality*. A supplement clarifying the life-table and annuity calculations.
- **1694:** *Methodus nova, accurata & facilis inveniendi radices aequationum quarumcunque generaliter*. A Latin manifestation and development of Halley’s numerical root-finding method.
- **1695–1696:** *A General Method for the Construction of Equations*. A sequence of geometrical-algebraic papers concerning cubic and higher equations rather than one conventional book.
- **1706:** *Apollonii Pergaei de sectione rationis libri duo ex Arabico manuscripto Latine versi; accedunt ejusdem de sectione spatii libri duo restituti*. Halley’s Latin translation of the surviving Arabic *De sectione rationis*, joined to his conjectural restoration of Apollonius’s lost *De sectione spatii*. Authorship is composite: Apollonius supplied the ancient work, an Arabic transmission preserved part of it, and Halley translated and reconstructed it.
- **1710:** *Apollonii Pergaei conicorum libri III posteriores*. Halley’s Latin translation and editorial reconstruction of the later books of Apollonius’s *Conics*, made from the Arabic tradition.
- **1710:** *Apollonii Pergaei conicorum libri octo et Sereni Antissensis de sectione cylindri et coni libri duo*. A collected scholarly edition combining Apollonius and Serenus with Halley’s translations, restorations, editorial work, and apparatus. MacPike dated one 1710 item to 1700; the *Dictionary of Scientific Biography* explicitly corrects that error.
- **before 1742; published 1758:** *Menelai Alexandrini sphaericorum libri III*. Halley’s Latin translation and editorial reconstruction of Menelaus’s *Spherics* from Arabic manuscripts. Joseph Costard brought it to print after Halley’s death, making the 1758 volume a posthumous and editorially mediated manifestation.

### Historical, archaeological, and chronological studies

- **1695:** *Some Account of the Ancient State of the City of Palmyra, with Short Remarks upon the Inscriptions Found There*. An antiquarian and epigraphic paper based principally on material communicated by William Halifax; the evidence was not gathered by Halley himself.
- **1697:** *A Letter concerning the True Year of the Birth of Christ*. A chronological paper using astronomical and historical evidence; its exact title varies in bibliographical descriptions.
- **1717:** *Remarks on the Astronomical Part of the Ancient Chronology*. A chronological-astronomical intervention associated with the disputes surrounding Newton’s chronology.
- **1726:** *Remarks upon the Dissertation Lately Published at Paris by the Reverend P. Souciet against Sir Isaac Newton’s Chronology*. A polemical paper defending Newton’s chronological project; a manuscript survives in the Royal Society archive.

### Instruments, engineered systems, and operational designs

- **c. 1684–1685:** *Gunnery rule*. A calculating device or set of operational rules for projectile problems. Surviving Royal Society papers are cautiously catalogued “by Edmond Halley?”, so authorship is not secure.
- **c. 1690–1692:** *Diving bell and replenishing-air system*. An engineered apparatus allowing divers to remain submerged while weighted barrels replenished the bell’s air. The invention existed through apparatus, demonstrations, diagrams, and reports rather than as one stable text.
- **c. 1690–1692:** *Diver’s helmet and air conduit*. A companion apparatus enabling a diver to leave the bell while receiving air. Its precise construction and priority are reconstructed from descriptions rather than a surviving complete machine.
- **1692–1696:** *Marine-salvage enterprise*. A commercial and technical project formed to exploit Halley’s diving apparatus. It resists being called a single work: the made thing was the conjunction of machinery, operating procedures, capital, and attempted wreck recovery.
- **1703:** *Plans concerning the harbour and fortifications of Trieste*. Advisory engineering work undertaken for Emperor Leopold I. The project is documented through missions and reports, but no secure, self-contained Halley design survives that can be given a fabricated title.
- **1721–1724:** *Greenwich transit instrument and mural-quadrant observing system*. An observatory project initiated under Halley as Astronomer Royal. The instruments were physically made by specialist craftsmen, so they are not solely authored objects; Halley specified, obtained, installed, and operationalized the system.

### Editorial, publishing, and institutional works

- **1685–1693:** *Philosophical Transactions*, volumes and numbers edited during Halley’s first secretaryship. An editorial body of work rather than a single authored text: Halley selected, translated, abridged, arranged, and sometimes anonymously framed contributions by many people.
- **1687:** *Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica* by Isaac Newton, first edition. Newton is the author, but Halley solicited the work, managed its passage through the Royal Society, edited and proofed it, financed its printing, and composed its prefatory Latin ode. The volume is therefore not “a work by Halley,” yet his material and editorial making of this manifestation cannot responsibly be omitted.
- **1687:** *In viri praestantissimi D. Isaaci Newtoni opus hocce mathematico-physicum saeculi gentisque nostrae decus egregium*. Halley’s Latin hexameter ode prefixed to Newton’s *Principia*; it is a distinct poem and a public manifesto for Newtonian natural philosophy.
- **1712:** *Historia coelestis libri duo* by John Flamsteed. Halley prepared this unauthorized edition from Flamsteed’s deposited observations at Newton’s direction. Authorship and ownership were bitterly disputed: Flamsteed repudiated the edition and later destroyed many copies.
- **1712–1713:** *Commercium epistolicum D. Johannis Collins et aliorum de analysi promota*. A Royal Society report in the Newton–Leibniz priority dispute issued under a committee’s authority. Halley participated and lent institutional authority to it, but Newton’s concealed control of its composition makes individual authorship disputed.
- **1713:** *Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica*, second edition. Roger Cotes was the principal editor and Newton the author; Halley’s role belongs to the continuing publication project, not to sole authorship of this edition.
- **1714–1720:** *Philosophical Transactions*, volumes 29–30 edited during Halley’s second secretaryship. A second editorial corpus combining correspondence, translation, selection, scientific mediation, and Halley’s own papers; it is closer to a collective knowledge-machine than to an authored book.

### Surviving manuscript bodies and unfinished projects

- **1676–1742:** *Scientific and official correspondence*. A large, dispersed body of letters concerning astronomy, mathematics, navigation, instruments, publishing, administration, and patronage. It is a made documentary corpus but not one work conceived or completed as such; MacPike’s collection is a later editorial manifestation.
- **1676–1678:** *St Helena observing journals and working papers*. The notebooks, calculations, and reports underlying the southern-star catalogue. Their survival is incomplete, and they should not be collapsed into the printed *Catalogus*.
- **1698–1701:** *Journals of the voyages of the Paramore*. Shipboard records of magnetic, astronomical, hydrographic, meteorological, and navigational observations. Portions survive in several repositories and later editions; the original documentary project is incomplete.
- **c. seventeenth–eighteenth century:** *Commonplace Book of Edmond Halley*. A manuscript compilation of notes, excerpts, calculations, and practical information. It resists easy authorship language because some contents are copied or adapted rather than composed by Halley.
- **undated:** *Catalogue of plants found in North Wales*. A botanical project mentioned in Royal Society archival records, but the catalogue’s authorship, extent, and survival are uncertain.
- **undated:** *Odd leaves of manuscript labelled “imperfect”*. Fragmentary papers catalogued by the Royal Society under Halley’s name. Their resistance to classification is literal: they lack sufficient completeness and context to constitute secure, separately titled works.
