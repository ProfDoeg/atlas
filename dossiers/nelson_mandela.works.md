## Works by Nelson Rolihlahla Mandela

### Political and legal projects

- **1952:** *Mandela & Tambo*. A legal practice created with Oliver Tambo, commonly identified as South Africa’s first Black-owned law firm; it is an institutional obra rather than a text. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/biography-timeline))

- **1952–1953:** *The M-Plan*. A clandestine organizational protocol devised by Mandela for preserving ANC communication and local organization if the movement were banned; its name and implementation were collective, and surviving descriptions vary in detail. ([Nelson Mandela, *Long Walk to Freedom*](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/za-com-mr-t-1-2-1))

- **1959–1961:** *The armed-struggle project that became Umkhonto we Sizwe*. Mandela helped formulate and launch the ANC-allied organization known as MK in December 1961. This is a collective political project—not an individually authored obra—and the precise institutional relationship between MK, the ANC and the South African Communist Party has remained historically contested. ([South African History Online](https://www.sahistory.org.za/article/umkhonto-wesizwe-mk); [Nelson Mandela Foundation timeline](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/biography-timeline))

- **16 December 1961:** *Manifesto of Umkhonto we Sizwe*. An unsigned collective manifesto distributed at MK’s public emergence; Mandela participated in creating the organization and its strategy, but the text should not be assigned to him as sole author. ([African National Congress archive](https://www.anc1912.org.za/manifesto-of-umkhonto-we-sizwe-16-december-1961/))

- **1994–1999:** *The Mandela presidency*. A governmental and constitutional project encompassing reconciliation, democratic institution-building and the transition from apartheid; it resists treatment as a personal “work” because its authorship belongs to a government, Parliament, negotiating parties and a society rather than Mandela alone. ([South African Government biography](https://www.gov.za/mandela100/biography))

### Political writings, manifestos and courtroom performances

- **1953:** *No Easy Walk to Freedom*. An address to the ANC’s Transvaal conference that functions partly as manifesto and partly as organizational analysis; Mandela was prohibited from attending, so the text was delivered for him. It later supplied the title for a collected volume. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation speech archive](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/za-com-mr-s-16))

- **1950s–1962:** *Articles, underground statements and political correspondence*. This is a dispersed body of work rather than one fixed text, including journalism, ANC arguments and statements issued while Mandela was underground; selections were later gathered in *No Easy Walk to Freedom* and *The Struggle Is My Life*. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation archive](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/acom); [Pathfinder Press](https://www.pathfinderpress.com/products/struggle-is-my-life_by-nelson-mandela))

- **c. 1962:** *How to Be a Good Communist; Dialectical Materialism; Political Economy*. A manuscript in Mandela’s handwriting, seized at Rivonia, that reworked communist instructional material for an African audience. Mandela described it as an argumentative exercise based on Liu Shaoqi rather than a declaration of party membership; both its political significance and the conclusions drawn from it remain disputed. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation archival description](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/informationobject/browse?places=23520&showAdvanced=1&sort=alphabetic&sortDir=desc&subjects=40914&topLod=0&view=table); [Stephen Ellis, “The Genesis of the ANC’s Armed Struggle”](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstream/handle/2263/19620/Ellis_Genesis_2011.pdf))

- **15 October–7 November 1962:** *Statement in the Pretoria trial*. A courtroom text delivered from the dock during Mandela’s trial for leaving South Africa without a passport and inciting a strike. It is simultaneously legal defence, political manifesto and performed act of defiance. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation trials chronology](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/trials-and-prison-chronology))

- **20 April 1964:** *Statement from the Dock at the Rivonia Trial* (“I Am Prepared to Die”). A lengthy courtroom manifesto composed with legal and political consultation but spoken and claimed by Mandela; manuscript fragments and revisions survive, so the familiar published text represents a documented performance rather than an entirely solitary literary composition. ([University of the Witwatersrand, Nelson Mandela Papers](https://researcharchives.wits.ac.za/uploads/r/historical-papers-research-archive-library-university-of-witwatersrand/3/b/a/3baedc352abd5a3778468aa2c6c8a7fbfc1d741a26baa49702f77c76e3da30bc/A2519-8-01-jpeg.pdf); [Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/news/entry/i-am-prepared-to-die))

- **10 February 1985:** *Statement rejecting P. W. Botha’s conditional-release offer*. A political manifesto written in prison and publicly read by Mandela’s daughter Zindzi because he could not deliver it himself; the separation of writer, voice and public performance is essential to the work. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation speech archive](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/za-com-mr-s-1000))

- **11 February 1990:** *Address from Cape Town City Hall after release from prison*. A public political performance announcing continuity with the ANC struggle and renewed negotiations; although associated with Mandela’s voice, it emerged from collective movement politics. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation timeline](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/biography-timeline))

- **10 May 1994:** *Inaugural Address*. A presidential speech that also acts as a civic manifesto for democratic South Africa. Its obra includes the ceremonial performance, not merely the printed wording. ([South African Government](https://www.gov.za/news/speeches/president-nelson-mandela-inauguration-10-may-1994))

- **1994–1999:** *Presidential speeches, messages and statements*. A large, partly collaborative corpus made with speechwriters and government staff; individual authorship cannot safely be inferred from Mandela’s delivery alone. The official government and Foundation archives, rather than any single book edition, define this open-ended body of work. ([South African Government speeches](https://www.gov.za/news/speeches); [Nelson Mandela Foundation databases](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/databases))

- **1950s–2008:** *Public speeches, interviews and impromptu remarks*. The Foundation preserves thousands of records rather than a complete authorial canon, and many were delivered from prepared collective drafts or reconstructed from recordings. This corpus resists division into a stable list of individually authored literary “works.” ([Nelson Mandela Foundation Archive at the Centre of Memory](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/acom); [Nelson Mandela Foundation, *By Himself* introduction](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/introduction-from-the-book))

### Autobiographical projects and books

- **1975–1976:** *Robben Island autobiographical manuscript*. A clandestine memoir drafted by Mandela, transcribed in tiny handwriting by Laloo Chiba and smuggled out by Mac Maharaj; prison authorities discovered Mandela’s concealed draft. It is both an unfinished collective production and the principal precursor of *Long Walk to Freedom*. ([The Presidential Years project](https://tpy.nelsonmandela.org/about); [Nelson Mandela Foundation archive](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/za-com-mr-t-1-2-1))

- **1965:** *No Easy Walk to Freedom*. A collection of Mandela’s articles, speeches, letters and trial addresses edited by Ruth First. The book is therefore an editorial assemblage of earlier manifestations, not a single newly written work, and later editions differ in contents. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation FAQ](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/faqs); [Nobel Prize selected bibliography](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/1993/mandela/biographical/))

- **1978:** *The Struggle Is My Life*. A documentary collection of speeches and political writings combined with historical documents and accounts by fellow prisoners. Its shifting editions and collective documentary apparatus make it a constructed archive as much as a Mandela-authored book. ([Library of Congress bibliography](https://www.loc.gov/loc/lcib/9501/mandela.html); [Pathfinder Press](https://www.pathfinderpress.com/products/struggle-is-my-life_by-nelson-mandela))

- **1994:** *Long Walk to Freedom*. Mandela’s autobiography, developed from the prison manuscript and completed through extensive work with Richard Stengel and other collaborators. It is conventionally attributed to Mandela, but its polished published form should not be mistaken for an unmediated transcription of his manuscript. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation FAQ](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/faqs); [The Presidential Years project](https://tpy.nelsonmandela.org/about))

- **1999:** *Nelson Mandela’s Favorite African Folktales*. A curated anthology of African traditional narratives selected and introduced under Mandela’s name; he did not originate the folktales, so the obra is his act of selection and framing rather than authorship of the stories. ([Smithsonian Institution catalogue record](https://www.si.edu/object/nelson-mandelas-favorite-african-folktales%3Asiris_sil_700509))

- **2003:** *In His Own Words: From Freedom to the Future*. A thematically arranged collection of speeches and writings edited by Kader Asmal, David Chidester and Wilmot James. It manifests Mandela’s public corpus but is not a new continuous composition by him. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation archival catalogue](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/nelson-mandela-in-his-own-words-from-freedom-to-future-tributes-and-speeches-bibliography))

- **2010:** *Conversations with Myself*. An authorized archival montage of letters, notebooks, calendars, recorded conversations and an unfinished memoir sequel. Its editors made the book after arranging pre-existing materials, so it is a post-compositional assemblage rather than a memoir Mandela wrote in that form. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/news/entry/official-south-african-launch-of-conversations-with-myself-held-today))

- **2011:** *Nelson Mandela by Himself: The Authorised Book of Quotations*. An authenticated reference work compiled from speeches, interviews, letters and diaries and co-edited by Sello Hatang and Sahm Venter. Mandela made the underlying utterances, but not the book’s selection and taxonomy; it actively corrects numerous false attributions. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation introduction](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/introduction-from-the-book); [Nelson Mandela Foundation quotations app](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/publications/entry/mandela-in-his-own-words))

- **1998–2017:** *The Presidential Years* / *Dare Not Linger*. Mandela’s unfinished project for a sequel to *Long Walk to Freedom* survives as draft chapters, notes and interview material. Mandla Langa transformed that incomplete archive into the posthumous 2017 book *Dare Not Linger*, making the publication a dual-authored completion rather than a finished Mandela manuscript. ([The Presidential Years project](https://tpy.nelsonmandela.org/about); [Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/publications/entry/dare-not-linger))

- **1962–1990; published 2018:** *The Prison Letters of Nelson Mandela*. A corpus of correspondence written during imprisonment, first issued as an authorized and authenticated collection under Sahm Venter’s editorship. The letters are Mandela’s works; the 2018 book, published after his death, is an editorial construction and does not claim to contain every surviving prison letter. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/publications/entry/the-prison-letters-of-nelson-mandela); [Nelson Mandela Foundation overview](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/nm100-films-books-publications))

### Diaries, notebooks and correspondence

- **January–August 1962:** *African journey diary and military-training notebooks*. Handwritten records made during Mandela’s clandestine trip through Africa and to Britain, including notes connected with political support and military training. They survive as archival objects and later supplied material for edited books rather than having one stable publication title. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation, *By Himself* introduction](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/introduction-from-the-book); [Nelson Mandela Foundation archive](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/acom))

- **1940s–2010s:** *Personal and political correspondence*. Thousands of letters—to family, comrades, officials and institutions—form an incompletely preserved corpus spread across repositories. It resists classification as one work: individual letters were made for particular acts and recipients, while published selections impose later boundaries. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation Archive at the Centre of Memory](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/acom); [Mandela Materials Database](https://archive.nelsonmandela.org/index.php/za-com-mr-rt-162))

- **Various dates:** *Calendars, prison notebooks, draft memoirs and recorded reflections*. These working documents are creations and traces of thought, but many were never intended as autonomous works. Their appearance in *Conversations with Myself* does not convert every fragment into a separately titled publication. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/news/entry/official-south-african-launch-of-conversations-with-myself-held-today))

### Visual works

- **May 2002:** *Robben Island I: The Cell; The Window; The Church; The Lighthouse; The Harbour*. Five color sketches made from photographs of Robben Island under artist Varenka Paschke’s guidance and subsequently translated into signed print editions. The underlying drawings are attributed to Mandela, but the commercial editions involved printmakers and publishers. ([House of Mandela Art catalogue](https://houseofmandelaart.com/collections/robben-island-i-signed-prints); [*The Guardian*](https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2009/jul/14/nelson-mandela-artworks))

- **2002–2003:** *Robben Island and Struggle-series drawings and lithographic editions*. A broader body of brightly colored prison-related images, hand studies and associated prints marketed under Mandela’s name. Some editions and signatures became the subject of legal and authenticity disputes; consequently the advertised corpus cannot safely be treated as a settled catalogue raisonné. ([*The Guardian*](https://www.theguardian.com/theguardian/2009/jul/14/nelson-mandela-artworks); [Independent Online](https://iol.co.za/news/south-africa/2007-11-10-mandela-artwork-saga-continues/))

### Institutions and legacy projects

- **1995:** *Nelson Mandela Children’s Fund*. A charitable institution established by Mandela, initially financed in part through his donation of one-third of his presidential salary. It is an ongoing collective project rather than a personal possession or finite work. ([Nelson Mandela Children’s Fund](https://www.nelsonmandelachildrensfund.com/our-history/))

- **1999:** *Nelson Mandela Foundation*. Created as Mandela’s post-presidential office and subsequently developed into a centre for memory, dialogue and archival work. Its institutional continuity makes it an obra that deliberately exceeds its founder’s individual authorship. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/about-the-centre-of-memory1))

- **2002–2003:** *46664*. An HIV/AIDS-awareness and fundraising campaign built around Mandela’s former prison number and manifested through concerts, recordings and public advocacy. It is a collective, cross-media project—close to a political Gesamtkunstwerk—rather than music composed or performed solely by Mandela. ([Nelson Mandela Foundation annual report](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/uploads/files/ANNUAL-REPORT-2013-WEB2.pdf))

- **2003:** *Mandela Rhodes Foundation and Mandela Rhodes Scholarships*. An institutional project founded by Mandela in partnership with the Rhodes Trust to support African postgraduate education and leadership development. Its use of Cecil Rhodes’s legacy was intentionally framed as reconciliation and reparation, though that symbolic conjunction remains politically contestable. ([Mandela Rhodes Foundation](https://www.mandelarhodes.org/about/story/))

- **2007:** *The Elders*. An independent group of senior international leaders co-founded publicly by Mandela, Graça Machel and Desmond Tutu from an idea developed with Peter Gabriel and Richard Branson. It is a collective peace-making project rather than Mandela’s sole creation. ([The Elders](https://theelders.org/history))

- **2008–2009:** *Mandela Day*. Mandela’s call for individuals to devote time to public service became an annual campaign and, in 2009, a United Nations international observance. The manifestation was collectively institutionalized and should not be confused with a text authored by Mandela. ([United Nations](https://www.un.org/en/events/mandeladay/); [Nelson Mandela Foundation](https://www.nelsonmandela.org/mandela-day))
