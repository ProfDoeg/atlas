## Works by Cornelius Vanderbilt

### Vessels commissioned, financed, or supervised

- **1818:** *Bellona*. A steamboat whose construction Vanderbilt supervised for his employer Thomas Gibbons; it is therefore a managerial creation, not a vessel built for Vanderbilt’s own account. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **Reported as 1820, but dating disputed:** *Caroline*. The 1853 inventory calls this Vanderbilt’s first steamer built “on his own account,” but its printed/OCR date conflicts with later accounts and with records associating a *Caroline* built in 1822 with Vanderbilt; the identification and exact date should not be silently resolved. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Shotline wreck record](https://shotlinediving.com/wreck/caroline-1822/))
- **1821:** *Fanny*. A steamboat attributed to Vanderbilt as builder-proprietor in the contemporary inventory of his vessels. Here “built” means commissioned and commercially produced under his direction, not necessarily constructed by his hands. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1822:** *Thistle*. A Vanderbilt-built steamboat in the 1853 inventory. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1822:** *Emerald*. A Vanderbilt-built steamboat; the same inventory later names another *Emerald* without a date, and it is unclear whether that is a second vessel or a duplicated name. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1824:** *Swan*. A steamboat commissioned or produced under Vanderbilt’s direction. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1826:** *Citizen*. A steamboat commissioned or produced under Vanderbilt’s direction. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Cinderella*. A steamboat named in the contemporary inventory of Vanderbilt’s constructions. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Clifton*. A steamboat named in the same inventory. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Union*. A steamboat named in the same inventory; it should not be confused automatically with “Union Line,” the transport service Vanderbilt managed for the Gibbons family. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **1827–1828:** *Champion*. A steamboat commissioned or produced under Vanderbilt’s direction. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Nimrod*. A steamboat commissioned or produced under Vanderbilt’s direction. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Livingston*. A steamboat commissioned or produced under Vanderbilt’s direction. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Cleopatra*. A steamboat used in Vanderbilt’s Long Island Sound trade; surviving litigation documents its continuing commercial operation in 1839. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **1827–1828:** *Sound Champion*. A steamboat commissioned or produced under Vanderbilt’s direction. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *North Carolina*. One of four steamers Vanderbilt reportedly built for a company operating the regular mail line between Washington and Charleston; this was contract production rather than an individually owned obra. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Governor Dudley*. Another of the four steamers reportedly built for the Washington–Charleston mail-line company. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Vanderbilt*. Another of the four mail-line steamers; this early vessel is distinct from Vanderbilt’s much larger 1850s ocean steamship of the same name. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1827–1828:** *Gladiator*. The fourth steamer attributed to Vanderbilt’s production for the Washington–Charleston mail-line company. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Sylph*. A steamboat attributed to Vanderbilt in the 1853 inventory, which supplies no individual construction date. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Augusta*. A steamboat attributed to Vanderbilt without a securely stated construction date. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Emerald*. A vessel listed among Vanderbilt’s later boats, but possibly the 1822 *Emerald* repeated rather than a separate creation; completeness and identity remain uncertain. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Red Jacket*. A steamboat attributed to Vanderbilt without a securely stated construction date. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Hugenot* [probably *Huguenot*]. A steamboat whose name appears in this spelling in the 1853 printed inventory; the spelling and date have not been normalized without a vessel register. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Hannah Burt*. A steamboat attributed to Vanderbilt without a securely stated construction date. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Date not fixed:** *Eastern*. A steamboat attributed to Vanderbilt without a securely stated construction date. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1830s:** *C. Vanderbilt*. A steamboat created for the combined rail-and-water Boston route through Stonington; it formed a paired service with *Commodore*. The source does not provide a precise construction year. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1830s:** *Commodore*. The companion steamboat to *C. Vanderbilt* in Vanderbilt’s Stonington–Boston service; no precise construction year is supplied by the contemporary inventory. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1835:** *Lexington*. A fast Long Island Sound paddle steamer commissioned by Vanderbilt. He sold it before its catastrophic 1840 fire, so the later loss belongs to the vessel’s history but not to his period of operation. ([Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Lexington”](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Lexington-American-steamboat))
- **Dates and names not fully recovered:** *Eight steamers for the Transportation Company*. The 1853 account attributes this group to Vanderbilt but does not name its members; it resists treatment as eight securely catalogued individual works. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **Dates and names not fully recovered:** *Five Havana–Matanzas steamers*. A group of five vessels reportedly built by Vanderbilt for Cuban service; even the contemporary printed account signals uncertainty, and supplies neither names nor dates. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1850:** *Prometheus*. An ocean steamship built for Vanderbilt’s projected Nicaragua transit and subsequently used on the Atlantic portion of his California route. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Western Cover Society](https://www.westerncoversociety.org/literature/books/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861-chapter-8/))
- **1850:** *Director*. A shallow-draft steamer built specifically for the river-and-lake portion of Vanderbilt’s Nicaragua route; it was the route’s first steam vessel on Lake Nicaragua. ([California State Lands Commission historical report](https://www.slc.ca.gov/Meeting_Summaries/1988_Documents/09-14-88/Items/091488R31.pdf))
- **1851:** *Daniel Webster*. An ocean steamship counted among Vanderbilt’s “American productions” and employed in the Nicaragua–California system. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Western Cover Society](https://www.westerncoversociety.org/literature/books/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861-chapter-8/))
- **1851:** *Northern Light*. A wooden side-wheel ocean steamship built for Vanderbilt’s California service and later used in his Atlantic operations. ([TheShipsList fleet history](https://www.benjidog.co.uk/TheShipsList/Vanderbilt.php); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1852:** *Star of the West*. An ocean steamship produced for Vanderbilt’s California route; it later became independently famous for its attempted 1861 resupply of Fort Sumter. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Western Cover Society](https://www.westerncoversociety.org/literature/books/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861-chapter-8/))
- **1852–1853:** *North Star*. A large steam yacht commissioned by Vanderbilt to his requirements for his family’s European tour and subsequently placed in commercial service. Its physical builders and designers executed the vessel, so Vanderbilt’s authorship is properly that of proprietor, commissioner, and design participant rather than shipwright. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/); [Vanderbilt University Special Collections](https://exhibitions.library.vanderbilt.edu/strengthening-ties/item/vanderbilts-private-steamship/))
- **1855:** *Ariel*. A wooden side-wheel steamship constructed for Vanderbilt’s transatlantic line. ([TheShipsList fleet history](https://www.benjidog.co.uk/TheShipsList/Vanderbilt.php))
- **1856–1857:** *Vanderbilt*. Vanderbilt’s flagship transatlantic passenger and mail steamship, physically built by Jeremiah Simonson at Greenpoint. Vanderbilt later proposed converting it into a ram and donated it to the United States; the Navy, rather than Vanderbilt, performed its military fitting-out. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/v/vanderbilt.html))
- **1857:** *Ocean Queen* (built as *Queen of the Pacific*). The ship entered Vanderbilt’s fleet only after his 1859 purchase and renaming of it; it is consequently an acquisition and commercial remanifestation, not a hull he made. ([TheShipsList fleet history](https://www.benjidog.co.uk/TheShipsList/Vanderbilt.php))
- **1851; acquired 1860:** *Illinois* (formerly *Louisiana*). Vanderbilt purchased rather than created this steamship, so it belongs only at the resistant edge of the catalogue: he made it part of his transport system but did not make the vessel. ([TheShipsList fleet history](https://www.benjidog.co.uk/TheShipsList/Vanderbilt.php))
- **Dates not fixed:** *New Haven*, *Huntress*, *Water-Witch*, and *Worcester*. Vanderbilt purchased, refitted, and operated these four vessels. Their refitting constitutes alteration or remanifestation rather than original authorship, and the summary source supplies no reliable individual dates. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))

### Transport systems, companies, and unrealized projects

- **1810 onward:** *Staten Island–Manhattan sailboat service*. Vanderbilt’s first independent enterprise was a ferry-and-freight service rather than a single material object; its obra was a repeatedly performed route joining vessels, schedules, labor, and commerce. ([Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/v/vanderbilt.html))
- **1817–1829:** *Gibbons’s Union Line*. Vanderbilt managed and materially developed Thomas Gibbons’s combined steamboat-and-overland service between New York and Philadelphia. It was not solely his company, so it resists attribution as an exclusively Vanderbilt work. ([Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **1829–1840s:** *Vanderbilt’s New York regional steamboat lines*. A shifting body of services on the Hudson River and Long Island Sound, made through vessels, fare policies, schedules, and competitive agreements rather than through one stable corporation. Treating it as a single titled work would falsely impose coherence on changing partnerships and routes. ([T. J. Stiles, author’s account of *The First Tycoon*](https://www.tjstiles.net/disc.htm); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1830s–1840s:** *New York–Boston route via Stonington*. A coordinated steamboat-and-rail transport service built around the steamers *C. Vanderbilt* and *Commodore*. It is best classified as an infrastructural system, not merely a fleet or company. ([Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-steam-yacht-north-star-1853-06-18/))
- **1849–1861:** *American Atlantic and Pacific Ship Canal project*. Vanderbilt acquired control of a concession-holding company and pursued a canal across Nicaragua, but the canal was never built. Its obra is therefore the project—the charter, surveys, finance, and intention—not a completed waterway. ([U.S. Naval Institute](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1939/july/proposed-nicaragua-canal); [Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **1850–1851:** *Nicaragua transit route*. Vanderbilt’s organization cleared river obstructions and cut a roughly twelve-mile carriage road between Lake Nicaragua and San Juan del Sur, joining ocean steamships, river and lake craft, road vehicles, ports, schedules, and tickets into one passage. It is a transport Gesamtkunstwerk in the limited sense that no single medium—ship, road, corporation, or timetable—constitutes the work by itself. ([Western Cover Society](https://www.westerncoversociety.org/literature/books/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861-chapter-8/))
- **1850–1851:** *Accessory Transit Company of Nicaragua*. A corporation organized by Vanderbilt and associates to operate the Nicaragua crossing. Collective incorporation makes singular authorship inappropriate, though Vanderbilt was its central promoter and first president. ([U.S. Naval Institute](https://www.usni.org/magazines/proceedings/1939/july/proposed-nicaragua-canal); [Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **1851–1853:** *Vanderbilt Independent Line*. A twice-monthly New York–California steamship service integrated with the Nicaragua crossing and inaugurated in July 1851. Vanderbilt sold its steamships to the Accessory Transit Company in 1853, so the work was both a route and a deliberately transferable business system. ([Western Cover Society](https://www.westerncoversociety.org/literature/books/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861/mails-of-the-westward-expansion-1803-to-1861-chapter-8/))
- **1855–1860:** *Vanderbilt European Line / North Atlantic Mail Steamship Line*. A privately organized transatlantic service from New York to Le Havre, later also serving Southampton and Bremen. Its title varies between historical accounts, reflecting a service and fleet rather than a permanently fixed textual entity. ([TheShipsList fleet history](https://www.benjidog.co.uk/TheShipsList/Vanderbilt.php); [Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/v/vanderbilt.html))
- **1863–1870:** *Vanderbilt railroad system*. Vanderbilt assembled control of the New York & Harlem, Hudson River, and New York Central railroads and linked their operations. He acquired and reorganized existing corporations and infrastructure rather than originating every component, so the made thing is the integrated system, not the underlying railroads individually. ([Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php); [Naval History and Heritage Command](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/histories/ship-histories/danfs/v/vanderbilt.html))
- **1870:** *New York Central and Hudson River Railroad Company*. The consolidated corporation formed from Vanderbilt-controlled New York Central and Hudson River lines. This is an organizational obra—a legal and managerial construction whose material tracks substantially predated it. ([Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
- **1869–1870s:** *Expanded western rail network*. Vanderbilt joined his eastern system commercially and financially to the Lake Shore & Michigan Southern and Canada Southern interests, helping create a continuous New York–Great Lakes–Chicago network. Because these remained corporations with other shareholders, directors, and builders, the network cannot be credited to him as a solitary maker. ([Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))

### Architecture and institutional foundations

- **1869–1871:** *Grand Central Depot*. A railroad terminal commissioned by Vanderbilt and designed by architect John B. Snook, with engineering and train-shed work by additional specialists. Vanderbilt made the project possible and directed its corporate purpose, but the architecture is not his sole-authored work; the 1871 depot was later replaced and must not be confused with the present Grand Central Terminal of 1913. ([Grand Central historical account](https://srnteach.us/HIST1700/assets/projects/unit3/pdfs/U3Project_GrandCentral_History.pdf); [American Planning Association](https://www.planning.org/greatplaces/spaces/2013/grandcentral.htm))
- **1869–1870s:** *Fourth Avenue rail cut and approaches to Grand Central*. Vanderbilt’s railroads depressed and reorganized tracks leading to the new depot, beginning the transformation of Fourth Avenue into the later Park Avenue corridor. This was corporate urban infrastructure rather than a personally designed monument. ([Grand Central historical account](https://srnteach.us/HIST1700/assets/projects/unit3/pdfs/U3Project_GrandCentral_History.pdf))
- **1873–1875:** *Vanderbilt University*. Vanderbilt’s $1 million gift enabled the founding and endowment of Central University in Nashville, subsequently named Vanderbilt University. It resists being called his “work”: Bishop Holland N. McTyeire and the Methodist Episcopal Church, South, conceived and organized the institution, while Vanderbilt chiefly made its financial manifestation possible. ([Vanderbilt University, history of the founding gift](https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2011/04/11/the-commodores-civil-war/); [Stiles, notes to *The First Tycoon*](https://erenow.org/biographies/the-first-tycoon/21.php))
