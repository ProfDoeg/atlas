## Works by Johann Heinrich Wilhelm Tischbein

### Paintings and painted cycles

- **c. 1775:** *Portrait of Elisa von der Recke*. Oil portrait; the approximate dating, rather than a fixed year, is retained by the Dresden collection and later scholarship.

- **1782:** *Einer den andern gemalt* (*Each Painted the Other*). Double self-portrait made jointly with Tischbein’s brother Johann Heinrich Tischbein the Younger; because each artist painted the other, it resists attribution to a single hand.

- **c. 1782:** *Götz von Berlichingen mit eiserner Hand* (*Götz and Weislingen*). History painting derived from Goethe’s drama; related drawings and watercolours show that Tischbein treated the subject as a larger illustration project rather than as one isolated canvas.

- **1784:** *Conradin von Schwaben und Friedrich von Österreich vernehmen beim Schachspiel ihr Todesurteil*. Oil history painting of Conradin and Frederick of Austria receiving their death sentence while playing chess.

- **1786:** *Hektor wirft Paris seine Weiblichkeit vor und mahnt ihn, in den Kampf zu ziehen*. Large Homeric history painting conceived in Rome in artistic competition with Jacques-Louis David.

- **1787:** *Goethe in der römischen Campagna*. Monumental oil portrait and idealized landscape. Although conventionally dated 1787, its prolonged execution and condition have generated disagreement about how completely Tischbein finished it before leaving Italy.

- **1788:** *Ausreitende Amazonen*. Oil painting of mounted Amazons, now in Oldenburg; it belongs to Tischbein’s transformation of classical motifs into freely invented narrative scenes.

- **1802:** *Der als Hirte verkleidete Odysseus wird von seiner Gattin Penelope nicht erkannt*. Small oil painting whose Homeric episode exemplifies the intimate format in which Tischbein’s late classicism was most successful.

- **date uncertain, early nineteenth century:** *Hektors Abschied von Andromache*. Homeric oil painting discussed publicly in 1810; the documentary record establishes the subject more securely than an exact date.

- **date uncertain, early nineteenth century:** *Kassandra*. Homeric history painting discussed with *Hektors Abschied* in 1810.

- **date uncertain, early nineteenth century:** *Lasset die Kindlein zu mir kommen*. Religious painting made for Bremen’s Ansgarii church; its title is traditional and its precise execution date is not securely fixed.

- **1808:** *Bildnis der Schriftstellerin Engel Christine Westphalen*. Oil portrait of the Hamburg writer and salonnière.

- **c. 1810:** *Bildnis der Tochter Ernestine*. Small oil portrait; the Germanisches Nationalmuseum dates it only approximately.

- **1816:** *General Graf von Bennigsen mit seinem Stab vor Hamburg*. Large group portrait/history painting. Its combination of identifiable sitters with the commemoration of a military event makes it more than a conventional portrait.

- **1818–1819:** *Prinzen-Zyklus*. Didactic project originally comprising fifty sepia drawings of exemplary Homeric subjects for the education of the Oldenburg princes; only part of the original ensemble is represented by the surviving Oldenburg inventory sequence.

- **1819–1820:** *Idyllen-Zyklus*. A room-sized Gesamtkunstwerk of Arcadian landscapes, mythological figures and later poetic responses by Goethe. Sources variously count forty-three or forty-four panels, partly because the ensemble can be counted either as individual pictures or as an architectural whole.

- **1821:** *Die Stärke des Mannes* (also *Des Mannes Stärke*). Monumental allegorical oil painting, sometimes described as a *Vernunftbild* or “reason-picture”; its didactic allegory resists classification as straightforward narrative history painting.

### Drawings, watercolours and graphic suites

- **1782 onward:** *Illustrations to Goethe’s Götz von Berlichingen*. Project comprising drawings, watercolours and at least one painting. The sheets are studies and narrative inventions, not illustrations for a completed edition in the ordinary bibliographical sense.

- **1786–1788:** *Italian sketch and study corpus*. Numerous landscapes, architectural studies, figure studies, drawings after antiquity and records of Goethe’s Roman circle survive, especially in Weimar. This is a body of working material rather than a single fixed “work”; many sheets are untitled, double-sided or only approximately dated.

- **1786–1787:** *Goethe am Fenster der römischen Wohnung am Corso*. Watercolour, chalk, pen and pencil drawing of Goethe seen from behind at the window of their shared Roman lodging.

- **1787:** *Das verfluchte zweite Kissen*. Comic ink drawing arising from Goethe and Tischbein’s Roman domestic life; it is an anecdotal sheet rather than an illustration to a formal literary text.

- **c. 1790:** *Paris und Helena*. Pen-and-pencil Homeric composition. A colour annotation survives, but scholarship reports that the explanatory text is not in Tischbein’s own hand.

- **before 1800:** *Kopf des Achill* and related *Achill* studies. A group of watercolour, chalk and charcoal transformations of the ancient Ares Borghese type; repetition across several collections makes the motif, rather than any one sheet, the operative obra.

- **before 1800:** *Die sieben Homerischen Helden*. Chalk design showing Menelaus, Paris, Diomedes, Odysseus, Nestor, Achilles and Agamemnon; it became the basis for an engraved plate in the Homer project.

- **before 1800:** *Sieben Heldenköpfe*. Etched realization by Guglielmo Morghen after Tischbein’s design. It is a collaborative manifestation: Tischbein supplied the image, but did not execute the surviving print entirely himself.

- **c. 1799:** *Der Schwachmatikus auf dem Esel in der Campagna und die vier Brüder*. Drawing devised as a title image for the *Eselsgeschichte*. It deliberately reworks the landscape and pose of *Goethe in der Campagna* as self-parody.

- **c. 1799:** *Physiognomische Studie mit sechs Köpfen*. Study for the *Eselsgeschichte*, identifying the conventional temperaments; it belongs simultaneously to Tischbein’s narrative, physiognomic and visual research.

- **c. 1800:** *Der bunte Esel im Zimmer des Poeten*. Watercolour, pen and pencil image from the *Eselsgeschichte*. Its combination of image, labels and narrative implication makes it a picture-text rather than a freestanding illustration.

- **c. 1800:** *Die Geburt des Esels und der Zwist des Gärtnerpaares*. Narrative sheet from the *Eselsgeschichte* project.

- **c. 1820:** *Göttin des Tanzes*. Watercolour-and-pen version of a figure ultimately adapted from Pompeian imagery; a related oil panel belongs to the *Idyllen-Zyklus*, so the title denotes a migrating motif in several manifestations.

- **dates uncertain:** *Sibyllinische Bücher*. Five known albums containing drawings, fragments, repetitions and visual speculations. Their unstable, accumulative form resists treatment as five conventionally finished works.

- **dates uncertain:** *Einzelne Bemerkungen über Menschen und Thiere*. Album or portfolio of observations in image and text. It is closer to an experimental notebook and physiognomic research archive than to a completed book.

- **dates uncertain:** *Animal studies*. A large corpus of individual drawings and watercolours, frequently untitled and sometimes known only through estate or museum groupings. It cannot honestly be reduced to invented titles or dates; the corpus itself is the appropriate obra-level entry.

### Print and archaeological publication projects

- **1791–1795:** *Collection of Engravings from Ancient Vases, Mostly of Pure Greek Workmanship, Discovered in Sepulchres in the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies*. Four-volume archaeological image project based on Sir William Hamilton’s second vase collection, directed by Tischbein and executed with several draughtsmen and engravers. It is a collaborative scholarly publication and visual archive, not solely an author-written book.

- **1796:** *Têtes de différents animaux, dessinées d’après nature*. Two-volume suite of animal heads drawn from nature. The made thing is primarily a graphic and comparative-physiognomic project, even though it appeared in book form.

- **1801–1804:** *Homer nach Antiken gezeichnet*. Six-part illustrated Homer project published with explanatory text by Christian Gottlob Heyne; its eighty-one graphic leaves include plates, initials and ornaments executed by Tischbein and other printmakers after his designs. Authorship is therefore distributed among designer, engravers and commentator.

- **from 1801:** *Figures d’Homère dessinées d’après l’antique*. French-titled manifestation of the Homer material. It overlaps substantially with *Homer nach Antiken gezeichnet* and should not be counted as an entirely independent invention merely because copies, issues and titles differ.

- **after 1804–1820s:** *Continuation of the Homer project*. Later fascicles and reconfigurations extended the original undertaking beyond Heyne’s six Göttingen parts; bibliographical sources sometimes describe eleven parts in all. The shifting publishers, collaborators and titles make this a continuing project rather than one stable edition.

### Image-text and literary projects

- **c. 1799–1812:** *Die Eselsgeschichte; oder Der Schwachmatikus und seine vier Brüder … nebst zwölf Vorstellungen zum Esel*. Satirical image-text project begun through pictorial suites in Naples and elaborated in Eutin. Henriette Hermes shaped Tischbein’s ideas into the surviving extended prose around 1810–1812, so calling the whole text Tischbein’s sole-authored novel would conceal its collaborative authorship.

- **1811:** *Der Schwachmatikus und seine vier Brüder*. Published fragment of the *Eselsgeschichte* in the *Vaterländisches Museum*. It is a manifestation of the larger, still evolving project rather than the complete work.

- **from 1810; published posthumously in 1861:** *Aus meinem Leben*. Autobiographical recollections written and dictated over time, then edited for publication after Tischbein’s death. The 1861 book is an editorial manifestation of a manuscript process, not a volume issued by Tischbein in finished form.

- **dates uncertain:** *Letters*. More than a thousand manuscript letters and related documents survive in the Oldenburg artist’s estate. Individual letters are acts of communication rather than self-evidently autonomous “works”; collectively, however, they form an authored textual corpus essential to Tischbein’s practice.

### Decorative and architectural collaborations

- **early nineteenth century:** *The so-called Tischbein stoves at Schloss Eutin*. Ceramic heating stoves decorated with animals and classical motifs derived from Tischbein’s drawings and manufactured by the Niemann works. Tischbein supplied designs rather than making the ceramic bodies himself, so these are collaborative manifestations, not securely autograph sculptures.

- **1819–1820:** *Idyllenzimmer, Oldenburg Palace*. Interior ensemble created by installing the *Idyllen-Zyklus* as a unified room decoration. The room, rather than any single panel, is the fullest obra: architecture, painting, mythological narrative and Goethe’s subsequent poetry are meant to be apprehended together.

- **1821–1822:** *Goethe–Tischbein Idyll book*. Collaborative project initiated when Tischbein sent Goethe a booklet of seventeen watercolours and sketches, to which Goethe supplied verses and prose commentaries. The sheets are not identical to the Oldenburg panels, making this a separate image-text manifestation rather than a simple catalogue of the room.
