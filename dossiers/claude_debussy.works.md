## Works by Achille-Claude Debussy

### Songs for voice and piano

- **1879:** *Ballade à la lune* (L¹ 1). Lost song on a poem by Alfred de Musset; omitted without explanation from Lesure’s revised catalogue, so its attribution or the omission itself remains unresolved.
- **1879:** *Madrid* (L² 1). Song for voice and piano on Musset.
- **1880:** *Nuit d’étoiles* (L² 2). Song for voice and piano on Théodore de Banville.
- **1880:** *Rêverie* (L² 3). Song for voice and piano on Banville, distinct from the later piano piece of the same title.
- **1880:** *Caprice* (L² 6). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1880:** *Aimons-nous et dormons* (L² 7). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1881:** *Les Baisers* (L² 9). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1881:** *Rondel chinois* (L² 11). Song for voice and piano on Marius Dillard.
- **1881:** *Tragédie* (L² 12). Song for voice and piano on Léon Valade.
- **1881:** *Jane* (L² 13). “Chanson écossaise” for voice and piano on Leconte de Lisle.
- **1881:** *La Fille aux cheveux de lin* (L² 15). Song for voice and piano on Leconte de Lisle, unrelated musically to the later piano prelude bearing the title.
- **1881:** *Fleur des blés* (L² 16). Song for voice and piano on André Girod.
- **1881:** *Rondeau* (L² 17). Song for voice and piano on Musset.
- **1881:** *Souhait* (L² 18). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1881:** *Triolet à Phillis (“Zéphyr”)* (L² 19). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1881:** *Les Papillons* (L² 21). Song for voice and piano on Théophile Gautier.
- **1881:** *L’Archet* (L² 22). Song for voice and piano on Charles Cros.
- **1881:** *Fleur des eaux* (L² 23). Song for voice and piano on Maurice Bouchor.
- **1881:** *Chanson triste* (L² 24). Song for voice and piano on Bouchor.
- **1881:** *Les Elfes* (L² 25). Song for voice and piano on Leconte de Lisle.
- **1882:** *Fantoches* (L² 26). First setting of Verlaine’s poem; Debussy made a substantially revised second setting in *Fêtes galantes*, first series.
- **1882:** *Les Roses* (L² 28). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1882:** *Sérénade* (L² 29). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1882:** *Pierrot* (L² 30). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1882:** *Fête galante* (L² 31). Song for voice and piano on Banville, not the later Verlaine cycles.
- **1882:** *Il dort encore* (L² 34). Surviving voice-and-piano reduction of an aria from the unfinished, otherwise lost opera *Hymnis*.
- **1882:** *Le Lilas* (L² 36). Song for voice and piano on Banville.
- **1882:** *Flots, palmes et sables* (L² 38a–b). Song on Armand Renaud, surviving both with piano and in a voice-piano-harp arrangement.
- **1882:** *En sourdine* (L² 42). First setting of Verlaine’s poem, later recast for *Fêtes galantes*, first series.
- **1882:** *Mandoline* (L² 43). Song for voice and piano on Verlaine.
- **1882:** *Séguidille* (L² 44). Song for voice and piano on Gautier.
- **1882:** *Clair de lune* (L² 45). First setting of Verlaine’s poem, later revised for *Fêtes galantes*; it is not the piano piece in the *Suite bergamasque*.
- **1883:** *Pantomime* (L² 47). Song for voice and piano on Verlaine.
- **1883:** *Coquetterie posthume* (L² 50). Song for voice and piano on Gautier.
- **1883:** *Romance (musique pour éventail)* (L² 53). Song for voice and piano on Paul Bourget.
- **1883:** *Musique* (L² 54). Song for voice and piano on Bourget.
- **1883:** *Paysage sentimental* (L² 55). Song for voice and piano.
- **1884:** *Romance* (L² 56). Song for voice and piano on Bourget.
- **1884:** *Apparition* (L² 57). Song for voice and piano on Stéphane Mallarmé.
- **1884:** *La Romance d’Ariel* (L² 58). Song for voice and piano on Bourget.
- **1884:** *Regret* (L² 59). Song for voice and piano on Bourget.
- **1885–1887:** *Ariettes oubliées* (L² 63a). Cycle of six Verlaine songs for voice and piano: *C’est l’extase langoureuse*, *Il pleure dans mon cœur*, *L’Ombre des arbres*, *Chevaux de bois*, *Green*, and *Spleen*.
- **1885:** *Romance* (L² 65). Bourget setting later published as the first of *Deux Romances*.
- **1885:** *Les Cloches* (L² 66). Bourget setting later published as the second of *Deux Romances*.
- **1885:** *Barcarolle* (L² 67). Lost song on Édouard Guinand.
- **1887–1889:** *Cinq poèmes de Charles Baudelaire* (L² 70). Cycle comprising *Le Balcon*, *Harmonie du soir*, *Le Jet d’eau*, *Recueillement*, and *La Mort des amants*.
- **1890:** *Axel* (L² 73). Song for voice and piano on Villiers de l’Isle-Adam.
- **1890:** *La Belle au bois dormant* (L² 81). Song for voice and piano on Vincent Hyspa.
- **1890–1891:** *Beau soir* (L² 84). Song for voice and piano on Bourget.
- **1891:** *Trois mélodies de Verlaine* (L² 85). Cycle of *La Mer est plus belle*, *Le Son du cor*, and *L’Échelonnement des haies*.
- **1891–1892:** *Fêtes galantes, première série* (L² 86). Three Verlaine songs—*En sourdine*, *Fantoches*, and *Clair de lune*—reworking earlier settings.
- **1892:** *Les Angélus* (L² 88). Song for voice and piano on Grégoire Le Roy.
- **1892–1893:** *Proses lyriques* (L² 90). Four songs—*De rêve*, *De grève*, *De fleurs*, and *De soir*—for which Debussy wrote both words and music.
- **1897–1898:** *Chansons de Bilitis* (L² 97). Three songs on Pierre Louÿs: *La Flûte de Pan*, *La Chevelure*, and *Le Tombeau des Naïades*.
- **1898:** *Nuits blanches* (L² 101). Two songs, *Nuit sans fin* and *Lorsqu’elle est entrée*, with Debussy’s own texts.
- **1903:** *Dans le jardin* (L² 107). Song for voice and piano on Paul Gravollet.
- **Date uncertain:** *Colloque sentimental*. An earlier setting of the Verlaine poem later used in *Fêtes galantes*, second series; its date is not securely fixed.
- **1904:** *Fêtes galantes, deuxième série* (L² 114). Three Verlaine songs: *Les Ingénus*, *Le Faune*, and *Colloque sentimental*.
- **1904:** *Trois Chansons de France* (L² 115). Three songs—two rondels by Charles d’Orléans surrounding Tristan L’Hermite’s *La Grotte*.
- **1904–1910:** *Le Promenoir des deux amants* (L² 129). Three songs on Tristan L’Hermite.
- **1910:** *Trois Ballades de François Villon* (L² 126). Song cycle for voice and piano; Debussy also made an orchestral version.
- **1913:** *Trois poèmes de Stéphane Mallarmé* (L² 135). Cycle comprising *Soupir*, *Placet futile*, and *Éventail*.
- **1915:** *Noël des enfants qui n’ont plus de maison* (L² 147). Wartime song for voice and piano with words by Debussy.

### Vocal ensembles, cantatas, and choral works

- **1881:** *Hélène* fragment (L² 14). Fragment for soprano, mixed chorus, and orchestra from an unfinished opera project.
- **1881:** *Fugue pour le concours de fugue*. Academic four-part choral fugue, placed in the catalogue appendix rather than the principal numbered sequence.
- **1882:** *Chœur des brises* sketch. Preliminary version for soprano, women’s chorus, and piano four hands.
- **1882:** *Chœur des brises* (L² 32). Completed version for soprano and three-part women’s chorus on Louis Bouilhet.
- **1882:** *Daniel* (L² 35). Prix de Rome cantata for three solo voices and orchestra on Émile Cicile.
- **1882:** *Printemps* (L² 37a). Cantata for women’s chorus and orchestra on Anatole de Ségur.
- **1882:** *Printemps*, piano-four-hands version (L² 37b). Reduction of L² 37a whose authorship is questioned in the catalogue.
- **1882:** *Fugue pour le concours de fugue*. Academic four-part choral fugue preserved in the catalogue appendix.
- **1882:** *Fugue pour le concours d’essai*. Four-part choral fugue for the Prix de Rome preliminary competition.
- **1882:** *Églogue* (L² 27). Duet for soprano, tenor, and piano on Leconte de Lisle.
- **1882:** *Ode bachique* (L² 41). Vocal duet with piano, surviving as a reduction from *Hymnis*.
- **1883:** *Chanson espagnole* (L² 49). Duet for two voices and piano on Musset.
- **1883:** *Invocation* (L² 51). Work for male chorus and orchestra on Lamartine.
- **1883:** *Le Gladiateur* (L² 52). Prix de Rome cantata for three voices and orchestra on Émile Moreau.
- **1883:** *Fugue pour le concours de fugue*. Academic four-part choral fugue in the catalogue appendix.
- **1883:** *Fugue pour le concours d’essai*. Incomplete Prix de Rome choral fugue, breaking off after thirty-one measures.
- **1883–1884:** *Diane au bois* duet (L² 48). Fragmentary soprano-tenor duet with piano, one of only two surviving portions of the projected opera *Diane au bois*; revised in 1885.
- **1884:** *Le Printemps* (L² 60). Cantata for soprano, tenor, baritone, chorus, and orchestra on Jules Barbier.
- **1884:** *L’Enfant prodigue* (L² 61). Prix de Rome *scène lyrique* for soprano, tenor, baritone, and orchestra; Debussy revised it in 1907–1908, and André Caplet reorchestrated portions under his supervision.
- **1885:** *Zuléima* (L² 64). Lost choral-orchestral first movement of a projected three-part *Ode symphonique*; the other movements apparently were never made.
- **1887:** *Printemps* (L² 68a). Wordless mixed-chorus and piano-four-hands manifestation of the *Printemps* project.
- **1887:** *Printemps: scènes pour chœur et orchestre* (L² 68c). The supposed original orchestral manifestation is lost and may never have progressed beyond sketches; later “reconstructions” therefore resist being called Debussy’s completed work.
- **1887–1888:** *La Damoiselle élue* (L² 69a). Lyric cantata or short oratorio for female soloists, women’s and children’s voices, and orchestra on Dante Gabriel Rossetti; revised in 1902.
- **1896–1900:** *La Saulaie* (L² 96). Work for baritone and orchestra on Rossetti.
- **1898; revised 1908:** *Trois Chansons de Charles d’Orléans* (L² 99). Three unaccompanied mixed-chorus settings.
- **1906:** *La Damoiselle élue*, voice-and-piano version (L² 69b). Debussy’s chamber-scale arrangement of the cantata.
- **1907:** *Prélude à “L’Histoire de Tristan”*. Unfinished choral-orchestral project, reconstructed by Robert Orledge in 2011; the reconstruction is a later realization, not simply a recovered Debussy work.
- **1910:** *Trois Ballades de François Villon*, orchestral version. Debussy’s concurrent orchestration of L² 126.
- **1916–1917:** *Ode à la France* (L² 149). Unfinished cantata draft for soprano, chorus, and orchestra on Louis Laloy; Marius-François Gaillard supplied posthumous completions in 1928 and 1958.

### Piano works

- **1880:** *Danse bohémienne* (L² 4). Early piano piece.
- **1882:** *Intermède*. Piano transcription of the second movement of the G-major Piano Trio.
- **1890–1891:** *Deux Arabesques* (L² 74). Pair of piano pieces.
- **1890–1891:** *Mazurka* (L² 75). Piano dance.
- **1890:** *Rêverie* (L² 76). Piano piece, distinct from the 1880 song.
- **1890; revised circa 1901:** *Tarantelle styrienne / Danse* (L² 77). Piano piece retitled and revised as *Danse*.
- **1890; revised circa 1901:** *Ballade slave / Ballade* (L² 78). Piano piece whose later revision drops “slave” from the title.
- **1890; revised circa 1901:** *Valse romantique* (L² 79). Piano waltz.
- **1890–1891; revised 1905:** *Suite bergamasque* (L² 82). Four-piece piano suite—*Prélude*, *Menuet*, *Clair de lune*, and *Passepied*—whose last two movements originally bore different titles.
- **1892:** *Nocturne* (L² 89). Piano piece.
- **1894:** *Images oubliées* (L² 94). Three-piece piano manuscript; its *Souvenir du Louvre* became the basis of the later *Sarabande*.
- **1894–1901:** *Pour le piano* (L² 95). Suite comprising *Prélude*, *Sarabande*, and *Toccata*.
- **1901–1905:** *Images, première série* (L² 105). Piano triptych: *Reflets dans l’eau*, *Hommage à Rameau*, and *Mouvement*.
- **1903:** *Estampes* (L² 108). Piano triptych: *Pagodes*, *La Soirée dans Grenade*, and *Jardins sous la pluie*.
- **1903–1904:** *L’Isle joyeuse* (L² 109). Piano piece.
- **1903–1904:** *Masques* (L² 110). Piano piece.
- **1904:** *D’un cahier d’esquisses* (L² 112). Piano piece whose title deliberately presents it as something taken from a sketchbook.
- **1904:** *Pièce pour piano (Morceau de concours)* (L² 117). Miniature written for a “guess the composer” competition in *Musica*.
- **1905:** *Les accords de septième regrettent !!!* Nine-measure piano fragment made as a birthday present for Emma Bardac; its brevity and occasional purpose make it closer to an album-leaf gesture than a conventional composition.
- **1906–1908:** *Children’s Corner* (L² 119). Six-piece piano suite whose French composer gave both collection and movement titles in English.
- **1907:** *Images, deuxième série* (L² 120). Piano triptych: *Cloches à travers les feuilles*, *Et la lune descend sur le temple qui fut*, and *Poissons d’or*.
- **1909:** *Le Petit Nègre* (L² 122). Piano piece also published as *Cake-Walk*.
- **1909:** *Hommage à Haydn* (L² 123). Piano contribution to the collective six-composer project *Hommage à Joseph Haydn*.
- **1909–1910:** *Préludes, livre I* (L² 125). Twelve piano preludes: *Danseuses de Delphes*, *Voiles*, *Le Vent dans la plaine*, *Les Sons et les parfums tournent dans l’air du soir*, *Les Collines d’Anacapri*, *Des pas sur la neige*, *Ce qu’a vu le vent d’Ouest*, *La Fille aux cheveux de lin*, *La Sérénade interrompue*, *La Cathédrale engloutie*, *La Danse de Puck*, and *Minstrels*.
- **1910:** *La Plus que lente* (L² 128). Piano waltz; Debussy also authorized an orchestral manifestation.
- **1912–1913:** *Préludes, livre II* (L² 131). Twelve piano preludes: *Brouillards*, *Feuilles mortes*, *La Puerta del Vino*, *Les Fées sont d’exquises danseuses*, *Bruyères*, *General Lavine—eccentric*, *La Terrasse des audiences du clair de lune*, *Ondine*, *Hommage à S. Pickwick Esq. P.P.M.P.C.*, *Canope*, *Les Tierces alternées*, and *Feux d’artifice*.
- **1914:** *Berceuse héroïque* (L² 140a). Piano memorial piece, later orchestrated by Debussy.
- **1915:** *Six Épigraphes antiques*, piano-solo version. Arrangement of the piano-four-hands cycle derived from the *Chansons de Bilitis* stage music.
- **1915:** *Pièce pour l’Œuvre du vêtement du blessé (Page d’album)* (L² 141). Fundraising piano album leaf for wounded soldiers.
- **1915:** *Douze Études* (L² 143). Two books of six piano studies addressing fingers, intervals, figurations, repeated notes, contrasting sonorities, arpeggios, and chords.
- **1915:** *Étude pour les arpèges composés* (L² 143a). Alternative, musically unrelated version of the eleventh étude; discovered in 1977 and published as *Étude retrouvée*.
- **1915:** *Élégie* (L² 146). Piano piece.
- **1917:** *Les Soirs illuminés par l’ardeur du charbon* (L² 150). Debussy’s last known piano piece, discovered in 2001 and published in 2003.
- **Date unknown:** *Rapsodie in the Style of Liszt*. Lost piano piece; no secure date survives.

### Piano duet and two-piano works

- **1880–1881:** *Symphonie en si mineur* (L² 8). Piano-four-hands symphony; published posthumously, and therefore not evidence of a completed Debussy orchestration.
- **1881:** *Andante cantabile et Tempo di marcia funebre* (L² 10). Piano-four-hands work whose second movement survives in only sixteen measures.
- **1881:** *Diane*, ouverture (L² 20). Piano-four-hands overture, one of the two surviving components of the unfinished opera *Diane au bois*.
- **1882:** *Le Triomphe de Bacchus* (L² 33a). Four-movement piano-four-hands form of an orchestral suite; its third movement is lost and the fourth survives only in fragments.
- **1882:** *Intermezzo* (L² 40c). Piano-four-hands arrangement of the cello-and-orchestra piece.
- **1883:** *Première Suite d’orchestre* (L² 46a). Piano-four-hands reduction of the orchestral suite.
- **Date uncertain:** *L’Enfant prodigue: Prélude, Cortège et Air de danse*. Piano-four-hands transcription of instrumental portions of the cantata.
- **1884:** *Divertissement* (L² 62). Work for piano four hands.
- **1888–1889:** *Petite Suite* (L² 71a). Four-movement piano-duet suite: *En bateau*, *Cortège*, *Menuet*, and *Ballet*.
- **1890:** *Marche écossaise sur un thème populaire* (L² 83a). Original piano-four-hands manifestation of the march.
- **1895:** *Prélude à l’après-midi d’un faune* (L² 87b). Debussy’s two-piano version of his orchestral prelude.
- **1901:** *Lindaraja* (L² 103). Work for two pianos.
- **1904:** *Danses*, two-piano version. Arrangement of *Danse sacrée* and *Danse profane*.
- **1914–1915:** *Six Épigraphes antiques* (L² 139). Piano-four-hands cycle reworking six numbers from the *Chansons de Bilitis* stage music.
- **1915:** *En blanc et noir* (L² 142). Three-part work for two pianos, initially titled *Caprices en blanc et noir*.

### Chamber and solo-instrument works

- **1880:** *Trio en sol majeur* (L² 5). Four-movement work for piano, violin, and cello; rediscovered in 1982 and published in 1986.
- **1882:** *Nocturne et Scherzo* for violin and piano. Lost work.
- **1882:** *Nocturne et Scherzo* for cello and piano (L² 39). Arrangement of the lost violin version; only the scherzo survives.
- **1882:** *Intermezzo* for cello and piano (L² 40b). Arrangement of the cello-and-orchestra work.
- **1892–1893:** *Quatuor à cordes en sol mineur* (L² 91; op. 10). Four-movement string quartet and the rare Debussy composition issued with an opus number.
- **1901–1911:** *Rapsodie* (L² 104). Work for alto saxophone and piano; its unusually long gestation and later orchestration by another composer complicate the idea of a single definitive manifestation.
- **1909–1910:** *Première Rhapsodie* (L² 124a). Work for clarinet and piano.
- **1910:** *Petite pièce* (L² 127). Clarinet-and-piano sight-reading piece for the Paris Conservatoire examinations.
- **1913:** *Syrinx* (L² 137). Unaccompanied flute piece originating as incidental music for Gabriel Mourey’s *Psyché*.
- **1915:** *Sonate pour violoncelle et piano* (L² 144). First of the projected six sonatas for diverse instruments.
- **1915:** *Sonate pour flûte, alto et harpe* (L² 145). Second completed work in the six-sonata project.
- **1916–1917:** *Sonate pour violon et piano* (L² 148). Third and final completed sonata; the intended six-work project remained unfinished at Debussy’s death.

### Concertante works

- **1882:** *Intermezzo* (L² 40a). Work for cello and orchestra, also arranged by Debussy for cello and piano and for piano four hands.
- **1889–1890:** *Fantaisie pour piano et orchestre* (L² 72). Concertante work withdrawn before its planned première; Debussy continued revising it, leaving questions about which state should count as definitive.
- **1894–1896:** *Trois Nocturnes* for violin and orchestra. Lost intermediate manifestation between *Trois Scènes au crépuscule* and the orchestral *Nocturnes*.
- **1904:** *Danse sacrée et Danse profane* (L² 113). Pair for chromatic harp and string orchestra, with the solo part also authorized for pedal harp or piano.
- **1911:** *Première Rhapsodie* (L² 124b). Debussy’s orchestration of the clarinet-and-piano work.
- **1919:** *Rapsodie* for alto saxophone and orchestra. Jean Roger-Ducasse’s posthumous orchestration of L² 104; this is a manifestation of Debussy’s project but not an orchestration he made himself.

### Orchestral works and orchestrations

- **1882:** *Le Triomphe de Bacchus*, orchestral suite (L² 33b). Lost orchestral work; Marius-François Gaillard’s 1927 orchestration from surviving piano-duet movements is a reconstruction, not the lost Debussy score.
- **1883:** *Première Suite d’orchestre* (L² 46b). Four-movement orchestral suite; the third movement’s original orchestration is lost.
- **1891–1894:** *Prélude à l’après-midi d’un faune* (L² 87a). Orchestral prelude after Mallarmé.
- **1892–1893:** *Trois Scènes au crépuscule* (L² 92). Lost first orchestral state of the project that ultimately became *Nocturnes*.
- **1893–1908:** *Marche écossaise sur un thème populaire* (L² 83b). Orchestration of the piano-duet march, revised over an extended period.
- **1897:** *Première et Troisième Gymnopédies de Satie*. Debussy’s orchestrations of Satie’s first and third piano pieces, published and performed in reversed order.
- **1897–1899:** *Nocturnes* (L² 98). Orchestral triptych—*Nuages*, *Fêtes*, and *Sirènes*, the last with women’s chorus—representing the surviving culmination of several earlier nocturne projects.
- **1903–1905; revised 1908:** *La Mer: trois esquisses symphoniques* (L² 111). Orchestral triptych: *De l’aube à midi sur la mer*, *Jeux de vagues*, and *Dialogue du vent et de la mer*.
- **1905–1912:** *Images pour orchestre* (L² 118). Three-part orchestral cycle—*Gigues*, *Ibéria*, and *Rondes de printemps*—whose components were composed in a different order from their final arrangement.
- **1910:** *La Plus que lente*, orchestral version. Debussy’s arrangement of the piano waltz.
- **1912:** *Ariettes oubliées*, orchestral suite (L² 63b). André Caplet’s orchestration of two songs under Debussy’s supervision; collaborative manifestation rather than Debussy’s unaided orchestral work.
- **1912:** *Printemps*, orchestral version (L² 68b). Henri Büsser’s orchestration under Debussy’s supervision, made because the supposed original orchestral score did not survive.
- **1914:** *Berceuse héroïque* (L² 140b). Debussy’s orchestration of the piano piece.
- **1907:** *Petite Suite*, orchestral version. Henri Büsser’s orchestration under Debussy’s supervision.
- **1910:** *Children’s Corner*, orchestral version. André Caplet’s orchestration under Debussy’s supervision.
- **1911:** *Le Martyre de saint Sébastien: Fragments symphoniques*. Caplet’s concert suite from the stage score, prepared under Debussy’s supervision.

### Operas and operatic projects

- **1881:** *Hélène*. Unfinished opera project after Leconte de Lisle; only a fragment for soprano, chorus, and orchestra survives.
- **1881–1886:** *Diane au bois*. Unfinished and otherwise lost opera after Banville; only the piano-duet overture and fragments of a vocal duet survive.
- **1882:** *Hymnis*. Unfinished, lost opera project after Banville; only *Il dort encore* and *Ode bachique* survive in piano reductions.
- **1885:** *Salammbô*. Opera project after Flaubert surviving only in sketches.
- **1890–1893:** *Rodrigue et Chimène* (L² 80). Unfinished three-act opera on Catulle Mendès; two scenes are lost and the orchestration remained largely sketched. Richard Langham Smith reconstructed the text in 1987 and Edison Denisov orchestrated it in 1993, neither result being simply Debussy’s completed opera.
- **1893–1902:** *Pelléas et Mélisande* (L² 93). Five-act opera on Maurice Maeterlinck, and Debussy’s only completed opera.
- **1902–1911:** *Le Diable dans le beffroi* (L² 106). Unfinished short opera after Edgar Allan Poe, with a libretto by Debussy.
- **1908–1917:** *La Chute de la maison Usher* (L² 121). Unfinished opera after Poe, with Debussy’s own text; Juan Allende-Blin and Robert Orledge produced materially different posthumous completions.
- **1912–1915:** *Fêtes galantes / Crimen amoris* (L² 134). Projected opera-ballet after Verlaine, with Louis Laloy and Charles Morice; it is unfinished, lost, and may never have been musically written, so it resists classification as an extant work.

### Ballets, theatre music, and multimedia works

- **1899:** *La Tragédie de la mort* (L² 100). Unfinished incidental music for René Peter’s play; only an unaccompanied solo-voice berceuse survives.
- **1900–1901:** *Musique de scène pour Les Chansons de Bilitis* (L² 102). Twelve pieces for narrator, two flutes, two harps, and celesta on Pierre Louÿs; a spoken, theatrical chamber *Gesamtkunstwerk* rather than merely a song cycle.
- **1904:** *Le Roi Lear* (L² 116). Unfinished incidental-music project with seven sketched sections; Jean Roger-Ducasse completed and orchestrated two in 1926.
- **1911:** *Le Martyre de saint Sébastien* (L² 130). Five-act “mystère” joining D’Annunzio’s drama, choreography, voices, chorus, and orchestra; André Caplet collaborated on the orchestration. Its refusal to separate theatre, ritual, dance, and concert music makes *Gesamtkunstwerk* an apt category.
- **1911:** *Le Martyre de saint Sébastien*, vocal score. Piano reduction made collaboratively by Debussy and Caplet; a practical manifestation of the stage work rather than a separate dramatic conception.
- **1911–1912:** *Khamma* (L² 132b). Original piano score of a danced legend, first titled *Isis*.
- **1912–1913:** *Khamma*, orchestral score (L² 132a). Debussy left the orchestration unfinished; Charles Koechlin completed it in 1913.
- **1912–1913:** *Jeux* (L² 133). One-act orchestral *poème dansé* made for the Ballets Russes.
- **1913:** *La Boîte à joujoux* (L² 136a). Piano score for a children’s ballet conceived with illustrations and scenario, making it more than an autonomous piano suite.
- **1913:** *Psyché*. Projected incidental music for Gabriel Mourey’s play; only the flute piece later titled *Syrinx* survives, and the remaining music may never have been written.
- **1914:** *No-Ja-Li ou Le Palais du silence* (L² 138). Unfinished Chinese-ballet project; Robert Orledge’s twenty-first-century reconstruction is a later realization of fragmentary material.
- **1919:** *La Boîte à joujoux*, orchestral score (L² 136b). Posthumous orchestration by André Caplet following Debussy’s indications; it manifests Debussy’s ballet project but is not wholly his made object.

### Arrangements, supervised manifestations, and doubtful boundaries of authorship

- **1893–1902:** *Pelléas et Mélisande*, voice-and-piano score. Piano reduction associated with the opera’s preparation and publication; it is a functional manifestation of L² 93, not another opera.
- **1904:** *Danses*, two-piano arrangement. Keyboard manifestation of *Danse sacrée et Danse profane*.
- **1907–1908:** *L’Enfant prodigue*, revision. Debussy substantially revisited the 1884 cantata; Caplet’s additional reorchestration was made under his supervision.
- **1926–1958:** Completions and reconstructions of *Le Roi Lear*, *Ode à la France*, and other fragments. These derive from Debussy’s projects but were made after his death by Roger-Ducasse, Gaillard, and others; they should not be silently counted as wholly authored Debussy works.

### Prose and compositional projects beyond the surviving catalogue

- **1901–1914:** *Monsieur Croche* writings. Debussy’s body of criticism, reviews, dialogues, and musical journalism—some signed “Monsieur Croche”—is a literary œuvre rather than a musical composition; later book collections reorganize periodical texts and should not be mistaken for books conceived in those collected forms.
- **1915:** *Six Sonates pour divers instruments*. Announced compositional project of six sonatas “by Claude Debussy, musicien français”; only the cello sonata, flute-viola-harp sonata, and violin sonata were completed. The project itself remains part of what he made because its incompleteness was historical, not accidental cataloguing.
- **Throughout his career:** Sketchbooks, corrected proofs, piano reductions, orchestrational drafts, and abandoned scenarios. These survive unevenly in archives and the critical edition; many resist treatment as independent “works,” but they document projects and alternative states rather than disposable non-works.
