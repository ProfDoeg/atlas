## Works by Giambattista Vico

### Poems, epigrams, inscriptions, and emblematic compositions

- **1692; published 1693:** *Affetti di un disperato*. A philosophical canzone—Vico’s earliest known publication—rather than a treatise, written in the voice of a despairing speaker and shaped by Lucretian themes. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vico/)
- **1693:** *Canzone in morte di Antonio Carafa*. A vernacular memorial ode published with Vico’s early poetry. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vico/)
- **1696:** *Discorso proemiale* for the miscellany marking the viceroy Francisco de Benavides’s departure from Naples. A commissioned prefatory performance embedded in a collective celebratory book, not an autonomous “book by Vico.” [Treccani, *Enciclopedia Italiana*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/)
- **1697:** *Per il ritorno in Ispagna di don Francesco Benavides*. A Latin occasional distich: a very small made thing whose original function was ceremonial rather than literary autonomy. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/minora/60)
- **Late 1690s–1730s:** *Italian poems*. A dispersed body of sonnets, canzoni, and other verse made largely for deaths, marriages, professions of religious vows, and public celebrations. The individual pieces were not conceived as a single authored book, so the corpus resists being treated as one work; the national critical-edition plan nevertheless recognizes *Poesie* as a distinct editorial corpus. [CNR critical-edition plan](https://www.cnr.it/en/focus/085-3/vico-s-works-critical-edition)
- **Late 1690s–1730s:** *Le iscrizioni e le composizioni latine*. A modern collective title for Vico’s surviving Latin inscriptions, wedding epigrams, occasional verses, and related compositions, some originally joined to allegorical images. It is a mixed-media corpus—verbal inscription plus visual apparatus—not a book Vico himself designed under this title. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/le-iscrizioni-e-le-composizioni-latine/309)

### Ceremonial, inaugural, and funeral orations

- **1697:** *In funere Catharinae Aragoniae*. A Latin funeral oration for Caterina d’Aragona, mother of the viceroy Luis de la Cerda; it was commissioned ceremonial eloquence and published in Vico’s lifetime. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/minora/60)
- **1699:** *Oratio inauguralis I*. The first of six Latin university inaugurals: a spoken academic act subsequently preserved as a text. The six were revised but not printed until 1869. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **1700:** *Oratio inauguralis II*. The second inaugural oration, made for the opening of the academic year and transmitted with the other five early prolusions. [Bibliotheca Augustana](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost18/Vico/vic_intr.html)
- **1702:** *Oratio inauguralis III*. The third inaugural oration, a university performance as well as a philosophical text. [Bibliotheca Augustana](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost18/Vico/vic_intr.html)
- **1704–1705:** *Oratio inauguralis IV*. The fourth inaugural oration; catalogues commonly date it 1705, while accounts that group the six as 1699–1706 expose some uncertainty between academic-year and calendar-year dating. [Bibliotheca Augustana](https://www.tha.de/~harsch/Chronologia/Lspost18/Vico/vic_intr.html)
- **1705–1706:** *Oratio inauguralis V*. The fifth inaugural oration; its date likewise varies with the convention used for the academic year. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **1706–1707:** *Oratio inauguralis VI*. The last of the six unpublished early inaugurals; sources variously label it 1706 or 1707. [CNR critical-edition plan](https://www.cnr.it/en/focus/085-3/vico-s-works-critical-edition)
- **1707:** *In funere Caroli de Sangro et Iosephi Capece*. A Latin funeral composition for Carlo di Sangro and Giuseppe Capece, made for a particular public rite. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/minora/60)
- **Delivered 1708; published 1709:** *De nostri temporis studiorum ratione*. Vico’s seventh inaugural oration, enlarged for print into a comparative manifesto for joining the strengths of ancient and modern education; the spoken and printed manifestations are therefore not quite identical. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/de-nostri-temporis-studiorum-ratione/11439)
- **1719:** *De divinarum atque humanarum rerum notitia*. An inaugural oration announcing the organizing idea of the future *Diritto universale*. It survives principally as the programmatic threshold of that larger project rather than as an independent finished book. [Critical edition of the *Diritto universale*](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/diritto-universale/8524)
- **1724:** *Oratio in funere Annae ab Aspermont*. A funeral oration for Anna d’Aspremont, countess of Althann and mother of the viceroy; ceremonial commemoration expands into political history of the War of the Spanish Succession. [Treccani, *Enciclopedia Italiana*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/)
- **1727:** *Orazione in morte di Angela Cimmino, marchesa della Perrella*. An Italian funeral oration combining lament, moral psychology, and Christian consolation; it is both social performance and literary prose. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1732:** *De mente heroica*. An inaugural address to students arguing for an intellectually and morally “heroic” education. Though usually printed among the *Varia*, it is a purposeful public exhortation—close to a manifesto—rather than a descriptive treatise. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/varia/107)
- **1734:** *Allocutio ad Carolum Borbonium*. A Latin ceremonial address to Charles of Bourbon, made for a dynastic and political occasion. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/minora/60)

### Histories, biographies, and political commissions

- **1701–1702; first published 1835:** *De Parthenopea coniuratione* / *Principum Neapolitanorum coniurationis anni MDCCI historia*. A Latin history of the Macchia conspiracy. It survives in two substantially different redactions—one represented by nine contaminated manuscript witnesses, the other by a manuscript with Vico’s corrections—so there is no single uncontested final text. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/la-congiura-dei-principi-napoletani/649)
- **1702:** *Panegiricus Philippo V Hispaniarum regi*. A Latin panegyric welcoming Philip V to Naples: commissioned political eloquence whose performative praise matters as much as its historical assertions. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/minora/60)
- **1713–1715; published 1716:** *De rebus gestis Antonii Caraphaei libri quattuor*. A four-book Latin life and military history of Marshal Antonio Carafa, commissioned by his nephew Adriano Carafa. It is simultaneously biography, historiography, and a prestige object made for a patron. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **1697–1734:** *Minora: scritti latini storici e d’occasione*. This is the critical edition’s collective category for smaller historical and ceremonial productions—including the Benavides distich, the Caterina d’Aragona and Sangro–Capece commemorations, the Philip V panegyric, and the address to Charles of Bourbon—not a unified work created by Vico under the title *Minora*. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/minora/60)

### Philosophy, philology, and polemic

- **1710:** *De antiquissima Italorum sapientia ex linguae Latinae originibus eruenda, liber primus sive metaphysicus*. A philosophical-philological treatise attempting to recover ancient Italian wisdom from Latin etymology. It is also the surviving first manifestation of a much larger three-book project that Vico did not complete. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vico/)
- **1711:** *Risposta del signor Giambattista Vico nella quale si sciogliono tre opposizioni fatte da dotto signore contro il primo libro “De antiquissima Italorum sapientia”*. A polemical reply to an anonymous review, accompanied by a summary of the *Liber metaphysicus*; it acts defensively within a live controversy rather than merely explaining the earlier book. [Treccani, *Enciclopedia dell’Italiano*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/)
- **1712:** *Seconda risposta del signor Giambattista Vico*. A second polemical answer to objections published in the *Giornale de’ letterati d’Italia*, further revising and clarifying the philosophical project. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vico/)
- **1729:** *Vici Vindiciae*. A rapid, combative reply to the hostile notice of the 1725 *Scienza nuova* in the Leipzig *Acta eruditorum*. The work is lost, so its status and contents must be reconstructed from Vico’s testimony and later references rather than from an extant text. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **Various dates:** *Varia: il “De mente heroica” e gli scritti latini minori*. A scholarly corpus comprising *De mente heroica* and five shorter Latin writings. *Varia* is an editorial obra, not evidence that Vico conceived those heterogeneous pieces as one work. [Critical-edition description](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/varia/107)

### Jurisprudential project

- **1720:** *Sinopsi del diritto universale*. A separately circulated epitome or manifesto announcing the scope and architecture of Vico’s projected universal jurisprudence. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/diritto-universale/8524)
- **1720:** *De uno universi iuris principio et fine uno*. The first major book of the *Diritto universale*, seeking a single divine origin and end for law. It is an autonomous printed treatise and also one component of a larger project. [Treccani, *Enciclopedia dell’Italiano*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/)
- **1721:** *De constantia iurisprudentis*. The second principal book of the *Diritto universale*, joining philosophy and philology in an account of the constancy of jurisprudence. [Treccani, *Enciclopedia dell’Italiano*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Enciclopedia-dell%27Italiano%29/)
- **1722:** *Notae in duos libros*. An extensive body of notes supplementing and revising the two books of the *Diritto universale*. They are neither merely private annotations nor a wholly independent treatise, but a third published layer of the project. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **1719–1722:** *Il diritto universale*. The collective obra formed by the inaugural program, *Sinopsi*, two principal books, and *Notae*. Calling it one “book” conceals its serial, revisable construction across several distinct printed objects. [CNR critical-edition plan](https://www.cnr.it/en/focus/085-3/vico-s-works-critical-edition)

### The New Science as a continuing project

- **1723–1725, lost:** *Scienza nuova in forma negativa*. The conventional—not authorially fixed—title for an approximately thousand-page first construction organized around a destructive or “negative” argument. Vico completed but abandoned it when publication became financially impossible, then radically compressed the project; because the manuscript is lost, it resists treatment as a recoverable text even though it was unquestionably a major work of making. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1725:** *Principj di una scienza nuova d’intorno alla natura delle nazioni*. The first published *Scienza nuova*, rewritten and drastically condensed after the larger project proved unaffordable. It is a distinct version, not merely an early printing of the later book; modern textual study has also identified press variants among surviving copies. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/la-scienza-nuova-1725/16714)
- **1728–1729, unfinished:** *Annotazioni alla Scienza nuova del 1725*. Roughly six hundred pages of additions made in preparation for a proposed Venetian reissue. The publishing project collapsed, and the material became an overgrown intermediate manifestation rather than a finished edition. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1729–1730, withdrawn/lost:** *Novella letteraria*. A prefatory account prepared for the second *Scienza nuova*, probably concerning the failed Venetian publication negotiations. Vico removed it in favor of the allegorical frontispiece and its explanation; its text is not securely recoverable. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1730:** *Cinque libri di Giambattista Vico de’ principj d’una scienza nuova d’intorno alla comune natura delle nazioni*. The second *Scienza nuova*, a thorough recomposition rather than a simple revision. Its obra includes an allegorical “dipintura,” explanatory verbal key, five books, and conclusion—an emblematic text-image apparatus approaching a small philosophical Gesamtkunstwerk. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/la-scienza-nuova-1730/310)
- **1730–1734:** *Correzioni, miglioramenti ed aggiunte alla Scienza nuova 1730*. Four successive series of corrections and additions, some printed separately and others surviving in manuscripts. They are moving strata of one continuing project, and the critical edition prints them in sequence rather than silently dissolving them into a supposed final text. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/la-scienza-nuova-1730/310)
- **1731–1743:** *Postille e redazioni preparatorie della Scienza nuova*. Autograph annotations spread across multiple corrected copies and the manuscript now identified as Naples, Biblioteca Nazionale, XIII D 79. These materials are workshop manifestations of the final project, not independent titled works. [Critical edition](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/catalogo/la-scienza-nuova-1744/813)
- **Prepared through 1743; published posthumously 1744:** *Principj di scienza nuova d’intorno alla comune natura delle nazioni*. The third *Scienza nuova*, issued after Vico’s death but based on his final sustained rewriting; he saw proofs and may have corrected some of them, although the exact extent of his last intervention is uncertain. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)

### Teaching texts and scholarly protocols

- **1711–1741:** *Institutiones oratoriae*. A rhetoric course repeatedly dictated and revised during Vico’s teaching career, surviving through manuscripts and student transcriptions rather than an author-published edition. It is best classified as a pedagogical protocol—a text for making orators—and its reconstructed critical text represents several chronological states. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vico/)
- **Date uncertain:** *Commento all’Epistola ai Pisoni*. A teaching commentary on Horace’s *Ars poetica*, associated with Vico’s university instruction and surviving outside his major author-published books. Its dating and textual autonomy are less secure than those of the principal works. [Treccani, *Enciclopedia Cattolica* entry](https://azioneromana.com/it/article/vico-giovanni-battista)

### Autobiographical and epistolary works

- **1725–1728:** *Vita di Giambattista Vico scritta da se medesimo*. An intellectual autobiography written for a collective publishing project and brought up to date while awaiting publication in 1728–1729. It is a deliberately fashioned account of an authorial life, not a neutral documentary record. [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1731; published 1818:** *Aggiunta alla Vita*. A substantial continuation of the autobiography, made at Muratori’s invitation and published only posthumously. It is both a separate textual object and the second part of the composite work now called the *Vita*. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **Surviving correspondence, various dates:** *Epistole*. A corpus of letters, not a single work planned by Vico; individual letters range from social and professional communications to compact critical essays. The standard critical edition distinguishes Vico’s letters from those of his correspondents and therefore also records a dialogic archive he did not make alone. [Critical-edition series](https://www.storiaeletteratura.it/collane/opere-di-giambattista-vico/187)
- **1725–1728:** *Four critical letters to Gherardo degli Angioli, the abbé Esperti, Claude-Joseph de Vitry, and Francesco Saverio Estevan*. These letters function as essays on poetry, Dante, European learning, and Cartesianism while remaining acts of correspondence; their resistance to the category “work” is part of their form. [Treccani, *Enciclopedia Italiana*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giambattista-vico_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/)

### Unfinished, lost, or only projected works

- **1710 and after, unfinished:** *De antiquissima Italorum sapientia, libri tres*. The intended project comprised metaphysics with an appendix on logic, physics, and ethics; only the metaphysical first book appeared. The title names both an extant book and a larger obra that never came into full existence. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **By 1713; lost:** *De aequilibrio corporis animantis*. A short physical treatise made from material intended for the second book of *De antiquissima*. Both manuscript and reported posthumous printing are lost, and even the circumstances of that printing remain uncertain. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
- **Before 1720, largely lost:** *Early drafts toward the Scienza nuova and Diritto universale*. These included annotations on Grotius and a three-book treatment preceding the surviving *Diritto universale*. Only fragments or indirect testimony remain, so this is a project-level entry rather than a falsely reconstructed title. [Fondazione Giambattista Vico](https://www.fondazionegbvico.it/Le-opere.htm)
