## Works by Pytheas de Massalia

### Lost geographical and astronomical obra

- **Late fourth century BCE (date uncertain):** *On the Ocean* (*Ta peri tou Okeanou*, “Matters concerning the Ocean”). A lost prose account combining travel narrative, coastal geography, ethnography, and astronomical observations; it survives only through one possible direct sentence and reports or paraphrases by later authors. The alternative descriptions *Circuit of the Earth* (*Periodos gēs*) and *Periplus* may be titles for this same work rather than separate compositions, so they should not be counted independently; even the extent of the recoverable corpus is disputed, with six of the thirty-nine references in Lionel Scott’s edition considered doubtful ([BMCR](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2022/2022.08.14/); [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/550489420)).

### Sources

- Christina Horst Roseman, ed. and trans., *Pytheas of Massalia: On the Ocean: Text, Translation and Commentary* (Ares Publishers, 1994), the standard collected edition of the testimonia and fragments ([bibliographic record and contents](https://books.google.com/books/about/On_the_Ocean.html?id=QQdgAAAAMAAJ)).
- Lionel Scott, *Pytheas of Massalia: Texts, Translation, and Commentary* (Routledge, 2022), especially the thirty-nine collected references and appendices on Pytheas’s astronomical contributions and the alleged Massiliote *Periplus*, consulted through José Leonardo Sousa Buzelli’s scholarly review ([Bryn Mawr Classical Review 2022.08.14](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2022/2022.08.14/)).
- Eric Herbert Warmington and Antony Spawforth, “Pytheas,” *Oxford Classical Dictionary*, identifying him as the author of the lost *About the Ocean* and summarizing its geographical and astronomical content ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/550489420)).
