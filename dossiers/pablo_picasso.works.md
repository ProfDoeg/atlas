## Works by Pablo Ruiz Picasso

Picasso made tens of thousands of objects across media. A complete object-by-object transcription would require tens of thousands of bullets: Christian Zervos alone records more than 16,000 paintings and drawings, while separate catalogues raisonnés cover prints, sculpture, ceramics, illustrated books, and posters. The entries below therefore identify the complete catalogue-defined bodies of work—the *obra* in the sense permitted by the brief—and separately name projects and creations that cross or resist those catalogue boundaries. Dates and titles should be taken from the cited catalogues rather than normalized from later editions or museum labels.

### Painted and drawn obra

- **1892–1972:** *Paintings and drawings catalogued by Christian Zervos*. This is the principal catalogue-defined *obra*: more than 16,000 paintings, gouaches, watercolours, pastels, collages, and drawings in thirty-three volumes, including supplements that revise the chronology. It is extensive rather than demonstrably complete; Zervos died before the last volumes appeared, and later discoveries, disputed attributions, and works omitted from his survey remain outside it. ([Cahiers d’Art](https://cahiersdart.com/fr/collections/zervos); [BnF catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb44472907j); [INHA bibliographic record](https://agorha.inha.fr/ark:/54721/9b5d95ee-aba9-4f4f-8935-7ed52326b145))

- **1900–1906:** *Painted oeuvre catalogued by Pierre Daix and Georges Boudaille*. This early-period catalogue is an alternative scholarly enumeration rather than a separate set of objects; its attributions and dates do not always coincide with Zervos. The Picasso Administration still asks claimants to supply Daix references independently of Zervos references. ([Picasso Administration](https://picasso.fr/?lang=en); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

- **1907–1916:** *Cubist painted oeuvre catalogued by Pierre Daix and Joan Rosselet*. This is another overlapping catalogue-defined body, especially important for works whose titles, dates, and distinctions between painting, collage, and constructed image have changed in the literature. It must not be added numerically to Zervos as though it described different objects. ([Picasso Administration](https://picasso.fr/?lang=en); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

- **1889–1972:** *Works on paper and paintings indexed by the On-line Picasso Project*. This continuing digital catalogue gathers dated and cross-referenced records, including Zervos numbers and works absent from Zervos. It is a research project rather than a closed posthumous canon, so its contents and attributions can change. ([On-line Picasso Project](https://picasso.shsu.edu/index.php); [INHA assessment](https://www.inha.fr/actualites/des-catalogues-raisonnes-en-ligne/))

- **1907:** *Les Demoiselles d’Avignon*. Oil painting and sustained project of preparatory drawings whose working title and eventual museum title differ; isolating only the canvas conceals the project’s extended, serial character. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/79766); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1912:** *Nature morte à la chaise cannée* (*Still Life with Chair Caning*). Painting-collage incorporating printed oilcloth and rope; it resists classification as painting alone because its material construction is part of the image. ([Musée Picasso collection](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/collection/); [Met Heilbrunn Timeline](https://www.metmuseum.org/toah/hd/cube/hd_cube.htm))

- **1937:** *Guernica*. Monumental painting made for the Spanish Republic’s pavilion at the Paris International Exposition, surrounded by studies and photographed stages that make it both an object and a political-exhibition project. The preparatory works are separate catalogue entries, not merely “versions” of the finished canvas. ([Museo Reina Sofía](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/collection/artwork/guernica); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1944–1945:** *Le Charnier* (*The Charnel House*). Oil-and-charcoal painting left visibly unresolved; its unfinished appearance is material evidence of process, not grounds for excluding it as a work. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/78752); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1957:** *Las Meninas* series. A sequence of reinterpretive paintings after Velázquez, made as a sustained serial inquiry rather than one composition with subsidiary studies. The Museu Picasso Barcelona preserves the group as a donated ensemble. ([Museu Picasso Barcelona](https://museupicassobcn.cat/en/collection/las-meninas); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1968:** *347 Suite*. A deliberately serial body of etchings whose individual plates are separately catalogued; “the suite” is also an *obra* because publication and exhibition bound the images into a named whole. ([National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/collection/artist-info.1785.html); [Rijksmuseum catalogue record for Baer](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/publication/Picasso-peintre-graveur--db5abc4773755732490ce224957d072f))

### Printed obra

- **1899–1972:** *Picasso peintre-graveur*. Brigitte Baer’s catalogue raisonné, continuing Bernhard Geiser’s work, enumerates Picasso’s etchings, engravings, drypoints, aquatints, lithographs, linocuts, monotypes, and related states. A “thing” here may be a plate, a state, a proof, or an edition; those are not interchangeable categories, and a printed impression is not automatically a separately authored composition. ([Rijksmuseum](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/publication/Picasso-peintre-graveur--db5abc4773755732490ce224957d072f); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

- **1904–1967:** *Catalogue de l’œuvre gravé et lithographié*. Georges Bloch’s catalogue is a second standard numbering system for the graphic *obra*. Its entries overlap Baer’s rather than constituting additional works, and Baer’s distinctions among states are often more granular. ([Ricker Library bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/arx/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2017/06/CataloguesRaisonnes_P.pdf); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

- **1930–1937:** *Suite Vollard*. One hundred intaglio plates commissioned by Ambroise Vollard; the suite is a publication project, while each plate and each printed state remains a distinct material manifestation. ([National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/features/slideshows/picassos-vollard-suite.html); [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/BIOG41907))

- **1937:** *Sueño y mentira de Franco* (*The Dream and Lie of Franco*). Two etched plates divided into postcard-like scenes, issued with Picasso’s accompanying text to raise funds for the Spanish Republican cause. It is simultaneously print sequence, political intervention, and manifesto-like object. ([Museo Reina Sofía](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/en/collection/artwork/sueno-y-mentira-franco-dream-and-lie-franco); [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/BIOG41907))

### Sculpture, constructions, and assembled objects

- **1902–1972:** *Picasso’s sculptures catalogued by Werner Spies*. This catalogue raisonné covers modeled, carved, constructed, assembled, and cast objects; revised editions altered the corpus as works and documentation emerged. Attribution is especially consequential where an original assemblage, plaster, authorized bronze cast, and posthumous cast are different manifestations. ([Picasso Administration](https://picasso.fr/?lang=en); [Ricker Library bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/arx/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2017/06/CataloguesRaisonnes_P.pdf))

- **1912:** *Guitare* (*Guitar*). A cardboard construction followed by sheet-metal manifestations; it behaves as a project or proposition for constructing space as much as a conventional sculpture. Different material realizations should not be silently collapsed into a single object. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/80934); [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/500060))

- **1914:** *Verre d’absinthe* (*Glass of Absinthe*). Six painted bronze casts incorporating an actual absinthe spoon; each was individually painted, so the edition consists of related but materially non-identical manifestations. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81547); [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/500061))

- **1928:** *Projet pour un monument à Guillaume Apollinaire*. Wire-and-sheet-metal construction made during Picasso’s unsuccessful monument commission; it is properly a project even though its unrealized public intention is inseparable from the surviving sculpture. ([Musée Picasso collection](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/collection/))

- **1929:** *La Femme au jardin*. Welded and painted iron construction related to the Apollinaire-monument project; its status shifts between autonomous sculpture and surviving manifestation of an unrealized monument. ([Musée Picasso collection](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/collection/))

- **1942:** *Tête de taureau* (*Bull’s Head*). Assemblage of a bicycle saddle and handlebars; the act of recognition and recombination is the work, although the components were industrially made by others. ([Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/); [Musée Picasso collection](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/collection/))

- **1943:** *L’Homme au mouton* (*Man with a Sheep*). Monumental modeled sculpture subsequently manifested in bronze casts; “the work” may mean the modeled original, an authorized cast, or the public installation, and catalogue records must distinguish them. ([Musée Picasso collection](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/collection/); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1950:** *Petite Fille sautant à la corde* (*Little Girl Jumping Rope*). Assemblage incorporating wood, ceramic, iron, plaster, and found objects; it is a constructed body before it is a bronze sculpture. ([Musée Picasso collection](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/collection/))

- **1951:** *La Chèvre* (*She-Goat*). Assemblage modeled from found materials and plaster and subsequently cast in bronze; the basket, vessels, and scrap objects remain legible inside the animal’s body. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81633); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1967:** *Chicago Picasso*. Monumental Cor-Ten steel sculpture made for Daley Plaza from Picasso’s design. The work has no title supplied by Picasso; its customary title names its civic location, while the maquettes and full-scale fabrication are distinct manifestations of the project. ([Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/144607/untitled-maquette-for-the-chicago-picasso); [City of Chicago](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/dca/supp_info/chicago_s_publicartthepicasso.html))

### Ceramic obra

- **1947–1971:** *Céramiques de Picasso*. Georges Ramié’s catalogue raisonné records the ceramics made with the Madoura workshop: unique painted or modeled objects as well as numbered editions made from Picasso’s prototypes. Authorship is collaborative and technical—Picasso supplied designs and interventions while Madoura potters fabricated and issued many manifestations—so “made by Picasso” cannot always mean physically made by him alone. ([Ricker Library bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/arx/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2017/06/CataloguesRaisonnes_P.pdf); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

### Illustrated books and textual-image constructions

- **1919–1972:** *Les livres illustrés de Pablo Picasso*. Sebastian Goeppert, Herma Goeppert-Frank, and Patrick Cramer’s catalogue raisonné treats the illustrated books as a distinct body. In many entries Picasso made prints or drawings around another author’s text rather than “writing a book”; the resulting object is collaborative and may approach a *Gesamtkunstwerk* of typography, image, paper, and binding. ([Ricker Library bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/arx/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2017/06/CataloguesRaisonnes_P.pdf); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

- **1931:** *Le Chef-d’œuvre inconnu* by Honoré de Balzac. Illustrated-book project containing Picasso’s etchings and wood-engraved drawings; Balzac made the text, Picasso the visual sequence, and the edition is their posthumous collaboration rather than a book authored solely by Picasso. ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/27131); [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/356472))

- **1931:** *Les Métamorphoses* by Ovid. Illustrated edition with Picasso’s etchings; it is an image-and-text construction, not a literary work by Picasso. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/en/node/91977))

- **1942:** *Histoire naturelle* by Buffon. A suite of Picasso etchings made for Buffon’s animal texts and later issued as a book; the prints and the bound publication are related but materially different manifestations. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/en/node/91977); [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/27400))

- **1948:** *Vingt poèmes de Góngora*. Illustrated-book project combining Góngora’s poems with Picasso’s handwritten transcriptions, ornaments, and aquatints. Picasso is not the author of the poems; his calligraphic rewriting nevertheless makes the pages more than neutral reproductions of another writer’s text. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/en/node/91977); [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/353646))

- **1950:** *Corps perdu* by Aimé Césaire. Collaborative book joining Césaire’s poems to Picasso’s etchings and page designs; neither the literary text nor the visual cycle alone exhausts the made object. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/en/node/91977))

### Writings and dramatic projects

- **1935–1959:** *Écrits* / *Escritos*. The scholarly collected-writings edition assembles Picasso’s dated poems, prose texts, dramatic writings, and textual drawings from this period. Many pieces lack conventional titles, punctuation, or stable separation between verbal and visual marks; they resist being treated as ordinary literary “works,” and later book titles are editorial containers rather than titles Picasso assigned to every text. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/auteur/91977/statut/ressources_doc/type_support/140?affichage=liste); [Picasso poète](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/parcours-thematiques/picasso-poete))

- **8 November 1935:** *pas des mensonges*. Dated poem preserved as part of Picasso’s poetic production; the lower-case title follows the museum’s identification and should not be expanded into a supplied conventional title. ([Musée Picasso Paris](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/fr/les-poemes-de-picasso-ecouter/))

- **1941:** *Le Désir attrapé par la queue* (*Desire Caught by the Tail*). Six-act play with illustrations, written during the Occupation; it is a dramatic text and performance project, not simply a “book,” and its later readings, productions, and editions are manifestations rather than new authored works. ([Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/); [Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/auteur/91977/statut/ressources_doc/type_support/140?affichage=liste))

- **1947–1948:** *Les Quatre Petites Filles*. Six-act play whose publication came substantially later than its composition; the date of a particular printed edition must not be substituted for the date of the dramatic project. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/en/node/91977); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/auteur/91977/statut/ressources_doc/type_support/140?affichage=liste))

- **1957–1959:** *L’Enterrement du comte d’Orgaz*. Extended poetic and prose construction later published with other writings; it is a textual *obra* assembled across time, not a report about El Greco’s painting and not identical with any one later edition. ([Musée Picasso research catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/en/node/91977))

### Theatre, ballet, and Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1917:** *Parade*. Ballet for which Picasso made the curtain, scenery, and costumes, with scenario by Jean Cocteau, music by Erik Satie, and choreography by Léonide Massine. No single collaborator made the whole event: Picasso’s surviving objects belong to a collective *Gesamtkunstwerk*, while the performed ballet is a manifestation that cannot be reduced to those objects. ([MoMA exhibition catalogue](https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2843_300061942.pdf); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1919:** *Le Tricorne* (*The Three-Cornered Hat*). Ballet production for which Picasso designed curtain, scenery, and costumes, with music by Manuel de Falla and choreography by Massine. The designs, fabricated stage objects, painted curtain, and performance are related but non-identical works and manifestations. ([Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/picasso-and-dance); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1920:** *Pulcinella*. Ballet production with Picasso’s scenery and costumes, music by Igor Stravinsky, and choreography by Massine. Picasso’s authorship applies to the visual production, not to the ballet’s music, libretto, or choreography. ([MoMA exhibition catalogue](https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2843_300061942.pdf); [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/picasso-and-dance))

- **1921:** *Cuadro Flamenco*. Stage-design project for Serge Diaghilev’s production; surviving designs and the performed environment are manifestations of a collaborative theatrical work rather than autonomous easel pictures. ([Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/picasso-and-dance); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1922:** *Antigone*. Sets and masks designed for Jean Cocteau’s adaptation; Picasso made the production’s visual elements, while the play as text is not his. ([Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1924:** *Mercure*. Picasso designed scenery and costumes for the “plastic poses” conceived by Étienne de Beaumont, with music by Satie and choreography by Massine. Its deliberate fusion of moving bodies, painted scenery, objects, and music makes *Gesamtkunstwerk* more accurate than “painting” or “ballet design.” ([MoMA exhibition catalogue](https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2843_300061942.pdf); [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/picasso-and-dance))

- **1924:** *Le Train bleu* curtain. Picasso permitted his 1922 image *Deux femmes courant sur la plage* to be enlarged as the production curtain; because the theatrical object was executed as an enlargement by others, it is best treated as an authorized manifestation of his design, not silently equated with the original gouache. ([MoMA exhibition catalogue](https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2843_300061942.pdf); [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/picasso-and-dance))

### Murals, chapels, and civic manifestations

- **1952:** *La Guerre et la Paix* (*War and Peace*). Monumental painted ensemble installed in the former chapel at Vallauris; architecture, curved panels, imagery, and the visitor’s movement form an environment closer to a civic *Gesamtkunstwerk* than to two detachable paintings. ([Musée national Picasso, Vallauris](https://musees-nationaux-alpesmaritimes.fr/picasso/); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))

- **1958:** *La Chute d’Icare* (*The Fall of Icarus*). Monumental mural for UNESCO’s Paris headquarters, executed from Picasso’s maquette by workshop fabrication. The design, maquette, fabricated panels, and architectural installation are distinct manifestations, and the familiar title was attached after the work’s making rather than fixed unambiguously by Picasso. ([UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/artcollection/en); [UNESCO Digital Library](https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000227664))

### Posters and reproducible public images

- **1945–1973:** *Picasso’s posters*. The catalogue raisonné of Picasso’s poster art distinguishes original poster designs from posters that reproduce pre-existing paintings or drawings. This body resists simple authorship: Picasso may have made the image while printers, typographers, and commissioning bodies made the final communicative object. ([Ricker Library bibliography](https://www.library.illinois.edu/arx/wp-content/uploads/sites/47/2017/06/CataloguesRaisonnes_P.pdf); [Musée Picasso documentary catalogue](https://cep.museepicassoparis.fr/explorer/type_support/64))

- **1949:** *Congrès mondial des partisans de la paix* peace-dove image. Lithographic image adopted and repeatedly transformed for peace-congress posters and publications; “Picasso’s dove” is not one stable object but a family of prints and political manifestations. ([Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/picasso-dove-p11366); [Musée Picasso timeline](https://www.museepicassoparis.fr/en/picasso-biography/timeline/))
