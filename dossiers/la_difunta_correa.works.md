## Works by La Difunta Correa

### Embodied works and projects attributed to Deolinda Correa

- **c. 1840:** Her child. Motherhood is the only material making securely central to every version of the legend, but neither the child’s name nor an independently documented biography is stable; the child was also, of course, not her creation alone. [Chertudi and Newbery](https://revistas.inapl.gob.ar/index.php/cuadernos/article/view/340/0) collected the variant narratives; the [Museo Histórico Sarmiento](https://museosarmiento.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/nacimiento-de-maria-antonia-deolinda-correa-en-san-juan/) summarizes their disagreements over names and dates.

- **c. 1840–1841:** The journey across the San Juan desert in pursuit of her conscripted husband. This is a project and bodily performance rather than an object: it remained unfinished when she died, and even its participants, motive, route, and date vary among the oral accounts. [Chertudi and Newbery](https://revistas.inapl.gob.ar/index.php/cuadernos/article/view/340/0); [Museo Histórico Sarmiento](https://museosarmiento.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/nacimiento-de-maria-antonia-deolinda-correa-en-san-juan/).

### Manifestations attributed to the dead woman

- **c. 1840–1841:** The continued nourishment and survival of her nursing child after her death. Devotees identify this as her first miracle, but it resists classification as an obra: it is a legendary manifestation attributed to her body, not demonstrably an intentional thing she made. [Graziano](https://academic.oup.com/book/27049/chapter-abstract/196351470); [Museo Histórico Sarmiento](https://museosarmiento.cultura.gob.ar/noticia/nacimiento-de-maria-antonia-deolinda-correa-en-san-juan/).

- **Late nineteenth century–2026:** The miracles attributed to La Difunta Correa. These reported cures, protections, recovered property, marriages, houses, businesses, safe journeys, and other favors constitute an open, uncatalogued body of manifestations rather than a finite oeuvre; their existence as testimonies is documentable, while supernatural authorship is a matter of belief. The ex-votos operate as material records of individual claims. [Chertudi and Newbery](https://revistas.inapl.gob.ar/index.php/cuadernos/article/view/340/0); [Graziano](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/petitionary-devotion-folk-saints-and-miraculous-images-spanish-america-0); [Zubillaga](https://ebuah.uah.es/xmlui/handle/10017/19740?show=full).

### Collective creations made in her name

- **By 1865–2026:** *La Difunta Correa*, the popular saintly persona. This is a collective creation assembled from oral narrative, images, petitions, miracles, ritual exchange, and repeated performance—not a self-authored identity and not an official Roman Catholic canonization. The personal name itself is unstable: the early recorded tradition gives Dalinda Antonia Correa, while later versions favor María Antonia Deolinda Correa. [Chertudi and Newbery](https://revistas.inapl.gob.ar/index.php/cuadernos/article/view/340/0); [Gentile](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/a7405148488c6b5e08b7e292e3bcd5e0.pdf); [Graziano](https://academic.oup.com/book/27049/chapter-abstract/196351470).

- **By 1865–2026:** The legend cycle of the journey, death, surviving child, burial, and miracles. It is an oral and subsequently printed corpus with no single author, fixed text, title, or definitive version; Chertudi and Newbery’s foundational investigation gathered numerous variants rather than reconstructing one authoritative life. [Chertudi and Newbery](https://revistas.inapl.gob.ar/index.php/cuadernos/article/view/340/0); [Gentile](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/a7405148488c6b5e08b7e292e3bcd5e0.pdf).

- **By 1865; enlarged c. 1890; transformed from the 1940s–2026:** The grave, cross, mausoleum, oratory, and sanctuary complex at Vallecito. This is a cumulative Gesamtkunstwerk of burial place, architecture, sculpture, landscape, ritual, commerce, and votive display. Tradition assigns the burial to unnamed arrieros and the early mausoleum to a grateful herdsman; later chapels and services were made by devotees and administrators, especially after the Fundación Cementerio Vallecito was established in 1948—not by Correa herself. [Gentile](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/a7405148488c6b5e08b7e292e3bcd5e0.pdf); [Zubillaga](https://ebuah.uah.es/xmlui/handle/10017/19740?show=full); [Argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/viaja-por-argentina/que-hacer/visitar-el-santuario-difunta-correa).

- **Twentieth century–2026:** The roadside-shrine network. Small chapels, niches, crosses, images, tires, vehicle parts, and accumulations of water bottles turn existing roads into a dispersed devotional geography. It is an anonymous, continually altered collective work made chiefly by travelers, truckers, and other devotees; calling it “made by La Difunta” records devotional agency, not physical authorship. [Griffith](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0277539586900646); [Graziano](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/petitionary-devotion-folk-saints-and-miraculous-images-spanish-america-0).

- **Twentieth century–2026:** The water-bottle offering system. Individual bottles belong to donors, but their repetition at shrines forms a recognizable material manifestation of the legend’s thirst and makes the cult visible along the road. It has no single title, designer, or completed state. [Griffith](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0277539586900646); [Graziano](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/petitionary-devotion-folk-saints-and-miraculous-images-spanish-america-0).

- **Twentieth century–2026:** The Vallecito ex-voto archive. Clothing, photographs, plaques, medical objects, vehicle parts, documents, sports objects, models, and other offerings collectively materialize favors attributed to the Difunta. The archive resists being called her work: devotees made or donated its objects, while her alleged intervention supplies their shared authorship claim and narrative function. [Zubillaga](https://ebuah.uah.es/xmlui/handle/10017/19740?show=full); [Graziano](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/petitionary-devotion-folk-saints-and-miraculous-images-spanish-america-0).

- **Twentieth century–2026:** The model houses and businesses at Vallecito. These *casitas* or maquettes are simultaneously ex-votos, testimonies, amateur constructions, and an expanding architectural image of benefits received; scholarship explicitly disputes or investigates their status as art. They were made by devotees, not physically by Correa. [Cotignola and Belén](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/servlet/articulo?codigo=9564590); [Graziano](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/petitionary-devotion-folk-saints-and-miraculous-images-spanish-america-0).

- **Twentieth century–2026:** Pilgrimages, penitential ascents, and ritual journeys to Vallecito. These are repeatable performances and protocols of promise, petition, payment, and thanks rather than durable objects; each is made jointly by a devotee, the route, and the saintly presence the devotee presumes. [Zubillaga](https://ebuah.uah.es/xmlui/handle/10017/19740?show=full); [Graziano](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/petitionary-devotion-folk-saints-and-miraculous-images-spanish-america-0).
