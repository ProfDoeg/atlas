## Works by Abu Abdallah Muhammad ibn Battuta

### Collaborative oral travel narrative

- **1355:** *Tuḥfat al-nuẓẓār fī gharāʾib al-amṣār wa-ʿajāʾib al-asfār* (*A Gift to Those Who Contemplate the Wonders of Cities and the Marvels of Journeys*), commonly called the *Riḥla*. This is neither simply a book Ibn Battuta wrote nor an unmediated record of his travels: commissioned by the Marinid sultan Abū ʿInān, it was dictated by Ibn Battuta and arranged, abridged, and stylistically fashioned by Ibn Juzayy, making it a collaborative oral-literary obra whose authorship is intrinsically divided. Its completeness and factual independence are also disputed: scholars question portions of the itinerary, while substantial passages have been traced to Ibn Jubayr and al-ʿAbdarī; the standard edition nevertheless preserves this as Ibn Battuta’s sole identifiable work rather than evidence for a larger written oeuvre. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ebn-battuta/); [Elad, *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0035869X00140651); [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcwdl.wdl_07470/?st=gallery))

### Print and translation history, beyond the base edition

European knowledge of the *Riḥla* began indirectly, through an abridged Arabic epitome: Samuel Lee translated this shortened version into English in 1829, decades before Defrémery and Sanguinetti worked from the fuller Paris manuscripts (one of which, now BnF MS Arabe 2291, specialists regard as Ibn Juzayy's own autograph or very close to it). Two later editions sit alongside the Gibb–Beckingham Hakluyt translation: a modern Arabic edition assembled by the Moroccan scholar ʿAbd al-Hādī al-Tāzī in five volumes (Rabat, 1997), and Tim Mackintosh-Smith's widely read abridged English edition drawn from Gibb–Beckingham (2002). (Wikipedia, "Ibn Battuta"; this project's own dossiers/ibn_battuta.dossier.md)
