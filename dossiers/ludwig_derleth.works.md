## Works by Ludwig Derleth

### Early poems and prose

- **1896–1910:** *Gedichte und Prosa aus „Pan“ und den „Blättern für die Kunst“*. This is an editorially assembled corpus of poems and prose first dispersed through two periodicals, not a book Derleth conceived under that collective title; the collected edition preserves it as his *Frühwerk*. [[1](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB28495228)] [[2](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd11852478X.html#ndbcontent)]

### Manifestos and proclamations

- **1904:** *Die Proklamationen*. A sequence of prophetic proclamations composed for oral, performative declaration: part poem, part religious-political manifesto, and a text intended to act upon its hearers rather than merely describe a doctrine. The 1904 text circulated before the substantially revised publication of 1919, so the date should not be mistaken for that later manifestation. [[2](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd11852478X.html#ndbcontent)] [[3](https://dokumen.pub/kunstreligion-band-2-die-radikalisierung-des-konzepts-nach-1850-9783110299571-9783110299304.html)]
- **1919:** *Proklamationen*. A revised and published manifestation of the 1904 project, sufficiently distinct that the critical collected edition prints the 1904 and 1919 texts separately. Derleth’s name was removed from the printed 1919 version, making its authorial presentation deliberately impersonal even though authorship is not disputed. [[1](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BB28495228)] [[3](https://dokumen.pub/kunstreligion-band-2-die-radikalisierung-des-konzepts-nach-1850-9783110299571-9783110299304.html)]

### The encompassing poem-project

- **c. 1890s–1948; partly published 1932/1933, complete edition 1971–1972:** *Der Fränkische Koran*. Derleth’s principal obra is a roughly 15,000-line “world poem,” religious summa, spiritual itinerary, and intended encyclopedia of his worldview, worked on for nearly forty years. Only about a third appeared during his lifetime; the complete six-part textual architecture was established posthumously by Christine Derleth and Dominik Jost, so the surviving whole is simultaneously Derleth’s project and an editorial reconstruction rather than an entirely author-supervised finished book. [[4](https://www.literaturportal-bayern.de/autorenlexikon?pnd=11852478X&task=lpbauthor.default)] [[5](https://www.zeit.de/1976/40/von-zeit-mitarbeitern)] [[6](https://books.google.com/books/about/Das_Werk.html?id=fD8f0gEACAAJ)]

### Books and cycles within *Der Fränkische Koran*

- **Published 1932/1933:** *Der Fränkische Koran: Des Werkes erster Teil*. This was the major lifetime manifestation of the larger poem-project, not the complete *Koran* later represented by volumes 2–6 of *Das Werk*. Sources differ between 1932 and 1933, apparently reflecting issue or bibliographical dating, and that discrepancy should be retained. [[2](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd11852478X.html#ndbcontent)] [[4](https://www.literaturportal-bayern.de/autorenlexikon?pnd=11852478X&task=lpbauthor.default)] [[5](https://www.zeit.de/1976/40/von-zeit-mitarbeitern)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Das Buch vom Orden*. A constituent book of the *Koran*, combining visionary poetry with a projected sacred order; its independent status is unstable because the collected edition also makes it one movement in the encompassing poem. [[7](https://opac.mnk.pl/index.php?KatID=1&lang=en_GB&plnk=__wydawca_Hinder+und+Deelmann&typ=repl)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Die Posaune des Kriegs*. A constituent poetic book of the *Koran*, organized around prophetic and martial annunciation rather than narrative in the ordinary sense. [[7](https://opac.mnk.pl/index.php?KatID=1&lang=en_GB&plnk=__wydawca_Hinder+und+Deelmann&typ=repl)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Die Apokalyptische Schlacht*. An apocalyptic poetic cycle within the *Koran*; it is a named component, though not securely documented here as a separately issued lifetime book. [[7](https://opac.mnk.pl/index.php?KatID=1&lang=en_GB&plnk=__wydawca_Hinder+und+Deelmann&typ=repl)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Das Buch der Geschichte*. A historical-cosmological poetic book within the *Koran*, subordinating history to Derleth’s religious scheme. [[7](https://opac.mnk.pl/index.php?KatID=1&lang=en_GB&plnk=__wydawca_Hinder+und+Deelmann&typ=repl)]
- **1945; also dated 1946 in some bibliographies:** *Der Tod des Thanatos*. A long meditative poem on sin, war, violence, and the overcoming of death, issued independently during Derleth’s lifetime and later incorporated into the *Koran*. The 1945/1946 discrepancy appears to concern publication dating rather than two different obras. [[2](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd11852478X.html#ndbcontent)] [[8](https://archive.org/search?query=title%3A%22Der+Tod+des+Thanatos%22+AND+creator%3A%22Derleth%2C+Ludwig%22)] [[7](https://opac.mnk.pl/index.php?KatID=1&lang=en_GB&plnk=__wydawca_Hinder+und+Deelmann&typ=repl)]
- **1947; posthumously collected 1971:** *Die himmlische Basilie*. A late constituent poem of the *Koran*; the collected-edition bibliography dates the text to 1947, but it did not receive its canonical placement until after Derleth’s death. [[9](https://diebuchsuche.de/ar_9783873480.html)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously published 1968 and collected 1971:** *Advent*. A late poetic component of the *Koran* whose publication was posthumous; consequently its 1968 date belongs to the manifestation, not necessarily to composition. [[9](https://diebuchsuche.de/ar_9783873480.html)]
- **1939:** *Seraphinische Hochzeit*. A hybrid sequence of prose parables and rhymed poetry concerning the visionary monk Bruder Immerwach. It appeared as a separate book during Derleth’s lifetime but was subsequently absorbed into the architecture of the *Koran*, resisting any simple choice between independent obra and constituent cycle. [[2](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd11852478X.html#ndbcontent)] [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Das Paradies*. A named poetic book within the *Koran*; no reliable independent lifetime publication has been established from the consulted collected-edition records. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **1935–1940; posthumously collected 1971:** *Das Sibyllinische Buch*. A prophetic historical poem composed across the Nazi period and organized through the voice or function of sibylline utterance. The composition range is reported in scholarship, while its canonical manifestation belongs to the posthumous collected edition. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)] [[11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Derleth)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Poem der magischen Natur*. A cosmological nature-poem and constituent cycle of the *Koran*; it circulated at least in excerpt before its placement in the collected whole. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)] [[12](https://www.abebooks.com/servlet/SearchResults?bsi=20&ds=10&prevpage=1&pt=book&rollup=on&sortby=17&tn=ludwig+derleth)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Vom Wingert zur Kelter*. A named poetic cycle within the *Koran*, using vineyard and winepress imagery as part of its religious-poetic structure. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Die Wandlungen der Pandora*. A mythological poetic cycle within the *Koran*; the available collected-edition record establishes the title and placement but not a secure composition date. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Der Liederdichter*. A named poetic cycle within the *Koran*, apparently reflecting on the figure or vocation of the lyric poet. Its status is that of a component rather than a securely documented separate lifetime book. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Der Heilige I*. The first of four large divisions forming the concluding movement of the *Koran*; whether it should be called an autonomous work is doubtful because the collected edition presents it as part of a continuous whole. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1971:** *Der Heilige II*. The second division of the concluding *Der Heilige* complex, published in volume 5 of the collected edition. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1972:** *Der Heilige III*. The third division of the concluding complex, first canonically manifested in volume 6 of *Das Werk*. [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]
- **Date not securely established; posthumously collected 1972:** *Der Heilige IV*. The fourth and final division of *Der Heilige* and of the collected *Koran*. Its ending supplies an editorially realized completion to a project Derleth did not himself publish in full. [[5](https://www.zeit.de/1976/40/von-zeit-mitarbeitern)] [[10](https://www.goodreads.com/series/321512-das-werk)]

### Extracts and separate manifestations

- **1937:** *Die Lebensalter*. A separately published group of poems extracted from *Der Fränkische Koran*, hence a new manifestation and reading-unit rather than an additional independent underlying obra. [[2](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd11852478X.html#ndbcontent)] [[4](https://www.literaturportal-bayern.de/autorenlexikon?pnd=11852478X&task=lpbauthor.default)]
