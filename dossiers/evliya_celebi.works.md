## Works by Evliya Çelebi

### Travel-writing Gesamtkunstwerk

- **c. 1672–1684/85?:** *Târîh-i Seyyâh Evliyâ Efendi* (usually *Seyahatnâme*, “Book of Travels”). One enormous Ottoman-Turkish obra arranged in ten books, not ten independent works: autobiography, itinerary, urban and architectural survey, geography, history, ethnography, linguistic record, anecdote, fiction and performance coexist without stable separation. It recounts journeys from the 1630s through 1681 and was assembled during Evliya’s last years in Egypt; whether the earliest surviving manuscript series is authorial, scribal, or prepared under his direction remains disputed, while the final book survives through a later manuscript and the whole text may never have received a definitive fair copy. ([TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/seyahatname--evliya-celebi); [Historians of the Ottoman Empire](https://ottomanhistorians.uchicago.edu/en/historian/evliya-celebi); [UNESCO Memory of the World nomination](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/turkey_evliya_celebi.pdf))

### Lost or unverified textual obra

- **No fixed date:** *Şakanâme*. Evliya reportedly names this additional work himself, but no manuscript is known; consequently it resists classification as a completed book and may have been a lost text, an unrealized project, or a textual joke whose independent existence cannot now be verified. ([Yapı Kredi Yayınları author record](https://kitap.ykykultur.com.tr/yazarlar/evliya-celebi); [Turkish Historical Society](https://ttk.gov.tr/evliya-celebi-1611-1685/))

### Cartographic obras and projects

- **c. 1682–1685:** *Dürr-i bî-misîl în ahbâr-ı Nîl* (the Vatican Map of the Nile, Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Vat. Turc. 73). A monumental illustrated river-map with roughly 450 textual rubrics, conceived as the cartographic counterpart to Book X’s Nile journeys; Dankoff and Tezcan attribute its conception and contents to Evliya, although the surviving object is not in his own hand, so it is better understood as an Evliyan obra executed through scribal or workshop mediation. ([Dankoff, Tezcan and Sheridan, *Ottoman Explorations of the Nile*](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctt207g548.6))

- **Seventeenth century; date unknown:** Map of the Tigris and Euphrates, now in a private Qatari collection. This very large illustrated map has no securely established authorial title or date: Zekeriya Kurşun’s comparison with the *Seyahatnâme* concludes only that Evliya himself, or somebody in his retinue working under his supervision, made it, while other scholarship continues to describe the attribution as a proposal rather than settled fact. ([Kurşun, “Does the Qatar Map of the Tigris and Euphrates Belong to Evliya Çelebi?”](https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/112212); [Qatar-map study record](https://avesis.marmara.edu.tr/yayin/2f1423e2-1647-4cdc-942c-05927ec3c7ed/does-the-qatar-map-of-the-tigris-and-euphrates-belong-to-evliya-celebi))

- **No fixed date; unfinished or only partly manifested:** An illustrated map of Evliya’s travels and of the wider world. In the *Seyahatnâme* he announces an intention to draw the fortresses, cities, rivers, mountains and lakes he encountered; no complete atlas answering that description is known, and the two river maps may be surviving manifestations of this larger project rather than independent commissions. ([Tezcan, “Evliyâ Çelebi’nin Freng-pesend Resim Tutkusu”](https://journals.openedition.org/ceb/3947?lang=en))

### Monumental calligraphy and epigraphic obras

- **1663:** Inscriptions for the converted churches of Uyvar (Nové Zámky). Evliya says that he executed decorative inscriptions—including the mihrab inscription of the Mehmed IV Mosque—after the Ottoman conquest; the originals are lost, so their existence depends upon his narrative and seventeenth-century copies made by Jacobus Tollius. ([Tütüncü, “On the Trail of Evliya Çelebi’s Inscriptions and Graffiti”](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1669:** Mihrab-wall inscription in the converted Sultan Mehmed Han Mosque, Kandiye (Heraklion). Evliya describes writing a Qur’anic dating inscription in Karahisârî calligraphic style and being paid for it; no trace survives, making it a self-reported public commission rather than an independently inspectable object. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1670:** Inscribed and colored marble plaque for the Gate of Conquest at Zarnata Castle. Evliya reports composing or selecting its chronogram and engraving or embossing it before it was gilded and painted; the plaque is lost, and the numerical value of the reported chronogram does not agree neatly with the written year. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1670:** Calligraphic ensemble for the Hünkâr Mosque at Zarnata. Evliya claims responsibility for differently styled inscriptions on the mihrab and minbar and for a carved seal placed at the castle gates; none survives, and the “thing” was therefore a coordinated architectural-textual installation rather than a book. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1670:** Tekke shops at Zarnata. In the same passage Evliya says that he built and endowed shops for a dervish lodge; no physical trace or corroborating building record is presently identified, so this remains a self-attributed architectural and charitable obra whose exact extent cannot be recovered. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

### Graffiti and memorial traces

- **1660:** Memorial graffito in the İnceli Ahmed Bey Mosque, Kyustendil. A signed, dated wall inscription identifying Evliya as Melek Ahmed Paşa’s muezzin; it is one of the few material traces attributed directly to his hand rather than known only through the *Seyahatnâme*. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1663/64:** Signed graffito in the Alaca Mosque, Foča. The mosque and inscription were destroyed during the Bosnian war, so the object survives only through earlier documentation. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1663/64:** Memorial inscription in the Atik Ali Paşa Mosque, Foča. Its inscribed stones were reportedly saved when the mosque was destroyed and incorporated into the reconstructed building in 2015, making the present installation partly original trace and partly modern reconstruction. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1663:** Graffito at an unspecified Ottoman building in Pécs. Known from an 1847 transcription and probably lost; Evliya’s presence in Pécs makes the attribution plausible, but neither its original location nor the physical inscription can now be checked. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1671/72:** Memorial inscription in the Hasan Ağa Mosque, Adana. Formerly visible beside the entrance but plastered over during post-1998-earthquake restoration; its continued physical existence beneath the plaster is uncertain. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **1671/72:** Memorial inscription in the Pir Ahmed Efendi or Boyalı Kadı Mosque, Karaman. Tütüncü attributes the dated carving to Evliya through comparison with the Adana inscription, whereas İbrahim Hakkı Konyalı assigned it to a later admirer; authorship is therefore disputed. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf))

- **c. 1660s–1670s:** Self-reported memorial graffiti on walls and trees across the Ottoman world. The *Seyahatnâme* mentions nearly thirty such interventions—including examples at the Osman Baba lodge near Haskovo, the Gazi Sinan Paşa Mosque in Elbasan, the Çelebi Hüseyin Paşa Mosque in Berat, the Prophet’s Mosque in Medina, and trees at Patras and Kos—but most are lost or unverified; as dispersed marks of presence, this corpus resists being called a conventional “work” at all. ([Tütüncü](https://edizionicafoscari.unive.it/media/pdf/books/978-88-6969-086-0/978-88-6969-086-0-ch-18.pdf); [TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/seyahatname--evliya-celebi))
