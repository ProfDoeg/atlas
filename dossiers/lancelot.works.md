## Works by Lancelot

### No attributable obra

- *No works securely attributable.* Lancelot is a legendary character whose biography changes across medieval romances, not a historically attested maker with a bibliography, catalogue raisonné, collected works, or discography. The supplied sixth-century dates are therefore a chronological convention rather than datable evidence of authorship; texts, images, buildings, arms, and other objects made **about** Lancelot belong to their actual authors or artisans, while his narrated deeds, vows, begetting of children, bestowal of knighthood, and renaming or possession of places do not constitute a stable oeuvre. The scholarly character bibliography accordingly lists sources and studies concerning Lancelot, not works made by him.

### Sources

- Alan Lupack, [“Lancelot,” *The Camelot Project*](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/theme/lancelot.html), Rossell Hope Robbins Library, University of Rochester. The essay documents mutually incompatible literary lives—including marriage and four children in *Lanzelet* versus the Galahad narrative in later traditions—and supplies the scholarly character bibliography.
- *Arthuriana* and the Camelot Project, [“Arthuriana / Camelot Project Bibliographies and Reprints”](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/text/arthuriana-camelot-project-bibliographies.html), University of Rochester. This describes the project’s peer-reviewed Arthurian bibliographies and distinguishes primary texts, editions, adaptations, and critical studies.
- Sir Thomas Malory, [*Le Morte d’Arthur*, vol. I](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1251/old/1251-h/1251-h.htm), ed. William Caxton, Project Gutenberg transcription. Consulted as a primary narrative witness: it presents Lancelot performing knightly and institutional acts—such as making Gareth a knight and granting Pendragon to La Cote Male Taile—but does not transmit an authored or fabricated oeuvre by him.
