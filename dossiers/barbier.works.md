## Works by Charles Barbier de la Serre

### Writing systems and pedagogical projects

- **1808:** *Tableau d’expédiographie*. A one-page protocol for Barbier’s first documented shorthand system. No copy has been securely identified in the consulted sources; its existence is known from an April 1808 notice, and the substantially enlarged publication of 1809 calls itself a second edition. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1809:** *Expéditive française*. A rapid-writing system meant to follow speech, constructed from six principal shapes and rules for sounds, accents, endings, and numerals. It is an obra larger than its printed manual: a proposed writing practice that Barbier also offered to teach. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1809:** *Écriture coupée*. A system for making letters by cutting paper rather than using ink, introduced within *Principes d’Expéditive française* and subsequently issued in instructional cards. It is simultaneously a script, a material procedure, and a project for writing without conventional penmanship. ([Musée Louis Braille](https://museelouisbraille.com/fr/barbier-l-intuitif); [Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1810:** *Alphabet d’écriture facile à l’usage des personnes qui savent lire et pas écrire*. A card-sized pedagogical protocol addressed to readers unable to form ordinary handwriting; its existence is documented by Barbier’s newspaper advertisements. The surviving evidence does not establish whether this was a wholly independent system or a separately marketed manifestation of the work developed in 1809. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1810:** *Alphabet d’écriture coupée pour prendre des notes à la fois sans plume ni crayon*. An instructional card for cut-paper notation. It is better understood as a portable protocol and material writing technique than as a book. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1810:** *Alphabet d’écriture coupée et le Calendrier du militaire et du voyageur pour l’année 1810*. A two-card combination of writing protocol and practical calendar, marketed to soldiers and travellers. Its mixed instructional and calendrical form resists treatment as a single autonomous “text.” ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1810:** *Double explication de l’écriture coupée et application à la plume ou au crayon*. A separately issued plate advertised with Barbier’s *Expéditive*, translating the cut-paper procedure back into marks made by pen or pencil. The evidence identifies a detachable instructional manifestation, not necessarily a separate authored work. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1815:** *Écriture ponctuée*, later called *écriture nocturne*. A coordinate-based tactile writing system in which letters or sounds are represented by two vertical groups of raised points, first published as Plate VII of the *Essai*. Later writers often called it “sonographie,” but Barbier did not; the once-common story that he designed it for soldiers communicating in darkness is contradicted by the 1815 text, which expressly proposes it for blind people. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/); [Musée Louis Braille](https://museelouisbraille.com/fr/barbier-l-intuitif))

- **1815:** *Alphabet usuel*. A 5-by-5 coordinate arrangement of the conventional French alphabet that could be manifested through several kinds of marks or gestures. It is not one fixed inscription but an underlying code used by multiple procedures in the *Essai*. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1815:** *Alphabet d’abréviation* / *alphabet méthodique*. A coordinate grid of speech sounds intended to replace conventional spelling and permit abbreviated phonetic writing. Barbier changed its contents and ordering in later manifestations, so it is a developing linguistic project rather than a text with one definitive state. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1815:** *Dactylologie à deux mains*. A coordinate-based fingerspelling procedure in which contact between one finger of each hand identifies a letter or sound. It belongs to Barbier’s project of communication between blind and deaf people, but deaf users preferred systems that did not occupy both hands. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1815:** *Procédés cryptographiques de l’Expéditive française*. Several coordinate ciphers—using dots, lines, connecting strokes, and marks disguised as musical notation—published among the twelve plates of the *Essai*. The consulted scholarly account treats them as related variations rather than supplying twelve stable independent work-titles, so they should not be falsely separated or retitled. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1821:** *Écriture nocturne*. A revised manifestation of raised-point writing incorporating both alphabetic and phonetic grids and simplified tools; it was sent to the Institution royale des jeunes aveugles and adopted there as an ancillary practice. The memorable title first appears in the collaborative *Annales de l’Industrie* tableau rather than in Barbier’s 1815 book, making its naming and graphic authorship partly collective. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1832:** *Émancipation intellectuelle d’expéditive française, no 1: typographie privée de poche et d’ambulance*. A 28-page prospectus-manifesto that presents simplified writing as a means of intellectual emancipation and promotes applications for blind people, hospitals, and the military. Its “no 1” suggests a projected sequence, but the consulted sources do not establish that further numbered parts appeared. ([Google Play Books](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Ch_Barbier_Emancipation_intellectuelle_d_exp%C3%A9ditiv?id=nb3uP1akyQoC); [AVH study](https://www.avh.asso.fr/sites/default/files/2016_charles_barbier.pdf))

- **1834:** *Tableau pour l’instruction familière des sourds-muets*. An instructional chart applying Barbier’s simplified alphabetic theory to deaf education. It was submitted for official consideration but rejected for institutional use in 1836; the chart is therefore both a protocol and an unrealized pedagogical project. ([AVH study](https://www.avh.asso.fr/sites/default/files/2016_charles_barbier.pdf))

- **1834–1835:** *Notice sur les salles d’asile: le retour à la simplicité primitive de la théorie alphabétique; l’instruction familière des enfants du premier âge, des aveugles de naissance et des sourds-muets*. A prospectus proposing Barbier’s systems for early-childhood, blind, and deaf education. Sources differ between 1834—the year authorities acknowledged receiving it—and 1835, when the Académie des sciences recorded the printed notice; these may represent circulation and publication dates rather than two works. ([Bibliography of elementary education](https://paris8-omekas.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/original/24715388f2aa8b38cd7b527ea91d7ed475ed23b2.pdf); [Académie des sciences record](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a4/Comptes_rendus_hebdomadaires_des_s%C3%A9ances_de_l%27Acad%C3%A9mie_des_sciences_%28IA_comptesrendusheb01acad%29.pdf))

- **1837:** *Instruction familière des classes laborieuses*. Barbier’s final known prospectus, advocating trials of simplified writing among working-class learners. It is a protocol and social project whose intended institutional adoption remained substantially unrealized at his death. ([AVH study](https://www.avh.asso.fr/sites/default/files/2016_charles_barbier.pdf); [bibliography of elementary education](https://paris8-omekas.s3.nl-ams.scw.cloud/original/24715388f2aa8b38cd7b527ea91d7ed475ed23b2.pdf))

### Composite publications

- **1809:** *Principes d’Expéditive française pour écrire aussi vite que la parole*. A printed manual containing ninety instructions, exercises, numerical notation, and examples of Barbier’s rapid-writing system, while also introducing cut-paper writing. It calls itself a considerably enlarged second edition; the presumed first is the documented but presently unlocated 1808 tableau. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1815:** *Essai sur divers procédés d’expéditive française, contenant douze écritures différentes, avec une planche pour chaque procédé*. A 33-page explanatory essay with twelve plates presenting tactile, graphic, gestural, cryptographic, and mechanical forms of writing. It functions as a small Gesamtkunstwerk of language engineering—book, visual atlas, collection of protocols, and designs for implements—and Barbier acknowledged that hurried printing prevented him from including everything planned. ([digitized copy](https://books.google.com/books?id=lkVzrUr1RvEC); [Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1821:** *Tableau synoptique des procédés d’expéditive française*. A single-sheet consolidation of eight methods—*télégraphique*, *facile*, *accélérée*, *nocturne*, *d’ambulance*, two forms *de combinaison*, and *mécanique*—published with an article in the *Annales de l’Industrie*. Barbier supplied the material, but editor Jean-Gabriel-Victor de Moléon designed the plate and acknowledged altering it, so sole authorship of this manifestation would be misleading. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

### Instruments and material apparatus

- **1815:** *Roulette for écriture ponctuée*. A metal wheel designed to impress regular raised dots into paper, diagrammed with Plate VII of the *Essai*. It is a purpose-built writing instrument rather than a text, and Barbier subsequently replaced its cumbersome action with simpler punch-and-guide tools. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **By 1819:** *Expéditeur*. A unique writing machine with a wooden frame and metal tracing mechanism, built to produce one form of *Expéditive française*. The Académie des sciences found it excessively complicated; the sole original survives in the Association Valentin Haüy museum. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **By 1821:** *Réglette, poinçon, and coulisse or guide-main for point writing*. A coordinated set of tools—a grooved wooden ruler, blunt punch, and metal alignment guide—that enabled blind writers to emboss and reread their own notes. Barbier donated many sets and equipment for making more; because the objects were produced in multiples and later modified, this is a designed apparatus and distributed material practice rather than one uniquely bounded work. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/); [Musée Louis Braille](https://museelouisbraille.com/fr/barbier-l-intuitif))

### Archival and occasional texts

- **1795–1798:** *Kentucky Gazette advertisements*. Notices offering Barbier’s services as a teacher of French, geography, mathematics, and surveying. These are authored public manifestations but resist being called works: the specialist account cites them as documentary traces of his American activity rather than as part of his later writing-system œuvre. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1809–1810:** *Newspaper advertisements for Expéditive and écriture coupée*. Notices placed in the *Moniteur universel*, *Journal de Paris*, and *Gazette de France* to advertise Barbier’s systems, cards, plates, and lessons. They act on behalf of the project but are commercial ephemera rather than independent theoretical works. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))

- **1821–1841:** *Correspondence concerning point writing and elementary instruction*. Dozens of letters to and from institutions, officials, Alexandre-René Pignier, and Louis Braille document Barbier’s continuing revisions and campaign for adoption. This dispersed correspondence is not one work and has no single fixed title or complete published corpus; surviving holdings are divided principally between the Association Valentin Haüy and the Institut national des jeunes aveugles. ([Campsie](https://www.napoleon.org/en/history-of-the-two-empires/articles/charles-barbier-a-language-innovator-in-the-first-empire/))
