## Works by Sor Juana Inés de la Cruz

### Lyric poems and poetic projects

- **c. 1668–1692:** *Lírica personal*. A body of 221 surviving compositions—romances, endechas, redondillas, décimas, glosas, sonnets, liras, ovillejos and silvas—rather than a book conceived by Sor Juana. Most have no authorial title and are identified by first line or by later editorial headings; Antonio Alatorre’s critical edition adds five poems to Méndez Plancarte’s corpus and removes one false attribution.
- **before 1689:** *Primero sueño* (also *El sueño*). A philosophical silva of 975 lines and Sor Juana’s most deliberately sustained personal poem; “Primero” was probably imposed when it entered print, since no second dream survives.
- **before 1689:** *Redondillas* (“Hombres necios que acusáis”). An untitled argumentative poem, conventionally named from its verse form or first line; it functions almost as a manifesto against the contradictory sexual standards imposed on women.
- **before 1689:** *Este que ves, engaño colorido*. An untitled sonnet about portraiture, appearance and mortality, normally identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *Detente, sombra de mi bien esquivo*. An untitled love sonnet, identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *Rosa divina que en gentil cultura*. An untitled philosophical sonnet, identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *En perseguirme, Mundo, ¿qué interesas?* An untitled poem of intellectual self-definition, identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *Finjamos que soy feliz*. An untitled philosophical romance, identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *Pues estoy condenada*. An untitled lira sequence on absence and grief, identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *A estos peñascos rudos*. An untitled lira sequence voiced by a bereaved woman, identified by its first line.
- **before 1689:** *Retrato de Lisarda*. A long burlesque portrait in the manner of Jacinto Polo de Medina; the familiar title is an editorial description, not securely an authorial title.
- **1691:** *Epinicio gratulatorio al conde de Galve* (also *Silva al conde de Galve*). A ceremonial silva celebrating the viceroy’s victory over a French expedition; it is public encomium rather than private lyric.
- **late 1692:** *Enigmas ofrecidos a la Casa del Placer*. A commissioned manuscript book for a Portuguese women’s literary circle, containing twenty riddles, a verse dedication and a sonnet-prologue. It is simultaneously a poetic sequence, social game and collaborative book-object; Antonio Alatorre established it as authentic and distinguished it from the spurious *Oráculo de los preguntones*.

### Villancico cycles: words made for music, ceremony and performance

- **1676:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. A cycle for three nocturns, made to be sung rather than merely read; the surviving obra is principally Sor Juana’s verbal architecture, since its original musical setting is not securely hers.
- **1676:** *Villancicos de la Concepción*. A three-nocturn cycle mixing learned Marian argument with popular and theatrical voices.
- **1677:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Nolasco*. A ceremonial cycle including captive, traveller, jácara, Black and Indigenous personae.
- **1677:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. A cycle combining Spanish and Latin verse, scholastic parody, jácara and multilingual comic performance.
- **1679:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. A distinct three-nocturn cycle, not a revision of the 1676 set.
- **1683:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. A distinct Petrine cycle employing pastoral, maritime and comic voices.
- **1685:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. A distinct cycle whose nocturns move among theology, astronomy, legal imagery and multilingual performance.
- **1689:** *Villancicos de la Concepción*. A Marian cycle incorporating riddling, scriptural exposition, jácara and other performative forms.
- **1689:** *Villancicos de Navidad*. A Nativity cycle for Puebla Cathedral, combining devotional, pastoral and comic dramatic voices.
- **1690:** *Villancicos de San José*. An unusually extensive cycle of thirteen numbers, including a jácara and Indigenous and Black personae.
- **1690:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. A distinct Marian cycle employing philosophical, architectural and theatrical conceits.
- **1691:** *Villancicos de Santa Catarina*. A cycle celebrating Catherine of Alexandria as learned woman and disputant—a particularly charged ceremonial analogue for Sor Juana herself.
- **undated:** *Letras de San Bernardo*. A sequence of thirty-one sacred song-texts on the dedication of a church, the Virgin, the Eucharist and Bernardine devotion.
- **undated:** *Letras a la Presentación de Nuestra Señora*. Three sacred song-texts conceived for liturgical-musical use.
- **undated:** *Letras a la Encarnación*. Three sacred song-texts, including Latin as well as Spanish.
- **undated:** *Letras para la solemnidad del Nacimiento*. Two Nativity song-texts.
- **undated:** *Letras para la profesión de una religiosa*. Four song-texts for the ceremonial entry of a woman into religious life.
- **1677:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. A cycle included by Méndez Plancarte only among “attributable” works; Sor Juana’s authorship is plausible but not secure.
- **1678:** *Villancicos de Navidad*. An attributable, not securely authenticated, cycle.
- **1680:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. An attributable Petrine cycle; the prose dedication and individual numbers do not all possess equally secure authorship.
- **1680:** *Villancicos de Navidad*. An attributable Nativity cycle.
- **1681:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. An attributable Marian cycle.
- **1684:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. An attributable cycle including folías, jácara and dramatic dialogue.
- **1686:** *Villancicos de la Asunción*. An attributable Marian cycle, multilingual and theatrically voiced.
- **1690:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. An attributable Petrine cycle.
- **1691:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. An attributable Petrine cycle.
- **1692:** *Villancicos de San Pedro Apóstol*. An attributable cycle and one of the latest bodies of verse connected with Sor Juana.

### Autos sacramentales and their loas

- **c. 1688–1689:** *El divino Narciso*. An auto sacramental transforming the Narcissus myth into Eucharistic allegory. Its prefatory loa stages Indigenous Mexican religion and Christian evangelization; together loa and auto form a musical-theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **before 1692:** *El cetro de José*. An Old Testament auto sacramental about Joseph in Egypt, accompanied by its own loa; it is allegorical theatre intended for performance, music and religious instruction.
- **before 1692:** *El mártir del Sacramento, San Hermenegildo*. A historical auto sacramental with a prefatory loa, centred on the Visigothic prince Hermenegild and the Eucharist.

### Court and convent loas

- **c. 1670–1675:** *Loa de la Concepción*. An independent allegorical and musical celebration of the Immaculate Conception.
- **c. 1675:** *Loa en celebración de los años del rey nuestro señor*. The first of several independent palace pieces for Charles II’s birthday.
- **late 1670s–1680s:** *Loas a los años del rey Carlos II*. Four further courtly performance-texts catalogued separately by Méndez Plancarte; their repetitive descriptive titles are editorial, while their allegorical designs and occasions distinguish them as individual works.
- **1670s:** *Loa a los años de la reina madre*. A court entertainment for Mariana of Austria.
- **1682:** *Loa a los años de la reina María Luisa de Borbón*. An allegorical-musical birthday piece structured around the powers of the soul.
- **1680–1683:** *Loa a los años del virrey marqués de la Laguna*. A palace performance joining political encomium to cosmological allegory.
- **1680–1683:** *Loa a los años de la condesa de Paredes*. A courtly performance for María Luisa Manrique de Lara.
- **1681–1683:** *Loa en las huertas donde fue a divertirse la excelentísima señora condesa de Paredes*. A site-specific convent or garden entertainment; its occasion, female performers and surroundings make it more than a freestanding text.
- **1688–1692:** *Loas para el conde y la condesa de Galve*. Separate birthday or ceremonial performance-texts for the succeeding viceregal household.
- **undated:** *Loa al padre fray Diego Velázquez de la Cadena*. A ceremonial dramatic homage to an ecclesiastical patron.

### Comedies and complete theatrical fiestas

- **1683:** *Los empeños de una casa*. A three-act secular comedy of intrigue and gendered disguise. Its full fiesta also comprises a loa, *Sainete primero de palacio*, *Sainete segundo*, sung interludes and the concluding *Sarao de cuatro naciones*, so the made thing can be treated as a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a play-script.
- **1689:** *Amor es más laberinto*. A mythological palace comedy on Theseus: Sor Juana wrote the first and third jornadas, while Juan de Guevara wrote the second. The associated loa belongs to the performed fiesta; sole authorship of the complete comedy must not be assigned to Sor Juana.
- **c. 1675–1676?:** *La segunda Celestina*. A completion of Agustín de Salazar y Torres’s unfinished comedy, attributed to Sor Juana on the basis of a discovered ending and later testimony. Guillermo Schmidhuber and Octavio Paz defended the attribution, but other scholars dispute it; it therefore remains a contested collaborative project, not a securely established work.

### Emblematic architecture and festival publication

- **1680:** *Neptuno alegórico*. A Gesamtkunstwerk devised for the ceremonial entry of the marqués de la Laguna: Sor Juana designed the allegorical programme for a temporary triumphal arch and wrote its accompanying explanation. The physical arch has vanished, so the surviving printed text is documentation and interpretation of a larger made object, not the whole obra.
- **1680:** *Razón de la fábrica alegórica y aplicación de la fábula*. The prose exposition of the arch’s intellectual programme, printed with *Neptuno alegórico*; it functions as both interpretive protocol and learned political allegory.
- **1680:** *Explicación del arco*. A verse explanation connected with the same ephemeral architecture and public ceremony.

### Polemical prose, letters and intellectual self-defence

- **c. 1681–1682:** *Carta al padre Antonio Núñez* (also *Carta de Monterrey* or *Autodefensa espiritual*). A private letter dismissing Núñez as Sor Juana’s confessor and defending her studies; discovered only in the twentieth century, it acts upon a relationship rather than merely describing one.
- **1690:** *Carta atenagórica* (also *Crisis de un sermón*). A theological critique of António Vieira’s sermon on Christ’s “finezas,” written earlier at another cleric’s request and published without Sor Juana’s control by Bishop Manuel Fernández de Santa Cruz.
- **1691:** *Respuesta de la poetisa a la muy ilustre Sor Filotea de la Cruz*. An autobiographical, theological and intellectual defence addressed to the pseudonymous “Sor Filotea.” It is simultaneously letter, polemic and manifesto for women’s learned activity, although framed in the rhetoric of obedience.
- **1691:** *Carta de Serafina de Cristo*. A satirical intervention in the controversy surrounding the *Carta atenagórica*, attributed by Elías Trabulse to Sor Juana under a pseudonym. Antonio Alatorre and Martha Lilia Tenorio rejected that attribution, so it cannot be listed as securely hers.

### Devotional protocols and late religious declarations

- **before 1691:** *Ofrecimientos para el Santo Rosario de quince misterios*. A devotional protocol prescribing meditations and verbal offerings for the rosary; an anonymous edition predating 1691 survives.
- **before 1700:** *Ejercicios devotos para los nueve días antes del de la Purísima Encarnación*. A novena: an ordered protocol of prayer and meditation for nine days and the feast itself.
- **1694:** *Docta explicación del misterio y voto que hizo de defender la Purísima Concepción*. A brief theological-devotional declaration first printed posthumously from a manuscript copy.
- **1694:** *Petición causídica*. A juridically modelled devotional petition; its form makes religious self-submission behave like legal pleading.
- **1694:** *Protesta de la fe y renovación de los votos religiosos*. One of three recorded late professions or protests of faith, reportedly written or signed in blood. It resists classification as literature: it is a performative religious act preserved as a document.
- **1694:** *Protesta que, rubricada con su sangre, hizo de su fe y amor a Dios*. A second late devotional declaration; textual history and naming vary among witnesses.
- **1694:** *Renovación de los votos religiosos*. A third associated profession or renewal. These declarations overlap in later printing and cataloguing, so the precise boundaries among the three documentary acts are not wholly stable.

### Documentary acts that resist being called authored works

- **24 February 1669:** *Profesión religiosa*. An autograph formula entered in the profession book of the Convent of San Jerónimo. It is a signed performative act—making Sor Juana’s vowed status—not a literary composition in the ordinary sense.
- **15 February 1669:** *Solicitud para otorgar testamento y renuncia de bienes*. A legal act bearing Juana Inés’s name; because a notary probably drafted it, the signature does not establish literary authorship.
- **23 February 1669:** *Testamento y renuncia de bienes*. A notarized disposition made before profession. It belongs to her acts and material history, but the collected-works editor explicitly declined to treat its legal wording as her authored literature.
- **6 June 1684:** *Venta de una esclava a Josefa María de Asbaje*. A signed legal transaction. It is something Sor Juana did and authorized, with grave material consequences for another person, but probably not language she composed.
- **12 March 1691:** *Petición para imponer a censo 1,400 pesos*. A signed financial request, probably written by a legal professional rather than by Sor Juana.
- **24 March 1691:** *Censo de 1,400 pesos a favor de Sor Juana*. A notarial financial instrument in which she is the interested party, not securely the writer.
- **18 August 1691:** *Imposición adicional de 600 pesos sobre bienes y rentas del convento*. A signed financial act, not safely attributable to her as verbal composition.
- **20 January 1692:** *Solicitud de licencia para comprar una celda*. A conventual-property petition associated with Sor Juana but probably drafted in protocol form by another hand.
- **9 February 1692:** *Venta de la celda de Catalina de San Jerónimo a Sor Juana*. A notarial act documenting her acquisition; it should not silently be converted into a literary “work.”

### Lost, unfinished or only reported projects

- **childhood; date unknown:** *Loa al Santísimo Sacramento*. Sor Juana later recalled composing a loa while still a child at Amecameca; no text has been identified securely.
- **date unknown:** *Súmulas*. A reported compendium or teaching text on elementary scholastic logic. No surviving manuscript is presently known, so even its exact form and completeness remain uncertain.
- **before 1689:** *El caracol*. A lost or unfinished music-theory project proposing an easier method of musical study. Sor Juana herself alluded to it disparagingly; whether it ever became a complete treatise is unknown, making “project” safer than “book.”
- **date unknown:** *El equilibrio moral* (also reported as *Direcciones prácticas morales en la segura probabilidad de las acciones humanas*). A lost work on moral probability mentioned by early witnesses. Since no text survives, its title, scope and completion cannot be independently tested.
- **date unknown:** *Poems, letters and occasional writings now lost*. Early testimony and Sor Juana’s own *Respuesta* indicate compositions beyond the surviving corpus. They cannot responsibly be supplied with invented titles or dates.
- **not authentic:** *El oráculo de los preguntones*. Frequently attributed to Sor Juana in later printings, but rejected by Méndez Plancarte and Alatorre as spurious; it belongs in the attribution history, not in her oeuvre.
