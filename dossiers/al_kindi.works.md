## Works by Abu Yusuf Ya'qub ibn Ishaq al-Kindi

### Scope and status

- *The attributed Kindian corpus*. This is not a stable shelf of surviving “books” but a bibliographical obra: Ibn al-Nadīm records roughly 240 titles, while Richard J. McCarthy’s critical concordance gathers 361 titles or title-variants from Ibn al-Nadīm, al-Qifṭī, Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿa, manuscripts, and later testimony. Most items are lost, many titles vary between witnesses, and duplication under variant titles cannot always be excluded; unless otherwise stated, the entries below are ninth-century epistles or treatises known only by title and datable no more precisely than before al-Kindī’s death.

### Philosophy and classification of knowledge

- **Before 873:** *On First Philosophy*. A metaphysical treatise dedicated to Aḥmad ibn al-Muʿtaṣim; only part survives, and its familiar modern form may represent the first section of a larger project.
- **Before 873:** *On Interior Philosophy*. A lost philosophical treatise whose title and precise subject remain uncertain.
- **Before 873:** *Logical and Difficult Questions*. A lost collection or problem-text rather than a continuous philosophical summa.
- **Before 873:** *On the Fact That Philosophy Cannot Be Acquired Except through Mathematics*. A lost methodological epistle presenting mathematical study as a protocol for philosophical formation.
- **Before 873:** *Exhortation to Learn Philosophy*. A lost protreptic epistle.
- **Before 873:** *On the Quantity of Aristotle’s Books and What Is Required for the Attainment of Philosophy*. A surviving bibliographical and curricular protocol arranging Aristotle’s writings and the preparatory sciences.
- **Before 873:** *On Aristotle’s Purpose in the Categories, Its Objects, and Its Subject Matter*. A lost interpretive epistle.
- **Before 873:** *On the Quiddity of Knowledge and Its Divisions*. A lost classification of knowledge.
- **Before 873:** *On the Divisions of Human Knowledge*. A lost taxonomic epistle.
- **Before 873:** *The Great Treatise on Scientific Examination*. A lost extended methodological treatise.
- **Before 873:** *Abridgment of Scientific Examination*. A lost epitome of the preceding or a closely related work.
- **Before 873:** *That All the Works of the Creator Are Just and Contain No Injustice*. A lost philosophical-theological epistle.
- **Before 873:** *On the Quiddity of That Which Is Said to Be Infinite, and in What Manner It Is Said to Be Infinite*. A lost analysis of infinity.
- **Before 873:** *On the Finitude of the Body of the World*. A surviving demonstration that the physical universe cannot be actually infinite; it may correspond to Ibn al-Nadīm’s title concerning proof “by intellectual power.”
- **Before 873:** *On Agents and Things Acted Upon among the First Natural Things*. A lost causal analysis.
- **Before 873:** *On the Clarification of the Intellectual Syntheses*. A lost work whose transmitted title is difficult and may designate combinations or common principles of thought.
- **Before 873:** *Questions Concerning the Utility of Mathematics*. A lost question-and-answer text.
- **Before 873:** *Investigation of the Claim That Natural Things Act Uniformly through Necessity of Creation*. A lost examination of natural causality.
- **Before 873:** *On the Principles of Sensible Things*. A lost natural-philosophical epistle.
- **Before 873:** *On Graciousness in the Arts*. A lost and textually uncertain title: “graciousness” may not preserve al-Kindī’s wording.
- **Before 873:** *On the Procedure for Letters to Caliphs and Viziers*. A lost protocol of chancery or courtly composition rather than a philosophical treatise in the narrow sense.
- **Before 873:** *On the Division of the Canon*. A lost work with an unstable classification: “canon” might be legal, geometrical, or musical.
- **Before 873:** *On Definitions and Descriptions of Things*. A surviving philosophical lexicon defining technical concepts; its manuscript transmission contains differing tables of contents.
- **Before 873:** *On the Intellect*. A surviving short taxonomy of intellect; the Arabic survives, as do medieval Latin versions.
- **Before 873:** *On the Intellect, with an Explanation of It*. A catalogue title that may be the surviving *On the Intellect*, an expanded recension, or a separate lost work.
- **Before 873:** *On the Proximate Efficient Cause of Generation and Corruption*. A surviving natural-philosophical demonstration concerning celestial causation.
- **Before 873:** *On the True, First, Complete Agent and the Deficient Agent That Is So Only Metaphorically*. A surviving causal-theological epistle; Ibn al-Nadīm lists a closely matching title among disputational works.
- **Before 873:** *That There Are Separate Incorporeal Substances*. A surviving philosophical epistle, but its attribution has been disputed because of its relation to Aristotle’s *Categories* and later Arabic philosophical terminology.
- **Before 873:** *A Concise and Brief Statement about the Soul*. A surviving epitome assembled from Greek philosophical materials.
- **Before 873:** *On the Prostration of the Outermost Body and Its Obedience to God*. A surviving interpretation of celestial obedience, joining philosophical cosmology to Qurʾānic exegesis.
- **Before 873:** *On God’s Unity and the Finitude of the Body of the World*. A surviving treatise or composite textual unit; its relation to separately catalogued works on unity and cosmic finitude is uncertain.

### Translation, revision, and collaborative textual productions

- **Before 842:** *The Theology of Aristotle*. An Arabic adaptation of portions of Plotinus’s *Enneads*, translated by ʿAbd al-Masīḥ ibn ʿAbd Allāh ibn Nāʿima al-Ḥimṣī and “corrected” or edited by al-Kindī for Aḥmad ibn al-Muʿtaṣim. It is therefore a collaborative manifestation of the Kindian circle, not straightforwardly a work authored or translated by al-Kindī.
- **Before 873:** *The Book of Causes*. An Arabic metaphysical adaptation ultimately based on Proclus and produced in the Kindian translation milieu. Traditional transmission sometimes associates it with al-Kindī, but modern scholarship does not regard him securely as its author.
- **Before 873:** *Alexander of Aphrodisias, On Providence*. An Arabic translation or adaptation associated with al-Kindī’s circle; the degree of al-Kindī’s personal editorial intervention is unknown.
- **Before 873:** *Alexander of Aphrodisias, On the Principles of the All*. An Arabic adaptation associated with the Kindian circle rather than a securely authored Kindian treatise.
- **Before 873:** *Proclus, On the Eternity of the World*. Arabic excerpting and refutation material associated with the Kindian translation project; its precise makers and stages of revision cannot be separated confidently.
- **Before 873:** *Greek astrological, mathematical, and philosophical translations revised in al-Kindī’s circle*. These form a collective scholarly project rather than a finite list of individually authored works: sources credit al-Kindī with commissioning, correcting, or furnishing terminology for translations, but calling every such manifestation “by al-Kindī” would erase the translators who made them.
