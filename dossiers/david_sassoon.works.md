## Works by David Sassoon

### Commercial obra

- **1832–1864:** *David Sassoon & Co.* A mercantile house and trans-Asian commercial network founded in Bombay, with branches subsequently established in Calcutta, Hong Kong, Shanghai, Canton, and elsewhere. It traded in cotton, textiles, spices, metals, and opium; the enterprise is better understood as Sassoon’s principal institutional obra than as a single object. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13218-sassoon); [The Jewish Museum](https://thejewishmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Sassoons_Exhibition_Texts_in_show_order_for_PR_02.23.2023-1.pdf))

### Synagogues and communal institutions

- **1857–1861:** *Magen David Synagogue*. A synagogue erected by Sassoon in Byculla, Bombay, and the first Baghdadi Jewish synagogue built in the city. Later enlargement by his grandson Jacob Sassoon is not David’s work. ([The Jewish Museum](https://thejewishmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Sassoons_Exhibition_Texts_in_show_order_for_PR_02.23.2023-1.pdf); [ANU Museum of the Jewish People](https://synagogues-360.anumuseum.org.il/gallery/magen-david/))

- **1861:** *David Sassoon Benevolent Institution*. A free Jewish school established beside Magen David Synagogue, combining Jewish studies with languages, mathematics, geography, and commercial training. Sources sometimes date it approximately to 1856; it was later enlarged and renamed the Sir Jacob Sassoon Free High School, so the surviving institution is not wholly David Sassoon’s obra. ([Nadia Valman and Sander L. Gilman, eds., *The Baghdadi Jews in India*](https://dokumen.pub/the-baghdadi-jews-in-india-maintaining-communities-negotiating-identities-and-creating-super-diversity-9780367203252-9780367197872.html); [Cecil Roth, *The Sassoon Dynasty*](https://www.seekingmyroots.com/members/files/G005666.pdf))

- **1863–1867:** *Ohel David Synagogue*. A neo-Gothic synagogue in Pune commissioned by Sassoon and designed by Henry St. Clair Wilkins. Sassoon died before its 1867 consecration, making it a completed posthumous project rather than a building he saw finished. ([The Jewish Museum](https://thejewishmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Sassoons_Exhibition_Texts_in_show_order_for_PR_02.23.2023-1.pdf); [Ohel David Synagogue history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_David_Synagogue))

### Hospitals, refuges, and welfare institutions

- **1863–1867:** *Sassoon General Hospital*. A public hospital for patients of every caste and creed, begun in Pune on 8 October 1863 and opened after Sassoon’s death on 7 October 1867. He supplied a major share, but not all, of its cost; Wilkins designed it and the colonial government administered its construction, so it is properly a sponsored collaborative project rather than Sassoon’s sole architectural creation. ([Bombay Presidency, *Poona District Gazetteer*](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Poona%20District/places_p.html); [Bombay Presidency administrative report, 1863–64](https://www.qdl.qa/en/archive/81055/vdc_100139330383.0x000091))

- **1863–1865:** *Sassoon Infirm Asylum* (later *Sassoon Asylum* or *Nivara*). A Pune home for aged, infirm, and sick poor people of all communities, created principally from Sassoon’s contribution but formally established through public subscription after his death. It resists designation as an individual “work” because its capital and governance were collective. ([Bombay Presidency, *Poona District Gazetteer*](https://www.gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Poona%20District/places_p.html))

- **Date uncertain, before 1864:** *Industrial school and reformatory for juvenile offenders*. Early biographical authorities credit Sassoon with founding this Bombay institution, later known as the David Sassoon Reformatory and Industrial Institution. The attribution is not sufficiently precise to distinguish his original foundation from later government administration, expansion, and renaming; it should therefore be treated as a disputed institutional obra, not as a securely attributable surviving building. ([Dictionary of National Biography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography%2C_1885-1900/Sassoon,_Albert_Abdullah_David); [Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13218-sassoon))

### Civic and educational projects

- **1862–1865:** *David Sassoon Clock Tower at the Victoria Gardens*. An illuminated Palladian-style clock tower for Bombay’s Victoria Gardens, funded by Sassoon with a gift of 17,000 rupees and constructed from a separately administered design and budget. This is a commissioned civic object rather than architecture authored by Sassoon himself. ([Agri-Horticultural Society of Western India, report for 1863](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/97/Report_of_the_Agri-Horticultural_Society%2C_for_the_Year_1863_%28IA_dli.granth.92435%29.pdf); [report for 1865](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/56/Report_of_the_Agri-Horticultural_Society%2C_for_the_Year_1865_%28IA_dli.granth.92437%29.pdf))

- **1863–1870:** *Mechanics’ Institution building* (later *David Sassoon Library and Reading Room*). Sassoon set aside 60,000 rupees for a permanent building for an existing mechanics’ association; J. Campbell and G. E. Gosling designed the structure, which was completed after his death. Sassoon made possible the building project, but neither created the institution—it dated from 1847—nor designed the eventual library. ([David Sassoon Library, institutional history](https://www.davidsassoonlibrary.com/about.html); [Maharashtra Ahead](https://dgipr.maharashtra.gov.in/sites/default/files/2020-08/MAhead-FEB%202012.pdf))

- **By 1862:** *Victoria Gardens and Victoria and Albert Museum*. Sassoon and Jagannath Shankarsheth supplied capital for this joint Bombay civic complex. Because it was a collective undertaking involving donors, officials, designers, and horticultural organizations, it resists attribution to Sassoon as a discrete personal work. ([Preeti Chopra, *A Joint Enterprise: Indian Elites and the Making of British Bombay*](https://dokumen.pub/a-joint-enterprise-indian-elites-and-the-making-of-british-bombay-1.html))

- **1864:** *Prince Albert memorial*. Sassoon’s last recorded public project was the erection or endowment of a memorial statue to Prince Albert at the Victoria and Albert Museum in Bombay. Sources variously describe his contribution as the statue, its pedestal, or the act of dedication, so the precise physical extent of his commission remains uncertain. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13218-sassoon); [“Queen Victoria’s world”](https://scroll.in/article/899205/queen-victorias-world-how-loyalty-to-the-british-crown-shaped-the-redevelopment-of-bombay))

### Educational endowments

- **Before 1864:** *Bombay university and art-school scholarships*. Scholarship funds founded by Sassoon rather than texts or buildings; the consulted standard biographies do not supply individual titles or secure foundation dates. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13218-sassoon); [Encyclopaedia Judaica, vol. 18](https://www.rfservicesltd.co.uk/Jewish/Encyclopaedia%20Judaica/encyclopedia%20judaica/Encyclopaedia%20Judaica%20-%20vol%2018%20%28San-Sol%29.pdf))

- **Before 1864:** *Elphinstone High School building contribution*. Sassoon contributed substantially to educational provision at Elphinstone High School, but the sources do not support calling the whole school or its later reconstructed building his work. It is included as a patronal contribution whose resistance to individual authorship is historically significant. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13218-sassoon); [Encyclopaedia Judaica, vol. 18](https://www.rfservicesltd.co.uk/Jewish/Encyclopaedia%20Judaica/encyclopedia%20judaica/Encyclopaedia%20Judaica%20-%20vol%2018%20%28San-Sol%29.pdf))

- **Date and attribution disputed:** *Alliance Israélite Universelle school building, Baghdad*. The 1905 *Jewish Encyclopedia* attributes a Baghdad school building to David Sassoon, but the Alliance school opened only in 1864–65 and later accounts associate its purpose-built premises with his son Albert. The project cannot securely be entered as David’s completed obra; at most, it may preserve a confused memory of a late gift or intention. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/13218-sassoon); [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/alliance-israelite-universelle/))
