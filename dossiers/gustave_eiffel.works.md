## Works by Alexandre Gustave Bonickhausen dit Eiffel

### Railway bridges and rail works

- **1858–1860:** *Passerelle Saint-Jean (Bordeaux railway bridge)*. Railway bridge whose construction Eiffel directed for the Compagnie générale de matériel de chemin de fer; it predates his independent firm and is therefore an engineering commission rather than a wholly personal design.
- **1860–1861:** *Bridges at Bayonne, Capdenac and Floirac*. Railway bridges for the Bordeaux–Bayonne and Brive–Rodez lines.
- **1867:** *Bridges of the Orsay–Limours and Poitiers–Limoges lines*. Groups of railway bridges supplied for French railway companies.
- **1867:** *Rouzat and Neuvial viaducts*. Iron railway viaducts on the Commentry–Gannat line; works of Eiffel’s newly established contracting enterprise rather than solitary-authored objects.
- **1869:** *Bridges of the Montpellier–Paulhan and Cahors–Vibos lines*. Groups of railway structures catalogued collectively, without stable individual titles.
- **1869:** *Mézy bridge*. Railway bridge in Yvelines.
- **1870:** *Bridges of the Latour-sur-Orb–Millau and Brive–Tulle lines*. Contract packages for the Compagnie du Midi.
- **1872:** *Thouars viaduct*. Railway viaduct for the Chemins de fer de la Vendée.
- **1872:** *Bridges of the Iași–Ungheni line*. Romanian railway-bridge commission.
- **1873:** *Bridges at Ornans, Champagne-Thémery and Villandraut*. Three separately located railway structures.
- **1873:** *Bridges of the Orléans–Gien line*. Multi-object railway commission.
- **1874:** *La Oraya bridge*. Brazilian railway bridge; the inherited catalogue’s spelling and identification should be treated cautiously.
- **1874:** *Magdalena bridge*. Colombian railway bridge.
- **1874:** *Bridges of the Bressuire–Tours line*. Railway-bridge group.
- **1874:** *Louviers and Amboise bridges*. Railway structures in Eure and Indre-et-Loire.
- **1874:** *Bridges at Chinon, Tours and Azay-le-Rideau*. Crossings respectively of the Vienne, Cher and Indre.
- **1875:** *Bridges of the Bernese Jura*. Swiss railway-bridge group.
- **1875:** *Tombo de Viso bridge*. Portuguese railway bridge.
- **1875:** *Bridges of the Chinon–Joué, Poitiers–Saumur and Épinay–Luzarches lines*. Three railway contract groups.
- **1875:** *Clichy bridge*. Railway bridge in Hauts-de-Seine.
- **1876:** *Bridges of the Girona line*. Spanish railway-bridge group.
- **1876:** *Maria Pia Bridge*. Iron arch railway bridge over the Douro at Porto; Eiffel’s firm won the competition, while Théophile Seyrig’s major design role makes sole attribution to Eiffel misleading.
- **1877:** *Bridges of the Minho railway*. Bridges at Viana do Castelo, Cávado, Âncora, Caminha, Villa Mea and Neiva in Portugal.
- **1877:** *Tâmega bridge and Palla viaduct*. Railway works for Portugal’s Douro line.
- **1877:** *Muga bridge*. Spanish railway bridge.
- **1877:** *Bridge over the Clain*. French railway bridge in Vienne.
- **1878:** *Empalot, Valentine and Sarrieu bridges*. Railway bridges for the Compagnie du Midi.
- **1878:** *Bridges of the Cáceres line*. Spanish railway-bridge group.
- **1878:** *Teticala and Cuenta bridges*. Mexican railway bridges; place-name forms follow the cited inventory and may reflect historical transcription.
- **1878:** *Bergerac, Niherne and Buzançais bridges*. French railway bridges.
- **1879:** *Bridges of the Ploiești–Predeal line*. Romanian railway-bridge group.
- **1879:** *Moutiers-sur-le-Lay bridge*. French railway bridge.
- **1880:** *Bridges of the Beira Alta line*. Portuguese group catalogued under the names Vaizeas, Trezaï, Côa, Deo, Beira Baixa, Frezse, Noemi, Meligroso, Criz, Cazillas, Perala, Tietar, Gouveia, Celorico and Breda; the inventory’s spellings are not consistently modern.
- **1880:** *Tagus bridge and the Tamujoso, Grande, Val de Haza and Guadancil bridges*. Spanish railway works on the Cáceres line.
- **1880:** *Cazillas, Perala and Ollas bridges*. Spanish railway bridges.
- **1880:** *Bình Điền, Tân An and Bến Lức bridges*. Railway bridges in colonial Vietnam.
- **1881:** *Évaux viaduct over the Tardes*. Railway viaduct on the Montluçon–Eygurande line.
- **1881:** *Normanville bridge*. Railway bridge in Calvados.
- **1882:** *Bergerac bridge over the Dordogne*. Railway bridge later converted to road use.
- **1882–1884:** *Garabit Viaduct*. Iron arch railway viaduct over the Truyère; Léon Boyer devised the railway project and Eiffel’s company developed and executed its metalwork, so it is a collaborative engineering obra.
- **1882:** *Six bridges of the Asturias, Galicia and León line*. Spanish railway group including the Cobas bridge.
- **1883:** *Charenton bridge*. Railway bridge for the Paris–Lyon–Méditerranée company.
- **1883:** *Portable bridges for Senegal*. A system and a supplied group rather than one fixed-location monument.
- **1884:** *Three bridges at Alcántara*. Spanish railway structures on the Cáceres line.
- **1884:** *Châteauroux bridge and bridge on Route Nationale 121 at Chaudes-Aigues*. French railway structures.
- **1885:** *Fifteen bridges of the Lisbon–Sintra line*. Portuguese contract including bridges identified as Torres, Veotras, Ramal and Morianna in the source inventory.
- **1885:** *Portable bridges for the Questembert–Ploërmel line*. Standardized Eiffel-system bridge units rather than a single unique work.
- **1885:** *Bridge over the Serre at La Fère*. French railway bridge.
- **1886:** *Collonges bridge*. Railway bridge at present-day Collonges-au-Mont-d’Or.
- **1886:** *Bridges of the Western Railway’s Évreux section*. Multi-object railway commission.
- **1887:** *Thirty-seven bridges of the Fréjus–Saint-Raphaël line*. A serial railway package, not thirty-seven individually titled works in the cited inventory.
- **1888:** *Bridges of the Poço do Bispo–Carregado line*. Portuguese railway group.
- **1889:** *Bridges of the Vendas Novas–Santarém line*. Portuguese railway group; the source renders the first place-name as “Nindas Novas.”
- **1889–1890:** *Conflans-Sainte-Honorine viaduct*. Railway viaduct later reconstructed in 1945.
- **1890–1892:** *Bridges of the Lisbon–Badajoz line*. Portuguese group listed as Almonda, Alviella, Asseca, Bleone, Ribeira, Mondego and Cocuminho; “Bleone” occurs twice in the inherited inventory.
- **1891:** *Bridges of the Jaffa–Jerusalem line*. Railway bridges in Ottoman Palestine.
- **1891:** *Var and Vésubie bridges*. Railway bridges on the Saint-André–Nice line.
- **1891:** *Font-Laugières viaduct*. Railway viaduct on the Grasse–Magne line; the cited catalogue prints “Mande.”
- **1892:** *Three viaducts of the Mezzana–Corte line*. Corsican railway group including the Vecchio viaduct.
- **1892:** *One hundred and eight bridge decks for the Lan-Ho–Kien Chan line*. Serially manufactured Chinese railway components rather than 108 separately conceived obras.
- **1893:** *Bridge over the Vesle*. Railway bridge on the Soissons–Reims line.
- **1893:** *Saint-Pierre-du-Vauvray viaduct*. Railway viaduct over the Seine.
- **1893:** *Rue Chanteraine bridge*. Railway bridge at Épinal.
- **1893–1895:** *Bridges of the Saint-Pierre–Les Andelys line*. These post-1893 works belong to the firm renamed Société de construction de Levallois-Perret after Eiffel’s withdrawal; their inclusion in a personal oeuvre is therefore contestable.
- **1894:** *La Taillée viaduct*. Railway viaduct on the Châteaubriant–Saint-Nazaire line, likewise executed after Eiffel’s withdrawal.
- **1895:** *Bridges of the Ryazan–Urals line and a Caucasus bridge*. Russian railway contracts of Eiffel’s successor company, not securely personal works.
- **1895:** *Bridges of the Glos-Montfort–Pont-Audemer line*. Successor-company railway works.
- **1895:** *Lou-Kou-Tiao bridge*. Chinese railway bridge attributed by the company inventory.
- **1895–1896:** *Bezons bridge over the Seine*. Successor-company railway work.
- **1895–1896:** *Bridges for the Charente economic railways*. Successor-company contract group.
- **1895–1896:** *Bình Điền bridge*. Vietnamese railway bridge, distinct in the inventory from the 1880 commission of the same place-name.
- **1895–1898:** *Mahinady bridge over the Bafing*. Sudanese railway bridge attributed to the successor enterprise.
- **1897:** *Saint-Jean and Bras de la Plaine bridges*. Railway bridges on Réunion.
- **1898–1900:** *Bridges of the Courcelles–Champ-de-Mars line*. Paris railway works including the Passy bridge; these are products of the successor firm, not demonstrably Eiffel’s own designs.

- **1875:** *Lamp-room pavilions and tanks for the Eastern Railway*. Standard railway-service structures.

- **1886:** *Constantine market*. Covered market in Algeria.
- **1886:** *Long Châu market*. Vietnamese market structure.
- **1886:** *Stores in the rues de Lorraine and d’Alsace*. Paris commercial structures.
- **1887:** *Cao Lãnh market*. Vietnamese market structure; spelling follows modern usage rather than the catalogue’s “Coalanh.”
- **1887:** *Filtering wells at Saigon*. Water-engineering installation.
- **1887–1889:** *Eiffel Tower*. A 300-metre iron tower and, in the atlas owner’s vocabulary, nearly a Gesamtkunstwerk of structure, machinery, architecture, exhibition spectacle and scientific instrument. Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier originated the structural project, Stephen Sauvestre developed its architectural treatment, and Eiffel acquired and championed it; sole invention by Eiffel is therefore untenable.
- **1887–1889:** *Panama Canal locks*. An uncompleted industrial project: Eiffel’s company designed and began supplying locks for the failed French canal scheme, but the intended system was never realized as planned.
- **1888:** *Ô Môn and Trahuse markets*. Vietnamese market halls; “Trahuse” remains an uncertain historical place-name.
- **1888:** *Saigon river-messaging halls*. Port or transport sheds.
- **1889:** *Tân An and Tân Qui Đông markets*. Vietnamese market structures.
- **1889:** *Dunkirk warehouse*. Port storage building.
- **1890:** *Pauillac landing stage*. Gironde river structure.
- **1890:** *Sèvres meteorological laboratory*. Scientific station built for Eiffel’s observations; both an instrument and a building.
- **1892:** *Club Equitos*. Peruvian reuse of a pavilion made for the 1889 Paris Exposition; relocation complicates whether this is a new work, a manifestation of the earlier pavilion, or both.
- **1893:** *La Villette locomotive shed*. Railway building appearing at the boundary of Eiffel’s withdrawal from his company; personal authorship is uncertain.
- **1894:** *Lock gates at Creil, Pontoise and L’Isle-Adam*. Oise navigation components made by the successor enterprise.
- **1896–1897:** *Charner and Messageries landing stages*. Saigon port works by the successor company.
- **1899:** *Tamatave landing stage*. Madagascar port work by the successor company.
- **1901:** *Beaulieu meteorological laboratory*. Scientific observation station personally connected with Eiffel’s meteorological programme.
- **1902:** *Vacquey meteorological laboratory*. Scientific station in Gironde.
- **1906:** *Ploumanac’h meteorological laboratory*. Scientific station in Brittany.
- **1909:** *Champ-de-Mars aerodynamic laboratory*. Wind-tunnel laboratory built at Eiffel’s expense; it functioned simultaneously as architecture, experimental apparatus and research programme.
- **1912:** *Auteuil Aerodynamic Laboratory*. Wind-tunnel laboratory in rue Boileau, replacing the Champ-de-Mars installation; this is both an engineered instrument and an enduring scientific institution.
- **1917:** *Laboratoire Eiffel experimental monoplane*. Single-seat low-wing aircraft project, developed with Louis Charles Breguet and commonly called the Breguet Laboratoire Eiffel or Breguet LE. Only a prototype was built, and the shared authorship makes “made by Eiffel” more accurate than “Eiffel’s airplane.”

### Standardized systems and protocols

- **1883–1885:** *Eiffel portable-bridge system*. A family of standardized, rapidly assembled bridges deployed in Senegal, Brittany, Italy, Russia and elsewhere; it is a system or protocol embodied in many objects, not one uniquely situated work.
- **Before 1890:** *Standardized trusses, tanks, caissons, barracks, cranes, floors, beams and frameworks*. Henri Loyrette’s scholarly account stresses this serial industrial production, but no complete object-by-object census exists; the body of products therefore resists a catalogue of discrete titled obras.

### Engineering monographs, notices and project documents

- **1879:** *Notice sur le pont du Douro à Porto*. Illustrated engineering notice documenting the Maria Pia Bridge.
- **1885:** *Notice sur les différents types de ponts portatifs, système Eiffel*. Technical notice and protocol describing Eiffel’s standardized portable bridges.
- **1888:** *Notice sur le viaduc de Garabit (près Saint-Flour)*. Engineering monograph with an atlas of plates.
- **1889:** *Mémoire présenté à l’appui du projet définitif du viaduc de Garabit*. Technical argument supporting the viaduct’s definitive project.
- **1900:** *La Tour de trois cents mètres*. Monumental engineering monograph and atlas documenting the conception, calculations, fabrication and erection of the Eiffel Tower; it is also an act of self-historicization by Eiffel and his enterprise.
- **1902:** *La Tour Eiffel en 1900*. Descriptive and scientific account of the tower at the close of the 1900 Exposition.

### Meteorological works and atlases

- **1900:** *Travaux scientifiques exécutés à la Tour de trois cents mètres, de 1889 à 1900*. Collected report on the tower’s scientific uses; it converts a building into an experimental platform.
- **1904:** *Dix années d’observations météorologiques à Sèvres, de 1892 à 1901*. Data-rich meteorological study with atlas.
- **1904:** *Étude comparée des stations météorologiques de Beaulieu-sur-Mer, Sèvres et Vacquey pour l’année 1902*. Comparative meteorological report with atlas.
- **1905:** *Études pratiques de météorologie et observations comparées de Beaulieu, Sèvres et Vacquey pour l’année 1903*. Comparative study and observational record.
- **1905:** *Étude comparée des stations météorologiques de Beaulieu, Sèvres et Vacquey pour l’année 1904*. Annual comparative report.
- **1905:** *Les observations courantes en météorologie*. Instructional scientific paper: in the proposed vocabulary, partly a protocol for observation.
- **1905:** *Analyse de l’ouvrage Études pratiques de météorologie*. Analytical paper listed among Eiffel’s meteorological pamphlets.
- **1906:** *Types généraux de comparaisons météorologiques appliqués à l’étude des stations de Beaulieu, Sèvres et Vacquey pour l’année 1905*. Two-volume comparative study.
- **1906:** *Mesures thermométriques en météorologie*. Methodological paper and observational protocol.
- **1906:** *Les observations météorologiques du Weather Bureau de Washington*. Scientific paper on American observational practice.
- **1907:** *Atlas météorologique pour l’année 1906 d’après vingt-deux stations françaises*. Atlas of comparative graphic records.
- **1907:** *Étude climatologique de Beaulieu-sur-Mer pendant la période du 1er décembre au 1er mai de 1902 à 1907*. Multi-year climatological study.
- **1908:** *Atlas météorologique pour l’année 1907 d’après vingt-quatre stations françaises*. Annual meteorological atlas.
- **1909:** *Atlas météorologique pour l’année 1908 d’après vingt-quatre stations françaises*. Annual meteorological atlas.
- **1910:** *Atlas météorologique pour l’année 1909 d’après vingt-cinq stations françaises*. Annual meteorological atlas and the last such atlas listed in Eiffel’s contemporary author bibliography.

### Aerodynamic studies and wartime research

- **1907:** *Recherches expérimentales sur la résistance de l’air exécutées à la Tour Eiffel*. Experimental report based on Eiffel’s tower-drop apparatus.
- **1910:** *La résistance de l’air: examen des formules et des expériences*. Critical synthesis of aerodynamic formulae and experimental results.
- **1910:** *La résistance de l’air et l’aviation: expériences effectuées au laboratoire du Champ-de-Mars*. Experimental monograph presenting wind-tunnel apparatus, measurements and their application to aircraft.
- **1911:** *Conférence faite à l’Aéro-Club de France sur la résistance de l’air et l’aviation*. Published lecture: simultaneously a scientific report and a public intervention on aviation.
- **1912–1913:** *Nouvelles recherches expérimentales sur la résistance de l’air et l’aviation faites aux laboratoires du Champ-de-Mars et d’Auteuil*. Expanded research report; bibliographic records variously date the society paper, offprint and book-form publication to 1912, 1913 or 1914, so a single date would conceal edition history.
- **1919:** *Résumé des principaux travaux exécutés pendant la guerre au Laboratoire aérodynamique Eiffel, 1915–1918*. Retrospective report on wartime experiments whose earlier publication had been restricted by military secrecy.
- **1909–1921:** *Aerodynamic test series of the Eiffel laboratories*. Thousands of tests on wings, complete aircraft models, propellers, airships, automobiles and other bodies constituted a collaborative experimental programme rather than a finite authored “work”; individual models usually belonged to outside manufacturers, while Eiffel’s contribution was the apparatus, method, testing and publication of results.

### Public interventions

- **14 February 1887:** *Réponse de Gustave Eiffel à la protestation des artistes*. Newspaper response defending the projected tower against the artists’ protest. It functions less as neutral description than as a manifesto for the legitimacy and prospective beauty of the engineering project.
