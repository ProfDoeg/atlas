## Works by Madame Chiang Kai-shek

### Books and extended political or religious texts

- **1937:** *Sian: A Coup d’État*. A first-person political memoir of the Xi’an Incident, also published with Chiang Kai-shek’s diary extracts under the title *General Chiang Kai-shek: The Account of the Fortnight in Sian When the Fate of China Hung in the Balance*. The variant title is a manifestation, not a separate obra. [1][2]
- **1938:** *我的宗教經驗譚* (*My Religious Experience*). A short autobiographical Christian testimony; some later religious compilations excerpt or re-title it rather than constituting new works. [1][3]
- **1939:** *戰時婦女動員問題* (*The Mobilization of Women in Wartime*). A wartime political protocol: it seeks to organize women for national service rather than merely describe their circumstances. [1]
- **1940:** *基督教與新生活* (*Christianity and the New Life*). A religious-political exposition connecting Christianity with the New Life Movement. [3]
- **1940:** *This Is Our China*. A jointly attributed book by Chiang Kai-shek and Madame Chiang presenting Nationalist China and its political program; its composite authorship makes it a collaborative obra rather than solely hers. [4]
- **1941:** *China Shall Rise Again*. A book-length wartime argument and appeal, incorporating official statements alongside Madame Chiang’s authored presentation; bibliographies disagree between 1940, the British issue, and 1941, the American issue, so those dates should be treated as publication manifestations rather than confidently distinct compositions. [2][4][5]
- **1943:** *We Chinese Women: Speeches and Writings During the First United Nations Year, February 12, 1942–November 16, 1942*. An authorial collection of speeches and articles, not a single newly composed continuous book. [6]
- **1945:** *蔣夫人論世界和平* (*Madame Chiang on World Peace*). A political-religious essay or address issued as a booklet; the bibliography records the publication but does not establish whether the text originated as a speech. [1]
- **1955:** *The Sure Victory*. A short Christian testimony and meditation, subsequently abridged and reprinted under Chinese titles including “The Power of Prayer” and “How I Became a Christian”; those are altered manifestations of the same work. [5][7]
- **1975:** *Don’t Say It, but We Have to Say It*. A political essay assessing the international situation. The Chinese titles *不要說它，但我們不得不說*, *不要說它，但是我們要說*, *我們不得不說*, and *我們不得不說：蔣夫人檢討世界局勢專文* designate publisher-specific manifestations whose contents are reported to be substantially the same, not four securely distinct obras. [1][5]
- **1976:** *總統蔣公追思錄* (*A Memorial Record of President Chiang*). A memorial text about Chiang Kai-shek, situated between personal recollection, political commemoration, and state hagiography. [1]
- **1977:** *與鮑羅廷談話的回憶* / *Conversations with Mikhail Borodin*. A roughly 20,000-character retrospective political memoir composed in the United States; the English and differently dated Chinese printings are manifestations of one obra. [1][5][7]
- **1977:** *蔣夫人西安事變回憶錄* (*Madame Chiang’s Memoir of the Xi’an Incident*). A later Chinese manifestation of her Xi’an narrative; the bibliography does not demonstrate that it is a wholly new composition rather than a revision or republication of *Sian: A Coup d’État*. [1]
- **1984:** *蔣夫人發表公開信：勸告鄧穎超信服三民主義統一中國* (*Open Letter Urging Deng Yingchao to Accept the Three Principles of the People and Unify China*). An open letter that functions as a manifesto: its intended action is political persuasion and public positioning. [1]
- **1987:** *我將再起* (*I Shall Rise Again*). A collection of political and religious essays; two 1987 publications with expanded and unexpanded subtitles are manifestations of substantially the same collection. [1]
- **1988:** *閱讀魏德邁將軍《論戰爭與和平》一書的感言* (*Reflections on Reading General Wedemeyer’s “On War and Peace”*). An extended political response to Wedemeyer, published as a separate booklet. [1]

### Speeches, addresses, broadcasts, and public letters

- **1930–1979:** *Articles by Generalissimo and Madame Chiang Kai-shek*. An archival body of jointly and separately authored articles rather than one bounded work; the Wellesley finding aid groups the material by date but does not supply a complete item-level catalogue or resolve joint authorship. [8]
- **1938–1972 and undated:** *Speeches by Madame Chiang Kai-shek*. An archival speech corpus. It resists treatment as one obra, while the surviving manuscript, print, audio, and film forms are separate manifestations whose completeness is not claimed by the archive. [8]
- **1939:** *Address by Madame Chiang Kai-shek to the World’s Woman’s Christian Temperance Union Convention*. A religious and political address calling women to oppose war and defend international morality. [9]
- **February 18, 1943:** *Address to the United States House of Representatives*. A diplomatic speech-act appealing for American commitment to China; manuscript, printed, sound-recorded, and filmed witnesses survive, so no single medium exhausts the work. [8]
- **March 2, 1943:** *Madison Square Garden Address*. A mass-audience wartime appeal surviving as an audio recording; it belongs to the 1943 American speaking project but is also an individually situated performance. [8]
- **March 7, 1943:** *Speech at Wellesley College*. A homecoming address surviving in manuscript, typescript, print, audio, and film-related documentation. These are manifestations of a performed text, not additional works. [8]
- **March 22, 1943:** *Chicago Stadium Address*. A recorded public appeal from her American wartime tour. [8]
- **March 27, 1943:** *San Francisco Civic Auditorium Address*. A recorded diplomatic and fundraising speech. [8]
- **April 4, 1943:** *Hollywood Bowl Address*. A recorded mass-audience speech near the end of her 1943 American tour. [8]
- **1943:** *American wartime speaking tour*. A project comprising congressional testimony, college addresses, arena speeches, broadcasts, press work, fundraising, and embodied public performance. It is larger than any single transcript and approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk of rhetoric, travel, radio, film, photography, and political theater, although its collaborative governmental production prevents attributing every component solely to Madame Chiang. [8][10]
- **March 20, 1953:** *Address at Wellesley College*. A later college address preserved separately from her 1943 homecoming speech. [8]
- **1955:** *Good Friday Radio Sermon*. Madame Chiang’s contribution to a jointly published broadcast sermon with Chiang Kai-shek; responsibility for the two constituent texts is distinguishable even though the publication binds them together. [1]
- **1956:** *Good Friday Meditation*. A religious meditation published alongside Chiang Kai-shek’s Good Friday sermon. [1]
- **1960:** *Good Friday Sermon*. Madame Chiang’s contribution to another jointly issued Easter broadcast publication. [1]
- **1981:** *Address to the Chinese-American Academic and Professional Association and the American Chamber of Commerce*. A filmed speech; the bibliographic record identifies the video manifestation but does not establish whether a complete authorized transcript survives. [1]
- **1987:** *Message to the Fu Jen Catholic University graduating class of academic year 1986–87*. A ceremonial address published separately by Taiwan’s Ministry of Education. [1]
- **1988:** *Message to the alumni of Fu Jen Catholic University*. A jointly attributed ceremonial text by Chiang Kai-shek and Madame Chiang; the posthumous joint publication complicates its status as a newly made 1988 work. [1]
- **1916–1987, plus undated material:** *Correspondence, class letters, and open letters*. A dispersed textual corpus addressed to classmates, professors, college presidents, political figures, and publics. It resists enumeration as a finished work: the Wellesley archive holds only part of her correspondence, and some letters were made public as political acts. [8]

### Compiled speech and writing corpora

- **1939:** *蔣夫人言論集* (*Collected Statements of Madame Chiang*). The earliest listed edited collection of her public language. It is a manifestation assembled by the National Publishing House, not evidence that she conceived the volume as a new unified obra. [1][5]
- **1944:** *蔣夫人訪美言論集* (*Collected Statements from Madame Chiang’s American Visit*). An edited record of the American tour; its selection overlaps the speeches’ independent print and recorded manifestations. [1]
- **1956:** *蔣夫人言論彙編*, four volumes (*Collected Statements of Madame Chiang*). A major edited corpus containing essays, speeches, telegrams, letters, and conversations. Later collections overlap it but sometimes revise their texts, so none should be assumed complete or textually final. [1][5]
- **1959:** *蔣夫人言論彙編（續編）*, two volumes (*Collected Statements of Madame Chiang: Continuation*). A supplement to the 1956 corpus rather than an independently authored continuous work. [1][5]
- **1959:** *蔣夫人抵美演講集* (*Speeches on Madame Chiang’s Arrival in America*). An edited speech collection whose exact relationship to other American-tour anthologies is not fully resolved in the bibliography. [1]
- **1960:** *蔣夫人演講選集：民國四十七年至四十八年* (*Selected Speeches of Madame Chiang, 1958–1959*). A chronologically bounded official selection. [1][5]
- **1961:** *蔣夫人演講選集* (*Selected Speeches of Madame Chiang*). An official edited selection, not necessarily a new authorial arrangement. [1]
- **1963:** *蔣夫人演講選集* (*Selected Speeches of Madame Chiang*). Another official selection; the reused title conceals changed contents and should not be mistaken for a simple reprint without collation. [1]
- **1966:** *蔣夫人思想言論集*, six volumes (*Madame Chiang’s Thought and Public Statements*). A large editorial corpus framing her speeches and writings as a body of political thought. [5]
- **1967:** *蔣夫人訪美言行集* (*Madame Chiang’s Words and Actions During Her American Visit*). A hybrid documentary collection of speech and conduct: it records a political performance rather than constituting a wholly authored book. [1]
- **1968:** *蔣夫人旅美演講集* (*Madame Chiang’s Speeches in America*). A further American-tour anthology whose overlap with the 1944, 1959, and 1967 compilations remains uncollated. [1]
- **1977:** *蔣夫人言論集* (*Collected Statements of Madame Chiang*). An edited collection issued by the Chinese Women’s Anti-Communist League. [1]
- **1979:** *指導長蔣夫人對婦女的訓詞* (*Director-General Madame Chiang’s Instructions to Women*). A collection of directive texts; “protocol” fits their instructive institutional purpose better than neutral “essays.” [1]
- **1980:** *蔣夫人言論選集* / *蔣夫人言論集* (*Selected/Collected Statements of Madame Chiang*). Two closely related editorial titles issued through the Kuomintang women’s organization; the bibliography does not establish whether they are distinct selections. [1]
- **1982:** *蔣夫人言論集* (*Collected Statements of Madame Chiang*). Another retrospective compilation rather than a newly composed work. [1]
- **1986:** *蔣夫人言論集* (*Collected Statements of Madame Chiang*). A further edition or selection; its precise textual differences from preceding collections require collation. [1]
- **1986:** *國父總統蔣公暨夫人宗教言論輯要* (*Selected Religious Statements of Sun Yat-sen, President Chiang, and Madame Chiang*). A posthumously edited multi-author anthology containing her religious writings; it is a manifestation of component texts, not solely her obra. [1]
- **1987:** *蔣夫人的話* (*The Words of Madame Chiang*). An editor-selected collection and therefore an afterlife of her speech corpus rather than a securely author-designed work. [1]
- **1998:** *蔣夫人宋美齡女士言論選集* (*Selected Statements of Madame Chiang Kai-shek, Soong May-ling*). A retrospective editorial selection. [1]
- **2015:** *宋美齡文集*, five volumes (*Collected Works of Soong May-ling*). The principal modern collected-works manifestation, published after her death. It supplies a broad textual corpus but, like earlier collections, does not erase variant versions, archival writings, or disputed completeness. [3][5]

### Painting and calligraphy

- **Dates not individually established:** *One hundred paintings in Madame Chiang’s painting album*. A museum-catalogued ensemble divided into horizontal and vertical formats, fifty paintings in each part, principally landscapes, flowers, birds, orchids, and bamboo. Chiang Kai-shek sometimes added inscriptions, making at least some sheets collaborative objects rather than exclusively authored paintings. [11]
- **1967:** *蔣夫人畫頁* (*Painting Leaves by Madame Chiang*). A published portfolio or album of paintings; it is a reproduction-object whose constituent originals may have different, unrecorded dates. [1]
- **1969:** *蔣夫人畫冊* (*Album of Paintings by Madame Chiang*). A painting album and reproductive manifestation, not one painting. [1]
- **1971:** *夫人蘭冊* / *蔣夫人蘭冊* (*Madame Chiang’s Orchid Album*). Two bibliographic titles apparently referring to the same or closely related album of orchid paintings; without collation they should not be split into separate obras. [1]
- **1973:** *夫人山水冊* (*Madame Chiang’s Landscape Album*). An album of Chinese landscape paintings whose individual sheets are not separately named in the consulted bibliography. [1]
- **1974:** *夫人花卉冊* (*Madame Chiang’s Flower Album*). An album of floral paintings; the album is the catalogued object, while its sheets remain individually under-described. [1]
- **1979:** *蔣夫人山水蘭竹花卉冊* (*Album of Landscapes, Orchids, Bamboo, and Flowers by Madame Chiang*). A National Palace Museum album assembling several traditional painting genres. It may reproduce or regroup earlier sheets, so its status as a wholly new body of work is uncertain. [1]
- **1979:** *蔣夫人國畫冊* (*Album of Chinese Paintings by Madame Chiang*). A painting album issued by the Kuomintang women’s organization; its relationship to the 1979 museum album is not clarified by the bibliography. [1]
- **1985:** *蔣夫人書畫集錦* (*Selected Calligraphy and Paintings of Madame Chiang*). A reproductive anthology joining two media; it is closer to a small Gesamtkunstwerk than to a conventional authored book. [1]
- **1990:** *蔣夫人蘭竹山水花卉冊* (*Album of Orchids, Bamboo, Landscapes, and Flowers by Madame Chiang*). A National Palace Museum album whose title and contents overlap earlier albums; whether it documents new works or republishes an existing ensemble is unresolved. [1]
- **2000:** *蔣夫人宋美齡暨書畫名家跨世紀千禧聯展* (*Madame Chiang Soong May-ling and Master Calligraphers and Painters Millennium Joint Exhibition*). A group exhibition and catalogue manifestation. Her contributions are works within it, but the exhibition as a whole was collaborative and cannot be attributed to her alone. [1]
- **1967 and other dates:** *Individual paintings and reproductions presented as gifts*. Wellesley preserves at least one 1967 reproduction, while other paintings survive in public and private collections. This dispersed visual corpus resists a complete work-by-work list because the standard publication bibliography catalogues albums, not every original sheet. [8][11]

### Organizational and humanitarian projects

- **1930s:** *Women’s work of the New Life Movement*. An organizational and ideological project in which Madame Chiang directed women’s activities and translated moral doctrine into programs of hygiene, discipline, welfare, and national service. It was collective and state-supported, so it resists attribution as her solitary obra. [10][12]
- **1937 onward:** *Chinese Women’s National War Relief Society*. A humanitarian organization established under her leadership to care for soldiers’ children and other war victims. It is an institutional creation—made through administration and networks—rather than a text. [12]
- **1937 onward:** *War-orphan care and the first Nanjing orphanage for soldiers’ children*. A continuing humanitarian project involving institutions, fundraising, education, and public imagery; individual facilities and programs were collective works, not personal possessions. [12]
- **1938 onward:** *Women’s Advisory Council / New Life Movement Women’s Advisory Committee*. A national mobilization network reorganized under Madame Chiang’s leadership to coordinate women’s wartime service. The project was intentionally plural and politically contested, so naming her as sole creator would erase its many organizers. [10][13]
- **1943:** *American campaign for China*. A diplomatic and fundraising project made through speeches, meetings, broadcasts, press appearances, and travel. Its obra is the campaign’s coordinated political effect, not merely its published transcripts. [8][10]
- **1950 onward:** *Chinese Women’s Anti-Communist and Anti-Russian League*, later the National Women’s League. Madame Chiang served as founder and chairwoman, but the organization was also a product of the Nationalist state and thousands of participants; it therefore resists classification as an individually authored work. [14]
- **1950s–1960s:** *Cheng Hsin Rehabilitation Medical Center project*. A medical and social-welfare institution developed under her sponsorship for polio patients. Scholarly bibliography credits her with founding the project, but an institution’s collective labor makes “patron-founder” more accurate than sole maker. [1]
- **1940s–1950s:** *Mayling Soong Foundation*. A philanthropic and educational project documented in the Wellesley archive. The surviving records establish the organization and her association with it, but not an individually authored founding text. [8]
