## Works by Edward Bellamy

### Novels and long romances

- **1878:** *Six to One: A Nantucket Idyl*. An anonymously published courtship novel and Bellamy’s first book-length fiction; its anonymity is part of the work’s original status, not an attribution dispute. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1878–1880:** *Dr. Heidenhoff’s Process*. A psychological romance about the attempted technological erasure of memory, serialized in the *Springfield Union* in 1878–79 and issued as a book in 1880. ([Bowman, bibliography and publication history](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1879; 1900:** *The Duke of Stockbridge: A Romance of Shays’ Rebellion*. A historical novel first serialized in 1879 and published as a book only after Bellamy’s death; the posthumous volume therefore manifests an earlier work rather than dating its composition. ([Bowman, bibliography and publication history](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1884:** *Miss Ludington’s Sister: A Romance of Immortality*. A speculative psychological romance about identity, memory, and spiritualism; some later references date it 1885, but Bowman’s bibliography records the first Boston book publication as 1884. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1888:** *Looking Backward, 2000–1887*. A utopian novel and programmatic social fiction whose imagined national economy became the point of departure for an organized political movement. ([Oxford World’s Classics chronology](https://leftypol.org/edu/src/1658415575069.pdf); [University of Geneva](https://www.unige.ch/vls/bibliography/author-bibliography/bellamy-edward-1850-1898))
- **1897:** *Equality*. A sequel to *Looking Backward* and Bellamy’s last completed novel, expanding the earlier utopia’s arguments about class, gender, consumption, environment, and animal life. ([University of Geneva](https://www.unige.ch/vls/bibliography/author-bibliography/bellamy-edward-1850-1898))

### Short fiction

- **1875:** *The Cold Snap*. A short story, first published in *Scribner’s Monthly* in September 1875 and later selected by Bellamy for *The Blindman’s World and Other Stories*. ([Brown and Widdicombe](https://www.peggyannbrown.net/_files/ugd/f390f7_567fe2817d3447b6ad4b6d0524da298a.pdf); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1876:** *A Providence*. A short story published in the July 1876 *Century Magazine*; unlike fifteen tales Bellamy later selected, it was not incorporated into his 1898 collection. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1876; 1898:** *The Old Folks’ Party*. A short story about age, memory, and temporary rejuvenation, subsequently selected for Bellamy’s collected stories; references that give only 1898 are dating the collection rather than necessarily the tale’s first appearance. ([Bowman, bibliography and discussion](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1877:** *A Superfluity of Naughtiness*. A short story published in *Lippincott’s Magazine* in May 1877 and omitted from the author-selected 1898 collection. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1877:** *A Summer Evening’s Dream*. A short story first published in *Lippincott’s Monthly Magazine* in July 1877 and later collected by Bellamy. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1877:** *A Mid-Night Drama*. A short story published in *Appletons’ Journal* in August 1877; later reprints modernize the title to *A Midnight Drama*. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1877:** *Hooking Watermelons*. A short story published in *Scribner’s Monthly* in September 1877 and later included in Bellamy’s selected collection. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1877:** *Extra-Hazardous*. A short story published in *Appletons’ Journal* in November 1877, addressing class and property through comic adventure; it was not selected for the 1898 collection. ([Bowman, bibliography and discussion](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1877:** *Lost*. A short story published in *Scribner’s Monthly* in December 1877 and later collected by Bellamy. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1878:** *Two Days’ Solitary Imprisonment*. A short story published in *Appletons’ Journal* in March 1878 and later selected for the 1898 collection. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1878:** *Deserted*. A short story published in *Lippincott’s Monthly Magazine* in November 1878 and later collected by Bellamy. ([Bowman, bibliography and discussion](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1879:** *Potts’s Painless Cure*. A comic short story published in *Scribner’s Monthly* in February 1879 and later selected for the collected stories; Bowman’s transcription sometimes gives the possessive as “Pott’s.” ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1879:** *Taking a Mean Advantage*. A short story published in *Sunday Afternoon* in March 1879; it remained outside Bellamy’s 1898 selection. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1879:** *Jane Hicks*. A short story published in volume IV of *Good Company*; it was one of the known magazine stories not selected for Bellamy’s collected volume. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1880:** *A Tale of the South Pacific*. A short story published in volume V of *Good Company*, drawing on the Pacific journey Bellamy made with his brother; it was not in the 1898 collection. ([Bowman, bibliography and biography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1880:** *That Letter*. A short story published in the September 1880 *Good Company*, concerned with suspicion and jealousy; Bellamy did not include it in his selected collection. ([Bowman, bibliography and discussion](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1886:** *The Blindman’s World*. A speculative short story whose Martian observers know their own futures; it later supplied the title of Bellamy’s collected stories. ([Brown and Widdicombe](https://www.peggyannbrown.net/_files/ugd/f390f7_567fe2817d3447b6ad4b6d0524da298a.pdf); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1887:** *At Pinney’s Ranch*. A supernatural-psychological short story first published in the December 1887 *Atlantic Monthly* and later collected; standalone records dated 1898 describe its collected manifestation, not its first appearance. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1888:** *A Love Story Reversed*. A short story published in the May 1888 *Century Magazine* and later selected for Bellamy’s collection. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *To Whom This May Come*. A speculative island-utopia and telepathy story first published in *Harper’s New Monthly Magazine* in February 1889, then positioned last in Bellamy’s 1898 collection. ([publication record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Edward_Bellamy); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *An Echo of Antietam*. A Civil War memory story published in the July 1889 *Century Magazine* and later collected. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *A Positive Romance*. A speculative short story, informed by Auguste Comte’s positivism, published in the August 1889 *Century Magazine* and later collected. ([Bowman, bibliography and discussion](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *With the Eyes Shut*. A speculative railroad story published in the October 1889 *Harper’s New Monthly Magazine* and later included in Bellamy’s collection. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))

### Author-shaped collection

- **1898:** *The Blindman’s World and Other Stories*. A book-object comprising fifteen short stories chosen by Bellamy shortly before his death, with a prefatory sketch by William Dean Howells; it is both a collection and Bellamy’s retrospective act of selection, not fifteen newly created works. ([Brown and Widdicombe](https://www.peggyannbrown.net/_files/ugd/f390f7_567fe2817d3447b6ad4b6d0524da298a.pdf); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))

### Manifestos, programs, addresses, and public interventions

- **1871:** *Woman Suffrage*. Bellamy’s first known published article, appearing in *The Golden Age*; it is an argumentative intervention rather than fiction. ([Oxford World’s Classics chronology](https://leftypol.org/edu/src/1658415575069.pdf))
- **1872:** *Railroad Disasters*. A signed social-policy article in *The Golden Age*, published on March 16. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1872:** *National Education*. A signed article in *The Golden Age*, published on March 30; it proposes policy and thus approaches a protocol as well as an essay. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1872:** *The Plea of Insanity*. A signed article published in *Christian Union* on May 8. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *How I Came to Write “Looking Backward”*. An autobiographical explanatory essay published in the first issue of *The Nationalist* in May. ([Brown and Widdicombe](https://www.peggyannbrown.net/_files/ugd/f390f7_567fe2817d3447b6ad4b6d0524da298a.pdf); [Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *How We Shall Get There*. A political-strategy essay published in the *Twentieth Century* on May 11. ([Bowman, citations and bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *Plutocracy or Nationalism—Which?*. An address delivered at Tremont Temple on May 31 and circulated as *Nationalist Extra* no. 1; as movement rhetoric intended to produce adherence, it functions as a manifesto. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *Brief Summary of the Industrial Plan of Nationalism Set Forth in “Looking Backward” for Class Study*. A condensed study text published in *The Dawn* in September; its explicit use “for class study” makes it a pedagogical protocol as well as a summary. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *Looking Forward*. A political essay published in the December *Nationalist*, distinguishing the practical Nationalist movement from the fictional future of *Looking Backward*. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *Principles and Purposes of Nationalism*. Bellamy’s address for the Boston Nationalist Club’s first anniversary, delivered at Tremont Temple on December 18 and subsequently printed as a pamphlet and in *The Nationalist*; it is a movement manifesto. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889:** *What “Nationalism” Is*. A tract defining Bellamy’s political Nationalism, issued as *Pacific Nationalist Tract* no. 1. ([Bowman, citations](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1890:** *“Looking Backward” Again*. A substantial reply to critics published in the March *North American Review*. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1890:** *A Personal Explanation*. A statement published in the April *Nationalist*, clarifying Bellamy’s position within the movement. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1890:** *First Steps Toward Nationalism*. A practical political essay published in the October *Forum*; its proposed transition from present institutions toward Nationalism gives it the character of a protocol. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1891:** *Our Prospective Sovereigns*. A political essay in *The Nationalist* considering the coming generation as the constituency and inheritor of reform. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1891:** *Woman in the Year 2000*. A speculative social essay published in the February *Ladies’ Home Journal*, extending Bellamy’s future history to women’s status. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy))
- **1891:** *Talks on Nationalism*. A sequence of short Socratic dialogues between “Mr. Smith” and successive objectors, published in *The New Nation* from February through October; it resists reduction to either essay or fiction because invented conversations perform movement instruction. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1892:** *Progress of Nationalism in the United States*. A movement report and argument published in the June *North American Review*. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1892:** *Fourth of July, 1992*. A future-dated civic satire published in the *Boston Globe* on July 4; it lies between political essay and speculative fiction. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1892:** *A Nationalist View of the Homestead Situation*. A topical political intervention on the Homestead conflict, published in the *Boston Globe* on July 7 and reprinted in *The New Nation*. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1893:** *How to Employ the Unemployed in Mutual Maintenance*. An eight-page plan for a self-supporting settlement with its own scrip; more protocol and social-design project than descriptive essay. ([standard published-works bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy))
- **1893:** *The Outcome of the Battle of Standards*. A political-economic article published in the *Boston Globe* on July 16 and later reprinted. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1894:** *The Programme of the Nationalists*. A manifesto published in the March *Forum*, setting out the movement’s political program rather than merely describing its beliefs. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1895:** *Christmas in the Year 2000*. A future-set seasonal sketch published in the January *Ladies’ Home Journal*; it occupies the border between social prophecy, journalism, and fiction. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1897:** *The Parable of the Water-Tank*. A political-economic parable embedded in *Equality* and subsequently circulated independently; its separate title names a detachable manifesto-like unit, but it is not a separately composed book. ([Wikisource bibliography](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Edward_Bellamy); [Bowman’s record of the later independent printing](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))

### Editorial and movement projects

- **1880–1881:** *Springfield Daily News*. A newspaper Bellamy established with his brother Charles and for which he wrote editorials; it is best treated as a collaborative editorial project and material platform, not as a wholly authored Bellamy text. Bowman identifies individual contributions but also records many anonymous pieces, so the exact boundary of Bellamy’s authorship remains incomplete. ([Bowman, biography and bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1889–1891:** *The Nationalist*. A movement periodical to which Bellamy contributed signed essays, statements, and correspondence; he did not make the entire journal, and it therefore resists inclusion as a single-authored work. ([Bowman, bibliography](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1891–1894:** *The New Nation*. Bellamy’s weekly newspaper and principal organizational instrument for Nationalism, combining editing, political organizing, serialized dialogue, reporting, and hundreds of signed or attributed editorials. It is a collaborative movement-project—nearly a political Gesamtkunstwerk of text, organization, and action—whose individual unsigned components cannot all be assigned to Bellamy with equal certainty. ([University of Geneva](https://www.unige.ch/vls/bibliography/author-bibliography/bellamy-edward-1850-1898); [Bowman, bibliography and attribution caveat](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))

### Unfinished, unpublished, and resistant projects

- **c. 1873:** *The Religion of Solidarity*. An unpublished philosophical manuscript formulating Bellamy’s account of the individual, humanity, and a universal spirit; he reread and reaffirmed it in 1887. Its manuscript status makes it a private intellectual project rather than a public work during his lifetime. ([Bowman, biography and manuscript study](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **late 1870s–1890s:** *Eliot Carson* story. An unfinished and unpublished narrative project about withdrawal from business into a modern hermitage devoted to intellectual and spiritual development; Bellamy returned to it intermittently for nearly twenty years, so the sustained project—not a nonexistent completed book—is the work. ([Bowman, manuscript account](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **date uncertain:** *Almost a Suicide*. An unpublished or previously uncollected fictional manuscript later printed under this editorial title in *Apparitions of Things to Come* (1990); its lifetime title, completion state, and intended publication status are not securely established by the accessible bibliographies. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy))
- **date uncertain:** *Plots for Stories*. A posthumously printed group of story-project notes rather than completed short stories. It resists being called one work because the surviving object is a cluster of unrealized intentions. ([chronological bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Bellamy))
- **date uncertain:** Untitled temperance-movement novel plan. A journal memorandum for a satirical and philosophical novel about temperance reform, drinking, and reformers; no completed novel is known, and imposing a title would falsely convert a project into a finished work. ([Bowman, manuscript account](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
- **1870s–1890s:** Journals, notebooks, correspondence, fragments, and newspaper editorials. These constitute a documentary body of work preserved principally in the Bellamy papers at Harvard’s Houghton Library, but they resist enumeration as stable titled works: much is unpublished, fragmentary, untitled, or anonymously printed, and Bowman explicitly says even her then-most-complete bibliography omitted identified but insignificant articles. ([Bowman, scope and completeness statement](https://manuals.plus/m/3a1dcf6351d8fb582e2601f653be46a59a033b12584150a353c1819c6bd7a4f0))
