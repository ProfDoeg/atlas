## Works by Ángel Gregorio Villoldo

### Tangos and tango-milongas

- **Date unknown:** *Acorazado Rivadavia*. Tango attributed to Villoldo; the surviving catalogues do not supply a secure composition date.
- **Date unknown:** *Bazar de la mescolanza*. Tango with words and music credited to Villoldo.
- **Date unknown:** *Brisas rosarinas*. Instrumental tango.
- **Date unknown:** *Calandria*. Tango with a dramatic, compadrito-centered text.
- **Date unknown:** *Cantar eterno*. Tango; its title identifies a composition, not the later recordings and editions through which it circulated.
- **Date unknown:** *Cariño criollo*. Tango credited to Villoldo.
- **Date unknown:** *Catorce*. Tango; not to be confused with *Trece*.

- **1910:** *Cuerpo de alambre*. Tango with words and music by Villoldo.

- **Date unknown:** *Desafío de un entrerriano*. Tango.

- **Date unknown:** *El cachorrito*. Tango.
- **Date unknown:** *El caprichoso*. Tango.

- **Date unknown:** *El criollo más criollo*. Tango with a character-portrait text.

### Milongas and other dances

- **Date unknown:** *Bolada de aficionado*. Milonga, although some modern catalogues classify it broadly as a tango.
- **Date unknown:** *El más pesao*. Milonga with a compadrito-centered text.
- **Date unknown:** *Matufias (o El arte de vivir)*. Socially satirical milonga; the parenthetical wording functions as an alternative title.
- **Date unknown:** *El carrero y el cochero*. Milonga structured as a confrontation between two popular urban types; it is documented in scholarship on Villoldo but omitted from Todo Tango’s shorter works index.
- **1905:** *La florista*. Mazurka.
- **Date unknown:** *Salve, furlana!*. Furlana, an Italian dance-form composition rather than a tango.
- **Date unknown:** *La promesa*. Zamba.
- **Date unknown:** *En la ausencia*. Waltz.
- **Date unknown:** *Belgique*. Waltz.

The genre identifications come from Todo Tango’s work and score indexes; *El carrero y el cochero* is identified in Rivadeneira’s discussion of Villoldo’s milongas. ### Words made for music by other composers

- **1905:** *La morocha*. Villoldo made the words; Enrique Saborido composed the music. The song is therefore a collaborative obra, not wholly a Villoldo composition.
- **Date unknown:** *El entrerriano*. Villoldo supplied words to Rosendo Mendizábal’s pre-existing instrumental tango; the underlying tango is not his.
- **1913:** *Trece*. Villoldo’s words were attached to music by Alberico Spátola; sources that simply file the title under Villoldo can obscure this divided authorship.
- **Date unknown:** *Apolo*. Villoldo supplied words to music by Alfredo Bevilacqua.
- **Date unknown:** *La bicicleta*. Villoldo recorded and apparently adapted or supplied a vocal text for a piece by Antonio Rodríguez Martínez. A modern scholarly edition explicitly calls the attribution to Villoldo erroneous or uncertain, so it cannot safely be counted as an original Villoldo composition.

These are textual contributions inside collaborative or pre-existing musical objects. The disputed attribution of *La bicicleta* is expressly noted in the modern critical transcription. [Todo Tango](https://www.todotango.com/creadores/ficha/33/angel-Villoldo) [Jurado and Enríquez, *Los pianistas del tango: Milonga*](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/161148/Documento_completo.pdf-PDFA.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

### Comic recitations, monologues, dialogues, and scenes

- **Date unknown:** *La farra de Rosa “La Piojito”*. Comic recitation: a spoken performance-text rather than a song.
- **Date unknown:** *Desgracias de un marido*. Comic recitation.
- **Date unknown:** *Doña Juana Rabanito*. Comic recitation or character monologue.
- **Date unknown:** *El baile de Ña Baldomera*. Comic recitation.
- **Date unknown:** *Las viejas solteronas*. Comic recitation.
- **Date unknown:** *Recetas del Dr. Macanitis*. Comic recitation organized as mock prescriptions.
- **Date unknown:** *Testamento de un paisano*. Comic recitation in the form of a mock testament.
- **1907:** *Celos de dos cordobeses*. Recorded recitation or dialogue attributed to Villoldo. Its existence is documented discographically, but whether the recorded wording preserves a separately published, stable text remains uncertain.

These pieces resist treatment as conventional literary “works”: their objects include a script or verbal design, Villoldo’s delivery, and a recorded or theatrical manifestation. The first seven appear in Todo Tango’s genre-indexed works list; the last is documented in the historical discography. ### Printed collections and serial writing

- **1889:** *Cantos criollos*. Collection of Villoldo’s verse or song texts. The volume is an anthology-object as well as a container for individual verbal works, and surviving references do not establish that every later song title was already present in this edition.
- **Dates unknown:** *Monologues, dialogues, and costumbrista scenes published in Caras y Caretas*. A dispersed body of short comic and topical texts rather than one securely bounded work; the consulted scholarship confirms the activity but does not provide a complete article-level bibliography.

The collection date is reported in biographical scholarship; Villoldo’s periodical production is described by the Biblioteca Nacional de Maestros in its account of Rivadeneira’s documentary research. [Biblioteca Nacional de Maestros](https://bnm.educacion.gob.ar/tertulia-americana-de-abril-angel-villoldo-en-el-inicio-del-tango-y-de-las-varietes/) [Biographical repertory summary](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Villoldo)

### Sound-recorded manifestations

- **1905–1914:** *Known commercial and cylinder recordings by Ángel Villoldo*. Rivadeneira’s discographical research counts 541 known phonograms featuring Villoldo, 477 of them performances of material attributed to him. This corpus resists being called 541 separate “works”: each disc or cylinder is a made sonic manifestation, while duplicated takes, reissues, performances of others’ compositions, and uncertain label attributions prevent a one-to-one equation between phonogram and obra.
- **26 December 1907:** *El negro alegre*. Villoldo’s voice-and-guitar recording, issued from Victor matrix/disc documentation as Victor 62133/F-27; it is a particular performance-object distinct from the tango itself.
- **1907:** *La bicicleta*. Recording by Villoldo with pianist Manuel Campoamor. It survives as a sonic manifestation even though the underlying composition is by Antonio Rodríguez Martínez and was formerly attributed to Villoldo.
- **Circa 1909–1910:** *El choclo*. Villoldo’s recorded performance of his tango; discographical dating varies between 1909 and 1910, so no single year should be silently imposed.

The phonogram count and authorship problem come from Mariana N. Tsiftsis’s scholarly study, which cites Rivadeneira’s discography and examines conflicting label attributions. [Tsiftsis, “Cuestiones de autoría en grabaciones de tangos de Villoldo (1910)”](https://www.ism.unl.edu.ar/investigacion/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2018/09/Actas_2016_AAM_INM.pdf) [Discography of American Historical Recordings](https://adp.library.ucsb.edu/index.php/matrix/detail/2000085801/55286-El_choclo)

### Instrument and performance system

- **Date unknown:** *Combined guitar-and-harmonica apparatus*. Villoldo devised a support placing a harmonica above his guitar so that he could play both instruments simultaneously. This is a made performance device or practical project, not a text or musical composition; the available description does not establish a patent, fixed title, or surviving specimen.

The device is described in biographical accounts of Villoldo’s performing practice. [Ángel Villoldo biography](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Villoldo)
