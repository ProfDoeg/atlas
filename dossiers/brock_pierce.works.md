## Works by Brock Jeffrey Pierce

### Screen performances

- **1992:** *The Mighty Ducks*. Feature-film performance as Gordon Bombay at age ten; the obra attributable to Pierce is the acted role, not the film as a whole. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1994:** *D2: The Mighty Ducks*. Feature-film performance reprising the young Gordon Bombay. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1994:** *Little Big League*. Feature-film performance as Sidney. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1995:** *Ripper Man*. Feature-film performance as Kevin. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1995:** *Problem Child 3: Junior in Love*. Television-film performance as Duke Phlim. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1995:** *Three Wishes*. Feature-film performance as Scott. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1996:** *First Kid*. Feature-film performance as Luke Davenport, the fictional son of the U.S. president. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1996:** *Earth Minus Zero*. Feature-film performance as Joey Heller. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1997:** *Two Small Voices* (also catalogued as *Two Voices*). Television-film performance as Brad; catalogues disagree over the displayed title but not the credit. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1997:** *The Ride*. Feature-film performance as Danny O’Neil; some databases incorrectly attach the character name Sidney, apparently importing his *Little Big League* role. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **1997:** *Legend of the Lost Tomb*. Television-film performance as John Robie. ([filmography](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **2025:** *The Last Gunfight*. Feature-film performance as Peter Demarest, marking Pierce’s return to a credited fictional role after his child-acting career. ([IMDb filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0682302/); [Parsifal Media](https://www.parsifal.com/))

### Documentary self-manifestations

- **2014:** *An Open Secret*. Documentary manifestation assembled from archive footage of Pierce rather than a new performance made for the film; it therefore resists classification as his work. ([filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Pierce#Filmography))
- **2015–2017:** *Play Money*. Documentary self-appearance; sources disagree between 2015 and 2017 for its date, apparently reflecting production and release rather than two separate works. ([TV Guide](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/); [Fandango](https://www.fandango.com/people/brock-pierce-1408668))
- **2018:** *BIT X BIT: In Bitcoin We Trust*. Documentary for which Pierce received an executive-producer credit and appeared as himself; it is consequently both a production obra and a self-manifestation. ([TV Guide](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **2020:** *Landfall*. Documentary self-appearance, not authorship of the documentary as a whole. ([filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Pierce#Filmography))

### Produced moving-image works

- **1998:** *Chad’s World*. Short-form web serial produced by Pierce for the Digital Entertainment Network; contemporary databases variously call it a short film or an online program, evidence of an early object that resisted the film/television distinction. ([IMDbPro production record](https://pro.imdb.com/title/tt0960743/))
- **2023:** *Christmas Couples Retreat*. Television movie credited to Pierce as producer. ([IMDb filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0682302/))
- **2024:** *Christmas Eve in Miller’s Point*. Feature film credited to Pierce as executive producer. ([TV Guide](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/); [Parsifal Media](https://www.parsifal.com/))
- **2024:** *Dark Night of the Soul*. Feature film credited to Pierce as executive producer. ([IMDb filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0682302/); [TV Guide](https://www.tvguide.com/celebrities/brock-pierce/credits/3030510014/))
- **2025–:** *CryptoKnights*. Television-series project on which Pierce is identified as executive producer and co-star; its status is less securely documented than his released films, and the producer’s own company presents it as an active project. ([Parsifal Media](https://www.parsifal.com/))
- **2026:** *Dark Reckoning*. Feature-film production credited to Pierce as executive producer; its catalogue date is 2026, but the available filmography does not establish a completed public release, so it remains at least partly a project. ([IMDb filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0682302/))

### Technical consultancy

- **2014:** *Silicon Valley*. Technical-consultant contribution to the television series. ### Networked-media and gaming projects

- **1998–2000:** *Digital Entertainment Network (DEN)*. Internet-video project that commissioned original episodic programs for distinct audience niches. Sources disagree over whether Pierce was a co-founder, a minor founding partner, or simply a key executive, so no sole authorship should be inferred. ([Los Angeles Times reporting summarized with references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Entertainment_Network))
- **2001:** *Internet Gaming Entertainment (IGE)*. Company and marketplace for exchanging MMORPG currency, accounts, and other virtual goods, co-founded with Alan Debonneville. It is a commercial system rather than a single text, and its practices were controversial because they commonly violated game publishers’ rules. ([IGE history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGE); [Wired profile](https://www.wired.com/2008/11/ff-ige/))
- **2003:** *ZAM*. Network of MMORPG information and community sites founded by Pierce; its changing aggregation of sites makes it a platform-project and body of work rather than a stable singular object. ([career account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Pierce#Internet_Gaming_Entertainment))
- **2012–2013:** *Playsino*. Social-casino game publisher and platform produced by rebranding the already-existing Titan Gaming while Pierce was chief executive. Because Titan predated his leadership, Playsino should not be treated unambiguously as a company created from nothing by Pierce. ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2012/04/19/new-funding-in-tow-playsino-places-its-bet-on-social-casino-gaming/))

### Cryptocurrency institutions, protocols, and financial instruments

- **2013:** *Mastercoin Foundation*. Institutional project for supporting the Mastercoin protocol and token sale. Pierce described himself as a founding board member, while explicitly rejecting claims that he invented either Mastercoin or the ICO; the protocol’s originating idea belonged to J. R. Willett. ([Pierce interview](https://ct.com/interview/blockchain-tech-may-allow-developing-world-to-leapfrog-developed-world-brock-pierce))
- **2013:** *GoCoin*. Cryptocurrency-payment company co-founded by Pierce. It is a payment infrastructure project, not a protocol authored by him alone. ([Pierce interview](https://thefintechtimes.com/brock-pierce-interview-tales-from-the-bitcoin-floor/))
- **2013:** *Blockchain Capital* (initially *Crypto Currency Partners*). Cryptocurrency-focused venture-capital firm co-founded with Bart and Bradford Stephens; accounts differ over the Stephens brothers’ and Pierce’s relative founding roles, so it is necessarily a collective obra. ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2017/03/16/investment-firm-blockchain-capital-is-launching-a-10-million-ico/); [origin interview](https://modernconsensus.com/people/innovators/interview-brad-stephens-blockchain-capital/))
- **2014:** *Realcoin / Tether (USDT)*. Stablecoin project co-founded with Reeve Collins and Craig Sellars, initially announced as Realcoin and subsequently renamed Tether. The token and its monetary mechanism are collaborative protocol-like and financial creations, not Pierce’s sole-authored text; Pierce says his involvement ended in 2015. ([Tether history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tether_(cryptocurrency)); [career account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brock_Pierce#Bitcoin_and_cryptocurrency))
- **2014:** *Noble Markets*. Cryptocurrency-market infrastructure venture which Pierce says he started and which was later associated with Noble Bank; the precise corporate relationship and his founding share are not consistently described in independent accounts. ([Pierce interview](https://thefintechtimes.com/brock-pierce-interview-tales-from-the-bitcoin-floor/))
- **2017:** *BCAP token*. Blockchain Capital’s digital security token, created to finance its third fund. Pierce participated through Blockchain Capital, but sources do not establish that he personally authored the token’s technical protocol. ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2017/03/16/investment-firm-blockchain-capital-is-launching-a-10-million-ico/))
- **2017:** *Block.one*. Blockchain-software company co-founded by Pierce. The company, rather than Pierce alone, developed EOSIO, and distinguishing the institution from its software is essential. ([SEC order concerning Block.one](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2019/33-10714.pdf); [Bit Digital SEC presentation](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1710350/000121390023050720/ea180717ex99-1_bitdigital.pdf))
- **2017–2018:** *EOSIO*. Open-source blockchain software developed and released by Block.one. Pierce’s co-founder status connects him to the project, but neither the white paper nor the code is securely attributable to him personally; calling EOSIO “his protocol” would therefore overstate authorship. ([SEC order](https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/admin/2019/33-10714.pdf))
- **2018:** *EOS Alliance*. Community organization co-founded by Pierce to coordinate and support the EOS ecosystem. It is an institutional project rather than the EOSIO protocol itself. ([DNA Fund biography](https://dna.fund/about-us))
- **2021:** *Helios*. EOS-oriented venture and infrastructure project led by Pierce, intended to fund ecosystem development, financial products, documentation, and community events. It is best understood as an open-ended project rather than a completed obra. ([Block.one announcement reported by Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/block-one-invests-150m-in-brock-pierce-led-eos-venture-capital-firm))
- **date uncertain:** *DNA Fund / DNA Holdings*. Web3, cryptocurrency, artificial-intelligence, and capital-markets investment platform co-founded with Scott Walker. Its own institutional history does not supply an unambiguous founding date, so none is imposed here. ([DNA Fund](https://dna.fund/about-us))

### Political manifestos and campaigns

- **2020:** *Brock Pierce for President / America 2.0*. Independent presidential campaign conceived as a technology-centered political project; its policy document, *Pillars for Policy*, functions as a manifesto because it seeks both to state and enact a program of universal basic income, digital-government reform, health-care reform, drug-policy change, and monetary modernization. Authorship was necessarily collective campaign labor rather than demonstrably Pierce’s alone. ([campaign policy document](https://d3n8a8pro7vhmx.cloudfront.net/themes/5f2971cb4764e844c5260adf/attachments/original/1599599969/Brock_Pierce_for_President_EPK_9.01.20.pdf); [FEC results](https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/2020presgeresults.pdf))
- **2020:** *Independent National Convention*. Announced gathering intended to provide a platform for minor-party and independent candidates. It is best classified as a political event-project rather than a conventional work. ([Casper Star-Tribune](https://trib.com/news/state-and-regional/govt-and-politics/independent-presidential-candidate-announces-plans-for-independent-national-convention-in-cheyenne/article_25c36ebf-55ea-509f-bab2-d153a2647eda.html))
- **2021–2022:** *Pierce for U.S. Senate exploratory campaign*. Political project initiated through an FEC organization filing but never completed as a ballot candidacy; its incompletion is part of the work’s status. ([Vermont Business Magazine](https://vermontbiz.com/news/2022/april/22/brock-pierce-ran-president-and-now-us-senate-vermont); [VTDigger](https://vtdigger.org/2022/06/06/does-brock-pierce-live-in-vermont-answering-yes-could-cost-the-senate-candidate-his-puerto-rican-tax-haven/))

### Philanthropic and educational projects

- **2020–:** *Brock Pierce Foundation*. Philanthropic institution founded by Pierce; publicly available profiles establish the entity but do not provide a catalogue of separately authored works. ([organizational profile](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/brock-pierce-foundation))
- **2021:** *NYPD Gaming Trucks initiative*. Mobile gaming facilities for New York schoolchildren funded by Pierce. These are functional social objects rather than texts, and reports credit him as funder rather than sole designer. ([ABC7 New York](https://abc7ny.com/nypd-gaming-trucks-brock-pierce-video-games/11147496/))
- **2025–:** *Anatevka business school*. Planned educational institution near Kyiv whose foundation stone Pierce laid jointly with Chief Rabbi Moshe Azman. It remains a project rather than a completed building or school, and current descriptions do not establish Pierce as its architect or sole creator. ([Office of the Chief Rabbi of Ukraine](https://www.officeofchiefrabbi.org/brock-pierce); [NaNovosti report](https://ukr.co.il/?p=53622))
