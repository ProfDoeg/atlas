## Works by Domingo Faustino Sarmiento

### Educational institutions and projects

- **1825:** *School at San Francisco del Monte*. A small literacy school organized with José de Oro; it is a pedagogical project and institution rather than a text. [Cronología]
- **1832:** *School at Pocuro*. A small school founded during Sarmiento’s second Chilean exile; contemporary documentation is insufficient to supply a more definite title. [Cronología]
- **1839:** *Prospecto de un establecimiento de educación para señoritas dirigido por D. Domingo F. Sarmiento*. A prospectus that functions as a protocol and founding manifesto for a school, not merely as its description. [Cronología; OC IV]
- **1839:** *Colegio de Pensionistas de Santa Rosa de América*. A girls’ school founded in San Juan and directed with Sarmiento’s collaborators; the institution, not its prospectus alone, is the made thing. Sources vary between shortened forms such as “Colegio de Señoritas” and the fuller contemporary name. [Cronología; OC IV]
- **1842:** *Escuela Normal de Preceptores de Santiago*. Founded through the Chilean government and directed initially by Sarmiento, it was the first normal school in Spanish America. It is necessarily a collaborative state institution, so sole authorship would overstate Sarmiento’s role. [Cronología; Memoria Chilena]
- **1856–1861:** *Buenos Aires school-building and teacher-training program*. As head of the provincial Department of Schools, Sarmiento devised a network of new schools, promoted teacher formation, languages, textbooks and purpose-built schoolhouses. This is a distributed administrative project rather than a single titled obra. [Cronología; OC XXVIII, XXX, XLIV]
- **1862–1864:** *Quinta Normal de Agricultura de San Juan*. An experimental agricultural and educational institution created during his governorship; legislation, land, cultivation and instruction form a composite obra. [Cronología]
- **1868–1874:** *Presidential education and science program*. The normal schools, subsidized provincial primary schools, popular libraries, National College expansion, Colegio Militar, Escuela Naval, Observatorio Nacional Argentino and Academia Nacional de Ciencias were made through laws, appropriations and many collaborators. They resist attribution as personal “works,” but together constitute Sarmiento’s governmental project. [OC XXVIII–XXX; Museo Histórico Sarmiento]
- **1869:** *Primer Censo Nacional de la República Argentina*. A state statistical protocol ordered by Sarmiento’s administration and executed by a large organization; it is attributable to his government, not to him as sole author. [OC L–LI]
- **1870:** *Comisión Protectora de Bibliotecas Populares*. An institutional mechanism created under Law 419 to establish and support public libraries. It is a governmental protocol and continuing network rather than a conventional authored object. [OC XXX]
- **1875–1881:** *Buenos Aires school administration and Consejo Nacional de Educación project*. Sarmiento’s work as provincial director of schools and later national superintendent included regulations, reports and institutional design; the surviving textual body is gathered, imperfectly, under editorial headings such as *Informes sobre educación* and *Educar al soberano*. [OC XLIV, XLVII; Cronología]

### Pedagogical protocols, linguistic proposals and textbooks

- **1843:** *Memoria sobre ortografía americana*. A report presented to the Faculty of Humanities of the University of Chile proposing a phonetic American spelling reform; it is both linguistic argument and reform manifesto. [BVMC; OC IV]
- **1845:** *Método de lectura gradual*. A literacy primer and classroom protocol, repeatedly revised and widely used in Chilean schools. Some summaries date it to 1842, when Sarmiento’s normal-school work began, but bibliographical accounts generally identify the first book publication with 1845; the discrepancy should not be silently harmonized. [Memoria Chilena; Cronología; OC IV]
- **1849:** *De la educación popular*. A comparative report and program for primary education derived from Sarmiento’s official journey; it moves between travel inquiry, policy proposal and operational educational protocol. [BVMC; OC XI]
- **1849:** *Manual de la historia de los pueblos antiguos y modernos*. Sarmiento’s Spanish translation and adaptation of a school manual by David Lévi Alvarès; the underlying work is Alvarès’s, while Sarmiento made the Spanish pedagogical version. [BVMC]
- **1849:** *Moción presentada al Congreso Nacional por el señor diputado don Manuel Montt sobre instrucción primaria*. A policy text associated with Montt and Sarmiento. The bibliographical record names Montt prominently and lists Sarmiento as joint author, so it should not be treated as an uncontested solo work. [BVMC]
- **1856:** *Educación común*. A policy treatise on common schooling, school finance and administration, produced from reports and earlier interventions rather than as a wholly self-contained literary composition. [OC XII]
- **1879–1882:** *La escuela ultrapampeana*. A polemical educational project assembled from writings against the school policies of the period. Its familiar title is also the retrospective heading of volume XLVIII of the collected works, so the boundaries of the “work” are editorially unstable. [OC XLVIII]
- **Various dates:** *Informes, regulations, speeches and circulars on education*. The collected edition preserves these in *Ideas pedagógicas*, *Las escuelas y las bibliotecas populares*, *Informes sobre educación* and *Educar al soberano*. These are bodies of administrative writing, not four securely bounded books made under those titles. [OC XXVIII, XXX, XLIV, XLVII]

### Newspapers and magazines

- **1839:** *El Zonda*. A San Juan newspaper founded and edited by Sarmiento; political pressure ended its first run. It was revived under his patronage in 1862, but the revival was a collective periodical rather than a wholly new solo text. [BVMC; Cronología]
- **1841:** *El Nacional*. A Chilean newspaper directed by Sarmiento for a period. Direction and editorial intervention make it part of his periodical work, although neither the paper nor every unsigned contribution can be assigned to him alone. [Cronología; OC I–II]
- **1842–1845:** *El Progreso*. A Santiago daily founded and initially edited by Sarmiento. It is a collective, serial political manifestation and the first publication site of several writings later treated as books. [Cronología; OC I–II, VI–VII]
- **1842:** *El Heraldo Argentino*. A short-lived émigré newspaper founded with Vicente Fidel López. Its collaborative authorship and serial form resist classification as a single Sarmiento text. [Cronología; OC VI]
- **1848:** *La Tribuna*. A Chilean newspaper founded with Julio Belín; Sarmiento’s role was entrepreneurial and editorial as well as authorial. [Cronología]
- **1849–1850:** *La Crónica*. A Santiago newspaper founded and edited by Sarmiento as an organ of political and educational intervention. Its articles later migrated into several volumes of the collected works. [Cronología; OC VI]
- **1851:** *Sud-América*. A newspaper founded during the campaign against Juan Manuel de Rosas. It is a political instrument and collective periodical rather than a stable authored book. [Cronología; OC VI]
- **1855–1860:** *El Nacional* of Buenos Aires: Sarmiento’s editorial corpus. Sarmiento did not create this newspaper, but his sustained articles form a distinct body of political work subsequently redistributed among the collected volumes. The corpus resists being called one work because attribution and boundaries depend on periodical research. [Cronología; OC XVI–XVII, XXIV–XXV]
- **1867–1868:** *Ambas Américas: Revista de Educación, Bibliografía i Agricultura*. A New York periodical conceived and directed by Sarmiento; three documented numbers appeared. It is simultaneously an educational project, trans-American network and serial publication. [BVMC; OC XXIX]
- **1875:** *La Educación en la Provincia de Buenos Aires*. An official educational magazine founded while Sarmiento directed the provincial schools. Its institutional authorship is collective even though he created and shaped the periodical. [Cronología]
- **1885–1888:** *El Censor*. A Buenos Aires newspaper founded by Sarmiento as an oppositional political platform. It is best understood as an acting text—a sustained polemical manifestation—rather than as a single authored volume. [Cronología; OC XXXIX–XL]
- **1839–1888:** *Journalistic articles*. Hundreds of signed, pseudonymous and unsigned pieces appeared across Argentine, Chilean, Uruguayan and United States newspapers. The 52-volume edition groups them by subject rather than preserving a definitive article-level catalogue; consequently, “every article” cannot honestly be converted into a secure list of independent works. [OC I–II, VI, XVI–XVII, XXIV–XXVI, XXXIV–XLII, XLVI, LII; Museo Histórico Sarmiento]

### Autobiography, travel and correspondence

- **1843:** *Mi defensa*. An autobiographical polemic written to rebut accusations by Manuel Godoy. It is at once self-portrait, legal-style defence and public act. [BVMC; OC III]
- **1845–1847; published 1849–1851:** *Viajes en Europa, África i América*. Travel letters and observations made during an official educational mission, first issued in two volumes. Its epistolary, documentary and report-like parts make it more than a unitary travel book. [BVMC; OC V]
- **1845–1847:** *Diario de gastos*. A working travel record surviving beside *Viajes*. Modern critical editions print it with that work, but its utilitarian form and separate manuscript history resist treating it simply as another literary book. [Bibliografía BVMC]
- **1850:** *Recuerdos de provincia*. A memoir, family genealogy, political self-justification and collective portrait of San Juan. Its hybrid construction is central to what the text is, not a defect in classification. [BVMC; OC III]
- **1833–1888:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of familial, political, diplomatic and intellectual letters survive in dispersed archives and later epistolaries. They are made texts, but not one work conceived by Sarmiento; published collections differ in addressees, coverage and completeness. [Bibliografía BVMC; Museo Histórico Sarmiento]
- **Late writings; collected posthumously:** *Reminiscencias de la vida literaria*. Autobiographical recollections preserved and titled through later editorial transmission. Because the collected form is posthumous, its boundary as an independently intended work is uncertain. [BVMC; OC XLVI]

### Political projects, manifestos and constitutional writing

- **1850:** *Argirópolis, o la capital de los Estados Confederados del Río de la Plata*. A geopolitical project proposing an island capital and a confederation of the Río de la Plata states. It is a manifesto for a future polity as much as a descriptive treatise. [BVMC; OC XIII]
- **1852:** *Campaña en el Ejército Grande aliado de Sud-América*. A campaign diary, documentary dossier, memoir and polemic against Justo José de Urquiza. Its incorporation of letters and military records makes it a composite political obra. [BVMC; OC XIV]
- **1853:** *Las ciento y una*. Five open letters answering Juan Bautista Alberdi’s *Cartas quillotanas*. The numerical title is deliberately hyperbolic: the work does not contain 101 letters. [Cronología; OC XV]
- **1853:** *Comentarios de la Constitución de la Confederación Argentina*. A constitutional commentary and intervention in the practical construction of the new polity. [OC VIII]
- **1853:** *Memoria enviada al Instituto Histórico de Francia*. A historical-political report issued separately; it should not be confused with the 1843 orthographic *Memoria*. [Portadas BVMC]
- **1855–1860:** *Provinciano en Buenos Aires, porteño en las provincias*. A political corpus whose familiar title comes from its arrangement in the collected works; it is not securely a single contemporaneously issued book. [OC XVI]
- **1859–1861:** *La unión nacional*. Newspaper interventions advocating and contesting the terms of national reunification. As collected, it is an editorially delimited political corpus rather than one original codex. [OC XVII]
- **1860:** *El camino del Lacio*. Political writings concerning the route from provincial conflict toward national organization. The book-like title belongs to the later collected arrangement, making its status as a discrete lifetime work uncertain. [OC XXVI]
- **1868–1874:** *Papeles del Presidente*. Messages, decrees, memoranda, correspondence and administrative papers generated during Sarmiento’s presidency. They form a governmental archive made with secretaries, ministers and institutions, not a solo literary work. [OC L–LI]
- **1874–1880:** *Las doctrinas revolucionarias*. A retrospective editorial grouping of Sarmiento’s writings on rebellion, elections and political legitimacy. It is a body of intervention rather than a book originally planned under this exact title. [OC XXXIX]
- **1880:** *Los desfallecimientos y los desvíos*. Another collected political corpus, centered on the crisis of 1880; the title and unity owe much to the editors of the complete works. [OC XL]
- **1883:** *Conflicto y armonías de las razas en América*, volume I. A historical and sociological synthesis framed by nineteenth-century racial theories. Those theories are integral to the work’s argument but are not scientifically accepted descriptions of human difference. [Cronología; OC XXXVII]
- **1883–1888; posthumously completed editorially:** *Conflicto y armonías de las razas en América*, projected continuation. Sarmiento intended a second part but did not bring the whole project to a final authorial state before his death; volume II of the collected works is therefore an unfinished project mediated by editors. [OC XXXVIII]

### Biography, history and memorial writing

- **1845:** *Vida del general fray Félix Aldao*. A political biography first serialized in *El Progreso* and later incorporated with *Facundo* and *El Chacho*. The 1868 issue sometimes catalogued as its publication date is a later edition, not the work’s first appearance. [Cronología; BVMC; OC VII]
- **1845:** *Civilización i barbarie: Vida de Juan Facundo Quiroga y aspecto físico, costumbres y hábitos de la República Argentina*. First serialized and then issued as a volume, it combines biography, political manifesto, geography, sociology and literary narrative. Successive authorial editions altered its title and contents, so no single version exhausts the obra. [Cronología; Bibliografía BVMC; OC VII]
- **1857:** *Biografía del general San Martín*. A short commemorative biography rather than Sarmiento’s projected comprehensive history of the independence leader. [BVMC]
- **1863; revised edition 1868:** *El Chacho, último caudillo de la montonera de los Llanos*. A partisan biography and justification written amid the conflict surrounding Ángel Vicente Peñaloza’s killing. Sources distinguish the 1863 text from the expanded or reissued 1868 book, and the dating should therefore remain double. [Cronología; BVMC; OC VII]
- **1869:** *Biografía de Santiago R. Albarracín*. A memorial biography issued separately during Sarmiento’s presidency. [BVMC]
- **1876:** *Bosquejo de la biografía de D. Dalmacio Vélez Sarsfield*. A biographical sketch of the jurist and political collaborator; the first edition spells the surname “Saarsfield” on its title page. [BVMC; OC XXVII]
- **1882:** *Darwin*. A commemorative address on Charles Darwin that became a separately recognized text. It is an occasional public act and scientific-cultural manifesto, not a systematic scientific treatise. [BVMC; OC XXII]
- **1885:** *Vida y escritos del coronel D. Francisco J. Muñiz*. A hybrid biography and documentary edition incorporating writings by Muñiz; Sarmiento is biographer and compiler, not author of every text in the volume. Some later accounts give 1886, but the digitized original is dated 1885. [BVMC; OC XLIII]
- **1886:** *Vida de Dominguito*. A biography and work of mourning about Domingo Fidel Sarmiento, first published serially in *El Censor*. Its documentary letters and paternal memorial voice make it both archive and biography. [Museo Histórico Sarmiento; OC XLV]
- **Various dates:** *Necrologías y biografías*. Numerous memorial sketches were written for newspapers, ceremonies and pamphlets. The headings in volumes III and XLV describe later editorial collections, not two books Sarmiento necessarily conceived as such. [OC III, XLV]

### Translations and editorial constructions

- **1848:** *Viaje a Chile del canónigo don Juan María Mastai-Ferretti, hoy sumo pontífice Pío IX*. A Spanish translation from Italian followed by Sarmiento’s appendix. The translated narrative is not his original authorship, while the translation and appended intervention are his made objects. [BVMC]
- **1867:** *Vida de Horace Mann*. A Spanish biographical construction based substantially on Mary Mann’s life of Horace Mann, with translation, adaptation and Sarmiento’s framing materials. Calling it simply an original biography obscures that layered authorship. [Cronología; OC XLIII]
- **1884–1903:** *Obras de D. F. Sarmiento*, 52 volumes. The series began during Sarmiento’s lifetime but was organized and completed by Augusto Belín Sarmiento and Luis Montt; it preserves the indispensable corpus while rearranging articles, speeches and papers under retrospective thematic titles. It is therefore both Sarmiento’s body of work and an editorial obra that cannot be attributed to him alone. [Bibliografía BVMC; Índice general]
- **Posthumous:** *El Carapachay*. A later collection of Sarmiento’s writings about the Paraná Delta. The constituent articles are his, but the familiar book is a posthumous editorial construction and should not be backdated as a lifetime book. [OC XLII; Índice general]

### Speeches, laws and unbounded textual bodies

- **1848–1888:** *Discursos*. Parliamentary, educational, diplomatic, ceremonial and popular speeches survive in contemporary printings and in volumes XVIII–XXII of the collected edition. Each occasion produced a text or performed manifesto, but the collected headings—*Discursos parlamentarios* and *Discursos populares*—are editorial bodies rather than original single works. [OC XVIII–XXII]
- **1857–1879:** *Legislative projects and parliamentary interventions*. These include school-building legislation, immigration and colonization measures, constitutional debates and speeches. Authorship is often shared with committees or transformed through legislative amendment, so the resulting laws cannot automatically be called Sarmiento’s solo works. [OC XVIII–XXV, XXXI–XXXVI]
- **1862–1879:** *Official messages, decrees and reports*. The texts made as governor, president, senator and minister are preserved unevenly in the collected works. They are acts of government—often protocols or manifestos in the strongest performative sense—but secretarial and institutional production makes individual authorship variable. [OC XXIV–XXV, XXXI–XXXVI, L–LI]
- **Undelimited:** *Páginas literarias*, *Costumbres*, *Progresos generales*, *Cuestiones americanas* and *Escritos diversos*. These names identify thematic volumes or sections of the posthumous corpus, not securely bounded works bearing fixed dates. Their resistance to itemization is bibliographical information: the original objects were articles, notes, speeches and occasional publications whose exact limits and attributions require article-level study. [OC XXXIV–XXXV, XLI–XLII, XLVI, LII]
