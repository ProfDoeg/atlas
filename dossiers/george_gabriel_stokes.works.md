## Works by Sir George Gabriel Stokes

### Hydrodynamical and mechanical papers

- **1842:** *On the Steady Motion of Incompressible Fluids*. A mathematical memoir on steady fluid flow, read to the Cambridge Philosophical Society in 1842.
- **1842–1843:** *On Some Cases of Fluid Motion*. A mathematical paper treating particular fluid-motion problems.
- **1843:** *On the Motion of a Piston and of the Air in a Cylinder*. A mathematical-physical paper on coupled piston and gas motion.
- **1845:** *On the Theories of the Internal Friction of Fluids in Motion, and of the Equilibrium and Motion of Elastic Solids*. A foundational memoir formulating viscous-fluid and elastic-solid dynamics.
- **1846:** *Report on Recent Researches in Hydrodynamics*. A critical survey rather than a wholly original memoir; it synthesizes work on fluid motion, waves, gases, sound, and fluid-solid oscillations.
- **1846:** *Supplement to a Memoir on Some Cases of Fluid Motion*. A continuation of the earlier memoir rather than an independent project.
- **1847:** *On the Theory of Oscillatory Waves*. A mathematical memoir on finite-amplitude water waves.
- **1847:** *On the Resistance of a Fluid to Two Oscillating Spheres*. A short hydrodynamical investigation.
- **1847–1880:** *Supplement to a Paper on the Theory of Oscillatory Waves*. A continuation printed in Stokes’s collected papers; its collected form incorporates later authorial work.
- **1848:** *Notes on Hydrodynamics, III: On the Dynamical Equations*. One installment in a pedagogical series planned with William Thomson; the series as a whole is collaborative, but this installment is Stokes’s.
- **1848:** *Notes on Hydrodynamics, IV: Demonstration of a Fundamental Theorem*. Stokes’s installment in the Thomson–Stokes teaching series.
- **1849:** *Notes on Hydrodynamics, VI: On Waves*. Stokes’s installment in the same jointly planned series.
- **1851:** *On the Effect of the Internal Friction of Fluids on the Motion of Pendulums*. A major analytical and experimental memoir containing what became known as Stokes’s law.
- **1868:** *On the Communication of Vibration from a Vibrating Body to a Surrounding Gas*. A paper on acoustic and mechanical energy transfer.
- **1872:** *Explanation of a Dynamical Paradox*. A brief explanatory paper rather than a full memoir.
- **1881:** *Discussion of the Results of Some Experiments with Whirled Anemometers*. An analytical report interpreting experimental measurements.
- **1881:** *Note on the Reduction of Mr Crookes’s Experiments on the Decrement of the Arc of Vibration of a Mica Plate Oscillating within a Bulb containing more or less Rarefied Gas*. A mathematical reduction of another experimenter’s data; it resists classification as an autonomous work because it was made in direct service to Crookes’s investigation.
- **1883:** *On the Highest Wave of Uniform Propagation: Preliminary Notice*. A preliminary scientific communication, explicitly provisional.
- **c. 1880; first published 1905:** *On the Maximum Wave of Uniform Propagation, being a Second Supplement to a Paper on the Theory of Oscillatory Waves*. An unpublished manuscript printed posthumously by Joseph Larmor; its date is editorially assigned from its apparent place in Stokes’s work and is not a publication date.
- **1891:** *Note on the Theory of the Solitary Wave*. A short continuation of Stokes’s water-wave project.
- **1895:** *The Outskirts of the Solitary Wave*. A very brief mathematical note.
- **1898:** *Mathematical Proof of the Identity of the Stream Lines obtained by means of a Viscous Film with those of a Perfect Fluid moving in Two Dimensions*. A mathematical demonstration connecting a physical model with ideal-fluid flow.

### Optics, light, colour, and spectroscopy

- **1845:** *On the Aberration of Light*. A theoretical optics paper proposing an ether-based account of stellar aberration.
- **1846:** *On Fresnel’s Theory of the Aberration of Light*. A critical and explanatory sequel.
- **1846:** *On a Formula for Determining the Optical Constants of Doubly Refracting Crystals*. A mathematical-optical paper.
- **1846:** *On the Constitution of the Luminiferous Ether, viewed with reference to the Phenomenon of the Aberration of Light*. A theoretical memoir belonging to Stokes’s ether project.
- **1848:** *On the Constitution of the Luminiferous Ether*. A further theoretical paper, distinct from the 1846 memoir despite the abbreviated title.
- **1848:** *On the Theory of Certain Bands seen in the Spectrum*. A mathematical explanation of spectral interference bands.
- **1849:** *On the Formation of the Central Spot of Newton’s Rings beyond the Critical Angle*. An optical paper combining theory and observed phenomenon.
- **1849:** *On the Perfect Blackness of the Central Spot in Newton’s Rings, and on the Verification of Fresnel’s Formula for the Intensities of Reflected and Refracted Rays*. An experimental-theoretical optics paper.
- **1850:** *On the Determination of the Wave Length corresponding with any Point of the Spectrum*. A measurement protocol supported by optical theory.
- **1850:** *On the Dynamical Theory of Diffraction*. A substantial theoretical and experimental memoir.
- **1850:** *On the Mode of Disappearance of Newton’s Rings in passing the Angle of Total Internal Reflection*. A short optical investigation.
- **1850:** *On Metallic Reflection*. A brief note.
- **1850:** *On a Fictitious Displacement of Fringes of Interference*. A brief diagnostic note about an optical appearance.
- **1850:** *On Haidinger’s Brushes*. A short paper on an entoptic polarization phenomenon.
- **1851:** *On the Colours of Thick Plates*. A substantial optical memoir.
- **1851:** *On a New Elliptic Analyser*. A paper describing an optical instrument: simultaneously a text and a design for a made apparatus.
- **1852:** *On the Total Intensity of Interfering Light*. A theoretical optics paper.
- **1852:** *On the Composition and Resolution of Streams of Polarized Light from Different Sources*. A theoretical paper introducing the quantities now called the Stokes parameters.
- **1852:** *Abstract of a Paper “On the Change of Refrangibility of Light”*. A preliminary manifestation of the larger fluorescence memoir, not a separate research project in the strongest sense.
- **1852:** *On the Change of Refrangibility of Light*. A major experimental memoir establishing the phenomenon Stokes named fluorescence.
- **1852:** *On the Optical Properties of a Recently Discovered Salt of Quinine*. A short experimental paper associated with the fluorescence project.
- **1853:** *On the Change of Refrangibility of Light—II*. A continuation of the 1852 memoir.
- **1853:** *On the Change of Refrangibility of Light and the Exhibition thereby of the Chemical Rays*. An experimental communication on ultraviolet-induced fluorescence.
- **1853:** *On the Cause of the Occurrence of Abnormal Figures in Photographic Impressions of Polarized Rings*. An explanation of a photographic-optical anomaly.
- **1853:** *On the Metallic Reflexion exhibited by Certain Non-Metallic Substances*. An experimental optics paper.
- **1854:** *Extracts from a Letter to Dr W. Haidinger: On the Direction of the Vibrations in Polarized Light; On Shadow Patterns and the Chromatic Aberration of the Eye; On Haidinger’s Brushes*. A published cluster of letter-extracts, not originally made as a unified paper.
- **1855:** *On the Achromatism of a Double Object-Glass*. An optical-design paper.
- **1856:** *Remarks on Professor Challis’s Paper entitled “A Theory of the Composition of Colours, etc.”*. A critical intervention whose object is another author’s paper.
- **1857:** *On the Polarization of Diffracted Light*. An experimental and theoretical optics paper.
- **1859:** *On the Existence of a Second Crystallizable Fluorescent Substance (Paviin) in the Bark of the Horse-Chestnut*. An experimental chemical-optical paper.
- **1859:** *On the Bearing of the Phenomena of Diffraction on the Direction of the Vibrations of Polarized Light, with Remarks on the Paper of Professor F. Eisenlohr*. A scientific argument partly framed as criticism of another paper.
- **1860:** *Note on Paviin*. A brief continuation of the 1859 investigation.
- **1860:** *Extracts relating to the Early History of Spectrum Analysis*. A documentary-historical text assembled from earlier correspondence and evidence; it resists classification as a new scientific discovery.
- **1861:** *Note on Internal Radiation*. A short optical paper.
- **1862:** *On the Intensity of the Light Reflected from or Transmitted through a Pile of Plates*. A theoretical optics paper.
- **1862:** *Report on Double Refraction*. A commissioned scientific survey rather than a single-purpose research memoir.
- **1862:** *On the Long Spectrum of Electric Light*. An observational spectroscopy paper.
- **1864:** *On the Supposed Identity of Biliverdin with Chlorophyll, with Remarks on the Constitution of Chlorophyll*. A spectroscopic and chemical investigation.
- **1864:** *On the Discrimination of Organic Bodies by their Optical Properties*. A methodological paper.
- **1864:** *On the Application of the Optical Properties of Bodies to the Detection and Discrimination of Organic Substances*. A fuller methodological treatment related to, but not identical with, the preceding paper.
- **1864:** *On the Reduction and Oxidation of the Colouring Matter of the Blood*. An experimental spectroscopy paper identifying the optical changes of haemoglobin.
- **1871:** *Notice of the Researches of the late Rev. W. Vernon Harcourt on the Conditions of Transparency in Glass and the Connexion between the Chemical Constitution and Optical Properties of Different Glasses*. A posthumous exposition of Harcourt’s experiments; Stokes made the notice and its scientific synthesis, but not the underlying experimental project alone.
- **1872:** *On the Law of Extraordinary Refraction in Iceland Spar*. A crystallographic-optics paper.
- **1873:** *Sur l’emploi du prisme dans la vérification de la loi de la double réfraction*. A French-language scientific note on testing double refraction with a prism.
- **1873:** *On the Principles of the Chemical Correction of Object-Glasses*. An optical-design paper emerging from Stokes’s work with Harcourt’s experimental glasses.
- **1874:** *On the Construction of a Perfectly Achromatic Telescope*. A design paper: both scientific text and project for an instrument.
- **1875:** *On the Optical Properties of a Titano-Silicic Glass*. An experimental paper connected with the Harcourt–Stokes glass project.
- **1876:** *On a Phenomenon of Metallic Reflection*. A brief experimental optics paper.
- **1876:** *Correspondence of Professor G. G. Stokes and Professor W. Thomson on the Nature and Possibilities of Spectrum Analysis*. A jointly made epistolary object, published as an appendix; neither authorship nor intellectual initiative belongs exclusively to Stokes.
- **1878:** *On the Question of a Theoretical Limit to the Apertures of Microscopic Objectives*. A theoretical paper on microscope design.
- **1878:** *On an Easy and at the same time Accurate Method of Determining the Ratio of the Dispersions of Glasses intended for Objectives*. A measurement protocol for optical-glass selection.
- **1881:** *On a Simple Mode of Eliminating Errors of Adjustment in Delicate Observations of Compared Spectra*. An observational protocol rather than a descriptive memoir.
- **1882:** *On the Cause of the Light Border frequently noticed in Photographs just outside the Outline of a Dark Body seen against the Sky, with some Introductory Remarks on Phosphorescence*. A photographic-optics paper.
- **1885:** *On a Remarkable Phenomenon of Crystalline Reflection*. An experimental and theoretical paper.
- **1887:** *On the Foci of Lines seen through a Crystalline Plate*. An optical investigation; the collected edition places it out of chronological order.
- **1891:** *On an Optical Proof of the Existence of Suspended Matter in Flames*. A short experimental paper.

### Mathematics, gravitation, heat, sound, and materials

- **1845:** *On the Proof of the Proposition that \((Mx+Ny)^{-1}\) is an Integrating Factor of the Homogeneous Differential Equation \(M+N\,dy/dx=0\)*. A brief mathematical proof.
- **1847:** *On the Critical Values of the Sums of Periodic Series*. A major paper in mathematical analysis.
- **1849:** *On a Difficulty in the Theory of Sound*. A short mathematical-physical paper.
- **1850:** *On Some Points in the Received Theory of Sound*. A broader acoustic investigation.
- **1850:** *On Attractions, and on Clairaut’s Theorem*. A mathematical paper on gravitational attraction and the figure of the Earth.
- **1850:** *On the Variation of Gravity at the Surface of the Earth*. A mathematical-geophysical memoir.
- **1850:** *On a Mode of Measuring the Astigmatism of a Defective Eye*. A measurement protocol with an instrumental dimension.
- **1850:** *Discussion of a Differential Equation relating to the Breaking of Railway Bridges*. A mathematical response to an engineering problem rather than a general treatise.
- **1851:** *An Examination of the Possible Effect of the Radiation of Heat on the Propagation of Sound*. A theoretical acoustics paper.
- **1851:** *On the Conduction of Heat in Crystals*. A mathematical-physical memoir on anisotropic heat conduction.
- **1857:** *On the Discontinuity of Arbitrary Constants which appear in Divergent Developments*. A mathematical paper anticipating what later became known as the Stokes phenomenon.
- **1867:** *On a Property of Curves which fulfil the condition \(\int ds/\rho^2=\mathrm{constant}\)*. A short mathematical paper; its formula is part of the title and should not be expanded into an invented verbal title.
- **1867:** *On the Internal Distribution of Matter which shall produce a Given Potential at the Surface of a Gravitating Mass*. A mathematical-physical paper.
- **1868:** *Supplement to a Paper on the Discontinuity of Arbitrary Constants which appear in Divergent Developments*. A continuation of the 1857 asymptotic-analysis project.
- **1886:** *The Coefficient of Viscosity of Air*. A paper by Herbert Tomlinson with Stokes’s scientific participation and analysis; it should not be treated as solely authored by Stokes.
- **1889:** *Note on the Determination of Arbitrary Constants which appear as Multipliers of Semi-Convergent Series*. A further contribution to Stokes’s long asymptotic-analysis project.
- **1891:** *On a Graphical Representation of the Results of Dr Alder Wright’s Experiments on Ternary Alloys*. A graphical and mathematical method applied to another investigator’s data.
- **1891:** *On the Reactions occurring in Flames*. A short physical-chemical paper.
- **1891:** *The Influence of Surface-Loading on the Flexure of Beams*. A paper by C. A. Carus Wilson carrying Stokes’s associated analysis; sole attribution to Stokes would be misleading.
- **1902:** *On the Discontinuity of Arbitrary Constants that appear as Multipliers of Semi-Convergent Series*. Stokes’s final return to the asymptotic phenomenon he had investigated since 1857.

### Appended notes, extracts, reports, and collaborative interventions

- **1854:** *On the Theory of the Electric Telegraph, by Professor W. Thomson—Extract*. The underlying paper is Thomson’s; Stokes’s contribution survives as an extract or associated intervention and resists treatment as an autonomous Stokes work.
- **1856:** *Supplement to the “Account of Pendulum Experiments undertaken in the Harton Colliery,” by G. B. Airy*. A supplement to Airy’s report, not an independently conceived memoir.
- **1860:** *On the Colouring Matters of Madder, by Dr E. Schunck—Extract*. Schunck is the principal author; Stokes’s optical analysis is an embedded contribution.
- **1863:** *On the Change of Form assumed by Wrought Iron and other Metals when heated and then cooled by Partial Immersion in Water, by H. Clark—Note appended by Professor Stokes*. Stokes made only the appended note.
- **1868:** *Account of Observations of the Total Eclipse of the Sun, by J. Pope Hennessy—Note added by Professor Stokes*. An appended scientific note, not authorship of Hennessy’s observations.
- **1874:** *On the Improvement of the Spectroscope, by Thomas Grubb—Note appended by Professor Stokes*. Stokes’s work is the note; the instrument and principal paper are Grubb’s.
- **1876:** *Preliminary Note on the Compound Nature of the Line-Spectra of Elementary Bodies, by J. N. Lockyer—Extract*. Lockyer is the author of the investigation; the collected Stokes papers preserve an extract because of Stokes’s associated commentary.
- **1876:** *Note on Certain Formulæ in the Calculus of Operations*. A note originally contained in a letter to Peter Guthrie Tait, occupying the unstable boundary between correspondence and publication.
- **1876:** *An Experiment on Electro-Magnetic Rotation*. A communication presented under William Spottiswoode’s name; Stokes’s precise share is editorially associated with it, so exclusive attribution is unsafe.
- **1878:** *On the Determination of the Constants of the Cup Anemometer by Experiments with a Whirling Machine*. Thomas Romney Robinson is the named principal author; Stokes supplied the reduction and discussion preserved in his papers.
- **1879:** *On a Method of Detecting Inequalities of Unknown Periods in a Series of Observations*. A note appended to a paper by Balfour Stewart and Walter Dodgson.
- **1882:** *Extracts from Reports of the Committee on Solar Physics*. Committee work selected for Stokes’s collected papers; institutional and individual authorship overlap.
- **1882:** *Memorandum for the Use of Observers with Professor Balfour Stewart’s Actinometer*. A protocol prepared by Stokes and formally adopted by the Committee on Solar Physics.
- **1886:** *On the Methods of Chemical Fractionation*. A scientific memorandum associated with committee and advisory work rather than a conventional journal memoir.
- **1903:** *The Ultra-Violet Spectrum of Radium*. A late spectroscopic communication included posthumously in the collected papers.

### Instruments and practical designs

- **1880:** *Description of the Card Supporter for Sunshine Recorders adopted at the Meteorological Office*. A text describing a practical instrument component; the made thing is both the design and its written specification.
- **1849–1875:** *The Harcourt–Stokes optical-glass project*. A long experimental project to connect glass composition with optical properties and produce improved achromatic objectives. It cannot be reduced to one paper or assigned solely to Stokes: William Vernon Harcourt performed much of the glass-making, while Stokes supplied optical testing, theory, and later exposition.
- **Before 1851:** *Elliptic analyser*. An optical instrument devised by Stokes and described in his 1851 paper; the apparatus is an obra distinct from its textual description.
- **By 1880:** *Card supporter for Campbell–Stokes sunshine recorders*. A practical design adopted by the Meteorological Office; surviving nomenclature can obscure the shared history of the recorder, whose earlier form was John Francis Campbell’s.

### Lectures, addresses, and books made from lectures

- **1869:** *Presidential Address to the British Association for the Advancement of Science, Exeter Meeting*. A public scientific address surveying recent progress rather than presenting a single new discovery.
- **1876:** *The Absorption of Light and the Colours of Natural Bodies*. A lecture to science teachers at South Kensington, subsequently printed.
- **1876:** *Fluorescence*. A companion South Kensington lecture, printed with the preceding lecture but intellectually continuous with Stokes’s earlier research.
- **1881:** *Solar Physics*. An introductory South Kensington lecture delivered for the Committee on Solar Research and reprinted in *Nature*.
- **1883:** *On the Nature of Light*. The first Burnett Lecture series at Aberdeen, subsequently issued as a volume.
- **1884:** *On Light as a Means of Investigation*. The second Burnett Lecture series and printed volume.
- **1885:** *On the Beneficial Effects of Light*. The third Burnett Lecture series and printed volume.
- **1886–1891:** *Anniversary Addresses to the Royal Society*. Six presidential addresses, largely institutional and commemorative. They form a serial body of public acts rather than six research memoirs.
- **1891:** *Natural Theology: The Gifford Lectures Delivered before the University of Edinburgh*. The first published Gifford course, a book made from lectures joining natural science to theological argument.
- **1893:** *Natural Theology: The Gifford Lectures Delivered before the University of Edinburgh*. The second course and volume; it is a continuation, not a later edition of the 1891 course.
- **1893:** *The Luminiferous Ether*. A Victoria Institute presidential address.
- **1895:** *The Perception of Light*. A Victoria Institute presidential address.
- **1896:** *The Röntgen Rays*. A Victoria Institute presidential address.
- **1897:** *On the Nature of the Röntgen Rays*. The Wilde Lecture; distinct in occasion and textual form from the 1896 Victoria Institute address despite the shared subject.
- **1898:** *The Perception of Colour*. A Victoria Institute presidential address.

### Religious and philosophical texts

- **1887:** *Science and Revelation*. A public argument about the relation between scientific and revealed knowledge, functioning more as an apologetic intervention than as a research paper.
- **1897:** *Conditional Immortality: A Help to Sceptics*. A series of letters addressed to James Marchant and issued as a book. It is an epistolary theological project, not a scientific monograph.

### Examinations, correspondence, and bodies of work that resist itemization

- **1849–1902:** *Mathematical Tripos and Smith’s Prize examination papers*. Problems set by Stokes over many years and printed as an appendix to volume V of the collected papers. These are authored mathematical objects and assessment protocols, but the collected edition does not make each question into a separately titled work.
- **c. 1840s–1903:** *Scientific correspondence*. Thousands of letters formed a major medium of Stokes’s scientific activity; Joseph Larmor’s two-volume *Memoir and Scientific Correspondence* prints only a selection. The corpus resists being called a single work, and the published selection must not be mistaken for a complete correspondence or for a book planned by Stokes.
- **1846–1869:** *Correspondence with William Thomson on spectrum analysis and related physics*. A jointly produced intellectual exchange, partially published during Stokes’s life and more extensively selected after his death; individual priority within it is sometimes difficult to separate.
- **1850s–1890s:** *Advisory calculations, referee reports, and committee memoranda*. Stokes made a large body of often anonymous or institutionally attributed scientific writing through the Royal Society, Meteorological Council, Solar Physics Committee, and other bodies. No standard collected edition claims to enumerate all of it, so treating it as a finite list of titled “works” would create false completeness.

### Authorial collected obra

- **1880–1901:** *Mathematical and Physical Papers, Volumes I–III*. Stokes’s own chronological selection and revision of his scientific papers, with corrections and newly written annotations. These volumes are themselves a made editorial obra, not merely neutral containers for earlier editions.
- **1904–1905:** *Mathematical and Physical Papers, Volumes IV–V*. Posthumous continuations edited by Joseph Larmor. They manifest Stokes’s remaining papers, notes, manuscript, and examination work, but their selection and arrangement are Larmor’s and therefore not wholly Stokes’s creation.
- **1907:** *Memoir and Scientific Correspondence of the Late Sir George Gabriel Stokes*. A two-volume posthumous construction selected and arranged by Joseph Larmor, with biographical material by Stokes’s daughter Isabella Humphry and others. Stokes made the letters, not the editorial obra bearing this title.
