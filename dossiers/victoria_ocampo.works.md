## Works by Victoria Ocampo

### Books, essays, lectures, dialogues, and mixed-media publications

- **1924:** *De Francesca a Beatrice*. Her first book, a French-language essay on Dante’s *Divine Comedy*, published with a prologue by José Ortega y Gasset; the title’s spelling was preserved in later bibliographies. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1926:** *De Francesca à Béatrice à travers la Divine Comédie*. Ocampo’s French recasting of her Dante essay: a new linguistic manifestation rather than an entirely separate project. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1926:** *La laguna de los nenúfares: fábula escénica en doce cuadros*. A dramatic fable conceived in twelve tableaux—not a conventional essay, although later accounts often absorb it into Ocampo’s prose bibliography. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1935:** *Supremacía del alma y de la sangre*. A lecture delivered in Madrid and issued as a 58-page pamphlet: simultaneously an occasional address, a political-autobiographical intervention, and a printed work. [Centro Virtual Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/el_rinconete/anteriores/mayo_16/13052016_01.htm), [bibliographic record](https://www.duran-subastas.com/files/001/HISTORICAS/LIBROS_558_1.pdf)

- **1936:** *Domingos en Hyde Park*. A collection of personal essays and travel impressions, written in the mobile, autobiographical form Ocampo made characteristic of her “testimonios.” [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1936:** *La mujer, sus derechos y sus responsabilidades*. A feminist lecture-pamphlet associated with the Unión Argentina de Mujeres; its public, persuasive action makes it closer to a manifesto than to detached description. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1936:** *La mujer y su expresión*. A radio address transmitted in Argentina and Spain and subsequently printed; it is both a broadcast performance and a feminist manifesto about women’s right to expression. [Historical account](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_Argentina_de_Mujeres_%281936%29), [bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1936/1941:** *San Isidro*. A Gesamtkunstwerk-like portrait of place combining Ocampo’s prose, a poem by Silvina Ocampo, and sixty-eight photographs by Gustav Thorlichen. Sources variously date it 1936 and 1941, apparently reflecting different manifestations; the specialist bibliography gives the Sur edition as 1941. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html), [FCE author record](https://www.fcede.es/site/es/fceespa/autoresdetalle.aspx?idAutor=2900)

- **1938:** *Emily Brontë (Terra incognita)*. A short biographical-critical essay that treats Brontë through landscape, objects, family, and imaginative identification rather than as a conventional scholarly life. [Bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Emily_Bront%C3%AB_terra_incognita.html?id=RyYwAQAAIAAJ), [scholarly discussion](https://www.bibliotecanacionaldigital.gob.cl/colecciones/BND/00/RC/RC0115643.pdf)

- **1938:** *Virginia Woolf, Orlando y Cía.* A critical-personal essay on Woolf and her milieu; its address to a living friend and fellow writer places it between literary criticism, portrait, and testimony. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1942:** *338171 T. E.* A biographical essay on T. E. Lawrence whose cryptic title uses Lawrence’s service number; it is an interpretive portrait rather than a documentary biography. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1945:** *Le vert paradis*. A French-language book of autobiographical prose. It is a distinct linguistic manifestation within Ocampo’s bilingual practice, though later summaries frequently omit it. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1947:** *388171 T. E. (Lawrence d’Arabie)*. The French manifestation of the Lawrence project; the specialist bibliography prints “388171,” although Ocampo’s Spanish title is *338171 T. E.*, so the number may represent either an edition-specific title or a bibliographic error. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1947:** *Enrique V y Laurence Olivier*. A short essay on Olivier’s cinematic and theatrical Shakespeare, occupying the border between film criticism and Ocampo’s longstanding project of writing about performance. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1949:** *El “Hamlet” de Laurence Olivier*. A 39-page essay on Olivier’s film and interpretation of Shakespeare, not a dramatic adaptation by Ocampo. [Bibliographic record](https://www.biblioteca.emad.edu.uy/pmb/opac_css/index.php?dcote=&id=30&lcote=0&location=5&lvl=section_see&main=&nb_per_page_custom=200&nbr_lignes=6111&nc=0&page=9&plettreaut=&ssub=0)

- **1951:** *El viajero y una de sus sombras: Keyserling en mis memorias*. A polemical autobiographical counter-portrait of Hermann von Keyserling; because it answers his representation of their relationship, the book acts as testimony and corrective intervention. [Scholarly bibliography](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/41618/CONICET_Digital_Nro.5fd151d2-2eb8-4115-9a0b-43874efe23b1_A.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=5)

- **1951:** *Lawrence de Arabia y otros ensayos*. A collected critical volume centered on T. E. Lawrence rather than a wholly new Lawrence biography. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1954:** *Virginia Woolf en su diario*. An urgent essay written after Ocampo read Leonard Woolf’s edited selection from Virginia Woolf’s diary; it is also a meditation on posthumous papers, editorial exclusion, and literary posterity. [Scholarly study](https://catedraltomada.pitt.edu/ojs/catedraltomada/article/view/588)

- **1959/1961:** *Habla el algarrobo (Luz y sonido)*. A brief text conceived through voice, illumination, place, and the emblematic tree at Villa Ocampo; its subtitle makes it resist classification as prose alone. Sources commonly give 1959, while the specialist bibliography records the Sur book in 1961. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html), [general bibliography](https://www.escritores.org/biografias/295-victoria-ocampo-aguirre)

- **1961:** *Tagore en las barrancas de San Isidro*. A memoir-essay about Rabindranath Tagore’s 1924 stay at Miralrío, joining literary portrait, landscape, hospitality, and autobiographical recollection. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1964:** *Juan Sebastián Bach: el hombre*. A short biographical and devotional essay on Bach—not musicology in the strict sense, but a personal portrait of the person imagined through the music. [Academia Argentina de Letras bibliography](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/91052/files/b123174302_C031203223.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)

- **1964:** *La Belle y sus enamorados*. A 27-page illustrated remembrance centered on Ocampo’s house and the writers who passed through it; part memoir, part portrait-gallery, and part account of an immaterial collection of presences. [Bibliographic description](https://www.uniliber.com/ficha/la-belle-y-sus-enamorados_117008220/), [scholarly discussion](https://idus.us.es/bitstreams/51b266c1-7f1f-4a86-9ced-a278fbb11493/download)

- **1966:** *Jawaharlal Nehru: antología*. A selected anthology with Ocampo’s prefatory and editorial framing. Authorship is therefore composite: Nehru made the constituent texts; Ocampo made the selection, arrangement, and interpretive presentation. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1968:** *Estado de plegaria*. A brief reflective work whose form lies between meditation, essay, and spiritual testimony. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1969:** *Diálogo con Mallea*. A published conversation with Eduardo Mallea; it is a jointly produced oral-textual object, not a sole-authored monograph. [Academia Argentina de Letras bibliography](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/91052/files/b123174302_C031203223.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)

- **1969:** *Diálogo con Borges*. A published conversation with Jorge Luis Borges, jointly made through speech and subsequent editorial transcription. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1972:** *Roger Caillois y la Cruz del Sur en la Academia Francesa*. An occasional homage connecting Caillois’s reception into the Académie française with Ocampo’s long transatlantic publishing project. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1977:** *Recepción de la académica de número doña Victoria Ocampo*. Her reception address as the first woman admitted to the Academia Argentina de Letras; a ceremonial speech whose utterance itself performs institutional entry. [Academia Argentina de Letras bibliography](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/91052/files/b123174302_C031203223.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)

### *Testimonios*: serial obra and archive of forms

- **1920–1934; published 1935:** *Testimonios, primera serie*. The first installment of Ocampo’s defining serial obra: essays, letters, lectures, travel writing, criticism, portraits, and autobiographical fragments gathered under a category deliberately broader than “essay.” [Specialist bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1937–1940; published 1941:** *Testimonios, segunda serie*. A second heterogeneous gathering in which previously occasional texts become parts of an evolving public self-portrait. [Scholarly chronology](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/103763/Tesis.pdf-PDFA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)

- **1938–1944; published 1946:** *Testimonios, tercera serie*. A wartime collection of criticism, political reflection, portraiture, and personal witness. Some secondary lists incorrectly date this volume 1950. [Scholarly chronology](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/103763/Tesis.pdf-PDFA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)

- **Published 1950:** *Soledad sonora: Testimonios, cuarta serie*. The fourth series bears an individual title, emphasizing that the serial project could also manifest as an autonomous book. [Specialist bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1950–1957; published 1957:** *Testimonios, quinta serie*. A composite volume of essays, chronicles, lectures, letters, denunciations, and criticism; some derivative lists misdate it 1954. [Contemporary bibliographic record](https://catalogobibliotecas.buenosaires.gob.ar/pergamo/documento.php?id=PERGAMO.1.95807&recno=95807&ui=1)

- **Published 1963:** *Testimonios, sexta serie*. The specialist bibliography and Academia Argentina de Letras give 1963, although some later lists give 1962 or 1964. [Academia Argentina de Letras bibliography](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/91052/files/b123174302_C031203223.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)

- **1962–1967; published 1967:** *Testimonios, séptima serie*. The seventh accumulation of Ocampo’s public interventions and personal-critical prose. [Academia Argentina de Letras bibliography](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/91052/files/b123174302_C031203223.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)

- **1968–1970; published 1971:** *Testimonios, octava serie*. A further installment of the open-ended archive; one scholarly chronology accidentally labels the same span “séptima” before correctly identifying it as the eighth series. [Scholarly chronology](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/103763/Tesis.pdf-PDFA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)

- **1971–1974; published 1975:** *Testimonios, novena serie*. Essays and interventions arranged by period rather than by a single subject or genre. [Scholarly chronology](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/103763/Tesis.pdf-PDFA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)

- **1975–1977; published 1977:** *Testimonios, décima serie*. The final series prepared and published during Ocampo’s lifetime. Some derivative references give 1998, confusing a later edition with the obra’s first publication. [Specialist bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html), [scholarly chronology](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/103763/Tesis.pdf-PDFA.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y)

### Autobiographical project

- **Begun 1952; published 1979:** *Autobiografía I: El archipiélago*. The first volume of a six-part project deliberately reserved for posthumous publication; its publication date therefore marks a manifestation, not its composition. [Manuscript study](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/275738/CONICET_Digital_Nro.11403e99-e9f8-4530-9995-26d25a2c749e_C.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=5)

- **Begun 1952; published 1980:** *Autobiografía II: El imperio insular*. The second posthumous volume, concerned with adolescence, early writing, and marriage. [Scholarly bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **Written 1952; published 1981:** *Autobiografía III: La rama de Salzburgo*. A volume first drafted in French and translated by Ocampo herself, according to its surviving typescript; it is thus both autobiography and self-translation. [Manuscript study](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/275738/CONICET_Digital_Nro.11403e99-e9f8-4530-9995-26d25a2c749e_C.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=5)

- **Begun 1952; published 1982:** *Autobiografía IV: Viraje*. A further volume of the posthumous life-project, narrating an intellectual and emotional turning point rather than simply advancing a linear chronology. [Scholarly study](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/002/NR77547.PDF)

- **Begun 1952; published 1983:** *Autobiografía V: Figuras simbólicas. Medida de Francia*. A double-titled volume organized around formative persons and Ocampo’s relation to French culture. [Scholarly study](https://www.collectionscanada.gc.ca/obj/thesescanada/vol2/002/NR77547.PDF)

- **Begun 1952; published 1984:** *Autobiografía VI: Sur y Cía.* The final volume, where individual autobiography becomes inseparable from the collective history of *Sur*; the title also circulated separately as *SUR y Cía*. [Scholarly bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html), [manuscript scholarship](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

### Translations, selections, and translated dramatic texts

- **1946:** *Calígula*, by Albert Camus. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of a play: an authored linguistic performance, though not the underlying drama. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1946:** *Gigi*, by Colette and Anita Loos. A Spanish translation credited to Ocampo; the source itself has composite authorship and adaptation history. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1953:** *El cuarto en que se vive*, by Graham Greene. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of Greene’s play. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1955:** *Vinoba*, by Lanza del Vasto. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of a work concerning Vinoba Bhave. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1955:** *El troquel*, by T. E. Lawrence. Ocampo’s Spanish translation, extending her Lawrence project from criticism and biography into linguistic remaking. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1957:** *La casilla de las macetas*, by Graham Greene. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of a dramatic work. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1957:** *El que pierde gana*, by Graham Greene. Ocampo’s Spanish translation; the bibliography records the title as *El que pierda gana* in one place, so the exact published form should be checked against the first edition. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1957:** *Réquiem para una reclusa*, by William Faulkner, in Albert Camus’s dramatic adaptation. Ocampo translated Camus’s adaptation of Faulkner, making this a layered object with distinct novelist, dramatist-adaptor, and translator. [Account of the translation](https://www.infobae.com/grandes-libros/2019/08/07/victoria-ocampo-y-albert-camus-una-amistad-amenazada-por-la-enfermedad-y-el-peronismo/), [translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1958:** *Recordando con ira*, by John Osborne. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of Osborne’s play. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1959:** *El amante complaciente*, by Graham Greene. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of a play. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1959:** *Bajo el bosque de leche*, by Dylan Thomas. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of Thomas’s work for voices, itself resistant to separation into radio play, poem, and drama. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1960:** *Los poseídos*, by Fyodor Dostoevsky, in Albert Camus’s dramatic adaptation. Ocampo translated Camus’s stage version rather than Dostoevsky’s novel directly; treating it simply as “Dostoevsky translated by Ocampo” would erase the adaptor. [Account of the translation](https://www.infobae.com/grandes-libros/2019/08/07/victoria-ocampo-y-albert-camus-una-amistad-amenazada-por-la-enfermedad-y-el-peronismo/)

- **1960:** *Libro de los cumpleaños*, by Rabindranath Tagore. Ocampo selected and translated the texts, so her made thing is the Spanish anthology rather than Tagore’s underlying poems. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1965:** *Tallando una estatua*, by Graham Greene. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of a play. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1966:** *Jawaharlal Nehru: antología*. Ocampo’s selection and translation of Nehru; also listed among her authored books because its organizing structure and preface are hers, while the selected writings are not. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1970:** *Mi vida es mi mensaje*, by Mahatma Gandhi. A selected or compiled Spanish presentation credited to Ocampo as translator; it should not be mistaken for a single book composed by Gandhi under that title. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1976:** *La vuelta de A. J. Raffles*, by Graham Greene. Ocampo’s Spanish translation of Greene’s play. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1979:** *Oda jubilar*, by Paul Claudel. Ocampo’s Spanish translation, published in the year of her death. [Translation bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

### Editorial and institutional obras

- **1931–1979:** *Sur*. A journal founded, financed, shaped, and directed by Ocampo: not a sole-authored text but a collective, serial obra and cultural network whose editorial decisions were among her principal acts of making. [Fondo de Cultura Económica](https://fce.com.ar/autores/victoria-ocampo/), [scholarly project and bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **From 1933:** *Editorial Sur*. A publishing project created to circulate Argentine and foreign writing—especially translations—and eventually responsible for nearly four hundred titles. Its books were made by many authors, translators, and editors, so the catalogue is Ocampo’s institutional obra but not four hundred individually authored works by her. [Biblioteca Nacional catalogue study](https://www.bn.gov.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/b5b10b82326ff3beba3300d584f11d6c.pdf), [Argentine cultural history](https://cultura.gob.ar/victoria-ocampo-una-vida-dedicada-a-la-cultura-8896/)

- **1936:** *Unión Argentina de Mujeres*. A feminist organization co-founded by Ocampo with Susana Larguía and María Rosa Oliver to oppose proposed restrictions on women’s civil rights. It is a collective political project, not a text, and sole authorship would be a false classification. [Historical account](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uni%C3%B3n_Argentina_de_Mujeres_%281936%29)

- **1940s–1979:** *The Villa Ocampo salon and hospitality network*. Ocampo made her residence into a sustained site for meetings, performances, translation, publishing, and international cultural exchange. It resists designation as a bounded “work”: its materials were rooms, meals, visits, correspondence, and social relations. [UNESCO Documentation Centre](https://www.unesco.org/en/villa-ocampo/documentation-centre), [Villa Ocampo history](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/413775)

### Architectural projects and commissioned spaces

- **1928, unbuilt:** *House project for the Salguero site*. Ocampo commissioned Le Corbusier after supplying ideas or sketches, but the project was not realized. It belongs here as an unfinished intention—precisely a project—while architectural authorship remains Le Corbusier’s rather than Ocampo’s alone. [Architectural history](https://www.archdaily.com/es/976466/clasicos-de-arquitectura-casa-de-victoria-ocampo-alejandro-bustillo), [scholarly study](https://wrs.ojs.upv.es/index.php/LC/article/view/16029)

- **1928–1929:** *Casa Victoria Ocampo, Rufino de Elizalde 2831*. Ocampo commissioned Alejandro Bustillo and imposed a radical modern, unornamented brief that conflicted with his usual classicism. The building is therefore her patronal and domestic project but Bustillo’s attributed architectural design; calling it simply “a house by Ocampo” would suppress that divided authorship. [Argentina’s heritage register](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/capital-humano/cultura/monumentos/casa-de-victoria-ocampo), [architectural study](https://www.archdaily.com/es/976466/clasicos-de-arquitectura-casa-de-victoria-ocampo-alejandro-bustillo)

- **1941–1973:** *Villa Ocampo as residence, editorial workplace, and eventual UNESCO bequest*. Ocampo did not design the original 1891 house, which had been commissioned by her family; what she made was its later cultural use and its conversion, through donation, into a public legacy. It therefore resists classification as her architectural work. [Villa Ocampo history](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/413775), [UNESCO Documentation Centre](https://www.unesco.org/en/villa-ocampo/documentation-centre)

- **1973:** *Donation of Villa Ocampo and Villa Victoria to UNESCO*. A legal and cultural act that transformed private houses, collections, gardens, and archives into an intended public heritage project. The donation is not a literary text, but it made a durable institutional object. [Villa Ocampo history](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/413775), [UNESCO Documentation Centre](https://www.unesco.org/en/villa-ocampo/documentation-centre)

### Performances and manifestations

- **1936 and 1939:** *Perséphone*. Ocampo performed the speaking role in Igor Stravinsky and André Gide’s opera-oratorio-ballet, first under Stravinsky at the Teatro Colón and later in Italy. She did not compose the work, but her recitation was a distinct performative manifestation selected and directed by the composer. [Musicological study](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0716-27902007000200002&script=sci_arttext)

### Letters and posthumously constructed epistolary works

- **Letters written throughout her life; first collected 1980:** *Correspondence*. Ocampo made thousands of letters, but the titled epistolaries are posthumous editorial constructions rather than books she designed. The corpus therefore resists treatment as a stable, complete work: archives remain dispersed, some material was deliberately destroyed after her death, and each edition selects a different relationship. [Archive guide](https://hollisarchives.lib.harvard.edu/download_collection_pdf/hou00038.pdf), [epistolary scholarship](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **1926–1956; published 2003 in English and 2007 in Spanish:** *This America of Ours / Esta América nuestra: correspondencia Gabriela Mistral–Victoria Ocampo*. A jointly authored correspondence reconstructed and titled by later editors Elizabeth Horan and Doris Meyer. [JSTOR record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7560/734555), [publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **1927–1959; published 2009:** *Cartas echadas: correspondencia Alfonso Reyes–Victoria Ocampo*. A bilateral epistolary corpus edited by Héctor Perea; the published book is not identical with the historical exchange and should not be assigned to Ocampo alone. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **1939–1978; published 1997 in French and 1999 in Spanish:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Roger Caillois*. A long bilateral correspondence whose book form, selection, and apparatus were made posthumously by editors. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html), [publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 1996:** *Dos Argentinas: Arturo Jauretche–Victoria Ocampo, correspondencia inédita*. A posthumously selected exchange; its title and argumentative pairing are editorial constructions by Norberto Galasso. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **Published 1997:** *Cartas a Angélica y otros*. Letters to Ocampo’s sister and family, selected, titled, annotated, and framed by Eduardo Paz Leston; the letters are Ocampo’s, while the book is a posthumous collaborative construction. [Bibliographic record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA48995146)

- **March–December 1946; published 2009:** *Cartas de posguerra: Nueva York, Londres, París*. Eighty-three letters, principally to Ocampo’s sisters, that also function as a serial travel record; Ocampo asked that they be preserved as documentation, but the book’s boundaries were fixed posthumously. [Scholarly study](https://badebec.unr.edu.ar/index.php/badebec/article/view/637), [publication account](https://www.clarin.com/ediciones-anteriores/victoria-ocampo-cuenta-posguerra_0_BJfQO0TKe.html)

- **Published 2006:** *Correspondance Ernest Ansermet–Victoria Ocampo*. A bilateral epistolary corpus published in French after both correspondents’ deaths; its book form is editorial rather than authorially finalized. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2010/2011:** *Fragmentos de un regalo: correspondencia con Thomas Merton*. A posthumous assemblage of correspondence alongside articles and reviews from *Sur*, making it an editorial dossier rather than a self-contained work planned by Ocampo. Sources give 2010 in one bibliography and 2011 in later scholarship. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html), [publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2011 in French; 2022 in Spanish:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Pierre Drieu La Rochelle*. A bilateral archive whose translated editions are later manifestations, not books completed by either correspondent. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2012:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–María Elena Walsh*. A posthumously delimited exchange; its incompleteness depends on what letters survived and were available to its editor. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2013:** *Epistolario: la correspondencia entre Victoria Ocampo y Ezequiel Martínez Estrada*. A jointly authored exchange edited after both writers’ deaths. [Bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)

- **Published 2019:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Albert Camus*. A posthumous bilateral corpus whose existence as a named book is the work of later editors; the letters themselves belong to the historical exchange. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2019:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Rabindranath Tagore*. A surviving exchange published as a book long after both correspondents’ deaths; it cannot be presumed complete. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2019:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Victoria Kent*. A posthumous epistolary construction documenting a relationship between two transatlantic feminist intellectuals. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2020; later edition 2024:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Virginia Woolf*. A selected bilateral corpus; the differing publication dates refer to editorial manifestations, not newly written letters. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335), [later-edition reference](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Woolf)

- **Published 2022:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–Jacques Maritain*. A posthumously edited exchange and therefore a documentary manifestation rather than an authorially closed book. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2022:** *Correspondence Victoria Ocampo–Hermann von Keyserling*. A posthumous reconstruction of an exchange central to Ocampo’s later autobiographical counter-narrative; survival and editorial selection determine its present form. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

- **Published 2023:** *Correspondencia Victoria Ocampo–José Ortega y Gasset*. A posthumously edited bilateral corpus; the published book is one manifestation of a historically dispersed exchange. [Publication history](https://www.revistas.usp.br/manuscritica/article/download/213284/199860/661335)

### Posthumous selections that are not independent obras

- **1987:** *Páginas dispersas de Victoria Ocampo*. A posthumous gathering of previously uncollected texts. The constituent writings are Ocampo’s, but the selection and book-boundary are editorial and should not be mistaken for a manuscript she completed under this title. [Bibliographic notice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Ocampo)

- **2010:** *La viajera y sus sombras: crónica de un aprendizaje*. Sylvia Molloy’s posthumous selection of Ocampo’s travel writings. It is a valuable manifestation of the travel corpus, but not a new book designed by Ocampo. [Fondo de Cultura Económica](https://fce.com.ar/autores/victoria-ocampo/), [bibliography](https://norabenedict.github.io/ocampo/bibliography.html)
