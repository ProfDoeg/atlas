## Works by Carl Theodor Dreyer

### Films directed

- **1919:** *Præsidenten* (*The President*). Silent feature; Dreyer wrote the adaptation and made his directorial debut with it. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1921:** *Blade af Satans Bog* (*Leaves from Satan’s Book*). Silent feature in four historical episodes, written and directed by Dreyer; production began in 1919, which accounts for its sometimes earlier dating. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1921:** *Prästänkan* (*The Parson’s Widow*). Swedish silent feature, adapted and directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1922:** *Die Gezeichneten* (*Love One Another*; Danish title *Elsker hverandre*). German silent feature, scripted and directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1922:** *Der var engang* (*Once upon a Time*). Danish silent feature, adapted, directed, and edited by Dreyer; only an incomplete fragment survives, so the extant manifestation is not the whole obra. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf) [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer)
- **1924:** *Michael* (also *Mikaël*). German silent feature directed by Dreyer from Herman Bang’s novel. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1925:** *Du skal ære din Hustru* (*Master of the House*; literally *Thou Shalt Honor Thy Wife*). Danish silent domestic comedy-drama, adapted and directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1926:** *Glomdalsbruden* (*The Bride of Glomdal*). Norwegian silent feature directed, adapted, and edited by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1928:** *La Passion de Jeanne d’Arc* (*The Passion of Joan of Arc*). French silent feature directed by Dreyer and scripted with Joseph Delteil; censorship, fire, recutting, and multiple later reconstructions make “the film” a contested family of manifestations rather than one uncomplicated object. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1932:** *Vampyr—Der Traum des Allan Grey* (*Vampyr*). Independently produced French-German sound feature, co-written, directed, and produced by Dreyer; it appeared in separately dubbed German, French, and English versions. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf) [[3]](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/a-z-carl-theodor-dreyer)
- **1942:** *Mødrehjælpen* (*Good Mothers*). Commissioned short documentary-fiction film about Denmark’s maternal-aid service; written and directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1943:** *Vredens Dag* (*Day of Wrath*). Danish feature adapted, directed, and co-produced by Dreyer from Hans Wiers-Jenssen’s play *Anne Pedersdotter*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1945:** *Två människor* (*Two People*). Swedish feature directed by Dreyer; he subsequently repudiated the finished film, making its place in his self-defined oeuvre unusually resistant. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf) [[4]](https://lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer)
- **1946:** *Vandet paa Landet* (*Water from the Land*). Commissioned documentary short about land reclamation and drainage, directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1947:** *Landsbykirken* (*The Danish Village Church*). Commissioned documentary short tracing the historical development of Danish rural churches, directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1947:** *Kampen mod Kræften* (*The Fight Against Cancer*). Commissioned public-information short, written and directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1948:** *De nåede færgen* (*They Caught the Ferry*). Road-safety short, adapted and directed by Dreyer; although commissioned instruction, it operates as a compact dramatic film rather than merely a protocol. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1949:** *Thorvaldsen*. Documentary portrait of sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen, written and directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1950:** *Storstrømsbroen* (*The Storstrøm Bridge*). Commissioned documentary short about the bridge and its surroundings, directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1954:** *Et Slot i et Slot* (*A Castle Within a Castle*). Documentary short about the restoration and museum installation of Rosenborg Castle, directed by Dreyer. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1955:** *Ordet* (*The Word*). Danish feature adapted, directed, and produced by Dreyer from Kaj Munk’s play. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1964:** *Gertrud*. Danish feature adapted, directed, and co-produced by Dreyer from Hjalmar Söderberg’s play; it was his final completed film. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)

### Early scenarios and screenplays for films directed by others

- **1912:** *Bryggerens Datter* (*The Brewer’s Daughter*; released abroad as *Dagmar*). Scenario co-written by Dreyer for Rasmus Ottesen’s film; the scenario itself is no longer extant. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1912:** *Dødsridtet* (*The Leap to Death*). Scenario for Rasmus Ottesen’s film; no copy of Dreyer’s scenario is known to survive, and some filmographies also credit him with a small acting appearance. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1913:** *Balloneksplosionen* (*The Hidden Message*). Scenario for Kay van der Aa Kühle’s film; the text is no longer extant. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1913:** *Chatollets Hemmelighed* (*The Secret of the Bureau*; also *Det gamle Chatol*). Surviving scenario for Hjalmar Davidsen’s film, catalogued in the Nordisk collection as no. 164b. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **c. 1913:** *Döden Forener* (*Death Unites*). Scenario sent to the Swedish Film Institute; the DFI-derived filmography does not identify a resulting film, so this is a project-text rather than a completed screen work. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1913:** *Hans og Grethe*. Scenario filmed by Sofus Wolder as *Elskovs Opfindsomhed* (*Love’s Ingenuity*), preserved under Nordisk no. 1062; manuscript and released film therefore bear different titles. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1913:** *Krigskorrespondenter* (*War Correspondents*). Scenario for Vilhelm Glückstadt’s film; the text is no longer extant. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1915:** *Skelethaanden* (*The Skeleton Hand*). Surviving scenario, Nordisk no. 1335, filmed by Alexander Christian as *Juvelerernes Skræk*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1915:** *Ned med Vaabnene!* (*Lay Down Your Arms!*). Scenario adapting Bertha von Suttner, filmed by Holger-Madsen; preserved as Nordisk no. 1163a. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *Esther*. Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1352a and filmed by Holger-Madsen as *Den Hvide Djævel* (*The Devil’s Protégé*). [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *Den Skønne Evelyn* (*Evelyn the Beautiful*). Scenario for A. W. Sandberg’s film, preserved as Nordisk no. 1371b. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *En Forbryders Memoirer* (*Memoirs of a Criminal*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1366b and filmed by Alexander Christian as *En Forbryders Liv og Levned*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *Lerhjertet* (*The Clay Heart*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1405 and filmed by Holger-Madsen as *Guldets Gift*; the released film also circulated as *The Temptation of Mrs. Chestney*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *Guldkuglen* (*The Golden Bullet*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1200 and filmed by Karl Mantzius as *Pavillonens Hemmelighed*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *Penge* (*Money*). Scenario for Karl Mantzius’s film, preserved as Nordisk no. 1236b. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1916:** *Den Røde Enke* (*The Red Widow*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1350 and filmed by August Blom as *Rovedderkoppen* (*The Spider’s Prey*). [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1917:** *Legationens Gidsel* (*The Embassy’s Hostage*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1488a and filmed by August Blom as *Den Mystiske Selskabsdame*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1917:** *Fange No. 113* (*Convict No. 113*). Scenario for Holger-Madsen’s film, preserved as Nordisk no. 1551. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1917:** *Miskendt* (*Misjudged*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1595a and filmed by Alexander Christian as *Glædens Dag*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1917:** *Hans Rigtige Kone* (*His Real Wife*; distributed as *Which Is Which?*). Scenario for Holger-Madsen’s film, preserved as Nordisk no. 1591. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1917:** *Hænderne* (*The Hands*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1557 and filmed by Alexander Christian as *Herregaards-Mysteriet*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1917:** *Hotel Paradis*. Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1621 and directed by Robert Dinesen. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1918:** *Lydia*. Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1479c and filmed by Holger-Madsen; it circulated in Britain as *The Music-Hall Star*. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1919:** *Gillekop*. Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1509c and filmed by August Blom; although released in 1919, it belongs to Dreyer’s earlier Nordisk screenwriting activity. [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)
- **1919:** *Kniplinger* (*Lace*). Scenario preserved as Nordisk no. 1732b and filmed by August Blom as *Grevindens Ære* (*The Countess’s Honour*). [[1]](https://escholarship.org/content/qt97m380jb/qt97m380jb.pdf)

### Later contributions to films by others

- **1946:** *De Gamle* (*The Seventh Age*). Short documentary directed by Torben Anton Svendsen from a screenplay by Dreyer; this is Dreyer’s made text but not his film as director. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)
- **1949:** *Radioens Barndom* (*Radio’s Childhood*). Documentary directed by Hagen Hasselbalch and edited by Dreyer; Dreyer’s contribution is an editorial construction, not a separately surviving authored text. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)
- **1950:** *Shakespeare og Kronborg* (*Shakespeare and Kronborg*). Documentary directed by Jørgen Roos and Erling Schroeder from Dreyer’s screenplay. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)
- **1954:** *Rønnes og Nexøs Genopbygning* (*The Reconstruction of Rønne and Nexø*). Poul Bang’s documentary originated in a screenplay or film idea by Dreyer; sources differ over whether to call his contribution a screenplay or only the original idea. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)
- **1956:** *Noget om Norden* (*Something about the North*). Bent H. Barfod’s animated short, made from a screenplay by Dreyer. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)

### Unrealised film projects and completed project-texts

- **1929–1968:** *Jesus fra Nazaret* (*Jesus of Nazareth*). Dreyer’s lifelong, extensively researched project for a film presenting Jesus within Jewish historical life; the film never appeared, but the completed screenplay was published posthumously in 1968. The project—not merely its unrealised cinematic manifestation—is one of Dreyer’s principal obras. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer) [[6]](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/why-carl-theodor-dreyers-unmade-jesus-film-one-cinemas-great-what-ifs)
- **1930s–1950s:** *Maria Stuart* (*Mary, Queen of Scots*). Unrealised colour-film project developed into a completed screenplay of roughly 240–250 pages with Dreyer’s son Erik; proposed producers rejected its scale, and Dreyer later attempted to revive it. [[3]](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/a-z-carl-theodor-dreyer) [[7]](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1553075/1/Thomson%20JSCA%202017.pdf)
- **1945:** *Därför dräper jag* / *Derfor dræber jeg* (*Therefore I Kill*). Unrealised crime-film project comprising a synopsis and screenplay, co-written with Sigurd Hoel for a proposed Swedish production. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer)
- **1950s–1960s:** *As I Lay Dying*. Unrealised project to adapt William Faulkner’s novel; sources document continued development but not a completed film, and its surviving materials should therefore be treated as a project rather than a cinematic manifestation. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)
- **1950s–1960s:** *Mourning Becomes Electra*. Unrealised project to adapt Eugene O’Neill’s dramatic trilogy; the available filmographies identify an intention under development, not a completed screen work. [[5]](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2002/great-directors/dreyer/)
- **1965:** *Medea*. Completed screenplay for an unrealised colour film, co-written with Elsa Gress; Lars von Trier’s 1987 television film used Dreyer’s scenario but is a later manifestation by another director, not a film made by Dreyer. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer) [[7]](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1553075/1/Thomson%20JSCA%202017.pdf)

### Writings on film and theatre

- **1920:** *Svensk Film* (*Swedish Film*). Critical essay later collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1920s:** *Nye Tanker om Filmen: Benjamin Christensen og hans Idéer* (*New Ideas about Film: Benjamin Christensen and His Ideas*). Film-critical essay later collected in *Om Filmen*; the consulted collected-work record does not supply a secure first-publication date. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1926:** *Fransk Film* (*French Film*). Critical essay later collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1929:** *Realiseret Mystik* (*Realized Mysticism*). Essay articulating Dreyer’s historical and aesthetic approach to *The Passion of Joan of Arc*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1933:** *Den virkelige Talefilm* (*The Real Talking Film*). Film-theoretical essay on sound cinema. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1936:** *Film og Kritik I–III* (*Film and Criticism I–III*). Three-part critical intervention; it acts partly as a manifesto for more serious film criticism rather than merely describing contemporary cinema. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1936:** *Bag Paris’ Boulevarder* (*Behind the Boulevards of Paris*). Article later collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1939:** *To Skuespil, der Faldt* (*Two Plays That Flopped*). Theatre-critical essay asking whether direction caused the failure of two productions. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1939:** *Når de Døde Lever* (*When the Dead Live*). Polemical reply in an exchange with theatre director Mogens Dam; as an intervention in an active dispute, it is closer to a manifesto than a neutral essay. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1943:** *Lidt om Filmstil* (*A Little on Film Style*). Film-aesthetic essay stating Dreyer’s principles of abstraction, rhythm, and psychological realism. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Peer Gynt paa Paladsteatret* (*Peer Gynt at the Palace Theatre*). Film-critical text collected in *Om Filmen*; no secure date is supplied by the consulted contents record. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Gulliver-Filmen* (*The Gulliver Film*). Film-critical text collected in *Om Filmen*; its first-publication date was not established from the consulted source. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Jannings-Filmen* (*The Jannings Film*). Film-critical text collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Om Skuespilkunst* (*About Acting*). Dreyer’s reply concerning the Jannings film, collected in *Om Filmen*; it belongs to a public exchange rather than standing as an isolated treatise. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Nye Veje for dansk Film—H. C. Andersen* (*New Roads for Danish Film—H. C. Andersen*). Programmatic essay proposing directions for Danish cinema; its prescriptive force makes “manifesto” an apt category. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Filmteknik og Manuskripter* (*Film Technique and Scripts*). Practical and critical comments addressed to Kjeld Abell; neither a screenplay nor a formal protocol, but a text about cinematic making. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Fotograferingen i dansk Film* (*Photography in Danish Film*). Critical essay collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Et Brev til Politiken* (*A Letter to Politiken*). Public letter collected among Dreyer’s film writings; its epistolary and occasional form resists classification as a conventional essay. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Min eneste store Lidenskab* (*My Only Great Passion*). Autobiographical statement on cinema, collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Hvem korsfæstede Jesus?* (*Who Crucified Jesus?*). Historical-religious essay connected to Dreyer’s Jesus project. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Filmatiseringen af Ordet* (*The Cinematization of Ordet*). Reflective account of transforming Kaj Munk’s play into a film. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Farvefilm og koloristisk Film* (*Color Film and Colored Film*). Film-aesthetic essay distinguishing mere colour reproduction from expressive colour design. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Fantasi og Farve* (*Imagination and Color*). Film-aesthetic essay collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Antisemitismens Rødder* (*The Roots of Anti-Semitism*). Historical and polemical essay associated with Dreyer’s sustained treatment of Jewish persecution and the Jesus project. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- *Ebbe Neergaard*. Commemorative or critical text on the Danish film historian, collected in *Om Filmen*. [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)

### Books and collected manifestations

- **1959:** *Om Filmen: Artikler og Interviews*. Authorised collection of Dreyer’s articles and interviews, edited by Erik Ulrichsen; it is a manifestation assembling previously separate texts, not a single newly composed treatise. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer) [[8]](https://www.videohistoryproject.org/dreyer-double-reflection-translation-carl-th-dreyers-writings-about-film-om-filmen)
- **1964:** *Fire Film: Jeanne d’Arc, Vampyr, Vredens Dag, Ordet*. Collected publication of four screenplays; the volume is a textual manifestation of films and working texts already represented above, not four additional films. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer)
- **1968:** *Jesus fra Nazaret: Et Filmmanuskript*. Posthumously published screenplay embodying the unrealised Jesus project; publication made the text public but did not complete the intended film. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer)

### Works that resist itemisation

- **1909–1913, with later returns to journalism:** *Newspaper reporting, aviation writing, portraits, theatre criticism, and film criticism*. Dreyer produced a substantial journalistic corpus for papers including *Berlingske Tidende*, *Riget*, *Ekstra Bladet*, and later *B.T.*; many surviving pieces lack bylines, and no consulted scholarly collected edition establishes an item-by-item canon, so claiming a complete list of titles would manufacture certainty. [[2]](https://biografiskleksikon.lex.dk/Carl_Th._Dreyer) [[3]](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/a-z-carl-theodor-dreyer)
- **1913–1918:** *Intertitles, script reports, adaptations, and editorial work at Nordisk Film*. These were made textual interventions in many studio productions, but often survive only as anonymous or corporate production labour; BFI reports more than thirty known Dreyer screenplays, while the DFI-derived catalogue securely identifies the scenarios listed above, leaving additional attribution and completeness unresolved. [[3]](https://www.bfi.org.uk/features/a-z-carl-theodor-dreyer) [[9]](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/film-and-television-biographies/carl-theodor-dreyer)
