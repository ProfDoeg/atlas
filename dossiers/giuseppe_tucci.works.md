## Works by Giuseppe Tucci

### Scholarly editions, translations, and textual reconstructions

- **1921:** *Scritti di Mencio*. An Italian translation and interpretive presentation of writings attributed to Mencius; a philological work rather than an original philosophical treatise. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 20]
- **1922:** *La Karpūramañjarī di Rājaśekhara*. Italian translation of a Prakrit drama, with Tucci acting as translator-editor. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 26]
- **1925:** *Su Sung-ku, apologia del confucianesimo*. Translation of a Chinese manuscript, presented as a Confucian apology. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 48]
- **1925:** *In cammino verso la luce di Çāntideva*. Italian translation of Śāntideva’s *Bodhicaryāvatāra*, with introduction. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 49]
- **1925:** *Le cento strofe (Çataçastra)*. Translation, introduction, and commentary based on the Chinese version of a Mahāyāna text; it revises and completes Tucci’s installments of 1922–23 and is therefore best understood as one evolving editorial project. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 30, 37, 52]
- **1929:** *Pre-Diṅnāga Buddhist Texts on Logic from Chinese Sources*. Edition, translation, and reconstruction of early Buddhist logical texts preserved in Chinese. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 71]
- **1930:** *The Nyāyamukha of Diṅnāga, Being the Oldest Buddhist Text on Logic after Chinese and Tibetan Materials*. A reconstructed and translated philosophical text assembled from Chinese and Tibetan witnesses. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 78]
- **1932:** *Madhyāntavibhāgasūtrabhāṣyaṭīkā of Sthiramati*. Critical edition made jointly with Vidhushekhara Bhattacharya; authorship of the modern object is collaborative, while Sthiramati is the historical author edited. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 92]
- **1932:** *Prajñāpāramitās: Commentaries on the Prajñāpāramitā, I: The Abhisamayālaṅkārāloka of Haribhadra*. A large critical edition of a Buddhist philosophical commentary. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 93]
- **1949:** *Il libro tibetano dei morti*. Translation and interpretive presentation of texts from the Tibetan *Bar do thos grol* tradition, not a single Tibetan “book” authored by Tucci; the substantially revised 1972 edition is a later manifestation. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 192]
- **1949:** *Tibetan Folksongs from the District of Gyantse*. Edition and translation of an oral-song corpus; the enlarged 1966 *Tibetan Folksongs from Gyantse and Western Tibet* is a revised manifestation, not an independent composition. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 195]
- **1956–1971:** *Minor Buddhist Texts*. A three-volume scholarly edition-and-translation project issued intermittently over fifteen years; its unity is editorial rather than that of a continuously written monograph. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 245, 261, 335]
- **1971:** *Deb t’er dmar po gsar ma: Tibetan Chronicles by bSod nams grags pa, vol. I*. Edition, emendation, English translation, and supplementary edition of two shorter chronicles; the projected wording “vol. I” resists closure because no second Tucci volume followed. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 334]

### Monographs and synthetic studies

- **1922:** *Storia della filosofia cinese antica*. A historical-philosophical monograph. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 25]
- **1923–1929:** *Linee di una storia del materialismo indiano*. A two-part research project published in academy memoirs; parts were later substantially revised or abridged in *Opera Minora*. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 41, 72]
- **1924:** *Apologia del Taoismo*. A short interpretive monograph framed as an “apology,” rhetorical in form but not a political manifesto. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 46]
- **1926:** *Il Buddhismo*. A general historical and doctrinal synthesis. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 59]
- **1926:** *Saggezza cinese*. An interpretive anthology and introductory study rather than a single authored argument. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 60]
- **1930:** *On Some Aspects of the Doctrines of Maitreya[nātha] and Asaṅga*. A monograph developed from Tucci’s Calcutta University readership lectures. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 77]
- **1931:** *Teorie ed esperienze dei mistici tibetani*. A short study first issued separately and in a periodical, then partly rewritten into *Forme dello spirito asiatico*; these are manifestations of an evolving text. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 89]
- **1934:** *L’Oriente nella cultura contemporanea*. A published address or position paper on the place of Asian cultures in contemporary thought. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 105]
- **1936:** *Ramakrishna Paramahamsa*. A short interpretive monograph; the later English article is a translated manifestation. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 119]
- **1940:** *Forme dello spirito asiatico*. A synthetic book assembled partly by revising earlier essays; it is simultaneously a monograph and a recomposed body of prior work. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 148]
- **1940:** *Travels of Tibetan Pilgrims in the Swat Valley*. A historical-geographical monograph based on Tibetan itineraries; a corrected, shortened form later entered *Opera Minora*. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 149]
- **1940:** *La crisi spirituale dell’India moderna*. A published lecture and diagnosis of modern Indian intellectual life. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 150]
- **1942:** *Il Buscido*. A short account of Bushidō; the title reflects the Italian transcription then used. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 168]
- **1943:** *Il Giappone: tradizione storica e tradizione artistica*. A synthetic cultural history. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 176]
- **1946:** *Asia religiosa*. A comparative synthesis of Asian religious forms. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 184]
- **1949:** *Teoria e pratica del Mandala*. An interpretive study of the mandala as doctrine, ritual method, and visual structure; revised editions and English, French, and Portuguese translations are later manifestations. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 194]
- **1949:** *Italia e Oriente*. A collection and synthesis concerning historical relations between Italy and Asia. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 196]
- **1950:** *The Tombs of the Tibetan Kings*. An archaeological and historical monograph inaugurating the *Serie Orientale Roma*. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 202]
- **1954:** *Marco Polo*. A short commemorative-historical study, later manifested in English and as an Italian periodical article. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 228–228a, 232]
- **1955:** *Fulvio Maroi*. A printed commemoration: an occasional speech made into a pamphlet, not a conventional monograph. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 240]
- **1956:** *Preliminary Report on Two Scientific Expeditions in Nepal*. A field report combining archaeological, art-historical, and expeditionary documentation. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 246]
- **1956:** *Vita nomade*. A short occasional publication produced for the Club Campeggiatori Romani. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 247]
- **1957:** *Storia della filosofia indiana*. A comprehensive history of Indian philosophy; the two-volume 1977 revision is a new manifestation of the same obra. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 255]
- **1958:** *Le grandi vie di comunicazione Europa-Asia*. A historical-geographical synthesis originating in radio culture and published as a book. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 262]
- **1958:** *Collaborazione con l’Oriente*. A programmatic address on cultural cooperation; it approaches a manifesto because it advocates institutional action rather than merely describing Asia. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 263]
- **1959:** *Orientalisti marchigiani*. A published lecture on scholars of the Marche and their engagement with Asia. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 273]
- **1969:** *Rati-līlā: An Interpretation of Tantric Imagery of the Temples of Nepal*. An illustrated art-historical and religious interpretation, issued concurrently in English, French, and German; these are linguistic manifestations of one work. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 321]
- **1970:** *Die Religionen Tibets*. Tucci’s book-length contribution to the jointly issued *Die Religionen Tibets und der Mongolei*; revised French, Italian, and English versions appeared later, so the obra exceeds any single edition. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 324]
- **1971:** *Ciro il Grande*. A bilingual printed commemorative address, preserving an act of public speech. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 331]
- **1971:** *Opera Minora*. A two-volume retrospective collection of Tucci’s scholarly articles. It is a new editorial object but not forty-odd newly authored works; several constituent essays were revised for inclusion. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 330]

### Expedition books, travel narratives, and visual atlases

- **1932–1941:** *Indo-Tibetica*. A seven-volume Gesamtkunstwerk of field observation, textual scholarship, architectural description, inscriptions, and photographic plates: I, *mC’od-rten e ts’a-ts’a* (1932); II, *Rin-c’en-bzan-po* (1933); III.1, *Spiti e Kunavar* (1935); III.2, *Tsaparang* (1936); and IV, *Gyantse e i suoi monasteri*, in three volumes (1941). Although bibliographically divided, Tucci conceived it as a single cumulative research project. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 94, 96, 109, 118, 161]
- **1934:** *Cronaca della missione scientifica Tucci nel Tibet occidentale (1933)*. Expedition chronicle made jointly with Eugenio Ghersi; *Secrets of Tibet* and *Shrines of a Thousand Buddhas* are adapted English-language manifestations rather than separate journeys. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 104]
- **1937:** *Santi e briganti nel Tibet ignoto*. A travel narrative combining expedition record, ethnography, and popular writing; reissued in 1978 as *Tibet ignoto*. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 129]
- **1949:** *Tibetan Painted Scrolls*. A two-volume study with a separate portfolio of 256 plates. Its inseparability of argument, catalogue, photography, and image sequence makes it a scholarly Gesamtkunstwerk rather than simply a “book.” [Petech–Scialpi, no. 193]
- **1950:** *A Lhasa e oltre*. Diary of the 1948 Tibetan expedition; revised Italian and English editions are later manifestations. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 201]
- **1953:** *Tra giungle e pagode*. Account of the 1952 journey to Mustang; the English *Journey to Mustang 1952* is a later translated manifestation. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 226]
- **1960:** *Nepal: Alla scoperta dei Malla*. An illustrated journey and art-historical book; revised Italian and English manifestations appeared later. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 283]
- **1963:** *La via dello Svat*. A travel-and-archaeology narrative about Swat, later reprinted but not thereby remade as another work. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 301]
- **1967:** *Tibet, paese delle nevi*. A large illustrated synthesis, simultaneously issued in Italian, English, and French. Its photographic and textual components make it closer to an atlas than to a conventional monograph. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 314]
- **1967:** *Il trono di diamante*. A travel-cultural narrative concerning Buddhist Asia. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 315]
- **1973:** *Tibet*. An illustrated archaeological atlas in the *Archaeologia Mundi* series; *The Ancient Civilization of Transhimalaya* and the German and Italian *Tibet* editions are manifestations of this same work. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 340]

### Essays, articles, reference works, reviews, and occasional texts

- **1911–1983:** *The periodical and occasional corpus*. Petech and Scialpi’s final bibliography enumerates 360 principal entries—including scholarly articles, encyclopedia entries, lectures, expedition reports, reviews, prefaces, memorial notices, popular essays, and annual clusters of short book notices—plus lettered records for translations and revised editions. This corpus resists treatment as 360 autonomous obras: installments were completed by later installments, articles were rewritten as book chapters, translations duplicate earlier texts, and the bibliography itself groups numerous unsigned-length notices under one annual number; it is therefore identified here as a body of work whose individual manifestations are preserved in the cited chronological bibliography. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 1–360]
- **1942–1943:** *Articles in Yamato and other unrecorded journalistic pieces*. These writings are known to have existed, but the standard 1971 bibliography expressly excluded daily-newspaper pieces and some occasional or simply popular articles, specifically mentioning *Yamato*. Their titles, dates, and completeness cannot safely be reconstructed from that bibliography, so this is a resistant, incompletely catalogued corpus rather than a falsely enumerated set of works. [*Opera Minora*, “Bibliografia degli scritti … dal 1911 al 1970,” prefatory note]
- **1942–1943:** *Paolo Emilio Pavolini: commemorazione tenuta il 26 novembre 1942*. A commemorative address printed in advance for an academy yearbook that was never published. The offprint exists, but the intended containing volume does not; its status is thus a surviving manifestation of an aborted publication project. [Petech–Scialpi, no. 182]
- **1950–1970:** *Notizie di libri in East and West*. Recurrent clusters of short book notices, grouped annually by the bibliography rather than treated as individually titled reviews. They resist singular-work classification because the standard bibliography supplies neither stable individual titles nor a one-notice/one-entry correspondence. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 225, 227, 237, 244, 254, 260, 272, 282, 285, 291, 300, 306, 313, 317, 320, 323, 329]
- **1933–1980:** *Encyclopedia articles*. A reference corpus including entries on Lamaism, Tibet, Neo-Indian literatures, Buddhism, Japanese demonic imagery, Tibetan schools, and Buddhism for the *New Encyclopaedia Britannica*. Each is a made text, but collectively they constitute Tucci’s recurring lexicographic project rather than a book. [Petech–Scialpi, nos. 100, 125, 135, 142, 297–298, 310, 345, 354]
- **1923–1981:** *Critical reviews and prefaces*. The chronological bibliography records substantial reviews, introductions, presentations, and prefaces separately from routine book notices. These are paratextual works: made texts that act upon another person’s publication and therefore resist being called independent obras without qualification. [Petech–Scialpi, passim, especially nos. 42–44, 57, 101, 116–117, 128, 143, 208, 243, 270–271, 281, 290, 295, 299, 305, 308, 312, 316, 336–339, 342–343, 346, 348, 351, 353, 355–360]

### Institutional, editorial, archaeological, and archival projects

- **1921–1924:** *Alle Fonti delle Religioni*. A journal directed by Tucci. It is a collective editorial project rather than a solely authored text, and its contents cannot be attributed wholesale to him.
- **1933–1984:** *Istituto Italiano per il Medio ed Estremo Oriente (IsMEO)*. An institution founded by Tucci with Giovanni Gentile and subsequently shaped by Tucci as its principal organizer. It is an institutional obra—an enduring apparatus for research, publishing, diplomacy, and fieldwork—not a text by a single maker. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1935–1943:** *Bollettino dell’IsMEO / Asiatica*. An editorial project directed by Tucci; the title changed from *Bollettino dell’IsMEO* to *Asiatica*. Authorship remains distributed among contributors. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1946:** *Le scienze del mistero e il mistero delle scienze*. A short-lived periodical directed by Tucci. It is a collective editorial manifestation, not a Tucci-authored book. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1950–1978:** *East and West*. An international journal directed by Tucci, who remained general editor thereafter. The journal is best classified as a long-duration editorial and intercultural project whose individual articles have many authors. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **from 1950:** *Serie Orientale Roma*. A scholarly publication series directed by Tucci. It is a publishing protocol and institutional body of work, not an individually authored text. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1928–1948:** *Tibetan, Ladakhi, and Spiti expeditions*. Eight expeditions organized and led by Tucci produced notebooks, photographs, architectural records, inscriptions, manuscripts, collected objects, and later books. The expeditions themselves are projects; their heterogeneous documentary residue is a distributed obra that cannot be reduced to the resulting publications. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”; Museo delle Civiltà, “Giuseppe Tucci”]
- **1929–1954:** *Nepal expeditions*. Five field projects generating archaeological, religious, photographic, and ethnographic documentation. Their products survive across publications, museum holdings, and archives rather than as a single fixed work. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **from 1955:** *Italian Archaeological Mission in Swat*. A long-running excavation and research program initiated by Tucci. It is a collaborative archaeological obra whose sites, finds, reports, and institutional continuities resist attribution to Tucci alone. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **from 1956:** *Italian archaeological research in Afghanistan*. A field-research program initiated under Tucci’s direction; collective and institutional authorship must be retained. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **from 1959:** *Italian archaeological research in Iran*. A research and conservation program directed by Tucci until 1978. It produced excavations, restorations, documentation, and reports rather than one bounded work. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1950s–1978:** *Reports and Memoirs*. An archaeological publication series directed by Tucci from 1962 and closely tied to the Asian field missions. It is an editorial infrastructure and continuing collective project. [Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1928–1978:** *Photographic archive of the Asian missions*. A large corpus made through Tucci’s expeditions but also by collaborators and mission photographers. Because individual exposures and attributions are not uniformly settled, it should not be silently described as “photographs by Tucci”; it is a mission-generated archival obra now preserved by the Museo delle Civiltà. [Museo delle Civiltà, “Giuseppe Tucci”; Treccani, “Tucci, Giuseppe”]
- **1928–1978:** *Collections of manuscripts, ritual objects, and works of Asian art*. Tucci’s expeditions assembled documentary and museum collections, but “made” here means selected, gathered, classified, and institutionally recontextualized—not fabricated by Tucci. Their makers are often Tibetan, Nepalese, Indian, Pakistani, Afghan, or Iranian artists and scribes whose authorship must not be displaced by the collector’s name. [Museo delle Civiltà, “Giuseppe Tucci”]
