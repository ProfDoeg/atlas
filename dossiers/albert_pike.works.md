## Works by Albert Pike

### Poetry, songs, and mixed literary books

- **1830–1891:** *Individual poems and songs*. Boyden identifies more than sixty separately published poems, songs, sonnets, elegies, hymns, and occasional verses, including *Ambition*, *The Brothers*, *To Apollo*, *To Neptune*, *To Ceres*, *To Somnus*, *Ariel*, *Dixie*, *Disunion*, *Every Year*, *The Fine Arkansas Gentleman*, *The Freemason’s Son*, *The Mocking Bird*, *To Spring*, and *Yes, Call Us Rebels*. Many first appeared anonymously or under initials, several lack fixed titles, and Boyden explicitly records some as absent from every collected edition; this corpus therefore resists reduction to the later poetry books. (Boyden, pp. 23–30.)
- **1834:** *Prose Sketches and Poems Written in the Western Country*. A mixed-media literary obra containing travel narrative, sketches, and verse rather than simply a poetry collection. (Boyden, pp. 23, 31.)
- **1854:** *Nugae*. A privately printed collection of poems, limited to 150 copies and expressly withheld from commercial publication. (Boyden, p. 23.)
- **1872–1882:** *Hymns to the Gods and Other Poems*. A continuing poetic project issued first as a privately printed volume, then as Part I in 1873 and Part II in 1882; the two parts were normally bound together. (Boyden, p. 23.)
- **1900:** *Gen. Albert Pike’s Poems*. A posthumous collected obra edited and introduced by his daughter Lilian Pike Roome; the compilation is hers, although the poems are Pike’s. (Boyden, p. 23.)
- **1916:** *Hymns to the Gods and Other Poems*. A posthumous selection edited by Lilian Pike Roome, not a new work made by Pike. (Boyden, p. 23.)
- **1916:** *Lyrics and Love Songs*. A posthumous thematic selection edited by Lilian Pike Roome; its status is editorial manifestation rather than authorial book-project. (Boyden, p. 23.)

### Essays, narratives, speeches, and biography

- **1830:** *The Philosophy of a Cigar*. A periodical essay signed only “P”; Boyden attributes it to Pike. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1830:** *The Philosophy of Walking*. A periodical essay later reprinted in the *Boston Pearl*. (Boyden, pp. 31–32.)
- **1831:** *The Philosophy of Bowling*. A humorous periodical essay. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1834–1835:** *Crayon Sketches and Journeyings*. A three-part periodical sequence of travel and observational prose. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1835–1836:** *Letters from Arkansas*. Two published epistolary sketches whose serial identity is incomplete: Boyden numbers the first but supplies “[no. 2]” for the second. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1835:** *Narrative of a Journey in the Prairie*. A travel narrative first contained in *Prose Sketches and Poems*, then serialized in the *Arkansas Advocate*; later publication is a manifestation of the same text. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1835:** *Western Superstitions*. A short periodical essay. (Boyden, p. 32.)
- **1834:** *Western Traveling*. A short periodical travel sketch. (Boyden, p. 32.)
- **1836:** *A Journey to Xemes*. A periodical narrative. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1836:** *Life in Arkansas*. A two-part periodical sketch. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1845–1846:** *The Walking Gentleman*. A discontinuous periodical series appearing in four installments; Boyden notes that it was unsigned but attributed to Pike by the magazine’s index. (Boyden, p. 32.)
- **1852:** *Address to the Young Ladies of the Tulip Female Seminary and Cadets of the Arkansas Military Institute*. A ceremonial educational address delivered at Tulip, Arkansas. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1875:** *Mexico: Anniversary of the Capture of the Capital*. A commemorative oration by Pike, printed from the *Washington Chronicle*. (Boyden, p. 31.)
- **1880:** *Essays*. A five-volume manuscript obra of twenty-nine named essays dedicated to sculptor Vinnie Ream Hoxie; it remained unpublished as a whole and is consequently a project and physical manuscript object, not an author-issued book. (Boyden, pp. 33–34.)
- **1886:** *Autobiography*. An 86-page typescript made from stenographic notes dictated by Pike; Boyden stresses that it is longer and different from the printed autobiographical speech. (Boyden, p. 33.)
- **date not fixed:** *Autobiography / “Resurrection Speech”*. A printed autobiographical address delivered at a Washington banquet prompted by a false report of Pike’s death; Pike corrected a copy of the later Hallum printing. (Boyden, p. 5.)
- **dates not fixed:** *Biographical sketches of James W. Bates, Robert Crittenden, Edward Cross, William Cummins, Terrence Farrelly, Samuel S. Hall, Thomas Hubbard, Thomas J. Lacy, John Taylor, David Walker, and Archibald Yell*. A series of biographical miniatures later printed in John Hallum’s history of Arkansas. (Boyden, p. 5.)

### Journalism and editorial projects

- **1834–1837:** *Arkansas Advocate*. Pike was associate editor and then editor-owner; the newspaper is a collective serial manifestation, so individual unsigned matter cannot safely be treated as wholly his. (Boyden, p. 19.)
- **1867–1868:** *Memphis Appeal*. Pike’s editor-in-chiefship was an editorial project rather than a single authored text; Boyden dates it only approximately. (Boyden, p. 19.)
- **1868–1870:** *The Patriot*. Pike served as associate editor of this Washington newspaper; again, the made thing is partly editorial and collective. (Boyden, p. 19.)
- **1870–1892:** *Official Bulletins of the Supreme Council*. A ten-volume serial compiled and edited by Pike, incorporating addresses, decisions, translations, histories, obituaries, correspondence, and polemic. Volume 10, no. 2 appeared after his death, so the entire surviving series is not wholly an author-supervised object. (Boyden, pp. 69–70.)
- **1887–1891:** *Occasional Bulletins of the Supreme Council*. Twelve separately issued compilations edited by Pike; no. 12 appeared after his death and is therefore a posthumous manifestation of his assembled material. (Boyden, pp. 68–69.)

### Law books, reports, and forensic works

- **1838:** *Revised Statutes of the State of Arkansas*. Pike supplied the notes and index to a statutory compilation revised by William McK. Ball and Sam C. Roane; it is collaborative legal infrastructure rather than a wholly authored book. (Boyden, p. 7.)
- **1840–1845:** *Reports of Cases Argued and Determined in the Supreme Court of Law and Equity of the State of Arkansas*. A five-volume reporter’s obra covering the court’s 1837–1844 terms. (Boyden, p. 7.)
- **1842:** *The Arkansas Form Book*. A protocol: legal forms and practical instructions for professional and popular use, accompanied by a summary of applicable law. (Boyden, p. 7.)
- **1840s:** *Anecdotes of the Arkansas Bar*. A short legal-literary sketch published under “a backwoods lawyer”; Boyden supplies the attribution to Pike. (Boyden, p. 7.)
- **1851–1879:** *Printed legal briefs, arguments, memorials, and appellate submissions*. Boyden separately describes scores of case-specific objects for Arkansas, Louisiana, Kentucky, Tennessee, federal, and United States Supreme Court litigation. Many are signed jointly by Pike with Cummins, Hunton, Robert W. Johnson, Luther H. Pike, or other counsel; authorship is therefore frequently collaborative, and the corpus should not be mistaken for conventional books. (Boyden, pp. 7–17.)
- **1852:** *Memorial of the Muscogee or Creek Nation to the Congress of the United States*. A legal-political petition signed by Pike as the Creek Nation’s attorney: a text intended to act upon government rather than merely describe a claim. (Boyden, p. 14.)
- **1855:** *Notes on the Civil Code of Louisiana*. A 218-page manuscript legal commentary made at New Orleans. (Boyden, p. 34.)
- **1876:** *Maxims of the Roman Law and Some of the Ancient French Law*. A thirteen-volume, 3,340-page manuscript compilation developed over many years; its form is closer to a research apparatus than a finished authorial book. (Boyden, p. 34.)
- **about 1834:** *Rules and Orders of the Supreme Court of the United States*. Not an independently authored work: Pike transformed a printed copy through twenty-nine manuscript pages of notes and amendments. (Boyden, p. 35.)

### Political, economic, diplomatic, and military interventions

- **1849:** *National Plan of an Atlantic and Pacific Railroad*. A printed economic-development address delivered at Memphis. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1854–1856:** *Addresses, resolutions, bills, and debates for the Southern commercial conventions*. A body of convention interventions concerning the Southern Pacific Railroad, commerce, and the slave trade; these are procedural and collective political texts rather than a single stable book. (Boyden, pp. 20–21.)
- **1855:** *Address on the Southern Pacific Railroad*. A public infrastructure address delivered before the Louisiana House of Representatives. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1855:** *Address by the President of the State Council of Arkansas*. A political address for the American Party. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1856:** *Letters to the People of the Northern States*. A political pamphlet signed “A. P.” and surviving in two differently paginated manifestations. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1856:** *To the American Party South*. A political appeal signed “A. P.” (Boyden, p. 21.)
- **1856:** *To the People of Arkansas and California*. A political address signed by Pike. (Boyden, p. 21.)
- **1857:** *Kansas State Rights: An Appeal to the Democracy of the South*. A party-political manifesto published under “A Southern State-Rights Democrat,” identified by Boyden as Pike. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **about 1857:** *Overland Route to the Pacific*. A transportation and development proposal published as by “a citizen of Arkansas,” whom Boyden identifies as Pike. (Boyden, p. 21.)
- **1859:** *Thoughts on Certain Political Questions*. A political treatise issued pseudonymously as “by a looker-on.” (Boyden, p. 21.)
- **1861:** *Draught of a Declaration of Independence*. A proposed manifesto for the Arkansas secession convention, withdrawn before adoption; its failed performative status is essential—it was designed to enact independence but did not. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1861:** *State or Province? Bond or Free?* A secessionist political address, followed by a separately issued appendix. (Boyden, p. 21.)
- **1861:** *Confederate treaties with the Creek, Choctaw and Chickasaw, Seminole, Comanche, Wichita and affiliated bands, Osage, Quapaw, Seneca and Shawnee, and Cherokee nations*. Diplomatic protocols negotiated and executed by Pike as Confederate commissioner. They are jointly made instruments—agreements among sovereign parties—not solely authored literary works. (Boyden, pp. 21–22.)
- **1861:** *Letters of Safeguard*. Protective instruments issued to Indigenous nations west of Arkansas; these are performative documents intended to confer protection. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1862:** *Battle of Pea Ridge, or Elkhorn Tavern*. Pike’s military account of the battle. (Boyden, p. 18.)
- **1862:** *Letter to the President of the Confederate States*. A printed military letter from Fort McCulloch concerning General Thomas C. Hindman. (Boyden, p. 18.)
- **1862:** *Letter to Major General Holmes*. A military-political letter printed both on wallpaper as a broadside and as a seven-page pamphlet. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1862:** *Order, Headquarters, Department of Indian Territory*. A satirical military order issued under Pike’s command; formally it acts as an order even though Boyden describes it as sarcastic. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1863:** *Charges and Specifications against Major General Thomas C. Hindman*. A military accusation intended to initiate official proceedings. (Boyden, p. 18.)
- **1863:** *Address to the Senators and Representatives of Arkansas in the Confederate Congress*. A political-military defense connected with Pike’s charges against Hindman. (Boyden, p. 18.)
- **1863:** *Second Letter to Lieutenant General Theophilus H. Holmes*. A continuation of Pike’s dispute over Confederate command in Indian Territory. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1863:** *Maxims of Military Science and Art*. A six-volume, 2,182-page manuscript compilation from military writers, with Pike’s applications and illustrations; it is a research project and compilation more than an original treatise. (Boyden, p. 34.)
- **1865:** *A Letter to the President of the United States*. A postwar political petition or defense. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1865:** *Indictment for Treason*. A printed legal response signed by Pike as defendant; its bibliographic title derives from the proceeding rather than an authorial literary title. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **about 1867:** *The Past Teaching the Present and the Future*. An unfinished political project comprising clippings of installments published in the *Memphis Appeal* and 122 manuscript pages of unpublished continuation. (Boyden, pp. 34–35.)
- **about 1873:** *The Emphatic Remonstrance of the People of Arkansas*. A political manifesto opposing interference with Arkansas self-government. (Boyden, p. 20.)
- **1874:** *The True Merits of the Controversy in Arkansas*. A jointly made legal-political pamphlet by the firm of Pike & Johnson concerning the Baxter–Brooks gubernatorial dispute. (Boyden, p. 22.)

### Linguistic, comparative-religious, and antiquarian projects

- **date not fixed:** *Ancient Alphabets*. A 65-page manuscript reference work without a securely recorded date. (Boyden, p. 33.)
- **1860:** *Extracts from and Comments upon the Kabbala*. A translated and annotated manuscript compilation. (Boyden, p. 33.)
- **1872–1873:** *Ancient Faith and Worship of the Aryans, as Embodied in the Vedic Hymns*. A monumental three-volume manuscript study; its physical calligraphy and decoration make it partly a crafted manuscript object. (Boyden, p. 33.)
- **about 1873:** *Lectures of the Arya*. An eight-volume manuscript project on Indo-Iranian language, religion, and mythology, supplemented by a study titled *Aryan Notions of Philo Judaeus*. (Boyden, pp. 34–35.)
- **1878:** *Commentaries on the Kabbala*. A 235-page illuminated manuscript commentary. (Boyden, p. 33.)
- **1870s:** *Translations of the Rig-Veda: The Maruts*. A four-volume manuscript translation project. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **1870s:** *Translations from the Rig-Veda: Friends of Indra; Svadha; The Purusha Sukta; Savitri; Names of Rishis*. A separate 562-page translation manuscript. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **1870s:** *Translations of the Rig-Veda: Hymns to Tvashtri and the Ribhus*. A 716-page manuscript translation. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **1870s:** *Translations of the Rig-Veda: Of the Devas Generally and of Passages Which Mention the Arya and Dasyu*. A 632-page translation and thematic compilation. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **1872–1886?:** *Translations of the Rig-Veda: Consecutive*. A seven-volume work with three supplementary volumes totaling 6,939 pages. Boyden’s date is expressly conjectural, and Pike described the underlying study as extending over more than fourteen years. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **1857 and 1861:** *Vocabularies of Indian Languages*. A manuscript linguistic fieldwork corpus whose contents Pike said he collected in those two years. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **date not fixed:** *Vocabularies of Sanskrit Words*. Seven manuscript working sections apparently assembled to support Pike’s translations; they resist treatment as a finished work. (Boyden, p. 35.)
- **date not fixed:** *Excerpts*. A 176-page manuscript commonplace book of multilingual prose, poetry, and epitaphs; selection and arrangement, rather than original authorship, constitute the making. (Boyden, pp. 33–34.)

### Masonic rituals, protocols, and ceremonies

- **1854–1855:** *Masonic Rituals*. An 889-page manuscript translation and compilation, lavishly bound and lockable; it is at once protocol, research archive, and crafted material object. (Boyden, p. 38.)
- **1857–1858:** *Revised Rituals of the Eighteenth, Thirtieth, Thirty-first, and Thirty-second Degrees*. Ritual protocols revised collaboratively by Pike and Charles Laffon de Ladébat; the collaboration should not be silently converted into sole authorship. (Boyden, pp. 39, 65.)
- **1858:** *Ceremony of Reception of a Louveteau by a Symbolic Lodge*. A ritual protocol prepared by Pike. (Boyden, p. 37.)
- **1858:** *The Degree of Master Mark Mason*; *Grand Maître Écossais*; *Knight of the Mediterranean Pass*; and *Lyceum of the Second Degree*. Manuscript copies or translations made by Pike from older archival rituals; the textual substance was inherited, while Pike made the surviving copies and translations. (Boyden, p. 37.)
- **1859:** *The Statutes and Regulations, Institutes, Laws, and Grand Constitutions of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite*. A compiled and annotated constitutional protocol. (Boyden, p. 71.)
- **1860:** *Ceremonial of a Lodge of Sorrow*. A funerary performance protocol, subsequently adapted in multiple manifestations. (Boyden, pp. 61–62.)
- **1861–1884:** *The Inner Sanctuary*. A multipart ritual system comprising the *Book of the Lodge of Perfection*, *Book of the Second Temple*, Part III, *Book of the Holy House*, and *Book of the Great Light*. Its components appeared in revised editions and translations, so it is better understood as an evolving ritual project than one fixed book. (Boyden, pp. 64–65.)
- **1866:** *The Masonry of Adoption*. A 234-page ritual or ceremonial system; it is a protocol for performance, not descriptive prose. (Boyden, p. 68.)
- **1868:** *Thirty-third Degree Ritual*. A manuscript protocol later labeled by Pike “Ritual of 1868” and “Disused in 1880”; obsolescence is part of its status. (Boyden, p. 39.)
- **about 1870:** *Excerpta Latomica*. A manuscript compilation of extracts on Masonic and philosophical subjects with occasional comments by Pike. (Boyden, p. 37.)
- **about 1870:** *Ceremonies of Constitution, Inauguration, and Installation*. A family of protocols for Lodges of Perfection, Councils of Princes of Jerusalem, Chapters of Rose Croix, Councils of Kadosh, and Grand Consistories; Boyden records both manuscripts and printed manifestations. (Boyden, pp. 36–37, 63–64.)
- **1870s:** *Funeral Ceremony of a Knight Rose Croix*. A manuscript performance protocol. (Boyden, p. 37.)
- **1871:** *Offices of Masonic Baptism, Reception of a Louveteau, and Adoption*. Three illustrated ritual protocols issued together. (Boyden, pp. 63–64.)
- **1872:** *The Porch and the Middle Chamber: The Book of the Lodge*. An illustrated lodge ritual and instructional protocol. (Boyden, p. 64.)
- **date not fixed:** *Esoteric Work of the First through Thirty-second Degrees*. A set of concise ritual protocols divided into degree groups; some manifestations appeared only after Pike’s death, and Boyden supplies no date for the underlying texts except “about 1879” for degrees XXXI–XXXII. (Boyden, p. 65.)
- **1888:** *Legenda of the Lodge of Perfection*. A ritual legend or prescribed interpretive reading. (Boyden, p. 65.)
- **date not fixed:** *Legenda XIX–XXX; Legenda XXXII A and B; Readings XXXII*. Undated instructional and legendary texts for Scottish Rite degrees. (Boyden, pp. 65–66.)
- **1875–1886:** *Offices of Consecration and Dedication*. Ceremonial protocols for halls, temples, and the House of the Temple, issued in several distinct forms. (Boyden, p. 63.)
- **1888:** *Esoterika: The Symbolism of the Blue Degrees of Freemasonry*. A 267-page text within a 400-page luxury manuscript copied for Pike by Edwin B. MacGrotty; Pike made the intellectual work, while MacGrotty made the surviving calligraphic object. (Boyden, p. 40.)

### Masonic philosophy, history, governance, and polemic

- **1851–1860:** *Masonic Addresses and Reports in Arkansas*. A corpus of lodge addresses, cornerstone speeches, grand-lodge and grand-chapter addresses, correspondence reports, and rulings on Masonic law. These are institutional acts and collective records rather than a single book. (Boyden, pp. 41–42.)
- **1860:** *Address on the Actions and Character of John Anthony Quitman*. A Masonic memorial address surviving both as manuscript and printed pamphlet. (Boyden, p. 36.)
- **1866:** *An Examination of a Report of a Masonic Committee*. A book-length intervention in Masonic institutional controversy. (Boyden, pp. 41–42.)
- **1867–1871:** *Vindication of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite against Certain Libels*. A polemical defense first published in the *Memphis Appeal* and then gathered as a pamphlet. (Boyden, p. 71.)
- **1868:** *Balustre on Certain Foreign Relations*. An institutional report by Pike as Sovereign Grand Commander. (Boyden, p. 66.)
- **1871–1872:** *Morals and Dogma of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite of Freemasonry*. A degree-by-degree body of philosophical lectures prepared for the Supreme Council. Its own prefatory account characterizes Pike as both author and compiler, so it should not be represented as wholly original composition; Boyden records several stereotype-based editions as manifestations of the same obra. (Boyden, pp. 62–63.)
- **1872:** *Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite of Freemasonry: Constitutions and Regulations*. A documentary compilation of constitutions, statutes, regulations, and later amendments; a reference protocol rather than an original treatise. (Boyden, p. 66.)
- **1873:** *The Holy Triad*. A polemical reply concerning Masonic religious symbolism, also circulated under the title *Baal and Aun*. (Boyden, p. 67.)
- **about 1875:** *Lecture on Masonic Symbolism*. A privately circulated illustrated lecture, limited to one hundred copies and restricted by Pike from sale. (Boyden, p. 67.)
- **about 1876:** *A Second Lecture on Symbolism: The Omkara and Other Ineffable Words*. An illustrated companion to the first lecture, likewise limited to one hundred copies. (Boyden, p. 67.)
- **1876–unfinished:** *Materials for the History of Freemasonry in France and Elsewhere on the Continent of Europe, 1718–1859*. A six-volume manuscript historical project; only its opening 78 pages appeared during Pike’s life, while a much larger portion was edited serially by George P. Moore after Pike’s death. It is expressly an unfinished project, not a completed authorial book. (Boyden, pp. 38, 68.)
- **1878:** *The Book of the Words*. A privately printed philological-symbolic study limited to 150 copies. (Boyden, p. 66.)
- **1879:** *The Supreme Council, 33°, and the Blue Degrees*. An institutional jurisdictional argument later retitled *The Decisions of the Supreme Council … in Regard to the Right of Supreme Councils to Administer the Blue Degrees*. (Boyden, p. 71.)
- **1881:** *The “Cerneau Supreme Council,” So-Called: A Caution to Master Masons*. The opening pamphlet in Pike’s extended anti-Cerneau polemical campaign. (Boyden, p. 59.)
- **1883:** *An Apology—So-Called*. A short polemical pamphlet in the Cerneau controversy. (Boyden, p. 59.)
- **1883:** *A Historical Inquiry in Regard to the Grand Constitutions of 1786*. A historical argument reusing an introduction first published in 1872. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1884:** *Foulhouzeism and Cerneauism Scourged: Dissection of a Manifesto*. A book-length institutional polemic; a later printing contains the typographical variant “Dissention.” (Boyden, p. 59.)
- **1884:** *Of Cerneauism*. A polemical memoir, normally joined to a separately paginated supplement. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1884:** *A Reply … to the Encyclical Letter “Humanum Genus” of Pope Leo XIII*. A Masonic counter-manifesto appearing in several differently paginated manifestations, sometimes joined to the papal text and later translated into Spanish and Italian. (Boyden, pp. 70–71.)
- **1885:** *A Few More Cerneauisms*. A pamphlet continuing Pike’s institutional polemic. (Boyden, p. 59.)
- **1885:** *History vs. Cerneauism*. A historical-polemical pamphlet. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **about 1885:** *Masonic Origines*. A historical pamphlet issued in multiple versions and a revised 1887 edition. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **about 1885:** *A Slight Contribution to the History of Cerneauism*. A pamphlet signed with Pike’s cross-like pseudonymous mark. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1886:** *Getting Recognized Rapidly: Cold Comfort for Dupes*. A Cerneau polemic. (Boyden, pp. 59–60.)
- **1886:** *The Modern Caliban*. A polemical pamphlet signed with Pike’s customary symbol. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1887:** *Beauties of Cerneauism*. A six-number pamphlet sequence with supplements and appendices; its serial structure makes it one campaign composed of several physical objects. (Boyden, p. 59.)
- **1887:** *Pertinent Questions to Be Asked by the Victims of the Twin Bastards of Cerneauism*. A polemical questionnaire designed to provoke institutional action. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1889:** *Chastisement of a Bearer of False Witness*. A Cerneau polemic with appendix. (Boyden, p. 59.)
- **1889:** *The Ignobility of Cerneauism Exposed*. A late pamphlet in the controversy. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1889:** *Indictment and Proof*. A prosecutorially framed Masonic polemic. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1889:** *Squirmings*. An anonymously issued Cerneau pamphlet attributed by Boyden to Pike. (Boyden, p. 60.)
- **1889:** *A Letter Touching Masonic Symbolism*. A published letter to historian Robert Freke Gould. (Boyden, p. 67.)
- **1889:** *The Regius Manuscript*. A brief commentary framed as a letter and later reprinted in Masonic periodicals. (Boyden, p. 70.)
- **date not fixed:** *The Seven Lights: A Reading*. An undated interpretive Masonic reading printed sometime in the 1880s. (Boyden, p. 71.)
- **1860–1891:** *Ex Corde Locutiones*. A posthumous 1899 compilation of Pike’s obituary notices, memorial addresses, and institutional death notices. Pike made the constituent texts; Joseph C. Taylor made the collected book. (Boyden, pp. 45–49.)
- **1861–1891:** *Official letters, notices, decisions, commissions, appeals, and circulars of the Supreme Council*. Boyden catalogs a large corpus of individually issued institutional acts concerning recognition, jurisdiction, ritual, discipline, relief funds, meetings, publications, and foreign relations. Most lack authorial titles and some are jointly issued; their resistance to “work” status is informative—they are governance performed through print. (Boyden, pp. 49–58.)

### Administrative and archival objects

- **1846:** *Muster Rolls of Captain Albert Pike’s Company*. Two signed military administrative records covering August–October and October–December 1846. They are official records made under Pike’s command, not literary works. (Boyden, p. 34.)
- **1838–1891:** *Letters and Documents, Personal and Official*. A fifteen-volume archive of approximately 4,000 pages assembled after the fact by William L. Boyden. Pike made the documents but not this retrospective archival obra. (Boyden, p. 34.)
- **1861–1878:** *Account as Grand Commander*. A manuscript cash-book and loose accounts; an administrative record whose status as a “work” is doubtful but whose making is documented. (Boyden, p. 36.)
- **1870–1891:** *Subscription List of the Official Bulletins*. A manuscript administrative register covering volumes 1–7; it resists literary classification altogether. (Boyden, p. 39.)
