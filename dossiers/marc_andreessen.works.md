## Works by Marc Lowell Andreessen

### Software and networked systems

- **c. 1982–1984:** *Untitled virtual calculator and Commodore 64 games*. Juvenile programs made for his own schoolwork and family computer; no stable titles, surviving code, or exact dates are documented, so they resist treatment as recoverable works.
- **1992–1993:** *NCSA Mosaic*. A graphical World Wide Web browser co-created with Eric Bina and an NCSA team; the first public Unix alpha appeared on January 23, 1993. “Mosaic” names a collaborative, evolving software system rather than a single fixed text or edition.
- **1994:** *Netscape Navigator*. A commercial Web browser developed collectively at the company Andreessen co-founded; Andreessen directed the technical project but should not be represented as its sole programmer. Its preview appeared as Mosaic Netscape 0.9 before trademark concerns produced the Netscape name.
- **2004–2011:** *Ning*. A platform for creating customized social networks, co-founded with Gina Bianchini. It is simultaneously software, hosted service, company, and user-generated ecosystem—a made framework whose resulting networks were chiefly made by others.

### Institutional projects

- **1994–1999:** *Netscape Communications Corporation*. A company co-founded with Jim Clark, initially as Mosaic Communications Corporation. As an obra it is institutional and collective: Andreessen helped formulate and direct it, but its browsers, servers, standards work, and eventual open-source release cannot be assigned to him alone.
- **1999–2002:** *Loudcloud*. A cloud-computing and managed-hosting company co-founded with Ben Horowitz, Tim Howes, and In Sik Rhee. It was an institutional project rather than a discrete software object.
- **2002–2007:** *Opsware*. Loudcloud’s transformed manifestation as an enterprise data-center automation company; the change of name accompanied the sale of the hosting business, making continuity between the two projects real but incomplete.
- **2009–present:** *Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)*. A venture-capital firm co-founded with Ben Horowitz. It resists classification as Andreessen’s individual “work”: it is a continuing collective institution that produces investments, organizational services, publications, podcasts, and political interventions.

### Technical paper

- **1994; republished 2010:** *NCSA Mosaic: A Global Hypermedia System*. A technical paper co-authored with Eric Bina describing Mosaic’s architecture and uses. Bibliographic records can misleadingly date it to 2010 because *Internet Research* republished the 1994 article in volume 20, issue 4.

### Protocols and practical guides

- **2007:** *The Pmarca Guide to Startups*. A nine-part protocol for founding and operating startups: “Why not to do a startup,” “When the VCs say ‘no’,” “‘But I don’t know any VCs!,’” “The only thing that matters,” “The Moby Dick theory of big companies,” “How much funding is too little? Too much?,” “Why a startup’s initial business plan doesn’t matter that much,” “Hiring, managing, promoting, and firing executives,” and “How to hire a professional CEO.”
- **2007:** *The Truth About Venture Capitalists*. A three-part explanatory and practical sequence. Its repeated title conceals three separate blog installments, so it is best treated as one serialized protocol rather than three independently titled essays.
- **2007:** *How to Hire the Best People You’ve Ever Worked With*. A hiring protocol originally published as a blog essay.
- **2007:** *Serial Entrepreneurs and Today’s Silicon Valley*. An interpretive essay on repeated company formation, included in the curated archive of Andreessen’s early blog.
- **2007:** *The Psychology of Entrepreneurial Misjudgment: Biases 1–6*. The surviving archive presents this as one installment; its numbering implies a larger projected sequence, but the archive does not establish that Andreessen completed it.
- **2007:** *Age and the Entrepreneur: Some Data*. A short evidence-based argument about age and creative or entrepreneurial output.
- **2007:** *Luck and the Entrepreneur: The Four Kinds of Luck*. An entrepreneurial essay that recasts an existing fourfold taxonomy of luck; the taxonomy itself was not Andreessen’s invention.
- **2007:** *Guide to Personal Productivity*. A practical protocol for organizing work.
- **2007:** *Guide to Career Planning*. A four-part protocol comprising an introduction and installments on opportunity, skills and education, and where to work.
- **2007:** *Guide to Big Companies*. A two-part, apparently incomplete guide consisting of “Turnaround!” and “Retaining great people.” Its fragmentary state is part of the work: the archive supplies no later installments.

### Essays, criticism, and blog manifestations

- **2007:** *Why There’s No Such Thing as Web 2.0*. A polemical essay disputing the usefulness of “Web 2.0” as a distinct historical category.
- **2007:** *Top 10 Science Fiction Novelists of the ’00s—So Far*. A ranked critical list; it is a small work of literary recommendation rather than a book or scholarly bibliography.
- **2007:** *Why Ning?*. A programmatic explanation of Ning and of social networks as extensible platforms.
- **2007:** *Book of the Week: Best Book for Tech Entrepreneurs This Year*. A review-and-recommendation post; its generic heading is the title preserved by the curated archive.
- **2007:** *The Three Kinds of Platforms You Meet on the Internet*. A classificatory essay about software platforms.
- **2007:** *Music of the Week: Three Views of the Blues, Through Jazz*. A recommendation post concerning three recordings, not a musical composition by Andreessen.
- **2007:** *Eleven Lessons Learned About Blogging, So Far*. A reflective protocol whose provisional title explicitly leaves the project open.
- **2007:** *OK, You’re Right, It IS a Bubble*. A satirical blog essay about claims of a technology bubble.
- **2007:** *Counterpoint: Ben Horowitz on Micromanagement*. A guest text housed within Andreessen’s blog archive but expressly attributed to Ben Horowitz. It resists inclusion as an Andreessen work and belongs here only as an editorial manifestation he hosted, not as a text he made.
- **2007:** *An Hour and a Half with Barack Obama*. A first-person account of a meeting with Obama, published as a blog essay.
- **2011:** *Why Software Is Eating the World*. A programmatic essay published by *The Wall Street Journal*. Though often treated as a slogan, it is a sustained thesis about software companies transforming existing industries.
- **2020:** *It’s Time to Build*. A manifesto issued during the COVID-19 pandemic, calling for renewed institutional capacity in housing, education, health care, manufacturing, and infrastructure. Andreessen also made an audio reading; that recording is a manifestation of the essay rather than a separate authored text.
- **2023:** *Why AI Will Save the World*. A long polemical essay defending artificial intelligence and opposing catastrophic accounts of it.
- **2023:** *The Techno-Optimist Manifesto*. A manifesto in the strict sense: a declarative text intended to assemble a constituency and authorize technological growth, markets, energy, intelligence, and material abundance.
- **2024:** *The Little Tech Agenda*. A political manifesto co-authored with Ben Horowitz on the regulatory and electoral interests of technology startups; its collective authorship should not be collapsed into Andreessen’s alone.

### Recurring media projects

- **2007–2009:** *Pmarca blog*. A continuing Web publication rather than a fixed book. The surviving Pmarchive is explicitly selective—an archive of the “best articles”—so neither it nor this list can establish the complete contents of the original blog.
- **2023–present:** *The Ben & Marc Show*. A recurring audiovisual conversation with Ben Horowitz about technology, business, culture, and politics. Individual episodes are collaborative performances, and duplicate audio, video, transcript, and reissue pages are manifestations rather than distinct works.
- **2023–present:** *Marc Andreessen’s a16z author stream*. An open-ended mixture of essays, investment announcements, interviews, and podcast appearances. It resists treatment as a coherent individual oeuvre because the index mixes works authored by Andreessen with conversations, corporate communications, reposts, and items bearing several contributors.
