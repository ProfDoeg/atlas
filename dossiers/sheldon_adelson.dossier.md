# Sheldon Adelson: Research Dossier

**Full name:** Sheldon Gary Adelson  
**Lifespan:** 4 August 1933–11 January 2021  
**Primary roles:** Entrepreneur; trade-show organizer; casino and integrated-resort developer; founder, chairman, and chief executive of Las Vegas Sands Corp.; newspaper proprietor; political donor; philanthropist  
**Principal places:** Dorchester/Boston and Newton, Massachusetts; New York City; Las Vegas, Nevada; Macau; Singapore; Israel; Malibu, California  
**Prepared:** 30 August 2026  

## Scope and evidentiary method

This dossier reconstructs Adelson's life from corporate filings, court decisions, government enforcement documents, campaign-finance records, contemporaneous reporting, interviews, institutional histories, and later biographies and obituaries. It distinguishes among:

- **documented fact:** supported by a primary record or convergent reliable accounts;
- **government finding:** a finding in an agency order or negotiated resolution, not necessarily a criminal conviction;
- **court finding or verdict:** a ruling or jury result, with later reversals or settlements noted;
- **allegation:** a claim in a pleading, complaint, political petition, or report that was not adjudicated as true;
- **disputed account:** competing testimony or interpretations remain;
- **myth/reputation:** a story important to Adelson's public image whether or not every detail can be independently proved.

Adelson was never personally indicted or convicted for bribery, money laundering, organized-crime association, political violence, or espionage. Las Vegas Sands and subsidiaries did, however, enter major federal resolutions concerning internal-accounting controls, Foreign Corrupt Practices Act conduct, and anti-money-laundering failures. Those corporate resolutions must not be silently converted into personal criminal convictions; nor should their documented findings be erased because Adelson was not personally charged.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents, ancestry, and siblings

Adelson was the son of **Arthur Adelson**, a Boston taxi driver who also sold advertising, and **Sarah Adelson, née Tonkin**, who operated a knitting shop from the family home. Accounts consistently describe Arthur as a Lithuanian Jewish immigrant or of Lithuanian/Ukrainian Jewish origin. Sarah came from England. Adelson sometimes recounted that his maternal grandfather was a Welsh coal miner. The exact ancestral path behind that remark is not well documented in accessible primary records.

The family lived in a one-room Dorchester tenement. Connie Bruck's 2008 profile reported that Sheldon, his parents, and three siblings slept in the same room. Public genealogical sources identify at least a sister, Gloria Rita Adelson, but strong biographical sources do not provide a complete, consistent sibling list. Claims about all siblings should therefore be treated cautiously.

Adelson recalled anti-Jewish street violence by Irish neighborhood youths and said that he had to fight his way to school. This became part of his explanation for the intensity of his Jewish identification and his insistence on self-reliance.

### First marriage and adopted children

Adelson married **Sandra Shapiro**; sources generally place the family together in Massachusetts in the 1970s but do not establish a precise wedding year. He adopted Sandra's three children:

- **Mitchell Adelson** (died 2005 after long drug dependence; contemporary accounts describe an overdose involving heroin and cocaine);
- **Gary Adelson**;
- **Shelley Faye Adelson**.

The marriage ended in divorce in 1988.

Mitchell and Gary later sued Sheldon Adelson and business associate Charles Forman over a family-stock transaction. They alleged fraud and breach of fiduciary duty, contending that their father had induced them to sell interests on unfair terms before the business dramatically appreciated. A jury and judge found against them. The Massachusetts Appeals Court affirmed in 2004. The trial judge's often-quoted formulation was that Adelson may have lacked “paternal kindliness” but had not committed the alleged fraud. Shelley did not join her brothers' suit.

### Marriage to Miriam Farbstein Ochshorn

Adelson met **Miriam Farbstein Ochshorn**, an Israeli physician, on a blind date in 1989 and married her in 1991. She was born in Mandatory Palestine in 1945 to Polish Jewish refugees, trained in microbiology/genetics and medicine in Israel, and specialized in internal medicine and addiction treatment. She had previously been married to physician Ariel Ochshorn. Her daughters from that marriage were **Yasmin Lukatz** and **Sivan Dumont**; Sivan married Patrick Dumont, later a senior Las Vegas Sands executive.

Sheldon and Miriam had two sons, **Adam** and **Matan**. Public death notices sometimes count four surviving children and sometimes six children/stepchildren; the difference is whether Miriam's two daughters are included. A precise formulation is: four surviving children of Sheldon (Gary, Shelley, Adam, Matan), two stepdaughters (Yasmin and Sivan), and the predeceased adopted son Mitchell.

Miriam became Adelson's closest partner in philanthropy and politics. Their funding and federal political donations were often joint or split into equal checks. Her addiction medicine and Mitchell's death helped make drug treatment—and opposition to marijuana legalization—central family causes.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1933–1950s

Adelson grew up during the Depression and Second World War in working-class Jewish Dorchester. His later authorized narrative cast poverty not as a condition to escape privately but as the source of a permanent entrepreneurial reflex: find a constrained market, acquire access, and sell access at scale.

The best-known childhood story says that at about twelve he borrowed **$200 from an uncle** to purchase the right or license to sell newspapers at valuable Boston street corners. Some retellings say he began at ten; the detailed New Yorker profile and his corporate legend generally say twelve. At sixteen, he established a candy-vending-machine operation, reportedly placing machines at filling stations used by taxi drivers, including his father. Later accounts inflate the second loan to $10,000 and date it to age fifteen; those exact figures are not independently documented. The newspaper and vending episodes are credible in outline but have been polished into a founder myth.

He attended a vocational or trade school to learn court reporting. He then enrolled at **City College of New York**, reportedly studying real estate and finance, but left without a degree. Roughly three and a half years after high school he entered the **U.S. Army**, where court reporting/stenography was reportedly his assignment. He did not become a public military figure, and no evidence reviewed indicates combat service.

## Education and Formation

Adelson had no completed university degree and identified experience, risk tolerance, salesmanship, and pattern recognition—not academic instruction—as his education. His recurring maxims included thinking on a larger scale than competitors, distrusting expert consensus, and treating convention traffic as the economic anchor around which hotels, retail, restaurants, and casinos could be built.

Formative influences discernible from his own accounts and conduct were:

1. **Poverty and immigrant family life:** the origin of his rags-to-riches self-description.
2. **Street sales and vending:** early lessons in controlling locations and distribution.
3. **Court reporting and Army stenography:** exposure to organized information and business/legal speech, though he never entered that profession permanently.
4. **Trade publishing and exhibitions:** the realization that connecting producers with distributors could be more profitable than producing a commodity.
5. **Convention economics:** his central commercial insight that rooms, meetings, retail, and gaming could reinforce one another.
6. **Jewish vulnerability and the Holocaust:** increasingly explicit grounds for Zionism and large gifts to Yad Vashem, Birthright Israel, and pro-Israel organizations.
7. **Labor conflict:** a major factor in his movement away from the Democratic Party and toward Republican institutional power.
8. **Miriam Adelson and Israel:** their marriage intensified his residence, travel, philanthropy, and political involvement in Israel.

## Early Career, 1950s–1978

After the Army, Adelson passed through a large number of ventures. Retellings attribute more than fifty businesses to him, but there is no complete audited list. Documented or repeatedly reported activities include:

- selling toiletry kits to motels;
- **De-Ice-It**, a windshield de-icing spray;
- mortgage brokerage;
- real-estate investment and development;
- financial and investment consulting;
- charter-tour and travel businesses;
- publishing trade magazines;
- organizing trade shows.

He later said that he became and ceased to be a millionaire more than once before his mature success. The precise number of fortunes made and lost—often stated as two—is autobiographical shorthand rather than an independently reconstructed balance sheet.

In the early 1970s he acquired or controlled interests in computer-industry publications and moved into technology exhibitions. His close business partners included **Richard Katzeff**, **Irwin Chafetz**, **Ted Cutler**, and **Jordan Shapiro**, though partnership configurations changed over time. The business that mattered was the **Interface Group**, based in Massachusetts, which combined publishing, travel, and trade-show activities.

## Chronological Life History

### 1979–1988: COMDEX and the economics of convening

In December 1979 Adelson and partners launched **COMDEX**—Computer Dealers Exposition—at the MGM Grand in Las Vegas. The show was designed for computer manufacturers, distributors, and dealers during the personal-computer industry's explosive growth. Rather than inventing a computer technology, Adelson built the market's temporary city: booths, hotel rooms, meetings, travel, publicity, and distribution contacts.

COMDEX became one of the world's largest technology trade shows in the 1980s and 1990s. A period Forbes description said Interface could rent exhibition space at cents per square foot and resell it to exhibitors at tens of dollars per square foot, producing exceptional margins. Exact margin figures vary and should be treated as illustrative, but the basic model is documented.

COMDEX brought Adelson repeatedly to Las Vegas and revealed a bottleneck: during large conventions, hotel owners captured much of the value generated by show organizers. His answer was vertical integration—own the convention center, rooms, restaurants, retail, and casino that served the attendees.

### 1988–1996: purchase of the Sands, convention center, COMDEX sale

In 1988–89 Adelson and partners purchased the aging **Sands Hotel and Casino**, famous for the Rat Pack, for a reported **$110–128 million**. Variations reflect transaction descriptions and associated assets. In 1990 the group opened the **Sands Expo and Convention Center**, described as the largest privately owned convention facility in the United States.

Adelson's emphasis on conventions ran against a Las Vegas orthodoxy centered on gaming. He argued that business travelers would fill rooms during weekdays, spend on food and retail, and still gamble. This convention-led “integrated resort” concept became his main innovation: not the invention of casinos or themed hotels, but their assembly into a scale platform aimed at meetings and mass tourism as well as gaming.

In 1991, on a honeymoon in Venice with Miriam, the couple developed the idea for a Venice-themed resort. Company legend credits Miriam with proposing that Venice's atmosphere be combined with Las Vegas luxury. The story is plausible and repeated by Sands itself, but its highly polished form functions as corporate origin mythology.

In February 1995 the Interface partners agreed to sell COMDEX and other exhibition properties to Japan's **SoftBank** for approximately **$800–862 million**. Most retrospective accounts use $862 million and put Adelson's personal share above $500 million. Contemporary AP reporting initially described an $800 million price. The sale provided capital for casino expansion.

Adelson bought out some partners, closed the old Sands in June 1996, and imploded it on 26 November 1996. Former employees sued over alleged failure to provide the notice required by federal labor law; the closure also intensified his already bitter conflict with Culinary Workers Union Local 226.

### 1997–2001: The Venetian and the labor war

Ground was broken in 1997 for the **Venetian Resort Las Vegas**, a roughly $1.5 billion complex with suites, canals, replicas, convention space, retail, restaurants, and gaming. It opened in May 1999. The project suffered construction disputes and skepticism, but became the prototype for Adelson's resort strategy.

The Venetian opened nonunion. Culinary Local 226 organized a long campaign against Adelson. In February 1999 more than a thousand demonstrators, joined by Representative **John Lewis**, protested on sidewalks adjoining the resort. The Venetian and associated entities argued that portions were private property and pursued litigation; labor and civil-liberties disputes reached federal courts and the U.S. Supreme Court in related proceedings. The property remained nonunion throughout Adelson's life. A first union contract for Venetian and Palazzo workers was not reached until 2024, after the properties had been sold.

Former employee and later Democratic congresswoman **Shelley Berkley** became a durable antagonist. She said Adelson's hostility to unions drove his realignment: because Democrats were allied with labor, he sought to “break the back” of the party as well as the union. Adelson's defenders emphasized his direct employment, benefits, and construction investment. The documentary record supports both the scale of job creation and his sustained opposition to union representation.

In 2001 Adelson was diagnosed with **peripheral neuropathy**, eventually limiting walking and standing. He increasingly used a motorized scooter or wheelchair and a magnifying device for documents, but retained close managerial control.

### 2001–2004: Beijing, Macau concession, and Sands Macao

After Portugal returned Macau to China in 1999, the territory ended Stanley Ho's casino monopoly and invited foreign bidders. Adelson pursued access through multiple intermediaries. One critical chain involved Hong Kong businessman **Richard Suen** and associates, who arranged meetings in Beijing. Adelson met Vice Premier **Qian Qichen** and other officials in 2001. Court testimony later made these meetings central to a contract dispute over who deserved credit and compensation.

During this period Adelson also contacted his political ally, House Majority Whip **Tom DeLay**, about a House resolution critical of China's human-rights record and Beijing's Olympic bid. Former Sands president William Weidner testified that after Adelson's call, DeLay said the measure would not see the light of day. The resolution did not advance. Adelson disputed that his intervention caused its demise, pointing to the press of other legislation. The episode is documented influence-seeking, but a quid pro quo was not judicially established.

In 2002 the Macau government awarded a gaming concession to **Galaxy Casino Company**, with the Adelson group as a partner. The relationship fractured. Macau authorities permitted a **subconcession**, allowing Venetian Macao to operate separately. Competing accounts attribute the split to differences over financing, speed, control, and alleged concerns about Galaxy's proposed associates. No single account resolves every motive.

**Sands Macao** opened on 18 May 2004 at a reported construction cost around $265 million. It was Macau's first major American-operated Las Vegas-style casino and recovered its investment remarkably quickly—often reported as within a year. Adelson recognized that Macau's market was not limited to a single peninsula property: he promoted development of reclaimed land between Coloane and Taipa as the **Cotai Strip**, a trademarked analogy to Las Vegas Boulevard.

### 2004–2007: public company, enormous wealth, Cotai

Las Vegas Sands Corp. was incorporated in its public-company form in August 2004 and completed an initial public offering in December at **$29 per share**. The prospectus shows Adelson receiving 58,625,638 post-merger shares in exchange for Interface Holding interests, valued at roughly $1.70 billion at the offering price. Contemporary reporting put his post-IPO voting/economic stake near 88 percent and estimated that the debut added billions to his net worth.

The **Venetian Macao** opened on 28 August 2007 at a reported cost of about $2.4 billion. It contained thousands of suites, vast gaming and retail space, and convention facilities. The **Palazzo** opened beside the Venetian in Las Vegas in stages in late 2007 and early 2008. Four Seasons Macao and additional Cotai projects followed.

Forbes estimated Adelson at about **$26.5 billion in 2007**, then the third-richest American. The frequently repeated claim that his wealth rose by roughly $1 million per hour after the IPO is a rhetorical conversion of a rapid paper increase, not cash income.

### 2005–2008: personal loss, philanthropy, Israeli press, Freedom's Watch

Mitchell Adelson died in 2005 after years of addiction. Sheldon and Miriam funded addiction-treatment clinics in Tel Aviv and Las Vegas and medical research. Sheldon treated marijuana as a gateway drug and later spent millions opposing medical- and recreational-marijuana initiatives. His position combined personal grief, Miriam's clinical work, and public policy; critics noted the contrast between opposition to cannabis and ownership of gambling businesses.

In 2006 the Adelsons pledged **$25 million to Yad Vashem**. They became the largest private supporters of **Taglit-Birthright Israel**, eventually giving well over $100 million during his life, and funded Jewish education, the Adelson Educational Campus, medical research, the Republican Jewish Coalition, the Zionist Organization of America, the Israeli-American Council, Friends of the Israel Defense Forces, Ariel University, and organizations opposing the boycott-divestment-sanctions movement.

Adelson helped finance a short-lived Israeli free newspaper, **Israeli**, then left that partnership. On 30 July 2007 he launched **Israel Hayom**, a free national daily. Its distribution model rapidly damaged paid competitors and by 2010–11 it led weekday readership measures. It was widely called **“Bibiton”**—a blend of “Bibi” and the Hebrew word for newspaper—because of its favorable line toward Benjamin Netanyahu. Supporters described it as a corrective to a hostile Israeli media establishment; critics described it as a loss-making political instrument. Both descriptions concern editorial function, while precise cumulative losses remain private.

In the United States Adelson was principal financier of **Freedom's Watch**, formed in 2007 to support the Iraq War surge and conservative candidates. Internal strategic disputes and Adelson's controlling style contributed to the group's collapse after the 2008 election.

### 2008–2010: financial crisis and Singapore

The global financial crisis nearly destroyed Las Vegas Sands. By mid-2008 it carried approximately **$8.8 billion in long-term debt**; in November it warned of possible covenant default and bankruptcy risk. Shares that had exceeded $140 in 2007 fell below $2 in 2009. Estimates of Adelson's personal paper loss range from roughly $13 billion to $25 billion depending on endpoints. His remark—“So I lost $25 billion. I started out with zero”—became another founder legend.

Adelson and his family injected about **$1 billion**, including purchases of preferred securities, while the company raised additional capital, suspended projects, and renegotiated financing. The rescue diluted shareholders and left litigation over what management had disclosed, but it kept the company out of bankruptcy. Macau and Singapore cash flow then drove a striking recovery.

Singapore selected Las Vegas Sands in May 2006 to build the Marina Bay integrated resort. The bid's MICE—meetings, incentives, conventions, and exhibitions—strength and Moshe Safdie's design helped overcome skepticism about Sands' lack of a local partner. The original investment proposal was S$3.85 billion plus S$1.2 billion for land; construction inflation took total stated cost toward **S$8 billion**. **Marina Bay Sands** opened in phases beginning 27 April 2010, with three hotel towers, convention facilities, mall, museum, theaters, casino, and rooftop SkyPark. It became one of the company's most profitable assets and a global architectural icon.

### 2010–2014: Jacobs, federal scrutiny, political megadonor status

In July 2010 Sands dismissed **Steven C. Jacobs**, president and chief executive of Sands China. Jacobs sued in Nevada in October, alleging wrongful termination and contending that he had resisted instructions involving surveillance of Macau officials, payments to Macau legislator/lawyer **Leonel Alves**, and relationships with junket operators connected to organized crime. Adelson said Jacobs was fired for incompetence and rejected the claims as fabrications.

The case exposed internal emails, reports, and disputes over whether Sands China was controlled from Las Vegas. Nevada courts held that jurisdiction existed. Adelson testified combatively over several days in 2015. The parties reached a confidential settlement in May 2016; informed reporting put payment above **$75 million**, but the amount was never officially disclosed. Settlement ended the claims without findings that Jacobs' full narrative was true.

Federal investigators separately examined Las Vegas Sands' handling of high rollers and its China/Macau transactions. In 2013 a Venetian operating entity entered a non-prosecution agreement and surrendered **$47.4 million** connected to customer **Zhenli Ye Gon**. The government said the casino failed to appreciate and report suspicious patterns in tens of millions of dollars wired through Mexican exchange houses and unrelated originators. Sands acknowledged in hindsight that it should have filed earlier and more complete suspicious-activity reports. The agreement did not find that Adelson personally laundered money or knew Ye Gon's alleged drug activity before it became public.

The 2010 *Citizens United* environment made Adelson's scale of election spending possible. In 2012 he and Miriam first kept **Newt Gingrich** viable through large gifts to Winning Our Future, then shifted to **Mitt Romney** and congressional super PACs. The Center for Public Integrity calculated **$93.3 million** in Adelson-family super-PAC contributions that cycle, and other totals that include nonprofits and electoral vehicles run higher. Candidates' journey to his Venetian gathering became known as the **“Adelson primary.”** Most of his chosen 2012 presidential spending did not produce victory, but it established his capacity to set agendas and prolong campaigns.

In October 2013, at Yeshiva University, Adelson proposed detonating a U.S. nuclear weapon in an unpopulated Iranian desert, then threatening Tehran unless Iran abandoned its nuclear program. His spokesman called the remarks hyperbole. The words and video are authentic; the later softening concerned whether he intended literal policy.

Adelson also mounted a national campaign against internet gambling. His 2013 Forbes essay, “Online Gambling Is Fool's Gold,” called it a societal train wreck and urged Congress to rewrite or restore the Wire Act. He funded the Coalition to Stop Internet Gambling, and allied legislators introduced the **Restoration of America's Wire Act**. Competitors argued he was protecting land-based casinos; Adelson framed the issue as protection of children, vulnerable gamblers, and payment systems. Sands had itself explored internet gaming earlier, creating an unresolved tension between commercial history and later moral argument.

### 2014–2016: Israeli newspapers, Review-Journal, regulators, Trump

In 2014 Adelson obtained approval to acquire the religious-nationalist weekly **Makor Rishon** and assets associated with *Maariv*. Ownership structures around *Israel Hayom* were later described legally as belonging to a relative, but Adelson financed and controlled the publishing project in practical public understanding; Miriam eventually became publisher.

In December 2015 an initially anonymous company, News + Media Capital Group LLC, paid **$140 million** for Nevada's largest newspaper, the **Las Vegas Review-Journal**. The price was widely considered above market. Reporters at the paper discovered that Patrick Dumont had arranged the acquisition for the Adelson family. The secrecy itself became a national story.

Before and around the sale, reporters had received an unexplained assignment to monitor three judges, including **Elizabeth Gonzalez**, who presided over the Jacobs litigation. A Connecticut paper connected to acquisition manager Michael Schroeder published a hostile article about Gonzalez under a pseudonym. No court found that Adelson ordered an intimidation campaign, but the sequence generated serious conflict-of-interest concerns. Editor Mike Hengel took a buyout; several journalists left; columnist John L. Smith resigned after restrictions on writing about Adelson. The paper later became the first major U.S. newspaper to endorse Donald Trump in 2016. The episode forms a principal segment of the 2017 documentary *Nobody Speak: Trials of the Free Press*.

On 7 April 2016, the **SEC** entered a settled cease-and-desist order against Las Vegas Sands. Without admitting or denying the findings, the company accepted findings that it failed from 2006 through at least 2011 to maintain adequate books, records, and internal accounting controls over China/Macau operations. The order described more than **$62 million** transferred to a consultant for transactions including a Beijing basketball team and a building project, without adequate documentation or controls. Sands paid a **$9 million civil penalty**.

On 19 January 2017, the **Department of Justice** announced a related criminal resolution: Las Vegas Sands paid a **$6.96 million penalty** for FCPA books-and-records/internal-controls violations. The resolutions established corporate compliance failures and improperly recorded payments. They did not charge Adelson personally with bribery, and the public resolutions did not establish that every payment was a bribe to an official.

Adelson did not initially support Trump in the 2016 Republican primary. In May he published a Washington Post endorsement. He and Miriam supplied roughly **$20 million** to the main pro-Trump super PAC and tens of millions more to Republican committees and groups; exact cycle totals vary by definition. They gave a record **$5 million** to Trump's inaugural committee. Access followed: calls, White House visits, and attention to Jerusalem, Iran, Israel, and proposed casino markets.

### 2017–2020: Trump policy, Netanyahu rupture, late political spending

Adelson had long urged recognition of Jerusalem as Israel's capital and movement of the U.S. embassy. Trump announced recognition in December 2017 and the embassy opened in May 2018. Adelson attended and helped finance celebrations. The administration also withdrew from the Iran nuclear agreement, recognized Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights, cut aid to Palestinians, and presented a peace plan favorable to Israeli control over settlements. Adelson advocated several of these positions. Documentary reporting establishes lobbying and access; it does not reduce each presidential decision to a purchased instruction, because Trump, evangelical constituencies, Republican platforms, and other donors also supported them.

The Trump administration's Justice Department issued a 2018/2019 Wire Act interpretation broadening federal restrictions beyond sports betting, a result aligned with Adelson's campaign. Courts later rejected that interpretation. Records litigation sought to clarify industry lobbying. No court found that Adelson illegally bought the opinion.

Adelson's relationship with **Benjamin Netanyahu** became more complicated. Israeli police questioned Sheldon twice in 2017 as a witness—not a suspect—in **Case 2000**, concerning recorded negotiations in which Netanyahu allegedly offered regulatory or legislative help that would weaken *Israel Hayom* in exchange for favorable coverage from rival publisher Arnon “Noni” Mozes. Leaked accounts say Adelson was surprised, angry, and felt betrayed; he reportedly said Netanyahu had not told him of the talks. Phone records reportedly showed very frequent Netanyahu-Adelson contact over prior years, while testimony indicated requests concerning *Israel Hayom* content and editions. Adelson was listed as a potential prosecution witness but died before testifying at trial. The evidence demonstrates patronage and later rupture, not that Adelson was charged in Netanyahu's case.

In 2018 Trump awarded **Miriam**, not Sheldon, the Presidential Medal of Freedom. Confusion on this point is common.

During 2018 the Adelsons gave more than **$100 million** to Republican midterm efforts. In 2020, despite a reported angry phone call in which Trump complained that Adelson had not done enough, they made a late surge: $75 million to Preserve America, followed by additional large checks to pro-Trump and Senate Republican groups. Depending on whether party committees, super PACs, and nonprofit vehicles are included, 2020 family spending is reported from about $170 million to roughly $250 million. Federal records support the broader conclusion that Sheldon and Miriam gave **more than $500 million to Republican campaigns and super PACs from 2010 through 2020**, making them the dominant donor couple of that period.

Adelson also used a private aircraft in December 2020 to bring released American-Israeli spy **Jonathan Pollard** and his wife to Israel. This was public political-symbolic support for Pollard, not evidence that Adelson worked for an intelligence service.

### 2019–2021: illness, pandemic, final months

In February 2019 Las Vegas Sands confirmed that Adelson had **non-Hodgkin lymphoma** and was receiving treatment. The disclosure came after lawyers argued that treatment made him unable to sit for additional deposition in the Richard Suen litigation. The case settled in March 2019 after opening statements in a third damages trial.

COVID-19 devastated Las Vegas Sands revenue in 2020. Adelson retained his titles and controlling interest while Robert Goldstein acted operationally during medical leave. In early January 2021 the company announced another leave for cancer treatment.

Adelson died on 11 January 2021 at his Malibu home. Las Vegas Sands stated that death resulted from complications of treatment for non-Hodgkin lymphoma. His coffin, draped with U.S. and Israeli flags, arrived at Ben Gurion Airport on 14 January. Netanyahu paid respects there. Adelson was buried the next day in a private ceremony on Jerusalem's Mount of Olives.

### Business model and technological contribution

Adelson was not principally an inventor or patent holder. His innovation was organizational:

- using trade shows as high-margin marketplaces;
- combining convention centers with all-suite hotels, gaming, retail, dining, and entertainment;
- treating non-gaming convention demand as an anchor rather than an accessory;
- cloning and enlarging the integrated-resort model in Macau and Singapore;
- using themed architecture and branded “cities within cities” to increase dwell time and capture spending.

No important personal patent portfolio was located. References to Adelson as a “technology entrepreneur” mean that COMDEX served the technology industry, not that he invented computing hardware or software.

### Wealth, ownership, and risk

Adelson's wealth was overwhelmingly concentrated in Las Vegas Sands equity. Key benchmarks:

- 1995: more than $500 million reported personal share of Interface/COMDEX sale;
- 2004: IPO prospectus valued one major block received in the Interface transaction at about $1.70 billion; contemporary coverage estimated total post-IPO holdings far higher;
- 2007: Forbes estimate about $26.5 billion;
- 2008–09: paper fortune fell by more than 90 percent at some endpoints; family injected about $1 billion into Sands;
- 2013: recovery and Asian earnings restored fortune above $30 billion;
- 7 September 2020: Forbes 400 estimate **$29.8 billion**; some year-end/death estimates were around $35 billion.

Different figures measure different dates and asset prices. They are estimates, not audited personal net-worth statements.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### From Democrat to Republican

Adelson was once a Democrat and retained some nonconservative social views, including reported support for abortion rights. He became a Republican power broker in the 1990s. Labor conflict was a documented catalyst; Israel policy, taxation, regulation, and hostility to Democratic foreign policy later became equally important.

He opposed unions, estate taxes, online gambling, marijuana legalization, the Iran nuclear agreement, aid to the Palestinian Authority, and a territorial two-state settlement on terms contemplated by mainstream peace-process diplomacy. He supported lower taxes, aggressive U.S. power against Iran, Israeli retention of settlements, recognition of Jerusalem and the Golan Heights, and Republican control of Congress and the presidency.

### Judaism and Zionism

Adelson's Jewish practice is less documented than his Jewish political identity. He presented support for Israel and Jewish continuity as obligations arising from family memory, antisemitism, and the Holocaust. He financed Yad Vashem, Birthright, Jewish schools, campus advocacy, Israeli-American organization, settlement-linked institutions, and pro-Israel lobbying.

He broke with **AIPAC** over its tolerance of aid to the Palestinian Authority and its two-state orientation, then helped expand the **Israeli-American Council** into a national advocacy organization. He supported the Republican Jewish Coalition and Zionist Organization of America. In 2011 he endorsed Gingrich's statement that Palestinians were an “invented people,” a claim rejected by historians as a denial of modern Palestinian national development even though all national identities have historical formation.

### Political method

Adelson favored transparent personal checks over concealed networks and said he was proud to attach his name to political giving. Yet the broader system also included nonprofits that did not disclose all donors, and the Review-Journal purchase was initially concealed through an LLC. His method combined:

- direct candidate and party contributions within legal limits;
- super PAC checks after *Citizens United*;
- nonprofit advocacy and think-tank funding;
- newspapers;
- personal access, phone calls, dinners, and candidate auditions;
- lobbying on gambling, Israel, Iran, taxes, and regulation.

He insisted that he sought policy, not personal enrichment. Some goals—such as a national internet-gambling ban and international casino licensing—overlapped with business interests. Others, notably Jerusalem and opposition to Iran, were primarily ideological.

The Adelsons' philanthropy was large and strategically concentrated. Documented recipients included:

- **Yad Vashem:** initial $25 million commitment announced in 2006, followed by further support;
- **Taglit-Birthright Israel:** at least $140 million by the mid-2010s and higher lifetime estimates;

The family described philanthropy as Jewish continuity, medical innovation, education, and defense of Israel. Critics argued that some ostensibly charitable funding also built a political ecosystem. Both functions can coexist: tax-exempt giving produced real educational, medical, memorial, and travel programs while also advancing ideological commitments.

### Anti-money-laundering resolution

The 2013 Ye Gon resolution is sometimes described loosely as “Adelson laundering drug money.” The accurate formulation is narrower. Federal prosecutors investigated the casino's failure to identify and report suspicious transactions by Ye Gon, then accused in Mexico of drug trafficking. Money arrived through layered transfers, multiple exchange houses, and third parties; Ye Gon reportedly indicated a desire to avoid government knowledge. The operating company forfeited/surrendered $47.4 million and received a non-prosecution agreement. Sands said it had not known of alleged criminal activity before March 2007 but admitted that compliance should have acted earlier and more completely.

### FCPA and China/Macau consultant

The SEC order described a consultant retained to promote Sands' interests in China. Transactions included attempts to acquire a Beijing basketball team and develop a building. Payments were recorded inadequately and controls were overridden or absent despite red flags. DOJ gave credit for cooperation and remediation but required a criminal penalty. Public materials focus on corporate accounting and controls; they do not authorize the statement “Adelson was convicted of bribery.”

### Junket operators and organized crime

Macau casinos relied heavily on junket promoters who supplied high rollers, extended credit, and collected debts in mainland China. U.S. diplomatic and commission reports described criminal infiltration of this sector. Documents aired in the Jacobs case linked Sands operations to rooms or activities involving figures alleged by authorities to have triad associations, notably **Cheung Chi Tai**. Adelson denied a direct relationship and said expelling all junkets would have destroyed much of the business.

The strongest defensible conclusion is that Sands knowingly operated in an industry structurally dependent on a junket system widely penetrated by organized crime, despite internal warnings. The available record does not prove that Adelson personally joined a triad or entered a criminal conspiracy.

### Richard Suen and competing Macau intermediaries

Suen claimed an agreement for a $5 million success fee plus two percent of Macau profits. A 2008 jury awarded $43.8 million; the Nevada Supreme Court reversed/remanded. A 2013 jury awarded $70 million, later exceeding $100 million with interest; appellate review again required a damages retrial. The case settled in 2019, reportedly for $96 million in later trade reporting, but official terms remained confidential. Other intermediaries also claimed credit, showing how contested the concession-access process was.

### Family-stock litigation

Mitchell and Gary Adelson alleged that their father and Forman cheated them in buying out stock. They lost before the jury and judge, and the appellate court affirmed. This is a documented intra-family rupture, but “Adelson defrauded his sons” contradicts the adjudicated outcome.

### Defamation litigation and aggressive legal practice

Adelson frequently threatened or filed libel actions.

- He won a substantial English libel settlement/judgment against the *Daily Mail* in 2008 over claims of corrupt purchase of political favor; he said proceeds would go to the Royal Marsden Hospital.
- The Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee withdrew and apologized for 2012 posts that associated his donations with “Chinese prostitution money.”
- He sued the National Jewish Democratic Council over a similar petition. The suit was dismissed under Nevada's anti-SLAPP principles after appellate litigation, and in 2018 he was ordered to pay nearly $2 million in fees.
- Former Sands executive Jacobs added a defamation claim after Adelson called his accusations lies and delusions; the Nevada Supreme Court held the statements privileged in the litigation context.
- His failed libel litigation against Las Vegas journalist John L. Smith and restrictions after the Review-Journal acquisition became part of his reputation as an opponent willing to exhaust critics through legal expense.

### Labor and workplace power

Adelson's Las Vegas properties remained the Strip's conspicuous nonunion holdouts. The dispute involved picketing rights, access to sidewalks, employee organizing, litigation, and political warfare. Union sources call him virulently antiunion; Adelson framed compulsory unionization as destructive and emphasized direct worker relations. The eventual 2024 contract, after his death and the 2022 property sale, ended a twenty-five-year campaign.

### Political spending and access

His giving was legal under the post-*Citizens United* system, subject to reporting and vehicle-specific rules. The controversy concerns democratic concentration, not a proved campaign-finance conviction. Candidates appeared at his resort, adjusted schedules to meet him, and competed for support. His policy victories under Trump create a strong inference of influence; inference is not the same as evidence of an illegal sale of official acts.

### Marijuana campaigns

Adelson spent millions against Florida medical-marijuana initiatives and other legalization efforts. He believed cannabis led to harder-drug addiction, interpreting Mitchell's death through that model. The gateway hypothesis remains scientifically contested. His funding was documented; claims that his position was purely casino self-interest ignore the family history, while claims that it was purely medical ignore his political and commercial context.

### EuroVegas and other failed expansions

Adelson pursued giant resort proposals in Spain, Japan, and elsewhere. **EuroVegas**, proposed near Madrid, collapsed in 2013 after Sands sought tax, smoking, labor, and regulatory assurances the Spanish government would not provide. Sands later abandoned Japan after years of lobbying and uncertainty. These failures demonstrate that political access and capital did not guarantee concessions.

## Intelligence Connections, Political Violence, and Security Claims

No documentary evidence reviewed shows Adelson employed by or formally acting for the CIA, Mossad, Shin Bet, or another intelligence service.

Relevant, but often conflated, facts are:

1. A security/intelligence report commissioned in the Macau context recorded Chinese fears that American intelligence could exploit casino records and compromised officials. A fear recorded in a report is not proof that such an operation existed.
2. Macau high rollers included officials vulnerable to corruption or blackmail, creating genuine intelligence risk.
3. Adelson was close to senior U.S. and Israeli politicians, supported Jonathan Pollard, and flew Pollard to Israel after release.
4. He advocated extreme coercive policy toward Iran, including the authentic 2013 nuclear-warning-strike remarks.
5. His charities funded the Friends of the IDF and organizations defending Israeli state policy.

These facts support analysis of political and security influence. They do not establish an intelligence-service relationship or personal participation in political violence.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### 1. The penniless newsboy who built an empire

**Story:** At twelve Adelson borrowed $200, bought a newspaper corner, repeatedly made and lost fortunes, and through fearless scale became one of history's richest self-made men.  
**Appearance/circulation:** Company biographies, speeches, obituaries, authorized tributes, and the forthcoming memoir.  
**Evidence:** Poverty, newspaper selling, vending, serial ventures, COMDEX, and later wealth are well supported. Exact ages, loan sizes, and number of fortunes vary.  
**Reputational effect:** Became the master narrative legitimating wealth as earned vision rather than inherited power.

### 2. Venice conceived on the honeymoon

**Story:** Miriam, walking Venice on their 1991 honeymoon, suggested combining Venice's romance with Las Vegas luxury; the Venetian was born.  
**Appearance:** Sands corporate history and repeated profiles.  
**Evidence:** Consistent couple/company recollection; architectural planning necessarily involved a longer process.  
**Effect:** Gives a romantic marital origin to an industrial-scale themed property.

### 3. “He made a million dollars an hour”

**Story:** After the 2004 IPO Adelson earned $1 million every hour.  
**Evidence:** A conversion of appreciation in publicly traded shares over a selected interval, not wages or realized hourly cash.  
**Effect:** Symbol of pre-crisis billionaire acceleration and Macau's scale.

### 4. The casino visionary who invented the integrated resort

**Story:** Adelson invented the integrated resort.  
**Evidence:** Hotels, conventions, malls, and casinos predated him. He did pioneer a distinctive convention-led, all-suite, mega-scale combination and exported it unusually successfully.  
**Effect:** Corporate history sometimes turns an important business-model innovation into sole invention.

### 5. Netanyahu's owner or patron

**Story:** Adelson “owned” Netanyahu through *Israel Hayom*.  
**Evidence:** He financed a mass free paper highly favorable to Netanyahu, sustained frequent contact, and supported aligned institutions. Case 2000 evidence also shows Netanyahu pursued a rival-media arrangement harmful to Adelson, angering him.  
**Effect:** “Bibiton” captured real structural patronage but oversimplified a relationship capable of conflict and betrayal.

### 6. Adelson bought Jerusalem, the Golan, and the Iran withdrawal

**Story:** Checks to Trump directly purchased specific foreign-policy acts.  
**Evidence:** Huge donations, repeated lobbying, access, and policy alignment are documented. No contract or adjudicated quid pro quo has been produced. Evangelical voters, Republican ideology, other donors, and Trump's own politics also mattered.  
**Effect:** A powerful shorthand for plutocratic influence; too categorical when stated as proved bribery.

### 7. Triad money financed the Republican Party

**Story:** Profits laundered by Macau organized crime became Adelson's American campaign donations.  
**Circulation:** Advocacy complaints and partisan commentary from 2012–15.  
**Evidence:** Macau junket criminal penetration and Sands' dependence on Macau profits are real. The FEC complaint did not trace a particular contribution to a foreign national or company; enforcement counsel noted that evidentiary failure.  
**Effect:** Connected legitimate concerns about global casino finance to a specific campaign-finance claim that was not proved.

### 8. CIA/Mossad casino operation

**Story:** Adelson's casinos harvested secrets for American or Israeli intelligence.  
**Evidence:** Chinese fears of casino-based entrapment appeared in a commissioned report; casinos generate sensitive information. No verified operational evidence connects Adelson to such an intelligence program.  
**Effect:** His transnational wealth, Israeli politics, Pollard support, and opaque high-roller systems made the narrative durable.

### 9. Philanthropist versus political purchaser

Two hostile simplified images competed after his death: saintly benefactor of Jewish survival and medicine; or casino oligarch who converted addiction losses into political power. The factual record contains elements used by both. His gifts funded real treatment, research, schools, memorial work, and travel, while also building ideological and political institutions. Neither one-dimensional image reconstructs the whole system.

### 10. Nathan Adelson Hospice

Las Vegas residents sometimes assume **Nathan Adelson Hospice** was named for Sheldon's relative or funded as a Sheldon Adelson family memorial. Its namesake Nathan Adelson was a Las Vegas hospital administrator and was **not related** to Sheldon Adelson.

## Awards, Honors, and Offices

Adelson held no elected public office. His principal formal office was chairman and chief executive of Las Vegas Sands.

Reported honors include:

- **Woodrow Wilson Award for Corporate Citizenship**, jointly with Miriam, 2008;
- **Nevada Policy Research Institute Chairman's Award**, for free-market advocacy;
- **American Gaming Association Gaming Hall of Fame** induction;
- **Hotel Investment Conference Innovation Award**;
- **Armed Forces Foundation Patriot Award**;
- inclusion in CNBC's 2014 list of business figures who transformed industry;
- honorary degrees and donor recognitions from institutions he supported.

Some honors came from beneficiaries or ideologically aligned institutions and should be understood in that context. Miriam's 2018 Presidential Medal of Freedom is often wrongly attributed to Sheldon.

## Illness, Addiction in the Family, Injuries, and Cause of Death

- **Peripheral neuropathy:** diagnosed in 2001; progressively impaired mobility and standing.
- **Vision/reading limitations:** courtroom appearances showed him using a large magnifying device; a precise diagnosis was not established in reviewed sources.
- **Non-Hodgkin lymphoma:** publicly confirmed February 2019; treatment limited travel and depositions.
- **Medical leave:** renewed in January 2021.
- **Death:** 11 January 2021; Las Vegas Sands attributed it to complications related to lymphoma treatment.
- **Family addiction crisis:** Mitchell Adelson's long heroin/cocaine addiction and 2005 overdose death profoundly affected family medical and drug-policy work. No reliable evidence reviewed shows Sheldon himself suffered substance addiction or a documented psychiatric crisis.

## Death, Succession, and Estate

Robert Goldstein became permanent chairman and chief executive of Las Vegas Sands after Adelson's death; Patrick Dumont rose to president and chief operating officer. The family, led publicly by Miriam, retained a controlling block. The estate's detailed private trust structure is not fully public.

In March 2021 Sands announced sale of the Venetian, Palazzo, and Sands Expo real estate and operations for **$6.25 billion** to VICI Properties and Apollo affiliates; closing occurred in 2022. This removed the company Adelson founded from direct Las Vegas resort operations and concentrated it in Macau and Singapore. It was a posthumous strategic decision, not one he completed in life.

Miriam continued political giving, became publisher of *Israel Hayom*, and with Patrick Dumont's family acquired control of the Dallas Mavericks in 2023. These later acts continue the family network but should not be retroactively attributed to Sheldon.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

Adelson's business legacy rests on four linked achievements:

1. converting the computer trade show into a spectacularly profitable marketplace;
2. making conventions a core resort demand generator;
3. building the modern mega-scale integrated resort in Las Vegas;
4. exporting that model to Macau and Singapore, shifting Las Vegas Sands' center of gravity to Asia.

His political legacy is equally structural. He demonstrated how one household could deploy hundreds of millions through super PACs, nonprofits, newspapers, and direct access. Even losing bets changed primary duration, candidate positioning, and party incentives. “Adelson primary” survives as a term for billionaire audition politics.

In Israel, *Israel Hayom* permanently altered newspaper economics and the balance of media power. It helped normalize a donor-subsidized free daily aligned with a political leader, while Case 2000 revealed the limits and contradictions of that patronage.

His philanthropic legacy includes durable medical research, addiction clinics, Jewish schools, Holocaust remembrance, and Birthright travel on a massive scale. It also institutionalized a hard-line Zionist political network in the United States and Israel.

His regulatory legacy is double: an executive celebrated for scale and long-term investment, and a controlling leader whose corporation paid tens of millions in U.S. resolutions for deficient controls and suspicious-money handling. His confrontational legal and antiunion methods remain central to labor and press-freedom histories.

There is no stable single posthumous image. Casino and beneficiary institutions memorialize a visionary and benefactor; labor, campaign-finance, press-freedom, and Palestinian-rights critics memorialize concentrated private power. The documentary record supports major parts of both descriptions without requiring either as a moral conclusion.
