## Works by Leonhard Euler

### Complete scholarly obra

- **1726–1862:** *The Eneström corpus (E1–E866)*. The standard bibliography recognizes 866 separately numbered books, treatises, memoirs, journal articles, prefaces, notices, and selected published letters; this is Euler’s principal textual obra, not a single book. Publication dates extend long after his death because the St Petersburg Academy continued issuing manuscripts he had left ready or nearly ready for print; individual composition dates are often earlier and sometimes conjectural.

### Books, treatises, and independently issued projects

- **1727 (E2):** *Dissertatio physica de sono*. Euler’s Basel dissertation on the physics of sound, issued as a short independent treatise.

- **1736 (E15–E16):** *Mechanica, sive motus scientia analytice exposita*. A two-volume analytical-mechanics treatise: one intellectual project manifested as two separately catalogued volumes.

- **1738 (E17):** *Einleitung in die Arithmetik / Руководство к арифметике*. An elementary arithmetic textbook produced for the St Petersburg Academy and published in German and Russian; the parallel-language manifestations should not be mistaken for two independently conceived works.

- **1739 (E33):** *Tentamen novae theoriae musicae ex certissimis harmoniae principiis dilucide expositae*. A mathematical theory of music, neither musical composition nor performance manual but an attempted mathematization of harmony.

- **1744 (E65):** *Methodus inveniendi lineas curvas maximi minimive proprietate gaudentes*. A foundational treatise on variational problems, accompanied by appendices that develop what became the Euler–Lagrange theory and the mechanics of elastic curves.

- **1744 (E66):** *Theoria motuum planetarum et cometarum*. An astronomical treatise and computational method for deriving planetary and cometary orbits from observations.

- **1744 (E67–E68):** *Beantwortung verschiedener Fragen über die Beschaffenheit, Bewegung und Würckung der Cometen* and its continuation. Two linked German pamphlets answering questions about the nature, motion, and effects of comets.

- **1745 (E77):** *Neue Grundsätze der Artillerie*. Euler’s German translation and extensive analytical reworking of Benjamin Robins’s *New Principles of Gunnery*; authorship is therefore composite, and the object is simultaneously translation, commentary, and new ballistics treatise rather than an wholly original book.

- **1746–1751 (E80–E81, E156):** *Opuscula varii argumenti*. A three-volume miscellany of Euler’s papers on mechanics, astronomy, physics, and mathematics. Its long publication interval and separately issued volumes make it a serial collected-work project rather than a unitary monograph.

- **1746 (E87):** *Novae et correctae tabulae ad loca lunae computanda*. Astronomical tables: a computational instrument in printed form, not merely a discursive text.

- **1747 (E92):** *Rettung der Göttlichen Offenbahrung gegen die Einwürfe der Freygeister*. An apologetic theological pamphlet defending divine revelation against freethinking objections.

- **1748 (E101–E102):** *Introductio in analysin infinitorum*. A two-volume mathematical treatise that reorganizes analysis around functions, infinite series, exponentials, logarithms, and analytic geometry.

- **1749 (E110–E111):** *Scientia navalis, seu tractatus de construendis ac dirigendis navibus*. A two-volume work of naval architecture and ship dynamics, combining mathematical theory with design and navigation.

- **1750 (E121):** *Conjectura physica circa propagationem soni ac luminis, una cum aliis dissertationibus analyticis*. A separately issued gathering of four dissertations. It resists treatment as one new argument because it is a composite manifestation containing pieces that the Eneström catalogue also distinguishes individually.

- **1752 (E176):** *Exposé concernant l’examen de la lettre de M. de Leibnitz alléguée par M. le professeur Koenig*. A polemical documentary pamphlet in the least-action controversy; it acts institutionally in a priority dispute and consequently approaches the function of a manifesto.

- **1753 (E186):** *Dissertation sur le principe de la moindre action, avec l’examen des objections de M. le Prof. Koenig*. A polemical and mathematical defense of the principle of least action.

- **1753 (E187):** *Theoria motus lunae exhibens omnes eius inaequalitates*. A systematic lunar-theory treatise intended to turn gravitational analysis into usable prediction.

- **1755 (E212):** *Institutiones calculi differentialis cum eius usu in analysi finitorum ac doctrina serierum*. A comprehensive differential-calculus textbook and research synthesis.

- **1755 (E156):** *Opusculorum tomus III, continens novam theoriam magnetis*. The third volume of the *Opuscula* project, prominently incorporating Euler’s prize-winning theory of magnetism; its delayed appearance makes the displayed date one of publication rather than inception.

- **1762 (E266):** *Constructio lentium obiectivarum ex duplici vitro*. An optical design treatise specifying compound objectives intended to reduce spherical and chromatic aberration; it functions partly as a protocol for making instruments.

- **1765 (E289):** *Theoria motus corporum solidorum seu rigidorum*. A treatise on rigid-body dynamics.

- **1768–1770 (E342, E366, E385):** *Institutionum calculi integralis*, volumes I–III. A three-volume integral-calculus project published during Euler’s lifetime, covering integration techniques, differential equations, and related analysis.

- **1768–1772 (E343, E344, E417):** *Lettres à une princesse d’Allemagne sur divers sujets de physique et de philosophie*. Three published volumes formed from instructional letters to Friederike Charlotte of Brandenburg-Schwedt. The work is at once correspondence, popular-science exposition, and philosophical pedagogy; later translations and rearrangements are manifestations, not additional Euler works.

- **1769–1771 (E367, E386, E404):** *Dioptrica*. A three-part theoretical and practical optics project treating the principles and construction of telescopes and microscopes; portions operate as instrument-making protocols.

- **1770 (E387–E388):** *Vollständige Anleitung zur Algebra*. A two-part algebra textbook, originally dictated in German and prepared with the assistance of Euler’s amanuensis because of his blindness. The mediation complicates the physical act of writing but not the conventional attribution of the mathematical work.

- **1770 (E389):** *Recherches et calculs sur la vraie orbite elliptique de la comète de l’an 1769 et son temps périodique*. A separately issued computational investigation of the 1769 comet’s orbit.

- **1772 (E418):** *Theoria motuum lunae, nova methodo pertractata*. Euler’s second major lunar theory, not a mere new edition of E187 but a substantially new analytical project.

- **1772:** *Novae tabulae lunares*. Printed lunar tables derived from Euler’s revised theory: a calculational tool whose “text” consists substantially of organized numerical data.

- **1773 (E426):** *Théorie complète de la construction et de la manœuvre des vaisseaux*. A French naval-architecture and ship-handling treatise related to, but not simply identical with, the earlier Latin *Scientia navalis*.

- **No secure publication date:** *Éclaircissemens sur les établissemens en faveur tant des veuves que des morts*. An actuarial explanation concerning funds for widows and the deceased; standard older bibliographies report it without a fixed date, which should not be silently supplied.

- **1783–1785:** *Opuscula analytica*. A two-volume collection of analytical papers, begun in print at the end of Euler’s life and completed posthumously. It is a collected manifestation containing separately identifiable works rather than a single newly composed monograph.

- **1794:** *Institutionum calculi integralis volumen quartum*. A posthumous fourth volume assembling supplements, some previously unpublished and some already printed elsewhere. Its status is editorially composite and it cannot straightforwardly be treated as a fourth lifetime-authored volume matching the first three.

### Academic memoirs, articles, notices, and published letters

- **1726–1783, with posthumous publication through 1862 (within E1–E866):** *Hundreds of academy memoirs, journal articles, prize essays, reviews, prefaces, notices, and selected letters*. These are individually named and numbered in the Eneström index; treating them as an anonymous mass would obscure their identity, while reproducing all 866 records here would duplicate the catalogue rather than describe Euler’s kinds of making. The corpus includes mathematics, mechanics, astronomy, optics, acoustics, hydrodynamics, naval science, cartography, actuarial science, philosophy, and theology.

- **Published chiefly after 1783 (within E1–E866):** *Posthumous memoirs from Euler’s manuscript reserve*. These are works made by Euler but manifested as publications by later academy editors; dates printed as late as 1862 are publication dates, not claims that Euler composed anything after his death.

- **Various dates (within E1–E866):** *Published correspondence*. Eneström counted only some letters as independent works—usually where a letter had already acted as a scientific publication—so the E-numbered “letters” are not a complete correspondence and should not be confused with it.

### Correspondence as an unfinished editorial obra

- **1720s–1783:** *Euler’s surviving correspondence*. Thousands of letters to and from Euler form a collaborative epistolary archive rather than a finite Euler-authored book. The *Opera Omnia*, Series IVA, edits this material by correspondent; its completeness remains contingent because originals and copies are dispersed, some letters are lost, and the correspondence edition remains an editorial project rather than a closed work.

- **1729–1764:** *Correspondence with Christian Goldbach*. A sustained collaborative mathematical exchange later edited as a discrete collection. The modern edition is an editorial manifestation; the made objects attributable to Euler are his individual outgoing letters, not Goldbach’s replies or the later bound book as a whole.

- **1740s–1760s:** *Letters to the future Princess of Anhalt-Dessau*. The manuscript correspondence is the pedagogical project from which *Lettres à une princesse d’Allemagne* was selected and reshaped; manuscript letters and printed chapters therefore overlap without being identical objects.

### Manuscripts, notebooks, and unfinished projects

- **Eighteenth century; many dates uncertain:** *Unpublished scientific manuscripts and handwritten papers*. Eneström issued a separate inventory of such material, and the *Opera Omnia*, Series IVB, treats manuscripts and notebooks omitted from the 866-item publication index. Survival, attribution, dating, and completeness vary; this is precisely where “work” resists a clean boundary between finished treatise, draft, calculation, and private note.

- **Circa 1746–1748:** *Catalogue of Euler’s private library*. A handwritten notebook inventory rather than a scholarly publication. It is a documentary text Euler made, but its entries describe books owned by him and must not be mistaken for works authored by him.

- **Undated or incompletely dated:** *Adversaria and mathematical notebooks*. Working collections of calculations, excerpts, drafts, and fragments. They resist being called autonomous works because their internal units and intended endpoints are often uncertain; modern editors have published selected fragments under supplementary E* designations rather than retroactively forcing them into Eneström’s E1–E866 sequence.

### Cartographic Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1753; revised edition 1760 (E205 for the preface):** *Atlas geographicus omnes orbis terrarum regiones in XLI tabulis exhibens*. A school atlas combining cartographic design, mathematical projection, engraved maps, typography, and multilingual prefatory explanation. Euler supervised the Berlin Academy’s geographical work and wrote the preface, but the plates involved other cartographers and engravers; the atlas is therefore collaborative and approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than being solely an Euler-authored “book.”

- **1753–1760:** *The atlas map plates*. Forty-five maps are associated with the atlas manifestation described by the Euler Archive. Their individual graphic execution was collaborative—among those involved were Johann Christoph Rhode and engravers—so attribution to Euler should be stated as direction, mathematical/cartographic participation, or editorial responsibility rather than solitary engraving.

### Conceptual creations that are not discrete texts

- **1730s–1770s:** *Euler’s mathematical notation and terminology*. Euler introduced or normalized such devices as \(f(x)\), the letter \(e\) for the base of natural logarithms, \(i\) for the imaginary unit, and much modern trigonometric notation. These are creations dispersed across texts and scholarly practice, not separately bounded works; the now-standard use of \(\pi\) was popularized rather than invented by him.

- **1736:** *The Königsberg-bridges abstraction*. Euler’s reduction of a geographical route problem to incidences between land regions and bridges created a mathematical object now read as an origin of graph theory. The abstraction survives through its paper rather than as an independently titled artefact.

- **1730s–1760s:** *The calculus of variations and the Euler–Lagrange framework*. A research project distributed across memoirs, correspondence, and the *Methodus inveniendi*, not one isolable text or theorem made at a single moment; Lagrange’s later participation is built into the modern compound name.

- **1750s–1770s:** *Rigid-body equations, fluid equations, and analytical models*. These conceptual constructions occur throughout Euler’s mechanics and physics writings. Their modern names are retrospective manifestations of a body of work, not titles Euler gave to independent creations.

- University of the Pacific, Euler Archive, [*All Works by Source Publication*](https://scholarlycommons.pacific.edu/euler-publications/), used to identify the academy series, journals, miscellanies, and posthumous collections in which Euler’s works appeared.

- Bernoulli–Euler Center, University of Basel, [“Archiv und Datenbanken”](https://bez.unibas.ch/de/projekte/archiv-und-datenbanken/), for the correspondence finding aid, work catalogues, portrait register, and Euler’s handwritten private-library catalogue.

- University of the Pacific, Euler Archive, [“Atlas Geographicus, Praefatio” (E205)](https://scholarlycommons.pacific.edu/euler-works/205/), for the atlas preface, editions, multilingual form, and forty-five-map manifestation.
