## Works by Sergei Pavlovich Diaghilev

### Editorial obras, publications, and manifestos

- **1896–1904:** *Art criticism, open letters, reviews, and interviews*. Diaghilev’s dispersed critical corpus began in 1896 and was later gathered—without becoming a single author-designed “book”—in the scholarly collection *Sergei Diaghilev and Russian Art* and its expanded French edition, *L’Art, la musique et la danse*. It is therefore best treated as a body of work rather than one fixed text. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/); [Royaumont bibliography](https://expositions.bibliotheques-royaumont.com/en/ballets-russes/credits/))
- **1898–1904:** *Mir iskusstva* (*The World of Art*). An illustrated art journal founded with Alexandre Benois and Léon Bakst and shaped by Diaghilev as editor, publisher, critic, and visual organizer; collective authorship makes it an editorial obra rather than a book “by” him alone. ([Morgan Library](https://www.themorgan.org/sites/default/files/pdf/exhibitions/BalletsRussesLargePrintLabels_0.pdf); [Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/mu003011/mu003011.pdf))
- **1899:** *Slozhnye voprosy* (*Difficult Questions*). A multipart aesthetic manifesto published across nos. 1–2 and 3–4 of *Mir iskusstva*; its subsections are sometimes catalogued as separate essays, but scholarship also treats them as parts of one programmatic text. ([East–West Cultural Passage bibliography](https://www.eb-j.org/pdfViewer/articles/MzMxNA%3D%3D); [New York Review of Books](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2011/01/13/great-adventure-sergei-diaghilev/))
- **1899–1900:** *Ezhegodnik Imperatorskikh teatrov* (*Yearbook of the Imperial Theatres*), season 1899–1900 and three supplements. An official serial radically redesigned and edited by Diaghilev, combining documentary record, criticism, photography, and graphic design; it is an editorial Gesamtkunstwerk, not a solely authored text. ([study of the yearbook](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348060451_Effect_of_Yearbook_of_the_Imperial_Theatres_to_Culture_of_the_Silver_Age_Society))
- **1904:** *Dmitrii Grigor’evich Levitskii, 1735–1822*. The first—and only completed—volume of Diaghilev’s projected history of eighteenth-century Russian painting; simultaneously a monograph, catalogue, and fragment of an unrealized larger project. ([Tretyakov Gallery Magazine](https://www.tretyakovgallerymagazine.com/img/mag/2009/3/022-033.pdf))

### Exhibitions and curatorial manifestations

- **1897:** *Exhibition of English and German Watercolours*. Diaghilev’s first documented exhibition, mounted in Saint Petersburg’s Stieglitz Museum; the made thing was a temporary arrangement of other artists’ works, so it survives chiefly as a curatorial event and record. ([Encyclopædia Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/serge-de-diaghilev/2-un-agitateur-d-images-1897-1905/))
- **1898:** *Exhibition of Russian and Finnish Artists*. A Saint Petersburg exhibition organized by Diaghilev and retrospectively understood as the first public manifestation of the *Mir iskusstva* circle. ([State Russian Museum catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb42295085c); [Royaumont](https://expositions.bibliotheques-royaumont.com/en/ballets-russes/before-the-ballets/))
- **1899:** *International Exhibition of Painting*. A second international exhibition at the Stieglitz Museum; documentation varies in its naming and boundaries, so the event resists a wholly stable work-title. ([Encyclopædia Universalis](https://www.universalis.fr/encyclopedie/serge-de-diaghilev/2-un-agitateur-d-images-1897-1905/))
- **1899–1903:** *Mir iskusstva exhibitions*. Three collective exhibitions organized around the journal’s artistic circle. They constitute a recurring curatorial project rather than three securely delimited authorial works by Diaghilev alone. ([Royaumont](https://expositions.bibliotheques-royaumont.com/en/ballets-russes/before-the-ballets/))
- **1905:** *Historical Exhibition of Russian Portraits*. A vast exhibition at the Tauride Palace, Saint Petersburg, assembled through Diaghilev’s research and travel and documented in a catalogue of roughly 2,300 objects; exhibition, archive, and historiographic project coincide. ([State Russian Museum catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb42295085c); [Canadian-American Slavic Studies](https://brill.com/downloadpdf/journals/css/51/2-3/article-p376_10.pdf))
- **1906:** *Exhibition of Russian Art*, Salon d’Automne, Paris. Diaghilev’s large historical survey of Russian art, subsequently shown in modified form elsewhere in Europe; its changing contents make it a travelling curatorial manifestation rather than an immutable object. ([State Russian Museum catalogue record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb42295085c))

### Concert, company, and unrealized projects

- **1900–1901:** *Sylvia*. A projected Imperial-Theatres staging of Delibes’s ballet, planned with Alexandre Benois but cancelled amid Diaghilev’s conflict with the administration. It is a project rather than a completed production. ([Serge Diaghilev biography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Diaghilev))
- **1907:** *Concerts historiques russes*. Five Paris concerts conceived and organized by Diaghilev to present Russian music historically; their obra was the programmed series, not the compositions performed. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1908–1929:** *Saisons Russes*. The continuing enterprise through which Diaghilev presented Russian opera, concerts, and ballet abroad; it is an institutional and itinerant obra whose individual manifestations appear below. ([Russian Theatre Museum](https://collectiononline.gctm.ru/en/entity/THEATRE/13651))
- **1909–1929:** *Ballets Russes de Serge Diaghilev*. The company founded and artistically directed by Diaghilev: a twenty-year collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk joining choreography, music, painting, costume, typography, performance, and touring. The individual scores, choreographies, and designs belong to their named makers; Diaghilev’s authorship lies in commissioning, selection, combination, and production. ([Library of Congress overview](https://www.loc.gov/static/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/about-this-collection/overview.html))
- **1914–1915:** *Ala and Lolli*. A ballet score commissioned from Serge Prokofiev and rejected by Diaghilev before production; Prokofiev reused it in the *Scythian Suite*. It belongs here only as an unrealized Diaghilev project, not as a Ballets Russes manifestation. ([IRCAM](https://resources.ircam.fr/en/composer/serge-prokofiev/workcourse))

### Operatic and choreographic Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1908:** *Boris Godunov*. Diaghilev’s Paris production of Mussorgsky’s opera, using Rimsky-Korsakov’s revision, with Feodor Chaliapin and a large collaborative design team; revived by him in 1913. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/b/))
- **1909:** *Le Pavillon d’Armide*. Ballet production with music by Nikolai Tcherepnin, choreography by Michel Fokine, and designs by Alexandre Benois. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1909:** *Danses polovtsiennes* (*Polovtsian Dances from Prince Igor*). A staged extraction from Borodin’s opera, transformed into an autonomous Ballets Russes spectacle. ([Library of Congress collection overview](https://www.loc.gov/static/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/about-this-collection/overview.html))
- **1909:** *Le Festin*. A divertissement assembled from music by several Russian composers; its composite construction makes it a production rather than a newly composed ballet. ([1926 *Encyclopædia Britannica* repertory](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1926_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Ballet))
- **1909:** *Les Sylphides*. Fokine’s non-narrative ballet to orchestrated Chopin, produced by Diaghilev from the earlier *Chopiniana*. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1909:** *Cléopâtre*. Ballet production derived from Fokine’s earlier *Une Nuit d’Égypte* and substantially reconceived with Léon Bakst’s designs; Diaghilev restaged it in 1918 with Robert and Sonia Delaunay and a new Massine duet. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/c/))
- **1910:** *Le Carnaval*. Fokine’s ballet to Schumann, presented by Diaghilev after an earlier Saint Petersburg performance; thus a Ballets Russes production, not an entirely new choreography. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1910:** *L’Oiseau de feu* (*The Firebird*). Ballet commissioned from Igor Stravinsky, with Fokine’s scenario and choreography and designs by Aleksandr Golovin and Léon Bakst; Diaghilev commissioned a new Natalia Goncharova staging in 1926. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/f/))
- **1910:** *Schéhérazade*. Choreographic drama constructed from Rimsky-Korsakov’s score, with Fokine’s choreography and Bakst’s designs. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/s/))
- **1910:** *Les Orientales*. A composite divertissement of “Oriental” dances to music by several composers; it resists the unity implied by “ballet” because it was an assembled suite. ([1926 *Encyclopædia Britannica* repertory](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1926_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Ballet))
- **1910:** *Giselle*. Diaghilev’s production of the pre-existing Romantic ballet, with sets and costumes by Alexandre Benois; the obra is the staging, not Adam’s score or the inherited choreography. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1911:** *Le Spectre de la rose*. Fokine’s one-act ballet to Weber, with designs by Bakst, made famous by Tamara Karsavina and Vaslav Nijinsky. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1911:** *Narcisse*. Ballet with music by Tcherepnin, designs by Bakst, and choreography by Fokine. ([1926 *Encyclopædia Britannica* repertory](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1926_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Ballet))
- **1911:** *Pétrouchka*. Ballet commissioned from Stravinsky and made collaboratively with Alexandre Benois and Fokine. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1911:** *Sadko*. A staged extraction from Rimsky-Korsakov’s opera, presented as a choreographic tableau; it is not a separately composed ballet. ([1926 *Encyclopædia Britannica* repertory](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1926_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Ballet))
- **1911:** *Swan Lake*. A condensed two-act Ballets Russes staging derived from the 1895 Petipa–Ivanov version rather than a new full production. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/s/))
- **1912:** *Le Dieu bleu*. Ballet with music by Reynaldo Hahn, a scenario by Jean Cocteau and Federico de Madrazo, choreography by Fokine, and designs by Bakst. ([1926 *Encyclopædia Britannica* repertory](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1926_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Ballet))
- **1912:** *Thamar*. Ballet to Balakirev, choreographed by Fokine and designed by Bakst. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1912:** *Daphnis et Chloé*. Ballet commissioned from Maurice Ravel, choreographed by Fokine and designed by Bakst. ([Serge Lifar Collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/2006568220/))
- **1912:** *L’Après-midi d’un faune*. Nijinsky’s first choreography, made to Debussy’s pre-existing score with designs by Bakst; its flattened movement and concluding gesture made it a manifesto in performance. ([Library of Congress exhibition](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/ballets-russes/overview.html))
- **1913:** *Jeux*. Ballet commissioned from Debussy and choreographed by Nijinsky, with designs by Bakst. ([Serge Grigoriev Archive](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/15/resources/995))
- **1913:** *Le Sacre du printemps*. Ballet commissioned from Stravinsky, choreographed by Nijinsky and designed by Nicholas Roerich; the notorious premiere is only one manifestation of a collaboratively made work. ([Serge Grigoriev Archive](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/15/resources/995))
- **1913:** *La Tragédie de Salomé*. Ballet to Florent Schmitt, choreographed by Boris Romanov and designed by Serge Sudeikin. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1913:** *Khovanshchina*. Diaghilev’s production of Mussorgsky’s opera, incorporating orchestration by Rimsky-Korsakov, Ravel, and Stravinsky. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/k/))
- **1914:** *Papillons*. Ballet to Schumann, orchestrated by Tcherepnin and choreographed by Fokine; it had first been performed at the Mariinsky in 1912. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/p/))
- **1914:** *La Légende de Joseph* (*Josephslegende*). Ballet commissioned from Richard Strauss, choreographed by Fokine and designed by José-María Sert. ([Serge Lifar Collection](https://www.loc.gov/item/2006568220/))
- **1914:** *Le Coq d’or*. An opera-ballet production of Rimsky-Korsakov’s opera, with singers placed offstage while Fokine’s dancers embodied the action; its hybrid form deliberately resists separation into opera and ballet. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1914:** *Midas*. Ballet to Maximilian Steinberg, choreographed by Fokine and designed by Mstislav Dobuzhinsky. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/m/))
- **1914:** *Le Rossignol*. Diaghilev’s production of Stravinsky’s opera, staged by Alexandre Benois and Alexander Sanin; the later ballet *Le Chant du rossignol* is a distinct transformation. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1914:** *May Night*. Diaghilev’s London production of Rimsky-Korsakov’s opera, with choreography by Fokine. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/m/))
- **1915:** *Le Soleil de nuit* (*Midnight Sun*). Ballet assembled from Rimsky-Korsakov, with choreography by Léonide Massine and designs by Mikhail Larionov; retitled in 1918. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1916:** *Las Meninas*. Ballet to Gabriel Fauré, conceived and choreographed by Massine with designs by Carlo Socrate and Sert; Library of Congress records vary between *Menias* and the historically usual *Meninas*. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1916:** *Kikimora*. One-act ballet to Anatoly Liadov, choreographed by Massine and designed by Larionov; absorbed into *Contes russes* in 1917. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/k/))
- **1916:** *Till Eulenspiegel*. Ballet to Richard Strauss, choreographed and designed by Vaslav Nijinsky for the American tour; contractual circumstances complicate whether it should be called a fully authorized Diaghilev creation, though it entered the company’s production history. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1917:** *Feu d’artifice*. A production of Stravinsky’s symphonic poem as Giacomo Balla’s abstract set-and-light spectacle; explicitly not a ballet, but perhaps Diaghilev’s clearest nonhuman theatrical manifestation. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/f/))
- **1917:** *Contes russes*. A suite of dances by Massine and Larionov incorporating the earlier *Kikimora* and subsequently expanded in London as *Children’s Tales*. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/c/))
- **1917:** *Les Femmes de bonne humeur*. Ballet to Domenico Scarlatti arranged by Vincenzo Tommasini, choreographed by Massine and designed by Bakst. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1917:** *Parade*. Ballet by Satie, Cocteau, Picasso, and Massine; an exemplary Gesamtkunstwerk whose noise-making costumes and collision of popular entertainment with ballet also gave it the force of a theatrical manifesto. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/p/))
- **1919:** *La Boutique fantasque*. Ballet constructed from Rossini music orchestrated by Ottorino Respighi, with choreography by Massine and designs by André Derain. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1919:** *Le Tricorne*. Ballet commissioned from Manuel de Falla, with choreography by Massine and designs by Picasso. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1920:** *Le Astuzie femminili*. Diaghilev’s production of Cimarosa’s opera-ballet, reconstructed and orchestrated by Respighi with choreography by Massine; its final act was later extracted as *Cimarosiana*. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/c/))
- **1920:** *Le Chant du rossignol*. A ballet made from Stravinsky’s symphonic adaptation of *Le Rossignol*, choreographed by Massine and designed by Henri Matisse; George Balanchine supplied a new staging in 1925. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1920:** *Pulcinella*. Ballet made by Stravinsky, Massine, and Picasso from music then attributed largely to Pergolesi; later research reassigned several source pieces, so its musical attribution is historically unstable. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/p/))
- **1921:** *Chout* (*The Buffoon*). Six-scene ballet commissioned from Prokofiev, with designs and partial choreographic responsibility attributed to Larionov and choreography credited by the Library of Congress to Tadeusz Sławiński and Larionov. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/c/))
- **1921:** *Cuadro flamenco*. A suite of traditional Andalusian dance and music arranged by Falla and designed by Picasso, performed by Spanish dancers rather than the Ballets Russes company; it resists classification as a Diaghilev “ballet.” ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/c/))
- **1921:** *The Sleeping Princess*. A full-length production of Tchaikovsky and Petipa’s *The Sleeping Beauty*, newly designed by Bakst; it was an ambitious restaging of an inherited ballet, not a new composition, and its cost nearly ruined the company. ([Serge Grigoriev Archive](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/15/resources/995))
- **1922:** *Aurora’s Wedding*. A one-act condensation of *The Sleeping Princess* made for touring; its identity depends on adaptation rather than autonomous composition. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1922:** *Le Renard*. Ballet with songs by Stravinsky, designed by Larionov and choreographed by Bronislava Nijinska; Serge Lifar supplied a new choreography in 1929. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1922:** *Mavra*. One-act opera commissioned from Stravinsky, with libretto by Boris Kochno and designs by Léopold Survage. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1923:** *Danses russes*. A suite rather than a continuously plotted ballet; its documentary boundaries and constituent dances are less stable than those of the major repertory works. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1923:** *Les Noces*. Stravinsky’s dance cantata, long in development and ultimately choreographed by Nijinska with designs by Natalia Goncharova; voices, percussion, dance, and ritual make “ballet” an incomplete category. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *Les Tentations de la bergère, ou L’Amour vainqueur*. Ballet assembled from eighteenth-century French music, with scenario by Kochno and choreography by Nijinska. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *Les Biches*. Ballet commissioned from Francis Poulenc, choreographed by Nijinska and designed by Marie Laurencin; its deliberately slight narrative makes it closer to a social and choreographic tableau. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *Cimarosiana*. A divertissement extracted from *Le Astuzie femminili*, with added Nijinska choreography; catalogue traditions consequently treat it either as a new work or a revision. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/c/))
- **1924:** *Les Fâcheux*. Ballet to Georges Auric, with libretto by Kochno, choreography by Nijinska, and designs by Georges Braque. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *La Colombe*. Diaghilev’s production of Gounod’s opera, with new recitatives by Poulenc and designs by Juan Gris; it was a revision of an 1860 work. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1924:** *Le Médecin malgré lui*. Diaghilev’s production of Gounod’s pre-existing comic opera, one of a cluster of short operas mounted at Monte Carlo. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *Philémon et Baucis*. Diaghilev’s Monte Carlo production of Gounod’s opera; the made thing attributable to him is the staging and programmed conjunction, not the score. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *Une Éducation manquée*. Diaghilev’s production of Chabrier’s operetta, mounted as part of the Monte Carlo opera project. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *La Nuit sur le mont Chauve*. Ballet fashioned from Mussorgsky’s music, choreographed by Nijinska and designed by Goncharova; it should not be confused with the orchestral composition alone. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1924:** *Le Train bleu*. Ballet by Darius Milhaud, Cocteau, Nijinska, Henri Laurens, and Coco Chanel, with a Picasso image used for the curtain; its modern-sport imagery makes it a collaborative theatrical assemblage. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1925:** *Le Festin*. A newly constituted suite of dances bearing the same title as the 1909 divertissement; repertory chronologies distinguish the manifestations imperfectly. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1925:** *Les Contes de fées*. A suite extracted from act III of *The Sleeping Princess*; it is an adaptation and touring unit rather than a newly composed ballet. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1925:** *L’Assemblée*. A composite suite of dances with music by several composers and choreography by Balanchine and others; its heterogeneous contents resist singular authorship. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1925:** *Le Bal du “Lac des cygnes”*. A suite extracted from *Swan Lake*, made as an independent repertory unit. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1925:** *Zéphire et Flore*. Ballet commissioned from Vladimir Dukelsky, with libretto by Kochno, designs by Braque, and choreography by Massine. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/z/))
- **1925:** *Les Matelots*. Ballet to Auric, with libretto by Kochno, designs by Pedro Pruna, and choreography by Massine. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1925:** *Barabau*. Ballet with chorus, music and libretto by Vittorio Rieti, designs by Maurice Utrillo, and choreography by Balanchine. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/b/))
- **1926:** *Romeo and Juliet*. Ballet to Constant Lambert, with libretto by Kochno, choreography by Nijinska, and designs by Max Ernst and Joan Miró; its rehearsal-room framing made the act of theatrical construction part of the spectacle. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1926:** *La Pastorale*. Ballet to Auric, with libretto by Kochno, designs by Pruna, and choreography by Balanchine. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1926:** *Jack-in-the-Box*. Ballet to Erik Satie, orchestrated by Milhaud, choreographed by Balanchine, and designed by André Derain. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1926:** *The Triumph of Neptune*. English pantomime-ballet to Lord Berners, choreographed by Balanchine and designed by Pruna; its genre is intentionally theatrical rather than purely balletic. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1927:** *La Chatte*. Ballet to Henri Sauguet, with Kochno’s Aesopic libretto, constructivist designs by Naum Gabo and Antoine Pevsner, and choreography by Balanchine. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/l/))
- **1927:** *Mercure*. Diaghilev’s new production of Satie, Massine, and Picasso’s 1924 “poses plastiques”; because it originated with Étienne de Beaumont’s *Soirées de Paris*, it is a Diaghilev staging rather than a Diaghilev commission. ([Library of Congress catalogue](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/works-listing-by-title/m/))
- **1927:** *Le Pas d’acier*. Constructivist ballet commissioned from Prokofiev, with scenario and designs by Georgii Yakulov and choreography by Massine; Diaghilev helped determine its final title. ([IRCAM](https://resources.ircam.fr/en/composer/serge-prokofiev/workcourse))
- **1927:** *Oedipus Rex*. Stravinsky and Cocteau’s opera-oratorio, produced by Diaghilev with staging by Serge Grigoriev; its deliberately static form resists ordinary operatic classification. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1928:** *Ode*. Ballet to Nicolas Nabokov, with libretto by Kochno, designs by Pavel Tchelitchev, choreography by Massine, and complex projected lighting; the unstable technical realization made light itself one of the work’s media. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1928:** *Apollon musagète*. Ballet commissioned from Stravinsky and choreographed by Balanchine; Diaghilev’s Paris production followed an earlier American premiere, so “premiere” depends on whether the score, choreography, or Diaghilev manifestation is meant. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1928:** *The Gods Go A-Begging*. Ballet arranged from Handel by Thomas Beecham, choreographed by Balanchine and designed by Léon Bakst. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
- **1929:** *Le Bal*. Ballet commissioned from Rieti, with libretto by Kochno, designs by Giorgio de Chirico, and choreography by Balanchine. ([Serge Grigoriev Archive repertory](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/mu010001/mu010001.pdf))
- **1929:** *Le Fils prodigue* (*The Prodigal Son*). Diaghilev’s final new ballet: music by Prokofiev, libretto by Kochno, designs by Georges Rouault, and choreography by Balanchine. ([Library of Congress chronology](https://www.loc.gov/collections/ballets-russes-de-serge-diaghilev/articles-and-essays/timeline-of-ballets-russes/))
