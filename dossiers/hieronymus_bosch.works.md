## Works by Jheronimus van Aken

### Painted obras: surviving panels and ensembles accepted by the BRCP

- **c. 1470–1480:** *The Adoration of the Magi* (New York). An oil-on-panel painting, probably once the central portion of a larger ensemble; accepted as Bosch by the Bosch Research and Conservation Project (BRCP cat. 10).
- **c. 1475–1485:** *Ecce Homo* (Frankfurt). An oil-on-oak devotional painting whose donor figures were subsequently overpainted; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 11).
- **c. 1485–1495:** *Saint Jerome at Prayer*. An oil-on-panel image of the penitent saint, now in Ghent; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 1).
- **c. 1490–1495:** *Saint John the Baptist in the Wilderness*. An oil-on-panel wing probably associated with the Confraternity of Our Lady’s altarpiece; accepted as Bosch, although its original architectural setting is reconstructed rather than preserved (BRCP cat. 5).
- **c. 1490–1495:** *Saint John on Patmos / Scenes from the Passion*. A double-sided altarpiece wing, probably paired with *Saint John the Baptist* in the Confraternity of Our Lady ensemble; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 6).
- **c. 1490–1500:** *Saint Christopher Carrying the Christ Child*. A large devotional panel in Rotterdam; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 7).
- **c. 1490–1500:** *The Adoration of the Magi* (Madrid). A triptych whose closed shutters show the Mass of Saint Gregory; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 9).
- **c. 1490–1500:** *Christ Crowned with Thorns* (London). An oil-on-oak devotional panel; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 14).
- **c. 1490–1500:** *Calvary with a Donor*. An oil-on-panel memorial or devotional image in Brussels; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 15).
- **c. 1490–1510:** *Christ Carrying the Cross / Christ Child*. A double-sided, fragmentary panel in Vienna. The BRCP assigns its execution jointly to Bosch and his workshop, so it resists treatment as an entirely autograph obra (BRCP cat. 12).
- **c. 1495–1505:** *Triptych of the Hermit Saints*. A three-panel altarpiece depicting Saints Jerome, Anthony, and Giles, now in Venice; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 2).
- **c. 1495–1505:** *Triptych of Saint Wilgefortis*. A three-panel altarpiece in Venice, formerly identified with Saint Julia; accepted as Bosch, but the identity of its crucified female saint remains debated (BRCP cat. 8).
- **c. 1495–1505:** *Christ Carrying the Cross* (El Escorial). A large oil-on-panel Passion image; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 13).
- **c. 1495–1505:** *The Last Judgment* (Bruges). A triptych, now lacking or altered in parts, whose function may have been private moral contemplation rather than a conventional church altarpiece; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 16).
- **c. 1495–1505:** *The Garden of Earthly Delights*. A monumental triptych with a grisaille image of the created world on its closed shutters. Its subject does not yield a single uncontested interpretation, making the ensemble a pictorial argument—or visual Gesamtkunstwerk—rather than merely an illustration (BRCP cat. 21).
- **c. 1498–1503:** *The Temptation of Saint Anthony*. A large Lisbon triptych with painted exterior shutters; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 4).
- **c. 1500–1505:** *The Last Judgment* (Vienna). A triptych executed by Bosch with workshop participation; its blank heraldic shields and unfinished or altered passages make its commission and completion uncertain (BRCP cat. 17).
- **c. 1500–1510:** *The Temptation of Saint Anthony* (Kansas City fragment). A small surviving fragment of a larger composition; accepted as Bosch, but the complete obra to which it belonged is lost (BRCP cat. 3).
- **c. 1500–1510:** *The Wayfarer Triptych*. A dispersed and physically dismantled ensemble reconstructed from *The Wayfarer* (Rotterdam), *The Ship of Fools* (Paris), *Allegory of Gluttony and Lust* (New Haven), and *Death and the Miser* (Washington). The BRCP treats these pieces as one original triptych, although its modern title, arrangement, and overall program are reconstructions (BRCP cat. 19).
- **c. 1505–1515:** *Visions of the Hereafter*. Four painted panels—*Terrestrial Paradise*, *Ascent of the Blessed*, *Fall of the Damned*, and *Hell*—now in Venice. They survive as fragments of an unidentified larger ensemble and therefore resist classification as four independent works (BRCP cat. 18).
- **1510–1516:** *The Haywain Triptych* (Madrid). A triptych with *The Wayfarer* on its closed shutters; accepted as Bosch, while a second version at El Escorial is generally assigned to workshop or followers (BRCP cat. 20).

### Drawn obras: sheets accepted wholly or partly as Bosch’s work

The BRCP deliberately gives these drawings no dates; dates are therefore not manufactured here. Recto and verso are treated as one physical obra where they occupy the same sheet.

- *The Tree-Man*. A pen-and-ink drawing with grey wash in Vienna; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 35).
- *The Owl’s Nest*. A pen-and-brown-ink nature study in Rotterdam; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 36).
- *The Field Has Eyes, the Forest Has Ears / Study of a Beggar and Workshop Sketches*. A two-sided sheet in Berlin. The principal recto is Bosch’s, while the BRCP detects both Bosch and workshop hands on the verso (BRCP cat. 37).
- *Two Monsters / Two Monsters*. A two-sided pen-and-ink sheet in Berlin; both sides are accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 38).
- *Model Sheet with “Witches.”* A pen-and-ink sheet with black chalk in the Louvre; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 39).
- *Woman at a Spinning Wheel and Old Woman / Fox and Rooster*. A two-sided miniature study sheet in Rotterdam; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 40).
- *The Birds and the Mammals Declare War*. A pen-and-ink drawing in Berlin; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 41).
- *Battle of the Birds and Mammals*. A separate pen-and-ink treatment of the same fable-like conflict, also in Berlin; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 42).
- *The Temptation of Saint Anthony / Singers in an Egg and Two Monster Sketches*. A two-sided Berlin sheet. The recto is assigned to the workshop, while Bosch’s participation is accepted on the verso; it is therefore a collaborative workshop object rather than an uncomplicated autograph drawing (BRCP cat. 43).
- *Infernal Landscape*. A pen drawing in brown and red ink, privately owned; formerly assigned to an assistant but reattributed to Bosch by the BRCP (BRCP cat. 44).
- *Infernal Ship*. A brush-and-ink drawing in Vienna; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 45).
- *Man in a Basket, Old Woman with Tongs, and Children / Sketch of a Man in a Basket and a Head on Legs*. A two-sided Vienna sheet; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 46).
- *The Conjurer / Burlesque Concert and Miscellaneous Studies*. A two-sided Louvre sheet: the BRCP accepts the *Conjurer* recto as Bosch but assigns the reverse to a follower, so the physical object contains incompatible attributions (BRCP cat. 47).
- *Christ Carrying the Cross and Beggars / The Conjurer*. A two-sided sheet in Liège. The BRCP rejects the recto as follower work but accepts the verso as Bosch, making the sheet only partly his obra (BRCP cat. 48).
- *Ten Spectators*. A small pen-and-ink group study in the Morgan Library; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 49).
- *Two Men / The Temptation of Eve*. A privately owned two-sided pen drawing; both sides are accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 50).
- *Two Monsters / Head on Legs and a Monster*. A small two-sided Berlin sheet; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 51).
- *Studies of Saint Anthony / Sketch Sheet with Monsters*. A two-sided Louvre sheet; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 52).
- *Infernal Scene with an Anvil and Monsters / Sketch Sheet with Monsters*. A two-sided Berlin sheet; accepted as Bosch (BRCP cat. 53).
- *The Entombment*. A highly finished drawing in pen, brush, grey and black ink, and black chalk in the British Museum; accepted by the BRCP as Bosch, although some earlier and later cataloguers have questioned the attribution (BRCP cat. 54).
- *Two Oriental Men*. A pen-and-brush drawing heightened with white in Berlin; catalogued by the BRCP as “Bosch?” rather than securely autograph, so its resistance to attribution is part of its status (BRCP cat. 55).

### Documented projects and lost manifestations

- **c. 1489:** *Lower Wings of the Confraternity of Our Lady Altarpiece*. Painted shutters for Adriaen van Wesel’s carved Marian altarpiece at Saint John’s Church in ’s-Hertogenbosch, reportedly showing Abigail before David and Solomon honoring Bathsheba. The paintings are lost; their subjects and Bosch’s responsibility survive through confraternity documentation and early descriptions.
- **1491–1492:** *Design for a Stained-Glass Window for the Confraternity of Our Lady*. A documented design associated with glazing for the confraternity chapel. The design itself does not survive, so this is a recorded project rather than an extant object.
- **1504:** *The Last Judgment commissioned by Philip the Handsome*. A “large painting” commissioned from “Jeronimus van Aeken, called Bosch,” for which an advance payment is documented. Its survival or completion is unknown; neither the Vienna triptych nor the Munich fragment can securely be identified with it, so the commission remains a project with no agreed surviving manifestation.
- **1511–1512 and 1514–1515:** *Embroidery Designs for the Confraternity of Our Lady’s Blue Vestments*. Designs supplied by Bosch and his nephew Anthonis van Aken for matching liturgical vestments. The records establish collaborative design work, but neither the drawings nor the embroidered manifestations are securely preserved.
- **Before 1516; recorded in 1545–1546:** *Painted Doors of the Confraternity of Our Lady Altarpiece*. The confraternity accounts explicitly remembered the doors as the work of “Master Jeronimus.” The surviving *Saint John the Baptist* and *Saint John on Patmos* are very probably upper wings from this changing, multi-stage ensemble, while its lower narrative wings are lost; the “work” therefore survives only incompletely and across separate objects.

### Contested obras retained by museums but rejected or downgraded by the BRCP

- **c. 1495–1520:** *The Adoration of the Magi* (Philadelphia). The Philadelphia Museum has historically attributed this panel to Bosch, but the BRCP assigns it to his workshop; it cannot securely be called something Van Aken personally made (BRCP cat. 25).
- **1501–1505 according to the Prado; c. 1510–1520 according to the BRCP:** *The Extraction of the Stone of Madness*. The Prado maintains an attribution to Bosch, while the BRCP assigns it to workshop or follower. Its authorship and dating therefore remain explicitly disputed (BRCP cat. 33).
- **c. 1510–1516 according to the MSK Ghent; c. 1530–1540 according to the BRCP:** *Christ Carrying the Cross* (Ghent). The owning museum retains Bosch’s name but acknowledges the dispute; the BRCP regards it as follower work executed after his death (BRCP cat. 29).
- **c. 1510–1520:** *The Seven Deadly Sins and the Four Last Things*. The Prado attributes this painted tabletop to Bosch, whereas the BRCP assigns it to workshop or follower. It is simultaneously picture, moralizing diagram, and inscribed furniture-like object, and its authorship is unresolved (BRCP cat. 34).
- **c. 1525–1550 according to the BRCP:** *The Haywain Triptych* (El Escorial version). Long attributed to Bosch and sometimes dated within his lifetime, this second version is excluded from the autograph corpus by the BRCP as a workshop or later production; it should not silently be merged with the accepted Madrid triptych.
- **c. 1530–1540 according to the BRCP:** *The Temptation of Saint Anthony* (Madrid panel). Formerly treated as Bosch and still associated with his name in older literature, but assigned by the BRCP to a follower working after his death (BRCP cat. 31).
