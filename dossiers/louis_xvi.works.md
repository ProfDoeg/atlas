## Works by Louis XVI

### Juvenile compositions and exercises

- **c. 1766:** *Réflexions sur mes entretiens avec M. le duc de La Vauguyon*. A manuscript sequence of educational and moral reflections written while Louis-Auguste was dauphin; it is a school exercise shaped by his governor’s curriculum, but recent scholarship treats it as evidence of the pupil’s own coherent ethical thought.

- **1766:** *Maximes morales et politiques tirées de Télémaque*. A compilation and adaptation of political maxims from Fénelon, composed as a princely exercise and then typeset and printed by the twelve-year-old dauphin in twenty-five copies. It is therefore both text and handmade typographical object, although its ideas and wording remain deliberately derivative of *Télémaque*.

### Journals, accounts, and records

- **1766–1792:** *Journal de Louis XVI*. An autograph documentary assemblage rather than a reflective diary: terse daily records of journeys, ceremonies, reviews, diversions, and other activities. The Archives nationales manuscripts occupy the first of three portfolios conventionally called the king’s “Journal”; published editions are partial.

- **1771–1791:** *Cahiers de chasse*. Registers of hunts, quarry, locations, and results, now gathered in the third “Journal” portfolio. These are operational records rather than narratives, and some associated tables and lists were compiled by household personnel, so the portfolio should not be treated as uniformly autograph.

- **1772–1791:** *Comptes et dépenses particulières*. Household account books recording payments from the king’s private purse. They belong to Louis XVI’s documentary practice but resist simple authorship: he entered or supervised parts of them while clerks produced others.

- **1776–1792:** *Pensions et gratifications sur la cassette*. Registers of private pensions and gifts, preserved with the accounts. They are administrative instruments made under the king’s authority, not a continuously authored literary work.

- **1769–1790:** *Listes de chevaux*. Working lists preserved among the hunting papers. Their inclusion in Louis XVI’s papers establishes their practical use, but not that every entry is in his hand.

### Correspondence

- **1774–1787:** *Correspondence with the comte de Vergennes*. Nearly two hundred letters from Louis XVI to his foreign minister survive and have been critically edited by John Hardman and Munro Price. They form a political working correspondence—responses to dispatches and instruments of decision—not a book conceived as a unit; the editors note that many earlier printed “letters” attributed to the king were of doubtful authenticity.

- **1789–1792:** *Revolutionary and secret correspondence*. Letters and memoranda to ministers, foreign sovereigns, agents, Mirabeau, Breteuil, and members of the royal family survive in dispersed archives and in the papers seized at the Tuileries. This is a body of work rather than one fixed text, and signatures, secretarial drafting, copies, and collaborative composition make item-by-item authorship uneven; no complete critical edition equivalent to the Vergennes correspondence exists.

### Protocol and expedition project

- **1785:** *Mémoire du Roi pour servir d’instruction particulière au sieur de La Pérouse*. A protocol governing the proposed circumnavigation: itinerary, scientific observations, diplomacy, trade, and conduct toward Indigenous peoples. Louis XVI personally shaped and annotated the project, but it is a composite state document: naval officials and savants supplied preparatory memoranda, and Charles-Pierre Claret de Fleurieu is credited with drafting at least the geographical portion, so sole authorship should not be asserted.

### Political manifestations and manifestos

- **October 1789:** *Protestation secrète contre les actes arrachés au roi depuis le 15 juillet 1789*. A juridical manifestation intended to invalidate acts performed under coercion after the royal family’s removal to Paris. Most modern historians attribute it personally to Louis XVI, but Paul and Pierrette Girault de Coursac assigned it to Marie-Antoinette and her circle; the apparent autograph once reported in the Spanish archives is now missing.

- **20 June 1791:** *Déclaration du roi adressée à tous les Français, à sa sortie de Paris*. A manifesto left during the flight to Montmédy, retrospectively declaring measures accepted since October 1789 void because the king lacked liberty and setting out his political case against the revolutionary settlement. The surviving manuscript supports Louis XVI’s personal authorship, although preparation of such a public act also involved his political circle.

### Prison writings and translation

- **1792–1793:** *Le Règne de Richard III, ou Doutes historiques sur les crimes qui lui sont imputés*. An unfinished prison translation of Horace Walpole’s *Historic Doubts on the Life and Reign of Richard the Third*. Louis XVI’s heavily revised manuscript survives, but the French book published in 1800 was extensively completed and reshaped by the editor-translator Louis-Charles Roussel; that printed text is consequently a composite creation rather than simply “a book by Louis XVI.”

- **25 December 1792:** *Testament de Louis XVI*. An autograph spiritual and familial testament written in the Temple prison. It is a testament in both the legal-adjacent and devotional senses, not the “political testament” sometimes used as a name for the 1791 declaration.

- **20 January 1793:** *Dernière lettre de Louis XVI à Marie-Antoinette*. A farewell letter written after sentence and before execution. It is an intimate prison document, not a public manifesto; later facsimiles and devotional editions should be distinguished from the autograph text.

### Royal acts and spoken texts whose authorship resists classification

- **1774–1792:** *Acts issued in the name of Louis XVI*. Thousands of edicts, declarations, ordinances, letters patent, proclamations, sanctioned decrees, and diplomatic instruments bear the royal name. They are manifestations of kingship and acts of government—often performative texts in the strongest sense—but were normally drafted by ministers, councils, secretaries, or legislative bodies and authenticated by formula and signature; the Bibliothèque nationale’s catalogue of royal acts documents the corpus but does not make Louis XVI their literary author.

- **1787–1792:** *Royal speeches to the Assemblies of Notables, Estates-General, and revolutionary legislatures*. These speeches acted politically when delivered by the king, but their texts were commonly prepared or revised by ministers. They belong to Louis XVI’s performed voice and constitutional office; without manuscript evidence for a particular speech, they resist classification as his personal compositions.

### Printing and metalwork

- **March 1766:** *Twenty-five copies of Maximes morales et politiques tirées de Télémaque*. These physical impressions were reportedly typeset and printed by Louis-Auguste under the direction of the royal printer Augustin-Martin Lottin. Each copy is thus a material obra as well as an edition of the juvenile text.

- **No securely catalogued title or date:** *Locksmithing and mechanical pieces*. Contemporary testimony and later biographies describe Louis XVI working at a forge and lathe, sometimes with the locksmith François Gamain, but no scholarly catalogue raisonné securely identifies a surviving lock, key, tool, or clock as made by the king’s own hands. “Louis XVI’s locks” therefore resist treatment as nameable works: the practice is documented more securely than any particular product.
