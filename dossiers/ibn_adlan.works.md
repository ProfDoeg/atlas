## Works by Afif al-Din Ali ibn Adlan al-Mawsili

### Cryptanalytic protocols

- **1229–1237:** *Al-Muʾallaf li-l-malik al-Ashraf* (المؤلف للملك الأشرف; also transmitted as *Fī ḥall al-mutarjam* or *Ḥall al-mutarjam*). A practical protocol commissioned by or dedicated to the Ayyubid ruler al-Ashraf Mūsā during his rule in Damascus: its preface, twenty rules, and worked conclusion instruct a cryptanalyst how to break substitution ciphers, including texts without word-dividers. It survives in Süleymaniye MS 5359; the alternative titles appear to designate this same text, not additional works. ([Mrayati, Meer Alam, and al-Tayyan, pp. 13–18, 28–31](https://pdfcoffee.com/arabic-origins-of-cryptology-vol-2-pdf-free.html); [Christensen, “From Text to Technological Context”](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01611194.2014.885801))
- **Before 1229–1237:** *Al-Muʿlam* (المُعْلَم). A more extensive cryptanalytic protocol, now lost, whose tables treated ciphers having more or fewer signs than the letters they represented. Its existence is not merely a later bibliographer’s attribution: Ibn ʿAdlān directs readers to it in rule twenty of *Al-Muʾallaf*, which establishes that it preceded the surviving manual but supplies no exact date. ([Mrayati, Meer Alam, and al-Tayyan, pp. 13, 18 and 110](https://pdfcoffee.com/arabic-origins-of-cryptology-vol-2-pdf-free.html))

### Philological obra

- **Undated; before 1268:** *Al-Intikhāb li-kashf al-abyāt al-mushkilat al-iʿrāb* (الانتخاب لكشف الأبيات المشكلة الإعراب). A compact grammatical commentary explaining the syntax of 165 difficult or deliberately riddling Arabic verses; it is partly an abridgment and critical reworking of earlier collections by Ibn al-Mufajjaʿ and al-Fāriqī rather than a freestanding theoretical grammar. The work survives uniquely, so far as the Cambridge hand-list records, in Cambridge University Library MS Or. 996(8), copied on 16 Rajab 720/3 August 1320; modern references sometimes corrupt *al-abyāt* (“verses”) into *al-āyāt* (“verses/signs”). ([Cambridge, *Second Supplementary Hand-list*, item 4(a)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/second-supplementary-handlist-of-the-muhammadan-manuscripts-in-the-university-and-colleges-of-cambridge/second-supplementary-handlist-of-the-muhammadan-manuscripts-in-the-university-colleges-of-cambridge/C30777D26AC80275B624A2B877E5E1A5); [Ḥātim Ṣāliḥ al-Ḍāmin’s edition record](https://search.mandumah.com/Record/235866))

### Riddle collection

- **Undated; before 1268:** *ʿUqlat al-mujtāz fī ḥall al-alghāz* (عقلة المجتاز في حل الألغاز). A collection or handbook for solving literary and linguistic riddles, known from bio-bibliographical notices but not represented by a presently identified text; its survival and completeness therefore cannot be assessed. The title itself is unstable in the sources: *ʿUqlat al-majāz* and, less commonly, *ʿUqlat al-muḥtāz* also occur, so these should not be multiplied into separate works. ([al-Baghdādī, *Īḍāḥ al-maknūn*, II, p. 112](https://almntqim.com/%D9%A3%D9%A3%D9%A6%D9%A1_%D8%A5%D9%8A%D8%B6%D8%A7%D8%AD-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%83%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A5%D8%B3%D9%85%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%8A%D9%84-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B4%D8%A7-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A8%D8%BA%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A-%D8%AC-%D9%A2/%D9%A1%D9%A1%D9%A2_%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%81%D8%AD%D8%A9); [Mrayati, Meer Alam, and al-Tayyan, p. 13](https://pdfcoffee.com/arabic-origins-of-cryptology-vol-2-pdf-free.html))

### Poetry and occasional manifestations

- **Undated; thirteenth century:** *Scattered poems and verse riddles*. This is not a securely bounded *dīwān* or a single obra: biographical compilations preserve isolated occasional poems, epigrams, complaints, and riddling exchanges attributed to Ibn ʿAdlān, including a verse-riddle composed in reply to Abū al-Ḥusayn al-Jazzār. No authoritative collected-works edition establishes their complete number, and later statements that his “poetry collection” is lost should not be converted into a securely attested titled book. ([biographical notice preserving the al-Jazzār exchange](https://www.taraajem.com/persons/98607/%D8%B9%D9%81%D9%8A%D9%81-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%86-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%AD%D9%88%D9%8A); [study noting that the poetry is scattered in the sources](https://www.legal-science.com/index.php/SoL/article/download/485/231))

### Projects attested only as intentions

- **Undated; before 1268:** *A comprehensive book on syntactically difficult early Arabic poetry* (no recorded title). At the close of *Al-Intikhāb*, Ibn ʿAdlān announces an intended larger treatment of difficult pre-Islamic, mukhaḍram, and early Islamic verse. This is a project rather than an extant text: no source consulted establishes that he completed it, and it must not be silently identified with the shorter *Al-Intikhāb*. ([text and colophon of *Al-Intikhāb*](https://al-maktaba.org/book/31874/27472))
- **1229–1237 or later:** *A projected special study of steganography* (no recorded title). Near the end of *Al-Muʾallaf*, Ibn ʿAdlān indicates an intention to devote a separate treatment to concealed writing. It resists classification as a made treatise because the surviving evidence records only the project; neither a completed text nor a title is known. ([Mrayati, Meer Alam, and al-Tayyan, pp. 18–19](https://pdfcoffee.com/arabic-origins-of-cryptology-vol-2-pdf-free.html))
