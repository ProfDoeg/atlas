## Works by Bailey Whitfield Diffie

### Cryptographic concepts and protocols

- **1975–1976:** *Public-key cryptography*. A conceptual obra devised publicly with Martin E. Hellman: a general framework for communicating securely without a previously shared secret and for creating digital signatures. Attribution is not exclusive—Ralph Merkle independently developed public-key distribution, while James Ellis, Clifford Cocks, and Malcolm Williamson had produced related classified GCHQ work earlier; Diffie and Hellman were unaware of it. [Hellman’s historical account](https://www-ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/77.pdf)

- **1975–1976:** *Digital signatures*. A cryptographic concept proposed by Diffie and Hellman as part of their public-key project, not initially a complete implementation. Hellman describes it as absent even from the earlier classified GCHQ work. [Hellman’s historical account](https://www-ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/77.pdf)

- **1976:** *Diffie–Hellman key exchange*. A protocol for deriving a shared secret over a public channel, created by Diffie and Hellman and published in “New Directions in Cryptography.” Its name and priority resist simplification: Hellman has advocated “Diffie–Hellman–Merkle,” and Diffie has also acknowledged Merkle and the earlier secret work of Malcolm Williamson. [Diffie’s ACM oral history](https://amturing.acm.org/pdf/DiffieTuringTranscript.pdf) [Hellman’s historical account](https://www-ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/77.pdf)

### Books and editorial obras

- **1979:** *The New UCI LISP Manual*. A collaboratively authored software manual with James R. Meehan and numerous other contributors: a protocol-like text that instructs users in a programming system rather than a conventional monograph. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1998; expanded edition 2006:** *Privacy on the Line: The Politics of Wiretapping and Encryption*. A coauthored book with Susan Landau joining technical history to an argument about surveillance, privacy, and public policy; later impressions and the expanded edition are manifestations of the same obra, not separate titles. MIT Press dates the updated edition differently in some listings, so the edition chronology is not completely consistent across sources. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262514002/privacy-on-the-line/) [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **2015:** *Breaking Teleprinter Ciphers at Bletchley Park: An Edition of I. J. Good, D. Michie and G. Timms, General Report on Tunny with Emphasis on Statistical Methods (1945)*. An edited historical document prepared with James Reeds and J. V. Field; Diffie is an editor and mediator of an earlier wartime text, not its original author. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

### Technical and historical papers

- **1976:** *A Critique of the Proposed Data Encryption Standard*. A short technical critique coauthored with Martin E. Hellman. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1976:** *Multiuser Cryptographic Techniques*. A conference paper with Martin E. Hellman presenting public-key techniques for multiuser communications. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/afips/DiffieH76.html)

- **1976:** *New Directions in Cryptography*. The foundational journal paper with Martin E. Hellman that manifests the public-key project, digital-signature concept, and practical key-exchange method in one text. The 2022 appearance in *Democratizing Cryptography* is a reprint, not a new obra. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/tit/DiffieH76.html) [*Democratizing Cryptography* contents](https://www.morganclaypoolpublishers.com/catalog_orig/product_info_cpath-24-products_id-1722.html)

- **1977:** *Exhaustive Cryptanalysis of the NBS Data Encryption Standard*. A technical paper with Martin E. Hellman evaluating brute-force attacks on DES. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/computer/DiffieH77.html)

- **1978:** *Data Security for EFT and Automated Business: New Problems—New Solutions*. A separately issued technical study on electronic-funds-transfer security. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1978:** *The Outlook for Data Security*. A magazine article surveying emerging data-security problems. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1979:** *Privacy and Authentication: An Introduction to Cryptography*. An invited survey paper with Martin E. Hellman; its later inclusion in *Democratizing Cryptography* is a manifestation of the 1979 work. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/) [DBLP reprint record](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/books/acm/22/DiffieH22b.html)

- **1981:** *Cryptography, the Next Two Decades*. A forward-looking CRYPTO conference paper. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/93/2898.html)

- **1981:** *National Security and Commercial Security: Division of Responsibility*. A brief collaboratively produced conference text with Melville Klein, Michael L. Dertouzos, Andrew Gleason, and Dean Smith; its brevity and multiple authors make it closer to a recorded panel position than a freestanding research paper. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/DiffieKDGS81.html)

- **1982:** *Cryptographic Technology: Fifteen Year Forecast*. A technological forecast published both in an AAAS symposium volume and in *SIGACT News*; these are manifestations of one text. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/sigact/Diffie82.html) [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1982:** *Conventional vs. Public Key Cryptosystems*. A comparative chapter in *Secure Communications and Asymmetric Cryptosystems*. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1983:** *Securing Networks: End-to-End Encryption vs. Link Encryption and Trusted Systems*. A short conference paper; DBLP preserves the published title’s typographical form as “Encrpytion.” [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sp/Diffie83.html)

- **1984:** *Network Security, Problems and Approaches*. A National Communications Forum proceedings paper. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1985:** *Security for the DoD Transmission Control Protocol*. A CRYPTO paper proposing security mechanisms for the Defense Department’s TCP environment. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/Diffie85.html)

- **1985:** *Analysis of a Public Key Approach Based on Polynomial Substitution*. A cryptanalytic paper with Harriet J. Fell. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/FellD85.html)

- **1986:** *Communication Security and National Security: Business, Technology, and Politics*. A National Communications Forum proceedings paper joining technical and policy analysis. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1987:** *Encryption and ISDN—A Natural Fit*. A technical conference paper with Brian O’Higgins, Leo Strawczynski, and Rudi de Hoog. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1987:** *Secure Communications with the Integrated Services Digital Network*. A symposium paper with Brian O’Higgins, Leo Strawczynski, and David Steer. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1987:** *An ISDN Secure Telephone Unit*. A National Communications Forum paper with Brian O’Higgins, Leo Strawczynski, and David Steer describing a designed communications device. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1988:** *The First Ten Years of Public-Key Cryptography*. An invited historical and technical survey. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/pieee/Diffie88.html)

- **1988:** *A Secure Audio Teleconference System*. A systems paper with David G. Steer, Leo Strawczynski, and Michael J. Wiener; Diffie’s bibliography still labels it “to appear,” while DBLP confirms its publication in the CRYPTO ’88 proceedings. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/SteerSDW88.html) [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1989:** *Protection of Terminal-to-Host Communications by End-to-End Cryptography*. A Securicom proceedings paper. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1989:** *The Adolescence of Public-Key Cryptography*. A published EUROCRYPT abstract rather than a full paper; it resists treatment as a substantial independent work. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/eurocrypt/Diffie89.html)

- **1989; published 1991:** *Digital Signatures, Electronic Negotiation, and Tamper-Resistant Audit Trails*. A Compsec keynote subsequently made into a proceedings paper in *SmartCard 2000*. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1990:** *Authenticated Key Exchange and Secure Interactive Communication*. A Securicom proceedings paper. Diffie’s bibliography runs this entry into the following 1992 paper, but the different title and venue indicate a distinct text. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1992:** *Authentication and Authenticated Key Exchanges*. A research paper with Paul C. van Oorschot and Michael J. Wiener formalizing authenticated key-exchange properties. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/dcc/DiffieOW92.html)

- **1992:** *Who Holds the Keys?* A published conference-panel record with Dorothy E. Denning, James Bidzos, David Bellin, John Gilmore, and John Perry Barlow; because it records a collective event, its status as an authored obra is weak. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/cfp/DenningBBGDB92.html)

- **1993:** *Objectives and Costs in Peer Entity Negotiation*. A proceedings paper on cryptographic negotiation. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1994:** *Privacy and Authentication for Wireless Local Area Networks*. A technical paper with Ashar Aziz, closely related to their patented wireless-security apparatus but a distinct explanatory text. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/wc/AzizD94.html)

- **1996:** *Special Issue Dedicated to Gustavus J. Simmons—Foreword to This Issue*. A jointly written editorial foreword with Thomas Beth, not a research article. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/dcc/BethD96.html)

- **1996:** *The National Security Establishment and the Development of Public-Key Cryptography*. A short historical article. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/dcc/Diffie96.html)

- **2001:** *Ultimate Cryptography*. A speculative essay in *Communications of the ACM*. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/Diffie01.html)

- **2002:** *September 11th Did Not Change Cryptography Policy*. A policy essay with Susan Landau; Diffie’s bibliography records it while accepted, and the AMS issue confirms its subsequent publication. [AMS](https://www.ams.org/journals/notices/200204/200204FullIssue.pdf) [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **2006:** *Chattering about SIGINT*. A one-page commentary in *IEEE Security & Privacy*, not a full research paper. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/ieeesp/Diffie06.html)

- **2007:** *Cryptography: Past, Present and Future*. A two-page conference contribution, probably the textual trace of a talk rather than a developed paper. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/otm/Diffie07.html)

- **2008:** *Information Security: 50 Years Behind, 50 Years Ahead*. A historical and prospective essay in *Communications of the ACM*. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/Diffie08.html)

- **2008:** *What Did We Do Before Biometric Passports?* A review of Valentin Groebner’s *Who Are You?*; it is criticism about another person’s book, not a book by Diffie. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cryptologia/Diffie08.html)

- **2008:** *Information Assurance Technology Forecast 2008*. A collaborative forecast with Steven M. Bellovin, Terry V. Benzel, Bob Blakley, Dorothy E. Denning, Jeremy Epstein, and Paulo Veríssimo. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/pid/93/2898.html)

- **2008:** *SMS4 Encryption Algorithm for Wireless Networks*. An IACR ePrint with George Ledin documenting an encryption algorithm; DBLP classifies it as an informal or other publication. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/iacr/DiffieL08.html)

- **2009:** *Communications Surveillance: Privacy and Security at Risk*. An article with Susan Landau appearing in both *ACM Queue* and *Communications of the ACM*; the two appearances are manifestations of one obra. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/DiffieL09.html)

- **2011:** *As Simple as Possible—but Not More So*. A collaborative article with Steven M. Bellovin, Scott O. Bradner, Susan Landau, and Jennifer Rexford. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/BellovinBDLR11.html)

- **2012:** *Can It Really Work? Problems with Extending EINSTEIN 3 to Critical Infrastructure*. A technical-policy article with Steven M. Bellovin, Scott O. Bradner, Susan Landau, and Jennifer Rexford. [Harvard National Security Journal](https://harvardnsj.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/01/Vol.-3_Bellovin_Bradner_Diffie_Landau_Rexford.pdf)

- **2016:** *The Evolving Meaning of Information Security*. A one-page KDD conference record, likely an abstract or textual manifestation of a presentation rather than a full paper. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/kdd/Diffie16.html)

- **2022:** *The Challenges of IoT, TLS, and Random Number Generators in the Real World: Bad Random Numbers Are Still with Us and Are Proliferating in Modern Systems*. A technical article with James P. Hughes. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/queue/HughesD22.html)

### Reports, manifestos, testimony, and public interventions

- **1992:** *Public Policy Issues in Cryptography*. A Computers, Freedom & Privacy proceedings paper intended to intervene in policy, not merely describe cryptographic technology. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1993:** *The Impact of a Secret Cryptographic Standard on Encryption, Privacy, Law Enforcement and Technology*. Testimony before a House subcommittee: an official speech-act whose purpose was to influence legislation and oversight. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1993:** *The Impact of Regulating Cryptography on the Computer and Communications Industries*. Congressional testimony and thus an institutional intervention rather than an ordinary publication. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1994:** *Codes, Keys, and Conflicts: Issues in U.S. Crypto Policy*. A collective USACM panel report with Susan Landau, Stephen Kent, Clinton Brooks, Scott Charney, Dorothy Denning, Anthony Lauck, Douglas Miller, Peter G. Neumann, and David Sobel. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1994:** *Crypto Policy Perspectives*. A journal article by the same ten-person USACM group; it is closely related to *Codes, Keys, and Conflicts* but catalogued as its own text. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/LandauKBCDDLMNS94.html)

- **1994:** *Key Escrow: Its Impact and Alternatives*. Senate Judiciary Committee testimony: a public-policy act preserved as text. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1995:** *Washington’s Computer Insecurity*. A *New York Times* op-ed—an argumentative public intervention rather than a scholarly paper. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1996:** *Minimal Key Lengths for Symmetric Ciphers to Provide Adequate Commercial Security*. A collective technical-policy report with Matt Blaze, Ronald L. Rivest, Bruce Schneier, Tsutomu Shimomura, Eric Thompson, and Michael Wiener. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1996:** *Testimony before the U.S. Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation*. The bibliography supplies no separate title; it is therefore named here only by its institutional form and date rather than given an invented title. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1996:** *Cryptology, Technology, and Politics*. An invited CRYPTO address; the cited bibliography documents the performance but does not establish a separately published paper, so it resists classification as a fixed text. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1997:** *The Risks of Key Recovery, Key Escrow, and Trusted Third-Party Encryption*. A collective Center for Democracy and Technology report with Hal Abelson, Ross Anderson, Steven M. Bellovin, Josh Benaloh, Matt Blaze, John Gilmore, Peter G. Neumann, Ronald L. Rivest, Jeffrey I. Schiller, and Bruce Schneier; its report form and prescriptive purpose make it manifesto-like. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/wwwj/AbelsonABBBDGNR97.html) [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1997:** *Comments on the Computer Security Enhancement Act of 1997*. House Science Committee testimony, acting directly within a legislative process. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1997:** *Control: FBI Wants It, but Why?* A Christian Science Monitor op-ed with Susan Landau. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **1998:** *E-Commerce and Security*. A brief standards-policy article. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/standardview/Diffie98.html)

- **2000:** *We Can Tap It for You Wholesale*. A published record of a Computers, Freedom & Privacy lunch address; the one-page proceedings object is a trace of a performance and not necessarily a complete authored text. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/cfp/Diffie00.html)

- **2015:** *Keys Under Doormats: Mandating Insecurity by Requiring Government Access to All Data and Communications*. A collective technical-policy report with fourteen coauthors. Its journal article and shortened *Communications of the ACM* appearance are manifestations of the same intervention rather than separate obras. [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/AbelsonABBBDGGL15.html) [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **2021; journal manifestation 2024:** *Bugs in Our Pockets: The Risks of Client-Side Scanning*. A collective report opposing mandated client-side scanning, made public in 2021 and later published in the *Journal of Cybersecurity*; those dates describe successive manifestations of one obra. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/) [DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cybersecurity/AbelsonABBBCDLNRSSTT24.html)

### Patented apparatus and methods

- **Filed 1977; issued 1980:** *Cryptographic Apparatus and Method* (US 4,200,770). A patented cryptographic apparatus and method credited to Martin E. Hellman, Whitfield Diffie, and Ralph C. Merkle; the patent is a legal-technical protocol and claim set, not simply a paper. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4200770A/en)

- **1993:** *Method and Apparatus for Privacy and Authentication in Wireless Networks* (US 5,371,794). A patented wireless-security method and apparatus with Ashar Aziz; Diffie’s bibliography gives 2 November 1993, apparently a filing-related date rather than the later patent-issue date. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **2009:** *Method for Generating Mnemonic Random Passcodes* (US 7,552,469). A patented method with William Woods for producing memorable random passcodes. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)

- **2016:** *Symmetric NAT Traversal for Direct Communication in P2P Networks When Some of the Routing NATs Are Symmetric* (US 9,497,160). A patented networking method with Anatoli Bolotov, Mikhail I. Grinchuk, Ivan Danov, and Anton Sabev. [Diffie’s bibliography](https://whitfielddiffie.com/)
