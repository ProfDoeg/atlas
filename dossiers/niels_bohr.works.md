## Works by Niels Bohr

### Theses and early experimental work

- **1909:** *Determination of the Surface-Tension of Water by the Method of Jet Vibration*. An experimental paper originating in Bohr’s prize essay. [BCW 13, no. 1]
- **1909:** *An Account of the Application of the Electron Theory to Explain the Physical Properties of Metals*. Bohr’s master’s thesis; the bibliography records both its English and Danish titles. [BCW 13, no. 2]
- **1910:** *On the Determination of the Tension of a Recently Formed Water-Surface*. An experimental research paper on surface tension. [BCW 13, no. 3]
- **1911:** *Studies on the Electron Theory of Metals*. Bohr’s doctoral dissertation, originally published in Danish. [BCW 13, no. 4]
- **1911:** *[Autobiography]*. A short institutional autobiography without an independent title; its resistance to ordinary “work” status is preserved by the editors’ supplied title. [BCW 13, no. 5]
- **1912:** *Note on the Electron Theory of Thermoelectric Phenomena*. A theoretical-physics journal note. [BCW 13, no. 6]

- **1963:** *Essays 1958–1962 on Atomic Physics and Human Knowledge*. A posthumously issued continuation assembled from Bohr’s final essays; its publication as a book was completed after his death.

### Projects and institutional obras that are not discrete publications

- **1913 onward:** *The quantum theory of atomic constitution (“the Bohr atom”)*. A theoretical project manifested across many papers rather than a single object; calling it one “work” conceals its repeated revision.
- **1918–1922:** *The correspondence-principle programme*. A continuing research project distributed through the line-spectra treatise, lectures, papers, and collaborations; it has no single definitive text.
- **1921–1962:** *Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of Copenhagen*. Bohr was the founding director and principal intellectual organizer of an institution, an institutional obra rather than a text and not solely his creation.
- **1927 onward:** *Complementarity*. A conceptual project repeatedly reformulated across physics, biology, anthropology, and epistemology; it resists identification with any one paper.
- **1944–1950:** *The “open world” initiative*. A political project manifested in memoranda, private approaches to Allied leaders, articles, and the 1950 open letter; much of it was collaborative, confidential, or unpublished during Bohr’s life.
- **1950s:** *European cooperation in nuclear research*. Bohr’s advocacy and institutional labor contributed to CERN and related Scandinavian initiatives, but the resulting organizations cannot responsibly be attributed to him as sole-made works.
- **1955–1957:** *Risø research establishment*. Bohr participated in shaping Denmark’s atomic-energy research institution; this is collective institution-building, not an individually authored object.
