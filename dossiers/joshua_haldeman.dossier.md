# Joshua Norman Haldeman: Research Dossier

## Scope, Method, and Evidentiary Labels

This dossier reconstructs Joshua Norman Haldeman’s life from genealogical records, contemporary newspapers and political publications, election returns, library catalogues, chiropractic institutional records, Haldeman’s surviving writings, family memoirs, and later historical investigations.

The record is uneven. His Canadian chiropractic career is unusually well documented through professional journals and the surviving Haldeman papers. His South African political beliefs are documented principally through his own published writings and contemporaneous press coverage. Several biographical accounts were written by relatives or professional admirers and require allowance for their commemorative purpose.

Labels used below:

- **Documented fact:** supported by a contemporary record, Haldeman’s own writing, institutional record, election return, or multiple independent sources.
- **Reported fact:** stated by a generally credible secondary source but not independently confirmed here from the underlying document.
- **Self-report:** stated by Haldeman or in material apparently supplied by him.
- **Family account:** reported by a relative or family memoir.
- **Allegation:** asserted by an identifiable person without adequate documentary confirmation.
- **Disputed claim:** competing accounts or interpretations exist.
- **Rumor or myth:** a circulating story unsupported by sufficient evidence.
- **Unresolved:** available evidence does not permit a confident conclusion.

## Basic Identifying Information

- **Full name:** Joshua Norman Haldeman.
- **Common forms:** Joshua N. Haldeman; J. N. Haldeman; Josh Haldeman; Dr. Joshua Haldeman.
- **Technocracy designation:** **10450-1**, derived, according to contemporary accounts, from the latitude and longitude of his Technocracy section in the Regina area.
- **Professional title:** Doctor of Chiropractic, normally abbreviated D.C.
- **Birth:** 25 November 1902.
- **Birthplace:** Pequot—normally identified as present-day Pequot Lakes—Crow Wing County, Minnesota, United States.
- **Death:** 13 January 1974, aged seventy-one.
- **Place of death:** in an aircraft accident in the Pretoria/Brits area of the Transvaal, South Africa. Sources commonly give Pretoria as the place of death; the precise crash locality is inconsistently described.
- **Citizenship/national association:** American-born; raised and professionally established in Canada; emigrated permanently to South Africa in 1950. The exact chronology of his Canadian and South African citizenship or naturalization has not been established here.
- **Occupations:** chiropractor, farmer, political organizer, lecturer, writer, amateur aviator and explorer, competitive pistol shooter, and briefly cowboy, construction worker, rodeo performer, and itinerant laborer.
- **Political organizations:** Saskatchewan cooperative and farm movements; Co-operative Commonwealth Federation; Technocracy Incorporated; Total War and Defence; Social Credit Party of Saskatchewan; Social Credit Association/Party of Canada.
- **Professional organizations:** Saskatchewan Chiropractors’ Association; Dominion Council of Canadian Chiropractors; Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College; International Chiropractors’ Association; South African Chiropractors’ Association; Second World Chiropractic Congress.
- **Principal residences:** Minnesota; Herbert and other communities in Saskatchewan; farming areas around Waldeck, Lienan, Ardill and Vantage; Assiniboia; Regina; and, after 1950, Rietondale, Pretoria, South Africa.
- **Known Regina addresses:** professional offices included 614 Broder Building; the family lived in a twenty-room residence at 2302 Cornwall Street by 1950.
- **Known Pretoria address:** 267 Soutpansberg Road, Rietondale, appears in the later Haldeman chronology.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and ancestry

Haldeman was the son of:

- **John Elon Haldeman**, born in Illinois according to genealogical accounts.
- **Almeda Jane Norman Haldeman**, born in Minnesota and later described as the first chiropractor known to have practiced in Canada.

A 1945 campaign biography, evidently based on information supplied by Haldeman, called his ancestry English, Irish, and Dutch. Later family accounts trace the paternal Haldeman line to Mennonites who migrated from Signau in the Swiss canton of Bern to North America during the eighteenth century. These descriptions are not necessarily contradictory: “Dutch” was sometimes used imprecisely in North American family traditions, including for German- or Swiss-German-speaking ancestry.

Haldeman had at least one sister, **Almeda Haldeman**. A complete, independently verified sibling list has not been recovered.

### Father’s illness and mother’s chiropractic career

In 1904 John Elon Haldeman developed diabetes. A family-authored account says physicians gave him approximately six months to live. He obtained chiropractic treatment, reportedly improved for a time, and Almeda studied the method so that she could care for him and others. She graduated from E. W. Lynch’s Chiropractic School and Cure in Minneapolis on 20 January 1905.

The family moved to Herbert, Saskatchewan, in 1907, where Almeda practiced chiropractic. Chiropractic historians consequently identify her as the earliest known chiropractor practicing in Canada. This designation is well established within the profession, although it is not equivalent to proof that no unrecorded practitioner preceded her.

John Elon Haldeman died on 9 August 1909. On 2 March 1915, Almeda married **Heseltine Wilson**, described as a Yorkshire-born schoolteacher, farmer, and longtime reeve of the Rural Municipality of Excelsior. Joshua was largely raised on Wilson’s stock farm.

### First marriage

On 28 December 1927 Haldeman married **Wanda Eva “Eve” Peters** in Winnipeg, Manitoba. Sources often shorten her name to Eve Peters.

Their son:

- **Joshua Jerry Noel Haldeman**, born 12 December 1934, generally known as Jerry. He later operated a local airline company in British Columbia.

The marriage deteriorated amid the Depression, the loss of Haldeman’s farm, and his heavy political activity. The couple had separated by 1937 and subsequently divorced. No evidence located suggests that either spouse publicly litigated responsibility for the breakdown.

### Second marriage

Haldeman met **Winnifred Josephine “Wyn” Fletcher** through ballroom-dancing lessons in Regina. Wyn, born in Moose Jaw on 10 March 1914, was a dance and drama instructor. She had studied in Chicago, New York, Toronto, Vancouver, Winnipeg, and at the Banff School of Fine Arts. Her credentials included a teaching certificate from Trinity College of Music and an elementary ballet certificate from the Royal Academy of Dance.

They married in Regina on 7 June 1942. Wyn continued operating a dance studio from their large Regina home.

They had four children:

- **Edith Lynne Haldeman**, commonly Lynne.
- **Scott Haldeman**, who became a chiropractor, physician, neurologist, and medical researcher.
- **Kaye Haldeman**, twin sister of Maye.
- **Maye Haldeman**, born 19 April 1948 in Regina, later Maye Musk, model, dietitian, and author.

Together with Jerry, these children account for the five children generally attributed to Joshua Haldeman.

Wyn survived Joshua by thirty-eight years and died at Canmore, Alberta, on 16 March 2012, aged ninety-eight.

### Descendants

Through Maye Haldeman’s marriage to Errol Musk, Joshua was the maternal grandfather of:

- **Elon Reeve Musk**
- **Kimbal Musk**
- **Tosca Musk**

Other descendants include filmmaker and entrepreneur **Lyndon Rive**, a grandson through another branch of the Haldeman family. Public genealogies sometimes supply extensive lists of great-grandchildren, but those later generations are beyond the subject’s own life history.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1902–1921

Haldeman was born in a Minnesota log cabin. He was approximately four when the family migrated to Saskatchewan and seven when his father died.

His surviving educational and campaign biographies place him at schools in:

- Donnellyville
- Spenst
- Waldeck
- Swift Current

Both his mother and stepfather worked as teachers at different times. Haldeman grew up in a farm environment emphasizing physical work and self-sufficiency. Family and professional biographies describe him as practicing bronco riding, boxing, wrestling, rope spinning, and target shooting.

These accounts contributed to a later public image of Haldeman as a prairie individualist, but much of the childhood narrative comes from family papers and campaign literature rather than independent contemporary reporting.

## Education and Formation

Haldeman later said he had attended nine colleges and universities, apparently counting short courses and postgraduate chiropractic programs. Institutions associated with his education include:

- Moose Jaw College
- Regina College
- Winnipeg Agricultural College
- University of Chicago
- Palmer School of Chiropractic, Davenport, Iowa
- National College of Chiropractic, Chicago
- Lincoln Chiropractic College, Indianapolis
- Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College, Toronto

There is no evidence that he received a conventional academic degree from Moose Jaw, Regina, Winnipeg, Chicago, or an agricultural institution.

### Palmer School of Chiropractic

Haldeman attended Palmer from approximately 1922 to 1926. Before he could sit the Iowa licensing examination, A. I. Naumann of Davenport’s Sudlow Intermediate School examined him and certified on 20 January 1926 that his educational level was equivalent to that of a regular high-school graduate.

On 4 February 1926 Palmer awarded him:

- a Doctor of Chiropractic diploma following its three-year course;
- an X-ray and spinography certificate.

His listed instructors included B. J. Palmer, Mabel H. Palmer, A. B. Hender, James N. Firth, H. E. Vedder, S. J. Burich, J. H. Craven, Ray Richardson, and Henri L. Gaddis.

Haldeman later attributed recurring eye trouble during his student years to spinal problems and claimed that chiropractic adjustments relieved it. This is a **self-report**, not a medically verified diagnosis or treatment outcome.

### Further training

Known certificates included:

- St. John Ambulance first aid, 1927.
- National College of Chiropractic postgraduate course, 1935, covering chiropractic technique, physiotherapy, colonic therapy, dissection, laboratory and physical diagnosis, gynecology, eye-ear-nose-and-throat work, X-ray, first aid, and minor surgery.
- Palmer postgraduate review courses in 1935, 1937, 1938, and 1949.
- Lincoln Chiropractic College postgraduate work, listed in his records as 1936.
- National College postgraduate work in 1938.
- Saskatchewan competency certificate for X-ray use, 1943.
- Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College technique course, 1947.

He maintained a lifelong friendship and professional alliance with **Bartlett Joshua “B. J.” Palmer**, although Haldeman also studied with chiropractic “mixer” schools that incorporated therapeutic methods beyond spinal adjustment. He built relationships with osteopaths J. V. McManis and H. G. Swanson.

## Early Career: Chiropractic, Farming, and Depression

Following graduation, Haldeman practiced chiropractic for only a few years before returning to farming. From the late 1920s until approximately 1934 he farmed in the Waldeck, Lienan, Ardill, and Vantage districts of Saskatchewan.

He joined or participated in:

- Saskatchewan cooperative societies, 1927–31;
- United Farmers of Canada, Saskatchewan Section;
- Saskatchewan Farmer’s Political Association.

The Depression and prairie drought destroyed the viability of his farm. He was unable to maintain payments on machinery bought with bank credit and lost the property. Both Haldeman and his son Scott later treated this experience as formative: it left him suspicious of banks, centralized financial institutions, and bureaucracies.

After losing the farm, he worked at various times as a construction worker, cowboy, and rodeo performer. His 1945 campaign literature claimed that he:

- crossed Canada on freight trains and in passenger-train coal tenders;
- lived among Peace River trappers and bush homesteaders;
- stayed in “hobo jungles” outside Canadian cities;
- stowed away on an ocean-going ship.

These are **self-promotional autobiographical claims**. They are plausible in general outline but have not been independently documented journey by journey.

## Political and Intellectual Development

### Cooperative and CCF phase, 1927–1936

Haldeman’s first political affiliations were with agrarian cooperatives and the political left. From 1931 to 1934 he directed a “Research and Open Forum Debating Society” concerned with economics and political science.

From 1934 to 1936 he chaired the Co-operative Commonwealth Federation organization in the Assiniboia federal constituency. The CCF was a coalition of labor, farmer, cooperative, and socialist organizations advocating public economic intervention during the Depression.

A September 1934 letter by Haldeman in *The Western Producer* already supported Social Credit monetary ideas. He argued that both capitalism and socialism suffered from insufficient consumer purchasing power and recommended works by Maurice Colbourne and C. Marshall Hattersley. His transition from the CCF to Technocracy and later Social Credit was therefore gradual rather than a clean ideological reversal.

### Technocracy Incorporated, 1936–1941

After returning to full-time chiropractic practice and moving to Regina in 1936, Haldeman joined **Howard Scott’s Technocracy Incorporated**.

Technocracy proposed replacing money with energy certificates and superseding existing North American governments with a continental “Technate” administered by technicians and engineers. Its publications rejected electoral democracy and treated society as a technical control system. Members wore gray uniforms, used salutes, and sometimes replaced conventional names with geographic membership numbers.

Haldeman became:

- Regina-area organizer;
- authorized instructor;
- Canadian director of research;
- eventually the movement’s leading Canadian officer.

As **10450-1**, he wrote in Technocracy’s magazine that a social “smashup” was approaching and that the organization was preparing for a “New Social Order.”

The exact size of Technocracy in Canada is uncertain. Later sensational accounts attribute enormous membership figures to it, but firm national numbers have not been established.

#### Wartime ban and border exclusion

Technocracy opposed sending Canadian manpower to wars outside North America and presented itself as a continental government-in-waiting. In 1940 the Canadian government banned it under wartime Defence of Canada Regulations as an organization considered prejudicial to the war effort.

Haldeman attempted to enter the United States to address a meeting in Minnesota but was refused entry, despite his American birth. Contemporary reporting described him as an alien whose admission might be prejudicial to public safety. The exact immigration-law basis and whether his American citizenship had lapsed or been relinquished remain unclear.

#### Arrest and conviction

After the ban, Haldeman placed a 26 June 1940 advertisement in the *Regina Leader-Post* titled “Statement of Patriotism by Those Who Were Technocrats.” It called the ban unjustified, described it as possible “subversive sabotage” by Canada’s political government, and asserted that Technocracy opposed Nazism, Fascism, and Communism.

The RCMP arrested him in Vancouver on 8 October 1940. He was transported to Regina and released on substantial bail, reported as $8,000.

He faced three charges connected with writing, publishing, or circulating material considered likely to interfere with prosecution of the war or cause disaffection toward the Crown. Contemporary reports and later summaries agree that he was convicted. They disagree on the penalty:

- The Atlantic’s reconstruction reports **$100 plus costs, or two months’ imprisonment**.
- Other summaries report a **$200 fine**.
- A family-professional biography loosely states that his opposition to Communism “briefly landed him in jail,” but does not establish that he served a custodial sentence.

The most responsible conclusion is: **documented conviction; exact fine unresolved; no reliable evidence found that he served a substantial prison term**.

#### Break with Technocracy

Haldeman left Technocracy in 1941. The family-authored account says he resigned when its headquarters advocated collaboration with the Soviet Union after Germany invaded the USSR. He considered Stalin’s government irreligious and dictatorial.

This account is credible and consistent with his later anticommunism, but it does not mean he rejected Technocracy’s antidemocratic institutional vision. His stated objection centered on Soviet collaboration.

### Total War and Defence, 1941–1943

Haldeman then organized **Total War and Defence**, which conducted volunteer research on Canada’s war mobilization. His biographical papers claimed that sixty-five nationally prominent Canadians participated.

His legal adviser and principal assistant was identified as **Mr. Turnbull, K.C.**, formerly a Conservative member of Parliament and research or speech-writing aide to former prime minister R. B. Bennett. Exact membership records and the organization’s practical influence have not been recovered.

Haldeman published a newsletter or pamphlet under the same name. A February 1941 issue advocated “total conscription.” The movement failed to acquire a durable national following.

### Social Credit, 1943–1950

Haldeman joined the Social Credit Association of Canada by 1943, although he had promoted its monetary ideas since at least 1934.

Social Credit, developed by C. H. Douglas, held that wages and purchasing power systematically lagged behind prices and production. Its remedies included public creation of consumer credit or dividends. Douglas also opposed majority rule, secret ballots, and political parties and promoted antisemitic conspiracy theories.

Haldeman served as:

- Saskatchewan provincial coordinator;
- provincial secretary and vice-president;
- leader of the Saskatchewan Social Credit Party;
- chairman of the National Council of the Social Credit Association of Canada;
- administrator of the national office;
- director of economic research.

He depended heavily on organizational and financial assistance from Alberta Social Credit leaders, particularly Premier **Ernest Manning** and **Robert Jorgenson**. He also worked with federal leader **Solon Earl Low**.

#### Election record

**1945 federal election, Prince Albert**

- Edward LeRoy Bowerman, CCF: 7,928 votes.
- William Lyon Mackenzie King, Liberal: 7,799.
- Walter Hemming Nelson, Progressive Conservative: 2,768.
- Joshua Norman Haldeman, Social Credit: **847 votes, 4.38 percent**.

Haldeman finished fourth. The sitting prime minister, Mackenzie King, also lost the constituency.

**1948 Saskatchewan election, Yorkton**

- Arthur Percy Swallow, CCF: 3,795.
- Andrew M. Kindred, Liberal: 3,256.
- Joshua N. Haldeman, Social Credit: **1,792, or 20.26 percent**.

Haldeman again lost. Province-wide, the party ran thirty-six candidates, received 40,268 votes—approximately 8.09 percent—and won no seats.

**1949 federal election, Lake Centre**

- John Diefenbaker, Progressive Conservative: 8,845.
- Delmar Storey Valleau, CCF: 5,413.
- Donald Arthur MacRae, Liberal: 3,061.
- Joshua Norman Haldeman, Social Credit: **856, or approximately 4.7 percent**.

He finished fourth and lost to future prime minister John Diefenbaker.

Haldeman resigned as national council chairman in autumn 1949. The resignation was reportedly not accepted immediately. He left Canadian party politics when the family emigrated in 1950.

## Chiropractic Career and Institutions

### Assiniboia and Regina practice

Haldeman briefly maintained an office in Assiniboia in 1935–36, then practiced in Regina from 1936 until 1950. By 1950 he described the practice as highly successful and his twenty-room Cornwall Street residence and airplane indicate substantial prosperity after the farm failure.

His clinic offered X-ray and neurocalometer services. The neurocalometer, promoted by B. J. Palmer, was claimed to detect spinal “subluxations” through temperature differences. Its diagnostic assumptions were contested even within chiropractic and are not accepted as established medical science.

He gave weekly chiropractic broadcasts on Regina station CKCK, documented in 1942 at Wednesdays at 4:15 p.m.

### Saskatchewan chiropractic legislation

Haldeman took a leading role in the campaign for Saskatchewan’s 1943 Chiropractic Act. Professional histories credit him with drafting the statute’s language. Lawyer John S. Burton also claimed responsibility for drafting and guiding the act, so exclusive authorship is **disputed**.

After passage, Haldeman served from 1943 to 1950 on:

- the executive board of the Saskatchewan Chiropractors’ Association;
- its examining board.

### Dominion Council and Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College

Beginning in 1942–43, Haldeman represented Saskatchewan in the organization of the Dominion Council of Canadian Chiropractors, predecessor of the Canadian Chiropractic Association.

On 11 January 1943 he participated in an Ottawa delegation seeking commissioned military status for chiropractors. Military officials rejected the request, leaving chiropractors to enlist in ordinary classifications.

Haldeman helped found the Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College in Toronto. He was an original sponsor and director from 1944 to 1950. He favored a curriculum centered on chiropractic, diagnosis, laboratory work, and basic sciences, without compulsory physiotherapy. He nevertheless resisted direct control from B. J. Palmer or other Americans over Canadian professional affairs.

He became vice-president of the Dominion Council in 1947 and served as the Canadian member of the International Chiropractors’ Association Board of Control from 1948 to 1950.

In August 1949 he addressed approximately 5,000 participants at the Palmer Lyceum in Davenport on “Freedom.”

### South African practice

The Haldeman Chiropractic Clinic opened in Pretoria on 21 November 1950. By later professional accounts it occupied more than 4,000 square feet and saw as many as 600 patients per week. These numbers derive from chiropractic memorial literature and have not been independently audited.

Haldeman encouraged South Africans to study chiropractic abroad and was credited with directly or indirectly sending more than one hundred students for training.

His South African organizational positions included:

- secretary of the South African Chiropractors’ Association, 1952–59;
- president, conventionally dated 1959–69, although a 1955 journal already called him president;
- chairman of a joint chiropractic legislative committee, 1962;
- honorary life member, 1969;
- president of the Second World Chiropractic Congress, 1973.

The discrepancy over the beginning of his presidency may reflect temporary offices, changing organizational names, or an error in the later chronology.

## Migration to South Africa

In 1950 Haldeman sold or closed his Canadian practice, shipped his 1948 Bellanca airplane to Africa, and moved with Wyn and their four young children to Pretoria.

Several explanations survive:

- **Family account:** Maye Musk wrote that her parents met missionaries who described South Africa’s beauty.
- **Family-professional biography:** he was inspired by an Anglican minister from South Africa at a 1949 Toronto trade fair.
- **Haldeman’s own political writing:** the minister told him that South Africa would become the leader of white civilization.
- **Public explanation:** dissatisfaction with Canadian government control, a desire for individual freedom, adventure, a better climate, and new professional opportunities.
- **Documented political factor:** he supported the newly established apartheid government.

The move occurred shortly after the Population Registration and Group Areas Acts gave statutory form to central features of apartheid. In an interview with the National Party newspaper *Die Transvaler*, Haldeman said the government’s controversial policies had encouraged, rather than deterred, his settlement. The newspaper presented his immigration as favorable publicity for the National Party government.

The interview alarmed the South African Jewish Board of Deputies. Its secretary-general contacted a Canadian counterpart seeking information about Haldeman and Social Credit’s anti-Jewish record.

## South African Political Views and Racial Order

In 1951 Haldeman sent the *Regina Leader-Post* an account of life in South Africa. He described Black Africans as “very primitive,” asserted that they could not responsibly exercise authority, and praised the National Party government’s handling of the “native question.”

He disclosed that his household employed two Black male garden workers during summer, one in winter, and a Black female domestic worker. He reported paying approximately C$10–15 monthly plus food and some clothing. He maintained that Black labor was nevertheless expensive because, in his view, Black workers were unproductive. He described Black South Africans as “happy and contented” unless outside forces agitated them.

He encouraged white immigration, claiming South Africa and the Rhodesias could support fifty million white settlers.

These are **documented statements by Haldeman**, not allegations about his private opinions. They place him in explicit support of racial hierarchy and apartheid.

Haldeman does not appear to have become a prominent official in an organized South African far-right party. Historian Milton Shain, after decades studying South African antisemitism, told Joshua Benton that he did not remember encountering Haldeman as a major activist. His political significance in South Africa lay more in his publications, public commentary, and support for the governing order than in leadership of an extremist organization.

## Antisemitism and Conspiracy Politics

### Social Credit newspaper controversy

The Canadian Social Credit movement contained a significant antisemitic faction influenced by C. H. Douglas’s claims about Jewish finance and world control. **John Patrick Gillese**, editor of the *Canadian Social Crediter*, repeatedly published antisemitic material.

Alberta premier Ernest Manning wanted Gillese removed because he believed the editor was damaging the party. Haldeman and Solon Low defended Gillese’s continued editorship.

In 1946 the Quebec Social Credit organization published excerpts from *The Protocols of the Elders of Zion*, a fabricated text alleging a Jewish conspiracy for world domination. Haldeman publicly asserted that Social Credit opposed antisemitism and expressed sympathy for Jewish victims of Nazi Germany. At the same time, he:

- claimed international financiers, many though “not all” Jewish, had financed Hitler’s rise;
- suggested Jews promoted antisemitism to obtain sympathy;
- argued that whether the *Protocols* were authentic was less important than his belief that their supposed program was unfolding.

A local rabbi described Haldeman’s speeches as “shot through with anti-Semitic talk.” The Canadian Jewish Congress monitored him and other Social Credit figures.

Accordingly, the statement that Haldeman denied being antisemitic is documented, but so are his promotion of classic antisemitic conspiracy claims. His denial does not resolve the content of the statements.

### The 1960 world-conspiracy pamphlet

Shortly after the 21 March 1960 Sharpeville massacre and the resulting international condemnation of South Africa, Haldeman self-published *The International Conspiracy to Establish a World Dictatorship and the Menace to South Africa*.

The pamphlet presented apartheid South Africa as a potential leader of “White Christian Civilization.” It alleged that an international conspiracy controlled banks, universities, medicine, and the press and manipulated anti-apartheid politics.

Haldeman asserted that:

- an invasion by “hordes of Coloured people” threatened South Africa;
- Black opposition to apartheid was externally manufactured;
- international actors deliberately caused the Sharpeville crisis to profit from a decline in South African share prices;
- communism and Wall Street worked together to establish world government;
- major historical events were engineered by international financiers.

He named numerous Jewish financiers, officials, and intellectuals, including Jacob Schiff, Paul Warburg, Herbert Lehman, Bernard Baruch, Felix Frankfurter, Samuel Bronfman, and the Rothschild family. He described communism as a “Jewish moral philosophy” and the London School of Economics as the “Zion of Economists.”

His recommended publications included those of the League of Empire Loyalists, the *South African Observer*, the Australian League of Rights’ *New Times*, and the *American Mercury*. These outlets had documented connections to antisemitic and fascist political networks.

The conspiracy alleged in the pamphlet was never substantiated. Its importance is as direct evidence of Haldeman’s later worldview.

### Health conspiracy writing

Haldeman subsequently issued *The International Conspiracy in Health*, probably in the mid-1960s, although no securely established original publication year was found.

It argued that promoters of world government were behind:

- compulsory or mass vaccination;
- fluoridation of drinking water;
- pasteurization requirements;
- national or compulsory health insurance;
- control of medicine and public-health policy.

He called fluoridation part of a “brain-washing programme.” No evidence has substantiated the unified conspiracy he alleged.

## Aviation, Travel, and Exploration

### Learning to fly

Haldeman began flight instruction in Regina on 16 July 1947 at age forty-four and received his license on 15 March 1948, aged forty-five.

His initial reason was practical. Meetings in Toronto, Edmonton, Saskatchewan communities, and Davenport required lengthy rail travel. He calculated that his own aircraft reduced a Regina–Davenport journey from approximately forty-eight hours by train or thirty-six by commercial air connections to six or eight flight hours.

He first acquired a Luscombe and later a 1948 Bellanca. The “Flying Haldemans,” including the infant twins Kaye and Maye, became regular subjects of chiropractic publicity.

### North American flying

The family flew across Canada and the United States to political meetings, professional conventions, and Palmer events. At three months old, the twins accompanied their parents to Edmonton and Davenport. At approximately one year old they appeared with Joshua on Davenport television station WHO-WOC.

### African and intercontinental flights

After shipping the Bellanca to South Africa, Joshua and Wyn made extensive journeys:

- **1952:** approximately 25,000 miles around Africa and through Europe, reaching Scotland and Norway.
- **1953:** approximately 8,000 miles through central Africa with Wyn and Scott.
- **1954:** approximately 30,000 miles from South Africa along the African and Asian coasts to Australia, around Australia, and back.

Professional accounts called the 1954 journey the longest yet made by a private pilot in a single-engine aircraft. That superlative has not been independently verified against complete international aviation records.

By 1970 Haldeman was credited with flying through eighty countries and territories.

### Aviation institutions

He helped establish the Aircraft Owners and Pilots Association of South Africa, served approximately four years as president, and spent at least eight years on its national council. He represented private pilots on:

- the South African Civil Aviation Advisory Council;
- the Air Navigation Regulations Committee.

### Kalahari “lost city” expeditions

From 1953 onward, Haldeman conducted twelve expeditions seeking the supposed Lost City of the Kalahari, inspired by nineteenth-century traveler and showman Guillermo Antonio Farini, born William Leonard Hunt.

One expedition reportedly covered 8,400 miles at an altitude of about 200 feet over poorly mapped desert. Wyn and whichever children were available accompanied him.

The family encountered San and Khoikhoi communities—described in older sources by terms now considered obsolete—as well as lions, leopards, and other wildlife. They found no lost city. Haldeman nevertheless remained convinced that one existed.

His account formed part of Fay Goldie’s *Lost City of the Kalahari* and was discussed in A. John Clement’s *The Kalahari and Its Lost City*.

### Motor rally

In 1956 Joshua and Wyn entered the roughly 8,000-mile Algiers–Cape Town automobile rally in a Ford Ranch Wagon and shared first prize in their category or classification. Secondary retellings sometimes call this an overall victory; the surviving reports support a tie or shared first prize but should not be expanded beyond the recorded classification without the complete official results.

## Competitive Shooting

Target shooting was a longstanding interest.

After reaching Pretoria, Joshua and Wyn helped establish the Pretoria Pistol Club. Haldeman became the first chairman or president of the South African Pistol Association in 1958 and was made honorary president in 1961.

Recorded honors include:

- Wyn Haldeman: South African national women’s pistol championship, 1961.
- Joshua Haldeman: two first prizes in the 1961 national competition.
- Joshua and Wyn: honorary life membership in the Pretoria Pistol Club, 1963.
- Joshua: “Top Gun” award for highest aggregate score, 1968.
- Wyn: third place in the same 1968 competition.

He was also a founder member of the Pretoria Combat Pistol Club. The clubs promoted pistol instruction for women and household self-defense.

## Business, Property, and Finances

No comprehensive estate, tax, company, or investment record was located.

The reconstructable financial history is:

1. Haldeman acquired farmland and equipment on credit in Saskatchewan.
2. Drought and Depression conditions made payments impossible, and he lost the farm around 1934.
3. His Regina chiropractic practice became financially successful.
4. By 1950 the family occupied a twenty-room home at 2302 Cornwall Street, Regina, and owned a private aircraft.
5. He stated that his income came solely from his practice and that he initially had no assistant; professional and political travel therefore directly reduced his earnings.
6. In Pretoria he built what chiropractic sources described as South Africa’s largest chiropractic clinic, with more than 4,000 square feet and as many as 600 patient visits weekly.
7. The family employed Black domestic and garden labor under the highly unequal wage structure of apartheid South Africa.
8. He owned and operated several aircraft over his lifetime and financed extensive private travel.
9. No substantiated evidence was found of patents, major industrial investments, secret financial partnerships, fraud, money laundering, or intelligence-service funding.

Claims that Haldeman was extraordinarily wealthy or that later Musk family fortunes originated in a Haldeman financial empire go beyond the available evidence. He was evidently prosperous after the mid-1930s but is not documented as an industrial magnate.

Haldeman sometimes disputed institutional control and curriculum with Sturdy, Clubine, Burton, and eastern Canadian chiropractors. The disagreements remained professional rather than personal feuds.

### Political associates and influences

- **Howard Scott:** founder and leader of Technocracy Incorporated; principal ideological influence from 1936 to 1941.
- **C. H. Douglas:** founder of Social Credit theory; major economic and political influence.
- **William Aberhart:** Alberta Social Credit founder; movement predecessor rather than a close documented associate.
- **Ernest Manning:** Alberta premier who provided organizational assistance but clashed with Haldeman over antisemitic editor John Patrick Gillese.
- **Solon Earl Low:** federal Social Credit leader and Haldeman ally.
- **John Patrick Gillese:** party newspaper editor whom Haldeman defended against dismissal.
- **Mr. Turnbull, K.C.:** legal adviser in Total War and Defence; full identity uncertain from the consulted record.
- **South African Anglican minister, name unknown:** credited by Haldeman with predicting South Africa’s leadership of white civilization.

### Rivals and institutional opponents

- Canadian wartime authorities and RCMP officers who enforced the Technocracy ban.
- Saskatchewan CCF and Liberal opponents.
- Edward Bowerman, Arthur Swallow, Andrew Kindred, John Diefenbaker, and other electoral rivals.
- Organized medical bodies and the Quebec Royal Commission critical of chiropractic.
- South African anti-apartheid movements, although no personal meeting or direct dispute with their leaders was found.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, and Evidentiary Findings

### Wartime subversion case

- **Status:** documented arrest, prosecution, and conviction.
- **Conduct:** publishing or circulating a statement attacking the government’s ban on Technocracy.
- **Outcome:** fine reported variously as $100 plus costs or $200, with imprisonment as the alternative sanction.
- **Unresolved point:** whether he spent any meaningful time in jail.

### Antisemitism

- **Status:** documented writings and speeches combined with his denial of antisemitism.
- **Evidence:** defense of the supposed truth of the *Protocols*’ program; claims about Jewish financiers and Hitler; support for Gillese; later world-conspiracy writing centered on named Jewish individuals and institutions.
- **Counter-evidence/qualification:** he publicly said Social Credit opposed antisemitism, expressed sympathy for Jewish victims of Nazism, and formally denied advocating racial hatred.
- **Assessment of record:** the denials are genuine historical evidence, but the content of his other statements reproduces established antisemitic conspiracy motifs.

### Alleged Nazi membership or support

In 2024 Errol Musk reportedly claimed that Joshua and Wyn Haldeman had supported Hitler or belonged to a Canadian Nazi organization.

- **Status:** allegation by a son-in-law, made decades after their deaths.
- **Evidence for:** Haldeman defended antisemitic conspiracism, joined authoritarian movements, and later praised apartheid.
- **Evidence against or complicating:** his 1940 advertisement explicitly said Technocracy opposed Nazism and Fascism; he promoted Canadian defense; he later expressed sympathy for Jewish victims of Nazi Germany.
- **Finding:** no membership card, official Nazi-party roster, or contemporary document proving that Joshua Haldeman belonged to a Canadian Nazi party was found. It is inaccurate to present Nazi-party membership as established fact.

### Apartheid

- **Status:** documented support.
- **Evidence:** his *Die Transvaler* interview, 1951 *Leader-Post* article, 1960 pamphlet, migration context, racial statements, and praise of National Party policy.
- **Qualification:** no evidence located that he held National Party office or participated personally in apartheid policing or political violence.

### Medical and public-health claims

Haldeman represented chiropractic as capable of restoring health by removing spinal nerve pressure and promoted claims of success against illnesses including polio. He later denounced vaccination, fluoridation, and health insurance as conspiratorial instruments.

- **Status:** documented advocacy.
- **Scientific standing:** the universal disease theory of vertebral subluxation and the unified public-health conspiracy alleged by Haldeman are not established by the evidence he provided.
- **Legal history:** no malpractice judgment or patient lawsuit involving him was found in the consulted sources.

### Fraud, corruption, intelligence connections, or financial crime

No reliable evidence was found that Haldeman:

- committed commercial fraud;
- laundered money;
- operated a corrupt political financing scheme;
- worked for an intelligence agency;
- participated in political violence;
- held secret corporate interests;
- was investigated for crimes other than his Technocracy-related wartime prosecution.

Internet narratives linking him to Rockefeller control, Freemasonry, the CIA, occult orders, or a centuries-old banking conspiracy are unsupported.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, and Propaganda

### “Founder of Technocracy”

- **Story:** Haldeman founded Technocracy or Technocracy Incorporated.
- **Circulation:** recent online posts and conspiratorial biographies.
- **Evidence:** Technocracy preceded his membership and was led by Howard Scott. Haldeman joined around 1936 and became the leading Canadian organizer.
- **Finding:** false.
- **Effect:** exaggerates his role and creates a simplified hereditary narrative about Elon Musk.

### “Leader of a Canadian Nazi party”

- **Story:** Haldeman was a formal Nazi-party leader or member.
- **Circulation:** social media, podcasts, and Errol Musk’s later recollections.
- **Evidence:** no documentary membership record located; contemporary evidence shows authoritarian and antisemitic beliefs but also explicit anti-Nazi statements.
- **Finding:** unverified allegation, often repeated as fact.
- **Effect:** has become central to hostile mythology surrounding the Musk family.

### The heroic “Flying Haldemans”

- **Story:** Joshua and Wyn were fearless aviation pioneers who carried small children across continents in a private plane.
- **Origin:** the family’s own book, chiropractic publicity, press features, and later Musk biographies.
- **Evidence:** extensive flights are well documented; distances and several “first” or “longest” claims derive largely from family and professional sources.
- **Finding:** genuine aviation achievement surrounded by promotional embellishment.
- **Effect:** created a durable family mythology of risk, endurance, and unconventional parenting.

### Lost City of the Kalahari

- **Story:** ancient ruins or a city lay concealed in the Kalahari and could be discovered from the air or by expedition.
- **Origin:** Farini’s nineteenth-century travel claims; revived by twentieth-century explorers including Haldeman.
- **Evidence for:** unusual natural formations and Farini’s account.
- **Evidence against:** twelve Haldeman expeditions and other searches failed to find archaeological remains; later researchers generally regard Farini’s “ruins” as natural geology, exaggeration, or invention.
- **Finding:** unresolved in Haldeman’s mind but unsupported as an archaeological city.
- **Effect:** established his South African reputation as an explorer and supplied later family stories told to Elon Musk.

### Hereditary technocracy narrative

- **Story:** Elon Musk’s political and technological program directly implements Joshua Haldeman’s Technocracy beliefs.
- **Circulation:** journalism, political commentary, podcasts, and conspiracy communities, especially since 2023–26.
- **Evidence:** documented family relationship; Musk has praised his grandfather’s risk-taking; both have expressed suspicion of conventional government and interest in technocratic administration.
- **Evidence against direct transmission:** Joshua died when Elon was two. No evidence shows systematic personal instruction or that Elon formally studied or adopted Technocracy Incorporated’s energy-certificate program.
- **Finding:** influence is plausible through family mythology but direct ideological inheritance remains an interpretation, not a documented teacher-student relationship.

### “No Haldeman can fail”

Family accounts preserve variants of Joshua’s maxim that there was nothing a Haldeman could not do. Maye Musk says she transmitted the idea to her own children.

- **Status:** family tradition.
- **Effect:** it supports a hagiographic narrative of independence, danger tolerance, and exceptional capacity.

### Intelligence, occult, and ancient-banking myths

Online posts have alleged that the Haldeman and Musk families are connected to the CIA, occult societies, Freemasonry, Rothschild banking networks, ancient “Radhanite” religion, or an inherited world-government operation.

- **Status:** rumor or conspiracy mythology.
- **Evidence:** none found.
- **Historical significance:** these narratives often invert Haldeman’s own conspiratorial rhetoric, turning a man who alleged secret financial control into a supposed agent of the same imagined system.

### Misidentified photographs

In 2025 a photograph of Howard Scott in a Technocracy uniform circulated as a portrait of Haldeman.

- **Status:** documented image misidentification.
- **Effect:** visually strengthened the false impression that Haldeman personally appeared in every famous Technocracy publicity photograph.

## Health, Accidents, and Death

No reliable record was found of chronic addiction, diagnosed mental illness, psychiatric hospitalization, or major psychological crisis.

Documented or self-reported health information includes:

- childhood exposure to his father’s diabetes;
- recurrent eye difficulties during college, which he said improved after chiropractic treatment;
- regular chiropractic adjustments throughout his life.

His aviation practices involved considerable risk. On 13 January 1974, while practicing aircraft landings, he failed to see a wire suspended between two poles. The aircraft’s wheels caught the wire and the plane overturned or crashed. Haldeman and a passenger were killed.

Sources differ on whether the crash should be localized to Pretoria, Brits, or the surrounding Transvaal district. No complete official accident investigation report was located in the consulted material.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### Chiropractic legacy

Within chiropractic history, Haldeman is remembered as:

- son of Canada’s earliest known chiropractor;
- a drafter or principal advocate of Saskatchewan’s 1943 Chiropractic Act;
- an organizer of national Canadian chiropractic institutions;
- an original sponsor and director of Canadian Memorial Chiropractic College;
- a bridge between Palmer “straight” chiropractic and broader professional schools;
- a central organizer of the profession in South Africa;
- mentor or recruiter of South African chiropractic students.

His son Scott Haldeman became an internationally prominent figure in spinal medicine and chiropractic research, extending the professional family line.

### Aviation and adventure legacy

For several decades, Haldeman’s public memory centered on:

- intercontinental private flights;
- taking young children on remote expeditions;
- the Algiers–Cape Town rally;
- pistol competition;
- the Lost City of the Kalahari.

This was the version emphasized in family memoirs and early biographies of Elon Musk.

### Political rediscovery

After Elon Musk became globally prominent, scholars and journalists revisited Joshua’s political record. The 2023 Atlantic investigation and the 2025 CBC reconstruction shifted attention from adventure to:

- Technocracy’s rejection of electoral democracy;
- Haldeman’s wartime conviction;
- Social Credit antisemitism;
- his support for apartheid;
- his conspiracy pamphlets.

Later comparisons between Technocracy and Elon Musk’s government-efficiency activities are historical interpretations. They are not proof that Joshua designed or directly inspired any specific contemporary program.

### Family influence

Elon Musk was born in 1971 and was two when Joshua died. Direct personal influence was therefore limited. Maye and Wyn preserved stories of Joshua’s flights, desert expeditions, mechanical improvisation, and willingness to accept danger. Elon has publicly described him as a model of genuine risk-taking.

The strongest documented transmission is of **family mythology and temperament**, not a demonstrated line-by-line inheritance of Joshua’s political writings.

## Works by Joshua Norman Haldeman

The following list distinguishes books, pamphlets, articles, speeches, and archival writings. Some dates remain approximate.

### Books and pamphlets

1. **“Chiropractic: the Safest, Quickest, Surest Way to Health.”** Regina, April 1950.  
   Patient-information and autobiographical pamphlet. Lists his credentials, institutional offices, family history, and health claims. Genuine authorship.

2. **The Flying Haldemans: “Pity the Poor Private Pilot.”** With Winnifred J. Haldeman. Self-published, undated, approximately 1955.  
   Family aviation memoir. Some catalogues and secondary sources use the singular *The Flying Haldeman*, but professional citations give *The Flying Haldemans*. Genuine co-authorship; publication date approximate.

3. **The International Conspiracy to Establish a World Dictatorship and the Menace to South Africa.** Rietondale, Pretoria: self-published by Haldeman, May 1960. Approximately 42–48 pages depending on catalogue description.  
   Political-conspiracy and pro-apartheid pamphlet written after Sharpeville. Genuine authorship.

4. **The International Conspiracy in Health.** Self-published, probably South Africa, date uncertain, likely mid-1960s.  
   Opposes vaccination, fluoridation, health insurance, and centralized public-health regulation through a world-conspiracy framework. Genuine authorship; exact first publication date unresolved.

5. **“The Haldeman Expedition,” in Fay Goldie, Lost City of the Kalahari.** Cape Town: A. A. Balkema, 1963.  
   Account of Haldeman’s Kalahari search, incorporated as one section of Goldie’s book. Genuine contributed account, not sole authorship of the volume.

### Political writings and broadcasts

6. **“Social Credit.”** Letter to the editor, *The Western Producer*, 13 September 1934.  
   Early statement of his monetary views.

7. **Technocracy writings under “J. N. Haldeman” and “10450-1.”** Technocracy publications, approximately 1936–40.  
   Includes an article predicting social “smashup” and preparation for a “New Social Order.” A complete bibliographic inventory has not been recovered.

8. **“Statement of Patriotism by Those Who Were Technocrats.”** Advertisement, *Regina Leader-Post*, 26 June 1940; reproduced in other Canadian newspapers.  
   Publication led to criminal charges and conviction.

9. **Total War and Defence.** Newsletter or pamphlet series, beginning by February 1941.  
   Advocated wartime mobilization and total conscription. Exact run and issue list unresolved.

10. **Social Credit correspondence and letters concerning the Protocols controversy.** Saskatchewan newspapers, 1946.  
    Genuine letters defending the proposition that the *Protocols* described a real unfolding plan even if the document was forged.

11. **“Social Credit vs. Communism.”** CBC radio address, 1948.  
    Presented Social Credit as a Christian alternative to Communism. An archival recording or transcript has circulated through Social Credit organizations.

12. **“Freedom.”** Address to the Palmer Lyceum, Davenport, Iowa, 29 August 1949.  
    Text not located in complete published form.

13. **Article or letter on South African life.** *Regina Leader-Post*, 1951.  
    Defended apartheid and encouraged white Canadian immigration.

### Chiropractic and aviation articles

14. **“News Concerning My Trip This Summer.”** Letter to patients, 1938.  
    Report on continuing education at Toronto, Chicago, and Davenport.

15. **“Chiropractic in South Africa: Former ICA Board Member Reports Excellent Opportunities for Chiropractors.”** *ICA Review*, May 1951, 5(11):12.

16. **“Flying … Two Continents.”** *ICA Review*, September 1952, 7(3):5–6, 34, 36.

17. **“We Fly Three Continents … Josh and Wyn Haldeman.”** *ICA International Review of Chiropractic*, December 1954, 9(6):4–5, 27–28.

18. **Letters and reports to the Dominion Council of Canadian Chiropractors**, 1943–50.  
    Surviving archival correspondence covers military commissions, chiropractic curriculum, Canadian autonomy, professional legislation, and organizational policy.

19. **Radio talks on chiropractic**, CKCK Regina, documented in 1942.  
    Broadcast weekly; no complete episode list or recordings located.

20. **Haldeman Chiropractic Clinic bulletins**, Pretoria, 1950s.  
    Mentioned in professional journals; exact issue inventory unresolved.

### Works wrongly or loosely attributed

- Haldeman did **not** author the general theory of Technocracy.
- He did **not** found Technocracy Incorporated.
- He was a contributor to, not sole author of, Fay Goldie’s *Lost City of the Kalahari*.
- Modern reprints of his conspiracy pamphlets dated 2025 are posthumous editions, not original publications.

## Atlas Connections

Only connections supported by the reviewed sources are included. Mere ideological resemblance or shared geography is excluded.

### Elon Reeve Musk — documented family connection

- **Nature:** maternal grandfather and grandson.
- **Dates and places:** Elon Musk was born in Pretoria on 28 June 1971; Joshua Haldeman died on 13 January 1974, when Elon was two.
- **Evidence:** family biographies, Maye Musk’s memoir, and multiple genealogical and journalistic sources.
- **Influence:** Musk has described Haldeman as a risk-taking adventurer and said that “risk energized him.” Stories of Joshua’s flights and Kalahari expeditions were transmitted through Maye and Wyn.
- **Qualification:** a direct political teacher-student relationship is impossible to establish because of Elon’s age at Joshua’s death. Claims that Elon’s views constitute a direct continuation of Joshua’s Technocracy program are **interpretations or theories**, not documented instruction.

### George Orwell — no documented personal connection

Haldeman’s world-dictatorship rhetoric existed in a period shaped by *Nineteen Eighty-Four*, but no consulted source documented a meeting, correspondence, or explicit acknowledged influence from Orwell. Shared subject matter is insufficient to establish an Atlas connection.

### Paul Moritz Warburg — ideological target, not personal connection

Haldeman named **Paul Warburg** in his conspiracy writing as one of the financiers supposedly involved in international control.

- **Nature:** one-sided published accusation; no meeting or correspondence.
- **Date:** 1960 pamphlet.
- **Evidence label:** documented mention by Haldeman; underlying conspiracy allegation unsupported.
- **Qualification:** this is not a personal relationship and therefore does not constitute a normal biographical crossing. It is recorded because the atlas roster includes Paul Moritz Warburg and the named attack is explicit.

### Mayer Amschel Rothschild — dynastic myth, not a personal connection

Haldeman blamed the Rothschild family for revolutions, wars, political movements, and assassinations.

- **Nature:** conspiratorial historical attribution.
- **Date:** principally the 1960 pamphlet.
- **Evidence label:** documented claim by Haldeman; unsupported conspiracy theory.
- **Qualification:** Mayer Amschel Rothschild died in 1812, ninety years before Haldeman’s birth. No actual personal connection existed.

### Other roster members

No reliable evidence was found that Haldeman met, corresponded with, collaborated with, studied under, employed, opposed personally, or was directly influenced by any other member of the supplied atlas roster. Absence of additional crossings is therefore the present finding.
