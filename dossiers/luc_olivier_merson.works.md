## Works by Luc-Olivier Merson

### Paintings and independent painted compositions

- **1867:** *Leucothoé et Anaxandre*. Mythological painting exhibited at the Paris Salon; its present location is not established in the Salon database.
- **1868:** *Pénélope*. Painting exhibited at the Paris Salon; the title is documented, but the work is not securely located.
- **1869:** *Apollon exterminateur*. Homeric history painting exhibited at the Salon.
- **1869:** *Le Soldat de Marathon*. Prix de Rome painting, now at the École nationale supérieure des beaux-arts; it is a competition work as well as an independent history painting.
- **1871:** *Saint Edmond, roi d’Angleterre, martyr*. First-year Rome submission, now at the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Troyes.
- **1871:** *Le Sacrifice des poupées*. Oil painting now in the Musée d’arts de Nantes.
- **1872:** *Vision, légende du XIVe siècle*. Religious painting sent from Rome and exhibited at the 1873 Salon; some older records describe its legend as belonging to the fourth rather than the fourteenth century.
- **1873:** *La Dispute du Saint-Sacrement, d’après Raphaël*. Painted copy made as a Rome exercise. Surviving fragments and studies resist classification as a wholly independent invention by Merson because the composition is Raphael’s.
- **1873:** *Saint François et le loup d’Agubbio*. Rome-period treatment of the Franciscan legend, distinct from the better-known painting of 1877.
- **1875:** *Le Sacrifice à la Patrie*. Large commemorative painting concerning the dead of the Franco-Prussian War; once celebrated at the Salon, it is now considered lost.
- **1875:** *Saint Michel terrassant le dragon*. Oil painting now in the Musée Mandet, Riom.
- **1875–1876:** *Saint Louis, à son avènement au trône, fait ouvrir les geôles du royaume*. Monumental history painting made for the Galerie Saint-Louis of the Paris Palais de Justice.
- **1875–1876:** *Saint Louis, malgré les supplications des nobles et des barons, condamne le sire Enguerrand de Coucy (1259)*. Companion history painting for the Palais de Justice; the spelling “Concy” occurs in the Salon transcription, but “Coucy” is the historically correct and commonly used title.
- **1877:** *Le Loup de Gubbio* (also catalogued as *Le Loup d’Agubbio*). Oil painting dated 1877 and exhibited in 1878, now at the Palais des Beaux-Arts de Lille.
- **1878:** *Saint Isidore, laboureur*. Religious oil painting, now at the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Rouen.
- **1879:** *Le Repos en Égypte*. First version of the Holy Family composition, exhibited at the Salon of 1879 and now in the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston.
- **1880:** *Le Repos pendant la fuite en Égypte*. A second version of the composition, now at the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Nice; sources frequently confuse its date and identity with the Boston painting.
- **1881:** *Saint François d’Assise prêche aux poissons*. Religious painting now in the Musée d’arts de Nantes; museum and older sources differ between 1881 and 1886 because they variously date its conception, exhibition, or acquisition.
- **1881:** *L’Homme et la Fortune*. Painting exhibited at the Salon; it should not be confused with Merson’s later decorative allegory *La Fortune*.
- **1881:** *L’Annonciation*. Salon painting; Merson returned repeatedly to this subject, so the title alone does not identify it with his later versions.
- **1883:** *Angelo pittore*. Oil painting dated 1883 and exhibited at the 1884 Salon; long recorded as unlocated, it reappeared on the art market in 2026.
- **1884:** *Le Jugement de Pâris*. Painting exhibited at the Salon, also recorded under the variant title *L’Amour au jugement de Pâris*.
- **1885:** *L’Arrivée à Bethléem*. Religious painting exhibited at the Salon; another work or version bearing the same title appears in provincial Salon records.
- **1885:** *L’Or du Rhin*. A cycle of Wagnerian watercolours or drawings exhibited in two frames; individual sheets, including *Alberich et les filles du Rhin*, survive, so the cycle is simultaneously an ensemble and a group of separable works.
- **1885:** *Alberich et les filles du Rhin*. Signed and dated watercolour belonging to the *Or du Rhin* cycle; its identification with a sheet in the 1921 retrospective is probable rather than certain.
- **c. 1888:** *Saint Louis entre l’Église et saint Thomas d’Aquin*. Oil painting associated with a decoration for the church of Saint-Thomas-d’Aquin, now at the Musée d’Orsay.
- **c. 1889:** *Orphée*. Independent painted or drawn composition; the surviving work has been dated approximately rather than by a secure contemporary inscription.
- **1889:** *À l’ombre d’Isis*. Egyptian religious fantasy conceived as a sequel or pendant to *Le Repos en Égypte*.
- **1890:** *Adoration des bergers*. Small oil painting on panel, signed and dated.
- **1890:** *La Salutation angélique*. Religious painting now at the Musée des Beaux-Arts de Bordeaux.
- **1890:** *L’Annonciation*. Salon painting; because Merson produced several Annunciations, it cannot safely be identified with every work carrying this title.
- **1892:** *Mortes ?*. Painting exhibited at the Salon; its interrogative title is original and should not be normalized.
- **1895:** *Entrée de Jeanne d’Arc à Orléans*. Ink-and-wash composition now in the Musée des Beaux-Arts d’Orléans.
- **date uncertain:** *Jeanne d’Arc entendant les voix*. Drawing now in the National Gallery of Art, Washington; it formed part of a group in the 1921 retrospective rather than being catalogued there as an unequivocally autonomous painting.
- **date uncertain:** *L’Ermite*. Painting recorded in a Salon catalogue; no secure date or present location was established from the consulted sources.
- **date uncertain:** *Les Vierges mères*. Egyptianizing composition related to *Le Repos en Égypte* and *À l’ombre d’Isis*; long treated as lost, and its exact date and status remain uncertain.
- **1901:** *La Vérité*. Allegorical oil painting now at the Musée d’Orsay.
- **1901:** *La Famille*. Allegorical oil on canvas mounted on plaster, now at the Musée d’Orsay.
- **1901:** *La Fortune*. Allegorical oil painting now at the Musée d’Orsay; distinct from *L’Homme et la Fortune*.
- **1903:** *Une larme pour une goutte d’eau* (also described as *Esmeralda donnant à boire à Quasimodo*). Large painting derived from Merson’s earlier illustration for *Notre-Dame de Paris*, commissioned for the Maison de Victor Hugo.
- **1908:** *L’Annonciation*. Oil painting now in the Musée Thomas-Henry, Cherbourg; it is one of several distinct works by Merson with this title.
- **date unknown:** *Après la bataille*. Drawing dedicated to the painter Albert-Pierre-René Dawant; the surviving sheet bears no securely established date.

### Monumental decorations and architectural ensembles

- **1875–1876:** *Décor de la Galerie Saint-Louis du Palais de Justice*. Two-painting public commission comprising Saint Louis freeing prisoners and Saint Louis condemning Enguerrand de Coucy; the ensemble is an architectural decoration rather than a single portable picture.
- **1883:** *Le Serment du Jeu de paume*. Reconstruction of Jacques-Louis David’s unfinished composition for the restored Salle du Jeu de paume at Versailles. Merson interpreted David’s wash study and Jazet’s engraving, so this is neither wholly an original Merson composition nor a mechanically faithful copy.
- **1888:** *La Physique*. Decorative panel made for the Argentine Republic pavilion at the 1889 Exposition universelle; formerly in the Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes, Buenos Aires, and no longer recorded there after 1932.
- **1888:** *La Chimie*. Companion panel to *La Physique*, likewise made for the Argentine pavilion and subsequently removed from the Buenos Aires museum record.
- **1888–1920:** *Décor de l’escalier des Fêtes de l’Hôtel de Ville de Paris*. Vast project led by Merson with ornamental collaboration from Adolphe Giraldon. Conceived as panels, vault figures, and ceiling allegories, it remained unfinished at Merson’s death; here the project—not a completed Gesamtkunstwerk—is the work.
- **1888–1898:** *La Danse*. Painted allegory and surviving preparatory design for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *La Musique*. Painted allegory for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *Le Chant*. Painted allegory for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *Les Fruits*. Projected allegorical panel for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *La Chasse*. Projected allegorical panel for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *Les Fleurs*. Projected allegorical panel for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *La Toilette féminine*. Projected allegorical panel for the Hôtel de Ville project.
- **1888–1898:** *L’Éclairage et les Rafraîchissements*. Projected element of the Hôtel de Ville decoration; contemporary descriptions are not wholly consistent about whether these constituted one panel or two associated allegories.
- **1888–1898:** *Les Fêtes de la vie parisienne*. Project for eight compartmented ceilings at the Hôtel de Ville, including the National Holiday, horse show, Salon opening, and gingerbread fair. As the decoration remained incomplete, this corpus resists division into securely finished individual works.
- **c. 1890:** *Décor du cabinet du recteur de la Sorbonne*. Public mural project; studies survive, but the consulted records do not supply a stable collective title for the decoration.
- **1895–1896:** *Décor de la crypte de Louis Pasteur*. Neo-Byzantine mosaic program designed by Merson and executed by Auguste Guilbert-Martin. It combines ornamental inscriptions with images of Pasteur’s work on fermentation, wine, silkworms, poultry cholera, and rabies and is best understood as an architectural Gesamtkunstwerk shared by painter, mosaicist, and architect Charles Girault.
- **1897–1898:** *Le Chant au Moyen Âge*. Mural painting for the grand staircase of the Opéra-Comique.
- **1897–1898:** *La Poésie*. Mural painting for the Opéra-Comique.
- **1897–1898:** *La Musique*. Cartoon and mural component of the Opéra-Comique decoration; a signed and dated 1897 cartoon survives in the Louvre.
- **1897–1898:** *La Chanson, l’Élégie et l’Hymne en triomphe*. Ceiling composition at the Opéra-Comique.
- **1911–1923:** *Le Triomphe du Sacré-Cœur de Jésus* (also called *Christ en gloire*). Design for the enormous apse mosaic of Sacré-Cœur, Montmartre, developed under Merson and completed after his death by Henri-Marcel Magne and the Guilbert-Martin workshop. The dates 1918–1922 or 1923 refer to execution and completion, not solely to Merson’s act of design, making sole authorship and dating misleading.

### Stained glass and designs for glass

- **1882:** *Vanderbilt window for the Petit Château*. Stained-glass project designed by Merson and fabricated by Eugène-Stanislas Oudinot for William K. Vanderbilt’s New York residence, demolished in 1926.
- **1882–1885:** *La Danse des fiançailles*. Stained-glass composition designed by Merson and executed by Oudinot; the surviving triptych is in the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Sources variously date the design, manufacture, and exhibition between 1882 and 1885.
- **1885:** *Les Pèlerins d’Emmaüs*. Cartoon for stained glass exhibited at the Salon; the cartoon is the documented Merson work even where fabrication of a corresponding window is uncertain.
- **c. 1890:** *Belmont Memorial Chapel windows*. Program of chancel and nave windows in Newport, Rhode Island. Merson supplied designs, while Oudinot’s workshop and, after 1890, Félix Gaudin executed the glass; it is therefore a collaborative decorative ensemble rather than wholly autograph glasswork.
- **date uncertain:** *Michel-Ange*. Design for an Oudinot stained-glass window associated with Merson’s American commissions.
- **date uncertain:** *Léonard de Vinci*. Companion stained-glass design; surviving documentation makes the collaboration with Oudinot clearer than the precise date and destination.

### Tapestry cartoons and woven manifestations

- **c. 1888–1893:** *L’Art*. Painted model for the woven decoration of the administrator’s vestibule at the Bibliothèque nationale.
- **c. 1888–1893:** *La Poésie*. Cartoon and Savonnerie wall tapestry for the Bibliothèque nationale ensemble. The tapestry was woven by the state manufactory after Merson’s design, so the textile is a manifestation collaboratively made by named and unnamed weavers.
- **c. 1888–1893:** *La Musique*. Cartoon and woven panel belonging to the same Bibliothèque nationale decorative ensemble.
- **c. 1888–1893:** *Saint Michel*. Painted model subsequently translated into a high-warp Gobelins tapestry; the cartoon and textile are related but materially distinct objects.
- **date uncertain:** *Tenture de la légende de saint Anatole*. Fourteen-piece tapestry project attributed in the Gobelins documentation to Merson. Its scale makes it a body of linked designs, while the degree of completion of every projected piece is not clear from the consulted catalogue.

### Book-illustration obras and graphic cycles

- **1878:** *La Vie de sainte Élisabeth de Hongrie*. Merson’s first substantial published book-illustration project, made for Charles de Montalembert’s text and issued by Mame; the obra is the suite of compositions, not Montalembert’s text or any particular later binding.
- **1888–1889:** *Notre-Dame de Paris*. Extensive illustration cycle for Victor Hugo’s novel, engraved principally by Adolphe-Alphonse Géry-Bichard for the Édition nationale. Twelve original full-page drawings survive together in a presentation copy, but the printed book and Merson’s drawings are distinct manifestations.
- **1895:** *La Légende de saint Julien l’Hospitalier*. Suite of twenty-six etched plates after Merson’s drawings for Gustave Flaubert’s tale, engraved by Géry-Bichard.
- **1895:** *Trois contes*. Illustrated edition of Flaubert’s three stories incorporating Merson’s *Saint Julien* cycle; it is a collaborative book-object rather than a text authored by Merson.
- **date uncertain:** *Représentation du mystère de Noël au XVe siècle*. Cycle of nine drawings made for *Harper’s Magazine* and exhibited together; Salon records alternately describe it as a medieval mystery and a “mystère de Noël.”
- **date uncertain:** *L’Imagier*. Ten documented drawings for Jules Lemaître’s text, exhibited in two groups of four and six. The cycle is Merson’s graphic obra, not Lemaître’s literary work.
- **1903:** *Chronique du temps qui fut la Jacquerie*. Illustration suite for Léon Hennique’s text, engraved by Carlo Chessa.
- **1907:** *Les Trophées*. Fifty-composition illustration cycle for José-Maria de Heredia’s sonnets, engraved in etching by Léopold Flameng; authorship of text, drawings, and printed plates is deliberately distributed among poet, designer, and engraver.
- **1907:** *La Légende de Jaccopo Luna*. Periodical illustration cycle for Ivan Strannik’s story in *L’Illustration*; Merson made images, not the literary text.
- **1908:** *Le Bois sacré*. Illustrations for Edmond Rostand’s work in *L’Illustration*.
- **1911:** *Les Nuits*. Illustration suite for Alfred de Musset’s poems, engraved by Carlo Chessa and published alongside a separate suite by Adolphe Giraldon and Ernest Florian.
- **date uncertain:** *La Mosaïque*. Body of periodical illustrations rather than a single fixed work; without a complete issue-by-issue inventory it resists treatment as one autonomous obra.
- **date uncertain:** *Revue illustrée*. Body of contributions to the periodical; it likewise resists classification as a single work.
- **date uncertain:** *L’Estampe moderne*. Graphic contribution or contributions documented under Merson’s name; the consulted retrospective sources do not establish a complete title-level inventory.
- **date uncertain:** *Paris illustré*. Illustrated cover design documented in contemporary writing on French book decoration.

### Postal, monetary, and reproducible designs

- **1900:** *Type Merson*. Bicolour definitive postage-stamp design first issued by France in several denominations and subsequently adapted through new values, colours, and colonial or foreign-office overprints. These numerous stamps are manifestations of one reproducible matrix rather than separate original compositions by Merson.
- **c. 1900:** *Monaco definitive stamp designs*. Postal designs made for Monaco; catalogue traditions group them with Merson’s French stamp work, but the individual issues and later overprints should not be mistaken for newly authored works.
- **1901:** *Carte no 7 de la Collection des Cent*. Colour-postcard composition inaugurating Merson’s participation in the *Collection des Cent*; the commercially printed cards are manifestations of his design.
- **1906–1908:** *100 francs Luc-Olivier Merson*. Polychrome banknote design commissioned by the Banque de France, approved in 1908 and circulated from 1910. Engravers and printers translated Merson’s model, so the issued note is a collaborative mechanical manifestation.
- **1906–1927:** *50 francs Luc-Olivier Merson*. Banknote project based on a pre-war Merson model but issued only after his death, in 1928. His heirs successfully argued that the Bank’s altered printed note mutilated the original design; attribution and completeness are therefore explicitly disputed.
- **from 1906:** *500-franc banknote project*. Third denomination commissioned from Merson by the Banque de France but not issued in his lifetime. It is a project in the strict sense: the intention and models constitute the work even though no circulating note resulted.
