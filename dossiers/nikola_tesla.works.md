### Patents outside the United States and the limits of the patent corpus

- **1888–1928:** *International patent families*. Tesla obtained patents in at least 27 countries, but most repeat inventions protected by the US and British “basic patents”; the Museum currently identifies at least 311 grants, 116 basic patents, and 125 protected inventions. The totals remain unstable: the Museum’s English page contains an internal inconsistency (“119” US basic patents plus seven British ones), while its Serbian page gives 109 US plus seven British, and scholarship explicitly describes the catalogue as still developing.
- **1880s–1920s:** *Unsuccessful and unfiled patent projects*. The Museum archive contains records of roughly 33 unsuccessful American applications and additional applications prepared but never filed. This is an incomplete, partly unpublished project corpus and cannot honestly be converted into a definitive list of independent works.
- **1880s–1930s:** *Unpatented inventions*. Tesla also devised apparatus and procedures for which he sought no patent, including medical applications of high-frequency current. They resist item-by-item enumeration because the surviving archive has not been published as a complete critical catalogue.

### Lectures and experimental manifestations

A lecture here is both text and event: demonstrations, apparatus, light, sound, and bodily performance often make it closer to a technical Gesamtkunstwerk than to a conventional publication.

- **1888:** *A New System of Alternate Current Motors and Transformers*. Lecture and demonstration delivered to the American Institute of Electrical Engineers in New York on May 16.
- **1891:** *Experiments with Alternate Currents of Very High Frequency and Their Application to Methods of Artificial Illumination*. Lecture-demonstration delivered at Columbia College on May 20.
- **1892:** *Experiments with Alternate Currents of High Potential and High Frequency*. Lecture-demonstration delivered in London on February 3 and 4 and subsequently repeated in Paris.
- **1893:** *On Light and Other High Frequency Phenomena*. Lecture-demonstration given at the Franklin Institute on February 24 and the National Electric Light Association on March 1.
- **1893:** *On Mechanical and Electrical Oscillators*. Chicago World’s Fair lecture-demonstration of August 25; it was not published, its manuscript does not survive, and knowledge of it depends on contemporary reporting.
- **1897:** *High-Frequency Oscillators and Controls for Electric Circuits*. Lecture-demonstration delivered to the New York Academy of Sciences on April 6.
- **1898:** *High Frequency Oscillators for Electrotherapeutic and Other Purposes*. Lecture delivered to the Electro-Therapeutic Society in Buffalo on September 13.
- **1899:** *On the Art of Teleautomatics*. Chicago Business Club lecture of May 13; its surviving archival text is incomplete and unfinished.
- **1901:** *The Phenomenon of High Frequencies*. Experimental manifestation staged in Columbia University’s Havemeyer Hall on April 12.
- **1911:** *New Inventions by Tesla*. Lecture delivered to the New York branch of the National Electric Light Association on May 15.

### Laboratories, installations, and unfinished systems

- **1899–1900:** *Colorado Springs experimental station*. A purpose-built laboratory and experimental manifestation for high-voltage, high-frequency, and wireless-transmission research. Its surviving record includes Tesla’s experimental diary; the installation itself was dismantled, so it resists reduction to either a building or a text.
- **1901–1917:** *Wardenclyffe wireless station and tower*. An unfinished technological and architectural project intended first for communications and, in Tesla’s enlarged conception, worldwide transmission of information and power. The system never entered its projected operation, and the tower was demolished in 1917; its incompletion is part of the obra.
- **1898:** *Radio-controlled boat or “teleautomaton”*. A working experimental object publicly demonstrated at Madison Square Garden and embodied legally in US 613,809. It is simultaneously vessel, control system, performance, and military project.
- **1906:** *Tesla oscillator demonstration at the Long Island station*. A mechanical/electrical manifestation reported as a bladeless turbine or oscillator demonstration; surviving accounts do not justify treating every later anecdote about it as a separate invention.
- **1909–1913 and after:** *Tesla turbine and pump project*. A family of prototypes, demonstrations, licenses, and patents rather than one stable object. US 1,061,142 and 1,061,206 are its clearest fixed textual manifestations.
- **1913–1914:** *Tesla fountain*. A patented hydraulic and illuminated-display project. The patent establishes the design, but should not be confused with every later fountain built in Tesla’s name.
- **1921–1928:** *Vertical-takeoff aerial-transportation project*. An aircraft intention preserved chiefly through paired method and apparatus patents; the catalogue does not establish a completed flying machine.
- **1930s:** *Teleforce or “peace ray”*. A proposed defensive particle-beam system manifested in technical papers, negotiations, and press statements. It remained a project rather than a demonstrated operational weapon, and sensational later claims should not be mistaken for completed apparatus.

### Archive as resistant corpus

- **1856–1943:** *Nikola Tesla personal archive*. The Nikola Tesla Museum records 163,911 archival call numbers encompassing manuscripts, calculations, diagrams, plans, drawings, correspondence, photographs, patent documentation, and ephemera. This is a body of work, not 163,911 independently authored obras: it includes received documents, duplicates, administrative matter, and material by other people.
- **Dates various:** *Plans and drawings*. The Museum maintains this large corpus separately from its “Activity” records because of its physical form and conservation needs. No complete public item-level critical catalogue currently permits an honest claim to have named every drawing.
- **Dates various:** *Scientific and technical working papers*. The Museum’s “Activity” division contains 45,575 records arranged under patents, strong-current technology, high-frequency technology, mechanical engineering, turbines, aviation, telemechanics, oscillators, lighting, fountains, optics, mathematics, physics, chemistry, and other subjects. These are process remains as much as finished works, and many resist the status of titled, dated obra.
