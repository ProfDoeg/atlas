## Works by Silvina Ocampo

### Story collections

- **1937:** *Viaje olvidado*. Ocampo’s first book: a collection of short, formally unstable narratives that transform childhood memories rather than recording them autobiographically. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1948:** *Autobiografía de Irene*. Five long stories, including the title novella; despite its title, this is fiction rather than autobiography. ([ALADA catalogue](https://www.alada.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Catalogo-Alberto-Casares-ALADA-Abril-2023.pdf))
- **1959:** *La furia y otros cuentos*. Collection of fantastic and psychologically violent stories, frequently shortened in citations to *La furia*. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1961:** *Las invitadas*. Story collection; its original book form included illustrations by Norah Borges. ([La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/cultura/norah-borges-ilustradora-nid215752/))
- **1970:** *Los días de la noche*. Story collection. ([ALADA catalogue](https://www.alada.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Catalogo-Alberto-Casares-ALADA-Abril-2023.pdf))
- **1987:** *Y así sucesivamente*. Ocampo’s penultimate collection of stories published during her lifetime. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1988:** *Cornelia frente al espejo*. Her last lifetime story collection; the title text approaches the dimensions and discontinuous form of a novella. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))

### Poetry

- **1942:** *Enumeración de la patria y otros poemas*. Ocampo’s first poetry collection. The shorter form *Enumeración de la patria* is also commonly used bibliographically. ([Argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/389889))
- **1945:** *Espacios métricos*. Poetry collection dominated by measured and traditional verse forms. ([Argentina.gob.ar](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/389889))
- **1946 or 1948:** *Los sonetos del jardín*. A sequence of garden sonnets issued as a small book. Bibliographies disagree over its publication year, giving 1946 or 1948, so the date should not be silently normalized. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf), [Russian National Library-derived chronology](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BF%D0%BE%2C_%D0%A1%D0%B8%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B2%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B0))
- **1949:** *Poemas de amor desesperado*. Poetry collection whose lyric and visual imagination retains Ocampo’s earlier practice as a painter. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1953:** *Los nombres*. Poetry collection, later recognized with Argentina’s National Poetry Prize. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1962:** *Lo amargo por dulce*. Poetry collection; catalogues sometimes misstate the title as *Lo amargo por lo dulce*. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1972:** *Amarillo celeste*. Poetry collection whose title itself joins ostensibly incompatible colors. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1973:** *Cinco poemas*. A privately printed, extremely small-format publishing project rather than an ordinary trade book; copies were accompanied by an original artwork by Norah Borges. ([El Enamorado rare-book description](https://elenamorado.com/producto/cinco-poemas/))
- **1979:** *Canto escolar*. Poetry collection. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1984:** *Breve santoral*. A poem-cycle about saints, materially conceived as an illustrated book with twelve drawings by Norah Borges and a prologue by Jorge Luis Borges; sources sometimes date it 1985, but the volume itself bears 1984. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes catalogue](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Publicaciones/Norah_Borges_cat_ok2.pdf), [bibliographic description](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA67795144))
- **Written principally before 1993; published 2001:** *Poesía inédita y dispersa*. A posthumous selection by Noemí Ulla of Ocampo’s unpublished and periodical verse. It is an archival manifestation assembled after Ocampo’s death, not a book whose final architecture she authorized. ([UNLP study and bibliographical note](https://sedici.unlp.edu.ar/bitstream/handle/10915/45676/Documento_completo__.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1))
- **Written before 1993; collected 2002 and expanded 2025:** *Poesía completa*. The collected-poetry project incorporates lifetime books, dispersed poems, translations, and previously unpublished verse; its successive forms are editorial manifestations rather than a single composition finalized by Ocampo. ([2025 publisher dossier](https://proapi.planetadelibros.com.ar/descargas/sala-prensa-libro/sinopsis-y-biografia/428228/poesia-completa.pdf?_locale=ar))

### Children’s narratives and cross-age fictions

- **Written from approximately the mid-1950s; published 1972:** *El caballo alado*. A collection of stories addressed to younger readers, although Ocampo’s unsettling transformations resist a firm division between children’s and adult literature. ([University of Rosario study](https://rephip.unr.edu.ar/bitstreams/7ed0ce8b-aabd-4ca7-bbf5-1a7087b34b0d/download))
- **Written partly in the 1950s; published 1974:** *El cofre volante*. Children’s story collection assembled from a longer period of writing and revision. ([University of Rosario study](https://rephip.unr.edu.ar/bitstreams/7ed0ce8b-aabd-4ca7-bbf5-1a7087b34b0d/download))
- **Written partly in the 1950s; published 1975:** *El tobogán*. Children’s story collection whose publication belongs to Ocampo’s 1970s recovery of earlier writing for children. ([University of Rosario study](https://rephip.unr.edu.ar/bitstreams/7ed0ce8b-aabd-4ca7-bbf5-1a7087b34b0d/download))
- **1977:** *La naranja maravillosa: cuentos para chicos grandes y para grandes chicos*. Collection deliberately presented to both children and adults, making resistance to a single age category part of the work. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **Begun around the mid-1950s; published 1986:** *La torre sin fin*. A children’s fantasy novel or *nouvelle*, first published in Spain during Ocampo’s lifetime and only published in Argentina posthumously in 2007. ([University of Rosario study](https://rephip.unr.edu.ar/bitstreams/7ed0ce8b-aabd-4ca7-bbf5-1a7087b34b0d/download))

### Novels and novellas

- **1946:** *Los que aman, odian*. Detective novel made collaboratively with Adolfo Bioy Casares; some summary bibliographies give 1945, but the documented first book edition is generally dated 1946. Its divided authorship should be preserved rather than assigned to either writer alone. ([Notre Dame Special Collections](https://rarebooks.library.nd.edu/collections/latin_american/south_american/southern_cone_lit/ocampo_s.html), [Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **Dates uncertain; published 2006:** *El vidente*. An unpublished novella recovered within *Las repeticiones y otros relatos inéditos*; it survives as a posthumously edited work rather than a lifetime book. ([Study of Ocampo’s posthumous books](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/321/3214978003/))
- **Dates uncertain; published 2006:** *Lo mejor de la familia*. A second posthumously recovered novella included in *Las repeticiones y otros relatos inéditos*. Its autonomous status is textual, while its first public manifestation was inside Montequin’s collection. ([Study of Ocampo’s posthumous books](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/321/3214978003/))
- **Begun circa 1963; substantially completed 1988–1989; published 2011:** *La promesa*. A novel Ocampo repeatedly abandoned, resumed, and rewrote; illness left uncertainty around absolute finality, and Ernesto Montequin established the posthumous edition. It is therefore both an obra and a long, possibly not definitively closed project. ([Study of Ocampo’s posthumous books](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/321/3214978003/))

### Theatre

- **1956:** *Los traidores*. A dramatic work in verse written jointly with Juan Rodolfo Wilcock. It is a play-text—not a story collection—and its collaborative authorship is integral to its identity. ([Notre Dame Special Collections](https://rarebooks.library.nd.edu/collections/latin_american/south_american/southern_cone_lit/ocampo_s.html))
- **Dates and titles not yet securely established:** *Unpublished dramatic works*. Scholarship on the archive reports additional plays awaiting publication, but the accessible bibliographical record does not yet establish a complete, authorized list of titles or dates; these remain projects or archival texts rather than safely nameable public works. ([CONICET-hosted study](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/163219/CONICET_Digital_Nro.8492b782-da2f-4c5e-bb8c-9f9c4857fabf_A.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2))

### Anthologies made as editorial works

- **1940:** *Antología de la literatura fantástica*. An anthology compiled collaboratively by Jorge Luis Borges, Adolfo Bioy Casares, and Ocampo. Its obra is the selection and arrangement of other writers’ texts, not authorship of those texts; later editions substantially expanded this editorial construction. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/silvina-ocampo/))
- **1941:** *Antología poética argentina*. Collaborative anthology made by Borges, Bioy Casares, and Ocampo; again, the work attributable to the three is curatorial selection and ordering. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/silvina-ocampo/))
- **1954:** *Pequeña antología*. A compact selection of Ocampo’s own poetry and thus an act of self-curation as well as a book. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1966:** *El pecado mortal*. A selected collection of Ocampo’s stories with an introduction by José Bianco; it is a retrospective manifestation, not a wholly new story cycle. ([Bibliografía de Silvina Ocampo](https://es.scribd.com/document/238547499/Bibliografia-Sobre-Silvina-Ocampo))
- **1970:** *Informe del cielo y del infierno*. A story anthology selected by Edgardo Cozarinsky. The component stories are Ocampo’s obras, but the book’s particular architecture belongs partly to its editor. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1981:** *“La continuación” y otras páginas*. Noemí Ulla’s anthology of Ocampo’s writing; it is an editorial manifestation rather than a new unitary work conceived by Ocampo. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1984:** *Páginas de Silvina Ocampo seleccionadas por la autora*. A self-selected anthology with a prologue by Enrique Pezzoni; the authorial selection makes the book a retrospective self-portrait as well as a republication. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **1991:** *Las reglas del secreto*. A broad retrospective anthology selected and organized by Matilde Sánchez. Its contents are Ocampo’s, but the collected object is partly Sánchez’s editorial construction. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))

### Hybrid image–text books

- **1979:** *Árboles de Buenos Aires*. A collaborative image–text object combining Ocampo’s poems with Aldo Sessa’s photographs. It approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk at book scale because neither the verbal nor photographic sequence adequately describes the whole. ([ALADA catalogue](https://www.alada.org.ar/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Catalogo-Alberto-Casares-ALADA-Abril-2023.pdf))
- **1984:** *Breve santoral*. In addition to being a poem-cycle, this is a deliberately integrated verbal and visual book made through the conjunction of Ocampo’s poems, Norah Borges’s drawings, Jorge Luis Borges’s prologue, and graphic design; authorship of the total object is consequently distributed. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes catalogue](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Publicaciones/Norah_Borges_cat_ok2.pdf))

### Translation and linguistic recreation

- **Dates dispersed; collected in later editions:** *Translations of English-language lyric poetry*. Ocampo translated English-language poets for periodicals, notably a group published in *Sur* in 1947; because translation is a remaking rather than original sole authorship, this corpus resists classification as either simply “hers” or “not hers.” The later *Poetas líricos en lengua inglesa* presents this activity through an anthology and preliminary study, while the 2025 *Poesía completa* incorporates the documented translations. ([2025 publisher dossier](https://proapi.planetadelibros.com.ar/descargas/sala-prensa-libro/sinopsis-y-biografia/428228/poesia-completa.pdf?_locale=ar), [contemporary notice of the anthology](https://www.bibliotecanacionaldigital.gob.cl/colecciones/BND/00/RC/RC0136136.pdf))

### Conversation, recollection, and autobiographical constructions

- **1982:** *Encuentros con Silvina Ocampo*. A book of conversations made with Noemí Ulla. Ocampo is a speaking participant rather than its sole author, so it sits between interview document, dialogic creation, and editorial construction. ([Biblioteca del Congreso de la Nación](https://bcn.gob.ar/uploads/Compilacion-bibliografica-silvina-ocampo.pdf))
- **Written over many years; published 2006:** *Invenciones del recuerdo*. A posthumous autobiographical sequence in free verse. Its remembered life is repeatedly fictionalized, so it is neither conventional memoir nor simply a poetry collection; Montequin established its book form from the archive. ([SciELO critical study](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?pid=S0718-71812013000200016&script=sci_arttext), [study of the posthumous books](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/321/3214978003/))
- **Written before 1993; published 2014:** *El dibujo del tiempo: recuerdos, prólogos, entrevistas*. Posthumous gathering of recollections, essays, prologues, interviews, and previously unpublished material. It is best understood as an editorial montage of distinct manifestations rather than a single book Ocampo herself completed. ([Study of Ocampo’s posthumous books](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/321/3214978003/))

### Fragmentary and posthumously assembled writing

- **Written before 1993; published 2006:** *Las repeticiones y otros relatos inéditos*. Montequin’s posthumous assembly of mostly unpublished stories and two novellas. The texts are Ocampo’s, but the selection, classification, sequence, and identity of the volume are the editor’s archival montage; its completeness is not equivalent to a final collection authorized by her. ([CiNii bibliographic record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA84559079), [study of the posthumous books](https://portal.amelica.org/ameli/journal/321/3214978003/))
- **Written chiefly during periods of insomnia before 1993; published 2008:** *Ejércitos de la oscuridad*. A deliberately fragmentary constellation of notes, miniatures, memories, dreams, and observations. Ocampo mentioned an unpublished book of notes during her lifetime, but Montequin determined the surviving book’s arrangement; it therefore lies between an authorial project and an editor-created manifestation. ([Penguin’s editorial description](https://www.penguinlibros.com/ar/literatura-contemporanea/328552-ebook-ejercitos-de-la-oscuridad-9788426481627/fragmento), [La Nación review](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/cultura/digresiones-del-insomnio-nid994699/))

### Drawing, painting, illustration, and photography

- **Childhood–circa 1930s, with later examples:** *Drawings and paintings*. Ocampo trained in Paris with Giorgio de Chirico and Fernand Léger and made visual art before becoming publicly identified as a writer. No catalogue raisonné or ordered visual archive survives, and known drawings are dispersed in private hands; consequently this body of work cannot responsibly be converted into a complete list of invented titles and dates. ([Study of Ocampo’s visual practice](https://eprints.rclis.org/44807/), [account of the dispersed works](https://www.juanjoseflores.com.ar/2018/07/dibujos-y-fotografias-de-silvina-ocampo.html))
- **Dates dispersed:** *Portrait drawings*. Drawings in multiple media and from different periods have been exhibited collectively as *Silvina Ocampo: el arte del retrato*. The exhibition confirms the body of work but does not establish that phrase as Ocampo’s title for a unified series. ([Exhibition notice](https://www.juanjoseflores.com.ar/2018/07/dibujos-y-fotografias-de-silvina-ocampo.html))
- **Dates dispersed:** *Photographs*. Ocampo also made photographs, including portraits, but the surviving photographic practice has not been published as a complete authorial corpus and individual works generally lack secure public titles and dates. It resists treatment as a closed oeuvre. ([Exhibition notice](https://www.juanjoseflores.com.ar/2018/07/dibujos-y-fotografias-de-silvina-ocampo.html))
- **Dates dispersed:** *Periodical illustrations*. Ocampo worked not only as a writer and translator but also as an illustrator in periodicals for different audiences. This is a genuine field of making, although the available scholarship describes its circulation without supplying a complete catalogue raisonné of individual images. ([María de los Ángeles Mascioto, “Silvina Ocampo: visualidad de la escritura entre revistas y libros”](https://eprints.rclis.org/44807/))

### Musical manifestations of her texts

- **1943:** *Las horas de una estancia*. A song cycle for voice and piano composed by Alberto Ginastera to five poems by Ocampo. The words are Ocampo’s creation and the musical setting Ginastera’s; the resulting song-cycle is therefore a collaborative manifestation rather than a composition by Ocampo alone. ([CiNii author bibliography](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/author/DA0373566X))
