## Works by Vladimir Medem

### Appeals and manifestos

- **1900:** *Appeal to Christian workers for May Day* (fixed title not recorded). A Russian-language political appeal commissioned by the Bund and signed with the pseudonyms “Mark” and “M. Vinitski”; as an intervention meant to mobilize workers, it is better classified as a manifesto than as an essay. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

### Political and theoretical pamphlets

- **1903–1906:** *Di Sotsyal-Demokratye un di Natsyonale Frage* (*Social Democracy and the National Question*; Russian title *Sotsialdemokratiia i natsionalʹnyi vopros*). A theoretical pamphlet establishing national-cultural autonomy as a basis of the Bund’s nationality program. The underlying Russian articles appeared in *Vestnik Bunda* in 1903; YIVO dates the influential pamphlet to 1904, while bibliographic records date Russian and Yiddish editions to 1906, so no single publication date describes the whole textual history. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Medem_Vladimir_Davidovich))

- **1903:** *Pamphlet on the Sixth Zionist Congress* (title not recovered). A Bund Foreign Committee pamphlet reporting and interpreting the Basel congress; the standard biographical bibliography attests the object but does not supply its title. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1905:** *Report on the Fifth Congress of the Bund* (fixed title not recovered). A jointly compiled party report by Medem and Mark Liber; it is a documentary and organizational text rather than Medem’s individually authored treatise. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1917:** *Uchreditelʹnoe sobranie i demokraticheskaia respublika* (*The Constituent Assembly and the Democratic Republic*). A Russian political pamphlet about democratic constitutional government; some catalogue-derived records give 1900, but the bibliographic record also gives 1917, and the dating should therefore be treated as unsettled pending inspection of the imprint. ([Open Library record](https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL137638A/Vladimir_Medem))

- **1918:** *O politicheskikh partiiakh* (*On Political Parties*). A Russian political-theory pamphlet published in Berlin; stray catalogue records assign an earlier date, but the identified Berlin imprint is dated 1918. ([WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/O-politicheskih-partiyah/oclc/64037454); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/browse?c=x&key=Political+parties&type=lcsubc))

- **By 1916; publication history uncertain:** *The Doctrine of the Bund*. A programmatic synthesis of Bundist national-cultural autonomy, sometimes treated as a separately titled work and sometimes as a formulation extracted from Medem’s broader political writing; its resistance to stable bibliographic identification should be preserved rather than disguised. ([Global Policy Archive](https://www.archive.globalpolicy.org/component/content/article/173-sovereign/30380.html))

### Essays and polemical articles

- **1900:** *Der Ershter Mai un der Sotsyalizm* (*May First and Socialism*). A Russian-language May Day article printed in the Brushmakers’ Association organ *Der Veker*, one of Medem’s first two contributions to the Jewish socialist press. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1900:** *Der Ershter Mai un der Politisher Kamf* (*May First and the Political Struggle*). The companion May Day article in *Der Veker*, combining political explanation with agitation. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1903:** *Two articles on the nationality question in Vestnik Bunda* (individual titles not supplied by the standard bibliography). These Russian essays became the textual basis of *Di Sotsyal-Demokratye un di Natsyonale Frage*; whether they are counted as two works or as an earlier manifestation of the pamphlet is intrinsically ambiguous. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1903:** *Article on Zionism in Arbayter Shtime* (title not recovered). An illegal-press polemic written after Medem attended the Sixth Zionist Congress; it is distinct from his pamphlet about that congress, although the bibliography does not provide its title. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1906–1907:** *Series on restoring the Bund’s relationship with the Russian Social Democratic Labour Party* (individual titles and number not recovered). Published in *Nashe Slovo* and *Nasha Tribuna*, this is an explicitly attested serial body of polemical writing but resists honest conversion into item-by-item “works” without the periodicals themselves. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1908–1910:** *Di Yidishe Kehile* (*The Jewish Community*). An essay in the second number of the Bundist collection *Tsaytfragen*, examining the Jewish communal institution as a possible vehicle of autonomy. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1908–1910:** *Natsyonalizm oder Naytralizm* (*Nationalism or Neutralism*). A theoretical essay in *Tsaytfragen*, nos. 3–4, articulating the much-disputed Bundist position of “neutralism”; Medem’s later cultural activity effectively repudiated that earlier position. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Date not independently established; collected 1917:** *Natsyonalizm, Asimilatsye un Sotsyal-Demokratye* (*Nationalism, Assimilation, and Social Democracy*). A political essay later incorporated into *Zikhroynes un Artiklen*; the consulted standard bibliography supplies no reliable first-publication date. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Date not independently established; collected 1917:** *Di Alveltlekhe Yidishe Natsye* (*The Universal Jewish Nation*). A theoretical essay conceiving Jewish nationhood across territorial borders. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Date not independently established; collected 1917:** *Natsyonalizm un Naytralizm* (*Nationalism and Neutralism*). A political essay whose title is close to, but bibliographically not identical with, *Natsyonalizm oder Naytralizm*; the sources do not establish whether these are wholly separate compositions, variants, or retitled manifestations. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Date not independently established; collected 1917:** *Tifer in Lebn* (*Deeper in Life*). Programmatic propositions on secular Jewish schooling and cultural work—part essay, part project for institutions that Medem subsequently helped build. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Date not independently established; collected 1917:** *Nokh a Mol, Mir un Undzer Natsyonalizm* (*Once Again, We and Our Nationalism*). A polemical reconsideration of Bundist nationalism. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Date not independently established; collected 1917:** *Yidish* (*Yiddish*). A programmatic essay on the social and national role of the Yiddish language. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1918:** *Der Goyrl fun der Rusisher Revolutsye* (*The Fate of the Russian Revolution*). An essay in *Unzer Shtime*, nos. 1–3, arguing that Bolshevik minority rule necessarily produced repression; it functions as both analysis and anti-authoritarian intervention. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1918:** *On Terror*. A September 1918 article in *Lebensfragen* attacking Bolshevik terror; surviving English republication is slightly abridged and is therefore a later manifestation, not the original text. ([Marxists Internet Archive scan](https://www.marxists.org/subject/jewish/medem-terror.pdf))

### Memoir and autobiographical obra

- **Written after 1913–1915 imprisonment; collected 1917:** *Turme-Zikhroynes* (*Prison Memoirs*). A prison memoir cast as a letter to a friend, making it simultaneously testimony, memoir, and epistolary text. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1917:** *Zikhroynes un Artiklen: Mitn Bild un Byografye fun Oytor* (*Memoirs and Articles: With a Portrait and Biography of the Author*). A composite volume containing Medem’s prison memoir and six political-cultural essays, together with a biography by Dovid Kasel; the volume as material obra is collaborative even though the seven principal texts are Medem’s. Some records give 1918, but the modern biographical lexicon identifies the Warsaw edition as 1917. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Medem_Vladimir_Davidovich))

- **1920:** *Fun Mayn Notits-Bukh* (*From My Notebook*). A 135-page collection of articles or notebook pieces; its form lies between miscellany, essay collection, and intellectual journal. The 1929 *Folks-Tsaytung* issue is a re-edition, not another work. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **Published 1923:** *Fun Mayn Lebn* (*From My Life*). A two-volume autobiography ending with Medem’s imprisonment and release in 1915; publication occurred in the year of his death under the Vladimir Medem Committee, with a foreword by Abraham Cahan, so the finished printed object contains editorial work not made by Medem himself. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923); [Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/medem-vladimir))

### Editorial and journalistic projects

- **1900:** *Der Minsker Arbayter* (*The Minsk Worker*). An illegal Bund newspaper made collectively by an editorial board that included Medem; it is not securely divisible into individually attributable Medem texts and should therefore be treated as a collaborative editorial project rather than his sole work. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1906–1907:** *Nashe Slovo* (*Our Word*). A Russian-language Bundist periodical on whose editorial board Medem served; the periodical is a collective manifestation, while his identifiable contribution was the series on relations with the RSDLP. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1906–1907:** *Nasha Tribuna* (*Our Tribune*). A Russian-language Bundist periodical co-edited by Medem; as with *Nashe Slovo*, collective editorial production prevents simple sole-author attribution. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1906–1907:** *Di Folks-Tsaytung* (*The People’s Newspaper*). A legal Bundist daily whose editorial board included Medem; this is an ongoing collective project, not a single authored text. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1906–1907:** *Di Hofnung* (*The Hope*). A Vilna Bundist publication co-edited by Medem, again resisting classification as an individually authored work. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1907:** *Der Morgenshtern* (*The Morning Star*). A Vilna Bundist weekly edited by Medem; the editorial shaping of a serial publication is the made thing here, not merely whichever unsigned articles may have been his. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1912:** *Di Lebensfragen* (*The Life Issues*). A legal Bundist weekly for which Medem was a leading member of the Vienna editorial board; police suppressed it after its second issue, making it an interrupted editorial project whose incompleteness is part of the work. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1912–1913:** *Di Tsayt* (*The Times*). A weekly with linked Vienna and St. Petersburg editorial boards, the former including Medem; it was a distributed collective project rather than a text attributable to him alone. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1915–1920:** *Lebensfragen*. The revived Bundist periodical, initially weekly and daily from late 1918, edited by Medem and transformed by him into a principal vehicle of Yiddish socialist argument; the newspaper as an evolving collective body is closer to an editorial obra than to a book. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1909–1923:** *Uncollected journalism and correspondence*. The bibliography records hundreds of contributions—often pseudonymous—to *Evreiskii Mir*, *Evreiskoie Obozrenie*, *Vestnik Evropy*, *Denʹ*, *Neue Zeit*, *Fraynd*, *Forverts*, *Tsukunft*, *Naye Velt*, and other papers. This corpus demonstrably exists, but it resists treatment as a finite titled “work”: the standard sources do not enumerate every article, and Medem used the signatures Mark, M. Vinitski, G. Raf, V-ki, Ben Dovid, V. M., and others, leaving completeness and sometimes attribution unresolved. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

### Institutional projects

- **1915–1920:** *The Yiddish secular-school campaign*. Medem helped lead the petition movement for Yiddish schools and served on the Dinezon–Medem–Raykhman Committee; this was a political-educational project made through petitions, curricula, organization, and negotiation rather than a bounded text. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

- **1919–1920:** *Unzere Kinder school and children’s-home network*. Medem participated in the organization’s attempt to construct a network of secular Jewish schools and homes. It is a collective social project, not an individually authored obra, and its institutional effects continued beyond his participation. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923))

### Posthumous literary bequest

- **Published 1943; composition dates various or unstated:** *Fun Medems Literarisher Yerushe* (*From Medem’s Literary Bequest*). Twelve Medem articles gathered posthumously in *Vladimir Medem: Tsum Tsvantsikstn Yortsayt*; the standard lexicon confirms the series but does not name all twelve, so the group is traceable while its item-level titles and dates remain unresolved. ([Congress for Jewish Culture](https://congressforjewishculture.org/people/2815/Medem-Vladimir_1879-1923); [Yiddish Book Center scan record](https://www.yiddishbookcenter.org/collections/yiddish-books/spb-nybc200784/medem-vladimir-vladimir-medem-tsum-tsvantsikstn-yortsayt))
