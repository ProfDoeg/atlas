## Works by Saint Mercurius of Caesarea

### No securely attested obras

- **Traditional lifetime c. 225–250; historicity uncertain:** No text, artifact, building, protocol, project, or other made thing is securely attributable to Mercurius. The standard Greek hagiographic dossier (BHG 1274–1277a) consists of anonymous Passions and narratives about him and a later encomium by Nikephoros Gregoras—not works by him; the extensive Coptic dossier likewise comprises Passions, encomia, and miracle collections produced by later, frequently anonymous or pseudonymous authors. Even Mercurius’s historical existence is disputed: he is absent from Eusebius, the major Cappadocian writers, and the early Graeco-Syriac martyrology, while his Caesarean cult is not securely attested before the later fifth century. His legendary military deeds, martyrdom, posthumous miracles, and alleged killing of Julian are narrated actions or manifestations rather than surviving obras and therefore are not catalogued here.
