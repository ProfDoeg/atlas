## Works by Marco Polo

### Collaborative geographical narrative

- **1298–1299, with possible later revisions:** *Devisement dou monde* (also transmitted as *Divisament dou monde*, *Livre de Marco Polo*, *Il Milione*, and *The Travels of Marco Polo*). A prose description of peoples, places, courts, commerce, and marvels across Asia, created collaboratively in Genoese captivity from Marco’s reported knowledge and Rustichello da Pisa’s literary workmanship; no authorial manuscript or single authoritative text survives, and the markedly divergent manuscript families may preserve subsequent revisions in which Marco participated. ([ARLIMA](https://www.arlima.net/mp/marco_polo/fr_devisement_du_monde.html); [Simion critical edition](https://doi.org/10.30687/978-88-6969-321-2))

### Legal instrument

- **9 January 1324 (1323 *more veneto*):** *Testament* (*Ego Marcus Paulo volo et ordino*). A dispositive legal text—closer to a manifesto that changes rights and obligations than to a literary work—declared by the dying Marco and formally written in Latin by the priest-notary Giovanni Giustiniani; the surviving parchment is therefore authentically Marco’s testamentary act, but not an autograph manuscript. ([Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana/Scrinium](https://www.scrinium.org/en-us/Commission/Biblioteca-Nazionale-Marciana/category/biblioteca-nazionale-marciana/marco-polos-will))

### Rejected or unresolved attribution

- **No secure date or original title:** *Map with Ship* (catalogued as *[Map of the Far East and adjacent Pacific]*). This vellum map has been attributed by its modern owners to Marco Polo or someone close to him, but it resists inclusion as one of his works: the Library of Congress records its authenticity as undetermined, while scholarly criticism dates the Rossi collection’s evidence substantially after Marco’s lifetime and finds no basis for a medieval Polo original. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/2005633013/); [Akbari’s review](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhg.2015.11.011))
