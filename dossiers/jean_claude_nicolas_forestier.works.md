## Works by Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier

### Landscape works: parks, gardens, promenades, and planted infrastructure

- **1890:** *Piste cyclable du bois de Vincennes*. A planted recreational route generally credited as Paris’s first cycle track; it belongs equally to transport infrastructure and landscape design. ([Éditions Norma](https://www.editions-norma.com/en/products/grandes-villes-et-systemes-de-parcs-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier))

- **1896 or 1898:** *Aménagement de l’avenue de Breteuil, Paris*. An avenue-promenade whose date varies between accounts; it is consequently safer to regard 1896–1898 as the documented dating range. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html), [Éditions Norma](https://www.editions-norma.com/en/products/grandes-villes-et-systemes-de-parcs-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier))

- **1904:** *Réaménagement du Champ-de-Mars, Paris*. A rehabilitation project rather than a wholly new park; Forestier defended the open ground against proposed development, so advocacy and preservation are part of the obra. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf))

- **1904–1908:** *Restauration du parc de Bagatelle*. A garden restoration and partial recreation, including the celebrated rose garden; it resists classification as an exclusively original creation because Forestier consciously reworked a historic estate. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf), [Éditions Norma](https://www.editions-norma.com/en/products/grandes-villes-et-systemes-de-parcs-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier))

- **1911:** *Parque de la Ascensión, Badajoz*. A public park attributed to Forestier and inaugurated in 1911; the surviving summary record does not establish how much of the executed park preserves his design. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1911–1929:** *Parque de María Luisa and its extensions, Seville*. A long-running transformation of an existing donated estate into a public park and exhibition landscape, executed with local architects and craftspeople; it is a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk of planting, water, ceramics, architecture, and urban display rather than a single autonomous garden. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html), [Andalusian Historical Heritage article](https://www.centrodeestudiosandaluces.es/datos/publicaciones/AH_59.pdf))

- **1912:** *Jardines de la Casa del Rey Moro, Ronda*. A terraced private garden descending through a steep site, designed in Forestier’s modern interpretation of the Hispano-Moorish garden. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf))

- **1914 or 1916:** *Jardines del Palacio de Moratalla, Hornachuelos*. An enlargement and reordering of an older estate garden. Sources assign Forestier’s intervention variously to 1914 and 1916, while elements on the estate predate him; the whole present garden should therefore not be attributed to Forestier without qualification. ([Quaderns Agraris](https://publicacions.iec.cat/repository/pdf/00000225/00000022.pdf), [Jardines sin Fronteras](https://jardinessinfronteras.com/2017/11/27/jardines-de-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier-en-espana/))

- **1915–1916:** *Jardín posterior del Palacio de Liria, Madrid*. A classical parterre and remodelling of part—not all—of the ducal gardens; scholarly accounts differ by a year on the design date. ([Quaderns Agraris](https://publicacions.iec.cat/repository/pdf/00000225/00000022.pdf), [Catharum](https://iehcan.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Catharum13-para-web.pdf))

- **1915–1923:** *Aménagement paysager de Montjuïc, Barcelona*. A landscape-urban project for the projected international exhibition, developed with Nicolau Maria Rubió i Tudurí and other designers. Its authorship is necessarily collective, and “Montjuïc” names a system of related works rather than one garden. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html), [Barcelona–Lisbon scholarly study](https://www.ub.edu/geocrit/sn/sn-296/sn-296-2.htm))

- **1915–1923:** *Jardins de Laribal, Montjuïc*. A terraced Mediterranean and neo-Hispano-Arab garden within the larger Montjuïc project, designed by Forestier with Rubió i Tudurí and completed in stages. ([Quaderns Agraris](https://publicacions.iec.cat/repository/pdf/00000225/00000022.pdf), [Barcelona parks record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_and_gardens_of_Barcelona))

- **1915–1923:** *Jardins de Miramar, Montjuïc*. A sequence of maritime-facing gardens within the Montjuïc ensemble, generally credited jointly to Forestier and Rubió i Tudurí. ([Barcelona–Lisbon scholarly study](https://www.ub.edu/geocrit/sn/sn-296/sn-296-2.htm))

- **1915–1923:** *Jardins de l’Umbracle, Montjuïc*. A shaded garden component of the Montjuïc exhibition landscape; its separation as an individual “work” is conventional because it was conceived inside the larger system. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1915–1923:** *Landscape setting of the Teatre Grec, Montjuïc*. Forestier and Rubió’s garden composition surrounds a theatre designed by Ramon Reventós; attribution of the entire object to Forestier would therefore be misleading. ([History of gardening in Spain](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historia_de_la_jardiner%C3%ADa_en_Espa%C3%B1a))

- **1915–1929:** *Planting of the exhibition axis, now Avinguda de la Reina Maria Cristina, Barcelona*. An avenue-promenade with formally clipped plane trees, made as connective landscape infrastructure for the exhibition rather than as an independent garden. ([Barcelona–Lisbon scholarly study](https://www.ub.edu/geocrit/sn/sn-296/sn-296-2.htm))

- **c. 1916:** *Garden of the present Fundació Julio Muñoz Ramonet, Barcelona*. A private urban garden attributed to Forestier; later ownership and alteration make the surviving site a manifestation of the design rather than an untouched original work. ([Quaderns Agraris](https://publicacions.iec.cat/repository/pdf/00000225/00000022.pdf))

- **1916:** *Plaza de Armas garden, Parc de la Ciutadella, Barcelona*. A formal garden intervention associated with Forestier’s Barcelona activity; it should be distinguished from the much older Ciutadella park as a whole. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1916:** *Jardin du Sultan, Casablanca*. A garden plan produced in the context of French-protectorate urban development. Its colonial administrative setting and subsequent implementation by others complicate the boundary between Forestier’s project and the realized landscape. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1916:** *Jardin d’Essais botaniques, Rabat*. A botanical and acclimatization garden for which Forestier prepared the principal landscape plan; execution and later development involved other administrators and horticulturists. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf), [Projets de paysage](https://journals.openedition.org/paysage/pdf/16248))

- **1917:** *Jardin de la terrasse des écuries du Palacio de la Magdalena, Santander*. A limited palace-garden intervention, not a design for the entire royal estate. ([Quaderns Agraris](https://publicacions.iec.cat/repository/pdf/00000225/00000022.pdf))

- **1918:** *Parc del Guinardó, Barcelona*. A hillside public-park project attributed to Forestier, subsequently shaped by municipal collaborators and later interventions; its realized form is therefore not solely his. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1918–1920:** *Jardins de la Villa Guy, Béziers*. A private garden designed for Jean and Joseph Guy and executed over roughly two years; contemporary family documentation records Forestier’s close supervision. ([Association des Parcs et Jardins du Languedoc-Roussillon](https://www.parcsetjardins.fr/data/diaries/files/apjlr-jard-info-74-revu-1.pdf))

- **1919:** *Jardín de Ángel Pérez, Valdenoja, Santander*. A private-garden commission recorded in the Spanish corpus; readily accessible documentation does not establish its survival or completeness. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1920:** *Parque René Fraga, Matanzas*. A Cuban public park attributed to Forestier; the exact division of design and execution among Forestier and local professionals remains insufficiently documented in the consulted sources. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1923–1924:** *Restauration du domaine de Sceaux*. A restoration proposal followed by work with architect Léon Azéma; because multiple schemes and hands intervened, “Forestier’s Sceaux” is best understood as a project layer rather than the entire reconstructed park. ([Domaine départemental de Sceaux](https://domaine-de-sceaux.hauts-de-seine.fr/100-ans/exposition-1923-renaissance), [Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1920s:** *Parc de la Cité internationale universitaire de Paris*. A campus landscape developed with Léon Azéma and connected conceptually to Forestier’s southern Paris park system; it is a collaborative urban landscape, not an individually authored garden. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1924–1925:** *Jardin de la villa Jane Renouardt, Saint-Cloud*. A garden contribution to a house-and-interior ensemble directed by Louis Süe with André Mare and the Compagnie des Arts français; the whole is closer to a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk than to a Forestier work alone. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, French biographical record](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1926–1930:** *Gardens of the Capitolio Nacional, Havana*. Formal civic gardens designed during Forestier’s work on Havana’s master plan. They are both a discrete landscape and one manifestation of the larger, incompletely executed urban project. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf), [Plan Forestier overview](https://social1916.com/plan-forrestier/))

- **1926–1930:** *Plaza de la Fraternidad and adjoining Central Park intervention, Havana*. A civic-space and planting scheme associated with the Havana plan; sources sometimes date an earlier “Parque de la Fraternidad” to 1920, but the major Forestier manifestation belongs to the late-1920s programme. ([Plan Forestier overview](https://social1916.com/plan-forrestier/))

- **1926–1930:** *Paseo del Prado remodelling project, Havana*. A proposed Art Deco-inflected redesign within the Havana plan. The scheme was not executed as designed, so the project—not the present promenade—is Forestier’s work. ([Plan Forestier overview](https://social1916.com/plan-forrestier/))

- **1926–1930:** *Avenida del Puerto and Avenida de las Misiones landscape schemes, Havana*. Urban-road and monumental-approach projects belonging to the larger Havana plan; their partial execution makes them difficult to detach as finished works. ([Plan Forestier overview](https://social1916.com/plan-forrestier/))

- **1926–1930:** *Extension of the Malecón and landscape treatment of Avenida G and Paseo, Havana*. A group of coastal-road and planted-avenue projects. They are best understood as components of a park-and-boulevard system rather than autonomous gardens. ([Plan Forestier overview](https://social1916.com/plan-forrestier/))

- **1927:** *Jardín de los Condes de Castilleja, Castilleja de Guzmán*. A private Andalusian garden subsequently incorporated into the Colegio Mayor Santa María del Buen Aire; sources also associate the present institutional garden with 1929, so dating and the limits of Forestier’s executed portion remain uncertain. ([Jean-Claude Nicolas Forestier, Spanish biographical record](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Nicolas_Forestier))

- **1927–1929:** *Jardin Stern, Saint-Cloud*. A private Art Deco garden whose surviving fabric is protected as a historic monument; it is one of Forestier’s securely attributed late works. ([French Ministry of Culture record cited in the archival bibliography](https://www.pop.culture.gouv.fr/notice/merimee/PA92000017), [Histoire des arts](https://histoiredesarts.culture.gouv.fr/Fiches-reperes/Arts-des-jardins))

- **from 1927:** *Jardin de la Bastide du Roy, Biot*. A private Mediterranean garden commissioned by the princesse de Polignac and identified by the archival biography as Forestier’s last major garden project. Later development means the extant estate cannot automatically be equated with his complete design. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf))

- **1928–1930:** *Landscape contribution to the château de Sept-Saulx*. A collaborative estate project with Louis Süe and Richard Desvallières, unfinished at Forestier’s death. The attribution is to his participation, not to sole authorship of the château or its entire grounds. ([Louis Süe biographical record](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_S%C3%BCe))

### Urban projects and territorial park systems

- **1913–1914:** *Programme de réserves et d’espaces libres pour les villes impériales du Maroc*. A landscape-urban protocol prepared after Forestier’s mission for Lyautey, covering Rabat, Marrakech, Fez and Meknes. It is simultaneously a report, a planning project, and an instrument of French colonial government; much was implemented or transformed by Henri Prost and others rather than built directly by Forestier. ([Projets de paysage](https://journals.openedition.org/paysage/pdf/16248), [Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf))

- **1913:** *Avant-projet d’aménagement de Marrakech*. A city-scale open-space and expansion project produced within the Moroccan mission. It survives principally as intention and planning influence, not as a wholly executed authored object. ([Projets de paysage](https://journals.openedition.org/paysage/pdf/16248))

- **1915–1923:** *Projet du parc de Pedralbes, Barcelona*. A major urban-park project developed during Forestier’s Barcelona period but not carried out in its proposed form. Its unrealized status does not diminish it as project, while preventing identification with the later palace gardens. ([Barcelona–Lisbon scholarly study](https://www.ub.edu/geocrit/sn/sn-296/sn-296-2.htm))

- **1915–1923:** *Projects for Tibidabo, Barcelona*. Park and landscape proposals cited in the standard account of Forestier’s oeuvre; the consulted summary does not clearly separate individual schemes or establish their degree of execution, so “Tibidabo” resists treatment as one finished work. ([Éditions Norma](https://www.editions-norma.com/en/products/grandes-villes-et-systemes-de-parcs-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier))

- **1920:** *Plan des espaces libres de Paris et du département de la Seine*. A metropolitan open-space plan drawn in Louis Bonnier’s Direction de l’extension; it is a planning document and project framework rather than a built object. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1923:** *Système de parcs pour la région parisienne*. A regional project linking existing and proposed parks, notably Saint-Cloud and Sceaux, by promenades and open-space corridors. Only fragments were realized, making the intention itself the principal obra. ([Éditions Norma](https://www.editions-norma.com/en/products/grandes-villes-et-systemes-de-parcs-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier), [Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1923–1925:** *Proyecto de espacios libres para Buenos Aires*. A citywide park-system proposal prepared as adviser to the Comisión de Estética Edilicia and incorporated into the 1925 organic plan. It is both an independent landscape project and a collaborative component of a larger municipal planning document. ([Observatorio Metropolitano de Buenos Aires](https://observatorioamba.org/planes-y-proyectos/caba/planes/1925-proyecto-organico/proyectos-asociados/proyecto-espacios-libres-1925-forestier), [Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1924–1929:** *Avenida Costanera, Buenos Aires*. A waterfront-avenue and park project emerging from the Buenos Aires plan. Sources distinguish the 1924 memorandum/project from a 1929 realization; the two dates describe stages of one obra rather than separate works. ([Observatorio Metropolitano de Buenos Aires](https://observatorioamba.org/planes-y-proyectos/caba/planes/1925-proyecto-organico/proyectos-asociados/proyecto-espacios-libres-1925-forestier), [Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.html))

- **1925/1926–1930:** *Plan directeur de La Havane*, commonly called the *Plan Forestier*. A metropolitan plan of roads, parks, monumental axes, waterfronts, and civic spaces prepared with French and Cuban collaborators, including Théodore Leveau. Only parts were executed before economic and political conditions halted the programme; “Plan Forestier” is thus a collective, partly manifested project rather than a completely built city. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf), [Projets de paysage](https://journals.openedition.org/paysage/pdf/27577))

- **1926–1930:** *Projet du Castillo del Príncipe, Havana*. A proposed conversion of the fortress into a museum with a monumental stair aligned on Avenida Carlos III. It remained substantially unexecuted and should be named as a project, not as a building Forestier made. ([Plan Forestier overview](https://social1916.com/plan-forrestier/))

- **1927:** *Plan général d’améliorations de Lisbonne*. A metropolitan expansion project centred on the continuation of Avenida da Liberdade, Parque Eduardo VII, a new residential centre, garden suburbs, transport connections, playing fields, and a large northern park. It was not realized, and survives above all as drawings, municipal deliberation, and a published explanation. ([Barcelona–Lisbon scholarly study](https://www.ub.edu/geocrit/sn/sn-296/sn-296-2.htm), [University of Tours/OpenEdition study](https://books.openedition.org/pufr/pdf/167))

- **1927:** *Plan d’extension de l’Avenida da Liberdade et d’expansion nord de Lisbonne*. The principal drawn manifestation of the Lisbon project, including a monumental ascent, park avenue, and metropolitan road branches. It is often treated separately, but was also the operative centre of the broader improvement plan. ([University of Tours/OpenEdition study](https://books.openedition.org/pufr/pdf/167))

### Books, reports, and operative texts

- **1906 or 1908:** *Grandes villes et systèmes de parcs*. A short theoretical book that defines the park system as a hierarchy of protected landscapes, metropolitan reserves, parks, recreation grounds, and avenues-promenades. The archive biography dates its appearance to 1906, while the standard printed edition and publisher identify 1908; the discrepancy should not be silently resolved. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf), [Éditions Norma](https://www.editions-norma.com/en/products/grandes-villes-et-systemes-de-parcs-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier))

- **1910:** *Bagatelle et ses jardins*. A co-authored historical and descriptive book arising from the restoration of Bagatelle; because it explains and publicly frames the restored estate, it is both documentation and part of the garden’s larger obra. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP bibliography](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf))

- **1913:** *Rapport sur les réserves à constituer au dedans et aux environs des villes capitales du Maroc*. A planning report from the Moroccan mission, later published under varying editorial titles. It acts as a protocol for reserving landscapes and organizing colonial-city expansion, rather than merely describing gardens. ([Projets de paysage](https://journals.openedition.org/paysage/pdf/16248), [1997 critical edition record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL309002M/Grandes_villes_et_syste%CC%80mes_de_parcs))

- **1920:** *Jardins: carnet de plans et de dessins*. An illustrated design manual combining principles, generic plans, sections, perspectives, construction details, and planting lists. It is not simply a book “about” gardens: it is a protocol and portable repertoire intended to generate gardens, while some plates idealize types rather than record identifiable commissions. ([Cité de l’architecture/IEJP bibliography](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf), [1920 edition contents](https://librairielecabanon.com/f/7642-jean-claude-nicolas-forestier-carnet-de-plans-et-de-dessins-1920-.html))

- **1924:** *Mémoire sur l’embellissement et l’extension de Buenos Aires* / memorandum on the coastal avenue and park system. A technical planning text written in connection with the Buenos Aires commission. The exact title varies in later references and translations; the 1997 critical edition publishes it as one of two major supplementary memoranda. ([1997 critical edition record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL309002M/Grandes_villes_et_syste%CC%80mes_de_parcs), [Observatorio Metropolitano de Buenos Aires](https://observatorioamba.org/planes-y-proyectos/caba/planes/1925-proyecto-organico/proyectos-asociados/proyecto-espacios-libres-1925-forestier))

- **1928:** *O embelesamento de Lisboa*. A newspaper interview or article in which Forestier set out the Lisbon scheme and reproduced its plan. It is neither a conventional treatise nor merely publicity: it is the principal known direct textual manifestation of an otherwise unrealized project. ([Barcelona–Lisbon scholarly study](https://www.ub.edu/geocrit/sn/sn-296/sn-296-2.htm))

### Works that resist individual enumeration

- **c. 1900–1930:** *Numerous private gardens not securely enumerated in the accessible scholarly corpus*. The critical-edition description explicitly speaks of “innombrables jardins privés,” while the surviving Forestier archive is only a small group of documents embedded in Théodore Leveau’s papers. This is not a defensible single work, nor a licence to invent a list: it records the resistance of Forestier’s dispersed practice to complete work-by-work reconstruction. ([1997 critical-edition description](https://maktabati.univ-usto.dz/pmb_arch/index.php?lvl=notice_display&id=1561), [Cité de l’architecture/IEJP fonds description](https://pdn-patrimonia.unicaen.fr/iejp/france/archives/Archives_nationales/FRIEJP_IFA_ead.xml/ead/FRIEJP_IFA_Arch4.pdf))
