## Works by Antonin Artaud

### Poems, prose-poems, essays, manifestos, adaptations, and composite books

- **1913–1923:** *Premiers poèmes*. A posthumously constituted corpus of early poems rather than a book designed by Artaud; the collected edition preserves it as a distinct chronological group. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1921–1923:** *Premières proses*. Early prose pieces, reviews, and chronicles assembled posthumously; their identity as one “work” is editorial rather than authorial. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1923:** *Tric Trac du ciel*. A small illustrated poetry book, with woodcuts by Élie Lascaux. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud))
- **1923:** *Bilboquet*. A short-lived review created and edited by Artaud, containing his poetry and polemical prose; it is both periodical and authorial project. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1923–1924; published 1924:** *Correspondance avec Jacques Rivière*. An exchange occasioned by the rejection of Artaud’s poems, deliberately converted by both correspondents into an epistolary work about the inability to produce “literature.” ([Jean Paulhan/NRF archive](https://jean-paulhan.fr/en/auteurs/antonin-artaud), [Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1924–1935:** *Poèmes*. A collected-edition corpus of separately written or published poems, not a single collection fixed by Artaud. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1925:** *L’Ombilic des limbes*. A deliberately heterogeneous book of prose, poems, letters, dialogue, and theatre; it resists classification more strongly than the convenient label “poetry collection” suggests. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud), [Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1925:** *Le Pèse-nerfs*. A sequence of prose fragments about thought, bodily suffering, and the failure of language, first issued as a small book. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud), [Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1925:** *Adresse au pape*. A Surrealist collective declaration drafted by Artaud: less descriptive essay than manifesto performing a repudiation of ecclesiastical authority. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1925:** *Adresse au Dalaï-Lama*. A collective Surrealist address drafted by Artaud and operating as an invocation and manifesto, not as reportage about Tibet. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1925:** *Lettre aux écoles du Bouddha*. A public epistle and polemical manifestation associated with Artaud’s direction of the Bureau de recherches surréalistes. ([Gallimard/Cercle de l’enseignement](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Blanche/OEuvres-completes31))
- **1925:** *Lettre aux recteurs des universités européennes*. A collective Surrealist manifesto drafted by Artaud; its mode is performative address rather than academic argument. ([Gallimard/Cercle de l’enseignement](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Blanche/OEuvres-completes31))
- **1925:** *Lettre aux médecins-chefs des asiles de fous*. A collective polemical address generally attributed to Artaud within the Surrealist campaign; because it appeared as a group declaration, individual authorship is not wholly separable from collective authorship. ([Gallimard/Cercle de l’enseignement](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Blanche/OEuvres-completes31))
- **1925:** *La Révolution surréaliste*, no. 3. Artaud directed this issue and shaped several of its collective addresses; the issue is a collaborative editorial manifestation, not solely his authored book. ([Gallimard/Cercle de l’enseignement](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Blanche/OEuvres-completes31))
- **1926:** *Le Centre nerveux*. A small constellation of fragments continuing the inquiry of *Le Pèse-nerfs*; publication history and later regrouping make its boundary unstable. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1926–1927:** *Fragments d’un journal d’enfer*. Fragmentary confessional prose, subsequently printed alongside *Le Pèse-nerfs* but not reducible to an ordinary diary. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud))
- **1927:** *À la grande nuit, ou le Bluff surréaliste*. A pamphlet-manifesto explaining Artaud’s break with the Surrealist group and attacking its alignment with communism. ([Gallimard/Cercle de l’enseignement](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Blanche/OEuvres-completes31))
- **1927:** *Point final*. A privately printed polemical text long thought lost and recovered in 1971; its disappearance and recovery are part of its bibliographical status. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1925–1927; collected 1929:** *L’Art et la Mort*. A composite book bringing together prose-poems, dream texts, criticism, and metaphysical reflection rather than a single continuous essay. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud))
- **1929–1930:** *Le Théâtre Alfred Jarry et l’hostilité publique*. A collaboratively written retrospective and manifesto by Artaud and Roger Vitrac, with Gaston-Louis Roux responsible for the cover design; authorship therefore cannot be assigned to Artaud alone. ([French biographical account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1931:** *Le Moine, de Lewis, raconté par Antonin Artaud*. A radical condensation and rewriting of Matthew Lewis’s novel: neither straightforward translation nor wholly independent novel. ([Bibliographic summary](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1934:** *Héliogabale ou l’Anarchiste couronné*. A hybrid historical novel, essay, mythography, and political-metaphysical tract, made with extensive use of documentary sources. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud))
- **1936:** *La Conquête du Mexique*. A scenario for the projected Theatre of Cruelty, drawing on the Spanish conquest as ritual and political conflict; it remained a project rather than a realized production. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1936–1937; collected 1945:** *D’un voyage au pays des Tarahumaras*. Essays and visionary ethnographic testimony arising from Artaud’s Mexican journey; later editions regrouped and retitled materials whose textual boundaries changed over time. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1937:** *Les Nouvelles Révélations de l’Être*. An apocalyptic, astrological manifesto issued under the name “Le Révélé”; its pseudonymous voice deliberately troubles ordinary authorship. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1937:** *La Nouvelle Jérusalem*. An apocalyptic text connected with Artaud’s Irish journey and his belief in the staff he carried; manuscript, letter, prophecy, and manifesto overlap. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1937:** *Lettres d’Irlande*. Letters, declarations, and appeals written during Artaud’s journey; their later presentation as a corpus is editorial, while the original objects were communications and performative proclamations. ([Gallimard, *Lettres 1937–1943*](https://www.gallimard.fr/catalogue/lettres-1937-1943/9782070146680))
- **1937–1943:** *Lettres 1937–1943*. A posthumously established body of correspondence spanning Ireland, imprisonment, and psychiatric confinement; the modern edition notes difficult readings and contested editorial history rather than pretending it was an author-designed book. ([Gallimard](https://www.gallimard.fr/catalogue/lettres-1937-1943/9782070146680))
- **1943–1946:** *Lettres de Rodez*. A posthumously constituted correspondence-corpus in which letter, poem, accusation, supplication, and self-theory repeatedly exchange functions. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1945–1947:** *Artaud le Mômo*. Poems and performative verbal compositions from Artaud’s final period, published as a small book in 1947. ([Wikisource bibliographic record](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Auteur%3AAntonin_Artaud))
- **1946:** *Révolte contre la poésie*. A poem-manifesto in which poetic form is made to attack “poetry” as an institution; its contradiction is constitutive, not a classification error. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1946:** *Le Retour d’Artaud, le Mômo*. A poem-sequence and self-manifestation announcing the post-Rodez persona “Artaud le Mômo.” ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1946–1947:** *Van Gogh, le suicidé de la société*. A polemical essay and prose-poem occasioned by the 1947 Van Gogh exhibition; it indicts society and psychiatry rather than offering a conventional artist’s biography. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1947:** *Ci-gît, précédé de La Culture indienne*. A composite poetry book joining a polemic on culture to a mock epitaph or anti-autobiography. ([Poetry Foundation](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poets/antonin-artaud))
- **1947:** *Suppôts et Suppliciations*. Artaud’s large, unfinished attempt to organize late texts into three movements; it is a project whose incompleteness is part of the work, and later editors disagree over transcription and arrangement. ([Gallimard edition cited by Érudit](https://www.erudit.org/fr/revues/annuaire/2012-n52-annuaire01550/1027015ar.pdf), [account of the editorial dispute](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2012/11/30/artaud-dernier-cri_5989166_3260.html))
- **1947:** *Lettre contre la Kabbale*. A polemical epistle published only after Artaud’s death; its first separate publication should not be mistaken for its date of composition. ([Critical bibliography](https://www.ips.it/vrttiopera/teatro/artaud_biblio.html))
- **1947–1948:** *Cahiers d’Ivry*. Cahiers 233–406: notebooks in which writing, glossolalia, diagrams, portraits, bodily notation, and drawing are inseparable. They are a material Gesamtkunstwerk-like process rather than 174 conventional “texts,” and their transcription became the subject of a prolonged dispute between Artaud’s heirs and Paule Thévenin’s editorial legacy. ([Gallimard edition discussed in *Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2012/11/30/artaud-dernier-cri_5989166_3260.html))
- **1943–1948:** *Cahiers de Rodez et d’Ivry*. Taken together, the surviving school notebooks form a numbered, posthumously edited body of writing-drawing extending through cahier 406; the numbering provides archival order, not evidence that Artaud intended 406 autonomous works. ([MoMA, *Antonin Artaud: Works on Paper*](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf), [*Le Monde*](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2012/11/30/artaud-dernier-cri_5989166_3260.html))

### Plays, theatre protocols, and unrealized stage projects

- **1925:** *Le Jet de sang*. A very short play embedded in *L’Ombilic des limbes*; announced for the Théâtre Alfred Jarry but not staged by Artaud’s company, it remained an unrealized performance project during his lifetime. ([Theatre Alfred Jarry history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Alfred_Jarry), [reference account](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/french-literature-biographies/antonin-artaud))
- **1926:** *Le Théâtre Alfred Jarry*. A collaborative theatre project founded by Artaud, Roger Vitrac, and Robert Aron; because it had no permanent venue or stable company, the project itself—not a building—is the obra. ([Theatre Alfred Jarry history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Alfred_Jarry))
- **1927:** *Ventre brûlé ou la Mère folle*. Artaud’s short music-hall-like play, presented in the first Théâtre Alfred Jarry programme; it was one component of a mixed collaborative evening. ([Theatre Alfred Jarry history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Alfred_Jarry))
- **1927:** *Les Mystères de l’amour / Ventre brûlé ou la Mère folle / Gigogne*. The Théâtre Alfred Jarry’s first programme, directed collectively around works by Vitrac, Artaud, and Robert Aron; it is a staged assemblage rather than an exclusively Artaudian play. ([Theatre Alfred Jarry history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Alfred_Jarry))
- **1928:** *Le Songe*. Artaud’s staging of August Strindberg’s play for the Théâtre Alfred Jarry, disrupted at its performances by Surrealist opponents; the production, not Strindberg’s underlying text, is Artaud’s made thing. ([BnF theatre ephemera record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb46975569v))
- **1928:** *Partage de midi*, Act III. An unauthorized Théâtre Alfred Jarry staging of the third act of Paul Claudel’s play; it was an intervention and provocation, not an authored Artaud drama. ([Theatre history](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A9%C3%A2tre_surr%C3%A9aliste))
- **1928–1929:** *Victor ou les Enfants au pouvoir*. Artaud’s production of Roger Vitrac’s play, the Théâtre Alfred Jarry’s largest undertaking; authorship of the text belongs to Vitrac, while Artaud’s contribution is the staging. ([BnF theatre ephemera record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb46975569v))
- **1928:** *Manifeste pour un théâtre avorté*. A manifesto defining the Théâtre Alfred Jarry through the acknowledged prospect of theatrical failure; the “abortive theatre” is both protocol and deliberately unstable project. ([Theatre Alfred Jarry history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Alfred_Jarry))
- **1928–1930:** *La Pierre philosophale*. An unfinished or unrealized dramatic project preserved among Artaud’s theatre writings; it resists treatment as a completed play. ([Répertoire du théâtre surréaliste](https://www.theatrales.uqam.ca/TheatreSurr.html))
- **1928–1930:** *Samouraï ou le Drame du sentiment*. An unrealized dramatic scenario or project rather than a finished, staged play. ([Répertoire du théâtre surréaliste](https://www.theatrales.uqam.ca/TheatreSurr.html))
- **1930–1931:** *Il n’y a plus de firmament*. An unfinished apocalyptic film-and-theatre scenario conceived through sound, projected images, movement, and architecture; it is closer to a Gesamtkunstwerk project than to a literary play. ([Répertoire du théâtre surréaliste](https://www.theatrales.uqam.ca/TheatreSurr.html))
- **1931:** *Le Théâtre balinais*. An essay-manifesto derived from Artaud’s encounter with Balinese dance at the Colonial Exhibition; its descriptions become a protocol for a non-literary theatre. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1932:** *Le Théâtre de la cruauté (Premier manifeste)*. A manifesto prescribing a theatre of space, sound, gesture, danger, and bodily address rather than illustrating a pre-existing dramatic text. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1933:** *Le Théâtre de la cruauté (Second manifeste)*. A second, more programmatic manifesto specifying themes and means for Artaud’s proposed theatre. ([Chronological bibliography](https://www.idixa.net/Pixa/pagixa-0604111032.html))
- **1933:** *Le Théâtre et la peste*. A lecture first delivered at the Sorbonne and later reshaped as an essay; contemporary accounts describe a vocal and bodily event that exceeded its written text. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1935:** *Les Cenci*. A tragedy in four acts and ten tableaux, adapted from Shelley and Stendhal and staged by Artaud with music, movement, mechanically amplified sound, and décor; the 1935 production is a Gesamtkunstwerk, while the full text was not published until 1964. ([BnF performance record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb39460385k), [publication history](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_Cenci_%28Artaud%29))
- **1938:** *Le Théâtre et son double*. A book assembled from lectures, essays, letters, and the two cruelty manifestos; it is a theatre-making protocol and metaphysical manifesto rather than a conventional systematic treatise. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1947:** *Histoire vécue d’Artaud-Mômo*. The three-cahier verbal score Artaud brought to his Vieux-Colombier appearance; it belongs simultaneously to manuscript, autobiographical fiction, and performance. ([French biographical account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **13 January 1947:** *Tête à tête par Antonin Artaud*. A Vieux-Colombier lecture-performance built from speech, cries, gesture, and readings from notebooks; its event-character resists reduction to the surviving written materials. ([French biographical account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **7 June 1947:** *Aliéner l’acteur*. A public lecture-performance at the Galerie Pierre, associated with the exhibition of Artaud’s drawings; it was an embodied manifestation as much as a text. ([MoMA chronology](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))

### Cinema scenarios and cinematic projects

- **1924:** *Le Boucher*. A surviving film scenario; like most of Artaud’s scenarios it was not filmed and therefore exists as a cinematic project rather than a motion picture. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1924–1925:** *La Révolte du boucher*. A film scenario related to *Le Boucher*; titles and versions overlap in the manuscript record, so they should not be silently treated as wholly independent completed films. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1925:** *Dix-huit secondes*. A film scenario organized around an eighteen-second mental crisis; it remained unrealized. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1925:** *Les Deux Nations sur les confins de la Mongolie*. An unrealized film scenario; its existence is textual and projective, not filmic. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1926:** *La Coquille et le Clergyman*. A film scenario by Artaud, filmed by Germaine Dulac and released in 1928; the surviving film is a contested collaboration, since Artaud publicly repudiated Dulac’s realization and disrupted its première. ([MoMA chronology](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf), [BnF ephemera record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb46975569v))
- **1927:** *La Sorcière*. An unrealized film scenario or synopsis preserved among Artaud’s cinema writings. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1927:** *Le Maître de bal*. An unrealized film scenario; it is a project, not a lost completed film. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1927:** *La Révolte des anges*. A proposed film adaptation after Anatole France that was not realized. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1927–1928:** *La Femme de l’ombre*. A surviving cinema scenario, unfilmed. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1928:** *L’Osselet toxique*. A cinema project or scenario whose fragmentary status prevents secure treatment as a completed screenplay. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1928:** *Les Dix-huit Secondes* and other surviving scenarios. The collected works preserve ten film scenarios in all; manuscript variants make their exact individuation less stable than a filmography implies. ([Film-scenario summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud), [Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))

### Performances made as a film actor

- **1923–1924:** *Fait divers*. Claude Autant-Lara’s short film, in which Artaud plays “Monsieur 2”; Artaud made a performance within another director’s collaborative obra, not the film as sole author. ([Film biography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1924:** *Surcouf*. Luitz-Morat’s serial film, with Artaud in a supporting role; surviving versions and release dates vary. ([Film biography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1926:** *Graziella*. Marcel Vandal’s film, containing an acting performance by Artaud. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1926:** *Le Juif errant*. Luitz-Morat’s film, with Artaud as Jacques Dupuis. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1927:** *Napoléon*. Abel Gance’s film, for which Artaud created the performance of Jean-Paul Marat; the film survives in multiple historically incomplete and reconstructed versions. ([Cinémathèque restoration account](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2024/06/28/napoleon-d-abel-gance-le-poeme-epique-ressuscite-d-un-cineaste-visionnaire_6245110_3246.html))
- **1927:** *Mathusalem ou l’Éternel Bourgeois*. Five short film sequences directed by Jean Painlevé for Ivan Goll’s stage production; Artaud’s appearance belongs to a theatre-cinema hybrid rather than a conventional standalone feature. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1928:** *La Passion de Jeanne d’Arc*. Carl Theodor Dreyer’s film, in which Artaud plays the sympathetic cleric Jean Massieu. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1928:** *Verdun, visions d’histoire*. Léon Poirier’s film, with Artaud as the intellectual; a sound version appeared in 1931 under a variant title. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1928:** *L’Argent*. Marcel L’Herbier’s film, in which Artaud appears as Mazaud. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1929:** *Tarakanova*. Raymond Bernard’s film, with Artaud as a young gypsy. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1930:** *La Femme d’une nuit*. Marcel L’Herbier’s film, containing an Artaud performance. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1931:** *Mater Dolorosa*. Abel Gance’s sound remake, with Artaud in the role of L’Hardy. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1931:** *L’Opéra de quat’sous*. G. W. Pabst’s French-language version, in which Artaud plays an apprentice beggar. ([Paris performance filmography](https://www.offi.fr/artiste/antonin-artaud-9185.html))
- **1931:** *Faubourg Montmartre*. Raymond Bernard’s film, with Artaud as Follestat. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1932:** *Les Croix de bois*. Raymond Bernard’s film, containing an Artaud performance. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1932:** *Coup de feu à l’aube*. Serge de Poligny’s film, with Artaud as the informer Karl. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1932:** *L’Enfant de ma sœur*. Henri Wulschleger’s film; Artaud’s precise role is not securely identified in standard filmographies. ([Filmography summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1933:** *Liliom*. Fritz Lang’s film, in which Artaud plays the knife-grinder and guardian angel. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1934:** *Sidonie Panache*. Henry Wulschleger’s film, containing a supporting performance by Artaud. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))
- **1935:** *Lucrèce Borgia*. Abel Gance’s film, in which Artaud plays Girolamo Savonarola. ([Cinémathèque-linked film summary](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0037625/))
- **1935:** *Kœnigsmark*. Maurice Tourneur’s film, containing one of Artaud’s last screen performances. ([Filmography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonin_Artaud))

### Radio, voice, percussion, and recorded Gesamtkunstwerk

- **22–29 November 1947:** *Pour en finir avec le jugement de dieu*. A radio work written, spoken, shouted, drummed, and recorded by Artaud with Roger Blin, Maria Casarès, and Paule Thévenin; Artaud described it as an initial realization of the Theatre of Cruelty. Its scheduled February 1948 broadcast was prohibited, so the made thing is simultaneously score, recording, censored transmission, and Gesamtkunstwerk. ([Radio France](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/antonin-artaud-pour-en-finir-avec-le-jugement-de-dieu))
- **1947:** *Tutuguri, le rite du soleil noir*. A vocal-poetic and percussive component incorporated into *Pour en finir avec le jugement de dieu*; it also has a textual existence, but the recorded manifestation cannot be reduced to that text. ([Radio France](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/antonin-artaud-pour-en-finir-avec-le-jugement-de-dieu))
- **1947:** *La Recherche de la fécalité*. A spoken and percussive component of the radio work, functioning as poem, polemic, and vocal performance. ([Radio France](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/antonin-artaud-pour-en-finir-avec-le-jugement-de-dieu))
- **1947:** *La Question se pose de…*. A dialogic component of the radio work performed with other voices; its authorship is Artaud’s, while its acoustic realization is collaborative. ([Radio France](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/antonin-artaud-pour-en-finir-avec-le-jugement-de-dieu))
- **1947:** *Conclusion*. The final spoken-percussive movement of *Pour en finir avec le jugement de dieu*, conceived as enacted bodily language rather than merely a closing essay. ([Radio France](https://www.radiofrance.com/les-editions/disque/antonin-artaud-pour-en-finir-avec-le-jugement-de-dieu))
- **1947–1948:** *Le Théâtre de la cruauté*. A late radio text and recording project related to, but distinct from, *Pour en finir avec le jugement de dieu*; its title reactivates Artaud’s earlier theatrical protocol in a vocal-acoustic medium. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))

### Drawings, portraits, written-drawings, and gris-gris

- **1939–1944:** *Sorts*. Individually addressed sheets combining writing, drawing, burning, piercing, stains, and magical action. They resist being called either correspondence or pictures: Artaud treated them as operative objects intended to protect, attack, or alter reality. ([MoMA, *Antonin Artaud: Works on Paper*](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
- **January 1945–May 1946:** *Rodez drawings*. A catalogue-defined corpus of large drawings, portraits, written-drawings, and bodily diagrams; the corpus is not one work, but many sheets whose words and images cannot consistently be separated. ([MoMA, *Antonin Artaud: Works on Paper*](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
- **1945:** *La Pendue*. A large drawing or written-drawing from Rodez; its graphite, coloured chalk, inscriptions, and bodily image form one material object. ([MoMA, *Antonin Artaud: Works on Paper*](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
- **1945:** *L’Être et ses fœtus*. A large written-drawing combining anatomical, genealogical, and verbal marks. ([MoMA, *Antonin Artaud: Works on Paper*](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
- **1945:** *La Maladresse sexuelle de dieu*. A written-drawing dated approximately February 1946 by the Centre Pompidou; titles and dates attached to these sheets sometimes derive from Artaud’s inscriptions or later cataloguing and remain bracketed when uncertain. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **January 1946:** *Le Minotaure*. A drawing catalogued by the Centre Pompidou. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **February 1946:** *Les Illusions de l’âme*. A drawing whose date is catalogued as approximate rather than certain. ([Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/c7Gpz5j))
- **March 1946:** *L’Inca*. A written-drawing with an approximate catalogue date. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **March 1946:** *Le Théâtre de la cruauté*. A drawing sharing its title with Artaud’s theatrical and radio projects; it is a visual-bodily manifestation of that project, not an illustration subordinate to the prose. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **April 1946:** *La Mort et l’homme*. A large drawing or written-drawing. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **3 May 1946:** *Les Corps de terre*. A dated drawing combining figures, graphic violence, and inscription. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **11 May 1946:** *Autoportrait*. A dated self-portrait made at Rodez, inseparable in Artaud’s practice from his attempted reconstruction of the body. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **1946:** *La Tête bleue*. A drawing from the final Rodez period. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1946:** *La Projection du véritable corps*. A written-drawing treating the body as projected construction rather than represented anatomy. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **circa 1946:** *La Machine de l’être ou Dessin à regarder de traviole*. A drawing whose alternative title functions partly as a viewing protocol: it tells the beholder how the object is to be encountered. ([Centre Pompidou checklist](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/media/document/06/78/0678b4935a1216ea1f5af1cbe6800626/normal.pdf))
- **1946:** *Portrait de Roger Blin*. A portrait that is also part of Artaud’s late network of protective, accusatory, and relational images. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1946–1947:** *Portraits et dessins par Antonin Artaud*. The body of works shown at Galerie Pierre in July 1947; the exhibition was a manifestation assembled from portraits and written-drawings rather than a newly made single work. ([MoMA chronology and catalogue](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
- **1947:** *Portrait de Domnine Thévenin*. A portrait drawing. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1947:** *Portrait de Paule Thévenin*. A portrait drawing; a second sheet catalogued as *Paule aux ferrets* is a separate object, not merely an edition. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1947:** *Paule aux ferrets*. A separate portrait of Paule Thévenin whose alternative catalogue wording is *Portrait de Paule Thévenin*. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1947:** *Portrait d’Yves Thévenin*. A portrait drawing. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1947:** *Portrait de Colette Thomas*. A portrait drawing. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1947:** *Portrait de Georges Pastier*. A portrait drawing. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **May 1947:** *Portrait de Minouche Pastier*. A portrait drawing; it must not be conflated with the distinct 1948 portrait of the same sitter. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **2 July 1947:** *Portrait de Jany de Ruy*. A dated portrait drawing. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **25 August 1947:** *Portrait de Colette Allendy*. A dated portrait drawing. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **21 November 1947:** *Portrait d’Henri Pichette ou Gris-gris*. A dated portrait whose alternate title identifies it as a magically operative object as well as a likeness. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **1947:** *Autoportrait*. A self-portrait distinct from the dated May 1946 sheet. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **January 1948:** *Sans titre*. Two separate untitled drawings catalogued for this month; lack of title is preserved rather than repaired with descriptive inventions. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **February 1948:** *Sans titre*. An untitled drawing catalogued for Artaud’s final month of sustained work. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1948:** *Portrait de Minouche Pastier*. A late portrait, distinct from the sheet catalogued to May 1947. ([Centre Pompidou exhibition catalogue](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/Antonin_Artaud%2C_dessins))
- **1948:** *Portrait de Minouche Pastier*. Another late portrait catalogued by the Centre Pompidou with an approximate date; duplicate titles designate different physical sheets. ([Centre Pompidou collection](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))
- **1945–1948:** *Untitled notebook drawings, marginal figures, perforated sheets, portraits, and written-drawings*. Hundreds of further graphic acts survive inside and outside the cahiers. They resist enumeration as securely titled autonomous works: the scholarly catalogues distinguish sheets by collection, date, inscription, and catalogue number, while warning through brackets and descriptive titles where Artaud supplied no fixed title. ([MoMA, *Antonin Artaud: Works on Paper*](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf), [Centre Pompidou collection database](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/recherche/oeuvres?artiste=Antonin+Artaud+%28Antoine+Artaud%2C+dit%29))

### Correspondence as an obra without fixed edges

- **1921–1948:** *Correspondance*. Artaud repeatedly incorporated real letters into books and treated correspondence as intrinsic to his work; no single author-authorized object called the complete *Correspondance* exists, and posthumous volumes divide it by recipient or period. ([Gallimard, *Œuvres complètes*, I](https://www.fnac.com/a109015/Antonin-Artaud-%C5%92uvres-completes))
- **1921–1932:** *Letters to doctors, healers, seers, and friends*. A collected-edition corpus of appeals concerning pain, thought, treatment, and occult aid; it was made as communication, not planned as a unified literary title. ([Gallimard/Cercle de l’enseignement](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Blanche/OEuvres-completes31))
- **1923–1924:** *Letters to Jacques Rivière*. Individual letters that also became components of the collaboratively authorized *Correspondance avec Jacques Rivière*; the letter-objects and the published correspondence are related but not identical works. ([Jean Paulhan/NRF archive](https://jean-paulhan.fr/en/auteurs/antonin-artaud))
- **1931–1932:** *Letters on Héliogabale*. Research, proposal, and drafting correspondence surrounding *Héliogabale*; these remain documents of a project unless separately incorporated into an edited work. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1936:** *Letters from Mexico*. Travel correspondence and public statements intertwined with the Tarahumara writings; the later corpus is editorial and its edges do not coincide perfectly with the named Mexican essays. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1943–1946:** *Letters to Henri Parisot*. Correspondence containing poems, translations, glossolalia, and theoretical declarations; several letters function as made verbal objects beyond ordinary communication. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1944–1946:** *Letters to Marthe Robert*. Correspondence from Rodez in which personal address, spiritual accusation, self-construction, and literary composition are inseparable. ([MoMA chronology](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
- **1945–1946:** *Letters to André Breton*. Correspondence negotiating Surrealism, Artaud’s identity, and his return from Rodez; it is a relational corpus, not an author-designed book. ([Évelyne Grossman, ed., *Œuvres*](https://search.worldcat.org/fr/title/300265035))
- **1946–1948:** *Letters to Paule Thévenin*. Working correspondence closely entangled with the preservation and projected publication of Artaud’s late writings; its posthumous editorial history is contested by the estate. ([Account of the editorial dispute](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2012/11/30/artaud-dernier-cri_5989166_3260.html))
- **1946–1948:** *Letters to Jacques Prevel*. Letters and notes belonging to Artaud’s final circle; they survive alongside Prevel’s testimony and should not be confused with Prevel’s own writings about Artaud. ([MoMA chronology](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_292_300001756.pdf))
