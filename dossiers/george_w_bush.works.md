## Works by George Walker Bush

### Books and book-length texts

- **1999:** *A Charge to Keep*. Campaign autobiography and political credo, later subtitled *My Journey to the White House*; although published under Bush’s name, the White House biography of Karen Hughes says that she helped write it, and contemporary accounts call her its co-writer or ghostwriter.[^1][^2]
- **2010:** *Decision Points*. Memoir organized around consequential decisions rather than as a continuous autobiography. Bush is the named author, but former speechwriter Christopher Michel collaborated extensively on the manuscript; accounts differ over whether “collaborator” or “ghostwriter” best describes his role.[^1][^3]
- **2014:** *41: A Portrait of My Father*. Biographical and filial portrait of George H. W. Bush, presented as the first book in which one United States president narrates the life of another who was also his father.[^1][^4]
- **2017:** *Portraits of Courage: A Commander in Chief’s Tribute to America’s Warriors*. A Gesamtkunstwerk-like book joining Bush’s painted portraits of post-9/11 service members to biographical narratives he wrote about them; it is simultaneously an illustrated book, a memorial project, and the catalogue of a touring exhibition.[^5]
- **2021:** *Out of Many, One: Portraits of America’s Immigrants*. A book and exhibition combining 43 oil portraits with Bush’s accounts of their subjects’ lives; the shared title names both the textual object and the larger visual project.[^6]
- **forthcoming 2027:** *The Call of Freedom: Portraits of Political Prisoners and Dissidents*. An announced book-project combining 44 portraits with biographical sketches; because publication is scheduled for March 9, 2027, it is presently a project rather than a completed published manifestation.[^7]

### Presidential discourse

- **2001–2009:** *Public Papers of the Presidents of the United States: George W. Bush*. The official collected-works corpus of Bush’s public messages, speeches, remarks, statements, and written communications as president, issued in chronological volumes by the Office of the Federal Register.[^8] These texts resist simple personal authorship: they act as presidential utterances under Bush’s authority but were routinely researched, drafted, and revised by speechwriters and other officials.
- **2001–2008:** *Selected Speeches of President George W. Bush*. A retrospective White House selection from the much larger presidential corpus, assembled as a single documentary volume rather than written as a discrete book.[^9] It is therefore a manifestation of speeches already present in the public papers, not an additional set of works.
- **2001–2009:** *Executive Orders of George W. Bush*. Protocols in the strongest sense: numbered presidential texts that direct the executive branch and alter legal-administrative reality rather than merely describe it.[^8] Bush signed and promulgated them, but their institutional drafting makes individual literary authorship inappropriate.

### Painting cycles and exhibitions

- **c. 2012–present:** *Private paintings, including dogs, landscapes, still lifes, and self-portraits*. An open and incompletely catalogued body of work begun after Bush left office; early works became public largely through unauthorized access to private correspondence, while other paintings remained gifts or domestic studies.[^10] No catalogue raisonné fixes its membership, titles, or dates, so it resists enumeration as a complete obra.
- **2014:** *The Art of Leadership: A President’s Personal Diplomacy*. A cycle of 30 painted portraits of world leaders, exhibited with photographs, artifacts, and Bush’s personal reflections.[^11] The title properly belongs to the exhibition-project as a whole; the accompanying institutional material was not made by Bush alone.
- **2017:** *Portraits of Courage*. A visual cycle comprising 66 individual portraits and a four-panel mural depicting 98 service members and veterans.[^5] It is both an autonomous body of oil paintings and one component of the identically titled book and exhibition.
- **2021:** *Out of Many, One*. A cycle of 43 portraits of American immigrants, accompanied in its book and exhibition manifestations by Bush’s written stories.[^6] Treating it solely as a book would erase the paintings; treating it solely as an exhibition would erase its constructed conjunction of image and life narrative.
- **2025:** *A Shining City on the Hilltop*. An exhibition-cycle of 35 paintings based on people and scenes at the George W. Bush Presidential Center and Southern Methodist University.[^12] Bush composed the paintings from photographs, making the cycle an interpretation of an institutional community rather than a documentary record made from direct observation.
- **2026:** *Remembering 9/11*. A cycle of 16 new oil-and-acrylic paintings made for the twenty-fifth anniversary of the September 11 attacks.[^13] The institutional title belongs to the commemorative collection and exhibition; the available catalogue does not establish independent titles for every canvas.
- **in progress; announced for 2027:** *The Call of Freedom: Portraits of Political Prisoners and Dissidents*. A project of 44 portraits intended for a book and an exhibition scheduled to open March 9, 2027.[^7] Its present status is prospective: the intention, announced subjects, and exhibition dates exist, but the public manifestation remains unfinished.
- **undated and intermittently produced:** *Individual commissioned or gifted portraits*. Bush has made paintings outside the named cycles, including a portrait of former South Korean president Roh Moo-hyun presented to Roh’s family in 2019.[^14] These dispersed objects have no authoritative complete catalogue and therefore cannot honestly be converted into a finite titled list.
