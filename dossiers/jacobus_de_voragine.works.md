## Works by Jacobus de Voragine

### Hagiographic compilations

- **c. 1260–1298:** *Legendae sanctorum* (later known as *Legenda aurea*, *Golden Legend*). A liturgically ordered compilation of saints’ lives and church feasts, begun around 1260 and repeatedly revised by Jacobus almost until his death; the familiar title is not the author’s earliest title, and later users extensively interpolated, abbreviated, and rearranged the text. The critical edition reconstructs 178 chapters in Jacobus’s final recension, rather than the 182 found in the older vulgate edition. ([Treccani, lines 62–76](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Preaching instruments

- **After the first redaction of the *Legenda aurea*, no later than 1286:** *Sermones de omnibus sanctis et festis*, diffuse recension. A repertory of 305 model sermons for saints’ days and liturgical feasts, made as reusable material for other preachers rather than as a record of sermons Jacobus actually delivered. Jacobus himself distinguishes this extensive collection from a second, shorter version. ([Treccani, lines 86–97](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **After the first redaction of the *Legenda aurea*, no later than 1286:** *Sermones de omnibus sanctis et festis*, abbreviated recension. Jacobus says that he made a second, more concise volume of sermons for saints and feasts, but no copy is presently known; it therefore survives as an author-attested project or lost obra, not as an examinable text. ([Treccani, lines 94–96](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **Probably after the *Sermones de sanctis*, no later than 1286:** *Sermones de omnibus Evangeliis dominicalibus* (also *Sermones de tempore per annum*, *Sermones dominicales*, or *Sermones festivales*). A collection of 160 model sermons, normally three for each Sunday Gospel; its several titles describe the same preaching instrument, not several distinct works. The relative chronology rests on the order of Jacobus’s own list rather than a dated manuscript. ([Treccani, lines 90–99](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **Probably completed by 1286:** *Sermones quadragesimales*. A protocol-like collection of 96 model sermons—two for each weekday of Lent—intended to generate actual preaching performances. The date 1286 occurs in the colophons of one branch of the manuscript tradition and is therefore probable, not securely authorial; two sermons appended in many printed editions do not belong to the original collection. ([Treccani, lines 100–106](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Mirabile, *Sermones quadragesimales*](https://www.mirabileweb.it/title/sermones-quadragesimales-title/3286))

- **1292–1298:** *Liber Marialis*. An alphabetically organized preaching aid treating 160 attributes, images, functions, and virtues of the Virgin. Although transmitted and printed as Marian sermons, it resists that classification: Jacobus calls it a small book of Marian praises, and scholarship identifies it as a reference repertory rather than a sermon collection. ([Treccani, lines 107–111](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Civic history and political instruction

- **1295/1296–1298:** *Chronica civitatis Ianuensis ab origine urbis usque ad annum 1297*. A twelve-part obra combining urban chronicle, universal history, episcopal catalogue, autobiography, civic praise, and a normative “mirror” for rulers and citizens. Its generic instability is part of the work: it narrates Genoa’s past while also instructing and attempting to shape the Christian city; the separately circulating lives of Genoese bishops and archbishops are a section of this work, not another work by Jacobus. ([Treccani, lines 112–124](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Local saints, relics, and translations

- **1282:** *Passio sancti Cassiani*. A commissioned legend of the martyr Cassian, compiled for Bishop Sinibaldo de’ Milotti of Imola. A prefatory letter dated 18 June 1282 secures the date and records Jacobus’s authorship. ([Treccani, lines 135–136](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **1281–1285:** *Tractatus miraculorum reliquiarum sancti Florentii*. A tract on miracles associated with the relics of St Florentius of Orange, preserved with a dedicatory letter and a companion translation narrative in a single fifteenth-century manuscript. Its attribution is traditional rather than certain: the manuscript names Jacobus, and stylistic resemblance supports the claim, but no authorial copy survives. ([Treccani, lines 133–134](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Degl’Innocenti, “James of Varazze and the Relics of St. Florence of Orange”](https://eurekamag.com/research/102/945/102945408.php))

- **1281–1285:** *Historia translationis reliquiarum et ossuum sancti Florentii*. The companion narrative of the translation of Florentius’s relics. Like the preceding tract, it survives in one late manuscript and is only traditionally attributed to Jacobus, although its style conforms closely to his securely attributed relic narratives. ([Treccani, lines 133–134](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Degl’Innocenti, “James of Varazze and the Relics of St. Florence of Orange”](https://eurekamag.com/research/102/945/102945408.php))

- **1286–1292:** *Historia reliquiarum quae sunt in monasterio sororum Sanctorum Philippi et Iacobi de Ianua*. An eleven-chapter account of the origins and powers of relics held by a Genoese Dominican convent. Its incipit attributes the compilation to Jacobus after his provincialate but before his archiepiscopate, which supplies the date range. ([Treccani, lines 131–132](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **1293:** *Legenda seu Vita sancti Syri episcopi Ianuensis*. A life of the Genoese bishop Syrus, made when Jacobus arranged the recognition of the saint’s relics. It resists treatment as a wholly independent work: although once edited separately, Jacobus describes it as an addition to an older legend and incorporated it into a late revision of the *Legenda aurea*. ([Treccani, lines 126–128](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **Probably 1296–1298:** *Historia sive legenda translationis sancti Iohannis Baptistae Ianuam*. A formal address to the Genoese recounting the Baptist’s relics from burial at Myra to their supposed arrival in Genoa in 1099. Jacobus confirms in the *Chronica* that he made a history and hymns on the subject; the history survives, while its dating depends on its close association with the late *Chronica*. ([Treccani, lines 129–130](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Lost manifestations

- **Probably 1296–1298:** Hymns concerning the translation of the relics of St John the Baptist to Genoa. Jacobus states that he composed “a history and hymns” about the relics, but no trace of the hymns is known and their individual titles, number, and texts cannot be recovered. They are therefore author-attested creations without surviving textual objects. ([Treccani, lines 129–130](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Disputed or derivative work

- **Date unknown:** *Tractatus de libris a beato Augustino episcopo editis*. A guide to Augustine’s writings attributed to Jacobus in several fourteenth- and fifteenth-century manuscripts. J. A. McCormick defended its authenticity, but Giovanni Paolo Maggioni identifies it with material inserted into the late recension of the *Legenda aurea*’s chapter on Augustine; it may therefore be an excerpt subsequently made to appear autonomous rather than a separate obra conceived by Jacobus. ([Treccani, lines 60–61](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/iacopo-da-varazze_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
