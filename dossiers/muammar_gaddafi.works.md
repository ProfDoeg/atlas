## Works by Muammar Muhammad Abu Minyar al-Gaddafi

### Political theory and manifestos

- **1973–1974:** *The Third International Theory*. A cluster of programmatic texts—published under such English titles as *The Broadlines of the Third Theory*, *The Aspects of the Third Theory*, *The Concept of Jihad*, *The Divine Concept of Islam*, and *The Fundamentals of the Third International Theory*—rather than a securely fixed single book. Some editions were assembled institutionally from Gaddafi’s speeches, so the boundary between authored treatise, transcript, and official exposition is uncertain. ([St John, “The Ideology of Muʿammar al-Qadhdhafi”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/ideology-of-muammar-alqadhdhafi-theory-and-practice/2FDF577650519B221ED4A1B480BCFCDC); [Hudson bibliography](https://www.islamawareness.net/Africa/Libya/libya_article0001b.pdf))

- **1975–1979:** *The Green Book* (*al-Kitāb al-Akhḍar*). A three-part political manifesto presenting Gaddafi’s “Third Universal Theory”: Part I addresses democracy, Part II economics and socialism, and Part III the social foundations of the theory. The parts appeared successively and were subsequently treated as one obra; its status is also performative, because the Libyan state attempted to make the manifesto into institutions and everyday protocol. ([St John](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/ideology-of-muammar-alqadhdhafi-theory-and-practice/2FDF577650519B221ED4A1B480BCFCDC); [CiNii bibliographic record for the three-part publication](https://ge.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA68193307); [Marxists Internet Archive publication note](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/gaddafi/))

- **2000 or shortly thereafter:** *Isratin: The White Book* (*Isrāṭīn: al-Kitāb al-Abyaḍ*). A geopolitical project and manifesto proposing a single Israeli–Palestinian state, its coined title combining “Israel” and “Palestine.” Sources disagree about whether the booklet first appeared in 2000 or in the early 2000s, and the proposal had previously been articulated by Gaddafi’s son Saif al-Islam, making priority and intellectual authorship less simple than the title-page attribution. ([WOZ](https://www.woz.ch/isratin/bruder-gaddafi); [Isratin publication history and references](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isratin))

- **2009:** *The One-State Solution*. An op-ed manifesto restating the Isratin project for an international audience; it acts as an intervention in contemporary diplomacy rather than as a detached description. ([The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2009/01/22/opinion/22qaddafi.html))

### Literary and hybrid prose

- **1993:** *The Village, the Village; the Earth, the Earth; and the Suicide of the Astronaut, with Other Stories* (*al-Qarya al-Qarya, al-Arḍ al-Arḍ, wa-Intiḥār Rāʾid al-Faḍāʾ*). A collection of stories, allegories, prose meditations, and political parables whose pieces resist stable classification as fiction or essay. The original Arabic collection is documented as a 1993 publication, although later editions bear later dates and altered English titles. ([Ahmida, *Forgotten Voices*](https://sahistory.org.za/sites/default/files/archive-files/ali_abdullatif_ahmida_forgotten_voices_power_anbook4you.pdf); [Google Books bibliographic record of a later Arabic edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/Al_Qarya_al_qarya_al_ar%E1%B8%8D_al_ar%E1%B8%8D_wa_i.html?id=xc2W0QEACAAJ))

- **1995:** *Long Live the State of the Wretched! (Publications against the Law)* (*Taḥyā Dawlat al-Ḥuqarāʾ! Manshūrāt Ḍidda al-Qānūn*), commonly shortened in English to *Illegal Publications*. Four hybrid essays or polemical prose pieces; the collection’s slogans, satire, and political argument make “literary work” an incomplete category. ([“Literary Springs” bibliographic citation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269284998_%27Literary_Springs%27_in_Libyan_Literature_Contributions_of_Writers_to_the_Country%27s_Emancipation); [Guardian account of the two original collections](https://www.theguardian.com/books/booksblog/2010/mar/24/dictator-lit-gaddafi))

- **1996–1999:** *Escape to Hell and Other Stories*. A translated composite edition, not a third independent Arabic obra: it combines material from the 1993 and 1995 collections and has circulated with differing publication dates, titles, selections, and paratexts. It should therefore be recorded as a new editorial manifestation of earlier works, not silently counted as another original collection. ([Cambridge scholarly bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/anatomy-of-authoritarianism-in-the-arab-republics/bibliography/81B19EC9E2BB3858B483EDD7F5FE80D3); [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/books/booksblog/2010/mar/24/dictator-lit-gaddafi))

### Conversations and retrospective self-presentation

- **2004–2005:** *My Vision: Conversations and Frank Exchanges of Views with Edmond Jouve*. A collaboratively made interview-book or authorized self-portrait rather than a conventionally authored monograph; bibliographies credit Gaddafi “with Edmond Jouve,” and the English edition appeared in 2005. ([Cambridge scholarly bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/anatomy-of-authoritarianism-in-the-arab-republics/bibliography/81B19EC9E2BB3858B483EDD7F5FE80D3); [Kirkus bibliographic entry](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/author/muammar-gaddafi/))

### Speeches, declarations, and collected discourse

- **No fixed date:** *The Story of the Revolution*. A published revolutionary narrative attributed to Gaddafi, but the consulted reference bibliography supplies no date; none should be invented. Its precise relation to speeches and later editions titled *Pages from the Story of the Revolution* remains bibliographically unclear. ([Contemporary Authors biographical bibliography](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED439031.pdf))

- **1973–1974; collected 1975:** *Discourses*. A collection of Gaddafi’s statements as published in the Arab and international press between 2 August 1973 and 29 April 1974. The made object is editorial as well as authorial: spoken interventions became a 150-page documentary compilation. ([CiNii bibliographic description](https://ge.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BC15986111))

- **16 April 1973:** *Zuwarah Speech*. A spoken manifesto proclaiming the “Popular Revolution” and its cultural, administrative, political, economic, and military program. It is not comfortably a literary work: its primary form was an event and command, while surviving texts are transcripts and later documentary manifestations. ([St John](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/ideology-of-muammar-alqadhdhafi-theory-and-practice/2FDF577650519B221ED4A1B480BCFCDC); [Tallinn University catalogue](https://elu.tlu.ee/sites/default/files/2024-05/IVAkataloogid2024.pdf))

- **1969–2011:** *Speeches, statements, interviews, and recorded conversations*. This is an open, incompletely delimited body of work rather than one obra: Libyan institutions issued annual and multivolume collections, including *al-Sijill al-Qawmī* (“The National Record”), while a modern research catalogue and one doctoral study identify only selected subsets. Because no consulted catalogue claims to enumerate every utterance—and because many publications excerpt, translate, or retitle the same speech—it would be false precision to list each printed manifestation as a separate work. ([Tallinn University, *The Catalogue of Muammar Gaddafi’s Speeches*](https://elu.tlu.ee/sites/default/files/2024-05/IVAkataloogid2024.pdf); [Sharp, *Talking to the Desert*](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/55894/); [Google Books record for *al-Sijill al-Qawmī*, vol. 21](https://books.google.com/books/about/%D8%B3%D8%AC%D9%84_%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D9%88%D9%85%D9%8A_%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A7%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%AA_%D9%88%D8%AE%D8%B7.html?id=v-Pk6c16FI8C))

### Political Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1973–2011:** *The Jamahiriya project*. Gaddafi attempted to turn manifesto, terminology, public ritual, congress structure, economic rules, architecture, iconography, and personal rule into a single social system. It resists being called his “work”: millions of Libyans and state institutions made and endured it, while the contradiction between formally leaderless popular government and Gaddafi’s extralegal authority prevents clean individual attribution. ([Vandewalle, *Libya Since 1969* bibliography and discussion](https://www.scribd.com/document/444577375/Libya-Since-1969-Qadhafis-Revolution-Revisited-Dirk-Vandewalle-2008-pdf); [St John](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-journal-of-middle-east-studies/article/abs/ideology-of-muammar-alqadhdhafi-theory-and-practice/2FDF577650519B221ED4A1B480BCFCDC))
