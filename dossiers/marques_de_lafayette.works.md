## Works by Marie-Joseph Paul Yves Roch Gilbert du Motier

### Memoir manuscripts

- **c. 1779–1780:** *Mémoires écrits par moi-même, jusqu’à l’année 1780*. An unfinished autobiographical narrative of Lafayette’s first American expedition and campaigns; Cornell describes the surviving manuscript as an extensive memoir reaching 1779, while the family edition gives it the editorial title above. [[Cornell](https://rare.library.cornell.edu/lafayette/); [family edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8376/pg8376-images.html)]

- **Date uncertain:** *Manuscrit no 1*. A second account of the American Revolution, not an independently titled book: the family editors assigned the identifying number and printed only extracts because it repeated parts of the memoir. [[scholarly edition](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/64a685b6-e9fb-4679-8bca-4467adf29588/content); [family edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8376/8376-h/8376-h.htm)]

- **Date uncertain:** *Manuscrit no 2*. Another parallel narrative of the American Revolution, likewise known by an editorial number rather than a title supplied by Lafayette and preserved only selectively in the early printed collection. [[scholarly edition](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/64a685b6-e9fb-4679-8bca-4467adf29588/content); [family edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/8376/8376-h/8376-h.htm)]

- **After 1797:** *Souvenirs en sortant de prison*. Retrospective fragments written after Lafayette’s release from Austrian imprisonment; this is memoir-work assembled from manuscripts, not a finished book prepared for publication. [[Sainte-Beuve’s review of the family edition](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Lafayette/02)]

- **After 1799:** *Mes rapports avec le Premier Consul*. A retrospective political memoir concerning Lafayette’s dealings with Napoleon Bonaparte; its familiar title comes from the organization of the posthumous family edition. [[bibliographical account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_du_Motier_de_La_Fayette#Sources_et_bibliographie)]

- **Various dates:** *Fragments extracted from various manuscripts*. Short recollections of such episodes as Lafayette’s departure for America, his first meeting with Washington, Barren Hill, the French fleet’s arrival, and the Franco-American dispute at Newport. They resist treatment as separately completed works: the family editors extracted and titled them from a larger, unfinished manuscript accumulation. [[family-edition contents](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8376/pg8376-images.html)]

### Manifestos, constitutional projects, and political arguments

- **1787:** *Mémoire sur l’état actuel des finances de la France*. A political-financial memorandum issued with a speech by Lafayette during the Assembly of Notables; it is an intervention in government rather than a descriptive book. [[Persée authority record](https://education.persee.fr/authority/458894); [Online Books Page record](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/search?amode=words&author=marquis&c=x&showtitles=1)]

- **1787:** *Discours de M. de La Fayette* [at the bureau presided over by the comte d’Artois]. A reform speech associated in print with the financial memorandum; the surviving printed title does not provide a more distinctive authorial name. [[Persée authority record](https://femenrev.persee.fr/authority/458894)]

- **June–July 1789:** *Projet de Déclaration des droits de l’homme et du citoyen*. A manifesto and constitutional project presented to the National Assembly on 11 July. It is Lafayette’s draft—not the Assembly’s final Declaration—and its formation was collaborative: Thomas Jefferson advised him, while the adopted text emerged from several competing projects and subsequent debate. [[Archives of Seine-et-Marne](https://archives.seine-et-marne.fr/sites/archives.seine-et-marne.fr/files/media/downloads/f_lancr_dossierlafayette.pdf); [Archives nationales](https://www.archives-nationales.culture.gouv.fr/ateliers-pedagogiques/1789-construire-un-nouvel-ordre-politique); [textual-history reference](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9claration_des_droits_de_l%27homme_et_du_citoyen_de_1789)]

- **1789–1791:** *Speeches, motions, proclamations, municipal addresses, and National Guard orders*. This is a dispersed body of performative political texts rather than one obra: the documents propose measures, command troops, announce policy, or answer public bodies. The six-volume family edition preserves only a selection, and later archival inventories identify additional pieces, including the address to the Paris municipality of 8 November 1790. [[BnF description of the six-volume edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb31624263c); [Persée authority record](https://femenrev.persee.fr/authority/458894)]

- **25 August 1830:** *Ordre du jour*. An official order produced while Lafayette commanded the National Guard during the July Revolution; as a command text, it acts upon an institution and is closer to a protocol or proclamation than to an essay. [[Persée authority record](https://femenrev.persee.fr/authority/458894)]

- **1830–1834:** *Public acts and speeches concerning the July Revolution*. A late corpus of declarations, parliamentary speeches, and National Guard texts rather than a single authored work; the family edition’s final volume couples these public acts with correspondence and acknowledges editorial suppressions. [[Sainte-Beuve’s account of volume VI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/M%C3%A9moires_de_Lafayette/02)]

- **1830–1834:** *Speeches for Poland*. Addresses and political interventions made in support of Polish independence, later collected as *Les Discours de Lafayette pour la Pologne*. The collection is posthumous and editorial, so its title should not be mistaken for a book designed by Lafayette. [[bibliographical account](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_du_Motier_de_La_Fayette#Sources_et_bibliographie); [Cornell collection description](https://rare.library.cornell.edu/lafayette/)]

- **30 March 1833:** *Discours du général La Fayette à la Chambre des députés sur la prolongation de la loi d’exception contre les réfugiés politiques*. A parliamentary speech opposing an exceptional measure affecting political refugees; its separate pamphlet form is a manifestation of the speech, not a different work. [[Persée authority record](https://education.persee.fr/authority/458894)]

### Correspondence and documentary acts

- **1776–1790:** *Selected letters and papers of the American-Revolution era*. Thousands of acts of communication—private letters, diplomatic memoranda, military reports, instructions, and plans—rather than a single book. The modern five-volume scholarly edition is explicitly selective, so it establishes a documented corpus but not a complete catalogue of everything Lafayette wrote in these years. [[Cornell University Press edition](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.7591/9781501736025/html); [editorial introduction](https://scholarsbank.uoregon.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/64a685b6-e9fb-4679-8bca-4467adf29588/content)]

- **1777–1834:** *Correspondence*. Lafayette’s surviving letters form an open, distributed obra extending across family life, war, diplomacy, abolition, imprisonment, and European liberal movements. It cannot honestly be reduced to one bullet per letter: Cornell alone holds more than 11,000 Lafayette-related manuscripts, while the 1837–1838 family edition translated, abridged, selected, and sometimes suppressed letters; papers were also lost during the French Revolution. [[Cornell](https://rare.library.cornell.edu/lafayette/); [BnF](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb31624263c); [Lafayette College manuscript guide](https://academicmuseum.lafayette.edu/special/marquis/MarquisFindAids/manuscriptcollection.htm)]

- **1793–1801:** *Prison and exile letters*. A historically distinct correspondence produced during and after Lafayette’s Austrian captivity. Later editors have collected it under titles such as *Correspondance inédite* and *Lettres de prison et d’exil*, but those collection titles are posthumous manifestations, not titles Lafayette gave the corpus. [[Persée authority record](https://femenrev.persee.fr/authority/458894); [Cornell](https://rare.library.cornell.edu/lafayette/)]

- **c. 1829–1834:** *Documents and letters of the Central Polish Committee in Paris*. Administrative correspondence and political acts created through Lafayette’s direction of the committee; Cornell holds more than 400 relevant documents and letters, many of which are collective institutional products rather than works attributable to him alone. [[Cornell](https://rare.library.cornell.edu/lafayette/)]

### Projects realized through institutions and action

- **1783–1792:** *Gradual-emancipation experiment at La Belle Gabrielle*. A political and economic project intended to demonstrate that a tropical plantation could function without slavery. Lafayette acquired the Cayenne property in 1785–1786 and placed enslaved people within a planned process of gradual emancipation, but revolution, confiscation, and sale prevented completion; calling it simply an abolitionist “work” would conceal both its unfinished purpose and the coercive plantation system through which it operated. [[Lafayette College](https://sites.lafayette.edu/slavery/the-cayenne-experiments/); [Center for the Study of the American Constitution](https://csac.history.wisc.edu/2021/01/28/lafayettes-emancipation-proposal/); [Cornell University Press scholarship](https://academic.oup.com/cornell-scholarship-online/book/60094/chapter-abstract/519844264)]

### Posthumous manifestations of the corpus

- **1837–1838:** *Mémoires, correspondance et manuscrits du général La Fayette*. A six-volume posthumous assemblage made by Lafayette’s family and edited by François de Corcelle from memoir fragments, letters, proclamations, speeches, and other acts. It is indispensable as the foundational collected edition but is not itself wholly Lafayette’s obra: letters were selected, translated, sometimes abridged, and arranged by others. [[BnF](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb31624263c); [American edition’s editorial notice](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/8376/pg8376-images.html)]
