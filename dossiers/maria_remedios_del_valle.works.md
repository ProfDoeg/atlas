## Works by María Remedios del Valle

### Petitionary project

- **1826–1828:** *Claim for compensation and a military pension*. A legal-political project initiated in her name on 23 October 1826 and pursued through the national accounting office and the Buenos Aires legislature. It is not securely an authored text: Manuel Rico composed and signed the opening submission “at the request of the party,” while Manuel Ramírez, Miguel Rebelo, military witnesses, and officials produced later parts of the composite dossier; it should therefore be attributed to del Valle as claimant and directing subject, not as sole writer. [[1]](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/45702/CONICET_Digital_Nro.961dc44b-386f-45b5-9f75-c7473093c154_A.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2) [[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352837981_Maria_Remedios_del_Valle_Nineteenth-Century_Argentina_in_As_If_She_Were_Free)

### Wartime praxis

- **1810–1814:** *Care, combat, and clandestine communications in the Army of Peru*. This is a body of practice rather than a discrete obra: witnesses describe del Valle nursing, provisioning, washing and mending for troops, encouraging soldiers under fire, participating in guerrilla action, carrying correspondence, and helping royalist prisoners escape. The surviving evidence is retrospective and embedded in her pension case; the later identification of her with the “Niñas de Ayohuma,” including claims about two accompanying daughters, remains historiographically disputed. [[1]](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/45702/CONICET_Digital_Nro.961dc44b-386f-45b5-9f75-c7473093c154_A.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2) [[3]](https://revistapaginas.unr.edu.ar/index.php/RevPaginas/article/download/898/1222?inline=1)

### Manifestations that resist attribution as works

- **By 1826–1828:** *The scarred body presented as evidence of service*. Del Valle’s bullet, saber, and flogging scars functioned within the pension proceedings as an embodied archive when conventional military records could not be found. They are manifestations of warfare and punishment—not things she intentionally “made”—so they resist classification as her obra even though scholarship identifies her strategic presentation of them as a political act. [[1]](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/45702/CONICET_Digital_Nro.961dc44b-386f-45b5-9f75-c7473093c154_A.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2) [[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352837981_Maria_Remedios_del_Valle_Nineteenth-Century_Argentina_in_As_If_She_Were_Free)

### Corpus boundary

- *No securely attributable autonomous writings, artworks, buildings, compositions, or other titled works are known.* The scholarly reconstruction rests principally on the collaboratively produced pension dossier, legislative proceedings, military records, and later accounts by other people; even the correspondence she reportedly carried is not identified as correspondence she composed. Modern portraits, monuments, biographies, and patriotic representations of del Valle are works made **about** her and therefore do not belong in her corpus.
