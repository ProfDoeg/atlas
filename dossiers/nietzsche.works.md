## Works by Friedrich Nietzsche

### Published philosophical and literary obras

- **1871:** *Sokrates und die griechische Tragödie*. A privately printed essay, expanded from Nietzsche’s 1870 lecture *Socrates und die Tragoedie* and subsequently absorbed into *Die Geburt der Tragödie*.
- **1872:** *Die Geburt der Tragödie aus dem Geiste der Musik*. Nietzsche’s first book: a philological-philosophical obra joining Greek tragedy, aesthetics, and Wagnerian cultural criticism; the 1886 issue retitled it *Die Geburt der Tragödie, oder: Griechentum und Pessimismus* and added *Versuch einer Selbstkritik*.
- **1873:** *Ein Neujahrswort an den Herausgeber der Wochenschrift „Im neuen Reich“*. A short public intervention defending Wagner’s cultural project.
- **1873:** *Unzeitgemässe Betrachtungen I: David Strauss, der Bekenner und der Schriftsteller*. The first of four published “Untimely Meditations,” conceived as acts of cultural criticism rather than a completed systematic treatise.
- **1874:** *Unzeitgemässe Betrachtungen II: Vom Nutzen und Nachtheil der Historie für das Leben*. An essay on historical knowledge and life.
- **1874:** *Unzeitgemässe Betrachtungen III: Schopenhauer als Erzieher*. A philosophical portrait that uses “Schopenhauer” partly as a model for Nietzsche’s own project of self-formation.
- **1876:** *Unzeitgemässe Betrachtungen IV: Richard Wagner in Bayreuth*. An essay poised ambiguously between public tribute and emerging estrangement.
- **1878:** *Menschliches, Allzumenschliches: Ein Buch für freie Geister*. An aphoristic book and decisive reorientation away from Nietzsche’s earlier Wagnerian and metaphysical commitments.
- **1879:** *Vermischte Meinungen und Sprüche*. An aphorism collection first issued as an appendix to *Menschliches, Allzumenschliches* and later incorporated into its second volume.
- **1880:** *Der Wanderer und sein Schatten*. An aphorism collection subsequently joined with *Vermischte Meinungen und Sprüche* as *Menschliches, Allzumenschliches II*.
- **1881:** *Morgenröthe: Gedanken über die moralischen Vorurtheile*. An aphoristic inquiry into morality; Nietzsche supplied a new preface for the 1887 edition.
- **1882:** *Die fröhliche Wissenschaft*. An aphoristic-philosophical book framed by poetry; the substantially enlarged 1887 edition added a fifth book, a new preface, and *Lieder des Prinzen Vogelfrei*.
- **1883:** *Also sprach Zarathustra I*. The first part of a hybrid philosophical fiction, prose poem, parody of scripture, and self-declared gift.
- **1883:** *Also sprach Zarathustra II*. The second part of the same hybrid obra.
- **1884:** *Also sprach Zarathustra III*. The third part; the first three parts were reissued together in 1887.
- **1885:** *Also sprach Zarathustra IV: Vierter und letzter Theil*. Privately printed in a very small edition and circulated selectively; its status initially lay between publication and private communication.
- **1886:** *Jenseits von Gut und Böse: Vorspiel einer Philosophie der Zukunft*. An aphoristic book explicitly presented as a prelude rather than the promised future philosophy itself.
- **1887:** *Zur Genealogie der Moral: Eine Streitschrift*. A polemical work of three linked essays, written partly to clarify and defend *Jenseits von Gut und Böse*.
- **1888:** *Der Fall Wagner: Ein Musikanten-Problem*. A published polemic against Wagner and Wagnerian culture.
- **1888:** *Götzen-Dämmerung, oder: Wie man mit dem Hammer philosophirt*. A compact philosophical polemic prepared in 1888 and published in January 1889.
- **1888:** *Nietzsche contra Wagner: Aktenstücke eines Psychologen*. A montage assembled largely from Nietzsche’s earlier writings. He prepared and corrected it but withdrew the first printing; an authorized version appeared only after his collapse, so its boundary between new obra and self-curated dossier is unstable.

### Authorized manuscripts completed for publication

- **1888:** *Der Antichrist: Fluch auf das Christenthum*. A completed, fair-copied polemic that Nietzsche treated as the first book—and at moments the whole—of his project *Umwerthung aller Werthe*; it was published posthumously in 1895 with editorial suppressions later restored.
- **1888:** *Ecce homo: Wie man wird, was man ist*. A completed autobiographical and self-interpretive manuscript, revised through Nietzsche’s final lucid weeks but not published until 1908; early editors altered or withheld portions.
- **1888:** *Dionysos-Dithyramben*. A prepared cycle of nine poems, partly reworking material from *Also sprach Zarathustra* and earlier verse. Nietzsche did not see the collection published; its first appearance was posthumous.
- **1888:** *Nietzsche contra Wagner: Aktenstücke eines Psychologen* (final manuscript state). The authorized manuscript differs from the withdrawn printing, making “the work” a textual history rather than a single uncontested object.

### Poetry and poetic collections

- **1882:** *Idyllen aus Messina*. A cycle of eight poems published in a periodical; six were later reworked for *Lieder des Prinzen Vogelfrei*.
- **1882:** *Scherz, List und Rache: Vorspiel in deutschen Reimen*. A sequence of epigrams forming the poetic prelude to *Die fröhliche Wissenschaft*, not a wholly independent book.
- **1887:** *Lieder des Prinzen Vogelfrei*. A poetic appendix to the enlarged *Die fröhliche Wissenschaft*, combining revised Messina poems with other verse.
- **1888:** *Dionysos-Dithyramben*. A lyric cycle and authorized manuscript; it is also part of Nietzsche’s late philosophical performance under the name “Dionysus,” resisting separation into poetry versus philosophy.
- **1850s–1888:** *Jugendgedichte und lyrischer Nachlass*. Numerous titled and untitled poems, drafts, and fragments survive in the notebooks. They are textual manifestations rather than a single Nietzsche-designed collection; later “complete poetry” volumes are editorial creations.

### Posthumous writings and unfinished projects

- **1858:** *Aus meinem Leben*. A youthful autobiographical composition preserved in the early notebooks, not the same obra as the late *Ecce homo*.
- **1870:** *Das griechische Musikdrama*. The manuscript of the first public lecture in Nietzsche’s pair on Greek tragedy.
- **1870:** *Socrates und die Tragoedie*. The second lecture manuscript; Nietzsche subsequently reworked it as *Sokrates und die griechische Tragödie*.
- **1870:** *Die dionysische Weltanschauung*. An unpublished essay and important workshop text for *Die Geburt der Tragödie*.
- **1870:** *Die Geburt des tragischen Gedankens*. A presentation manuscript derived, with changes, from *Die dionysische Weltanschauung*.
- **1872:** *Über die Zukunft unserer Bildungsanstalten: Sechs öffentliche Vorträge*. A lecture project announced as six talks but stopped after five; incompleteness belongs to the obra rather than being merely a lost ending.
- **1872:** *Fünf Vorreden zu fünf ungeschriebenen Büchern*. Five actual prefaces offered as a Christmas gift to Cosima Wagner for five books that Nietzsche did not write: the project makes the absent books part of its form.
- **1872:** *Über das Pathos der Wahrheit*. The first of the five prefaces.
- **1872:** *Gedanken über die Zukunft unserer Bildungsanstalten*. The second preface, related to but not identical with the education lectures.
- **1872:** *Der griechische Staat*. The third preface.
- **1872:** *Das Verhältnis der Schopenhauerischen Philosophie zu einer deutschen Cultur*. The fourth preface.
- **1872:** *Homer’s Wettkampf*. The fifth preface.
- **1873:** *Mahnruf an die Deutschen*. A public appeal composed for Wagner’s Bayreuth enterprise and prepared for print, but withheld when Wagner declined to approve it.
- **1873:** *Die Philosophie im tragischen Zeitalter der Griechen*. An unfinished philosophical-historical book project left unpublished.
- **1873:** *Über Wahrheit und Lüge im aussermoralischen Sinne*. An unpublished essay whose familiar status as a freestanding “work” is partly posthumous and editorial.
- **1873–1874:** *Die Diadochaí der vorplatonischen Philosophen*. A philological-philosophical study dictated to Carl von Gersdorff and left unpublished.
- **1875:** *Wir Philologen*. An unfinished project for another *Unzeitgemässe Betrachtung*, surviving as plans and notebook material rather than a completed continuous text.
- **1876:** *Die Teleologie seit Kant*. An unfinished study assembled from notebook material; its integrity as a work is less secure than that of Nietzsche’s prepared publications.
- **1878:** *Memorabilia*. A named sequence within the posthumous fragments, not a separately published book.
- **1884–1885:** *Mittag und Ewigkeit*. A projected continuation or transformation of the *Zarathustra* undertaking; it survives only in plans and fragments.
- **1885–1888:** *Der Wille zur Macht: Versuch einer Umwerthung aller Werthe*. A repeatedly revised book project, never completed as a work. The books later published as *Der Wille zur Macht* were posthumous compilations by editors, not a manuscript or final table of contents made by Nietzsche.
- **1887–1888:** *Die Umwerthung aller Werthe*. A late umbrella project whose projected divisions changed repeatedly; *Der Antichrist* is its only completed component under some plans, while Nietzsche briefly treated that text as the entire “revaluation.”
- **1888:** *Der Immoralist*. A projected title or authorial role appearing in late plans, not an extant completed book.
- **1888:** *Der Philosoph: Betrachtungen über den Kampf von Kunst und Erkenntniss*. A title associated editorially with notebook materials rather than a finished Nietzschean obra; it resists treatment as an autonomous work.

### Classical-philological obras, editions, reviews, and lectures

- **1867:** *Zur Geschichte der Theognideischen Spruchsammlung*. Nietzsche’s first substantial published philological article.
- **1868:** *Beiträge zur Kritik der griechischen Lyriker I: Der Danae Klage*. A published work of textual criticism.
- **1868:** Review of G. F. Schoemann’s *Die hesiodische Theogonie*. A signed or attributable scholarly review, not a book.
- **1868:** Review of Valentin Rose’s edition of the pseudo-Anacreontic *Symposiaka hemiambia*. A short scholarly review.
- **1868:** Review of Richard Nitzsche’s *Quaestionum Eudocianarum capita quatuor*. A short scholarly review.
- **1868–1869:** *De Laertii Diogenis fontibus*. A Latin source-critical study published in two installments.
- **1869:** *Homer und die klassische Philologie*. Nietzsche’s Basel inaugural lecture, also printed as a pamphlet.
- **1869:** Review of Christoph Ziegler’s *Theognidis Elegiae*. A short scholarly review.
- **1869:** Review of Jacob Bernays’s *Die Heraklitischen Briefe*. A short scholarly review.
- **1869:** Review of Paul Marquard’s edition of Aristoxenus. A short scholarly review.
- **1869:** Review of Erwin Rohde’s study of *Loukios ē Onos*. A short scholarly review.
- **1869–1878:** *Prolegomena zu den Choephoren des Aeschylus*. Lecture notes repeatedly corrected and reused; a teaching instrument rather than a publication-ready book.
- **1869–1879:** *Die griechischen Lyriker*. Lecture notes revised across several teaching cycles.
- **1869–1870:** *Vorlesungen über lateinische Grammatik*. Lecture notes for a university course.
- **1869–1878:** *Aeschylus: Choephoren, Verse 1–450*. A student transcript of Nietzsche’s teaching, corrected and supplemented over time; because the base document is another person’s writing, it is evidence of Nietzsche’s lectures rather than an uncomplicated Nietzsche autograph work.
- **1869–1876:** *Hesiod: Genos kai bios Hēsiodou; Certamen; Erga*. Another anonymously written lecture transcript subsequently corrected and supplemented.
- **1870:** *Einleitung in die Tragödie des Sophocles*. Notes for twenty lectures.
- **1870:** *Ciceros Academica*. Lecture notes, later revised for another course.
- **1870:** *Analecta Laertiana*. A published Latin philological article.
- **1870:** *Beiträge zur Quellenkunde und Kritik des Laertius Diogenes*. A published philological monograph.
- **1870:** Review of S. A. Byk’s *Der Hellenismus und der Platonismus*. A short scholarly review.
- **1870–1871:** *Griechische Rhythmik*. Lecture notes on Greek rhythm.
- **1870–1872:** *Aufzeichnungen zur Metrik und Rhythmik*. Research notes rather than a finished treatise.
- **1870–1872:** *Zur Theorie der quantitirenden Rhythmik*. An unfinished technical study.
- **1870–1872:** *Rhythmische Untersuchungen*. An unfinished body of philological research.
- **1870–1873:** *Der florentinische Tractat über Homer und Hesiod, ihr Geschlecht und ihren Wettkampf*. A philological study published in installments.
- **1871:** *Encyclopaedie der klassischen Philologie*. University lecture notes.
- **1871:** *Certamen quod dicitur Homeri et Hesiodi*. Nietzsche’s critical edition of a Greek text with apparatus: an editorial-scholarly obra whose ancient verbal matter is not authored by him.
- **1872–1873:** *Geschichte der griechischen Beredsamkeit*. University lecture notes.
- **1874:** *Darstellung der antiken Rhetorik*. University lecture notes.
- **1874–1875:** *Geschichte der griechischen Literatur I–II*. Lecture notes later reused in teaching.
- **1874–1878:** *Einleitung zur Rhetorik des Aristoteles*. Lecture notes reused in later courses.
- **1875–1876:** *Geschichte der griechischen Literatur III*. A further lecture course.
- **1875–1878:** *Der Gottesdienst der Griechen*. Lecture notes on Greek religious practice, later reused.
- **1878:** *Plato: Apologie*. Brief lecture notes.
- **1878–1879:** *Griechische Lyriker*. A lecture transcript in an unidentified hand; it manifests Nietzsche’s teaching but cannot simply be called a manuscript he made.

### Notebook corpora and correspondence

- **1852–1869:** *Nachgelassene Aufzeichnungen der Kindheit, Jugend und Studienzeit*. Hundreds of exercises, poems, autobiographical writings, plans, excerpts, and drafts preserved in KGW I–II. This is an archival corpus, not one obra, and its modern divisions and numbering are editorial.
- **1869–1872:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notebook fragments from Nietzsche’s early Basel period; individual entries may be drafts, excerpts, mnemonic notes, or abandoned projects, so the corpus resists being called a collection authored for readers.
- **1872–1874:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. The notebooks surrounding the first *Unzeitgemässe Betrachtungen* and unpublished essays.
- **1875–1876:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notes including the unfinished *Wir Philologen* project.
- **1876–1878:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. The workshop materials for Nietzsche’s transition to aphoristic writing.
- **1878–1879:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notes associated with *Menschliches, Allzumenschliches* and its sequels.
- **1880–1881:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notebook material surrounding *Morgenröthe*.
- **1881–1882:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notes surrounding *Die fröhliche Wissenschaft*, including formulations later recruited into other works.
- **1882–1884:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Drafts and notes from the first three parts of *Also sprach Zarathustra*.
- **1884:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. A separately edited chronological stratum from spring to autumn 1884.
- **1884–1885:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notes surrounding the fourth *Zarathustra* and projected continuations.
- **1885–1887:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Notes associated with *Jenseits von Gut und Böse*, the “will to power” plans, and the genealogy project.
- **1887–March 1888:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Late plans, excerpts, drafts, and philosophical notes.
- **1888–January 1889:** *Nachgelassene Fragmente*. Nietzsche’s final notebooks. Their fragments are genuine textual remains, but later arrangements—above all *Der Wille zur Macht*—are editorial manifestations and must not be mistaken for books Nietzsche completed.
- **1850–1889:** *Briefe*. Thousands of letters survive in the critical correspondence edition. Each is a made communicative text, but the collected correspondence is an archival body rather than a single work; some transmitted texts are drafts, copies, dictated letters, or documents whose dating and status remain editorial questions.

### Posthumous constructions that are not Nietzsche-made obras

- **1901/1906:** *Der Wille zur Macht*. These editions rearranged selected notebook fragments into a purported major book. Nietzsche made the underlying notes and several changing projects called *Der Wille zur Macht*, but not this published compilation.
- **1890s–present:** *Gesammelte Werke*, thematic excerpt books, and posthumous “systematic” anthologies. These are editorial manifestations made from Nietzsche’s texts, not additional works made by Nietzsche.
- **Various dates:** Student lecture transcripts. Where the surviving wording is in another hand, the document records Nietzsche’s oral teaching but is materially the student’s text; the critical edition correctly preserves this authorship problem rather than pretending every transcript is an autograph work.
