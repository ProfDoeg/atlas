## Works by Louis XIV

### Dynastic and political instruction

- **1661–1668; revised into the early 1670s:** *Mémoires pour l’instruction du Dauphin*. An unfinished, composite obra of political instruction and retrospective self-presentation, organized around the years 1661, 1662, and 1666–1668 rather than a continuous autobiography. Its first-person royal voice does not establish sole authorship: Colbert supplied records, Périgny drafted much of the 1666–1668 material, Pellisson reconstructed the earlier years, and Louis XIV dictated, corrected, approved, and sometimes annotated the evolving text; no surviving manuscript is wholly in his hand. ([Centre de recherche du château de Versailles](https://www.chateauversailles-recherche.fr/corpus/article630.html); [Henri Bouchitté, “Les mémoires de Louis XIV”](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhmc_0996-2727_1927_num_2_12_3958); [Jean Chagnon, study of the *Mémoires*](https://archipel.uqam.ca/3747/1/M11561.pdf))

- **1661:** *Lettre du roi à sa mère sur l’arrestation de Fouquet*. A justificatory political letter to Anne of Austria explaining the arrest of Nicolas Fouquet; modern scholarly editions treat it as a separate text adjoining the *Mémoires*, although, like much royal writing, it belongs to a secretarial system rather than uncomplicated autograph authorship. ([Jean-Christian Petitfils, ed., *Le métier de roi*](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=68347))

- **1679:** *Réflexions sur le métier de roi*. A compact mirror-for-princes or set of governing maxims, subsequently transmitted with the *Mémoires* but distinct from their annual narrative. It is better classified as political reflection than memoir, and its place in the royal corpus rests on editorial and archival attribution rather than on publication by Louis XIV. ([BnF, *Mémoires et réflexions*](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35774102n); [Jean-Christian Petitfils, ed., *Le métier de roi*](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=68347))

- **3 December 1700:** *Instructions au duc d’Anjou*. A protocol of kingship prepared for Louis XIV’s grandson as he departed to become Philip V of Spain: practical injunctions about government, counsel, war, finance, and Franco-Spanish relations. It circulates under several descriptive titles, including *Mémoire remis par Louis XIV à son petit-fils*; that variation should not be mistaken for several different works. ([BnF, *Mémoires et réflexions*](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35774102n); [A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions de Louis XIV pour le Dauphin*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

- **1700–1714:** *Letters to Philip V on the government of his states*. A body of dynastic counsel rather than one fixed-title book; the early collected edition isolates seventeen such letters. Their making is distributed between royal decision, dictation, secretarial formulation, signature, and dispatch, so the collection resists treatment as seventeen conventionally authored literary works. ([University of Pennsylvania, Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Louis+XIV%2C+King+of+France%2C+1638-1715); [Grouvelle and Grimoard, eds., *Œuvres de Louis XIV*](https://books.google.com/books?id=rXOVs2bng5IC))

- **1709:** *Lettre du Roi aux gouverneurs des provinces*. A circular political manifesto addressed to provincial governors during the gravest phase of the War of the Spanish Succession: it explains and seeks support for royal policy rather than merely conveying private information. As an official royal communication it is attributable to Louis XIV’s authority, but not safely to his unaided pen. ([Jean-Christian Petitfils, ed., *Le métier de roi*](https://www.bnfa.fr/livre?biblionumber=68347))

- **probably 1710:** *Projet de harangue à la nation française*. An unfinished project for a public address, not evidence of a speech actually delivered. The date is editorially inferred rather than supplied by a securely dated title, and modern editions appropriately retain “projet” in naming it. ([Jean Longnon, ed., contents of *Mémoires; Réflexions sur le métier de roi; Instructions au duc d’Anjou; Projet de harangue*](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL2958708M/Me%CC%81moires_suivi_de_Re%CC%81flexions_sur_le_me%CC%81tier_de_roi_Instructions_au_duc_d%27Anjou_Projet_de_harangue); [A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

### Military working texts

- **1667–1668:** *Letters, orders, and memoranda concerning the campaigns of 1667 and 1668*. A dispersed operational corpus—commands, correspondence, and responses—rather than a single composed history. The 1806 collected edition mixes Louis XIV’s documents with narratives supplied by its editor, General Grimoard, so the latter’s campaign accounts must not be reassigned to the king. ([Grouvelle and Grimoard, eds., *Œuvres de Louis XIV*, vol. 3](https://books.google.com/books/about/M%C3%A9moires_et_pi%C3%A8ces_militaires.html?id=LJxh4D14bAUC))

- **1672:** *Préambule sur la guerre de 1672, with campaign orders and operational notes*. A cluster of strategic working texts embedded in the military papers, including dated orders and descriptions of army movements. It resists classification as one finished work: the collected edition constructs a readable sequence from documents made to direct action. ([Grouvelle and Grimoard, eds., *Œuvres de Louis XIV*, vol. 3](https://books.google.com/books/about/M%C3%A9moires_et_pi%C3%A8ces_militaires.html?id=LJxh4D14bAUC))

- **1673:** *Campagne de Louis XIV en 1673*. An incomplete campaign mémoire presented by the first collected edition as “written by himself.” Scholarship notes that the surviving account does not cover the whole campaign and identifies further material copied by Pellisson and annotated by the king, making both completeness and the boundary between royal composition and collaborative redaction uncertain. ([Grouvelle and Grimoard, eds., *Œuvres de Louis XIV*, vol. 3](https://books.google.com/books/about/M%C3%A9moires_et_pi%C3%A8ces_militaires.html?id=LJxh4D14bAUC); [“Les brouillons de l’absolutisme”](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-dix-septieme-siecle-2004-1-page-25?lang=fr))

- **late 1673 or early 1674:** *Projets formés par Louis XIV à la fin de 1673 ou au commencement de 1674*. Strategic projects preserved among the military papers, not a retrospectively polished book. The title and approximate dating are editorial descriptions of working material, and should not be hardened into a title or exact date Louis XIV himself supplied. ([Grouvelle and Grimoard, eds., *Œuvres de Louis XIV*, vol. 3](https://books.google.com/books/about/M%C3%A9moires_et_pi%C3%A8ces_militaires.html?id=LJxh4D14bAUC))

- **1674:** *Notes de Louis XIV sur les principaux événements de 1674*. Operational and retrospective notes rather than a completed campaign narrative. Their fragmentary form is part of the obra: they record a king’s intervention in military planning without becoming a self-contained literary “work.” ([Grouvelle and Grimoard, eds., *Œuvres de Louis XIV*, vol. 3](https://books.google.com/books/about/M%C3%A9moires_et_pi%C3%A8ces_militaires.html?id=LJxh4D14bAUC))

### Garden protocol and spatial authorship

- **1689–1705:** *Manière de montrer les jardins de Versailles*. A protocol for moving through and perceiving Versailles—part itinerary, part performance score, and part assertion of control over a Gesamtkunstwerk made by many architects, gardeners, engineers, sculptors, and painters. It survives in at least seven successively altered versions: some are secretarial copies, one circa 1702–1704 is identified as being in Louis XIV’s hand but breaks off at section 17, the June/November 1704 manuscript was corrected by him, and the March 1705 version is again secretarial; the “work” is therefore the changing protocol, not any single definitive manuscript. ([Centre de recherche du château de Versailles, manuscript census](https://chateauversailles-recherche.fr/corpus/article1110.html); [Château de Versailles](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/discover/history/key-dates/louis-xiv-guide-gardens-versailles))

- **1714:** *Marche que le roy a ordonnée pour faire voir le jardin, les bosquets et les fontaines*. A royal viewing order manifested graphically on the *Nouveau plan des ville, château et jardins de Versailles*. The map was a commercial engraved object made by others, while Louis XIV’s contribution was the prescribed movement it records; it therefore resists being called a work by him without qualification. ([Centre de recherche du château de Versailles, itinerary census](https://chateauversailles-recherche.fr/corpus/article1110.html))

### Correspondence as an obra without fixed boundaries

- **1659–1714:** *Lettres particulières*. A large, open-ended body of diplomatic, military, governmental, familial, and personal correspondence, not a finite titled work. Grouvelle and Grimoard’s standard collected edition devotes volumes V and VI to selected letters, divided into 1659–1680 and 1682–1714; selection, survival, scribal mediation, and uncertain degrees of dictation make “everything Louis XIV wrote” impossible to equate with that editorial corpus. ([Gazette Drouot, description of the six-volume *Œuvres de Louis XIV*](https://www.gazette-drouot.com/lots/12543062-louis-xiv--oeuvres-de-louis---); [Google Play Books, *Œuvres de Louis XIV*, vol. 4 and series description](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/Louis_XIV_roi_de_France_Oeuvres_de_Louis_XIV?id=PjcTAAAAQAAJ))

### Testamentary acts and last instructions

- **2 August 1714:** *Testament de Louis XIV*. A legal manifesto intended to act upon the future regency and government after the king’s death, not merely to describe his wishes. Its authorship is juridical and sovereign—made through advisers, clerks, authentication, and royal assent—rather than literary in the modern sense. ([A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

- **13 August 1715:** *Premier codicille de Louis XIV*. A supplementary testamentary act modifying the 1714 project of succession and regency. It is a performative legal text whose “making” lies in its authenticated royal force, not necessarily in autograph composition. ([A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

- **23 August 1715:** *Second codicille de Louis XIV*. A further testamentary modification made shortly before the king’s death, with the same distributed relation between royal will, legal formulation, and scribal production. ([A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

- **1 September 1715:** *Discours de Louis XIV à ses officiers et aux courtisans*. A final oral address preserved through witnesses or later transcription, not an independently surviving authored manuscript; its textual form therefore belongs partly to transmission. ([A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

- **late August 1715:** *Dernières paroles au dauphin*. An orally delivered dynastic instruction known through reported versions. It resists treatment as a fixed text because neither wording nor title has the stability of a document prepared and preserved by the king. ([A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

- **late August 1715:** *Dernières paroles au duc d’Orléans*. A reported final political instruction to the future regent rather than a securely fixed written work. Its inclusion in the corpus records an attributed speech-act, while its exact verbal form belongs to witnesses and editors as well as to Louis XIV. ([A.-G. Marquet, ed., *Mémoires et instructions*, vol. 3](https://www.mollat.com/livres/737488/louis-14-memoires-et-instructions-de-louis-xiv-pour-le-dauphin-vol-3-1667-1671-annexes))

### Royal creations that resist attribution as works by Louis XIV

- **1661–1715:** *The architectural, garden, ceremonial, administrative, and legislative production of the reign*. Versailles, Marly, royal fêtes, academies, ordinances, medals, monuments, and court spectacles were manifestations of Louis XIV’s projects and authority, but they are not securely classifiable as works authored by him: named architects, gardeners, composers, dramatists, painters, sculptors, ministers, jurists, and institutions designed and executed them. The standard collected-works edition accordingly catalogues his memoirs, military papers, correspondence, literary pieces, and historical documents—not every object commissioned or every act issued under the royal name—so folding the whole reign into a personal œuvre would erase rather than clarify authorship. ([Gazette Drouot, analytical description of the 1806 *Œuvres*](https://www.gazette-drouot.com/lots/12543062-louis-xiv--oeuvres-de-louis---); [Centre de recherche du château de Versailles, introduction to the garden-sculpture catalogue](https://sculptures-jardins.chateauversailles.fr/essais/introduction.php))
