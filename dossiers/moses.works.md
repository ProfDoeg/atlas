## Works by Moses

### Textual obra explicitly assigned to Moses within the Pentateuch

- **Undated (wilderness narrative):** *Memorial concerning Amalek*. A commanded memorandum, written “in a book” after the battle at Rephidim; neither its original extent nor an independent manuscript survives ([Exodus 17:8–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2017%3A8-16&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (Sinai narrative):** *Words of the LORD*, conventionally associated with the *Book of the Covenant*. Moses is said to write the divine words and read a “book of the covenant” aloud; whether this means Exodus 20:22–23:33 precisely is disputed ([Exodus 20:22–24:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2020%3A22-24%3A8&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (Sinai narrative):** *Covenant words*. A written covenantal protocol commanded in Exodus 34:27. The following verse ambiguously says that “he” inscribed the covenant’s ten words on the tablets: Moses is the natural immediate antecedent, but Deuteronomy assigns the inscription to God ([Exodus 34:27–28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2034%3A27-28&version=NRSVUE); [Deuteronomy 10:1–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2010%3A1-4&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (wilderness narrative):** *Stages of Israel’s journey*. An itinerary or archival list recording Israel’s departure points “stage by stage,” explicitly assigned to Moses in Numbers 33:2 ([Numbers 33:1–49](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers%2033%3A1-49&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (plains-of-Moab narrative):** *This law* / *Book of the law*. A protocol written by Moses, delivered to the priests and elders, and placed beside the covenant ark as a witness. Its boundaries are disputed: it may denote a Deuteronomic law collection rather than the complete Pentateuch ([Deuteronomy 31:9–13, 24–27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2031%3A9-13%2C24-27&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (plains-of-Moab narrative):** *Song of Moses* (*Song of Ha’azinu*). A poem functioning as a covenant witness: Deuteronomy explicitly says Moses wrote and taught it, although its language and composition are widely assigned by critical scholarship to later Israelite tradition ([Deuteronomy 31:16–32:47](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2031%3A16-32%3A47&version=NRSVUE)).

### Collective, oral, and performative obra

- **Undated (Exodus narrative):** *Song of the Sea*. A collective victory song performed by Moses and the Israelites. Exodus says that they sang it, not unambiguously that Moses composed it; it therefore resists classification as his individual literary work ([Exodus 15:1–21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2015%3A1-21&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (plains-of-Moab narrative):** *Blessing of Moses*. A sequence of tribal blessings presented as Moses’ final spoken benediction. It is a performative text—a blessing that acts—rather than a document the narrative says he wrote, and critical scholarship commonly treats it as transmitted poetry ([Deuteronomy 33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2033&version=NRSVUE)).

### Corpora attributed by later tradition

- **Traditionally assigned to Moses; critically composed much later:** *Torah* / *Pentateuch*—*Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers,* and *Deuteronomy*. Jewish and Christian tradition made this five-book corpus Moses’ principal textual obra, sometimes understanding him as a divinely dictated scribe; the Pentateuch itself explicitly assigns him only several smaller written units, while the overwhelming critical consensus finds multiple compositional layers and authors ([Christopher Rollston](https://www.thetorah.com/article/who-wrote-the-torah-according-to-the-torah); [Konrad Schmid, Institute for Advanced Study](https://www.ias.edu/ideas/2018/schmid-torah)).

- **Traditionally assigned to Moses; undated:** *Book of Job*. The Babylonian Talmud names Moses as its writer, but Job itself is anonymous and modern critical scholarship does not accept Mosaic authorship; this is an attribution history, not a securely identifiable Mosaic obra ([Bava Batra 14b–15a](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.14b-15a)).

- **Traditionally assigned to Moses; undated:** *The portion of Balaam* (Numbers 22–24). The Babylonian Talmud unusually identifies this portion separately alongside Moses’ “book”; it survives only as part of Numbers and is not an independently recoverable work ([Bava Batra 14b–15a](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.14b-15a); [Numbers 22–24](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers%2022-24&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Traditionally assigned to Moses; undated:** *Psalm 90: A Prayer of Moses, the Man of God*. Its superscription associates the prayer with Moses but does not necessarily claim authorship, and neither its date nor Mosaic composition can be established ([Psalm 90](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2090&version=NRSVUE)).

### Constructed and inscribed objects

- **Undated (Rephidim narrative):** *YHWH-nissi* (“The LORD is my banner”). An altar built and named by Moses after the battle with Amalek; it is an architectural memorial rather than a text, although its name carries a declarative message ([Exodus 17:8–16](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2017%3A8-16&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (Sinai narrative):** *Altar and twelve stone pillars at Sinai*. A covenant installation built by Moses, with one pillar corresponding to each tribe. Its combination of architecture, sacrifice, recitation, blood rite, and written covenant approaches a ritual Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single autonomous object ([Exodus 24:3–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2024%3A3-8&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (Sinai narrative):** *Second pair of stone tablets*. Moses hewed the physical tablets after breaking the first pair, which had been made and inscribed by God. The attribution of the second inscription is internally unstable: Exodus 34 can imply Moses wrote it, whereas Deuteronomy 10 says God did ([Exodus 32:15–19; 34:1–4, 27–28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2032%3A15-19%3B34%3A1-4%2C27-28&version=NRSVUE); [Deuteronomy 10:1–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2010%3A1-5&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (Sinai narrative):** *Acacia-wood ark*. In Deuteronomy’s first-person account Moses says that he made an ark, but Exodus attributes the sanctuary ark’s fabrication to Bezalel; these may be competing accounts, two conceptions of the ark, or an attribution of commissioned work to Moses ([Deuteronomy 10:1–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Deuteronomy%2010%3A1-5&version=NRSVUE); [Exodus 37:1–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2037%3A1-9&version=NRSVUE)).

- **First day of the first month, second year in the narrative:** *Tabernacle installation*. Bezalel, Oholiab, and other artisans fabricated its components, but Moses erected, arranged, anointed, and consecrated the assembled sanctuary and “finished the work.” It is therefore best classified as a commissioned collective project and ritual Gesamtkunstwerk, not simply an object handmade by Moses ([Exodus 35:30–36:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2035%3A30-36%3A1&version=NRSVUE); [Exodus 40](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Exodus%2040&version=NRSVUE)).

- **Undated (wilderness narrative):** *Bronze serpent on a pole*. A metal cultic and healing object explicitly made and mounted by Moses. Numbers leaves it unnamed; 2 Kings later calls it *Nehushtan* and reports that Hezekiah destroyed it after Israelites made offerings to it ([Numbers 21:4–9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Numbers%2021%3A4-9&version=NRSVUE); [2 Kings 18:1–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Kings%2018%3A1-4&version=NRSVUE)).
