## Works by Miller Smith Puckette

### Software environments and instruments

- **1988–present:** *Max*. A graphical programming environment for real-time music and multimedia, first written at IRCAM and commercially released in 1990; subsequent commercial development involved David Zicarelli and others, so present-day Max is not solely Puckette’s obra. [UC San Diego identifies Puckette as its original author.](https://music-cms.ucsd.edu/people/faculty/emeritus_faculty/miller-puckette/index.html)

- **1996–present:** *Pure Data (Pd)*. An open-source, real-time music and multimedia programming environment: simultaneously a programming language, an instrument-building workshop, and infrastructure for other works. It remains a collective project despite Puckette’s primary authorship. [The first publication describing it dates from 1996](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html); [the maintained distribution is documented here](https://msp.ucsd.edu/software.html).

- **2001–present:** *Pd Repertory Project*. An open-source preservation and performance project developed by Puckette with Kerry Hagan and Arshia Cont: not a composition, but a changing collection of executable realizations, study patches, documentation, and protocols for live-electronic repertory. Its contents include realizations associated with works by Philippe Manoury, Rand Steiger, Karlheinz Stockhausen, Pierre Boulez, Jean-Claude Risset, James Tenney, John Chowning, Jonathan Harvey, and Charles Dodge; attribution for the underlying compositions remains with those composers. [Project documentation](https://msp.ucsd.edu/pdrp/latest/doc/)

- **2007–present:** *Smeck*. A Pure Data performance patch and six-channel guitar processor that treats the strings separately; it is both software instrument and mutable collection of presets, rather than a fixed musical composition. [Puckette’s documentation calls it the patch described in “Patch for Guitar.”](https://msp.ucsd.edu/smeck/latest/doc/index.htm)

- **2021:** *The Null Piece*. A deliberately under-composed starting framework for building live-performance patches, supplying routing and parameter control while withholding stylistic decisions. It resists being called a musical work: its purpose is to become the technical ground from which other works are made. [Article and project description](https://periodicos.unespar.edu.br/vortex/article/view/4538)

- **2021–present:** *Reality Check*. A preservation and verification framework that tests whether a real-time electronic realization still produces its intended results as hardware and software change. It is best classified as a protocol embodied in software, not as a composition. [Original description](https://periodicos.unespar.edu.br/vortex/article/view/4538); [later framework paper](https://msp.ucsd.edu/Publications/icmc24-reprint.pdf)

- **undated, current distribution:** *PdMax*. A port, made by Puckette and Ted Apel, that runs Pd through the `pd~` mechanism inside Max/MSP 6–8. It is a software bridge between environments rather than an independent language. [Official software distribution](https://msp.ucsd.edu/software.html)

- **undated, current version 0.92:** *Quacktrip*. An application and Pd patch for low-latency, uncompressed network audio connections between musicians. [Official documentation](https://msp.ucsd.edu/tools/quacktrip/)

- **undated, current version 0.92:** *Netty McNetface*. A multi-user manifestation of Quacktrip organized around a central repeater; it is distributed both as an application and as a Pd patch. [Official documentation](https://msp.ucsd.edu/tools/quacktrip/)

- **undated:** *Music101*. A network-audio application distributed alongside Quacktrip and Netty McNetface. The official distribution names it but supplies no authorship statement or creation date beyond its inclusion in Puckette’s software suite, so its precise status within his obra is uncertain. [Official distribution](https://msp.ucsd.edu/tools/quacktrip/)

- **2016–2025:** *Ideas*. An evolving repository of example patches, realizations, and support files associated with Puckette’s talks and publications—including the 3f algorithm, quaternion oscillator, neural-network examples, *All You Need Is Lunch*, ESP32 Pd, and historical *Jupiter* realizations. It resists division into autonomous works because most files depend on another publication, lecture, or musical realization for their meaning. [Repository index](https://msp.ucsd.edu/ideas/)

### Musical, audiovisual, and installation works

- **1993–present:** *Gandy Bridge*. The collective title for the Convolution Brothers’ early, partly composed and partly improvised electronic performances by Puckette, Cort Lippe, and Zack Settel. Its materials were repeatedly reworked, so it is better understood as an unstable performance project or Gesamtkunstwerk than as a fixed score. [Cort Lippe’s account of the group and project](https://www.cortlippe.com/convolution-brothers.html)

- **2012:** *Illud etiam*. Philippe Manoury’s work for soprano and live electronics, made with Puckette as scientific designer and electronic collaborator. It belongs primarily to Manoury’s catalogue, not Puckette’s, but Puckette made an indispensable computational realization; sources disagree between 2012 and 2013 because the latter is also used for the soprano–clarinet–electronics version. [IRCAM work record](https://ressources.ircam.fr/en/work/illud-etiam); [Manoury’s account](https://www.philippemanoury.org/illud-etiam/)

- **2018–2019:** *Remnant*. A sound installation and optional live ensemble performance by Kerry Hagan and Puckette, built from impulse responses that reveal how human bodies disturb an interior acoustic. Its coexistence as installation, generative process, score, recordings, and performance makes it a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a single fixed text. [Score and programme note](https://www.kerrylhagan.net/portfolio/media/remnant/remnant_score.pdf); [bibliographic record](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2020–2021:** *All You Need Is Lunch*. A six-minute fixed-media audiovisual work by Puckette and Kerry Hagan, presented under their duo identity “the Higgs whatever”; it transforms a montage of existing popular recordings through post-mix vocoding. Sources describe it as developed during the pandemic, with a dated video manifestation in January 2021 and conference presentation in 2023, rather than assigning a single unambiguous composition date. [Technical account](https://msp.ucsd.edu/Publications/smc23-reprint.pdf); [2023 concert programme](https://smcnetwork.org/smc2023/SMC_2023_Concert_Program.pdf)

### Book-length works and edited collections

- **1986:** *Shannon Entropy and the Central Limit Theorem*. Puckette’s 63-page Harvard doctoral dissertation in mathematics. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2007:** *The Theory and Technique of Electronic Music*. A book-length technical and pedagogical obra about digital sound synthesis and processing, also circulated online. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2020:** *Between the Tracks: Musicians on Selected Electronic Music*. An edited scholarly collection made with Kerry L. Hagan; Puckette also contributed its chapter on Charles Dodge’s *Speech Songs*. Editorial construction and authored chapter are distinct kinds of making within the same book-object. [MIT Press record](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262539302/between-the-tracks/)

### Manuals and protocols

- **1989:** *Max Reference Manual for Version 2.0*. Thirty-one pages of internal IRCAM documentation: a protocol for using Max rather than a description detached from the software. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **1991:** *MAX Development Package Manual*. A co-authored manual by Puckette and David Zicarelli, published by Opcode. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

### Patented inventions

- **1995:** *Synthèse parole musique*. French patent no. 94 02655, an industrial and legal manifestation of Puckette’s work on musical and vocal synthesis. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **1996:** *Process and Device for Musical and Vocal Dynamic Sound Synthesis by Non-linear Distortion and Amplitude Modulation*. U.S. patent no. 5,524,173; a technical invention expressed as an enforceable protocol rather than as a scholarly paper or composition. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2015:** *The Sampling Theorem and Its Discontents*. ICMC keynote presentation; its primary status is an acted, delivered text, with video and example files as additional manifestations. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html); [supporting files](https://msp.ucsd.edu/ideas/)

- **2017:** *The Oscillator as a Dynamical System*. Conference paper. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2017:** *Four Surprises of Electronic Music*. Journal article. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2019:** *Remnant: Exploring Presence and Absence Through Acoustic Disturbances in Space*. Conference paper by Puckette and Kerry Hagan about their installation; it is a textual manifestation distinct from the installation itself. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2020:** *Hand Gesture to Timbre Converter*. Conference paper by Puckette and Kerry Hagan. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2020:** *Five Incitements for Electronic Music Makers*. An essay in *Ideas Sonoras* whose title and hortatory form make it closer to a manifesto—an intervention intended to provoke practice—than to a neutral technical description. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)

- **2021:** *The Null Piece and Reality Check*. Journal article describing two software frameworks; it is the textual manifestation of the protocol and software projects listed above. [Journal record](https://periodicos.unespar.edu.br/vortex/article/view/4538)

- **2022:** *A Quaternion-Phase Oscillator*. Conference paper accompanied by executable Pd examples. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html); [supporting files](https://msp.ucsd.edu/ideas/)

- **2023:** *Post-Mix Vocoding and the Making of All You Need Is Lunch*. Conference paper by Puckette and Kerry Hagan explaining the technique and production of their audiovisual work. [Paper](https://msp.ucsd.edu/Publications/smc23-reprint.pdf)

- **2024:** *Reality Check—A Framework for Preserving Real-Time Electronic Music Realizations*. Conference paper describing the preservation framework’s later development. [Paper](https://msp.ucsd.edu/Publications/icmc24-reprint.pdf)

- **2025:** *Jupiter: Four Decades of Evolution of a Live Computer Music Piece*. Conference paper about successive realizations of Philippe Manoury’s *Jupiter*. Puckette made and maintained electronic realizations, but the underlying composition is Manoury’s. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html); [associated realizations](https://msp.ucsd.edu/ideas/)

- **2025:** *Future-Proofing a Large Electronic Music Realization*. Conference paper on preserving a substantial live-electronic realization. [Author’s bibliography](https://msp.ucsd.edu/publications.html)
