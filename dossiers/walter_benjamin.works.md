## Works by Walter Benjamin

### Books, dissertations, book-projects, and large-scale obra

- **1918–1919:** *Der Begriff der Kunstkritik in der deutschen Romantik*. Doctoral dissertation and Benjamin’s first completed book-length scholarly obra; submitted at Bern in 1919 and printed in 1920.
- **1921–1922:** *Goethes Wahlverwandtschaften*. Long philosophical-literary essay conceived on the scale of a book and first serialized in 1924–1925.
- **1923–1925:** *Ursprung des deutschen Trauerspiels*. Book-length study submitted unsuccessfully as a habilitation thesis; published as a book in 1928. Its rejection belongs to the work’s institutional history, not to its authorship or completion.
- **1923–1926:** *Einbahnstraße*. Montage-book of aphorisms, dream records, notices, slogans, and prose miniatures, published in 1928 as a typographically constructed brochure with a cover by Sasha Stone; it approaches a literary Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a conventional treatise.
- **1926–1927:** *Moskauer Tagebuch*. Private travel-and-love diary kept in Moscow; an autobiographical record rather than a book prepared for publication.
- **1927–1940:** *Pariser Passagen / Das Passagen-Werk*. Unfinished research-and-writing project composed of quotations, notes, convolutes, drafts, and two exposés. The familiar title *Das Passagen-Werk* is posthumous: the surviving materials resist being treated as a completed book.
- **1928–1929:** *Pariser Passagen I–II*. Early essayistic manifestations of the Arcades project, including *Der Saturnring oder Etwas vom Eisenbau*; neither constitutes its finished form.
- **1932:** *Berliner Chronik*. Autobiographical manuscript and mnemonic project from which *Berliner Kindheit* developed; it was not completed as a lifetime publication.
- **1932–1934; 1938:** *Berliner Kindheit um neunzehnhundert*. Constellation of prose miniatures surviving in several arrangements, notably the Adorno–Rexroth version and the 1938 “Fassung letzter Hand.” It is an obra with no single uncontested definitive form.
- **1931–1932; 1936:** *Deutsche Menschen*. Book assembled from twenty-five historical letters with Benjamin’s commentaries, published in Switzerland in 1936 under the pseudonym Detlef Holz; anthology, covert political intervention, and montage at once.
- **1934–1938:** *Charles Baudelaire. Ein Lyriker im Zeitalter des Hochkapitalismus*. Unfinished book-project connected to the Arcades project; its surviving essays and drafts are stages, not chapters of a completed final book.
- **1937–1938:** *Das Paris des Second Empire bei Baudelaire*. Major Baudelaire study rejected in its submitted form by the Institute for Social Research; a completed draft subsequently dismantled and reworked.
- **1938–1939:** *Zentralpark*. Sequence of numbered Baudelaire notes and reflections; neither a conventional essay nor merely preparatory jottings.
- **1939:** *Über einige Motive bei Baudelaire*. Essay made from the revision of the rejected Baudelaire study and published during Benjamin’s lifetime.
- **1940:** *Über den Begriff der Geschichte*. Theses and associated notes written in several manuscript and typescript witnesses. Benjamin did not authorize a final publication text, so the work’s title, ordering, and completeness retain editorial complications.

### Philosophical and critical essays

- **1912:** *Dialog über die Religiosität der Gegenwart*. Philosophical dialogue.
- **1913:** *Erfahrung*. Polemical essay against the adult appropriation of “experience.”
- **1913:** *Romantik*. Essay on historical and contemporary Romanticism.
- **1913:** *Der Moralunterricht*. Essay on ethics and education.
- **1913–1914:** *Metaphysik der Jugend*. Philosophical-literary essay assembled around conversation, youth, and experience.
- **1914–1915:** *Das Leben der Studenten*. Programmatic essay and manifesto for a transformed student life.
- **1914–1915:** *Zwei Gedichte von Friedrich Hölderlin*. Comparative philosophical interpretation of two poems.
- **1916:** *Trauerspiel und Tragödie*. Philosophical fragment distinguishing two dramatic and historical forms.
- **1916:** *Die Bedeutung der Sprache in Trauerspiel und Tragödie*. Companion fragment on language and dramatic form.
- **1916:** *Über Sprache überhaupt und über die Sprache des Menschen*. Philosophy-of-language essay circulated privately rather than published by Benjamin.
- **1917–1918:** *Über das Programm der kommenden Philosophie*. Programmatic philosophical project, left unpublished.
- **1919:** *Schicksal und Charakter*. Philosophical essay.
- **1920–1921:** *Theologisch-politisches Fragment*. Very short speculative text whose dating remains disputed; it is an editorially titled fragment, not a manifesto issued by Benjamin.
- **1921:** *Zur Kritik der Gewalt*. Philosophical and political essay on law, violence, and justice.
- **1921:** *Die Aufgabe des Übersetzers*. Philosophical preface written for Benjamin’s Baudelaire translations; both translation theory and a declaration of translational practice.
- **1922:** *Ankündigung der Zeitschrift: Angelus Novus*. Prospectus-manifesto for a journal that Benjamin planned but never produced; the project, rather than a missing periodical, is the work.
- **1929:** *Der Sürrealismus. Die letzte Momentaufnahme der europäischen Intelligenz*. Essay treating Surrealism as a political and historical manifestation.
- **1929:** *Programm eines proletarischen Kindertheaters*. Protocol-like theoretical program written in connection with Asja Lācis’s theatrical practice.
- **1929:** *Eine kommunistische Pädagogik*. Short pedagogical intervention.
- **1930–1931:** *Karl Kraus*. Large critical essay.
- **1931:** *Literaturgeschichte und Literaturwissenschaft*. Methodological essay.
- **1931:** *Kleine Geschichte der Photographie*. Historical-theoretical essay on photography and reproducibility.
- **1931:** *Paul Valéry*. Critical portrait.
- **1931–1933:** *Lehre vom Ähnlichen* and *Über das mimetische Vermögen*. Two related manifestations of Benjamin’s theory of mimetic capacity, not simply interchangeable versions.
- **1933:** *Erfahrung und Armut*. Essay-manifesto on modern poverty of experience.
- **1933:** *Zum gegenwärtigen gesellschaftlichen Standort des französischen Schriftstellers*. Political-literary essay completed across exile and published in 1934.
- **1934:** *Der Autor als Produzent*. Lecture-text and political intervention; its status as an actually delivered lecture is uncertain.
- **1934:** *Franz Kafka. Zur zehnten Wiederkehr seines Todestages*. Major critical essay.
- **1934–1935:** *Johann Jakob Bachofen*. Intellectual portrait written in French and German-related forms.
- **1935–1939:** *Das Kunstwerk im Zeitalter seiner technischen Reproduzierbarkeit*. Essay-project surviving in several materially and conceptually distinct German versions and a French publication, *L’œuvre d’art à l’époque de sa reproduction mécanisée*, translated with Pierre Klossowski. No single version should silently stand for the whole work.
- **1936:** *Der Erzähler. Betrachtungen zum Werk Nikolai Lesskows*. Essay on storytelling; Benjamin also made a French version, *Le Narrateur*.
- **1937:** *Eduard Fuchs, der Sammler und der Historiker*. Essay on collecting, caricature, and materialist historiography.
- **1938:** *Ein deutsches Institut freier Forschung*. Institutional and political account of the Institute for Social Research.
- **1938:** *Das Land, in dem das Proletariat nicht genannt werden darf*. Political-literary essay.
- **1938–1939:** *Kommentare zu Gedichten von Brecht*. Critical commentaries on Brecht’s poetry.
- **1939:** *Was ist das epische Theater?* Two substantially different essay versions, the first made around 1931 and the second in 1939.
- **1939:** *Die Rückschritte der Poesie von Carl Gustav Jochmann*. Edition and critical introduction, developed from work begun in 1937.
- **1939–1940:** *Sur Scheerbart*. French-language essay fragment.

### Literary portraits, city texts, thought-images, and feuilletons

- **1924:** *Neapel*. City portrait made with Asja Lācis; collaborative authorship must be retained.
- **1925:** *Traumkitsch*. Short theoretical prose on dream, kitsch, and Surrealism.
- **1926:** *Aussicht ins Kinderbuch*. Illustrated-book criticism and material history of children’s reading.
- **1927:** *Moskau*. City essay distinct from the private *Moskauer Tagebuch*.
- **1928:** *Weimar*. Travel and cultural-historical feuilleton.
- **1928:** *Kulturgeschichte des Spielzeugs*. Essay-review on the material history of toys.
- **1928:** *Spielzeug und Spielen*. Essay on play and the child’s transformation of objects.
- **1928:** *Gespräch mit Anne May Wong*. Published interview or staged conversational portrait; its dialogic mediation makes it more than a transparent transcript.
- **1928:** *Jahrmarkt des Essens*. Gastronomic city feuilleton.
- **1928:** *Goethe*. Encyclopedic biographical essay.
- **1929:** *Marseille*. City thought-image.
- **1929:** *San Gimignano*. City thought-image.
- **1929:** *Juden in der deutschen Kultur*. Cultural-historical intervention.
- **1930:** *Russische Spielsachen*. Material-cultural feuilleton.
- **1931:** *Ich packe meine Bibliothek aus*. Essayistic performance of collecting and autobiography.
- **1931–1933:** *Denkbilder*. A retrospectively grouped constellation of brief prose pieces, dreams, city images, and reflections rather than a single book Benjamin definitively assembled.
- **1932–1933:** *Kurze Schatten*. Two separately published sequences of thought-images.
- **1933:** *Agesilaus Santander*. Two dated manuscript versions of an enigmatic autobiographical-allegorical text; neither may be assumed to cancel the other.
- **1933:** *Kleine Kunst-Stücke*. Group of miniature prose pieces.
- **1933:** *Geschichten aus der Einsamkeit*. Story-cycle or grouping whose integrity is less secure than that of a lifetime book.
- **1934:** *Die Zeitung*. Short reflection on newspaper form.
- **1934:** *Käuflich doch unverwertbar*. Literary-critical miniature.
- **1934:** *Vier Geschichten*. Group of short narratives.
- **1934:** *Auf die Minute*. Short story.
- **1935:** *Gespräch über dem Corso*. Dialogic feuilleton.
- **1935:** *Die glückliche Hand*. Short narrative.
- **1935:** *Rastelli erzählt*. Story.
- **1936:** *Pariser Brief (1): André Gide und sein neuer Gegner*. Literary-political report.
- **1936:** *Pariser Brief (2): Malerei und Photographie*. Report on the relation between painting and photography.
- **1938:** *Peintures chinoises à la Bibliothèque Nationale*. French-language exhibition or collection report.
- **1938:** *Eine Chronik der deutschen Arbeitslosen*. Review-essay on unemployment and documentary writing.
- **1938:** *Roman deutscher Juden*. Literary-historical essay.

### Radio creations and broadcast performances

- **1927–1933:** *Rundfunkarbeiten*. A corpus of more than eighty broadcasts made for Berlin and Frankfurt radio: talks, children’s programs, literary presentations, interviews, readings, games, radio plays, and “Hörmodelle.” Many broadcasts survive only as scripts, announcements, or documentary traces, so broadcast event, written text, and lost sound-object must not be collapsed into one kind of work.
- **1929:** *Gides Berufung*. Literary radio talk.
- **1929:** *Bücher von Thornton Wilder und Ernest Hemingway*. Radio review.
- **1929:** *Berliner Dialekt*. Radio talk on speech and urban language.
- **1929–1930:** *Was die Deutschen lasen, während ihre Klassiker schrieben*. Radio series and literary-historical montage.
- **1930:** *Pariser Köpfe*. Radio portrait.
- **1930:** *Friedrich Sieburgs Versuch „Gott in Frankreich?“*. Radio review.
- **1930:** *Myslowitz–Braunschweig–Marseille: Die Geschichte eines Haschisch-Rausches*. Radio story derived from drug-experiment material.
- **1930:** *Radau um Kasperl*. Radio play for children; the original broadcast sound is lost, making the surviving script an incomplete manifestation of the work.
- **1930:** *Die Mietskaserne*. Radio talk or feature on urban housing.
- **1930:** *Straßenhandel und Markt in Alt-Berlin*. Radio talk.
- **1930:** *Berliner Spielzeugwanderung I–II*. Two-part children’s broadcast.
- **1930:** *Borsig*. Industrial-historical broadcast.
- **1930:** *Theodor Hosemann*. Radio portrait.
- **1930–1931:** *Die Stunde des Kindes*. Not a single authored text but the programming context for numerous children’s broadcasts Benjamin wrote and often delivered.
- **1931:** *Dr. Faust*. Radio play or dramatized broadcast.
- **1931:** *Cagliostro*. Radio play or dramatized historical narrative.
- **1931:** *Die Bastille, das alte französische Staatsgefängnis*. Historical children’s broadcast.
- **1931:** *Neapel*. Broadcast adaptation of the earlier city text.
- **1931:** *Untergang von Herculanum und Pompeji*. Historical radio talk.
- **1931:** *Erdbeben von Lissabon*. Historical radio talk.
- **1931:** *Der Mississippi, sein Lauf und seine Geschichte*. Geographical-historical broadcast.
- **1931:** *Die Berliner Schnauze*. Radio talk on Berlin speech.
- **1931:** *Wie nehme ich meinen Chef?*. “Hörmodell,” a didactic dramatic protocol for analyzing everyday conflict.
- **1931:** *Was die Deutschen lasen, während ihre Klassiker schrieben: E. T. A. Hoffmann und Oskar Panizza*. Literary radio montage.
- **1932:** *Was die Deutschen lasen, während ihre Klassiker schrieben: Johann Peter Hebel*. Literary radio broadcast.
- **1932:** *Das dämonische Berlin*. Urban-historical broadcast.
- **1932:** *Ein Tag in der Irrenanstalt*. Radio report or dramatized documentary.
- **1932:** *Die Unterschrift*. “Hörmodell” about authority, consent, and written commitment.
- **1932:** *Das kalte Herz*. Radio adaptation associated with Wilhelm Hauff’s tale.
- **1932:** *Kinderliteratur*. Radio talk on writing and books for children.
- **1932:** *Theater und Rundfunk*. Media-theoretical radio essay.
- **1932:** *Was ist ein Hörspiel?*. Radio-theoretical intervention.
- **1932:** *Aufklärung für Kinder*. Collective descriptive label used for Benjamin’s pedagogical broadcasts, not a single stable work-title.
- **1933:** *Briefmarkenschwindel*. Radio narrative.
- **1933:** *Das zweite Ich*. Radio play or dramatic broadcast.
- **1933:** *Lichtenberg: Ein Querschnitt*. Experimental radio play and documentary montage; Benjamin called its form a “cross-section,” and it survives chiefly as text rather than intact acoustic event.

### Translations and editorial manifestations

- **1923:** *Charles Baudelaire: Tableaux Parisiens*. Book of German translations from Baudelaire, with Benjamin’s *Die Aufgabe des Übersetzers* as preface; translation is the made work here, not Baudelaire’s poems.
- **1924:** *Die Photographie von der Kehrseite*. Translation of Tristan Tzara’s text on Man Ray.
- **1925:** *Anabasis*. Partial German translation of Saint-John Perse’s *Anabase*.
- **1925:** *Ursula Mirouet*. German translation of Balzac’s *Ursule Mirouët*.
- **1926:** *Die göttliche Eleonora Duse*. Translation of Gabriele D’Annunzio.
- **1927:** *Im Schatten der jungen Mädchen*. German translation of Proust’s *À l’ombre des jeunes filles en fleurs*, made with Franz Hessel; the collaboration precludes sole attribution.
- **1928:** *Don Juan und der Schuhputzer; Briefmarken; Damentoilette; Café Certâ*. Translated extracts from Louis Aragon’s *Le Paysan de Paris*.
- **1929–1932:** *Novellen* by Marcel Jouhandeau. A group of translations—including *Der Dorfbräutigam*, *Fräulein Zéline*, *Prudence Hautechaume*, *Léda*, *Die Schäferin “Nanou”*, and *Das Château de la Folie*—made and published separately rather than as one securely bounded Benjamin book.
- **1930:** *Guermantes*. German translation of Proust’s *Le Côté de Guermantes*, made with Franz Hessel; its publication history was interrupted and the complete translation appeared only posthumously.
- **1930:** *Über das Lesen*. Translation of an extract from Marcel Proust’s *Sur la lecture*.
- **1930:** *Vorrede zu Neue Französische Erzähler*. Translation of Félix Bertaux’s preface.
- **1932:** *Auslegung der Gemeinplätze*. Translation of Léon Bloy’s *Exégèse des lieux communs*.
- **1932:** *Kluge Jungfrau*. Translation of Adrienne Monnier’s *Vierge sage*, published under Monnier’s pseudonym J.-M. Sollier.
- **1933–1934:** *Enfance Berlinoise*. Partial French translation of *Berliner Kindheit*, made with Jean Selz; collaboration and incompleteness are part of its status.
- **1936:** *L’œuvre d’art à l’époque de sa reproduction mécanisée*. French manifestation of the artwork essay, translated with Pierre Klossowski and altered through editorial negotiation; it is not a transparent substitute for any German version.
- **1936:** *Le Narrateur*. French version of *Der Erzähler*.
- **1939:** *Allemands de quatre-vingt-neuf*. French-language manifestation related to *Deutsche Menschen*.
- **1939:** *Notes sur les Tableaux parisiens de Baudelaire*. French notes on Baudelaire’s poems.

### Reviews, journalism, and criticism as an obra of serial publication

- **1926–1940:** *Kritiken und Rezensionen*. Several hundred signed, pseudonymous, anonymous, and sometimes collaboratively mediated reviews, notices, surveys, and review-essays made for newspapers, journals, and the Institute for Social Research. This is not one text, but the critical edition treats it as a recoverable work-corpus; individual attribution and dating are occasionally uncertain.
- **1926–1933:** *Beiträge für Die literarische Welt*. A serial journalistic practice comprising reviews, interviews, reports, literary portraits, and polemics; it resists classification as a single work while remaining a sustained made corpus.
- **1926–1933:** *Beiträge für die Frankfurter Zeitung*. A dispersed corpus of feuilletons, reviews, travel pieces, city images, and cultural criticism.
- **1935–1940:** *Rezensionen für die Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung*. Reviews and review-essays written in exile, sometimes commissioned, revised, rejected, or published anonymously; documentary evidence does not always support a simple equation between manuscript and printed object.
- **1936:** *Vorschläge für den Besprechungsteil der Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung*. Editorial protocol made with Theodor W. Adorno; collaborative rather than solely Benjamin’s.

### Autobiographical records, protocols, notebooks, and documentary objects

- **1906:** *Pfingstreise von Haubinda aus*. Juvenile travel record and the earliest item retained in the standard chronological work-list.
- **1911:** *Tagebuch Pfingsten 1911*. Juvenile diary.
- **1911:** *Tagebuch von Wengen*. Travel diary.
- **1911:** *Von der Sommerreise 1911*. Travel record.
- **1912:** *Meine Reise in Italien Pfingsten 1912*. Travel diary or school-age journey account.
- **1912–1940:** *Lebensläufe / Curriculum vitae*. At least six distinct autobiographical documents made for different institutional and political circumstances; they are functional self-presentations, not one stable autobiography.
- **1916/1917–1940:** *Verzeichnis der gelesenen Schriften*. Long-running reading register; documentary tool and intellectual self-map rather than a literary work in the ordinary sense.
- **1927–1934:** *Protokolle zu Drogenversuchen*. Records of hashish, mescaline, and related experiments, written partly by Benjamin and partly by other participants. The corpus therefore resists undifferentiated attribution to him.
- **1928:** *Notizen von der Reise nach Frankfurt*. Travel notes.
- **1928:** *Verstreute Notizen Juni bis Oktober 1928*. Notebook sequence rather than a finished text.
- **1929:** *Notiz über ein Gespräch mit Balázs*. Documentary memorandum.
- **1931:** *Tagebuch Mai–Juni 1931*. Diary.
- **1931:** *Tagebuch vom siebenten August neunzehnhunderteinunddreißig bis zum Todestag*. Ten-day diary whose title names an anticipated or symbolic “death-day,” not Benjamin’s actual death.
- **1934:** *Notizen Svendborg Sommer 1934*. Working and travel notes from his stay with Brecht.
- **circa 1934:** *Materialien zu einem Selbstporträt*. Preparatory autobiographical dossier rather than a completed self-portrait.
- **1938:** *Tagebuchnotizen 1938*. Diary notes.
- **11–12 October 1939:** *Rêve du 11/12 octobre 1939*. French-titled dream record.
- **Not securely dated:** *Notizhefte und Notizblöcke*. Numerous numbered and unnumbered notebooks containing drafts, quotations, calculations, addresses, bibliographies, and everyday records. Each is a physical made object, but many do not possess authorial titles or the boundaries of independent “works.”
- **Not securely dated:** *Manuskriptkonvolute und Einzelblätter*. Archival bundles and loose leaves preserving drafts and notes; their present groupings may be authorial, editorial, or archival, and must not automatically be mistaken for finished works.
- **Not securely dated:** *Adressverzeichnisse*. Address books and lists—functional documentary artifacts included in the critical edition, although they resist being called literary works.
- **1920s–1940:** *Bibliographische Verzeichnisse*. Working bibliographies made for projects, reviews, and research; protocols of intellectual labor rather than completed texts.
- **circa 1930s:** *Lichtenberg-Bibliographie*. Specialized working bibliography connected with Benjamin’s Lichtenberg research and radio work.
- **1930s:** *Exzerpte aus der Neuen Zeit*. Reading excerpts from the socialist periodical; compilatory research objects whose authorship lies in selection and arrangement, not in the quoted language.
- **1910–1940:** *Briefe*. A vast epistolary corpus addressed to family, friends, collaborators, editors, and institutions. Individual letters are made texts, but correspondence editions also contain letters by others; the corpus is not one work and its survival is incomplete.

### Unrealized and incomplete projects

- **1921–1922:** *Angelus Novus*. Planned journal for which Benjamin wrote an announcement and developed an editorial program; no issue appeared, so the project—not a nonexistent periodical—is the obra.
- **1920s:** *Politik*. Planned book associated with essays and fragments including *Zur Kritik der Gewalt* and the *Theologisch-politisches Fragment*; its exact proposed contents changed and no finished manuscript survives.
- **1920s:** *Wahre Politik*. Projected political-philosophical work known through plans and fragmentary materials.
- **1920s:** *Das wahre Gesicht des Barock*. Uncompleted or proposed continuation of Benjamin’s Baroque research; it survives as intention and notes rather than a book.
- **1927–1928:** *Passagen*. First phase of the Paris arcades project, initially imagined with Franz Hessel. Hessel’s precise contribution and the project’s changing authorship-model remain matters for scholarly reconstruction.
- **1930s:** *Baudelaire book*. Repeatedly reconceived as part, model, or “miniature” of the Arcades project; *Das Paris des Second Empire*, *Zentralpark*, and *Über einige Motive bei Baudelaire* are manifestations of that project, not a recovered final book.
- **1930s:** *Brecht-Kommentare*. Planned series or book of commentaries on Brecht’s poems; only portions were completed.
- **1930s:** *Notizen zu einer Theorie des Spiels*. Dispersed project on play, toys, mimesis, and children’s activity; it has no securely bounded final text.
- **1939–1940:** *Fortsetzung der Berliner Kindheit*. Possible late revisions and transmission plans are documented, but no new complete authorially settled book resulted.
- **1940:** The manuscript carried during Benjamin’s attempted escape from France. Witnesses reported a heavy briefcase containing a manuscript, but its contents and fate are unknown. It cannot responsibly be given a title, identified with the Arcades project, or listed as a surviving work.
