## Works by Athanasius Kircher

### Authored books and treatises

- **1631:** *Ars magnesia*. Kircher’s first printed work: an experimental and mathematical treatise on magnets, organized around demonstrations, theorems, and proposed applications. It is a text, but also the record of an experimental project. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii, 1–3](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1635:** *Primitiae gnomonicae catoptricae*. An illustrated treatise on mirror-assisted sundials and reflected-light timekeeping; its diagrams function partly as construction protocols. [Merrill, p. xxxiii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1636:** *Prodromus Coptus sive Aegyptiacus*. A preliminary Coptic grammar and linguistic study conceived as preparation for Kircher’s larger Egyptian project. Its philology is historically important, although its projected recovery of ancient Egyptian wisdom was not achieved. [Merrill, p. xxxiii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1638:** *Specula Melitensis encyclica*. A short description of Kircher’s cylindrical calculating instrument for calendrical and astronomical problems. The surviving work is simultaneously text, diagram, and protocol for an instrument; Merrill dates the Naples printing to 1638, though it is frequently listed as 1637. [Merrill, p. xxxiii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1641:** *Magnes sive de arte magnetica*. A three-part natural-philosophical treatise making magnetism a model for physical, musical, cosmic, political, and divine attraction; substantially enlarged editions appeared in 1643 and 1654. The editions are manifestations of one expanding obra, not three independent works. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii, 7–13](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1643:** *Lingua Aegyptiaca restituta*. A Coptic dictionary and linguistic treatise intended to “restore” Egyptian; with the *Prodromus*, it became an early foundation of European Coptic studies, while its claims about hieroglyphic meaning proved untenable. [Merrill, p. xxxiii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1646:** *Ars magna lucis et umbrae*. An encyclopedic work on light, shadow, optics, projection, astronomy, color, and catoptrics, incorporating both experiments and designs for machines; a revised edition appeared in 1671. It is close to a paper Gesamtkunstwerk because metaphysics, images, instruments, spectacle, and experimental protocols are deliberately inseparable. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii, 14–16](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf); [Vermeir](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2007.03.008)

- **1647:** *Rituale ecclesiae Aegyptiacae sive Cophitarum*. A Latin rendering of Coptic liturgical material. It is better classified as a translation and editorial mediation than as an independently authored work. [Merrill, p. xxxiii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1650:** *Musurgia universalis sive ars magna consoni et dissoni*. A two-volume encyclopedia of sound, music, acoustics, composition, instruments, animal voices, and cosmic harmony. Its diagrams, compositions, combinatorial systems, and machine designs make it another multimedia obra rather than merely a prose book. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii, 17–20](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1650:** *Obeliscus Pamphilius*. A study and purported hieroglyphic interpretation of the obelisk erected in Bernini’s Fountain of the Four Rivers in Piazza Navona. The decipherment is erroneous, but the work also belongs to the public monument’s program and therefore sits between book, antiquarian project, and urban spectacle. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii, 21–23](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1652–1654:** *Oedipus Aegyptiacus*. A vast, multi-volume attempt to reconstruct a universal ancient theology through Egyptian hieroglyphs, comparative religion, languages, antiquities, Hermetism, and Cabala. It is the principal manifestation of Kircher’s unfinished—and fundamentally mistaken—project to decipher hieroglyphic writing symbolically. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii, 24–29](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1656:** *Itinerarium exstaticum sive opificium coeleste*. A cosmological dialogue in which the characters Theodidactus and Cosmiel travel imaginatively through the heavens. It mixes scientific exposition with visionary fiction; Gaspar Schott annotated and revised the 1660 manifestation, *Iter exstaticum Kircherianum*. [Merrill, pp. xxxiii–xxxiv, 30–34](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1657:** *Iter extaticum II, qui et Mundi subterranei prodromus dicitur*. A second imaginary journey, this time through the interior of the earth, serving as a prospectus for *Mundus subterraneus*. It is both autonomous dialogue and preliminary manifestation of the later project. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1658:** *Scrutinium physico-medicum contagiosae luis, quae pestis dicitur*. A medico-natural-philosophical investigation of plague, based partly on Kircher’s microscopic observations and proposing living agents of contagion. Its observations were suggestive, but they do not amount to a modern germ theory. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv, 35–37](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1661:** *Diatribe de prodigiosis crucibus*. A short investigation of cross-shaped markings that appeared after an eruption of Vesuvius, interpreting them through natural processes rather than accepting them simply as miracles. A possibly revised second edition is reported for 1666, but Merrill marks its status with a question. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1663:** *Polygraphia nova et universalis ex combinatoria arte detecta*. A treatise proposing systems of universal writing, cryptography, and multilingual communication. It is at once a text, a protocol, and an unrealized project for mechanically assisted universal language. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1664–1665:** *Mundus subterraneus*. A two-volume encyclopedia of the earth’s interior, volcanoes, waters, fossils, minerals, organisms, mining, and alchemy. Its speculative networks of underground fire and water turn the book into a model-world assembled from reports, experiments, images, and conjecture. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv, 38–43](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1665:** *Historia Eustachio-Mariana*. A devotional and antiquarian history of the sanctuary at Mentorella, written in conjunction with Kircher’s campaign to restore it. It acts as history, fundraising instrument, and manifesto for a physical place. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf); [Kučera and Kučerová](https://iris.unimol.it/handle/11695/89897)

- **1665:** *Arithmologia sive de abditis numerorum mysteriis*. A study of number symbolism, Pythagoreanism, Cabala, divination, and combinatorial correspondences. It is not simply mathematics but an attempted symbolic technology for reading creation. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv, 44–45](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1666:** *Obelisci Aegyptiaci … interpretatio hieroglyphica*. A description and purported interpretation of Egyptian obelisks in Rome, especially the obelisk erected at Santa Maria sopra Minerva. Its readings are no longer accepted; the book participates in a larger urban and antiquarian project rather than standing wholly apart from the monuments. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1667:** *China monumentis, qua sacris qua profanis … illustrata*. An illustrated compilation concerning China, Central Asia, languages, religions, natural history, and the Jesuit missions. Much of its material came from missionaries and collaborators, so “by Kircher” here means compiler, arranger, interpreter, and publicizer rather than sole eyewitness-author. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv, 46–49](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1667:** *Magneticum naturae regnum*. A compact synthesis presenting attraction and sympathy as organizing principles throughout nature. It is a late condensation of the larger magnetic project rather than an entirely separate intellectual undertaking. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv, 50–51](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1669:** *Ars magna sciendi*. An encyclopedic combinatorial system derived from Ramon Llull, intended to organize and generate knowledge across disciplines. It is less a conventional treatise than a protocol—and ultimately an unrealized machine-project—for universal knowing. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv–xxxv, 52–54](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1669:** *Latium, id est nova et parallela Latii tum veteris tum novi descriptio*. An illustrated historical, geographical, antiquarian, and mythographic survey of ancient and modern Latium. The work combines itinerary, regional atlas, historical reconstruction, and imagined antiquity. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv–xxxv, 55–57](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1669:** *Principis christiani archetypon politicum sive sapientia regnatrix*. A political and dynastic encomium, also issued under the title *Splendor domus Joanniae*. It is an occasional text designed to act through praise and patronage and therefore approaches a manifesto or courtly performance more closely than a disinterested description. [Merrill, pp. xxxiv–xxxv, 58–59](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1673:** *Phonurgia nova*. A treatise on acoustics, echoes, amplification, speaking tubes, architectural sound, and the marvelous effects of voices. Its “new art of sound” is simultaneously theory, instrument manual, collection of designs, and manifesto for sound as an operative force. [Merrill, pp. xxxv, 60–62](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1675:** *Arca Noë*. An illustrated reconstruction of Noah’s Ark and an attempted account of its dimensions, inhabitants, provisioning, and the postdiluvian distribution of animals. It is a scriptural commentary made as a model-building project on paper. [Merrill, pp. xxxv, 63–65](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1676:** *Sphinx mystagoga*. A hieroglyphic interpretation prompted by an Egyptian mummy and its inscriptions. It extends the *Oedipus* project, but its purported decipherments have no standing in modern Egyptology. [Merrill, pp. xxxv, 66–67](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1679:** *Turris Babel*. An illustrated investigation of the Tower of Babel, early architecture, language division, and postdiluvian migration. Like *Arca Noë*, it is a hybrid of scriptural exegesis, comparative linguistics, speculative engineering, and paper architecture. [Merrill, p. xxxv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1679:** *Tariffa Kircheriana, id est inventum auctoris novum*. A calculating table or “expanded Pythagorean table” designed to simplify arithmetic operations. It is properly a computational instrument and protocol manifested as a printed object, not merely a book to be read. [Merrill, pp. xxxv, 68–69](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

### Collaborative, edited, and posthumous textual objects

- **1660:** *Pantometrum Kircherianum*. Gaspar Schott’s description and explanation of a geometrical instrument invented by Kircher; a second edition appeared in 1669. The instrument is Kircher’s obra, but the printed exposition is Schott’s, so treating the whole book as solely Kircher-authored would erase the collaboration. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **1678:** *Romani Collegii Societatis Jesu Musaeum celeberrimum*. Giorgio de Sepi’s illustrated catalogue of Kircher’s museum, produced with substantial input from Kircher and incorporating images from his earlier books. It is neither securely a Kircher book nor independent of him: it is a collaborative “virtual museum” and a representation of a larger Gesamtkunstwerk. [Findlen’s review of the modern edition](https://brill.com/view/journals/jjs/3/4/article-p728_22.xml)

- **1680:** *Physiologia Kircheriana experimentalis*. Johann Stephan Kestler extracted and reorganized experiments from Kircher’s extensive writings in Rome in 1675, publishing the compilation in 1680. Its contents are Kircherian, but the selection, order, and resulting textual object are Kestler’s editorial creation. [ETH Zürich bibliographical record](https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/4849702)

- **By 1666; first published posthumously, 1682/1684:** *Vita admodum reverendi P. Athanasii Kircheri Societatis Jesu*. Kircher’s unfinished Latin autobiography covers his life only through 1666 and circulated posthumously. Its publication history is unsettled: Merrill found that the autobiography was absent from a copy of Langenmantel’s 1684 *Fasciculus epistolarum* despite repeated bibliographical claims that it appeared there. [Merrill, p. xxxii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf); [English presentation of the 1682 text](https://www.wichita.edu/academics/fairmount_las/geology/wparcell/Kircher/Autobiography/Kircher_autobiography.php)

- **Seventeenth century; principally unpublished during his life:** *Correspondence and manuscript papers*. More than 2,000 letters and manuscripts survive under Kircher’s name, but they are a documentary body rather than one fixed, authored work, and many letters participate in collaborative Jesuit information networks. Calling the archive a single obra would conceal its distributed authorship; listing every letter as an independent work would confuse acts of correspondence with finished works. [Merrill, p. 6](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf); [Kircher–Duke August correspondence edition](https://diglib.hab.de/edoc/ed000005/comment.htm)

### Instruments, machines, and operative designs

- **Before 1638:** *Specula Melitensis*. A cylindrical calculating device devised for calendrical and astronomical operations and documented in the similarly titled treatise. The instrument, rather than its printed description, is the made thing; the survival of an original physical example is not established by Merrill. [Merrill, p. xxxiii](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **Seventeenth century, before 1660:** *Pantometrum Kircherianum*. A multipurpose geometrical instrument for measurement and calculation, later explained in print by Gaspar Schott. Kircher’s authorship attaches to the device’s invention, not straightforwardly to Schott’s book. [Merrill, p. xxxiv](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documento970/JesuitScholar.pdf)

- **By 1650:** *Arca musarithmica*. A box-and-table system for generating contrapuntal music through combinatorial procedures, described in *Musurgia universalis*. At least three historical physical implementations survive, but the relationship of each surviving box to Kircher’s own hand or workshop is not uniform; it is safest to call the system his invention and the individual boxes manifestations made by others. [José María Domínguez](https://anuariomusical.revistas.csic.es/index.php/anuariomusical/article/view/390)

- **Mid-seventeenth century:** *Organum mathematicum*. A compartmented teaching and information device using inscribed rods or tablets for arithmetic, geometry, fortification, chronology, astronomy, music, and cryptography. Kircher devised an early form and sent operating instructions to Gottfried Aloys Kinner, but Gaspar Schott subsequently enlarged and published the system, making authorship of its final form collaborative. [Organum Mathematicum overview and primary-reference trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organum_Mathematicum)

- **1640s–1670s:** *Catoptric and optical theatres*. Kircher designed or displayed camera-obscura rooms, projection arrangements, anamorphic devices, burning mirrors, and mirror cabinets in which images appeared, multiplied, or transformed. Some were built museum apparatus, others paper designs, and surviving descriptions do not always let us decide which; that resistance to being counted as a stable set of “works” is part of their status. [Vermeir](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2007.03.008)

- **1640s–1670s:** *Magnetic devotional machines*. Kircher described or displayed devices in which magnets animated sacred narratives, including Christ rescuing Peter and a mechanically staged Resurrection. These were simultaneously experiments, toys, devotional manifestations, and arguments in “applied metaphysics,” making the separation of text, ritual image, and machine misleading. [Vermeir](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2007.03.008)

- **Seventeenth century:** *Sunflower or magnetic clock*. A device said to tell time through a sunflower or magnetic pointer following the sun became associated with Kircher’s museum and publications. Its operative reality and causal explanation were contested even in Kircher’s milieu, so it should be recorded as an attributed experimental project rather than an unquestionably successful clock. [Vermeir](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2007.03.008)

- **By 1650:** *Katzenklavier* or *cat piano*. Kircher reported a keyboard of cats as an anecdotal device for dispelling a prince’s melancholy; neither Kircher nor Schott claimed clearly to have constructed or witnessed it. It resists classification as Kircher’s work at all: scholarship treats it as a probably imaginary machine transmitted through overlapping stories, not as a securely made instrument. [Bonnie Gordon](https://doi.org/10.7208/chicago/9780226825151.003.0004)

- **Seventeenth century:** *Speaking and listening statues*. Acoustic figures and “oracles” using concealed speaking tubes formed part of Kircher’s sound experiments and museum dramaturgy. Individual mechanisms are difficult to separate between Kircher’s designs, assistants’ fabrication, and later catalogue representation; they are best understood as collaborative manifestations of his phonurgic project. [Waddell](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.endeavour.2009.11.003); [Vermeir](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2007.03.008)

- **Seventeenth century:** *Hydraulic automata and fountains*. Kircher’s museum included mechanically animated figures and water-driven displays, including a figure that dispensed liquids for visitors. These were experiential machines rather than texts, but their fabrication involved assistants and their exact individual authorship is not recoverable. [Waddell](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.endeavour.2009.11.003)

- **Seventeenth century:** *Aeolian and hydraulic musical instruments*. Kircher described and experimented with wind- and water-powered sound-making devices across *Musurgia universalis*, *Phonurgia nova*, and the museum. The corpus combines built objects, adaptations of older technologies, and unrealized diagrams, so it cannot honestly be converted into a definitive inventory of individually completed works. [Vermeir](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2007.03.008)

### Environments, spectacles, and institutional obras

- **1620s, probably 1625:** *Heiligenstadt ceremonial spectacle*. For a visit by the Elector-Archbishop of Mainz, Kircher devised a theatrical display involving fireworks and moving scenery. The precise title, date, physical extent, and division of labor are not securely preserved, so this is a documented event-manifestation rather than a fixed work. [Biblioteca Universitaria di Genova biographical study](https://www.bibliotecauniversitaria.ge.it/export/sites/bug/documenti/UetP/7_CONTESTO_TELOGICO_FILOSOFICO/Kircher.pdf)

- **From 1651 to 1680:** *Museum Kircherianum at the Collegio Romano*. Kircher transformed an inherited collection into a changing theatre of natural objects, antiquities, ethnographic material, relics, optical effects, acoustic devices, automata, and experiments. It is his clearest Gesamtkunstwerk, although not his alone: donations, Jesuit correspondents, assistants, artisans, and later custodians continually made and remade it. [Waddell](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.endeavour.2009.11.003); [Findlen’s review of the museum catalogue](https://brill.com/view/journals/jjs/3/4/article-p728_22.xml)

- **1661–1665 and after:** *Restoration of the sanctuary of Santa Maria della Mentorella*. Kircher located, promoted, raised support for, and organized the renewal of the dilapidated sanctuary and its associated sacred landscape. It is a building-and-landscape project rather than a solely authored building: patrons and craftsmen executed it, while Kircher supplied research, narrative, fundraising, and sustained intention. [Kučera and Kučerová](https://iris.unimol.it/handle/11695/89897); [Kircher’s 1665 correspondence](https://diglib.hab.de/edoc/ed000005/comment.htm)
