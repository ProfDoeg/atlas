## Works by Frances Amelia Yates

### Monographs: sustained scholarly obras

- **1934:** *John Florio: The Life of an Italian in Shakespeare’s England*. A scholarly biography and cultural history; Cambridge University Press required Yates to shorten the manuscript before publication.
- **1936:** *A Study of Love’s Labour’s Lost*. A short critical monograph treating Shakespeare’s comedy through its French and Italian intellectual settings.
- **1947:** *The French Academies of the Sixteenth Century*. A large institutional and intellectual history, developed from Yates’s earlier research on French social drama.
- **1959:** *The Valois Tapestries*. An illustrated iconographical and political study of eight festival tapestries; identification of their occasion remains an interpretive reconstruction rather than an uncontested attribution.
- **1964:** *Giordano Bruno and the Hermetic Tradition*. A revisionary intellectual history presenting Bruno within Renaissance Hermetism and magic; its broad “Yates thesis” became influential but has also been substantially disputed.
- **1966:** *The Art of Memory*. A history of artificial memory from antiquity through the Renaissance, joining intellectual history, literature, architecture, imagery, and mnemonic practice.
- **1969:** *Theatre of the World*. An interdisciplinary reconstruction linking Vitruvian architecture, Robert Fludd’s memory theatre, John Dee, and the Shakespearean stage; the proposed reconstruction of the Globe is conjectural.
- **1972:** *The Rosicrucian Enlightenment*. A history of the Rosicrucian manifestos and their religious, political, Hermetic, and scientific setting; “enlightenment” here names Yates’s historical construction, not a contemporary organization.
- **1975:** *Astraea: The Imperial Theme in the Sixteenth Century*. An obra assembled from related studies of imperial symbolism, monarchy, pageantry, Elizabeth I, and religious chivalry rather than conceived initially as one continuous book.
- **1975:** *Shakespeare’s Last Plays: A New Approach*. A book made from Yates’s Northcliffe Lectures, interpreting the late plays through chivalric, imperial, magical, and masque traditions. Its American manifestation was titled *Majesty and Magic in Shakespeare’s Last Plays*.
- **1979:** *The Occult Philosophy in the Elizabethan Age*. A synthetic study of Christian Cabala, Hermetism, magic, melancholy, John Dee, and Elizabethan literature.
- **1982:** *Lull and Bruno*. The first posthumous volume of *Collected Essays*, planned by Yates but completed editorially after her death; it is principally a new manifestation of six earlier studies, with her linking introductions.
- **1983:** *Renaissance and Reform: The Italian Contribution*. The second posthumous volume of *Collected Essays*. It realizes Yates’s intended project but its final selection and arrangement are editorial.
- **1984:** *Ideas and Ideals in the North European Renaissance*. The third posthumous volume of *Collected Essays*. Unlike the preceding volumes, its editors explicitly state that the selection was theirs; it also contains unfinished autobiographical writing and the standard bibliography of Yates’s publications.

### Theses and early research projects

- **1926:** *Contribution to the Study of the French Social Drama in the Sixteenth Century*. Yates’s University of London M.A. thesis, surviving as a manuscript scholarly project rather than a published book.
- **1930s:** *John Florio / the Florios / Torriano research papers*. A multi-box research corpus from which the Florio biography and later articles emerged; it resists treatment as a single finished work.
- **Undated:** *Leicester’s Church*. Notes for an intended article. This is evidence of a project, not of a completed or published text.
- **Undated:** *Melancholy*. A research dossier later feeding Yates’s work on Dürer, Agrippa, George Chapman, and inspired melancholy; its boundaries as an independent work are uncertain.
- **Undated–1981:** *Thirty Years’ War*. Notes filed as a distinct project in the Yates Papers, but no finished text is identified in the archive catalogue.

### Scholarly articles and chapters

- **1925:** *English Actors in Paris during the Lifetime of Shakespeare*. Yates’s first published scholarly article, reconstructing English theatrical activity in France.
- **1927:** *Some New Light on “L’Écossaise” of Antoine de Montchrestien*. A documentary literary-historical article.
- **1937–1938:** *Italian Teachers in Elizabethan England*. A study of Italian language teachers and cultural intermediaries in England.
- **1942:** *Shakespeare and the Platonic Tradition*. An essay placing Shakespearean literature in relation to Renaissance Platonism.
- **1943:** *An Italian in Restoration England*. A biographical-cultural study of the lexicographer Giovanni Torriano.
- **1944:** *Paolo Sarpi’s History of the Council of Trent*. A historiographical essay on Sarpi and his major history.
- **1947:** *Queen Elizabeth as Astraea*. A major iconographical essay on Elizabethan imperial symbolism, later incorporated into *Astraea*.
- **1949:** *The Italian Academies*. A survey of Italian academies as institutions of Renaissance culture.
- **1951:** *Transformations of Dante’s Ugolino*. A study of the changing reception and representation of the Ugolino episode.
- **1951:** *Giordano Bruno: Some New Documents*. A documentary article adding evidence about Bruno’s life and intellectual networks.
- **1954:** *The Art of Ramon Lull: An Approach to It through Lull’s Theory of the Elements*. A substantial reconstruction of Lull’s combinatory art; later reprinted in *Lull and Bruno*.
- **1954:** *Considérations de Bruno et de Campanella sur la monarchie française*. A French-language study, also known in Yates’s papers as *Bruno and Campanella on the French Monarchy*.
- **1956:** *The Religious Policy of Giordano Bruno*. An article interpreting Bruno’s projected religious reform; later reprinted in *Lull and Bruno*.
- **1957:** *The Emblematic Conceit in Giordano Bruno’s De gli eroici furori and in the Elizabethan Sonnet Sequences*. A comparative study of Bruno’s emblems and English poetic form.
- **1960:** *Ramon Lull and John Scotus Erigena*. A study of intellectual relationships behind Lull’s system; later reprinted in *Lull and Bruno*.
- **1964:** *Giordano Bruno’s Conflict with Oxford*. A study of Bruno’s Oxford controversy and its philosophical setting.
- **1967:** *The Allegorical Portraits of Sir John Luttrell*. An iconographical article based on two related portraits; its identifications are historical interpretations rather than inscriptions supplied by the objects themselves.
- **1967:** *The Hermetic Tradition in Renaissance Science*. A synthetic essay extending Yates’s Hermetic interpretation into the history of science.
- **1968:** *Architecture and the Art of Memory*. An illustrated essay connecting architectural form and mnemonic systems.
- **1969:** *Renaissance Philosophers in Elizabethan England: John Dee and Giordano Bruno*. A comparative intellectual-history essay, later reprinted in *Lull and Bruno*.
- **1973:** *Copernicus*. A commemorative essay on Copernicus and the intellectual setting of heliocentrism.
- **1974:** *The Renaissance Festival as Cultural History: The Joyeuse Magnificences, Paris 1581*. A festival study treating spectacle, imagery, politics, and urban performance as a composite historical object.
- **1974:** *Nicolas Houel: Parisian Apothecary of the Sixteenth Century*. A biographical study connected with Yates’s work on Parisian festivals and imagery.
- **1974:** *Les Fêtes de la Renaissance*. A French-language festival study appearing in two parts; the archive preserves offprints as well as drafts.
- **1976:** *Lodovico da Pirano’s Memory Treatise*. A study of a Renaissance mnemonic manuscript.
- **1977:** *Did Newton Connect His Maths and Alchemy?* A short intervention in the debate over the relation between Newton’s mathematical and alchemical pursuits.

### Review essays and critical interventions

- *The Spirit of Chivalry*. A review essay whose supplied title was added in the collected edition; it should not be mistaken for the title under which Yates originally submitted it.
- *Foxe as Propagandist*. A review essay on John Foxe and Reformation representation; its collected title may be editorial.
- *Broken Images*. A review essay on iconoclasm; the title belongs to its journal or collected manifestation rather than necessarily to Yates’s manuscript.
- *A Great Magus*. A review essay on John Dee.
- *Bacon’s Magic*. A review essay responding to Paolo Rossi’s *Francis Bacon: From Magic to Science*.

- *The Fear of the Occult*. A review of Brian P. Copenhaver’s study of Symphorien Champier.

### Autobiographical and unfinished literary projects

- **1913:** *Untitled or presently unidentified short story published in the Glasgow Weekly Herald*. A juvenile fiction publication reported by Yates’s biographers; without a securely verified title it should not be supplied with an invented one.
- **No date:** *Crystal Water*. A short family play performed at home by James, Hannah, Ruby, and Frances Yates. The surviving typescript and manuscript are filed among James S. Yates’s papers, so Frances’s precise share in its authorship is not established; it resists attribution as her independent obra.
- **Before 1981:** *Autobiographical Fragments*. Draft recollections intended by Yates to be worked into publishable form. J. N. Hillgarth selected and prefaced the posthumous 1984 manifestation, so the printed sequence is partly an editorial creation.
- **Before 1981:** *Goldsworthy Lowes Dickinson biography*. An unfinished biographical project left incomplete at Yates’s death; portions appeared posthumously, but the intended whole never became a finished book.
- **Before 1981:** *Yates family history*. Notebooks and research toward a publication on the Yates family and its theatrical connections. The archive describes it explicitly as a publication project, but no completed obra resulted.

### Editorial and composite manifestations

- **1941–1981:** *Journal of the Warburg and Courtauld Institutes editorial work*. A four-decade editorial practice rather than a single authored text. Yates helped make the journal as a scholarly manifestation, but individual volumes cannot simply be counted as her sole works.
- **1982–1984:** *Collected Essays*. A three-volume publication project initiated by Yates and completed by editors after her death. It is simultaneously a posthumous obra and a set of new manifestations of earlier articles, reviews, lectures, and fragments; counting every reprinted component again as a newly authored work would confuse creation with edition.
