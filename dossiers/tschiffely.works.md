## Works by Aimé Félix Tschiffely

### Journey-projects

- **1925–1928:** *The Buenos Aires–Washington ride*. A self-conceived equestrian demonstration-project undertaken with the Criollo horses Mancha and Gato: Tschiffely explicitly proposed the journey as a test of the breed, then covered the Americas in 504 stages. It is an embodied journey rather than a text; *Tschiffely’s Ride* is its later literary manifestation. ([La Nación](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/revista-lugares/el-profesor-suizo-que-llego-a-new-york-con-solo-dos-caballos-criollos-para-demostrar-la-destreza-de-nid21042025/); [Fundación Azara](https://www.fundacionazara.org.ar/img/libros/bienes-destacados-del-transporte-del-complejo-museografico-provincial-enrique-udaondo.pdf))

- **1930s:** *Ride through rural Britain*. An equestrian journey through England, Wales, and Scotland, later shaped into *Bridle Paths*. The estate bibliography does not supply sufficiently precise journey dates, so none are imposed here. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm))

- **1937–1938?:** *Journey through Patagonia and Tierra del Fuego*. A motor journey undertaken after Tschiffely returned to South America, subsequently manifested as *This Way Southward*. Sources place the journey in the later 1930s but do not agree on a narrower date. ([Literature of Travel and Exploration](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135456634_A23803512/preview-9781135456634_A23803512.pdf); [Patagonian bibliography](https://www.patbrit.org/bil/sams/sams-bibl-2012.pdf))

- **Post-1945, before 1952:** *Motorcycle journey through Spain*. A four-month, approximately 5,000-mile journey from Finisterre to Gibraltar, undertaken on a donated motorcycle because postwar currency restrictions made the intended horse journey impracticable. It became *Round and About Spain*. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm))

### Travel narratives and memoir

- **1933:** *Tschiffely’s Ride: Being the Account of 10,000 Miles in the Saddle through the Americas from Argentina to Washington*; also issued as *Southern Cross to Pole Star* and drafted as *10,000 Miles in the Saddle*. A book-length travel narrative made from the 1925–1928 ride; the differing titles are manifestations of the same obra, not separate works. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/tschiffelys-ride.htm); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **1936:** *Bridle Paths: The Story of a Ride through Rural England*. An equestrian travel narrative and portrait of rural Britain, produced from Tschiffely’s ride through England, Wales, and Scotland. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm); [Literature of Travel and Exploration](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135456634_A23803512/preview-9781135456634_A23803512.pdf))

- **1940:** *This Way Southward: A Journey through Patagonia and Tierra del Fuego*. A motor-travel narrative and ethnographic-historical account of southern South America. Its first editions appeared in New York and London in 1940; the frequently cited 1945 date belongs to a later London edition containing an addendum about Tschiffely’s subsequent return to Argentina. ([ABAA first-edition record](https://www.abaa.org/book/1702679736); [Patagonian bibliography](https://www.patbrit.org/bil/sams/sams-bibl-2012.pdf); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **1951:** *Bohemia Junction*. An autobiographical assemblage of roughly forty years of travel, encounters, and adventure rather than a continuous account of one journey; manuscript, corrected proof, and galley states survive. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **1952:** *Round and About Spain*. A motorcycle-travel narrative produced from Tschiffely’s postwar Spanish journey. The archive preserves a 1951 printer’s typescript, distinguishing the work’s composition from its 1952 publication. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

### Biography and history

- **1937:** *Don Roberto: Being the Account of the Life and Works of R. B. Cunninghame Graham, 1852–1936*. A biography of Tschiffely’s friend and literary mentor; it was later issued under the title *Tornado Cavalier*, a retitling rather than a second obra. ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/graham-robert-bontine-cunninghame); [Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm))

- **1943:** *Coricancha: Garden of Gold—Discovery of Peru and Conquest of the Inca Empire*. A popular historical narrative of the Inca empire and Spanish conquest, not a journey memoir despite its frequent placement beside Tschiffely’s travel books. ([Atenea](https://revistas.udec.cl/atenea/article/view/15535); [CiNii bibliographic record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA28699038))

- **1953:** *The Man from Woodpecker Creek*. A short biography for younger readers of Patagonian pioneer E. Lucas Bridges, constructed from Bridges’s autobiography and additional stories and notes. It should not be confused with Bridges’s own *Uttermost Part of the Earth*, which is a source, not a Tschiffely work; Tschiffely’s corrected typescript and an associated working map survive. ([Patagonian bibliography](https://www.patbrit.org/bil/sams/sams-bibl-2012.pdf); [Literature of Travel and Exploration](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135456634_A23803512/preview-9781135456634_A23803512.pdf); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

### Fiction and writing for children

- **1934:** *The Tale of Two Horses*. A children’s animal narrative retelling the trans-American ride through Mancha and Gato’s voices, with drawings by Tschiffely and a preface by R. B. Cunninghame Graham. Sources variously assign it to 1934, 1935, and 1949; 1934 is supported by the scholarly travel-literature reference, while 1949 appears to describe a later edition rather than the work’s first appearance. ([Literature of Travel and Exploration](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135456634_A23803512/preview-9781135456634_A23803512.pdf); [Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **1948:** *Ming and Ping*. An illustrated children’s animal story centered on Ping the penguin. The archive holds a 1947 typescript, revised conclusion, and two deleted conclusions; the published work therefore has several pre-publication textual states. ([U.S. Copyright Office, *Catalog of Copyright Entries*, 1948](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/06/Catalog_of_Copyright_Entries_1948_Books_Jan-Dec_3D_Ser_Vol_2_Pt_1A_%28IA_catalogofcopyrig321libr%29.pdf); [Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **1953:** *Matt Cass: A Tale of a Man from the North*. A novel or adventure tale rather than travel testimony; the standard estate bibliography provides little further description, and no stronger generic classification is imposed here. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm))

- **1957, posthumous:** *Little Princess Turtle Dove*. A children’s fantasy involving an Inca princess, a winged llama, and a realm of animals; it was published after Tschiffely’s death with a preface by Roy Campbell. Its exact composition date is not established by the consulted sources, so the publication date is not presented as a writing date. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm); [study of Roy Campbell](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/58915831.pdf))

- **Unfinished at Tschiffely’s death in 1954:** *Tarna*. A “lost” novel which the literary estate says Tschiffely was still writing when he died. It is therefore an unfinished project and surviving text, not securely a completed obra; no composition date or publication history has been established. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm))

### Editorial work and shorter texts

- **1936:** *Rodeo: A Collection of Tales and Sketches*. An editorial obra: Tschiffely selected and edited writings by R. B. Cunninghame Graham rather than authoring the constituent tales. ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/graham-robert-bontine-cunninghame))

- **20 December 1936:** *Tschiffely’s Ride* radio script. A nineteen-page broadcast adaptation with Tschiffely’s emendations, deletions, and inserted passages. It is a distinct performative manifestation of the ride narrative, although the archive does not establish from the surviving script alone whether or exactly how it was broadcast. ([Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **1947:** *A Gaucho in the Pampa*. A contribution to the multi-author volume *My First Horse*, not a separately authored book; the estate describes the volume only as a contribution, while the anthology contents identify Tschiffely’s individual text by this title. ([Tschiffely Literary Estate](https://www.aimetschiffely.org/publications.htm); [Antiqbook contents record](https://www.antiqbook.nl/book/1712397752/my-first-horse))

- **Undated:** *It Happened in the Far South (Historic Events in Tierra del Fuego)*. A twenty-seven-page corrected typescript preserved in the archive. Its publication status and relationship to *This Way Southward* or *The Man from Woodpecker Creek* are not established; it consequently resists classification as either an independent finished work or merely discarded preparatory material. ([Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))

- **Undated:** *Map for The Man from Woodpecker Creek*. A one-sheet visual working object comprising a rough map of Patagonia and “Fireland,” a colored map of Tierra del Fuego, marked story locations, and an initialed note. It is neither simply prose nor an autonomous atlas: it survives as the cartographic apparatus of a biographical project. ([Harry Ransom Center inventory](https://txarchives.org/uthrc/finding_aids/01008.xml))
