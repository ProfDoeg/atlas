## Works by Njall Thorgeirsson of Bergthorshvoll

### Constitutional projects

- **Shortly before Iceland’s conversion, in the saga’s chronology; historically c. 1005:** *The Fifth Court and its procedural law*. An oral legislative project proposing a supreme court, new chieftaincies, rules for pleadings, oaths, jury challenges, voting in the Lögrétta, and protest; Skapti Þóroddsson formally enacts it, so it is a collaborative legal institution rather than a text authored by Njáll alone. Other sources associate the historical reform with Skapti, not Njáll, making Njáll’s authorship a disputed saga attribution ([*Njáls saga*, ch. 97](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#97); [Miller, “A Tale of Two Hoskulds”](https://academic.oup.com/book/12580/chapter-abstract/162398044)).

- **Same Alþing as the Fifth Court project:** *The chieftaincy of Whiteness*. A new goðorð—an office, constituency, and institutional creation rather than a written work—devised by Njáll for his foster-son Höskuldr after existing chieftaincies proved unavailable. The assembly authorizes it, and Njáll then establishes it; its dependence on the wider constitutional reform makes the two obras related but not identical ([*Njáls saga*, ch. 97](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#97); [Miller, “A Tale of Two Hoskulds”](https://academic.oup.com/book/12580/chapter-abstract/162398044)).

### Settlements and arbitral awards

- **Undated; early in Njáll and Gunnarr’s feud-settlement sequence:** *Settlement for Atli’s killing*. An oral peace compact made by Njáll and Gunnarr after Brynjólfr kills Atli; Njáll determines that Atli must be compensated as a free man and formally calls witnesses. It resists classification as Njáll’s sole work because Gunnarr offers self-judgment and both men jointly conclude the peace ([*Njáls saga*, ch. 39](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#39)).

- **Undated; immediately after the Atli settlement:** *Award for Brynjólfr’s killing*. An arbitral award fixing compensation at one hundred in silver, pronounced and immediately paid by Njáll after his household’s Þórðr kills Brynjólfr. This is an enacted legal settlement—closer to a performative protocol or manifesto than to a literary text ([*Njáls saga*, ch. 40](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#40)).

- **Undated; later in the same feud sequence:** *Settlement for Þórðr Freedmanson’s killing*. A peace compact and self-award accepted by Njáll from Gunnarr, valuing Þórðr at two hundred in silver. It is Njáll’s decision but also a bilateral obra made operative by a handshake, payment, and the parties’ continuing obligations ([*Njáls saga*, ch. 43](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#43)).

- **Undated; three assemblies after Sigmundr’s killing:** *Settlement for Sigmundr and Skjöldr*. Njáll compels the previously reluctant Gunnarr to make an award: two hundred in silver for Sigmundr, none for Skjöldr, together with a prohibition against repeating the defamatory verses that occasioned the feud. Gunnarr fixes and publicly declares the terms, while Njáll initiates and accepts them, so attribution must remain joint ([*Njáls saga*, ch. 45](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#45)).

- **Undated; at a subsequent Alþing:** *Award for the conspiracy against Gunnarr*. Njáll brokers preliminary pledges, appoints a twelve-man tribunal, fixes differentiated monetary penalties for the conspirators and their leaders, receives the money in trust, and pronounces the final terms. This is a composite legal performance produced with the tribunal and consenting parties, not a freestanding written text ([*Njáls saga*, chs. 68–69](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#68); [scholarly Old Norse edition of the episode](https://skaldic.org/db.php?id=23931&if=onw&table=verses&val=interact)).

### Marriage project

- **Begun before the Fifth Court reform; completed afterward:** *Marriage settlement of Höskuldr Þráinsson and Hildigunnr Starkaðardóttir*. Njáll initiates and negotiates the match, promises its financial foundation, obtains a three-year delay, creates the necessary chieftaincy, and returns to complete the betrothal. The marriage itself belongs to Höskuldr, Hildigunnr, and their kin, so this is best classified as Njáll’s completed social and dynastic project rather than his sole creation ([*Njáls saga*, chs. 96–97](https://sagadb.org/brennu-njals_saga.en#96); [Miller, “A Tale of Two Hoskulds”](https://academic.oup.com/book/12580/chapter-abstract/162398044)).

### Works not attested

- *No securely attributed literary composition*. The saga gives Njáll speeches, legal formulas, counsel, predictions, and prayers, but it does not present him as their recorder or as the author of a named poem, book, law-code, or other independently transmitted text. The standard scholarly edition of the saga’s poetry attributes its verse corpus to other named speakers or to anonymous saga composition, not to Njáll ([Fulk, ed., “Njáls saga,” *Skaldic Poetry of the Scandinavian Middle Ages* 5](https://skaldic.org/m.php?p=text&i=55)).
