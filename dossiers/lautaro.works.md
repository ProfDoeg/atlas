## Works by Lautaro

### Strategic protocols

- **1553:** *Successive-wave battle plan at Tucapel*. An enacted military protocol rather than a written text: Lautaro is credited with selecting the terrain and rotating successive bodies of warriors against Pedro de Valdivia’s force. The attribution rests on hostile colonial narratives and cannot establish sole authorship; the plan was necessarily a collective Mapuche performance. [Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-721.html)

### Fortifications

- **1556:** *Fortified camp at Peteroa*. A collectively built field fortification associated with Lautaro’s first advance toward Santiago; Jerónimo de Vivar describes Spanish attempts to enter “the fort.” Its exact site is disputed, and later accounts sometimes conflate Peteroa with the different camp at which Lautaro died. [Vivar, chap. CXXVIII](https://es.scribd.com/doc/213730484/Cronicas-Geronimo-de-Vivar), [source comparison and documentation](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batalla_de_Peteroa#Referencias_y_notas)

- **1557:** *Malal or fortified camp in Mataquito*. An earth-and-timber defensive emplacement made by Lautaro’s forces as a base for controlling the region and advancing toward Santiago; it was also the place surprised by Francisco de Villagra’s force. The sources disagree over its precise location, the date of the attack, and whether “Peteroa” and “Mataquito” designate separate fortifications; it therefore resists treatment as a securely identified architectural work. [Memoria Chilena](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-721.html), [Vivar, chap. CXXIX](https://es.scribd.com/doc/213730484/Cronicas-Geronimo-de-Vivar), [Museo Chileno de Arte Precolombino](https://chileprecolombino.cl/el-encuentro/centro-sur/3/)

### Commissioned objects

- **1556:** Untitled bodies of throwing clubs, arrows, and lassos. Vivar reports that Lautaro sent one hundred men from his fortified position near the Maule to manufacture these weapons; they are an attested production project under his command, not objects demonstrably made by his own hands, and no individual example or inventory survives. [Vivar, chap. CXXVIII](https://es.scribd.com/doc/213730484/Cronicas-Geronimo-de-Vivar)

### Texts and attributed speech

- No securely attributable written text, manifesto, protocol, or titled oral composition by Lautaro survives. Colonial chroniclers place speeches and replies in his mouth, but these are mediated—sometimes extensively rewritten—Spanish narrative constructions rather than recoverable works by Lautaro; *La Araucana* is Alonso de Ercilla’s creation, not his. [Memoria Chilena, “Visión hispana de Lautaro”](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-100856.html)
