## Works by Leo Africanus

### Geographical obra

- **10 March 1526:** *Libro de la cosmographia et geographia de Affrica*. An Italian geographical, historical, ethnographic, and partly autobiographical obra in nine books, preserved in Leo’s manuscript as Rome, Biblioteca Nazionale Centrale, MS V.E. 953. Giovanni Battista Ramusio substantially edited it for publication in 1550 as *Della descrittione dell’Africa et delle cose notabili che ivi sono*, so Leo’s manuscript and Ramusio’s familiar printed text are not identical. [Schweickard](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/zrp-2015-0009/html); [specialist bibliography](https://www.leoafricanus.com/bibliography/bibliography_books1.html)

### Transcription, lexicography, and philological collaboration

- **January 1521:** *Arabic Epistles of Saint Paul* (descriptive title). A manuscript transcription in Arabic made for Alberto Pio’s library, now Modena, Biblioteca Estense, MS Or. I.6.3; scholarship sometimes calls it Leo’s “translation,” but the manuscript colophon establishes his role more securely as its copyist or transcriber. [Casari](https://itatti.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/itatti/files/ariosto-stampa-hr.pdf); [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf)

- **late January 1524:** *Arabic–Hebrew–Latin Medical Vocabulary* (conventional descriptive title). A collaborative lexicographical object prepared in Bologna for the Jewish physician Jacob ben Simon Mantino; Leo supplied and copied the Arabic material, while only that portion survives in El Escorial, MS Árabe 598. Its colophon says “1514,” but the corresponding Hijri year 930 makes 1524 the accepted correction, so the written date is itself disputed evidence. [Masonen](https://rodin.uca.es/bitstream/handle/10498/7923/20133662.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y); [specialist bibliography](https://www.leoafricanus.com/bibliography/bibliography_books1.html)

- **1525:** *Revision of Egidio da Viterbo’s Latin Qurʾān*. This is not an independently authored translation but a collaborative philological manifestation: Leo extensively corrected and reworked Juan Gabriel of Teruel’s 1518 Latin version and apparently supplied annotations. Leo’s corrected exemplar is lost; his intervention survives indirectly in later manuscript copies and has been reconstructed in the modern critical edition. [Starczewska, *Latin Translation of the Qurʾān (1518/1621)*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv11sn5pr); [study preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783447196901_A33587532/preview-9783447196901_A33587532.pdf)

### Linguistic and biographical treatises

- **c. 1522–1525; copied in 1527:** *De arte metrica liber* (“Book on the Art of Metre”). A short Latin-facing instructional treatise on Arabic language and poetic metre, surviving in only eight leaves; it is therefore both a protocol for reading Arabic verse and the fragmentary remainder of what Ramusio described more broadly as an Arabic grammar. Angela Codazzi edited the fragment in 1956, but whether it represents the whole projected grammar is uncertain. [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf); [Loop](https://brill.com/downloadpdf/book/edcoll/9789004338623/B9789004338623_011.pdf)

- **before 1527; copied in 1527:** *De viris quibusdam illustribus apud Arabes* and its section *De viris quibusdam illustribus apud Hebraeos*. A biographical compendium, resembling the Arabic *ṭabaqāt* tradition, containing lives of twenty-five Muslim and five Jewish scholars; the surviving Florence and Kassel witnesses are scribal copies rather than Leo’s autograph. The Jewish lives may be treated as a section or companion text rather than a fully independent obra, and the familiar Latin titles derive from the manuscript and later publication history. [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf); [Leiden catalogue of the manuscript tradition](https://brill.com/fileasset/downloads_products/36732_Meyier_Catalog_Part_2.pdf)

### Attested projects and works of uncertain completion

- **by 1526:** *Description of the places visited in the Middle East* (descriptive title). Leo announced this intended geographical volume inside the *Cosmographia* as a counterpart to the African description, but no manuscript is known and there is no proof that he completed it; it is securely a project, not securely a finished obra. [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf)

- **by 1526:** *Description of Europe* (descriptive title). Another geographical volume announced as part of the same larger cosmographical project; nothing survives, and scholarship cannot determine whether composition ever advanced beyond intention. [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf)

- **by 1526:** *Exposition of the faith and law of Muḥammad according to the Mālikī school* (descriptive translation of Leo’s cited title). Leo referred to such a work in the *Cosmographia*, but the reference does not establish whether he had written it or merely intended it. It therefore resists classification as a completed work: no independent witness survives, and Codazzi’s assertion that it predated Leo’s capture was unsupported by evidence. [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf)

- **by 1526:** *History of North Africa* (descriptive title; possibly the work elsewhere identified as *La brevità de le croniche mucamettani*). This is known only through Leo’s internal reference, and neither its exact scope nor its completion can be established; it should be retained as an attested project rather than silently promoted to a lost finished book. [Masonen](https://www.leoafricanus.com/pictures/bibliography/Masonen/Masonen.pdf)
