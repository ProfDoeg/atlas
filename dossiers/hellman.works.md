## Works by Martin Edward Hellman

### Dissertation

- **1969:** *Learning with Finite Memory*. Doctoral dissertation in electrical engineering at Stanford University and the first item in Hellman’s own numbered bibliography. [Bibliography no. 1](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)

### Research papers, technical notes, and scholarly correspondence

- **1969:** *The Effect of Neighboring Conductors on the Currents and Fields in Plane Parallel Transmission Lines*. Microwave-engineering paper coauthored with I. Palocz. [Bibliography no. 2](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1970:** *The Two-Armed Bandit Problem with Time-Invariant Finite Memory*. Information-theory paper coauthored with Thomas M. Cover. [Bibliography no. 3](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1970:** *Learning with Finite Memory*. Journal paper coauthored with Cover and distinct from, though derived from, Hellman’s dissertation. Its 1973 Russian translation is a manifestation of the same paper, not a new obra. [Bibliography nos. 4, 14](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1970:** *Finite Memory Decision Schemes*. Russian-language research paper coauthored with Cover; Hellman’s bibliography does not identify it as a translation of another item. [Bibliography no. 5](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1970:** *Finite Memory Hypothesis Testing: Comments on a Critique*. Scholarly correspondence coauthored with Cover. [Bibliography no. 6](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1970:** *Probability of Error, Equivocation, and the Chernoff Bound*. Information-theory paper coauthored with J. Raviv. [Bibliography no. 7](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1970:** *The Nearest Neighbor Classification Rule with a Reject Option*. Pattern-classification paper; its 1991 book appearance is identified by Hellman as a reprint, not a separate work. [Bibliography nos. 8, 67](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1971:** *On Memory Saved by Randomization*. Paper coauthored with Cover; a Russian translation appeared in 1973. [Bibliography nos. 9, 15](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1972:** *Hypothesis Testing with Finite Memory in Finite Time*. Scholarly correspondence coauthored with R. A. Flower. [Bibliography no. 10](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1972:** *The Effects of Randomization [on] Finite Memory Decision Schemes*. Information-theory paper; Hellman’s contents page omits “on” from the displayed title, so that bibliographic irregularity is retained rather than silently corrected. [Bibliography no. 11](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1972:** *A Confidence Model for Finite-Memory Learning Systems*. Scholarly correspondence coauthored with J. A. Horos. [Bibliography no. 12](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1973:** *A Review of Recent Results on Learning with Finite Memory*. Review paper coauthored with Cover for a symposium-derived volume. [Bibliography no. 13](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1974:** *Finite Memory Algorithms for Estimating the Mean of a Gaussian Distribution*. Technical correspondence in information theory. [Bibliography no. 16](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1974:** *On Using Natural Redundancy for Error Detection*. Communications-engineering correspondence. [Bibliography no. 17](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1975:** *Bistable Behavior of ALOHA-Type Systems*. Communications paper coauthored with A. B. Carleial; the 1993 anthology appearance is a reprint. [Bibliography nos. 18, 68](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1975:** *Error Detection in the Presence of Synchronization Loss*. Short communications paper. [Bibliography no. 19](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1975:** *On Tree Coding with a Fidelity Criterion*. Information-theory paper coauthored with C. R. Davis. [Bibliography no. 20](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1975:** *Convolutional Source Encoding*. Information-theory paper. [Bibliography no. 21](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1976:** *Optimal Finite Memory Learning Algorithms for the Finite Sample Problem*. Research paper coauthored with Cover and M. A. Freedman. [Bibliography no. 22](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1976:** *Concerning a Bound on Undetected Error Rate*. Scholarly correspondence coauthored with S. K. Leung-Yan-Cheong. [Bibliography no. 23](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1976:** *New Directions in Cryptography*. Invited paper coauthored with Whitfield Diffie. It proposed public-key cryptography and the key-agreement construction now called Diffie–Hellman; its 1981 anthology appearance is a reprint. [Bibliography nos. 24, 41](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1977:** *An Extension of the Shannon Theory Approach to Cryptography*. Cryptographic information-theory paper. [Bibliography no. 25](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1977:** *A Note on Wyner’s Wiretap Channel*. Scholarly correspondence coauthored with Carleial. [Bibliography no. 26](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1977:** *Exhaustive Cryptanalysis of the NBS Data Encryption Standard*. Computer-security paper coauthored with Diffie; its 1981 anthology appearance is a reprint. [Bibliography nos. 27, 40](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1978:** *An Improved Algorithm for Computing Logarithms Over GF(p) and Its Cryptographic Significance*. Paper coauthored with Stephen Pohlig; it embodies the algorithm now called Pohlig–Hellman. Its 1981 anthology appearance is a reprint. [Bibliography nos. 28, 43](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1978:** *The Gaussian Wire-Tap Channel*. Information-theory paper coauthored with Leung-Yan-Cheong. [Bibliography no. 29](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1978:** *Hiding Information and Signatures in Trap Door Knapsacks*. Paper coauthored with Ralph Merkle describing the Merkle–Hellman knapsack cryptosystem; its 1981 anthology appearance is a reprint. [Bibliography nos. 30, 42](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1978:** *An Overview of Public Key Cryptography*. Invited survey. The 1983 tutorial and anthology versions are reprints; the 2002 “landmark papers” version adds new material and is therefore a revised manifestation rather than a simple facsimile. [Bibliography nos. 31, 46, 51, 73](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1979:** *Privacy and Authentication: An Introduction to Cryptography*. Invited survey coauthored with Diffie. Its 1981 and 1983 appearances—one retitled *An Introduction to Cryptography*—are identified as reprints. [Bibliography nos. 32, 39, 44, 47](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1979:** *Convolutional Encoding for Wyner’s Wiretap Channel*. Information-theory paper coauthored with E. Verriest. [Bibliography no. 33](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1979:** *On Foiling Computer Crime*. Magazine article of mixed authorship: Hellman’s bibliography expressly assigns only pages 32–39 to him, so the whole published object should not be treated as solely his work. [Bibliography no. 34](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1979:** *The Mathematics of Public Key Cryptography*. Expository article for *Scientific American*; a 1988 version was updated rather than merely reprinted. [Bibliography nos. 35, 66](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1980:** *A Cryptanalytic Time-Memory Tradeoff*. Cryptanalysis paper establishing Hellman’s time–memory tradeoff and “Hellman tables.” [Bibliography no. 36](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1981:** *On the Security of Multiple Encryption*. Cryptography paper coauthored with Merkle. [Bibliography no. 37](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1981:** *Another Cryptanalytic Attack on “A Cryptosystem for Multiple Communication”*. Short cryptanalytic paper. [Bibliography no. 38](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1983:** *On Secret Sharing Systems*. Information-theory paper coauthored with E. Karnin and J. Greene. [Bibliography no. 45](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1983:** *The Largest Super-Increasing Subset of a Random Set*. Information-theory paper coauthored with E. D. Karnin. [Bibliography no. 48](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1983:** *Fast Computation of Discrete Logarithms in GF(q)*. Conference paper coauthored with J. M. Reyneri. [Bibliography no. 49](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1983:** *Drainage and the DES*. Short conference paper coauthored with Reyneri. [Bibliography no. 50](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1986:** *Encryption*. Invited technical overview in the *IEEE ElectroTechnology Review*. [Bibliography no. 55](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1987:** *Chosen-Key Attacks on a Block Cipher*. Cryptanalysis paper coauthored with R. Winternitz. [Bibliography no. 57](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1987:** *Commercial Encryption*. Invited paper on cryptography’s commercial use. [Bibliography no. 58](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1988:** *Time-Memory-Processor Tradeoffs*. Information-theory paper coauthored with H. R. Amirazizi. [Bibliography no. 65](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1992:** *Responses to NIST’s Proposal*. Intervention in the public debate over a federal cryptographic proposal. Its form is closer to policy response than to a conventional research article. [Bibliography no. 69](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1994:** *Linear-Differential Cryptanalysis*. Conference paper coauthored with Susan K. Langford. [Bibliography no. 70](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1995:** *Sending Secret Messages*. Short expository chapter in *Scientific American: Triumph of Discovery*. [Bibliography no. 72](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2017:** *Cybersecurity, Nuclear Security, Alan Turing, and Illogical Logic*. Published text of Hellman’s ACM Turing Lecture; the full lecture and a separate six-minute video are audiovisual manifestations of the address rather than additional authored papers. [Bibliography no. 77](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)

### Protocols, cryptosystems, algorithms, and patented inventions

- **1975–1976:** *Public-key cryptography*. A jointly created cryptographic project and architecture associated with Diffie, Hellman, and Merkle, not simply the title of one paper. Attribution resists a simple “sole inventor” label: Merkle’s independent key-distribution work contributed to the Stanford development, while secret earlier British work by James Ellis, Clifford Cocks, and Malcolm Williamson was disclosed only decades later. [Stanford account](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/crypto.html)
- **1976:** *Diffie–Hellman key agreement*. A protocol for establishing shared secret material over an insecure channel, introduced publicly in *New Directions in Cryptography*. Williamson had devised a secret GCHQ antecedent, so “first invention” depends on whether priority means secret conception or public, independently developed disclosure. [ACM Turing Award citation](https://amturing.acm.org/award_winners/diffie_8371646.cfm)
- **1976–1978:** *Merkle–Hellman knapsack cryptosystem*. A public-key encryption construction jointly made with Ralph Merkle and published in 1978. It later proved insecure; that failure changes its status as a usable protocol, not its status as a historical creation. [Bibliography no. 30](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1977–1980:** *Cryptographic apparatus and method* (US 4,200,770). Patent embodiment of public-key key agreement, filed with Diffie and Merkle in 1977 and granted in 1980; a patent is simultaneously a legal instrument, technical disclosure, and claim to an invention. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4200770A/en)
- **1977–1980:** *Public key cryptographic apparatus and method* (US 4,218,582). Patented public-key construction associated with Hellman and Merkle, filed in the Stanford public-key-cryptography patent group. Patent families and foreign equivalents are manifestations of the claimed invention rather than separate technical obras.
- **1977–1984:** *Cryptographic apparatus and method* (US 4,424,414). Another patent in the early Stanford public-key portfolio; its legal claims should not be conflated with the broader historical idea of public-key cryptography.
- **1978:** *Pohlig–Hellman algorithm*. A discrete-logarithm algorithm jointly created with Stephen Pohlig and disclosed in their 1978 paper; the algorithm is an operative procedure, while the paper is its textual manifestation. [Bibliography no. 28](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1980:** *Hellman time–memory tradeoff*. A cryptanalytic method for replacing some exhaustive computation with precomputation and stored tables. “Hellman tables” name an algorithmic apparatus, not a book or a single physical object. [Bibliography no. 36](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1983–1987:** *Software Distribution System* (US 4,658,093). A patented system for controlling software distribution through cryptographic authorization, filed in 1983 and granted in 1987. [USPTO patent copy](https://ptacts.uspto.gov/ptacts/public-informations/petitions/1548091/download-documents?artifactId=usxbl_wNBnOZe66P9_mMkRJdrSmT1kT0yP0dpQZ-jw4g2LKeQwHk3nQ)
- **1984–1986:** *Method and Apparatus for Use in Public-Key Data Encryption System* (US 4,633,036). Patented cryptographic method co-invented with Carl E. Bach. [Patent record](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4633036)
- **2004–2012:** *Dropout-Resistant Media Broadcasting System* (US 8,270,901). A patented broadcasting system that uses storage and segmented transmission to withstand reception dropouts; its long prosecution history produces multiple dated manifestations of one invention. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8270901B2/en)
- **2006–2013:** *FM Broadcast System Competitive with Satellite Radio* (US 7,840,178 and US 8,401,462). A media-distribution invention substituting storage for scarce transmission bandwidth. The two grants and associated applications are related legal manifestations, not necessarily two independent creations. [Patent-family listing](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/martin-edward-hellman)

### Books and edited collective works

- **1987–1988:** *Proriv: Stanovleniye Novogo Mishleniya / Breakthrough: Emerging New Thinking*. An edited international collective work made with Anatoly A. Gromyko. The Russian edition appeared in 1987 and the English edition in 1988; Hellman’s bibliography calls the former a Russian translation of the latter, so these are two linguistic manifestations of one editorial project, despite the Russian manifestation appearing first. [Bibliography, Books nos. 1–2](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2016:** *A New Map for Relationships: Creating True Love at Home & Peace on the Planet*. Book coauthored with Dorothie L. Hellman, combining memoir, relationship practice, and a peace project; its print and electronic formats are editions of the same obra. [Bibliography, Books no. 3](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)

### Peace, ethics, and national-security texts

- **1985:** *On the Inevitability and Prevention of Nuclear War*. Essay arguing for changed thinking about nuclear risk. Its appearances in *The Stanford Engineer* and *Peace Research Reviews*, and the retitled Swedish publication *Beyond War*, are identified by Hellman as reprints of the same text. [Bibliography nos. 52–54, 56](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1987–1988:** *A Message to the Scientific Community*. Preface coauthored with Sergei P. Kapitza for *Breakthrough*. The Russian *Nayuka i Covremenni Mir* is its translated manifestation. [Bibliography nos. 59–60](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1987–1988:** *Nuclear War: Inevitable or Preventable?* Chapter in *Breakthrough*; *Yadernaya Voina: Neizbezhna ili Predotvratima?* is its Russian translation. [Bibliography nos. 61–62](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1988:** *Scientists Must Help Stop the Arms Race*. Article coauthored with Kapitza and adapted from *A Message to the Scientific Community*; it is a derivative text rather than either an unrelated work or a verbatim reprint. [Bibliography no. 63](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1988:** *Harnessing Technology*. Short essay in *IEEE Spectrum* concerning the social direction of technological power. [Bibliography no. 64](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **1994:** *Resist Not Evil*. Essay in the collection *World Without Violence*; its title signals an ethical argument rather than a technical protocol. [Bibliography no. 71](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2008:** *Risk Analysis of Nuclear Deterrence*. Essay applying engineering risk analysis to the possible failure of nuclear deterrence. [Bibliography no. 74](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2011:** *How Risky Is Nuclear Optimism?* Peer-reviewed nuclear-risk article in the *Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists*. [Bibliography no. 75](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2012:** *Fifty Years After the Cuban Missile Crisis: Time to Stop Bluffing at Nuclear Poker*. Briefing paper made for the Federation of American Scientists and Nuclear Age Peace Foundation; it is an advocacy intervention as well as an analytical text. [Bibliography no. 76](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2019:** *Rethinking National Security*. Special report for the Federation of American Scientists and textual manifestation of Hellman’s continuing project of the same name. The project exceeds the report and therefore resists treatment as one bounded publication. [Bibliography no. 78](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2019:** *The Technological Imperative for Ethical Evolution*. Heidelberg Lecture delivered at the Lindau Nobel Laureate Meeting; the lecture, written version, and recorded video are manifestations of an address rather than separate works. [Bibliography no. 79](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2020:** *Comments on NRC Study Mandated by Section 1674 of the FY2020 NDAA*. Submitted comments to the National Research Council: a policy-act text intended to influence a mandated study, not merely to describe it. [Bibliography no. 80](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2021:** *An Existential Discussion: What Is the Probability of Nuclear War?* Dialogic article coauthored with Vinton G. Cerf. Its conversational form makes shared authorship integral to the object. [Bibliography no. 81](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2021:** *Briefing for the National Research Council Study on Risk Analysis Methods for Nuclear War and Nuclear Terrorism*. Briefing document prepared for the congressionally mandated NRC study; simultaneously analysis and an institutional intervention. [Bibliography no. 82](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2021:** *Probabilistic Risk Assessment*. Chapter in *On Assessing the Risk of Nuclear War*, applying probabilistic methods to nuclear catastrophe. [Bibliography no. 83](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)
- **2022:** *Sixty Years After the Cuban Missile Crisis: Time to Stop Playing Russian Roulette*. Nuclear-risk essay revisiting the argument of the 2012 briefing without being identified as a reprint. [Bibliography no. 84](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications.html)

### Continuing projects that resist classification as discrete works

- **1980s–present:** *Work on War and Peace*. A sustained civic and intellectual project encompassing education, nuclear-risk analysis, *Breakthrough*, and later national-security work. It has no single fixed title, medium, or completion date and should not be mistaken for one authored text. [Hellman’s project history](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/breakthrough.html)
- **2010s–present:** *Rethinking National Security*. An ongoing research-and-advocacy project, of which the 2019 special report, NRC submissions, lectures, and later nuclear-risk essays are manifestations. Its unfinished, distributed character is part of the work. [Project page](https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/rethinking.html)
- **1970s–present:** *Public-key cryptography*. Beyond the protocol and patents, this is a collaborative research project that became infrastructure maintained by innumerable later implementers. It resists attribution as a wholly self-contained “work by Hellman,” even though Hellman was one of its central public inventors. [Stanford Engineering](https://engineering.stanford.edu/about/history/heroes/2013-heroes/martin-hellman)
