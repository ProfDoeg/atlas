## Works by Martin Mordechai Buber

### Philosophical and dialogical works

- **1897–1898:** *Schopenhauers Lehre vom Erhabenen*. Dissertation on Schopenhauer’s aesthetics; it survives as an early academic work rather than a separately conceived book. [MBW 1]
- **1904:** *Zur Geschichte des Individuationsproblems: Nicolaus von Cues und Jakob Böhme*. Revised doctoral study of individuation in Cusanus and Böhme. [MBW 2.1]
- **1913:** *Daniel: Gespräche von der Verwirklichung*. A cycle of philosophical dialogues—part treatise, part literary composition—whose mystical language predates Buber’s mature dialogical thought. [MBW 1]
- **1913–1917:** *Ereignisse und Begegnungen*. A sequence of brief philosophical-literary pieces written over several years and published as a collection in 1917. [MBW 1]
- **1923:** *Ich und Du*. A compact philosophical obra establishing the I–Thou and I–It word-pairs; formally an essay, but deliberately written as an address that seeks to enact dialogue rather than merely describe it. [MBW plan]
- **1929:** *Zwiesprache*. A dialogical-philosophy essay developed from a lecture; later incorporated into *Das dialogische Prinzip*. [MBW plan]
- **1936:** *Die Frage an den Einzelnen*. A philosophical intervention concerning conscience, responsibility, and the individual’s resistance to collectivism; later incorporated into *Das dialogische Prinzip*. [MBW plan]
- **1943:** *Das Problem des Menschen*. A philosophical-anthropological study first published in Hebrew and subsequently reworked in German; consequently its “first” date depends on whether composition, Hebrew publication, or German publication is meant. [MBW plan]
- **1950:** *Urdistanz und Beziehung*. A philosophical-anthropological essay proposing distance and relation as the twofold basis of human existence. [MBW 10]
- **1952:** *Bilder von Gut und Böse*. Two linked interpretations of biblical narratives of evil; a theological anthropology rather than a systematic ethics. [MBW 10]
- **1952:** *Gottesfinsternis: Betrachtungen zur Beziehung zwischen Religion und Philosophie*. A collection of related essays on the modern “eclipse” of God; it is a constructed essay-book, not a single continuously composed treatise. [SEP]
- **1954:** *Elemente des Zwischenmenschlichen*. A philosophical account of genuine encounter between persons; later incorporated into *Das dialogische Prinzip*. [MBW 10]
- **1957:** *Schuld und Schuldgefühle*. A lecture-essay distinguishing existential guilt from psychological feelings of guilt. [MBW 10]
- **1962:** *Das dialogische Prinzip*. A retrospective constellation of *Ich und Du*, *Zwiesprache*, *Die Frage an den Einzelnen*, and *Elemente des Zwischenmenschlichen*, with an afterword; it is therefore both a book and a republication-framework rather than a wholly new work. [MBW plan]

### Religious studies, Judaism, and biblical interpretation

- **1905:** *Jüdische Märchen*. An edited anthology of Jewish tales; Buber’s making lies in selection, arrangement, and presentation, not original authorship of the transmitted stories. [MBW 2.1]
- **1908:** *Ekstase und Bekenntnis*. The programmatic introduction to *Ekstatische Konfessionen*, framing ecstatic testimony as comparative religious evidence. [MBW 2.1]
- **1909:** *Ekstatische Konfessionen*. A cross-cultural anthology of mystical testimony edited and introduced by Buber; an editorial montage whose constituent testimonies are not his creations. [MBW 2.2]
- **1911:** *Drei Reden über das Judentum*. Three lectures recast as a book; expanded editions added further addresses, so the title denotes a changing textual object rather than one invariant work. [SEP]
- **1916:** *Jüdische Religiosität*. An essay or address articulating lived religiosity against merely institutional religion. [MBW 2.1]
- **1918:** *Mein Weg zum Chassidismus: Erinnerungen*. An autobiographical account of Buber’s encounter with Hasidism; simultaneously memoir and retrospective intellectual self-construction. [SEP]
- **1919:** *Der heilige Weg: Ein Wort an die Juden und an die Völker*. A public theological address dedicated to Gustav Landauer; closer to a manifesto of religious action than to a descriptive treatise. [SEP]
- **1923:** *Religion und Gottesherrschaft*. A theological essay connecting religion with divine kingship. [MBW 9]
- **1932:** *Königtum Gottes*. A large biblical-theological study of divine kingship. It was announced as the first part of *Das Kommende*, but the projected multi-part obra was never completed in that form: the unfinished project is part of the work’s identity. [MBW 15]
- **1936:** *Der Glaube der Propheten*. A study of biblical prophecy, first substantially presented in Hebrew before its German form; its textual history crosses languages and publication dates. [MBW plan]
- **1945:** *Moses*. A biblical portrait combining historical inquiry, interpretation, and theological reconstruction; first issued in Hebrew and English contexts before the German edition.
- **1948:** *Der Weg des Menschen nach der chassidischen Lehre*. A short exposition structured around Hasidic teachings; neither a simple folktale collection nor a systematic philosophical treatise. [MBW 10]
- **1950:** *Zwei Glaubensweisen*. A comparative theological study distinguishing Jewish *emunah* from Christian *pistis*; its sharp contrast and treatment of Christianity generated sustained scholarly dispute. [MBW 9]
- **1952:** *Der Mensch und sein Gebild*. A group of essays on biblical and religious anthropology rather than a single continuous argument.
- **1964:** *Antwort*. Buber’s extensive reply to the contributors to *The Philosophy of Martin Buber*; a late philosophical self-interpretation made dialogically in response to critics, not an independent monograph in origin. [SEP]

### Hasidic retellings, anthologies, and literary constructions

- **1906:** *Die Geschichten des Rabbi Nachman*. Literary retellings based on Rabbi Nachman of Bratslav’s tales. Buber called his procedure a *Nacherzählung*; the resulting work is neither a translation nor wholly original fiction, and its reshaping of the sources has been contested. [SEP]
- **1907:** *Die fünfzigste Pforte*. A small collection of Hasidic material whose status lies between adaptation and authored literary work.
- **1908:** *Die Legende des Baalschem*. A shaped cycle of teachings and legends concerning the Baal Shem Tov; its stories derive from transmitted sources but were selected, rewritten, and architecturally arranged by Buber. [SEP]
- **1922:** *Der große Maggid und seine Nachfolge*. A collection of traditions surrounding Dov Baer of Mezeritch and his disciples; an editorial-literary reconstruction of a spiritual lineage. [SEP]
- **1924:** *Das verborgene Licht*. An anthology of Hasidic legends and teachings organized by Buber; selection and configuration, rather than primary invention of the stories, constitute the obra. [SEP]
- **1928:** *Des Baal-Schem-Tow Unterweisung im Umgang mit Gott*. A constructed sequence of teachings assembled from textual fragments; Buber is compiler and literary shaper, not the historical speaker of the sayings.
- **1935:** *Die chassidischen Bücher*. A synthetic collection that re-edits Buber’s earlier Hasidic presentations rather than constituting wholly new narrative material.
- **1943:** *Hundert chassidische Geschichten*. A compact Hasidic story anthology first issued in Hebrew; later German configurations overlap with other collections.
- **1949:** *Die Erzählungen der Chassidim*. Buber’s large, mature arrangement of Hasidic tales by masters and schools. It is often treated as a single authored book, but it resists that designation: its materials are inherited, translated, paraphrased, combined, and sometimes substantially rewritten.
- **1950:** *Gog und Magog: Eine Chronik*. A historical novel or “chronicle” set amid Hasidic responses to the Napoleonic wars; unlike the tale anthologies, it is an extended narrative obra, though built from legendary and documentary materials. [MBW 19]
- **1963:** *Der große Maggid und seine Nachfolge* (revised form in *Werke*, volume 3). Buber continued to revise and regroup the Hasidic corpus, making definitive separation between individual “works” and successive editorial manifestations difficult.

### Chinese literature and philosophy: translations, adaptations, and montages

- **1910:** *Reden und Gleichnisse des Tschuang-Tse*. A German rendering and interpretive arrangement of passages attributed to Zhuangzi. Buber did not translate directly from Chinese, so it is best classified as a mediated adaptation rather than a philological translation. [MBW 2.3]
- **1911:** *Chinesische Geister- und Liebesgeschichten*. Adaptations of Chinese stories, principally from Pu Songling, made through intermediary translations; an authored German literary recasting of inherited narratives. [MBW 2.3]
- **1924:** *Besprechungen mit Martin Buber in Ascona über Lao-tses Tao-te-king*. Notes recording discussions about the *Daodejing*. Because the surviving text was written down through collaborative conversation, it resists attribution as an independently authored Buber work. [MBW 2.3]
- **1925:** *Zwei Malergeschichten*. Two adaptations of Chinese artist tales; small literary manifestations rather than a book-length obra. [MBW 2.3]
- **1944:** *Weisheiten aus China*. A selection of Chinese sayings and texts assembled in Hebrew exile-era publication; the editorial act is Buber’s, while the sayings are not. [MBW 2.3]

### Bible translation as collaborative and continuing project

- **1925–1962:** *Die Schrift*. A German re-voicing of the Hebrew Bible begun collaboratively with Franz Rosenzweig and continued by Buber after Rosenzweig’s death in 1929. Issued first in installments and later as a revised four-volume whole, it is both an obra and a decades-long translation project; authorship of its principles and early execution is joint and should not be assigned silently to Buber alone. [Deutsche Biographie; SEP]
- **1925–1929:** *Die fünf Bücher der Weisung*. The Torah portion of *Die Schrift*, translated with Rosenzweig; a component that also circulated as a separately named book.
- **1926–1929:** *Die Bücher der Geschichte*. The historical-books portion of the Bible translation; collaborative where completed before Rosenzweig’s death and subsequently subject to Buber’s revision.
- **1927–1935:** *Das Buch der Preisungen*. The Psalms in Buber and Rosenzweig’s distinctive German; publication and revision continued across separate manifestations, making a single terminal date misleading.
- **1929–1938:** *Die Bücher der Kündung*. The prophetic-books portion; much of the labor fell to Buber after Rosenzweig’s death, within the conceptual project they had established together.
- **1936:** *Die Schrift und ihre Verdeutschung*. A collection of methodological essays and exchanges, several co-authored with Rosenzweig, explaining the Bible project; it is a protocol of translation as well as commentary upon a completed object. [MBW 14]
- **1954–1962:** *Die Schrift*, revised four-volume edition. A materially new manifestation incorporating extensive revision; not merely a photographic reprint of the 1925–1938 installments. [SEP]

### Political, social, Zionist, and educational interventions

- **1901:** *Jüdische Renaissance*. A cultural-Zionist programmatic essay; in function it approaches a manifesto. [MBW 3]
- **1901:** *Wege zum Zionismus*. A programmatic Zionist text proposing cultural and spiritual work rather than merely describing the movement. [MBW 3]
- **1905:** *Das jüdische Kulturproblem und der Zionismus*. An essay defining Zionism as cultural renewal. [MBW 3]
- **1914:** *Zionismus als Lebensanschauung und als Lebensform*. A lecture-essay presenting Zionism as an enacted way of life. [MBW 3]
- **1916:** *Völker, Staaten und Zion*. A wartime political-theological essay on nation, state, and Zion. [MBW 3]
- **1917:** *Unser Nationalismus*. A programmatic statement distinguishing Buber’s nationalism from state-centered nationalism. [MBW 3]
- **1918:** *Cherut: Eine Rede über Jugend und Religion*. An address to Jewish youth; pedagogical exhortation and manifesto rather than detached analysis. [MBW 8]
- **1921:** *Jüdischer Nationalismus*. A collection of speeches and essays, making it a retrospective political constellation rather than one newly composed treatise.
- **1926:** *Rede über das Erzieherische*. A lecture on education grounded in encounter and responsibility. [MBW 8]
- **1929:** *Verantwortung: Worte an die Jugend*. A public address whose action is exhortation. [MBW 8]
- **1930:** *Einige Leitsätze für Arbeitsgemeinschaften*. A concise educational protocol: propositions intended to organize collaborative study and work. [MBW 8]
- **1933:** *Kampf um Israel: Reden und Schriften 1921–1932*. A politically shaped collection of earlier writings, not a single-origin text.
- **1933–1938:** *Mittelstelle für jüdische Erwachsenenbildung*. An educational institution directed by Buber after Jews were excluded from German public education. It is not a text and not solely his creation, but it belongs to what he made as an organizational and pedagogical project.
- **1947:** *Towards Union in Palestine: Essays on Zionism and Jewish-Arab Cooperation*. A collective political volume edited by Buber, Judah L. Magnes, and Ernst Simon for Ihud; joint editorial action advocating binational cooperation, not a solely authored Buber book.
- **1948:** *Pfade in Utopia*. A history and defense of communitarian socialism, especially the cooperative community; first composed and published in Hebrew before its German manifestation.
- **1950:** *Israel und Palästina: Zur Geschichte einer Idee*. A history of the Zion idea; the underlying Hebrew work appeared earlier, so 1950 dates the German manifestation rather than the project’s origin.
- **1953:** *Das echte Gespräch und die Möglichkeiten des Friedens*. A peace address treating genuine dialogue as political practice. [MBW 6]
- **1953:** *Das Wort, das gesprochen wird*. Buber’s acceptance address for the Peace Prize of the German Book Trade; a ceremonial public act as much as an essay.
- **1957:** *Pointing the Way* / *Hinweise*. English and German collections assemble political, philosophical, and religious essays from different occasions; they are curated bodies of work rather than unitary compositions.

### Drama, poetry, memoir, and spoken encounters

- **1891:** *Bar Mitzwa-Rede*. Buber’s surviving bar-mitzvah address; an adolescent ceremonial speech, included by the critical edition but resistant to treatment as an intentionally published literary “work.” [MBW 1]
- **1892:** *Glaube, Hoffnung, Liebe (Ewige Jugend)*. An early youthful composition preserved by the critical edition; its title and status derive from archival transmission rather than a mature publication decision. [MBW 1]
- **1896–1897:** *Zarathustra*. An early dramatic or literary attempt shaped by Nietzschean language; unpublished in Buber’s lifetime and therefore better understood as an archival project than a completed public obra. [MBW 1]
- **1901:** *Die Abenteuer des kleinen Walther*. An early literary narrative. [MBW 1]
- **1913–1915:** *Ereignisse und Begegnungen*. Brief prose scenes combining memoir, parable, and philosophical vignette; their genre instability is deliberate. [MBW 1]
- **1914:** *Elija*. A mystery play drawing on the biblical Elijah traditions; drama rather than theological monograph, though it was published and translated only in later configurations.
- **1948:** *November*. A brief literary memorial text. [MBW 6]
- **1960:** *Autobiographische Fragmente*. Selective autobiographical pieces, not a continuous life narrative and not a claim to exhaustive memoir. [MBW 6]
- **1963:** *Philosophische Gespräche*. Recorded philosophical conversations; a dialogical manifestation whose wording and authorship depend partly on interlocutors and transcription. [MBW 6]
- **1965:** *Martin Buber und Carl Rogers*. A recorded public dialogue. It resists classification as a Buber-authored text: the event, interlocution, recording, and later transcription together make the surviving object. [MBW 10]
- **1965:** *Das Unbewußte*. A late spoken or dialogical contribution on the unconscious, preserved as a text through editorial mediation. [MBW 10]

### Editorial series, journals, organizations, and other collective obra

- **1901–1902:** *Jüdischer Verlag*. Buber helped create and shape this Zionist publishing institution. It is a collective organizational obra, not something attributable to him alone.
- **1902:** *Neue Gemeinschaft*. Buber participated in the brothers Hart’s communitarian circle and formulated its idea in lectures; the community itself is not safely classifiable as his work, but his programmatic contribution is.
- **1906–1912:** *Die Gesellschaft: Sammlung sozialpsychologischer Monographien*. A forty-volume series conceived and edited by Buber. Most individual volumes were written by others, so Buber made the editorial architecture and series-project, not their authored contents. [SEP]
- **1916–1924:** *Der Jude*. A journal founded and edited by Buber, to which he also contributed extensively. It is a collective forum and cultural intervention rather than a single authored text. [SEP]
- **1926–1929:** *Die Kreatur*. An interconfessional quarterly edited with Viktor von Weizsäcker and Joseph Wittig; a collaborative editorial project that deliberately joined Jewish, Protestant, and Catholic discourse. [SEP]
- **1933–1938:** Jewish adult-education programs in Germany. Under Buber’s direction these comprised curricula, lectures, teacher retraining, and institutions; they form a distributed pedagogical obra with many makers, not a single text.
- **1942–1948:** *Ihud* and its binational political program. Buber was a principal participant and writer, but the organization and program were collective creations; calling the whole movement “his work” would overstate authorship.
- **1958:** *Martin Buber Archives*. Buber’s deposited manuscripts, correspondence, notes, and working papers form an archival body, but the archive as ordered research object was also made by family members, archivists, and institutions; it should not be mistaken for a work intentionally completed by Buber.

### Retrospective collections and posthumous textual objects

- **1953:** *Hinweise: Gesammelte Essays*. A self-selected essay collection whose constituent works originated on different dates and occasions. [SEP]
- **1962–1964:** *Werke*, three volumes. Buber’s lifetime collected-works manifestation—*Schriften zur Philosophie*, *Schriften zur Bibel*, and *Schriften zum Chassidismus*—reorganizing previous books and essays rather than adding three new primary works. [SEP]
- **1963:** *Der Jude und sein Judentum: Gesammelte Aufsätze und Reden*. A late collection of essays and addresses on Judaism, shaped as Buber’s retrospective presentation of that field. [SEP]
- **1965:** *Nachlese*. A final lifetime collection of shorter writings; the book is an editorial constellation and should not be used to redouble every included text as a new 1965 work. [SEP]
- **1972–1975:** *Briefwechsel aus sieben Jahrzehnten*. A posthumous three-volume selection edited by Grete Schaeder. The letters are Buber’s communicative acts, but this titled, bounded book-object is the editor’s later construction from several thousand surviving letters. [SEP; MBW project]
- **2001–2019:** *Martin Buber Werkausgabe*. The 21-volume critical edition is the principal modern scholarly mapping of Buber’s corpus. It includes books, articles, speeches, drafts, letters, conversations, adaptations, and previously unpublished materials, but the edition itself is a posthumous scholarly creation, not an obra made by Buber. [MBW plan]
- **Unfinished and unpublished materials:** The Buber archive contains drafts, lecture notes, correspondence, and projects without fixed titles or dates. Their existence is documented, but completeness cannot responsibly be claimed: the critical edition selectively admits letters and does not treat the Bible translation itself as part of its remit. [MBW project]
