## Works by Lawrence Joseph Ellison

### Software and corporate projects

- **1977–present:** *Software Development Laboratories / Oracle Corporation*. An institutional obra co-founded with Bob Miner and Ed Oates: a company conceived to commercialize relational-database software. It cannot be treated as Ellison’s solitary creation; Oracle’s own history credits all three founders, while Ellison’s principal early contributions were strategy, sales, installation, training, and direction rather than sole authorship of the code.

- **1978:** *Oracle Version 1*. An unreleased relational database made collectively at Software Development Laboratories. Oracle’s historical account identifies Bruce Scott as coarchitect and coauthor of the first three versions, so the software belongs here only as an Ellison-led corporate project, not as code demonstrably authored by Ellison.

- **1979:** *Oracle Version 2*. The first commercially released Oracle database and an early commercial SQL relational database-management system. Ellison installed the first purchased copy and taught its users, but surviving accounts attribute the architecture and programming collectively—particularly to Bob Miner and Bruce Scott—making individual authorship inseparable from the company project.

- **1983:** *Oracle Version 3*. A portable rewriting of Oracle in C for mainframes, minicomputers, and personal computers. As with the preceding versions, it is a collective software obra produced under Ellison’s direction rather than a program securely attributable to his hand.

- **c. 1994–1995:** *Oracle Media Server / video-on-demand demonstration*. A working system that streamed multiple video channels from massively parallel computers. Ellison described proposing the application of nCUBE technology, but credited Bill Bailey, Mark Porter, and a small engineering team with making it work; it therefore resists classification as “a work by Ellison” and is better understood as his technical project realized by others.

- **1995–1999:** *The Network Computer*. A hardware-and-networking project—and, in its polemical opposition to the Windows personal computer, something close to a technological manifesto—promoted by Ellison and institutionalized through Network Computer, Inc. Prototype devices appeared in 1996, but the intended mass market did not; the company subsequently abandoned the general-purpose computer project for set-top-box and appliance software and became Liberate Technologies.

### Authored text

- **2003; expanded paperback 2004:** *Softwar: An Intimate Portrait of Larry Ellison and Oracle — Commentaries*. These are Ellison’s interspersed responses to Matthew Symonds’s biography, sometimes disputing the principal narrative. The book itself is Symonds’s work: only the separately identified commentaries are Ellison’s textual obra.

### Sailing projects and commissioned vessels

- **1995:** *Sayonara*. A 78-foot ILC Maxi racing yacht commissioned by Ellison for the Transpac and other maxi-yacht competition. Farr Yacht Design catalogues it as Design No. 323 and names Ellison as client, so it is a patronal project rather than a naval design authored by him.

- **2000–2017:** *Oracle Racing / BMW Oracle Racing / Oracle Team USA*. A competitive-sailing institution founded and financed by Ellison to challenge for the America’s Cup. This is best classified as a continuing project or collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk—combining naval architecture, composite construction, software, athletic performance, litigation, branding, and event design—rather than as a single yacht or an individually authored object.

- **2008–2010:** *BOR 90 / USA 17*. A 90-foot-waterline trimaran commissioned and built by BMW Oracle Racing for the 33rd America’s Cup, eventually fitted with a rigid wing sail. Ellison was team founder, owner, and a member of the afterguard, but BMW’s credits name Mike Drummond as design director and Tim Smyth and Mark Turner as construction managers; calling the vessel “made by Ellison” therefore means commissioned through his project, not personally designed.

- **2011–2013:** *Oracle Team USA 17*. The AC72 foiling, wing-sailed catamaran with which Ellison’s team defended the 34th America’s Cup. Ellison selected and promoted the AC72 format, but the vessel was the work of Oracle Team USA’s design and construction organization; it is another collective manifestation of his sailing project rather than his individual design.

### Research and philanthropic institutions

- **1997–c. 2017:** *Ellison Medical Foundation*. A grant-making institution founded by Ellison to support research into aging and age-related disease. Its own retrospective describes more than six hundred supported research programs over approximately twenty years; the foundation is consequently an enabling project whose “work” consists largely of research made by its grantees.

- **2016–present:** *Lawrence J. Ellison Institute for Transformative Medicine*. An interdisciplinary cancer-research and treatment institution established at the University of Southern California through Ellison’s $200 million gift. It is a patronal and institutional obra, not scientific research authored by Ellison; David Agus and the institute’s researchers are responsible for its medical work.
