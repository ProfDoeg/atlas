## Works by Lucy Parsons

### Authored pamphlets and manifestos

- **1884:** *To Tramps, the Unemployed, the Disinherited and Miserable*. A manifesto first printed in *The Alarm* on October 4 and then issued by the International Working People’s Association as a leaflet; because it directly exhorts dispossessed workers to act, “manifesto” is more exact than essay. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-to-tramps)]
- **1905–1910?:** *The Principles of Anarchism*. A lecture recast as an introductory pamphlet defining anarchism and proposing a voluntary social order. Bibliographical accounts place the pamphlet variously between 1905 and 1910, so its first publication date remains unsettled. [[Source](https://nnty.fun/downloads/books/ETYNTKE/The%20Anarchist%20Library/l/le/lucy-e-parsons-the-principles-of-anarchism.html)] [[Source](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/anthony-j-nocella-ii-mark-seis-and-jeff-shantz-classic-writings-in-anarchist-criminology)]

### Articles, letters, reports, stories, and historical sketches

- **September 7, 1878:** *On the “Harmony” Between Capital and Labor; or, The Robber and the Robbed*. A polemical letter to the editor of *The Socialist*, attacking the supposed identity of workers’ and employers’ interests. [[Source](https://itha-iath.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/casey-wiliams-whose-lucy-parsons.pdf)]
- **January 25, 1879:** *“Relics from the Late Carnage”: The New Slavocracy and the Unemployed*. A letter to *The Socialist* comparing wage dispossession with the unfinished emancipation of formerly enslaved people. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-relics-from-the-late-carnage)]
- **1878–1879:** *Workingwomen*. A newspaper address to working women: simultaneously commentary, organizing appeal, and proto-manifesto. The collected edition establishes the text but does not give a date in its table of contents. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1878–1879:** *Our Civilization: Is It Worth Saving?* A polemical newspaper essay testing “civilization” against workers’ material conditions; its exact issue date is not supplied by the collected edition’s public contents. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1878–1879:** *A Christmas Story*. A short work of political fiction about class inequality; it is one of the rare pieces in Parsons’s surviving corpus that presents its argument as a story. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **October 11, 1886:** *Challenging the Lying Monopolistic Press: A Letter to Joseph Labadie*. A private movement letter reporting on Parsons’s defense tour and its attempt to counter press accounts of the Haymarket trial. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-challenging-the-lying-monopolistic-press)]
- **1886:** *The Negro: Let Him Leave Politics to the Politician and Prayer to the Preacher*. A polemical article urging Black workers toward class organization rather than electoral or clerical leadership; its reduction of racial oppression to class is an important and disputed limitation of Parsons’s politics. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1886–1887:** *The Haymarket Meeting: A Graphic Description*. Eyewitness history and counter-narrative of the Haymarket meeting, made to contest official and newspaper versions of the event. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1886–1887:** *What Anarchy Means*. An explanatory interview or reported statement rather than a conventional authored essay; its textual form depends upon mediation by the press. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1887:** *Arrest in Columbus, Ohio*. A narrative of police suppression during Parsons’s Haymarket speaking campaign; part report, part testimony, and therefore not comfortably separable from the journey that produced it. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1889:** *Foreword to The Life of Albert R. Parsons*. The framing text for Parsons’s documentary memorial to her husband, establishing the volume as both biography and continuation of the Haymarket campaign. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1892:** *Southern Lynchings*. An article in *Freedom* calling for organized resistance to racist terror; its treatment of lynching through revolutionary class struggle remains contested by historians of Parsons’s racial politics. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
- **1890–1892:** *Ominous Times*. A short *Freedom*-period editorial intervention reading contemporary labor conflict as evidence of approaching social upheaval. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1890–1892:** *Rumblings of the Coming Storm*. A prophetic political article whose language is meant to precipitate consciousness as much as describe conditions. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1890–1892:** *The “Scab”*. A labor polemic defining strikebreaking as a social and moral relation rather than merely an individual act. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1890–1892:** *The Proposed Slaughter*. An extended protest article from Parsons’s *Freedom* period; the exact date is not fixed by the collected edition’s public contents. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **September 3, 1905:** *Salutation to the Friends of Liberty*. The opening editorial of *The Liberator*: a prospectus asking readers to help make the newspaper into a shared revolutionary forum. [[Source](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-salutation)]
- **1905:** *Our Label: The IWW Label*. A short article treating the Industrial Workers of the World emblem as a sign of class organization and collective identity. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **September 3, 1905:** *Are Class Interests Identical? A Synopsis of the Aims and Objects of the Industrial Workers of the World*. An explanatory article in the first issue of *The Liberator*, functioning partly as an organizing manifesto for the new union. [[Source](https://files.libcom.org/files/Anarchist_Periodicals_in_English.pdf)]
- **1905:** *On Revolution in Russia and the Chinese Use of the Boycott*. A comparative current-affairs article treating revolution and boycott as forms of direct action. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905:** *Industrial Evolution and Labor Day: The AFL vs. the IWW*. A polemical comparison of craft unionism and revolutionary industrial unionism. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Woman: Her Evolutionary Development*. A historical-theoretical article about women’s social position; its evolutionary framework makes it neither simply feminist history nor straightforward organizing literature. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *The Factory Child*. An exposé of child labor published in Parsons’s *Liberator* period. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *The Ballot Humbug*. An anti-electoral polemic arguing that political representation cannot replace workers’ direct organization. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Wage-Slaves vs. Corporations*. A class-conflict essay opposing workers’ collective power to corporate power. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *The Woman Question Again?* A polemical intervention into disputes over women’s emancipation, insisting upon the economic structure of women’s oppression. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *What Freedom Means*. A brief definitional article in which political vocabulary becomes an organizing instrument. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Famous Women of History*. The programmatic introduction to Parsons’s biographical series on women; it is better understood as a serial project than as an independent essay alone. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Florence Nightingale*. A biographical sketch in the *Famous Women of History* series. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Louise Michel*. A biographical-political portrait in the *Famous Women of History* series, joining commemoration to revolutionary instruction. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Property Rights vs. Human Rights*. A short polemic opposing legal ownership to human need. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Crime, Violence and Suicide*. A social analysis that treats crime and self-destruction as products of institutional conditions rather than isolated moral failures. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *The Negative of Government*. An anarchist theoretical essay presenting government as organized coercion; it also circulated as or within the pamphlet tradition associated with *The Principles of Anarchism*. [[Source](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/anthony-j-nocella-ii-mark-seis-and-jeff-shantz-classic-writings-in-anarchist-criminology)]
- **1905–1906:** *Agitation Trip*. A movement report in which travel, public speaking, and writing form a single organizing obra. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Who Were the Molly Maguires?* A historical rehabilitation of persecuted mineworkers and an intervention in labor memory. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Capitalist Conspiracy in Idaho*. A topical article defending leaders of the Western Federation of Miners against prosecution. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *Crime and Criminals*. A short social-theoretical article relocating responsibility for crime from individuals to prevailing institutions. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1905–1906:** *The IWW and the Short-Hour Workday*. An industrial-union argument linking reduced working hours to workers’ control. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1906–1907:** *New York Letter*. Travel correspondence reporting on people and conditions encountered during an organizing journey. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1906–1907:** *New York Vendors*. Urban reportage about street sellers and precarious labor. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1906–1907:** *The Importance of a Press*. An argument for movement-controlled publishing; it is also a statement of Parsons’s own editorial project. [[Source](https://api.drum.lib.umd.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/9721e214-3597-48ff-b0ed-c40af40744fa/content)]
- **September 4, 1907:** *The Haywood Trial and the Anarchist Trial*. An article in *The Demonstrator* comparing the Idaho prosecution of labor leaders with the Haymarket trial. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-the-haywood-trial)]
- **1907:** *A Wise Move: On Anarchist Organization*. An intervention advocating durable organization among anarchists, therefore functioning as both analysis and practical proposal. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1907:** *The IWW*. A concise assessment and defense of the Industrial Workers of the World. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **c. 1907–1910:** *A Stroll Through the Streets of Chicago*. Urban reportage converting observation of the city into social criticism; the date is not securely exposed in the collected edition’s public contents. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **March 1, 1911:** *Reflections of a Propagandist*. A report in *The Agitator* on Parsons’s New York work selling the Haymarket speeches and observing tenement and factory life. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-reflections-of-a-propagandist)]
- **c. 1911–1912:** *The Moving Inspiration of Our Age*. A brief theoretical article on the forces producing contemporary social change; its precise date is not fixed by the accessible contents. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **c. 1912:** *The Eight-Hour Strike of 1886*. Retrospective movement history made by a participant, preserving a counter-memory of Haymarket and the eight-hour campaign. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **c. 1912:** *Thoughts on May Day* (also catalogued as *Stray Thoughts on May Day*). A commemorative article joining personal recollection, labor history, and annual political ritual; the title varies between repositories. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/category/author/lucy-e-parsons?page=2&rows=20&sort=title_desc)]
- **c. 1912:** *The Eleventh of November, 1887*. A memorial history of the Haymarket executions; commemoration here is itself political action rather than neutral description. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/category/author/lucy-e-parsons?page=2&rows=20&sort=title_desc)]
- **1914–1915:** *On “Political Socialism”*. A short critique of electoral socialism from an industrial and direct-action position. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1914–1915:** *Workers and War*. An antiwar article interpreting military conflict through class relations. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1914–1915:** *Melville Stone’s Lies*. A press polemic challenging Associated Press executive Melville Stone; the title makes the intervention’s adversarial form explicit. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1915:** *Letter to Eugene V. Debs*. Political correspondence addressed to the socialist leader; its survival as a published text complicates the distinction between letter and public statement. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **c. 1920:** *Who Were the Pioneers of the Eight-Hour Day?* A corrective work of labor history claiming the Haymarket radicals’ place in the genealogy of May Day. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1930s:** *U.S. Anarchism in the 1930s*. A late assessment of an anarchist movement transformed by repression, syndicalism, and communism; the title may be editorial rather than Parsons’s own fixed title. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1930s:** *Letter to Tom Mooney*. Political correspondence supporting the imprisoned labor activist; like Parsons’s other public letters, it occupies the boundary between private address and campaign instrument. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1930s:** *On a New Biography of Albert Parsons*. A brief intervention in the continuing construction of her husband’s public memory. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1937:** *November 11 Fifty Years Ago*. A fiftieth-anniversary recollection of the Haymarket executions, functioning as testimony, memorial, and renewed indictment. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]
- **1937:** *Message to the IWW’s General Defense Committee*. A solidarity message to the union’s prisoner-defense body; its status is closer to a public salutation than a freestanding essay. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]

### Speeches and spoken manifestations

- **1886:** *I Am an Anarchist*. A speech preserved through the *Kansas City Journal* of December 21; because the surviving wording is a newspaper transcription, it is a manifestation of Parsons’s performance rather than an uncontested authorial manuscript. [[Source](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-i-am-an-anarchist)]
- **June–July 1905:** *Speeches at the Founding Convention of the Industrial Workers of the World*. Several convention interventions, not one continuous composition, arguing for women’s organization, industrial unionism, and workers’ possession of production. The collected title is editorial. [[Source](https://www.theanarchistlibrary.org/category/author/lucy-e-parsons?page=2&rows=20&sort=title_desc)]
- **1905:** *Speech to the IWW in 1905*. A convention address surviving in transcript; it may overlap with the collected *Speeches at the Founding Convention* and should not automatically be counted as a wholly separate composition. [[Source](https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/lucy-e-parsons-speech-to-the-iww-in-1905)]
- **May Day, c. 1930:** *I’ll Be Damned if I Go Back to Work Under Those Conditions!* A retrospective May Day speech whose familiar title is drawn from the speech and may be editorial; it is an oral performance preserved as text. [[Source](https://www.lucyparsonsproject.com/writings/be_damned_mayday.html)]

### Edited and published documentary obras

- **1887:** *Anarchism: Its Philosophy and Scientific Basis*. Albert R. Parsons wrote this prison text; Lucy Parsons made its posthumous publication possible. It is therefore her publishing project, not an authored work by her. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
- **1889:** *Life of Albert R. Parsons, with Brief History of the Labor Movement in America*. A documentary memorial edited and published by Lucy Parsons, combining autobiography, movement history, correspondence, and her framing material; its composite authorship resists classification as either her book or Albert Parsons’s alone. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
- **1910:** *The Famous Speeches of the Eight Chicago Anarchists in Court*. A documentary compilation edited and published by Parsons from the condemned defendants’ courtroom addresses. The speeches are not hers, but their selection, arrangement, preservation, and circulation constitute her editorial obra. [[Source](https://www.lucyparsonsproject.com/writings/FamousSpeeches.pdf)]
- **1915:** *Altgeld’s Reasons for Pardoning Fielden, Schwab and Neebe*. An edited documentary pamphlet preserving Governor John Peter Altgeld’s pardon argument; Parsons made the publication, not the underlying text. The scholarly reference spells Oscar Neebe’s surname “Nebbe,” apparently an error or edition-specific rendering. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]

### Periodical projects

- **1890–1892:** *Freedom: A Revolutionary Anarchist-Communist Monthly*. A periodical project published and edited by Parsons, with Lizzie Holmes participating in its early editorial work; it serialized Haymarket autobiographies and carried Parsons’s writing on labor, women, lynching, and peonage. Because each issue was collectively authored, the periodical is her editorial obra but not wholly her text. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)] [[Source](https://archive.iww.org/history/biography/LucyParsons/1/)]
- **September 3, 1905–April 15, 1906:** *The Liberator: A Weekly Devoted to Revolutionary Propaganda along Lines of Anarchistic Thought*. A thirty-issue newspaper edited by Parsons and published by the Liberator Publishing Group; it was designed as an open revolutionary forum and carried both her work and that of many contributors. [[Source](https://files.libcom.org/files/Anarchist_Periodicals_in_English.pdf)]
- **1897–1904:** *Chicago correspondence for Free Society*. A recurring newspaper column or body of correspondence rather than a single fixed work; surviving scholarship identifies the project but does not supply a complete enumerated bibliography of its installments. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
- **1905–1906:** *Famous Women of History*. A planned and partly realized serial within *The Liberator*, including the surviving sketches of Florence Nightingale and Louise Michel. Its surviving extent may not equal Parsons’s original intention, so it is best classified as a possibly incomplete project. [[Source](https://oneonta.ecampus.com/lucy-parsons-parsons-lucy/bk/9780882863009)]

### Organizational and performative obras

- **c. 1880–1883:** *Working Women’s Union organizing project*. Parsons helped form and sustain a Chicago organization of garment workers that advocated equal pay and treated unpaid housework as labor. It resists being called a discrete “work”: it was a collective social practice, with no single finished object or sole author. [[Source](https://charleshkerr.com/books/lucy-parsons-freedom-equality-solidarity-writings-speeches-1878-1937-by-lucy-parsons)]
- **1886–1888:** *Haymarket defense and memorial speaking tours*. A multi-state and international sequence of speeches, fundraising, press interventions, and confrontations over free speech. No complete authorized text survives, so the journey itself—not merely its fragmentary newspaper transcripts—is the made political form. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
- **1905:** *Industrial Workers of the World*. Parsons was a founding-convention participant and one of the new union’s formative speakers, but the IWW was a collective creation and cannot be assigned to her as an individual work. [[Source](https://charleshkerr.com/books/lucy-parsons-freedom-equality-solidarity-writings-speeches-1878-1937-by-lucy-parsons)]
- **January–February 1915:** *Chicago unemployed demonstrations*. Parsons helped organize and lead meetings and marches beginning at Hull House, including the renewed march after the courts rejected charges against demonstrators. These events are collective political manifestations, not texts, and their police-mediated documentary record is incomplete. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]

### Material work and the lost archive

- **1870s–1880s:** *Garments from Lucy Parsons’s dressmaking and tailoring work*. Parsons operated a small women’s tailoring shop and did the sewing herself. No catalogue of individual garments survives, so these unquestionably made objects resist entry as named works. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
- **Before March 7, 1942:** *Personal papers and working library*. Parsons assembled papers and a library reported at nearly three thousand volumes, but most disappeared after the fire that killed her; neither the FBI nor Chicago’s Red Squad accepted responsibility. This archive was a made intellectual environment rather than a formally published work, and its loss makes any claim to list “everything” she created impossible. [[Source](https://flamingo-keyboard-lbzk.squarespace.com/s/WBC_Entries_Parsons_Lucy.pdf)]
