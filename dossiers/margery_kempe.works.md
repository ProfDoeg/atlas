## Works by Margery Kempe

### Collaborative textual project

- **Before 1436 (possibly begun by the mid-1420s):** An untitled first draft of Kempe’s life. This lost manifestation was dictated to an amanuensis but judged difficult to understand; its existence and subsequent rewriting are reported within the surviving Book, while its date and the scribe’s identity remain disputed. ([Charity Scott Stokes](https://college.holycross.edu/projects/kempe/text/piety.html); [British Library](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001962059))

- **1436–1438:** *The Book of Margery Kempe*. An obra made collaboratively through Kempe’s oral narration, recollection, supervision, and correction and the textual labor of successive scribes: Book I is associated with 1436 and Book II with 1438. “Autobiography,” “spiritual autobiography,” and Lynn Staley’s “medieval female sacred biography” each describe only part of this hybrid devotional, hagiographic, travel, and life-writing project; Kempe’s precise share of its wording and organization remains disputed. The original is lost, and the sole substantially complete witness, British Library Add MS 61823, is a copy made about 1445–1450, so it is a later manifestation rather than the object Kempe and her amanuenses directly produced. ([Barry Windeatt, ed.](https://cdn.penguin.co.uk/dam-assets/books/9780140432510/9780140432510-sample.pdf); [Lynn Staley, ed.](https://metseditions.org/editions/zY0MDpwC644aFmAQ7HvV73C41mbVAZeK); [British Library](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001962059))

### Embedded devotional composition of uncertain independence

- **No securely established date:** Untitled sequence of fifteen prayers, sometimes called the *Great Prayer*. Copied continuously at the end of the sole manuscript, the sequence resists classification as an independent work: it can be read as part of the Book’s devotional self-fashioning rather than as a separately titled obra. Some prayers adapt recognizable contemporary materials, while others have no identified models and may be Kempe’s own compositions; consequently, both authorship and the boundaries of the made thing remain uncertain. ([Rebecca Krug, “The Prayers of Margery Kempe: A Reassessment”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/encountering-the-book-of-margery-kempe/prayers-of-margery-kempe-a-reassessment/E93A384C2858EE92EF7032E5B547B2B5); [British Library](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001962059))
