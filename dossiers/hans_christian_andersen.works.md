## Works by Hans Christian Andersen

### Fairy-tale and story cycles

- **1835–1842:** *Eventyr, fortalte for Børn* (*Fairy Tales Told for Children*). Six separately issued booklets forming Andersen’s first fairy-tale cycle; their constituent tales include *The Tinder Box*, *Little Claus and Big Claus*, *The Princess on the Pea*, *Thumbelina*, *The Little Mermaid*, *The Emperor’s New Clothes*, *The Steadfast Tin Soldier*, *The Wild Swans*, *Ole Lukoie*, and *The Swineherd*.
- **1839–1847:** *Billedbog uden Billeder* (*Picture Book without Pictures*). A sequence of thirty-three prose “Evenings”; neither conventional fairy-tale collection nor travel book, but a hybrid cycle of imagined pictures narrated by the Moon.
- **1843–1848:** *Nye Eventyr* (*New Fairy Tales*). Four collections containing such independently made tales as *The Ugly Duckling*, *The Nightingale*, *The Snow Queen*, *The Red Shoes*, *The Little Match Girl*, *The Shadow*, and *The Story of a Mother*.
- **1852–1855:** *Historier* (*Stories*). Three collections marking Andersen’s deliberate movement away from the label “tales told for children” toward experimental, sometimes adult-oriented short prose.
- **1858–1866:** *Nye Eventyr og Historier* (*New Fairy Tales and Stories*), first and second series. Eight collections whose tales range from folktale transformations to modern stories about technology, objects, social rank, and artistic creation.
- **1869:** *Tre nye Eventyr og Historier* (*Three New Fairy Tales and Stories*). A small independent collection, although its title says “three,” belonging to the continuing story cycle.
- **1872:** *Nye Eventyr og Historier*, third series, first and second collections. Andersen’s last lifetime collections of new tales and stories.
- **1835–1872:** *156 numbered Eventyr og Historier*. The canonical corpus recognized by the Hans Christian Andersen Centre comprises 156 tales and stories; the Centre’s broader register also includes precursors, the thirty-three “Evenings,” doubtful boundary cases, and posthumously recovered texts, so “the complete tales” is not a bibliographically stable object.
- **1847:** *A Christmas Greeting to My English Friends*. An English-language collection containing the first publication of several tales; it is therefore a distinct manifestation, not merely a translation of a Danish book.
- **1853:** *A Poet’s Day Dreams*. An English-language collection containing first publications and thus functioning as part of the creative history of the tales.
- **1875:** *Historien om en Moder i femten Sprog* (*The Story of a Mother in Fifteen Languages*). A deliberately multilingual book-object rather than an ordinary reprint of the tale.
- **1926:** *To ukendte Eventyr af H. C. Andersen*. Posthumous publication of *Qvæk* and *Skriveren*; the date belongs to their manifestation, not necessarily their composition.
- **1943–1972:** *Den første Aften; Folks Say—; The Poor Woman and the Little Canary Bird; Urbanus; Kartoflerne; Æblet; Temperamenterne; Vor gamle Skolemester; De blaae Bjerge; Hans og Grethe*. Posthumously recovered or newly printed prose pieces placed by the Centre at the disputed edge of the fairy-tale corpus; several are not among the canonical 156.

### Novels and unfinished novel projects

- **1835:** *Improvisatoren*. Novel; also inseparable from Andersen’s Italian journey and consequently readable as fictionalized travel writing.
- **1836:** *O. T.*. Contemporary novel of identity, social mobility, and provincial and metropolitan Denmark.
- **1837:** *Kun en Spillemand* (*Only a Fiddler*). Künstlerroman about artistic aspiration and social constraint.
- **1848:** *De to Baronesser* (*The Two Baronesses*). Novel first issued in English shortly before its Danish manifestation.
- **1857:** *At være eller ikke være* (*To Be or Not to Be*). Philosophical and religious novel.
- **1870:** *Lykke-Peer* (*Lucky Peer*). Short novel or extended tale about the formation and death of an artist; its scale resists a firm boundary between novel and story.
- **undated; published 1935:** *Sorøe Gang*. Unfinished prose project, known through a posthumous manifestation; the project, rather than a completed novel, is the obra.
- **undated; published 1935:** *Fragmenter af en ufuldført historisk Roman*. Fragments of an unfinished historical novel; its title itself records incompletion.
- **undated; published 1935:** *Christian den Andens Dverg*. Unfinished historical-fiction project, posthumously printed.

### Experimental and other prose

- **1822:** *Gjenfærdet ved Palnatokes Grav*. Early prose narrative published in Andersen’s pseudonymous debut book *Ungdoms-Forsøg*.
- **1829:** *Fodreise fra Holmens Canal til Østpynten af Amager i Aarene 1828 og 1829*. Fantastical mock travelogue and prose experiment; the journey is imaginary, making the book deliberately resistant to the category “travel writing.”
- **1831:** *Den skjønne Grammatica, eller Badens latinske Grammatik*. Comic prose work built around grammar and linguistic instruction but not itself a protocol.
- **1822:** *Ungdoms-Forsøg*. Pseudonymously published mixed book containing drama, prose, and poetry; a composite obra rather than a single-genre text.
- **1838:** *Tre Digtninger*. Composite volume joining three different literary works and therefore best treated as a collected manifestation.

### Travel writing and topographical sketches

- **1826:** *Fragment af en Reise fra Roeskilde til Helsingør*. Travel fragment.
- **1829:** *Brudstykke af en Udflugt i Sommeren 1829: Odense og dens Omegn*. Excursion sketch.
- **1831:** *Skyggebilleder af en Reise til Harzen, det sachsiske Schweitz etc. etc., i Sommeren 1831*. Full-length travel book mixing reportage, lyric description, and staged literary performance.
- **1835:** *Raphaels Begravelse*. Italian travel sketch.
- **1835:** *Thorvaldsen*. Travel-biographical sketch of the sculptor.
- **1835:** *En Spadseretour gjennem Posilip-Grotten ved Neapel*. Italian travel sketch.
- **1835:** *Italiensk Musik, Sang og Theatervæsen*. Cultural-reportage essay arising from Andersen’s Italian travels.
- **1836:** *Den fattige Dreng paa Frankerigs Throne*. Historical-travel sketch.
- **1841:** *“Mohammeds Fødselsdag” og “Jernbanen”*. Paired travel sketches juxtaposing spectacle and modern technology.
- **1842:** *Tre romerske Drenge*. Roman travel sketch.
- **1842:** *En Digters Bazar* (*A Poet’s Bazaar*). Large, multi-country travel book whose montage of observation, fiction, poetry, and reflection approaches a literary Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1851:** *I Sverrig* (*Pictures of Sweden*). Travel book composed as lyrical and historical “pictures,” not straightforward documentary reportage.
- **1853:** *Silkeborg*. Danish topographical sketch.
- **1858:** *Ragaz (Bad-Pfäffers)*. Travel and spa sketch.
- **1859:** *Skagen*. Danish travel sketch.
- **1860:** *Et Besøg hos Charles Dickens i Sommeren 1857*. Memoir of Andersen’s visit to Dickens.
- **1860:** *Liden Kirstens Grav*. Travel-antiquarian sketch.
- **1860:** *Passionskuespillet i Oberammergau i 1860*. Eyewitness cultural and theatrical report.
- **1861:** *Brudstykke af en Reise i Schweitz*. Swiss travel fragment associated with an image of the Lion Monument at Lucerne.
- **1863:** *I Spanien*. Full-length travel book.
- **1868:** *Et Besøg i Portugal 1866*. Full-length travel memoir.
- **1869:** *I Jurabjergene*. Travel sketch.
- **1872:** *Nürnberg: En Reise-Erindring fra Foraaret 1872*. Late travel recollection.
- **undated; published 1949:** *Et Blad, skrevet i Norge*. Posthumously printed travel leaf; publication date should not be mistaken for composition date.
- **undated; published 1964:** *Et Kapitel af en paatænkt historisk Sverigesroman*. A surviving chapter from an intended Swedish historical novel, classified as travel writing by the Centre but resisting that category because it belongs to an unrealized fiction project.

### Autobiographical projects

- **1832; published 1926:** *Levnedsbog*. Andersen’s earliest substantial autobiography, left unpublished in his lifetime; the 1926 date is the first manifestation, not its date of making.
- **1847:** *Mit eget Eventyr uden Digtning* (*The True Story of My Life*). Autobiography first manifested in German; it is not identical with the later expanded Danish autobiography.
- **1855:** *Mit Livs Eventyr* (*The Fairy Tale of My Life*). Major Danish autobiography; its fairy-tale framing makes the purported life record also a consciously fashioned literary work.
- **1871:** *The Story of My Life*. Revised and extended English autobiography, not merely a neutral translation of the 1855 book.
- **1855–1875; published 1877:** *Supplement til “Mit Livs Eventyr”*. Posthumously issued continuation of the autobiographical project.

### Poetry books and poem cycles

- **1830:** *Digte*. First substantial poetry collection.
- **1831:** *Phantasier og Skizzer*. Poetry collection.
- **1831:** *Vignetter til danske Digtere*. Cycle of verbal “vignettes” responding to Danish poets; image and literary criticism are compressed into poems.
- **1832:** *Aarets tolv Maaneder, Tegnede med Blæk og Pen*. Twelve-month poetic cycle conceived as pictures “drawn with ink and pen,” deliberately crossing verbal and visual vocabularies.
- **1833:** *Samlede Digte*. Collected poems.
- **1846:** *Digte, gamle og nye*. Collection of old and new poems.
- **1851:** *Fædrelandske Vers og Sange under Krigen*. Patriotic verses and songs produced in the context of war.
- **1867:** *Kjendte og glemte Digte*. Collection of “known and forgotten” poems.
- **1830:** *Smaadigte, skrevne til de 4 gamle Abelliner*. Small cycle addressed to four old poplars.
- **1831:** *Hjertets Melodier*. Lyric cycle; several poems acquired separate lives as songs through settings by composers.
- **1831:** *Studier efter Naturen*. Poetic cycle framed as studies from nature.
- **1846:** *Italien*. Poetic cycle.
- **1847:** *Thorvaldsen*. Poetic cycle about the sculptor, distinct from Andersen’s 1835 prose sketch.
- **1854:** *Billeder fra Jylland*. Cycle of verbal pictures from Jutland.
- **1827–1875:** *Individual poems, occasional verses, hymns, songs, album inscriptions, prologues, and verse riddles*. The Centre’s work index records well over a thousand individual poetic and occasional titles, many identified by first lines because Andersen supplied no stable title. These resist treatment as a finite set of “books”: some are autonomous poems, some components of plays, some manuscript gifts, and some texts whose first printed manifestation occurred only after his death.

### Articles, public statements, speeches, and protocols of publication

- **1829–1875:** *Newspaper articles, prefaces, subscription announcements, corrections, open letters, reviews, travel dispatches, speeches, and polemical replies*. Birger Frank Nielsen’s standard bibliography and the Centre’s derivative register identify forty-three such bibliographic works. They include texts that act—soliciting subscriptions, correcting records, protesting reviews, accepting honors—and thus sometimes approach manifestos or protocols more closely than descriptive essays.
- **1829:** *Til Kjøbenhavns-Postens Læsere, i Anledning af Herr XXX’s Critik over “Kjærlighed paa Nicolai Taarn”*. Public polemical reply.
- **1829:** *Et Stykke Kage til den lille XXX*. Satirical public response.
- **1829–1834:** *Subscriptionsplan: Digte; Subskriptionsplan til “Phantasier og Skizzer”; Fortale til Subskriptionsindbydelse paa “Improvisatoren”*. Publication protocols: texts intended to bring not-yet-existing books into material existence.
- **1833–1866:** *Published extracts and fragments of private letters from Paris, London, Spain, the Netherlands, and Portugal*. Letters transformed by publication into travel reportage; their status oscillates between correspondence and authored public text.
- **1851:** *Et Par Ord om “Hyldemoer”*. Authorial commentary on the stage work.
- **1856:** *Berigtigelse af en Fejl i “Mit Livs Eventyr”*. Public correction to the autobiography, acting upon the documentary record.
- **1867:** *Svarskrivelse til Odense Kommunalbestyrelse* and *Takketale ved Overrækkelsen af Diplomet*. Acceptance letter and ceremonial speech connected with Andersen’s honorary citizenship.
- **1829–1875:** *Et kjøbenhavnsk Eventyr Nytaarsnat; Tanker over et Nul; Tanker over nogle gamle forslidte Skoe; Aphorismer efter Lichtenberg, Jean Paul og L. Börne; En Samling Digterblomster og Aforismer af forskellige Forfattere; En geographisk Beskrivelse af det menneskelige Hoved; Akrostichon-Gaade*. Seven minor satirical, humorous, compilatory, or puzzle texts. The two collections derived from other writers complicate “authorship”: Andersen selected or adapted them rather than originating every sentence.

### Visual obra: drawings, collages, picture books, and paper cuts

- **c. 1820s–1870s:** *Drawings and sketches*. Andersen made hundreds of pencil, ink, and wash drawings, chiefly during travel. Many are untitled or descriptively titled by later cataloguers, so catalogue descriptions should not be mistaken for authorial titles.
- **1830s–1870s:** *Silhouettes and paper cuts*. A large corpus of folded-paper cutouts, often made while storytelling and commonly given away. Many are ephemeral, untitled, altered by unfolding, or survive only through provenance; they resist being treated as a closed series of individually titled works.
- **1840s–1870s:** *Scrapbooks and collage albums*. Composite objects assembled from prints, photographs, drawings, labels, handwritten matter, and found paper. Authorship is distributed between Andersen’s selection and arrangement and the makers of the appropriated images.
- **1859:** *Christine Stampe’s picture book*. Handmade collage-and-drawing album made as a gift for a child; a singular book-object rather than a reproducible publication.
- **1862:** *Astrid Stampe’s picture book*. Handmade visual-narrative gift album.
- **1868:** *Marie Henriques’s picture book*. Handmade collage album; one of the personalized children’s picture books preserved in museum collections.
- **nineteenth century; individual dates vary:** *Sixteen known handmade picture books for children*. Museum Odense catalogues sixteen such albums associated with families in Andersen’s circle. Their category is unstable: they are simultaneously gift, collage, illustrated narrative, social performance, and unique book.
- **date uncertain:** *A Fully Cut Fairy Tale*. Elaborate paper cut now in the Metropolitan Museum of Art. Its modern descriptive title may not be Andersen’s; the cutout manifests storytelling visually without being a written tale.
- **dates uncertain:** *Decorative screens and screen pictures*. Assemblages or images prepared for domestic screens; where the surviving object is collaborative or reconstructed, Andersen’s precise share cannot always be isolated.

### Diaries and correspondence

- **1825–1875:** *Dagbøger* (*Diaries*). A sustained manuscript record occupying multiple notebooks and later issued in scholarly editions. The diary is both documentation and continuing literary project, but its private manuscript sequence was not fashioned as a single lifetime publication.
- **c. 1820s–1875:** *Letters*. Thousands of letters survive. They are made texts but not a single authored obra: each is an act within a correspondence, often co-determined by recipient, occasion, preservation, and later editorial selection.
- **1831–1873:** *Travel diaries and almanac notes*. Working records that feed travel books, autobiography, fiction, and poems; their status lies between documentary trace, compositional workshop, and independent text.

### Collected manifestations rather than new constituent works

- **1853–1879:** *Samlede Skrifter af H. C. Andersen*, thirty-three volumes. The first Danish collected-writings project; its volumes are authorially and editorially significant manifestations, but most contents are earlier works rather than thirty-three newly made texts.
- **1876–1880:** *Samlede Skrifter, Anden Udgave*, fifteen volumes. Revised collected edition and therefore a new editorial manifestation, not a second body of works.
- **2003–2006:** *Andersens samlede værker*, eighteen volumes. Modern critical collected edition used to establish texts and variants; it is an editorial scholarly object, not a work made by Andersen.
