## Works by Lev Davidovich Bronstein

### Books, pamphlets, and extended political obras

- **1903:** *Report of the Siberian Delegation*. A congress report and polemical pamphlet, originally published as *Vtoroi Sʺezd RSDRP: Otchet Sibirskoi Delegatsii*; bibliographies sometimes date it 1904 because of later circulation. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Pre-revolutionary_period_(1903%E2%80%931916))
- **1904:** *Our Political Tasks*. Trotsky’s first extended political book, directed against Lenin’s organizational ideas after the RSDLP split. [Bibliographic record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1904/tasks/)
- **1905:** *Until the Ninth of January*. A pamphlet written around the revolutionary crisis of 1905; the English title is conventional rather than an original English publication title. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Pre-revolutionary_period_(1903%E2%80%931916))
- **1906:** *Our Revolution*. A constructed collection containing Trotsky’s writings on 1905, including the essay later known as *Results and Prospects*; it is therefore both an obra and a container for earlier texts. [Bibliographic record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1906/results/)
- **1906:** *History of the Soviet of Workers’ Deputies in the City of St. Petersburg*. An edited documentary history rather than a solely authored book; Trotsky made its editorial form while much of its substance belonged to the Soviet collectively. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Pre-revolutionary_period_(1903%E2%80%931916))
- **1906:** *Results and Prospects*. A theoretical essay within *Our Revolution* that first gave extended form to Trotsky’s theory of permanent revolution; its later independent editions should not be counted as new obras. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1931/tpr/)
- **1907:** *In Defense of the Party*. A factional pamphlet produced amid the disputes of Russian social democracy. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Pre-revolutionary_period_(1903%E2%80%931916))
- **1907:** *There and Back Again*. A prison-and-escape narrative, subsequently translated under such titles as *My Flight from Siberia*; the changing titles are manifestations of one autobiographical obra. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Pre-revolutionary_period_(1903%E2%80%931916))
- **1914:** *The War and the International*. An antiwar book written in German and later issued in English as *The Bolsheviki and World Peace*; the latter is an edition-title, not another work. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1914/war/)
- **1917:** *The Program of Peace*. A programmatic pamphlet assembled from wartime arguments and revised for revolutionary Russia; it lies between theoretical obra and political protocol. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1917:** *After the July Days: What Next?* A tactical pamphlet intervening in an immediate revolutionary situation. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1917:** *The Struggle for State Power*. A revolutionary pamphlet whose purpose is openly performative and strategic, not merely descriptive. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1918:** *History of the October Revolution*. A participant-history subsequently translated as *From October to Brest-Litovsk* and *The History of the Russian Revolution to Brest-Litovsk*; these are manifestations of the same work. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1919:** *Years of the Great Break: People of the Old and New Epochs*. A book of historical-political portraits and reflections rather than a continuous narrative. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1920:** *Terrorism and Communism: Anti-Kautsky*. A sustained polemic defending revolutionary dictatorship and coercion, also published as *The Defense of Terrorism*; the alternate title does not denote a separate obra. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_and_Communism)
- **1921:** *The New Stage*. A political collection whose integrity is editorial and topical rather than that of a book conceived as a single argument. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1922:** *Between Imperialism and Revolution*. An extended intervention on Georgia, published in English as *Between Red and White*. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1922/red-white/)
- **1923:** *Problems of Everyday Life*. A collection of essays on culture, family, habit, and the transformation of daily life; the collected form is an obra, while its chapters also began as separate interventions. [Bibliographic record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1923/newcourse/)
- **1923:** *The Communist Movement in France*. A political collection built from reports and interventions concerning the French Communist movement. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1923:** *Literature and Revolution*. A book of literary and cultural criticism assembled partly from previously published essays; it resists separation into “politics” and “art criticism.” [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1924/lit_revo/)
- **1923–1924:** *War and Revolution*. A two-volume collection of Trotsky’s writings on the First World War; it is a retrospectively assembled corpus rather than one continuously composed text. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1923–1925:** *How the Revolution Armed*. A three-volume, five-part Russian collection of military writings, orders, reports, and speeches; it is simultaneously documentary archive, military protocol, and retrospective obra. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotsky%27s_Military_Writings)
- **1924:** *The October Generation*. A political collection addressing the generation formed by the revolution. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1924:** *West and East*. A collection of writings on international revolutionary strategy rather than a single newly composed treatise. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1924:** *Five Years of the Communist International*. A retrospective collection of congress reports, speeches, and strategic writings, later expanded in English as *The First Five Years of the Communist International*. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1924:** *The New Course*. A connected series of articles and letters opposing bureaucratization inside the Communist Party; it acts as a factional intervention as well as a book. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1923/newcourse/)
- **1924:** *On Lenin: Materials for a Biographer*. A group of recollections and character studies, later titled simply *Lenin*; its subtitle accurately signals that it is material toward a biography, not a completed conventional life. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1924:** *Lessons of October*. A long essay first serving as an introduction to a collected-works volume and then circulating independently; its status shifts between paratext, pamphlet, and factional manifesto. Some bibliographies date the independent edition 1925. [Bibliographic note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#cite_note-7)
- **1925:** *1905*. A revised historical obra incorporating and reshaping writings first published in and around *Our Revolution*; it should not be confused with the events themselves or with every earlier component text. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1907/1905/)
- **1925:** *Where Is Britain Going?* A book-length political analysis also translated as *Whither England?*; the two English titles are manifestations of one work. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1925:** *Toward Socialism or Capitalism?* An analysis of the Soviet economy, expanded from the essay *The Language of Figures*. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1925/ts/)
- **1926:** *Europe and America*. A collection of speeches and essays on the altered relationship between European and American capitalism. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1926:** *Problems of the British Revolution*. A book or substantial pamphlet whose bibliographic identity overlaps thematically, but not completely, with *Where Is Britain Going?* [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1927:** *Platform of the Joint Opposition*. A collectively authored program associated with Trotsky, Zinoviev, and other oppositionists; it must not be attributed to Trotsky alone, although he was central to its making. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1928:** *The Real Situation in Russia*. An English-language compilation of Opposition documents; its book-form is editorial, and parts are collective rather than solely Trotsky’s. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Revolutionary_period_(1917%E2%80%931928))
- **1928:** *The Third International After Lenin*. Trotsky’s critique of the Comintern draft program, smuggled abroad after the Sixth Congress; later book titles and arrangements vary, but the core work is the 1928 critique. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1929:** *What Happened and How?* A political account of Trotsky’s expulsion and deportation, issued as a pamphlet in Paris. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1929–1930:** *My Life: An Attempt at an Autobiography*. A deliberately shaped autobiography, written in exile and published in 1930; it is both testimony and political self-construction. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1930/mylife/)
- **1928–1930:** *The Permanent Revolution*. A book-length polemic written after the Chinese revolutionary defeat; it systematizes and defends a theory first developed in *Results and Prospects*, but is a distinct obra. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1931/tpr/)
- **1930–1932:** *The History of the Russian Revolution*. A large historical narrative composed in exile and first published in Russian in two volumes in 1931 and 1933; English publication divided it into three volumes, which are manifestations rather than three works. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Russian_Revolution)
- **1931–1932:** *The German Revolution and the Stalinist Bureaucracy*. A strategic pamphlet on the rise of Nazism, also circulated under titles including *What Next?* [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1932:** *Problems of the Chinese Revolution*. An authored collection of earlier articles, letters, and theses; its book-form postdates many component texts. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1932:** *The Stalin School of Falsification*. A documentary polemic incorporating letters, records, and commentary intended to restore suppressed revolutionary history. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1933:** *What Hitler Wants*. A topical political pamphlet written for an American audience. [Publication record](https://books.google.com/books?id=ROg9AAAAYAAJ)
- **1933:** *The Only Road*. A strategic pamphlet on Germany and the workers’ united front; it acts as an urgent directive as much as an analysis. [Sinclair/Knei-Paz-based bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1934:** *The Fourth International and War*. A programmatic manifesto arguing for a new revolutionary International in anticipation of another world war. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1934–1936:** *Whither France?* A collected sequence of articles and programmatic interventions, first gathered as *Où va la France?*; it is a serial political campaign given retrospective book-form. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1935:** *Diary in Exile*. A private political and personal diary, not prepared by Trotsky as a finished public work; its posthumous publication in 1958/1976 turned a manuscript record into a book. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1933–1935:** *Notebooks on Lenin, Dialectics, and Evolutionism*. Working notebooks rather than a completed, titled obra; their familiar title and organization were supplied for posthumous publication. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1935–1936:** *The Young Lenin*. The completed first portion of a projected life of Lenin, published in French as *Vie de Lénine: Jeunesse*; the larger biography remained unrealized. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1936:** *The Revolution Betrayed: What Is the Soviet Union and Where Is It Going?* A book-length diagnosis of Soviet bureaucracy and a conditional forecast of the USSR’s future. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1936/revbet/)
- **1937:** *The Case of Leon Trotsky*. The verbatim record of Trotsky’s testimony before the Dewey Commission, edited by others; it is a jointly produced juridical manifestation, not a book authored by Trotsky in the ordinary sense. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1938:** *Their Morals and Ours*. A philosophical-political essay on revolutionary ethics, commonly issued as a pamphlet. Some bibliographies attach 1937 to it, but the essay was written and first published in 1938. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1938/morals/morals.htm)
- **1938:** *The Death Agony of Capitalism and the Tasks of the Fourth International*. A program and protocol for revolutionary action, better known as the *Transitional Program*; Trotsky drafted it through discussion with collaborators, and the founding congress adopted it, so individual authorship and collective institutional authority coexist. [Program history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Transitional_Program)
- **1939–1940:** *In Defense of Marxism*. A connected body of articles and letters produced during the factional dispute in the US Socialist Workers Party; the familiar book was assembled posthumously in 1942 and is therefore an editorial obra made from Trotsky’s separate interventions. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/idom/dm/)
- **1939–1940:** *The Living Thoughts of Karl Marx*. Trotsky’s selection, arrangement, and interpretive introduction to Marx’s *Capital*; it is an editorial and pedagogical construction, not a claim to authorship of Marx’s text. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Exile_period_(1929%E2%80%931940))
- **1938–1940:** *Stalin: An Appraisal of the Man and His Influence*. An unfinished biographical-political project left incomplete at Trotsky’s death; Charles Malamuth translated, arranged, and supplemented the posthumous edition, whose final shape therefore cannot be treated as wholly Trotsky’s. [Publication and editorial history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalin:_An_Appraisal_of_the_Man_and_His_Influence)

### Manifestos, theses, programs, and protocols

- **1905:** *Manifesto of the Petersburg Soviet*. Trotsky drafted or helped draft numerous proclamations for the Soviet, but surviving texts possess collective institutional authorship; Isaac Deutscher credits him with most of its manifestos and resolutions, not every document without qualification. [Bibliographic discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography)
- **1919:** *Invitation to the First Congress of the Communist International*. An institutional summons meant to convene and constitute a political body; it acts rather than merely describes. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1919/01/invitation.htm)
- **1919:** *Manifesto of the Communist International to the Workers of the World*. A founding manifesto drafted by Trotsky and issued under the authority of the Comintern’s First Congress. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1919–1920:** *Orders of the Day to the Red Army and Navy*. A serial body of military protocols and performative commands rather than one fixed literary work; individual orders have separate dates and titles. [Military-writings collection](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1918/military/)
- **1920:** *Manifesto of the Second World Congress of the Communist International*. A collectively authorized manifesto substantially drafted by Trotsky; its institutional voice complicates single authorship. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1920/07/manifesto.htm)
- **1921:** *Theses on the World Situation and the Tasks of the Communist International*. A strategic protocol adopted through a collective congress process; bibliographic attribution to Trotsky should not erase that institutional status. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1922:** *A Militant Labour Program for the French Communist Party*. A practical party protocol intended to organize action, not simply an essay about French politics. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1930:** *Manifesto on Unemployment*. A programmatic intervention associated with the International Left Opposition; its manifesto form is performative and organizational. [Collected-writings chronology](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1940:** *Manifesto of the Fourth International on the Imperialist War and the Proletarian World Revolution*. Trotsky’s final major manifesto, adopted in the name of the Fourth International; authorship is textual, while authority is collective. [Collected-writings record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1940/05/manifesto.htm)

### Periodicals and continuing editorial obras

- **1908–1912:** *Pravda* (Vienna). A Russian-language workers’ newspaper edited by Trotsky; it is a continuing collective obra rather than a single text, and it is distinct from the Bolshevik *Pravda* founded in 1912. [Historical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pravda)
- **1914–1916:** *Nashe Slovo*. A Paris-based Russian internationalist daily on whose editorial staff Trotsky served; no honest attribution can turn the whole collaborative newspaper into his sole work. [Chronological record](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/download/hrr-vol1.pdf)
- **1929–1940:** *Bulletin of the Opposition (Bolshevik-Leninists)*. A serial political and editorial obra founded and directed by Trotsky in exile; 87 issues appeared through 1941, but its articles had multiple authors and the last issues postdated him. [Bibliographic overview](https://iskra-research.org/Trotsky/bibliografiia.html)
- **1900–1940:** *Articles, reviews, correspondence, interviews, speeches, declarations, memoranda, and archival manuscripts*. This enormous documentary corpus—estimated at about 30,000 items—resists treatment as a finite list of “works”: many pieces are untitled, jointly signed, reconstructed from transcripts, unpublished, or accessible only in archives. Louis Sinclair’s approximately 1,100-page bibliography and the still-incomplete Trotsky Internet Archive demonstrate why a books-only enumeration cannot stand for everything Trotsky made. [Scope and completeness note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography) [Trotsky Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)

### Organizations, institutions, and material-political constructions

- **1897–1898:** *South Russian Workers’ Union*. A clandestine workers’ organization co-founded by Bronstein and others in Nikolayev; it was a collective political construction, not his individual obra. Its leaflets and newspaper likewise require shared or uncertain attribution.
- **1905:** *St Petersburg Soviet of Workers’ Deputies*. Trotsky became a leading figure and chairman, shaping its resolutions, newspaper, and public voice, but he neither invented nor singly made the Soviet; its resistance to individual authorship is essential to its form. [Deutscher citation summarized in the bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography)
- **1917–1918:** *People’s Commissariat for Foreign Affairs documentary practice*. As commissar, Trotsky produced and authorized diplomatic notes, proclamations, and Brest-Litovsk records; this is an administrative-documentary oeuvre with institutional, secretarial, and sometimes collective authorship. [Collected Soviet-government documents](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/1918/govtdocs/)
- **1918–1920:** *Trotsky’s armoured command train*. A mobile military headquarters combining transport, communications, command, propaganda, printing, and supply; as a political-machine assemblage it approaches a wartime Gesamtkunstwerk, though engineers, soldiers, printers, and administrators—not Trotsky alone—physically made and operated it.
- **1918–1924:** *The Workers’ and Peasants’ Red Army as reorganized during the Civil War*. Trotsky was its principal political organizer and military commissar, but “creator of the Red Army” is a contested shorthand that effaces collective construction, earlier formations, military specialists, party institutions, and millions of participants. It therefore resists classification as his personal work. [Biographical and archival overview](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)
- **1923–1927:** *The Left Opposition*. A factional political project co-created with other Bolshevik oppositionists; its platforms, declarations, networks, and practices form a collective obra rather than an individually authored organization.
- **1930:** *International Left Opposition*. An international organizational project initiated under Trotsky’s leadership; it remained a network and preparatory formation, making “project” more exact than a completed institutional work.
- **1933–1936:** *International Communist League*. The reorganized international opposition was an intermediary project directed toward founding a new International, not merely a renamed text-producing body.
- **1938:** *Fourth International*. A transnational political institution founded under Trotsky’s leadership but made collectively by its constituent organizations and delegates; its unfinished ambition to become a mass “World Party of Socialist Revolution” is part of the work’s identity. [Collected-writings biographical note](https://www.marxists.org/archive/trotsky/works/index.htm)

### Posthumous editorial manifestations, not new Trotsky obras

- **1942:** *In Defense of Marxism*. The first book manifestation of Trotsky’s 1939–1940 factional writings; the compilation is posthumous even though its constituent texts are authentic. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1946:** *Stalin: An Appraisal of the Man and His Influence*. The delayed posthumous manifestation of the unfinished *Stalin* project, editorially altered and supplemented by Charles Malamuth. [Bibliographic note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#cite_note-10)
- **1964:** *The Trotsky Papers, 1917–1922*. An archival edition of correspondence and documents, not a book Trotsky conceived or made as such. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1971:** *The Struggle Against Fascism in Germany*. A posthumous thematic construction made from Trotsky’s writings of 1931–1940, including material omitted from other collections. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1973:** *The Spanish Revolution, 1931–1939*. A posthumous editorial collection, not a single book planned by Trotsky; the component articles, letters, and statements remain the actual authored things. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spanish_Revolution,_1931%E2%80%931939_(Trotsky_book))
- **1972–1979:** *Writings of Leon Trotsky, 1929–1940*. A fourteen-volume editorial collection covering the exile years; it is indispensable as a collected-works apparatus but is not itself a fourteen-volume obra designed by Trotsky. [Publisher’s series description](https://www.pathfinderpress.com/collections/writings-of-leon-trotsky/products/writings-of-leon-trotsky-1929)
- **1975–1981:** *The Challenge of the Left Opposition*. Three posthumously arranged volumes covering 1923–1929; they manifest an editorial reconstruction of a political corpus rather than three works made under those titles by Trotsky. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1976:** *Leon Trotsky on China*. A posthumous thematic collection containing numerous texts absent from earlier book collections; its inclusiveness does not make it an author-conceived book. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1977:** *The Crisis of the French Section, 1935–36*. A posthumous documentary collection whose editorial boundaries define the volume. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1980:** *The Balkan Wars, 1912–13*. A posthumous collection of Trotsky’s war correspondence; the articles are his contemporary works, while their book-form and English title are later editorial creations. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1986:** *Trotsky’s Notebooks, 1933–1935*. A scholarly edition that makes private, unfinished notes publicly legible; its title, selection, translation, and sequence belong partly to its editors. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)
- **1999–2001:** *The Trotsky Archive: From the Archives of the Revolution, 1927–28*. A two-volume posthumous archival edition; it is evidence of Trotsky’s documentary production, not an obra he titled or prepared. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Trotsky_bibliography#Posthumous_publications)

- Pathfinder Press, [*Writings of Leon Trotsky, 1929–1940*](https://www.pathfinderpress.com/collections/writings-of-leon-trotsky/products/writings-of-leon-trotsky-1929), fourteen volumes. Used as the principal collected-works framework for the exile period.
- Wolfgang and Petra Lubitz, [*Lubitz’ Leon Trotsky Bibliography*](https://www.trotskyana.net/LubitzBibliographies/Trotsky_Bibliography/Leon_Trotsky_Bibliography.html), 31st online ed. (2026), ISSN 2190-0183. Consulted as a current bibliographic control, especially for distinguishing writings by Trotsky from the vastly larger literature about Trotsky and Trotskyism.
- Hoover Institution Archives, [*Register of the Leon Trotsky Collection, 1917–1980*](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf296n98nm/). Used to check the existence and archival status of manuscripts, correspondence, papers, and posthumous editorial materials.
