## Works by Ludwig Heinrich Edler von Mises

### Independent treatises, monographs, and dissertations

- **1902:** *Die Entwicklung des gutsherrlich-bäuerlichen Verhältnisses in Galizien, 1772–1848*. A historical dissertation and Mises’s first separately published scholarly work, examining agrarian relations and peasant emancipation in Galicia.

- **1912:** *Theorie des Geldes und der Umlaufsmittel*. A monetary-theory treatise. The substantially revised 1924 German edition and the expanded English *The Theory of Money and Credit* are later manifestations of this obra, not separate original works.

- **1914:** *Die Störungen im Wirtschaftsleben der österreichisch-ungarischen Monarchie während der Jahre 1912–13*. A commissioned empirical study of the economic disturbances in Austria-Hungary; it is closer to an institutional research report than to Mises’s later theoretical books.

- **1919:** *Nation, Staat und Wirtschaft: Beiträge zur Politik und Geschichte der Zeit*. A political-historical monograph on nationalism, war, and the liberal order, later manifested in English as *Nation, State, and Economy*.

- **1922:** *Die Gemeinwirtschaft: Untersuchungen über den Sozialismus*. A systematic economic and sociological treatise on socialism. The rewritten 1932 German edition and English *Socialism: An Economic and Sociological Analysis* are manifestations of the same developing obra.

- **1927:** *Liberalismus*. A compact political treatise defining the program and institutional logic of classical liberalism; English editions generally bear the title *Liberalism: In the Classical Tradition*.

- **1928:** *Geldwertstabilisierung und Konjunkturpolitik*. A monetary-policy monograph joining a critique of price-level stabilization to Mises’s theory of bank credit and the trade cycle.

- **1929:** *Kritik des Interventionismus: Untersuchungen zur Wirtschaftspolitik und Wirtschaftsideologie der Gegenwart*. A deliberately organized collection of six interconnected studies arguing that interventionism cannot constitute a stable economic system. Its English manifestation is *A Critique of Interventionism*.

- **1931:** *Die Ursachen der Wirtschaftskrise: Ein Vortrag*. A separately issued lecture-pamphlet diagnosing economic crisis through monetary expansion and interventionist policy.

- **1931:** *Probleme der Wertlehre: Die psychologischen Wurzeln des Widerstandes gegen die nationalökonomische Theorie*. A separately issued methodological and intellectual-historical essay; its scale and publication form place it between article and monograph.

- **1933:** *Grundprobleme der Nationalökonomie: Untersuchungen über Verfahren, Aufgaben und Inhalt der Wirtschafts- und Gesellschaftslehre*. A collected but conceptually unified methodological obra. The English *Epistemological Problems of Economics* is a translated and editorially reshaped manifestation.

- **1940:** *Nationalökonomie: Theorie des Handelns und Wirtschaftens*. Mises’s first comprehensive system of praxeology and economics. *Human Action* substantially reworked this project in English and should not be treated as a mere translation.

- **1944:** *Bureaucracy*. An English-language monograph distinguishing bureaucratic administration from management governed by market calculation.

- **1944:** *Omnipotent Government: The Rise of the Total State and Total War*. A political-historical treatise on statism, nationalism, National Socialism, and war.

- **1947:** *Planned Chaos*. A short polemical monograph against socialist and interventionist planning, first issued as a supplement to an English edition of *Socialism* but also circulated independently.

- **1949:** *Human Action: A Treatise on Economics*. A comprehensive English-language reformulation and expansion of the project first embodied in *Nationalökonomie*. Its extensive independent composition makes it a new obra as well as a later stage of the same intellectual project.

- **1951:** *Profit and Loss*. A separately published economic essay on entrepreneurial judgment, monetary calculation, profit, and loss; formally a pamphlet, substantively a compact theoretical treatise.

- **1956:** *The Anti-Capitalistic Mentality*. A social-philosophical monograph analyzing cultural and psychological hostility toward capitalism.

- **1957:** *Theory and History: An Interpretation of Social and Economic Evolution*. A methodological and philosophical treatise distinguishing theory, history, understanding, and materialist interpretations of social change.

- **1962:** *The Ultimate Foundation of Economic Science: An Essay on Method*. Mises’s final original book-length methodological statement, defending the epistemological foundations of praxeology.

- **1969:** *The Historical Setting of the Austrian School of Economics*. A long intellectual-historical essay issued as a small book; it combines institutional history, recollection, and methodological interpretation.

### Manifestos, protocols, and policy interventions

- **1918:** *Vom Ziel der Handelspolitik*. A programmatic policy essay advocating free trade; because it seeks to redirect public action, it functions partly as a liberal manifesto.

- **1918:** *Die Kriegsfinanzierung Österreich-Ungarns und Deutschlands*. A wartime fiscal memorandum explaining how war’s real costs are borne and comparing taxation, borrowing, and monetary finance.

- **1919:** *Die wirtschaftliche Lage Deutsch-Österreichs und die Bedingungen des Staatskredites*. A policy memorandum on the fiscal and monetary reconstruction of post-imperial Austria.

- **1919:** *Zur Frage des Anschlusses Deutschösterreichs an Deutschland*. A memorandum on the monetary and fiscal requirements of a proposed Austrian-German union; it is an intervention in a contingent political project rather than a timeless treatise.

- **1920:** *Die Wirtschaftsrechnung im sozialistischen Gemeinwesen*. A theoretical article that also acted as a challenge to socialist economists: its intervention initiated the modern socialist-calculation controversy and therefore has a manifesto-like historical force.

- **1921:** *Die Arbeit im sozialistischen Gemeinwesen*. A companion essay treating labor incentives and allocation under socialism.

- **1923:** *Die geldtheoretische Seite des Stabilisierungsproblems*. A monetary-stabilization memorandum completed amid Central European hyperinflation; it is both analysis and a practical reform protocol.

- **1926:** *Interventionismus*. An essay defining interventionism as a distinct policy system and arguing that successive controls propel it toward socialism.

- **1929:** *Verstaatlichung des Kredits?* A policy essay opposing proposals to nationalize credit; it was incorporated into later manifestations of *Kritik des Interventionismus*.

- **1934:** *Maxims for the Discussion of the Methodological Problems of the Social Sciences*. A paper prepared for Mises’s private seminar. Its numbered propositions work as a discussion protocol as much as a conventional essay.

- **1936:** *La théorie dite autrichienne du cycle économique*. A French-language exposition of Austrian business-cycle theory.

- **1938:** *Les équations de l’économie mathématique et le problème du calcul économique en régime socialiste*. A French-language methodological essay connecting the limits of mathematical equations with the socialist-calculation problem.

- **1938:** *The Non-Neutrality of Money*. A conference paper arguing that changes in money cannot affect all prices and persons uniformly.

- **1940:** *Interventionism: An Economic Analysis*. A book-length manuscript written in English but not published intact during Mises’s lifetime; its project was complete, while its public manifestation was deferred until 1998.

- **1940–1942:** *Erinnerungen* / *Notes and Recollections*. An intellectual autobiography drafted after Mises’s flight to the United States, then entrusted to his wife rather than published. It resists being called a lifetime “memoir”: it is a private retrospective ending around 1940, posthumously manifested in German and English in 1978.

- **1943:** *Postwar Economic Reconstruction*. A policy memorandum proposing liberal reconstruction, free trade, sound money, and private ownership after the Second World War; later editors supplied its publication context.

- **1944:** *A Noninflationary Proposal for Postwar Monetary Reconstruction*. A prescriptive monetary memorandum and therefore a protocol for institutional reconstruction rather than a descriptive essay.

- **1945:** *Economic Planning*. A separately issued pamphlet drawn from Mises’s arguments against central planning; its bibliographic independence exceeds its independence as a newly composed work.

- **1946:** *The Trade Cycle and Credit Expansion: The Economic Consequences of Cheap Money*. A memorandum on credit expansion and depression, subsequently printed as an independent essay.

- **1947:** *Observations on the Cooperative Movement*. A commissioned analytical report on consumer and producer cooperatives. It resists classification as a free-standing theoretical work because its form and purpose are institutional and advisory.

- **1948:** *Stones into Bread: The Keynesian Miracle*. A polemical essay criticizing Keynesian credit expansion and deficit finance.

- **1949:** *The Why of Human Action*. A methodological address explaining the scope of praxeology; it is related to, but not simply an extract from, *Human Action*.

- **1950:** *Middle-of-the-Road Policy Leads to Socialism*. A short political-economic manifesto arguing that cumulative intervention cannot remain a stable compromise.

- **1952:** *Planning for Freedom*. An author-supervised collection of essays and addresses rather than a single newly composed text. Enlarged editions changed its contents, so the title names an evolving anthology-project as well as a book.

- **1953:** *The Theory of Money and Credit*. The first English-language manifestation of the revised monetary treatise, translated by H. E. Batson and supplied with material reflecting Mises’s later thinking; translation and revision complicate any claim that it is simply the 1912 object in English.

- **1958:** *Liberty and Property*. A Mont Pelerin Society address later issued as a pamphlet; its defense of private property acts publicly and programmatically, giving it the character of a liberal manifesto.

### Short writings as a dispersed obra

- **1904–1973:** *Articles, addresses, reviews, forewords, letters, memoranda, and seminar papers*. The standard bibliographic tradition records hundreds of short items—307 are presently catalogued in the chronological Mises catalogue—including unsigned editorials, book reviews, policy memoranda, newspaper interventions, lectures, introductions, and texts recovered from Mises’s papers. This dispersed corpus resists treatment as 307 wholly independent obras: some records are translations, retitled reprints, excerpts, stenographic reports, or editorial reconstructions, while Greaves explicitly reported that earlier bibliographies became incomplete when additional papers were discovered.

- **1918–1938:** *Vienna Chamber of Commerce memoranda and policy papers*. This is a practical body of work made in Mises’s institutional role: reports on currency, taxation, banking, trade, labor, and Austrian reconstruction, many unpublished in his lifetime. The individual documents survive as distinct texts, but the recovered corpus also constitutes an advisory project whose boundaries depend on archival attribution.

- **1941–1944:** *Unsigned editorials for the National Association of Manufacturers*. The attributed pieces include writings on Nazi economic organization, inflation, transportation, securities, industrial concentration, and postwar policy. They resist secure work-by-work authorship because they appeared without signatures and entered the Mises corpus through later bibliographic and archival attribution.

- **1920s–1960s:** *Forewords, prefaces, and reviews*. These include engagements with works by Carl Menger, Karl Helfferich, Fritz Machlup, William H. Hutt, Louis Baudin, Murray N. Rothbard, and many others. Each is a made text, but many are paratexts whose identity depends on the book or edition in which they appeared rather than autonomous works.

### Lectures and posthumous editorial manifestations

- **1951; published 2004:** *The Free Market and Its Enemies: Pseudo-Science, Socialism, and Inflation*. A posthumous editorial manifestation of nine lectures delivered at the Foundation for Economic Education. The lectures are Mises’s performances and texts; the later book as an organized object was made by editors.

- **1952; published 2006:** *Marxism Unmasked: From Delusion to Destruction*. A posthumous reconstruction from a lecture series. It should not be silently counted as a book completed and published by Mises.

- **1959; published 1979:** *Economic Policy: Thoughts for Today and Tomorrow*. A posthumous book made from six Buenos Aires lectures, reconstructed from Bettina Bien Greaves’s notes. The lecture cycle is Mises’s obra; the continuous printed text is partly an editorial creation.

- **1960s; published 2010:** *Ludwig von Mises on Money and Inflation*. A synthesis derived from lectures that Greaves recorded, transcribed, and later arranged into twenty chapters. It resists attribution as a manuscript written by Mises, although the underlying spoken exposition is his.

- **1978:** *On the Manipulation of Money and Credit*. A posthumous editorial collection joining three earlier monetary and trade-cycle works. It is a new anthology-manifestation, not an additional work composed by Mises in 1978.

- **1978:** *The Clash of Group Interests and Other Essays*. A posthumous anthology of previously existing essays; its selection and book form belong partly to its editors.

- **1990:** *Money, Method, and the Market Process*. Richard M. Ebeling’s posthumous anthology of Mises essays. The contents are Mises’s works, but the volume as a total object is editorial rather than author-made.

- **1990:** *Economic Freedom and Interventionism: An Anthology of Articles and Essays*. Bettina Bien Greaves’s posthumous collection. It is a manifestation of dispersed writings rather than a new Mises manuscript.

- **2000–2012:** *Selected Writings of Ludwig von Mises*, three volumes. An editorial and archival Gesamtkunstwerk in the limited sense that it reunites memoranda, speeches, articles, translations, and institutional documents dispersed by war and seizure. Richard M. Ebeling constructed the volumes from Mises’s “lost papers,” recovered in a formerly secret Moscow archive; consequently the contents are Mises’s texts, while their ordering, English presentation, and collective identity are editorial creations.

- **2006:** *The Causes of the Economic Crisis, and Other Essays Before and After the Great Depression*. A posthumous compilation of five earlier monetary and trade-cycle writings. Its title and collected form should not be mistaken for a new work made by Mises in 2006.

### Misattributions and excluded manifestations

- **1976:** *My Years with Ludwig von Mises*. This is not a Mises work: it is Margit von Mises’s memoir about their life together. Its occasional placement inside Mises catalogues illustrates why catalogue records cannot automatically be equated with things he made.

- **Translations, revised editions, excerpts, and reprints:** Titles such as *Socialism*, *Liberalism*, *Nation, State, and Economy*, *Epistemological Problems of Economics*, and numerous later foreign-language editions are manifestations of works listed above, except where substantial recomposition—most importantly *Human Action*—creates a defensible new obra.
