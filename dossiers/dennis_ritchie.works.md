## Works by Dennis MacAlistair Ritchie

### Programming languages, compilers, editors, and operating systems

- **1967–1969:** *BCPL compilers for Multics and GECOS*. Software implementations of Martin Richards’s language, made while Ritchie was contributing to the Multics project; these were implementations rather than a language of his own design. [Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bigbio1st.html)

- **Late 1960s:** *ALTRAN compiler*. A compiler for the symbolic-algebra language and system ALTRAN. Ritchie identifies himself as its programmer, but his institutional bibliography does not treat the software as a publication. [Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bigbio1st.html)

- **Circa 1967–1968:** *GECOS QED*. Ritchie’s assembly-language implementation of the QED text editor for General Electric’s time-sharing system, later used as both editor and scripting language. It descended from Butler Lampson and Peter Deutsch’s QED and Ken Thompson’s versions, so it is a distinct implementation rather than an original claim to QED itself. [Ritchie’s history of QED](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html)

- **1969 onward:** *Unix*. A continuously developing operating system made collaboratively by Ken Thompson, Ritchie, and other Bell Labs researchers; Ritchie described his role as aiding Thompson, while their canonical papers and awards use joint authorship. Unix therefore resists treatment as a single-author, fixed-date obra. [Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bigbio1st.html) [Ritchie’s Unix history collection](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **Circa 1971–1973:** *NB*. An intermediate, weakly typed language between Thompson’s B and C. No source is known to survive, and even its name marks a transitional state, so it is better understood as a developmental manifestation than a finished language. [Ritchie on the early C compilers](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/primevalC.html)

- **1972–1973:** *C*. A programming language produced by Ritchie through the addition of types, syntax, and other facilities to Thompson’s B; its identity emerged gradually rather than on a single invention date. [Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bigbio1st.html) [“The Development of the C Language”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html)

- **1972–1973:** *last1120c*. The earlier of two surviving “primeval” C compilers, preserved as source files from the departure from the PDP-11/20. Ritchie cautioned that its tape dates may be wrong by a year, so no more precise date is defensible. [Ritchie’s archival description and source inventory](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/primevalC.html)

- **1972–1973:** *prestruct-c*. A second surviving early C compiler, captured immediately before Ritchie began changing the compiler to use structures itself. Its exact date is likewise uncertain, and the surviving tape is an archaeological snapshot rather than a formally released product. [Ritchie’s archival description and source inventory](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/primevalC.html)

- **1972–1973:** *Unix kernel rewritten in C*. A new implementation of the operating-system kernel, principally associated with Ritchie and Thompson, that made Unix substantially portable. It is both software and a transformation of an existing collective obra, not a separately titled publication. [“The Development of the C Language”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html)

- **1975–1976:** *Program implementing the ciphertext-only attack on the M-209*. Software written by Ritchie to realize Jim Reeds’s statistical cryptanalytic method, with combinatorial ideas and code also owed to Robert Morris. It was demonstrable by approximately autumn 1976 but was not publicly distributed. [Ritchie, “Dabbling in the Cryptographic World”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/crypt.html)

- **1976–1977:** *Interdata 8/32 port of Unix*. A port by Ritchie and Stephen C. Johnson, with assistance from Thompson, that demonstrated Unix’s portability. As a port it is simultaneously a new software manifestation and an intervention in the continuing Unix project. [Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bigbio1st.html)

- **Early 1980s:** *Streams*. An input/output framework for connecting devices, protocols, and applications, introduced in the Eighth Edition research Unix system. Ritchie called it his last important technical contribution to Unix; later systems called a related, expanded interface STREAMS, so the names should not be treated as perfectly interchangeable. [Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bigbio1st.html) [“A Stream Input/Output System”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/stream.html)

- **1980s:** *Unix guest facility for the Cray X/MP*. System software allowing an ordinary Unix process to control work performed under the Cray UNICOS operating system. The associated paper survives more visibly than the facility itself. [Ritchie’s Cray materials](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cray.html)

- **1990s:** *Plan 9 contributions*. Ritchie said that he contributed only “a few bits and pieces” and managerial support to Plan 9. The system is a collective Bell Labs obra, but the available evidence resists naming Plan 9 as a work made by Ritchie in the same sense as C or his Unix contributions. [Ritchie’s Plan 9 and Inferno note](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **1990s:** *Inferno project support*. Ritchie managed members of the group that created Inferno and co-wrote accounts of it, but explicitly characterized his technical role as more a matter of supporting the work than making the system. Inferno should therefore not be silently attributed to him as an individual creation. [Ritchie’s Plan 9 and Inferno note](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

### Books, manuals, and protocol-like reference texts

- **May 1972:** *QED Text Editor*. Bell Labs Computing Science Technical Report No. 5, jointly credited to Brian W. Kernighan, Ritchie, and Ken Thompson; it documents the editor and functions as an operational manual as much as a descriptive paper. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **January 1974:** *C Reference Manual*. An early Bell Labs memorandum by Ritchie defining the language then in use. It is a protocol-like text—prescriptive enough to constitute the language’s interface—and survives as a scan rather than a conventional book. [Ritchie’s C archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/) [January 1974 scan](https://www.bell-labs.com/usr/dmr/www/cman74.pdf)

- **May 1975:** *UNIX Programmer’s Manual, Sixth Edition*. A jointly credited Thompson–Ritchie manual and documentary manifestation of Sixth Edition Unix. Its many constituent manual pages and papers had additional authors, so joint credit for the compilation does not imply that Ritchie wrote every component. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **May 1975:** *C Reference Manual*. The revised specification distributed in the Sixth Edition manual’s documentary volume. Its structure and wording later passed into *The C Programming Language* and influenced the ANSI/ISO specification. [Ritchie’s C archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/) [Manual](https://www.bell-labs.com/usr/dmr/www/cman.pdf)

- **October 1975:** *The C Programming Language*. Bell Labs Computing Science Technical Report No. 31, credited to Ritchie, Brian W. Kernighan, and M. E. Lesk. This report is a predecessor of, not simply another printing of, the later Kernighan–Ritchie book. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1978:** *The C Programming Language*. A book jointly made with Brian W. Kernighan, combining tutorial exposition with Ritchie’s language reference and Unix-interface material. Ritchie stated that Kernighan wrote nearly all the exposition while he supplied the reference appendix and the Unix-interface chapter. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Ritchie on the division of authorship](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html)

- **1978:** *UNIX Programmer’s Manual, Seventh Edition*. A Thompson–Ritchie manual and the principal documentary manifestation of Seventh Edition research Unix. Like the Sixth Edition, it is a collective compilation whose blanket editorial credit should not erase the authors of its individual parts. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Ritchie’s Seventh Edition archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **Circa 1978:** *Recent Changes to C*. A one-page supplement included in locally printed copies of the Seventh Edition manual. Ritchie could not locate a contemporary machine-readable original and later retyped it, making the surviving file a reconstructed manifestation. [Ritchie’s C archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **July 1979:** *The M4 Macro Processor*. A manual by Kernighan and Ritchie, issued in Volume 2 of the Unix programmer’s manual and also identified as a Bell Labs technical report. It is a protocol in the atlas owner’s sense: a text teaching and defining the use of a software tool. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1980:** *The C Programming Language*. A short encyclopedia article by Kernighan and Ritchie, distinct from their identically titled book. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1980:** *The UNIX Time-Sharing System*. A short encyclopedia article by Kernighan and Ritchie, not to be confused with the longer Thompson–Ritchie journal paper bearing the same title. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1988:** *The C Programming Language, Second Edition*. A substantially revised Kernighan–Ritchie book aligned with the emerging ANSI C standard. It is a new manifestation of the 1978 book rather than a wholly unrelated obra. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Book homepage](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

### Research papers, technical reports, and conference texts

- **1967:** *The Complexity of Loop Programs*. A theoretical computer-science paper by Albert R. Meyer and Ritchie, published in the proceedings of the 22nd ACM National Conference. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1968:** *Program Structure and Computational Complexity*. Ritchie’s nearly completed Harvard doctoral dissertation on subrecursive hierarchies. Multiple near-final drafts survive, but it was never accepted by Harvard and Ritchie never received the doctorate; consequently it resists being called a completed institutional thesis even though the intellectual project was substantially complete. [Brailsford, Kernighan, and William Ritchie](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~bwk/dmr/doceng22.pdf) [Surviving thesis versions](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~bwk/dmr/index.html)

- **Circa 1972:** *Notes for a Unix Talk*. Notes for an oral presentation rather than a formal publication; the date supplied by Ritchie is approximate. [Ritchie’s Unix archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **July 1974:** *The UNIX Time-Sharing System*. The canonical Communications of the ACM paper by Ritchie and Thompson describing Unix. Its 1978 Bell System Technical Journal appearance is an updated manifestation, not an independently titled work. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Archived paper](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cacm.html)

- **1976:** *Untitled memorandum proposing a Unix port*. An internal memorandum arguing for the purchase of a machine on which to port Unix and outlining necessary changes to C. Ritchie described it as historically important but “pro forma”; the archive supplies no stable formal title, so one should not be invented. [Ritchie’s Unix archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **1977/1978:** *UNIX Time-Sharing System: A Retrospective*. A reflective essay first associated with the January 1977 Hawaii International Conference on System Sciences and published in the 1978 Bell System Technical Journal Unix issue. The bibliography calls it both *A Retrospective* and *Unix Time-Sharing System: A Retrospective*, so the title itself has variant manifestations. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Ritchie’s archival note](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/retro.html)

- **1978:** *Portability of C Programs and the UNIX System*. A paper by Stephen C. Johnson and Ritchie explaining the portability work surrounding C and Unix. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Ritchie’s portability collection](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/portability.html)

- **1978:** *The C Programming Language*. A Bell System Technical Journal article credited to Ritchie, Johnson, Lesk, and Kernighan. It shares a title with the book and technical report but is a distinct journal manifestation with a four-person author list. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1978:** *The Hagelin Cipher Machine (M-209): Cryptanalysis from Ciphertext Alone*. An unpublished Bell Labs technical memorandum by Jim Reeds, Ritchie, and Robert Morris, submitted to *Cryptologia* and then mutually withheld after consultation with the NSA. The official bibliography labels it “Cryptologia, 1979,” but Ritchie’s later account explicitly says publication was postponed and gives the memorandum date as 1978; the apparent publication claim is therefore disputed and probably erroneous. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Ritchie’s account](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/crypt.html)

- **Circa 1968, unfinished:** *Notes on the internal workings of GECOS QED*. An intended paper that Ritchie never completed. Its unfinished status is constitutive: it is a project and surviving fragment, not a completed publication. [Ritchie’s history of QED](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html)

- **1979/1980:** *The Evolution of the UNIX Time-Sharing System*. A historical paper presented at a Sydney symposium in September 1979 and published in its 1980 proceedings; a revised manifestation appeared in the 1984 AT&T Bell Laboratories Technical Journal Unix issue. Some bibliographic records give inconsistent journal pagination, so the date and venue should not be collapsed into a single unqualified citation. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Archived paper](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/hist.html)

- **1981:** *The C Language Calling Sequence*. Bell Labs Computing Science Technical Report No. 102, jointly written with Stephen C. Johnson, examining portable calling conventions before the ANSI standard. [Ritchie’s C archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/) [Report](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/clcs.html)

- **1984:** *A Stream Input/Output System*. Ritchie’s technical account of the Streams mechanism in Eighth Edition Unix. Some cataloguing renders the title as *The UNIX System: A Stream Input-Output System*; these are title variants of the same article, not two works. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Archived article](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/stream.html)

- **1985:** *Interprocess Communication in the Eighth Edition UNIX System*. A conference paper by David L. Presotto and Ritchie, presented at the USENIX Association Summer Conference in Portland. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1987:** *UNIX—A Dialectic*. A solo conference paper presented at the USENIX Winter Conference in Washington, D.C. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **August 1988:** *The State of C*. An article by Kernighan and Ritchie in *BYTE* assessing the language during standardization. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **September 1988:** *A Guest Facility for UNICOS*. A USENIX supercomputing paper describing Ritchie’s Unix/Cray work. It is the account of a software mechanism, not the mechanism itself. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Ritchie’s Cray materials](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/cray.html)

- **June 1990:** *Interprocess Communication in the Ninth Edition UNIX System*. A journal paper by Presotto and Ritchie describing the subsequent Unix IPC design. The official bibliography gives *Software—Practice & Experience* volume 20, supplement 1; Ritchie’s homepage instead says volume 19, an unresolved bibliographic discrepancy. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Archived article](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **September 1990:** *Variable-Size Arrays in C*. A language-design proposal by Ritchie in the *Journal of C Language Translation*. Ritchie expressly noted that its approach was not the one adopted in the 1999 ISO C standard, so it remains a rejected project rather than the protocol ultimately enacted. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Archived proposal](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/vararray.html)

- **1993:** *The Development of the C Language*. Ritchie’s historical paper for the second ACM History of Programming Languages conference. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html) [Archived paper](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/chist.html)

- **1993:** *Five Little Languages and How They Grew*. Ritchie’s HOPL-II talk and slides. He emphasized that it is substantially different from *The Development of the C Language*, despite their shared occasion, so it should be counted as a separate performative text. [Ritchie’s C archive](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/)

- **1997:** *Inferno*. A COMPCon paper by Sean Dorward, David Presotto, Howard Trickey, Rob Pike, Ritchie, and Phil Winterbottom introducing the distributed-system project. It is a collective description of Inferno rather than evidence that every named author built every component. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **Winter 1997:** *The Inferno Operating System*. A fuller Bell Labs Technical Journal article by Dorward, Presotto, Trickey, Pike, Ritchie, and Winterbottom. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

- **1999:** *The Styx Architecture for Distributed Systems*. A Bell Labs Technical Journal paper by Rob Pike and Ritchie describing Inferno’s resource-sharing protocol. “Styx” is a protocol both technically and in the atlas owner’s vocabulary: a textually specified set of rules that enables action between systems. [Official Bell Labs bibliography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/bib/dmr.html)

### Historical, autobiographical, and occasional writings

- **By 2000:** *An Incomplete History of the QED Text Editor*. A retrospective web essay by Ritchie that also preserves his unfinished internal notes and distinguishes his implementation from the many other QEDs. [Essay](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/qed.html)

- **2000:** *Dabbling in the Cryptographic World—A Story*. A retrospective account of the suppressed M-209 work and the ambiguity surrounding its publication. Ritchie labels it a personal recollection checked by Reeds and Morris, not a neutral institutional history. [Essay](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/crypt.html)

- **1999 or later:** *A Letter from Washington*. An autobiographical web text about receiving the National Medal of Technology with Thompson; its archived page supplies no firm composition date, so the medal ceremony provides only a terminus post quem. [Ritchie’s homepage description](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/) [Text](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/medal.html)

- **2002:** *Ken, Unix and Games*. A brief article for the *ICGA Journal* on the relationship between Thompson’s work on games and computer systems. It incorporates a mock memorandum by M. E. Lesk, whose embedded contribution should not be reassigned to Ritchie. [Ritchie’s homepage description](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/) [Article](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/ken-games.html)
