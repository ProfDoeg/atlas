## Works by Gaius Julius Caesar

### Extant commentarii

- **c. 52–50 BCE:** *Commentarii de bello Gallico*, Books I–VII. Seven third-person campaign narratives covering 58–52 BCE; whether Caesar released them annually or composed and published them together remains disputed. The transmitted eighth book is Aulus Hirtius’s continuation, not Caesar’s work ([Cambridge Companion](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-companion-to-the-writings-of-julius-caesar/cambridge-companion-to-the-writings-of-julius-caesar/D8A5690DB41A83C20FA1131FE9A66BC7); [Balsdon](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017383500015667)).

- **after 48 BCE, probably 47–46 BCE:** *Commentarii de bello civili*, Books I–III. A third-person political and military narrative of the civil war in 49–48 BCE; it breaks off during the Alexandrian campaign, so its intended extent, final state, and publication date remain uncertain ([Packard Humanities Institute canon](https://latin.packhum.org/canon); [Balsdon](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017383500015667)).

### Works transmitted with Caesar but not made by him

- **after 50 BCE:** *Commentarii de bello Gallico*, Book VIII. This bridge from Caesar’s Gallic narrative to the civil war belongs to Aulus Hirtius by ancient testimony and its own preface; it is part of Caesar’s textual monument without being Caesar’s obra ([Balsdon](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0017383500015667)).

- **after 47 BCE:** *Bellum Alexandrinum*. A continuation of the civil-war narrative, traditionally assigned to Hirtius or another Caesarian officer but now generally treated as anonymous; competing theories include compilation from several eyewitness sources ([Digital Latin Library](https://ldlt.digitallatin.org/editions/bellum_alexandrinum/77.html); [Gaertner and Krebs, eds.](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/23413/frontmatter/9781107023413_frontmatter.pdf)).

- **after 46 BCE:** *Bellum Africum*. An anonymous eyewitness-style continuation concerning the African campaign. It belongs to the *Corpus Caesarianum*, but linguistic and stylistic evidence resists attribution to Caesar or to the author of the other supplements ([Catalogus Translationum et Commentariorum](https://catalogustranslationum.org/PDFs/volume03/v03_caesar.pdf); [authorship study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0957417416303116)).

- **after 45 BCE:** *Bellum Hispaniense*. An anonymous account of the Spanish campaign culminating at Munda; its markedly different and often difficult Latin excludes confident attribution to Caesar, Hirtius, or the other continuators ([Catalogus Translationum et Commentariorum](https://catalogustranslationum.org/PDFs/volume03/v03_caesar.pdf); [authorship study](https://humanit.hb.se/article/viewFile/515/677)).

### Linguistic project

- **55 or 54 BCE:** *De analogia ad M. Tullium Ciceronem*, Books I–II. A lost grammatical treatise, written or dictated while Caesar was returning across the Alps, which advocated regularity and discriminating usage in Latin; only quotations and testimonia survive ([Cambridge Companion](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781108215541_A31873439/preview-9781108215541_A31873439.pdf); [PHI canon](https://latin.packhum.org/canon)).

### Political polemic

- **45 BCE:** *Anticato*, Books I–II. A lost polemical reply to Cicero’s memorial praise of Cato the Younger: a manifesto-like intervention in the struggle over Cato’s posthumous political meaning, preserved only in quotations and notices ([PHI text and Klotz edition](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/5/0); [Cambridge Companion contents](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/23413/frontmatter/9781107023413_frontmatter.pdf)).

### Poetry and drama

- **youth; exact date unknown:** *Laudes Herculis*. A lost composition in praise of Hercules, perhaps a poem but not securely classifiable from the surviving notice; Augustus later prohibited its publication ([Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 56](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/5_Lo_M_T_1934.pdf)).

- **youth; exact date unknown:** *Oedipus*. A lost verse tragedy; Augustus’s later ban on publication means that its known existence rests on testimony rather than a surviving text ([Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 56](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/5_Lo_M_T_1934.pdf)).

- **date unknown:** *Poem on Terence*. An untitled literary epigram evaluating the comic dramatist Terence, preserved fragmentarily by Suetonius; the title is modern description rather than an ancient title ([Klotz’s *Carmina et prolusiones* via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/6/0)).

- **46 BCE:** *Iter* (“The Journey”). A lost travel poem composed during Caesar’s rapid journey from Rome to southern Spain; it is unusually close to the atlas’s literal sense of a journey made into text ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/46946/chapter-abstract/422653843); [Cambridge Companion](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781108215541_A31873439/preview-9781108215541_A31873439.pdf)).

- **dates unknown:** *Other occasional poems*. Ancient testimony and several isolated words or lines establish a small poetic practice beyond the named works, but the fragments cannot reliably be divided into individual poems; this is therefore a damaged body of work rather than one identifiable obra ([Klotz’s *Carmina et prolusiones* via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/6/0)).

### Collected sayings

- **youth; exact date unknown:** *Dicta collectanea*. A lost collection of sayings or apophthegms, known only from Suetonius; whether Caesar coined, recorded, or merely assembled its contents is unknown, so it resists classification as a wholly authored literary work ([Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 56](https://www.ascsa.edu.gr/uploads/media/5_Lo_M_T_1934.pdf)).

### Orations

- **77 BCE:** *Orationes in Cn. Cornelium Dolabellam*. Speeches prosecuting the former governor Dolabella for extortion; the prosecution failed and only a fragment survives ([Malcovati’s *Oratorum Romanorum Fragmenta* via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/3/0)).

- **76 BCE:** *Speech against Gaius Antonius Hybrida*. A prosecution speech concerning alleged misconduct in Greece; its wording is lost, and the title is descriptive rather than transmitted ([Swain and Davies](https://ebooks.papacambridge.com/directories/OCR/OCR-ebooks/upload/aspects%20of%20roman%20history%2082bc-ad14%20a%20source-based%20approach%20%28aspects%20of%20classical%20civilisation%29%20by%20hilary%20swain%20mark%20everson%20davies%20%28z-lib-org%29.pdf)).

- **probably 70 BCE:** *Suasio legis Plautiae*. A speech supporting a Plautian law, preserved in a single fragment; the precise legislative occasion and therefore the date are not wholly secure ([Malcovati via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/3/0)).

- **69 BCE:** *Laudatio Iuliae amitae*. A public funeral oration for Caesar’s aunt Julia; the surviving fragment turns genealogy into a political claim linking his family with Roman kings and Venus ([Malcovati via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/3/0)).

- **date uncertain, probably the 70s BCE:** *Pro Bithynis*. A forensic or representative speech on behalf of Bithynians, surviving only in fragments; its exact occasion and original extent are uncertain ([Malcovati via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/3/0)).

- **46 BCE:** *Ad milites in Africa*. A field address threatening recalcitrant soldiers during the African campaign; it was an oral command-performance rather than a “book,” and survives only because Suetonius records part of it ([Malcovati via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/3/0)).

- **date unknown:** *Apud milites de commodis eorum*. A speech to soldiers concerning their rewards or interests, known from one brief fragment; its occasion cannot be identified securely ([Malcovati via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/3/0)).

- **date uncertain:** *Pro Q. Metello* or *ad Metellum*. A speech-like text defending Caesar and Metellus against their critics. Suetonius reports that Augustus doubted Caesar had edited it, believing it an inaccurate stenographic record; it therefore resists being called a work Caesar made, even if it records words he spoke ([Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 55](https://bcs.fltr.ucl.ac.be/suet/caes/55.htm)).

- **throughout his public career:** *Other speeches*. Ancient witnesses regarded Caesar as a major orator, but most performances have neither surviving wording nor recoverable titles; “Caesar’s orations” is consequently a lost practice and body of manifestations, not a finite shelf of authored books ([Cambridge Companion contents](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/23413/frontmatter/9781107023413_frontmatter.pdf); [Suetonius, *Divus Iulius* 55](https://bcs.fltr.ucl.ac.be/suet/caes/55.htm)).

### Correspondence and commands

- **principally 54–44 BCE:** *Epistulae ad Ciceronem*. A dispersed correspondence rather than a designed book: several letters to Cicero survive complete inside Cicero’s collections, while grammarians preserve additional fragments. Their dates and addressees are sometimes recoverable, but Klotz’s collective title is editorial ([Klotz via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/7/0)).

- **principally 49–44 BCE:** *Epistulae ad familiares*. Letters to associates including Oppius and Balbus, preserved independently or quoted in Cicero’s correspondence; these are practical political communications whose later collection should not be mistaken for a book assembled by Caesar ([Klotz via PHI](https://latin.packhum.org/loc/448/8/0)).

- **dates unknown:** *Other lost letters*. Ancient sources attest a much larger correspondence and Caesar’s practice of dictating several letters simultaneously, but most individual objects have vanished and cannot honestly be titled or counted ([Cambridge Companion](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781108215541_A31873439/preview-9781108215541_A31873439.pdf)).
