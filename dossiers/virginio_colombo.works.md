## Works by Virginio Colombo

### Architectural decoration and collaborative works

- **1906:** *Decorative work for the Palacio de Justicia de la Nación*. Architectural ornament executed while Norberto Maillart’s courthouse was nearing completion, in collaboration with Mario Baroffio Covati and Aquiles De Lazzari; Colombo was responsible for decoration, not for the building’s original architectural project.
- **1910:** *Pabellón del Servicio Postal, Exposición Ferroviaria y de Transportes Terrestres del Centenario*. Temporary exhibition architecture designed while Colombo was technical director in the office of Pedro Vinent, Ernesto Maupas and Emilio Jáuregui; the collaborative attribution matters, although contemporary accounts identify Colombo as its designer. The surviving, substantially stripped structure received a gold medal at the exhibition.
- **1910:** *Pabellón de Festejos y Actos Públicos, Exposición del Centenario*. Temporary festival and ceremonial pavilion attributed jointly to Colombo and the Vinent–Maupas–Jáuregui office; it was subsequently demolished.
- **1910:** *Unbuilt pavilion for public festivities, Exposición Ferroviaria y de Transportes Terrestres*. An architectural project rather than a building: period publication in *El Arquitecto* documents the proposal, but it was not erected.

### Houses, villas, and private residences

- **1909:** *Villa Raquel*. Colombo’s first known private house, made with sculptural work by his father-in-law Cesare Giovanola. Its location has not been securely identified and its survival is unknown, although the building is generally presumed demolished.
- *Casa y estudio de Virginio Colombo, Moreno 2091–2095*. His own combined residence and architectural studio; demolished. Its date is not securely established in the consulted catalogues.
- *Residencia de Charcas 2025*. An urban private residence; no reliable construction date is supplied by the catalogue.
- *Residencias de Yerbal 722–724*. A pair of private houses, now substantially altered; the façade carries Colombo’s signature, but the date and original patron remain undocumented.
- **1918:** *Villa Garbesi, Avenida Rivadavia 4787*. A suburban mansion for Cayetano Garbesi, now demolished.
- **1919:** *Villa Carú*. A suburban villa at Avenida Rivadavia and Añasco, made with sculptural contributions by J. Bianchi Peletti; demolished. It functioned as an integrated architectural and decorative ensemble and is therefore closer to a small Gesamtkunstwerk than to a façade design alone.
- **1919:** *Residencia Lagomarsino, Azcuénaga 1083–1087*. An urban mansion for the hat manufacturer Lagomarsino, now the Casa de San Luis; architecture, stained glass, sculpture, painted ornament and simulated-marble finishes were conceived as an integrated interior ensemble.
- *Residencia Benarós*. A private residence treated as an individual work in Nicolás Fratarelli’s monograph; the accessible portion of that source does not establish a secure date or address, so neither is supplied here.
- *Casa Gigena Seeber*. A private house catalogued together with Villa Raquel by Fratarelli; the accessible catalogue material does not provide a dependable date or address.
- *Casa Anda, Avenida Entre Ríos 1079–1083*. A residence and shop for shoe manufacturer Leandro Anda. One catalogue dates it to 1922, while older summaries leave it undated; the 1922 dating is therefore probable rather than silently treated as unanimous.
- *Residencia de Avenida Entre Ríos 1085*. An urban residence adjacent to Casa Anda; the consulted summary catalogue lists it separately but supplies no secure date.
- *Residencia de Avenida Rivadavia 5491*. An urban private residence without a confirmed date in the consulted catalogue.
- *Residencia de Tucumán 1961*. An urban private residence; neither a fixed historical title nor a reliable date is documented in the consulted catalogue.
- **after 1920:** *Residencia de Perú 1050*. An urban private residence known by its address; the catalogue gives only the approximate terminus “after 1920.”
- **after 1920:** *Residencia de Azcuénaga 1075*. An urban residence later occupied by the Casa de Entre Ríos; its precise date remains unconfirmed.
- **after 1920:** *Residencia de Azcuénaga 1129*. An urban residence later adapted as a private school; its precise date remains unconfirmed.
- *Casa Calise III, Quito 4047*. A large family house, now used by educational institutions. Earlier field research presented the attribution as stylistic and provisional, but Colombo’s signature on the building and its inclusion in later studies strengthen the attribution; no reliable construction date was found.
- *Casa Leveratto*. A private house treated as a distinct work in Fratarelli’s scholarly monograph; the accessible source does not yield a secure date or address.

### Rental housing and mixed-use buildings

- **1911:** *Casa Calise, Hipólito Yrigoyen 2562–2578*. A large rental-and-commercial building commissioned by the Calise family, with sculpture by Ercole Pasina and construction by P. Ferloni. Its coordinated architecture, sculpture, ironwork and stained glass make it a Gesamtkunstwerk-like urban building rather than merely a decorated apartment block.
- **1912:** *Casa de los Pavos Reales, Avenida Rivadavia 3216–3236*. A combined family residence, shops and rental dwellings commissioned by shoe manufacturer Luis Rossi. Catalogues sometimes abbreviate its address as Rivadavia 3222; these designations refer to the same extensive building.
- **1912:** *Casa de renta, Avenida Rivadavia 2330*. A rental building identified by its address; the standard summary catalogue supplies no historical house name.
- **1917:** *Casa Viacava, Avenida Rivadavia 3659–3673*. A rental-and-commercial complex commissioned by Francisco Viacava, comprising eleven dwellings and two street shops. Its address is variously shortened to Rivadavia 3659 or 3667.
- **1918:** *Casa Grimoldi, Avenida Corrientes 2548–2572*. A combined residence, shoe shop and rental building for the Grimoldi family, frequently called the Palacio or Edificio Grimoldi. Ornament, spatial planning and commercial imagery—including its lions—form an integrated architectural manifestation.
- **1920:** *Casa de renta, Agüero 1361–1369*. A six-level rental building constructed by C. S. Strona; sources vary between 1361 and 1369 when identifying it.
- **1920:** *Casa y depósito, Hipólito Yrigoyen 3447–3459*. A hybrid residence, rental property and industrial or storage building; its mixed programme resists classification as either simply a house or a factory.
- *Casa de renta, Hipólito Yrigoyen 3441*. A rental building adjoining the house-and-depot at 3447; field documentation identifies both façades as signed by Colombo, but does not securely date this companion building.
- **1923:** *Casa de renta y locales, Hipólito Yrigoyen 2563–2575*. A rental-and-commercial building commissioned by Francisco Calise, facing the much more exuberant Casa Calise of 1911.
- **1925:** *Casa de renta y locales, Avenida Cabildo 1152–1160*. A two-storey complex containing eighteen dwellings and two street shops; Colombo’s and builder C. Alfano’s signatures survive on the façade.
- *Casa de renta, Avenida Cabildo 1156*. Older summaries cite the central street number only; this appears to be the same 1925 complex rather than a separate obra.
- *Casa de renta, Hipólito Yrigoyen 2569*. Older summaries likewise use a single street number for the 1923 building at 2563–2575; it should not be counted as an additional work.
- *Casa de renta, Avenida Entre Ríos 1173*. A rental building treated as a distinct work in Fratarelli’s monograph; no secure date is available in the accessible catalogue.
- *Casa de renta y comercio, Alsina 1266*. A mixed-use building with shops and rental dwellings; the consulted catalogue gives no dependable date.
- *Casa de renta y comercio, Moreno 1390–1400*. A corner hotel or rental building with three shops, constructed by J. P. Boggio; it is signed by Colombo, but its date remains unconfirmed.
- *Casa de renta, Soler 4221–4241*. A signed Colombo building discovered through fieldwork after the earlier bibliography had been assembled. Its authorship is materially attested, but a reliable construction date has not been established.
- *Casa de renta y locales known as Casa Lasalle*. A documented design or lost building known through Colombo’s drawings and historical images; its exact location, date and whether the reproduced scheme was fully realized remain insufficiently established.

### Pharmacies and commercial buildings

- *Farmacia del Capitolio and rental apartments, Avenida Córdoba 2554*. A pharmacy, commercial premises and rental housing combined in one building; no reliable date is supplied by the standard summary catalogue.
- *Farmacia Podestá, Santiago del Estero 901–905*. A pharmacy, shop and rental building carrying Colombo’s signature. Its construction date is unknown, and later conversion to a workshop and hotel has altered its original commercial identity.
- *Farmacia San José*. A pharmacy project or building treated as a separate work in Fratarelli’s monograph; the accessible source does not establish a secure date or address.
- *Casa de renta y comercio, Alsina 1266*. A mixed commercial and residential obra; because its programme crosses catalogue categories, it also appears in discussions of Colombo’s commercial architecture, but it is a single building and is counted only once here.
- *Casa de renta y comercio, Moreno 1390–1400*. A signed corner commercial and residential building constructed by J. P. Boggio; it is likewise a hybrid rather than a freestanding shop.

### Factories, depots, and garages

- **1914:** *Fábrica de Calzados Oscaria, Hipólito Yrigoyen 2451–2459*. A shoe factory and depot for Juan Uboldi, later converted to a garage and partly demolished or modified. Some scholarship identifies it by its patron and address rather than by the Oscaria trade name.
- **1916:** *Fábrica y depósito de Calzados Anda, Humberto I 2048–2060*. An industrial building for Leandro Anda, constructed by A. Daverio; its comparatively restrained façade distinguishes it from Colombo’s highly sculptural residential works.
- **1920:** *Casa y depósito, Hipólito Yrigoyen 3447–3459*. An integrated domestic, rental and productive complex; its industrial hall and residence are parts of one obra, not separate works.

### Institutional architecture

- **1913:** *Sede de la Società Unione Operai Italiani, Sarmiento 1364–1382*. A comprehensive remodelling of an existing Italian mutual-aid society building, rather than an entirely new structure. Colombo’s intervention transformed its façade and principal interiors into an integrated institutional ensemble.
- **1910:** *Pabellones del Centenario*. The Postal Service and Public Festivities pavilions were institutional but temporary exhibition works; they are described individually above and are not additional works under this collective designation.

### Projects not realized or incompletely realized

- **1910:** *Unbuilt project for a festivities pavilion at the Centenary exhibition*. A project in the strict sense: an architectural intention preserved in publication, not a completed building.
- *Project for the competition of the Società di Patronato Italiano e Rimpatrio*. An unbuilt competition proposal, newly incorporated into Colombo scholarship by Fratarelli. Conceived as a Florentine-style institutional palace, it survives as a project rather than an obra in built form; the accessible source does not give a secure competition date.
- *Casa Lasalle*. Drawings and historical documentation establish a Colombo design with characteristic relief ornament, but the evidence available does not securely distinguish between a demolished completed building and an unexecuted project. Its resistance to being called a surviving “work” is therefore part of its status.
