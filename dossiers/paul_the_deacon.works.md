## Works by Paul the Deacon

### Histories and historical projects

- **c. 766–774:** *Historia Romana*. A sixteen-book recasting and continuation of Eutropius, prepared at Adelperga’s request: ten books adapt Eutropius and six extend the narrative through Justinian. It is simultaneously Roman history, Christianized universal chronicle, and an editorial project rather than a wholly new narrative. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3890)
- **c. 784:** *Liber de episcopis Mettensibus* (also *Gesta episcoporum Mettensium*). A commissioned history of the bishops of Metz that also constructs a genealogy and memorial narrative for the Carolingian dynasty; its latest datable notice is Charlemagne’s marriage to Fastrada, and the exact completion date remains debated. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3888), [Walter Goffart, “Paul the Deacon’s Gesta Episcoporum Mettensium”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/traditio/article/abs/paul-the-deacons-gesta-episcoporum-mettensium-and-the-early-design-of-charlemagnes-succession/17FC087335877E86C017EC47A66AF325)
- **c. 787–796:** *Historia Langobardorum*. A six-book history of the Lombards from legendary origins to the death of Liutprand in 744. Its stopping point resists classification: scholars disagree whether Paul deliberately ended before the Frankish conquest or left an unfinished project. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3889)

### Compilations, epitomes, and instructional texts

- **c. 770–782:** *Expositio Artis Donati*. A grammatical teaching text expanding Donatus’s *Ars minor*. Some scholars treat it as Paul’s authored exposition, others as a compilation from several grammarians; its dedication to Charlemagne and precise place of composition are also uncertain. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3887)
- **Probably c. 781; dating disputed:** *Excerpta ex libris Pompei Festi de verborum significatione*. An alphabetically rearranged epitome of Festus’s already-abridged Latin lexicon, made for Charlemagne’s library. It is an editorial and philological construction—an act of selection that also preserves material otherwise lost—not simply a “book written by” Paul. [Geschichtsquellen’ repertory of Paul’s works](https://geschichtsquellen.de/autor/4242?s=en), [Maria Sofia Merino Jaso, chronology and study](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/20278/Thesis-Maria-Sofia-Merino-Jaso-complete-version.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=2)
- **After 786, exact date uncertain:** *Homiliarium* (often called the *Homiliary of Paul the Deacon*). A large, ordered anthology of patristic readings compiled at Charlemagne’s command for the night office. Calling it an authored work is inadequate: Paul selected, excerpted, adjusted, and sequenced inherited texts into a new liturgical instrument, and the surviving manuscript tradition does not yield one simple, immutable text. [The Medieval Review of Jesse D. Billett Guiliano, *The Homiliary of Paul the Deacon*](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/34014), [Cambridge manuscript description](https://medieval.lib.cam.ac.uk/catalogue/Peterhouse/MS-PETERHOUSE-00130)

### Hagiography

- **Probably 770s–780s:** *Vita sancti Gregorii Magni*. A concise life of Pope Gregory the Great assembled from earlier testimony; it is hagiography and memorial compilation rather than critical biography. The work is securely present in Paul’s standard repertory, although its exact date is not fixed. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3891)

### Letters and documentary acts

- **c. 766–774:** *Letter to Adelperga*. The dedicatory letter prefacing the *Historia Romana*; it explains and performs the commission and therefore belongs both to the history and to Paul’s correspondence. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3885)
- **782–783:** *Letter to Abbot Theodemar*. A prose letter promising Paul’s return from Francia to Monte Cassino, ending with the verse pieces *Iam fluebat decima* and *Vale, salus patriae*. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3885), [Ross, verse-corpus study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably c. 781:** *Letter to Charlemagne dedicating the epitome of Festus*. A prefatory act that presents the lexical epitome to the king; it survives as part of that made object as well as among Paul’s letters. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3885)
- **c. 782–786:** *Letter to Charlemagne accompanying the homiliary*. A dedicatory letter framing Paul’s anthology, with the introductory and concluding poems *Ampla mihi vestro* and *Utere felix munere Christi*. [Ross, pp. 262, 279](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain, late eighth century:** *Letter to Abbot Adalhard of Corbie*. A letter accompanying a collection of Gregory the Great’s correspondence, closing with *Ante suos refluus*. Its superscription identifies the sender only as “Paulus supplex,” but the standard repertory and modern verse study accept Paul the Deacon’s authorship. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3885), [Ross, pp. 157, 262](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Between 787 and 797:** *Letter to Charlemagne in Abbot Theodemar’s name*. A commissioned letter accompanying an authoritative copy of the Rule of Saint Benedict; although formally Theodemar’s letter, scholarship identifies Paul as its actual composer. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3885), [Ross, p. 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain, late eighth century:** *Letter from “Paulus” to an unnamed priest*. One of the six items transmitted or catalogued with Paul’s correspondence, but the sender’s identity is not secure; it therefore resists admission as an unquestioned work by Paul the Deacon. [Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3885)

### Poems, inscriptions, riddles, and memorial objects securely attributed in the major editions

- **Date uncertain, probably Beneventan period:** *Aemula Romuleis consurgunt*. A verse *titulus* made for Arichis II’s architectural complex at Salerno. It is an inscription intended to inhabit a building, not merely a poem on a page. [Ross, pp. 118–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **After 786:** *Ampla mihi vestro*. The opening verse dedication of the homiliary to Charlemagne; it is a threshold within the compilation rather than an entirely independent book. [Ross, pp. 262, 279](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **After 786:** *Utere felix munere Christi*. The corresponding concluding dedication of the homiliary, addressing Charlemagne and closing the anthology as a presented object. [Ross, pp. 262, 279](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably after 774:** *Angustae vitae fugiunt*. An elegiac lament about the loss of secular freedom and confinement to monastic life; its date depends upon uncertain reconstructions of Paul’s movements. [Ross, pp. 119, 130](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Ante suos refluus*. An elegiac couplet concluding Paul’s letter to Adalhard. It is simultaneously poem and epistolary closure and should not be separated too sharply from the letter that carries it. [Ross, pp. 157, 262](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **763:** *A principio saeculorum*. A rhythmic acrostic addressed to Adelperga whose initials spell her name and epithet; its own chronological statement supplies the unusually secure date. [Ross, pp. 27, 133](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Aurea quae fulvis*. A funerary poem for a member of Charlemagne’s family, incorporated into the *Liber de episcopis Mettensibus*. It is both epitaph and component of a dynastic history. [Ross, pp. 262, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **c. 782–786:** *Candidolum bifido*. A short poem belonging to the riddling and learned exchanges of the Carolingian court circle; the “title” is its incipit, not a securely transmitted authorial title. [Ross, pp. 118–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably 760s–770s:** *Christe salus utriusque*. A verse inscription for the apse of Arichis II’s palace church of Saints Peter and Paul at Salerno. Its original manifestation was architectural and liturgical, even though it now survives as copied text. [Ross, pp. 119–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **c. 782–786:** *Cynthius occiduas*. A learned court poem participating in an exchange rather than a self-contained “book”; its incipit functions as the modern identifying title. [Ross, pp. 118–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably after 774:** *Fratres alacri pectore*. A poem or hymn in praise of Saint Benedict, also embedded in the *Historia Langobardorum*. Its status crosses devotional song, monastic exhortation, and historiographical quotation. [Ross, pp. 119, 262, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably 760s–770s:** *Haec domus est domini*. A verse *titulus* for the entrance to Arichis II’s basilica at Salerno. It was made to mark and interpret a physical threshold, so treating it solely as literary verse loses part of the obra. [Ross, pp. 119–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Hic ego quae iaceo*. A funerary poem for a female member of Charlemagne’s family, preserved within the Metz episcopal history. [Ross, pp. 262, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **783 or shortly afterward:** *Hildegard rapuit*. An epitaph associated with Charlemagne’s family and included in the *Liber de episcopis Mettensibus*; the identification of its Hildegard must be handled alongside the several deaths in the royal family in 783. [Ross, pp. 262, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Hoc tumulata iacet*. A funerary poem for a member of the Carolingian family, incorporated into the Metz history and thus serving both grave commemoration and dynasty-making. [Ross, pp. 262, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **782–783:** *Iam fluebat decima*. Six closing verses within Paul’s letter to Theodemar, measuring the length of his absence and reinforcing his promise to return. It resists treatment as wholly independent from its prose vehicle. [Ross, pp. 137, 262](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **c. 782–786:** *Iam puto nervosis*. A short learned poem from the Carolingian court’s culture of riddles and verbal exchange; its independent identity is editorially reconstructed from manuscript transmission. [Ross, pp. 118–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Before c. 787–796; exact composition date unknown:** *Ingenio clarus sensu*. An epitaph for the poet Venantius Fortunatus, commissioned by Abbot Aper of Poitiers and later inserted by Paul into the *Historia Langobardorum*. [Ross, pp. 262, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Lactea splendifico*. A short Latin poem accepted by both Dümmler and Neff; no secure date or original material setting survives, so the incipit is safer than an invented descriptive title. [Ross, master list and secure corpus, pp. 121, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **787 or shortly afterward:** *Lugentum lacrimis*. An epitaph or lament for Arichis II of Benevento. It is a memorial act for Paul’s former patron, not merely an abstract lyric. [Ross, pp. 45, 118–121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain, probably before Paul’s Frankish sojourn:** *Ordiar unde tuas laudes*. A praise poem on Lake Como. Dümmler and Neff accept it, while Dahn regarded it as the work of an unidentified imitator; the modern secure corpus rejects Dahn’s objection but the dispute remains part of the work’s history. [Ross, pp. 27, 119](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably after 774:** *Ordiar unde tuos, sacer*. An extended poem in praise of Saint Benedict, embedded in the *Historia Langobardorum*. It combines hymn, monastic petition, and autobiographical lament. [Ross, pp. 119, 262, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Perpetualis amor*. A Carolingian-family epitaph included in the *Liber de episcopis Mettensibus*, where private mourning becomes part of public dynastic memory. [Ross, pp. 262, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain, late eighth century:** *Post has nectit subsequentes*. A rhythmic acrostic whose initials assert “PAULUS FECI.” Its self-identification makes it unusually explicit as a made thing, although its original occasion is obscure. [Ross, pp. 261, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Roscida de lacrimis*. An epitaph for a Sophia whose identity is uncertain—possibly Paul’s niece or a granddaughter of Queen Ansa. The poem is secure in the principal editions, but its subject’s relationship to Paul remains disputed. [Ross, pp. 119, 266](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **c. 782–786:** *Sensi cuius verba*. Paul’s poetic reply to Peter of Pisa in a courtly learned exchange. Some editions append the translated fable *Trax puer adstricto* to it, although Paul appears to distance himself from authorship of that appended piece. [Ross, pp. 262, 280–281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Sic ego suscepi*. A short Latin poem accepted in both Dümmler’s and Neff’s editions; its incipit is the reliable identifier, while its exact date and original occasion are not fixed. [Ross, secure corpus and master list, pp. 121, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **782:** *Verba tui famuli*. A verse petition to Charlemagne for the release of Paul’s imprisoned brother Arichis and relief of his dispossessed family. It is not merely descriptive poetry but an intervention intended to cause royal action—close to a supplicatory manifesto. [Ross, pp. 31–32, 121](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)

### Verse with substantial but unresolved attribution

- **Date uncertain:** *Adam per lignum*, *Crux tua Christe potens*, *Crux tua lux lucis*, and *Crux tua rex regum*. Four related cross-poems accepted by Neff but not by Dümmler; they should be retained as disputed manifestations around Paul’s corpus, not silently counted as secure works. [Ross, pp. 123, 279](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Multicolor quali*. A verse inscription accepted by Neff but not Dümmler; manuscript association supplies only limited evidence for Paul’s authorship. [Ross, pp. 123, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *O una ante omnes*. A short poem accepted by Neff but excluded from Dümmler’s authentic corpus; attribution remains unresolved. [Ross, pp. 123, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably c. 782–786:** *Pulchrior me nullus*. A six-line acrostic riddle spelling “PAULUS.” Dümmler did not accept it, but Neff and the recent corpus study regard Paul the Deacon as the only credible candidate. [Ross, pp. 261, 267](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Adsunt quattuor in prima*. A poem accepted by Dümmler but not Neff; the evidence is principally manuscript association and does not secure authorship. [Ross, pp. 123, 279](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Aegrum fama fuit*. A fable-like poem accepted by Dümmler but placed outside Paul’s work by Neff; attribution remains weak. [Ross, pp. 123, 279](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Quaerebat maerens*. A narrative poem accepted by Dümmler but not Neff, with insufficient evidence to decide authorship. [Ross, pp. 123, 280](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Temporibus priscis*. A poem admitted by Dümmler and rejected by Neff; its association with Paul’s manuscripts is not proof that Paul made it. [Ross, pp. 123, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Probably late eighth century:** *Multa legit paucis*. A couplet presented in later transmission as part of Paul’s dedication of the Festus epitome to Charlemagne. Dümmler eventually abandoned the attribution, and its absence from early witnesses makes Paul’s authorship very unlikely. [Ross, pp. 122–123, 260](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **After 783:** *Qui sacra vivaci*. A verse history of the bishops of Metz. Dümmler accepted it and its subject closely matches Paul’s prose *Liber de episcopis Mettensibus*, but Neff rejected it; the absence of Paul’s characteristic address to Bishop Angilram weighs against attribution. [Ross, pp. 122–123](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Late eighth century:** *Vale, salus patriae*. An elegiac couplet within the letter written by Paul in Abbot Theodemar’s name to accompany the Rule of Saint Benedict. Its placement makes Paul’s authorship highly probable, although it was omitted from the older Dümmler and Neff poetic editions. [Ross, pp. 267, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Perge, libelle meus*. A defective farewell poem naming Benevento. Neff assigned it to Paulinus of Aquileia, but its meter and plausible address to Arichis and Adelperga make Paul the Deacon a credible alternative; the recent study stops short of certainty. [Ross, pp. 267–268](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Ut queant laxis*. A hymn for Saint John the Baptist, famous in later musical history. Its traditional attribution to Paul rests heavily on late and unreliable testimony; modern scholarship has alternately rejected and cautiously defended it, so it cannot be listed without qualification as something he made. [Ross, pp. 267–268](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Olim Romuleia sanctus*. A verse concerning Roman and Ambrosian chant once attributed to Paul on stylistic grounds; several later scholars contest that attribution. [Ross, p. 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Date uncertain:** *Rustice lustrivage*. A poem to Pan transmitted near works of Peter of Pisa and Paul. A tentative modern attribution rests on vocabulary, but proximity in a manuscript does not establish that Paul made it. [Ross, p. 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **768 or later:** *Martir Mercuri* and *Salve, miles egregie*. Hymns associated with the translation of Saint Mercurius’s relics to Arichis II’s foundation at Benevento. The historical association is suggestive, but there is no evidence of a commission and their style is uncharacteristic of Paul; they resist inclusion as his works. [Ross, pp. 260–261, 280–281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)

### Texts preserved by Paul but not made by him

- **Incorporated c. 787–796; original date sixth century:** *Clauditur hoc tumulo*. An epitaph for Droctulft quoted in the *Historia Langobardorum*. Its authorship is unknown; inclusion in Paul’s history is an act of preservation, not evidence that Paul composed it. [Ross, pp. 262, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Incorporated c. 787–796; original date c. 689:** *Culmen, opes, sobolem*. An epitaph on the converted West Saxon king Cædwalla quoted in the *Historia Langobardorum* and attributed to Archbishop Benedict of Milan. It belongs to Paul’s historical compilation but not to his authorial corpus. [Ross, pp. 262, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
- **Incorporated c. 782–786; original authorship uncertain:** *Trax puer adstricto*. A Latin version of a Greek poem appended in some editions to Paul’s response *Sensi cuius verba*. Paul’s wording appears to deny that he made the translation, so its presence in the exchange should not be converted into attribution. [Ross, pp. 262, 281](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/27469/1/Ross_108044487_CorrectedThesisClean.pdf.pdf)
