## Works by Peter of Ravenna

### Orations, sermons, and public interventions

- **1472:** *Oratio pro patria ad illustrissimum principem Nicolaum Tronum Venetum ducem*. A civic-diplomatic obra comprising four short orations addressed to Doge Nicolò Tron; its printing is variously dated 14 February or after 25 February 1472. ([Glasgow Incunabula Project](https://polyomics.gla.ac.uk/myglasgow/incunabula/a-zofauthorsa-j/p32/); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1500:** *Sermo quem habiturus erat … domini Martini episcopi Caminensis*. A protocol-like reform sermon prepared for a diocesan synod at Stettin but apparently never delivered; it survives as the opening component of *Aurea nonnulla opuscula*, so the projected speech and the printed text are not quite the same manifestation. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3991))
- **1503:** *Lectio pulcherrima de potestate summi pontificis et Romani imperatoris*. A ceremonial legal-political lecture delivered at Wittenberg on 3 May 1503, arguing about papal and imperial powers, including the emperor’s ability to found universities; later editions call it a *libellus*. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html); [Wittenberg University Library Catalogue](https://dokumen.pub/wittenberg-university-library-catalogue-of-1536-0866981829-9780866981828.html))
- **1503–1505:** *Sermones extraordinarii et pulcherrimi*. A cycle of twenty-four festive-day university addresses on religious and moral subjects, first printed at Wittenberg around 1505 and expanded or reissued in Cologne in 1506–07. These originated as performances before Frederick and John of Saxony, making the spoken series and printed collection distinct manifestations. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **16 April 1508:** *Farewell sermon on death*. A sermon delivered in Cologne’s Franciscan church before Peter’s departure; no secure formal title or surviving independent text is identified in the standard bibliography, so it resists classification as an extant work rather than a documented performance. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Extempore discourse on Hebrews and the power of a papal legate a latere*. A documented oral creation delivered at Mainz shortly after Peter’s arrival, apparently without a fixed title or surviving text; it should not be converted into a book merely because a biographical source records it. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))

### Memory art

- **1491:** *Phoenix, sive artificiosa memoria*. A protocol for constructing and using artificial memory, first printed at Venice; the colophon says 10 January 1491, while incunable cataloguing sometimes normalizes the date as 1491/92. Its accompanying Venetian privilege belongs to the book’s publication history, not automatically to Peter’s authorship. ([Croatian Incunabula Bibliography](https://bibliografija.nsk.hr/spec/Inkunabule852/glavni_niz.html); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Additiones quaedam ad artificiosam memoriam*. A short supplement and partial retractatio of the *Phoenix*, adding mnemonic rules while withdrawing one of its earlier recommendations; scholarship treats it as a separately identifiable text although it circulated with other works. ([Luis Merino Jerez, “The Fortune of Peter of Ravenna’s ‘Artificiosa memoria sive Phoenix’”](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CFCL/article/view/51101))

### Civil and canon law

- **before 1478:** *Singularia iuris*. A collection of individual legal propositions or notable determinations, known from a Venetian printing of 1478; the date of composition is not securely fixed by the standard bio-bibliographical guide. ([Ames Foundation, *Bio-Bibliographical Guide*](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/BioBibCanonists/Report_Biobib2.php?record_id=r481))
- **1477–1480:** *Statutes of the University of Pisa, first volume*. A collaborative institutional project in whose drafting Peter participated while teaching at Pisa. Because the statutes were corporate legislation rather than a solely authored book, his individual contribution cannot safely be isolated as an independent obra. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1498–1499:** *Repetitio in cap. Inter alia, de immunitate ecclesiae* (X 3.49.6). A canon-law repetition answering whether fugitives could be seized in sacred places, composed at the request of Greifswald citizens and first printed at Lübeck in 1499. It is also cited as *De immunitate ecclesiae*, but those labels should not be multiplied into two works. ([Ames Foundation](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/BioBibCanonists/Report_Biobib2.php?record_id=r481); [Biblissima](https://portail.biblissima.fr/en/ark%3A/43093/edata4de16f0fabd76b12efb5a3ad68ba0c4331ae53fa))
- **1500–1502:** *Libellus florum: Argumenta et responsa iuris*. A protocol-like collection of Roman-law rules (*argumenta*), explanations (*responsiones*), and guidance for an advocate’s conduct in court. It survives inside *Aurea nonnulla opuscula*, and its status as an autonomous work rather than a section of that compilation is therefore unstable. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3991))
- **1502:** *Aurea nonnulla opuscula*. A deliberately composite obra joining an undelivered synodal sermon, the legal *Libellus florum*, and Latin poems; it crosses sermon, protocol, legal handbook, and poetry collection and is Peter’s closest approach to a small textual Gesamtkunstwerk. The first securely traceable edition is Leipzig 1502; a reportedly different 1500 edition is not now demonstrably extant. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3991))
- **1503:** *Compendium iuris civilis*. A teaching compendium of Roman civil law, printed at “Albiburgum” and used as the basis of Peter’s lectures. He intended further work on it, but the plague-driven end of his Wittenberg teaching left that larger project incomplete. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html); [Merino Jerez bibliography](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CFCL/article/view/51101))
- **1503:** *Clypeus contra doctorem Caium impugnantem suum consilium*. A polemical “shield” defending one of Peter’s legal opinions against a Doctor Caius, printed at Wittenberg around 20 June 1503. The underlying *consilium* is not securely separable or datable as an independently transmitted work. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html); [Wittenberg University Library Catalogue](https://dokumen.pub/wittenberg-university-library-catalogue-of-1536-0866981829-9780866981828.html))
- **1504–1506:** *Compendium iuris canonici*. A multi-part canon-law teaching compendium: part one appeared in 1504, part two in 1506, and a third part followed; the Cologne edition reorganized the whole alphabetically. Bibliographies sometimes conflate it with the civil-law compendium under the convenient title *Compendium iuris civilis et canonici*, but the contemporary publication history distinguishes them. ([Ames Foundation](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/BioBibCanonists/Report_Biobib2.php?record_id=r481); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Alphabetum aureum*. An alphabetically arranged legal memory-book designed to let students reproduce arguments and determinations ex tempore. It is simultaneously a legal reference work and a mnemonic protocol, although its first bibliography described it as less systematic than a true encyclopedia. ([Ames Foundation](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/BioBibCanonists/Report_Biobib2.php?record_id=r481); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Dicta quaedam notabilia quasi extravagantia sine ordine alphabeti*. A short supplement of notable legal propositions placed after the *Alphabetum aureum*, including material related to the controversy over executed criminals’ bodies. Its dependence on the host volume makes its autonomy as a work uncertain. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Allegationes et conclusiones in materia consuetudinum*. A set of legal allegations and conclusions on custom, appended to the *Alphabetum aureum* but also circulated separately as *Enarrationes in titulum de consuetudine* (X 1.4); these are manifestations of one text, not two secure works. ([Ames Foundation](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/BioBibCanonists/Report_Biobib2.php?record_id=r481); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Consilium on whether the body of a repentant executed criminal should remain on the gallows*. A legal opinion asserting a right to Christian burial and provoking Peter’s Cologne controversy with Jacob van Hoogstraten and Gerard of Zutphen. Its exact independent title and textual boundary are uncertain: it appears within the canon-law material and survives partly through the attacks and Peter’s defenses, so it resists being treated as an ordinary standalone book. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1508:** *Compendium breve in materia consuetudinum feudorum*. Peter’s final declared legal compendium, on feudal customs, begun at Cologne and completed during his Mainz period. The prefatory dedication is dated 13 April 1508 and was written by his pupil William Harisius, so that paratext is not Peter’s own creation. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html); [Merino Jerez bibliography](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CFCL/article/view/51101))
- **date unknown; first printed 1574:** *Constitutio de statutis*. A legal tract on statutes published only decades after Peter’s death, first at Königsberg in 1574 and again at Venice in 1584. Its late manifestation makes the date and state of Peter’s original text uncertain. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html); [Encyclopedia of Jurisprudence](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783112379189_A42358111/preview-9783112379189_A42358111.pdf))

### Polemical manifestos

- **1508:** *Valete cum perpetuo silentio ad … magistrum Jacobum de Alta Platea*. A personal and juridical polemic against Jacob van Hoogstraten; its dismissive title makes it function as a manifesto intended to terminate controversy rather than merely describe it. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html); [Merino Jerez bibliography](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CFCL/article/view/51101))
- **1508:** *Prima pars egregii et salutiferi operis … contra Gherardum de Zutphania et fratrem Iacobum*. A Mainz polemic against Gerard of Zutphen and Jacob van Hoogstraten concerning the bodies of executed criminals. The words *Prima pars* announce a larger project, but no completed continuation is securely known; its incompleteness is therefore part of the obra. ([Merino Jerez bibliography](https://revistas.ucm.es/index.php/CFCL/article/view/51101); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))

### Poetry and smaller creations

- **before 1502:** *Carmina*. At least ten Latin poems—to the Virgin, saints, students, jurists, rulers, councillors, civic authorities, and women encountered during Peter’s travels—were gathered in *Aurea nonnulla opuscula*, while other verses accompanied separate publications. The standard bibliography does not supply stable individual titles and dates for all of them, so inventing a work-list from their addressees would be misleading. ([Geschichtsquellen](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/3991); [ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
- **1498 and later:** *Legal consilia and responsa*. Peter produced opinions for Duke Bogislaw X, urban petitioners, and other rulers on sanctuary, witness summonses, princely taxation, lapsed fiefs, and related questions. The sources establish this body of practical work but do not preserve a complete, securely titled catalogue; it is therefore a professional corpus rather than a list of recoverable individual texts. ([ADB](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/pnd100958141.html))
