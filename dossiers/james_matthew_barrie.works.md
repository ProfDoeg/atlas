## Works by Sir James Matthew Barrie

### Novels and extended prose fictions

- **1887:** *Better Dead*. A privately printed satirical novel—sometimes dated 1888 from its public issue—about political assassination; the discrepancy is bibliographical, not evidence of two separate works.
- **1888:** *When a Man’s Single*. A novel of journalism and literary ambition in London.
- **1889:** *A Window in Thrums*. A linked sequence of fictional sketches about a Scottish weaving community; it occupies the border between novel and story collection.
- **1891:** *The Little Minister*. A novel set in the fictional Scottish town of Thrums, distinct from Barrie’s later stage adaptation.
- **1896:** *Sentimental Tommy: The Story of His Boyhood*. A novel and the first part of the Tommy Sandys sequence.
- **1900:** *Tommy and Grizel*. A novel continuing and concluding *Sentimental Tommy*.
- **1902:** *The Little White Bird; or, Adventures in Kensington Gardens*. An adult novel whose embedded Kensington Gardens chapters introduced Peter Pan; it is therefore both an independent fiction and the first manifestation of the Peter Pan project.
- **1911:** *Peter and Wendy*. Barrie’s novelization and substantial re-creation of the stage *Peter Pan*, later commonly published under the shorter title *Peter Pan*.
- **1930:** *The Greenwood Hat: Being a Memoir of James Anon, 1885–1887*. An autobiographical novel or masked memoir: “James Anon” is Barrie, but the book deliberately resists a clean separation between fiction and autobiography.
- **1931–1932:** *Farewell Miss Julie Logan*. A supernatural novella first serialized in 1931 and issued as a book in 1932.

### Story, sketch, and essay books

- **1888:** *Auld Licht Idylls*. A collection of fictionalized Scottish sketches, developed from Barrie’s newspaper contributions rather than conceived as a conventional novel.
- **1890:** *My Lady Nicotine*. A sequence of linked tobacco-club sketches; the later subtitle *A Study in Smoke* belongs to the 1926 reissue.
- **1892:** *A Holiday in Bed and Other Sketches*. A short collection of humorous prose pieces.
- **1893:** *A Powerful Drug and Other Stories*. A story collection; some of its contents also circulated separately, so the collection should not be mistaken for a single newly composed narrative.
- **1893:** *A Tillyloss Scandal*. A separately issued Scottish tale.
- **1893:** *Two of Them*. A small book containing two stories rather than a novel.
- **1893–1894:** *A Lady’s Shoe*. A small collection containing “A Lady’s Shoe” and “The Inconsiderate Waiter”; bibliographies differ between the publication date printed for particular issues and the year in which copies circulated.
- **1894:** *Life in a Country Manse*. A separately issued autobiographical-family sketch.
- **1898:** *Jess*. A short prose work whose slight, separately printed form makes it closer to an illustrated booklet than to a full-length book.
- **1925:** *The Author*. A collection of addresses and occasional prose, not a single continuous work.
- **1926:** *Cricket*. An essay-book or sporting meditation, rather than a technical history or protocol of the game.
- **1938:** *M’Connachie and J. M. B.*. A posthumously published collection of addresses and personal prose. Its title essay presents authorship as a divided identity and thus resists being treated simply as autobiography.

### Poems and memorial texts

- **1895:** *Scotland’s Lament: A Poem on the Death of Robert Louis Stevenson*. A memorial poem issued as a small publication; it is not a book-length narrative.
- **1915:** *Charles Frohman: A Tribute*. A memorial text for the theatre manager who died in the sinking of the *Lusitania*.
- **1915:** *The Dramatic Profession and the War*. An occasional wartime address, acting publicly as advocacy as much as literary description.
- **1918:** *The New World*. A brief wartime or postwar occasional text; bibliographical descriptions vary, and it should not be confused with the play *The New Word*.

### Full-length and substantial stage works

- **1891:** *Richard Savage*. A play co-written with H. B. Marriott Watson; joint authorship must be retained rather than absorbed into Barrie’s solo oeuvre.
- **1891:** *Ibsen’s Ghost; or, Toole Up-to-Date*. A topical theatrical burlesque written for J. L. Toole.
- **1891:** *Becky Sharp*. A stage adaptation associated with Barrie and based on Thackeray’s *Vanity Fair*; it survives at the edge of his accepted dramatic canon and is not included in his principal 1928 collected plays.
- **1892:** *Walker, London*. A comedy written for Toole and first performed in 1892; later publication dates sometimes replace its performance date in bibliographies.
- **1892:** *The Professor’s Love Story*. A romantic comedy first staged in 1892 and published subsequently.
- **1893:** *Jane Annie; or, The Good Conduct Prize*. A comic-opera libretto with music by Ernest Ford. Barrie began it and Arthur Conan Doyle completed or revised it, so it is a collaborative music-theatre creation rather than solely a Barrie play.
- **1897:** *The Little Minister*. Barrie’s dramatic adaptation of his 1891 novel; the stage work is a distinct manifestation, not merely an edition of the book.
- **1900:** *The Wedding Guest*. A full-length play that was produced but did not enter Barrie’s later one-volume collected plays.
- **1901:** *Quality Street*. A comedy in four acts, later gathered into Barrie’s authoritative 1928 dramatic collection.
- **1902:** *The Admirable Crichton*. A social comedy in four acts.
- **1903:** *Little Mary*. A comedy whose apparently literal medical premise turns into an extended theatrical joke.
- **1904–1928:** *Peter Pan; or, The Boy Who Would Not Grow Up*. A “fairy play” first staged in 1904 and repeatedly revised before Barrie fixed a published dramatic text in 1928; it is therefore a long theatrical project rather than one stable 1904 text.
- **1905:** *Alice Sit-by-the-Fire*. A comedy about parents, children, and theatrical imagination.
- **1908:** *What Every Woman Knows*. A four-act comedy.
- **1914:** *The Legend of Leonora*. A comedy derived from *The Adored One*; sources sometimes treat the earlier and revised titles as separate works and sometimes as manifestations of the same dramatic project.
- **1916:** *A Kiss for Cinderella*. A full-length wartime fantasy-comedy.
- **1917:** *Dear Brutus*. A full-length fantasy play.
- **1920:** *Mary Rose*. A supernatural stage play.
- **1936:** *The Boy David*. Barrie’s last produced play, a dramatic reworking of the biblical Saul-and-David story.

### One-act plays, curtain-raisers, sketches, and dramatic fragments

- **1905:** *Pantaloon*. A one-act play drawing on commedia dell’arte figures and the pathos of theatrical aging.
- **1906:** *Josephine*. A short dramatic piece not included in Barrie’s principal 1928 collected volume; surviving lists provide less secure information about its textual form than about its production.
- **1906:** *Punch*. A theatrical sketch or short play; it resists classification as an independently published literary work.
- **1908:** *When Wendy Grew Up: An Afterthought*. A short sequel or epilogue to *Peter Pan*, performed in 1908 but not published until 1957; its status alternates between autonomous playlet and supplementary scene.
- **1910:** *Old Friends*. A one-act play later collected with Barrie’s short dramas.
- **1910–1911:** *The Twelve-Pound Look*. A one-act play; sources divide between its initial performance context in 1910 and its independent 1911 production.
- **1910:** *A Slice of Life*. A collaborative theatrical piece by Barrie and Arthur Conan Doyle; because individual contributions are difficult to separate, it belongs to neither author as an uncontested solo work.
- **1912:** *Rosalind*. A one-act comedy about an actress and the identities created around her.
- **1913:** *The Will*. A one-act play structured around successive visits to a solicitor.
- **1913:** *The Adored One*. A short play subsequently reworked as *The Legend of Leonora*; whether it is an independent obra or an earlier manifestation remains a cataloguing choice.
- **1913:** *Half an Hour*. A one-act tragedy, distinct from the 1914 collection *Half Hours*.
- **1913:** *The Dramatists Get What They Want*. A theatrical sketch whose title is sometimes reported inaccurately as *The Dramatics Get What They Want*.
- **1914:** *Der Tag; or, The Tragic Man*. A wartime one-act allegory.
- **1915:** *The New Word*. A one-act war play about a father and son.
- **1915:** *Rosy Rapture: The Pride of the Beauty Chorus*. A short theatrical fantasy written for Gaby Deslys.
- **1916:** *Shakespeare’s Legacy*. A short Shakespeare-tercentenary play or pageant-piece.
- **1917:** *A Strange Play*. A brief experimental dramatic piece; its generic title and limited circulation make it unusually resistant to identification as a standard commercial play.
- **1917:** *The Old Lady Shows Her Medals*. A one-act war play first known as *Charwomen and the War*; the two titles identify stages of one project, not securely separate obras.
- **1917:** *Seven Women*. A one-act play later included in *Shall We Join the Ladies? and Other One-Act Plays*.
- **1918:** *La Politesse*. A short wartime theatrical piece.
- **1918:** *A Well-Remembered Voice*. A one-act supernatural war play.
- **1920:** *Dear Dangers*. A revue-like theatrical entertainment or assemblage associated with Barrie; its mixed authorship and performance-specific construction make “a Barrie play” an uneasy description.
- **1921:** *Shall We Join the Ladies?*. The first act of an intended murder-mystery play. Barrie left the larger project unfinished, but the surviving act was performed and later published as a work in its own right.
- **1927:** *Barbara’s Wedding*. A one-act sequel to *The Old Lady Shows Her Medals*, first performed years after the earlier play but incorporated into the same wartime dramatic world.
- **Undated; published 2017:** *The Reconstruction of the Crime*. An unfinished or previously unpublished play co-written with E. V. Lucas; its late publication does not supply a secure composition date.

### Dramatic collections as composite obras

- **1914:** *Half Hours*. A constructed book of four one-act plays—*Pantaloon*, *The Twelve-Pound Look*, *Rosalind*, and *The Will*—and therefore both a publication-object and an arrangement of already distinct works.
- **1918:** *Echoes of the War*. A thematically composed volume containing *The New Word*, *The Old Lady Shows Her Medals*, *A Well-Remembered Voice*, and *Barbara’s Wedding*; the last play’s performance history postdates the wartime setting represented by the collection.
- **1928:** *Shall We Join the Ladies? and Other One-Act Plays*. A volume containing *Shall We Join the Ladies?*, *Half an Hour*, *Seven Women*, and *Old Friends*.
- **1928:** *The Plays of J. M. Barrie in One Volume*. Barrie’s major authorized dramatic collection, containing twenty plays or playlets. It is a retrospective corpus-object, not evidence that omitted produced pieces never existed.

### The Peter Pan project across media

- **1901:** *The Boy Castaways of Black Lake Island*. A privately made photographic album in which Barrie arranged photographs of the Llewelyn Davies boys with captions and a mock-adventure narrative. Only one of two copies survives; it is simultaneously family play, photo-book, and a material precursor of *Peter Pan*.
- **1902:** *The Little White Bird; or, Adventures in Kensington Gardens*. The first published appearance of Peter Pan, inside a novel not wholly about him.
- **1903–1904:** *Peter Pan: A Play*. The earliest surviving complete manuscript draft of the stage project; it differs materially from both the first production and Barrie’s 1928 published text.
- **1904:** *Peter Pan; or, The Boy Who Would Not Grow Up*. The initial stage manifestation, whose script continued to change during revivals and cannot be equated without qualification with the 1928 printed play.
- **1906:** *Peter Pan in Kensington Gardens*. A book made by extracting and revising the Peter Pan chapters of *The Little White Bird*, with Arthur Rackham’s illustrations. It is neither a wholly new narrative nor merely a reprint.
- **1908:** *When Wendy Grew Up: An Afterthought*. A theatrical afterpiece showing Wendy as an adult; it extends the project while resisting the status of a full sequel.
- **1911:** *Peter and Wendy*. The prose-novel manifestation of the Neverland story.
- **1912:** *Peter Pan Keepsake*. A commemorative or charity publication connected with Barrie’s Peter Pan project; it is an occasional object assembled around the creation rather than a new continuous fiction.
- **1921:** *Peter Pan* film scenario. Barrie’s treatment for a proposed silent-film adaptation; it is a production document and unrealized cinematic project, not the screenplay of the film ultimately released.
- **1928:** *Peter Pan; or, The Boy Who Would Not Grow Up*. The first authorized publication of the full play and Barrie’s late attempt to stabilize a work that had existed in performance for nearly twenty-four years.

### Film works and adaptation projects

- **1916:** *The Real Thing at Last*. A short film scenario and screen comedy parodying American-style Shakespearean cinema; Barrie supplied the scenario for a film produced by the British Actors Film Company.
- **1921:** *Peter Pan* film scenario. A silent-film treatment that was not used intact for the 1924 Paramount production.
- **1936:** *As You Like It* story treatment. Barrie contributed adaptation ideas or a treatment for Paul Czinner’s film; it is a collaborative production-stage text, not sole authorship of the finished screenplay.

### Memoir, biography, and public addresses

- **1896:** *Margaret Ogilvy*. A memoir of Barrie’s mother that combines biography, family recollection, and self-portraiture.
- **1913; revised 1925:** *Biographical Introduction to Scott’s Last Expedition*. Barrie’s memorial introduction concerning Robert Falcon Scott; the expanded biographical introduction belongs especially to the 1925 edition and should not be backdated wholesale to the first 1913 publication.
- **1917:** *The Russian Professors*. An occasional address or essay concerning displaced Russian academics; it acts partly as an appeal.
- **1922:** *Courage*. Barrie’s rectorial address delivered at the University of St Andrews on 3 May 1922; as an address it is both performed speech and published text.
- **1926:** *The Entrancing Life*. A rectorial or ceremonial address, published separately in limited form.
- **1928:** *Speech by Sir James Barrie at the Dinner to Rhodes Scholars, Oxford, June 20th, 1928*. A separately printed record of an occasional speech rather than a literary book conceived for general publication.
- **1930:** *The Greenwood Hat*. An autobiographical fiction whose pseudonymous self-construction prevents firm classification as either memoir or novel.
- **1934:** *The Rectorial Address at Edinburgh University*. A ceremonial public text associated with Barrie’s university office; catalogues vary in the title under which the address is recorded.

### Prefaces, editorial interventions, and collaborative book objects

- **1897:** *Stories by English Authors: London*. Barrie contributed to or helped select material for this Scribner series volume; the book as a whole is not his sole obra.
- **1897:** *Stories by English Authors: Scotland*. A selected anthology to which Barrie contributed; editorial participation and authorship of the constituent stories must not be conflated.
- **1899:** *The Allahakbarrie Book of Broadway Cricket for 1899*. A privately produced cricket-club book associated with Barrie’s amateur team, the Allahakbarries; it is a social artifact, sporting record, and comic publication rather than a conventional authored book.
- **1919:** *The Young Visiters; or, Mr. Salteena’s Plan*. Daisy Ashford wrote the novel as a child; Barrie made its famous preface. Only the preface belongs to Barrie, and attributing the book itself to him would erase Ashford’s authorship.
- **1920:** *The Story of a Red Cross Unit in Serbia*. Barrie supplied prefatory or supporting matter to this documentary work; the underlying record is not his creation.
- **Various dates:** Prefaces and introductions to books by friends and associates. Barrie made numerous short paratexts, but they do not turn the books they accompany into Barrie works; descriptive bibliographies enumerate them as contributions rather than independent obras.

### Juvenilia and posthumously recovered projects

- **c. 1870s:** *Bandelero the Bandit*. A schoolboy play preserved from Barrie’s juvenilia and first published in a scholarly edition in 2014; its date is approximate.
- **c. 1870s:** *Bohemia*. A juvenile play surviving in manuscript and published in 2014; the title belongs to the early dramatic project, but its exact composition date is not fixed.
- **c. 1870s:** *Caught Napping*. A juvenile play, likewise surviving outside Barrie’s lifetime canon and first made generally available in the 2014 scholarly edition.
- **1908; published 1957:** *When Wendy Grew Up: An Afterthought*. Its delayed publication makes it both a lifetime performance-text and a posthumously recovered printed work.
- **Undated; published 2017:** *The Reconstruction of the Crime*. A collaborative unfinished play by Barrie and E. V. Lucas; publication a century later establishes its survival, not a trustworthy original date.
