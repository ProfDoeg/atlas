## Works by Søren Aabye Kierkegaard

### Books, pamphlets, dissertations, and discourse collections published in his lifetime

- **1838:** *From the Papers of One Still Living, Published against His Will*. A critical book on Hans Christian Andersen, issued in Kierkegaard’s own name; it stands before the pseudonymous authorship proper. ([SKS 1](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-lp-root))
- **1841:** *On the Concept of Irony with Continual Reference to Socrates*. Kierkegaard’s university dissertation—an academic thesis as well as a philosophical obra. ([SKS 1](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-bi-root))
- **1843:** *Either/Or: A Fragment of Life, Part I*. A pseudonymous, composite book presented as papers discovered and edited by Victor Eremita; its essays, aphorisms, fiction, criticism, and “Seducer’s Diary” deliberately resist reduction to a single genre. ([SKS 2](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ee1-root))
- **1843:** *Either/Or: A Fragment of Life, Part II*. The companion volume, likewise edited by Victor Eremita, chiefly comprising Judge William’s ethical letters and a final sermon; the two volumes constitute one architectonic obra. ([SKS 3](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ee2-root))
- **1843:** *Two Upbuilding Discourses*. A signed pair of religious discourses, published as Kierkegaard’s direct-authorial counterpoint to *Either/Or*. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-2t43-root))
- **1843:** *Repetition: A Venture in Experimenting Psychology*. A pseudonymous philosophical novella or “psychological experiment” by Constantin Constantius, combining narrative, theory, letters, and staged authorship. ([SKS 4](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-g-root))
- **1843:** *Fear and Trembling: A Dialectical Lyric*. A pseudonymous philosophical-theological lyric by Johannes de silentio; its subtitle accurately signals a hybrid of meditation, narrative variation, and conceptual argument. ([SKS 4](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-fb-root))
- **1843:** *Three Upbuilding Discourses*. A signed collection of religious addresses, issued on the same day as *Repetition* and *Fear and Trembling*. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-3t43-root))
- **1843:** *Four Upbuilding Discourses*. A signed collection of religious addresses; “discourse,” rather than sermon or philosophical treatise, is Kierkegaard’s chosen form. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-4t43-root))
- **1844:** *Two Upbuilding Discourses*. A signed pair of religious discourses on patience and expectation. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-2t44-root))
- **1844:** *Three Upbuilding Discourses*. A signed collection treating remembrance of the creator, eternal salvation, and self-abnegation. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-3t44-root))
- **1844:** *Philosophical Fragments, or A Fragment of Philosophy*. A pseudonymous philosophical thought-experiment by Johannes Climacus, with Kierkegaard named as editor. ([SKS 4](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ps-root))
- **1844:** *The Concept of Anxiety: A Simple Psychologically Orienting Deliberation on the Dogmatic Issue of Hereditary Sin*. A pseudonymous psychological-dogmatic treatise by Vigilius Haufniensis. ([SKS 4](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ba-root))
- **1844:** *Prefaces: Light Reading for Certain Classes as the Occasion May Require*. A pseudonymous sequence by Nicolaus Notabene that makes the supposedly ancillary preface into the obra itself. ([SKS 4](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-f-root))
- **1844:** *Four Upbuilding Discourses*. A signed collection on dependence upon God, suffering, courage, and prayer. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-4t44-root))
- **1845:** *Three Discourses on Imagined Occasions*. Signed discourses for confession, marriage, and burial; they are literary addresses for imagined situations, not records of ceremonies actually performed. ([SKS 5](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ttl-root))
- **1845:** *Stages on Life’s Way: Studies by Various Persons*. A polyphonic pseudonymous Gesamtkunstwerk of banquet speeches, recollection, diary, letter, and psychological analysis, “compiled” by Hilarius Bookbinder. ([SKS 6](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-slv-root))
- **1845:** *Eighteen Upbuilding Discourses*. A collected manifestation of the six previously issued discourse booklets of 1843–44, not a new set of eighteen works. ([Stanford chronology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kierkegaard/#ChroKierWork))
- **1846:** *Concluding Unscientific Postscript to Philosophical Fragments*. A vast pseudonymous “mimetic-pathetic-dialectical compilation” by Johannes Climacus, published by Kierkegaard; formally a postscript, materially an independent major obra. ([SKS 7](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ae-root))
- **1846:** *A Literary Review: Two Ages*. A signed book-length review of Thomasine Gyllembourg’s novel that becomes an analysis of revolution, modernity, publicity, and “the present age.” ([SKS 8](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-la-root))
- **1847:** *Upbuilding Discourses in Various Spirits*. A signed three-part discourse collection containing an occasional discourse, three lily-and-bird discourses, and seven discourses on suffering. ([SKS 8](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ota-root))
- **1847:** *Works of Love: Some Christian Deliberations in the Form of Discourses*. A signed, two-series sequence of Christian ethical deliberations; it calls itself neither a systematic treatise nor a sermon collection. ([SKS 9](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-kg-root))
- **1848:** *Christian Discourses*. A signed four-part cycle of twenty-eight discourses addressing worldly cares, suffering, admonition, and Friday Communion. ([SKS 10](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ct-root))
- **1849:** *The Lily of the Field and the Bird of the Air: Three Devotional Discourses*. A signed triptych of devotional addresses. ([SKS 11](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-lf-root))
- **1849:** *Two Minor Ethical-Religious Essays*. A pseudonymous diptych by H.H., comprising a poetical investigation of martyrdom and an essay distinguishing genius from apostle. ([SKS 11](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-tsa-root))
- **1849:** *The Sickness unto Death: A Christian Psychological Exposition for Upbuilding and Awakening*. A pseudonymous Christian psychology by Anti-Climacus, published by Kierkegaard. ([SKS 11](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-sd-root))
- **1849:** *“The High Priest”—“The Tax Collector”—“The Woman Who Was a Sinner”: Three Discourses at the Communion on Fridays*. A signed Communion triptych. ([SKS 11](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-yts-root))
- **1850:** *Practice in Christianity*. Three pseudonymous exercises by Anti-Climacus, published by Kierkegaard; the work is a protocol of existential imitation as much as a theological book. ([SKS 12](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ic-root))
- **1850:** *An Upbuilding Discourse*. A signed discourse on the woman who was a sinner. ([SKS 12](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-eot-root))
- **1851:** *Two Discourses at the Communion on Fridays*. A signed pair of Communion addresses. ([SKS 12](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-taf-root))
- **1851:** *On My Work as an Author*. A short signed interpretation of the authorship; it is both retrospective account and manifesto about how the preceding books are to act upon readers. ([SKS 13](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-fv-root))
- **1851:** *For Self-Examination, Recommended to the Present Age*. A signed sequence of Christian exercises explicitly designed as a protocol for reading and self-application. ([SKS 13](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ts-root))
- **1855:** *This Must Be Said; So Let It Be Said*. A polemical pamphlet in the church campaign, functioning as an intervention or manifesto rather than detached description. ([SKS 13](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-dss-root))
- **1855:** *What Christ Judges of Official Christianity*. A polemical pamphlet attacking established Christendom. ([SKS 13](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-hcd-root))
- **1855:** *The Changelessness of God: A Discourse*. Kierkegaard’s last separately published discourse. ([SKS 13](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-gu-root))

### Newspaper and journal interventions

- **1834:** *Another Defense of Woman’s Great Abilities*. An early newspaper article signed “A.”; it lies outside what Kierkegaard later delimited as his authorship but belongs to the complete critical edition. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-fqa-root))
- **1836:** *The Copenhagen Post’s Morning Observations in No. 43*. An early polemical newspaper article signed “B.” ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-km-root))
- **1836:** *On The Fatherland’s Polemic, I–II*. A two-part newspaper intervention signed “B.” ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-fp-root))
- **1836:** *To Mr. Orla Lehmann*. A newspaper reply; like the other 1834–36 pieces, it was not counted by Kierkegaard as part of his mature authorship. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ol-root))
- **1842:** *An Open Confession*. A signed newspaper article answering criticism of his dissertation. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-aas-root))
- **1843:** *Who Is the Author of Either/Or?* A pseudonymous newspaper mystification signed “A. F. …,” extending the book’s staged uncertainty about authorship. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-fee-root))
- **1843:** *Thanks to Professor Heiberg*. A newspaper response signed Victor Eremita. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ph-root))
- **1843:** *A Little Explanation*. A signed newspaper clarification concerning the two 1843 discourse publications. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-elf-root))
- **1845:** *A Declaration and Something More*. A signed newspaper intervention concerning the authorship of *Stages on Life’s Way*. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-eel-root))
- **1845:** *A Passing Observation Concerning a Detail in Don Giovanni, I–II*. A two-part feuilleton signed “A.”, derived from the aesthetic world of *Either/Or*. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-dj-root))
- **1845:** *The Activity of a Traveling Aesthetician and How He Still Happened to Pay for the Dinner*. A Corsair-affair provocation by Frater Taciturnus; as an intervention, it helped bring about the public attack it ostensibly requested. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-aev-root))
- **1846:** *The Dialectical Result of a Literary Police Action*. Frater Taciturnus’s newspaper response during the Corsair affair. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-pf-root))
- **1848:** *The Crisis and a Crisis in the Life of an Actress, I–IV*. A four-part newspaper essay by Inter et Inter on Johanne Luise Heiberg and the art of acting; serial publication is intrinsic to this manifestation. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-kks-root))
- **1851:** *Occasioned by a Statement About Me by Dr. Rudelbach*. A signed open letter clarifying Kierkegaard’s relation to ecclesiastical reform. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ydr-root))
- **1854:** *Was Bishop Mynster a “Witness to the Truth,” One of “the True Witnesses to the Truth”—Is This the Truth?* The opening manifesto of Kierkegaard’s final public campaign against the Danish established church. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-bms-root))
- **1854:** *There the Matter Rests!* A second newspaper intervention in the church campaign. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-dbd-root))
- **1855:** *A Challenge to Me from Pastor Paludan-Müller*. A newspaper reply in the church controversy. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ppm-root))
- **1855:** *The Point at Issue with Bishop Martensen*. A newspaper polemic identifying the decisive issue in the established ecclesiastical order. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-sbm-root))
- **1855:** *Two New Witnesses to the Truth*. A brief feuilleton in the church campaign. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-tns-root))
- **1855:** *At Bishop Mynster’s Death*. A newspaper intervention on Mynster’s official commemoration. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-bmd-root))
- **1855:** *Is This Christian Worship, or Is It Making a Fool of God?* A polemical “question of conscience” addressed to public religious practice. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-cgn-root))
- **1855:** *What Must Be Done—Whether by Me or by Another*. An activist newspaper text: its form is closer to summons or manifesto than exposition. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-hg-root))
- **1855:** *The Religious Situation*. A compressed diagnosis of contemporary Christendom. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-drt-root))
- **1855:** *A Thesis—Just One Single One*. A newspaper thesis formulated as a polemical intervention. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-et-root))
- **1855:** *“Salt”; for “Christendom” Is the Decay of Christianity; “a Christian World” Is a Falling Away from Christianity*. A manifesto-like newspaper article whose title itself advances its thesis. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-sa-root))
- **1855:** *What Do I Want?* A signed declaration of the limited aim of Kierkegaard’s church campaign. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-hjv-root))
- **1855:** *On the Occasion of an Anonymous Proposal to Me in This Newspaper’s No. 79*. A newspaper response within the campaign. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-af-root))
- **1855:** *Would It Be Best Now to “Stop Sounding the Alarm”?* A polemical refusal to end the campaign. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-rk-root))
- **1855:** *Christianity with a Royal Appointment and Christianity without a Royal Appointment*. A feuilleton contrasting state office with Christian witness. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ck-root))
- **1855:** *What Cruel Punishment!* A brief newspaper polemic. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-hgs-root))
- **1855:** *A Result*. A newspaper statement drawing a conclusion from the public controversy. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-er-root))
- **1855:** *A Monologue*. A feuilleton staged as solitary speech. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-em-root))
- **1855:** *Concerning a Foolish Importance in Relation to Me and the Conception of Christianity I Am Making Known*. A corrective newspaper intervention. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-atv-root))
- **1855:** *For the New Edition of Practice in Christianity*. A newspaper notice that also reactivates the earlier book within the church campaign. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-oic-root))
- **1855:** *That Bishop Martensen’s Silence Is (1) Christianly Indefensible; (2) Ludicrous; (3) Obtusely Sagacious; (4) in More than One Respect Contemptible*. Kierkegaard’s final published newspaper article. ([SKS 14](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-bmt-root))

### Completed, abandoned, or repeatedly reworked unpublished projects

- **1840:** *A Moment, Mr. Andersen!* A short polemical article prompted by an H. C. Andersen play; Kierkegaard prepared but did not publish it. ([SKS 15](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-oia-root))
- **1842–1843:** *Johannes Climacus, or De omnibus dubitandum est*. An unfinished philosophical novella about the making of a doubter; incompletion is part of its surviving status, not a missing bibliographical detail. ([SKS 15](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-jc-root))
- **1843–1844:** *Polemic against Heiberg*. Constantin Constantius’s unpublished dossier responding to Heiberg’s criticism of *Repetition*; it survives as a gathered polemical project rather than a finalized book. ([SKS 15](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-pmh-root))
- **1846–1847, with later revisions:** *The Book on Adler*. A repeatedly rewritten investigation of the pastor Adolph Peter Adler, revelation, authority, and authorship; no single manuscript state can unproblematically be called the definitive work, although a shortened version appeared posthumously in 1872 and a fuller edition in 1916. ([SKS 15](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-boa-root))
- **July–October 1848:** *The Point of View for My Work as an Author*. A substantially completed retrospective and authorial manifesto withheld by Kierkegaard and first published posthumously in 1859; its account of the authorship is itself strategic and remains contested as autobiography. ([SKS 16](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-sfv-root))
- **1848–1849:** *Armed Neutrality, or My Position as a Christian Author in Christendom*. An unpublished declaration of authorial position, eventually published posthumously; it is closer to a manifesto of stance than a neutral memoir. ([SKS 16](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-bn-root))
- **December 1848:** *Mr. Phister as Captain Scipio*. An unpublished theatrical study signed Procul, concerning actor Ludvig Phister’s comic performance. ([SKS 16](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-pcs-root))
- **1851–1852:** *Judge for Yourselves! Recommended to the Present Age for Self-Examination, Second Series*. A completed continuation of *For Self-Examination*, withheld and first published posthumously in 1876. ([SKS 16](https://tekster.kb.dk/text/sks-ds-root))

### Journals: serial writing environments rather than finished works

- **1833–1846:** *Journals AA–KK*. Ten lettered manuscript journals—AA, BB, CC, DD, EE, FF, GG, HH, JJ, and KK—containing diaries, drafts, reading notes, travel observations, and materials later transformed into published books. They resist treatment as ten authored “works”: the critical edition preserves their physical order, including writing begun from both ends of several volumes. ([SKS 17–18 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1846–1854:** *Journals NB–NB36*. Thirty-six consecutively designated working journals, from NB through NB36; they form an evolving workshop of reflection, draft composition, self-interpretation, and plans rather than a diary prepared for publication. Treating later editorial selections from them as books would mistake posthumous manifestations for Kierkegaard’s own objects. ([SKS 20–26 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))

### Notebooks and study instruments

- **1833–1849:** *Notebooks 1–15*. Fifteen manuscript notebooks containing lecture notes, excerpts, sketches, accounts, and preparatory writing; individual ranges overlap, and Notebook 15 is separated chronologically from the first fourteen. They are material instruments of study and composition, not a deliberately unified obra. ([SKS 19 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))

### Loose papers, fragments, drafts, and unrealized projects

- **1830–1855:** *Loose Papers 1–596*. The critical edition preserves 596 numbered units—leaves, slips, cardboard pieces, gatherings, excerpts, sermon drafts, aphorisms, projected writings, and abandoned plans. This is an archival manifestation assembled from surviving objects, not a work Kierkegaard made as a whole; notable internal projects include the “green book” rudiments of *Either/Or*, the Pastoral Seminary sermon, *The Earthquake*, *Eulogy on Autumn*, projected lectures, draft discourses, and materials for the church campaign. ([SKS 27–28 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1841:** *Sermon Delivered at the Pastoral Seminary*. A manuscript probationary sermon preserved among the loose papers; it is a performed-and-written religious exercise rather than a book. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1841:** *The First Rudiments of Either/Or: The Green Book; Some Particulars That Were Not Used*. A preparatory manuscript complex whose unused status makes it evidence of composition rather than an independent finished work. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1843–1846:** *The Earthquake*. A retrospectively titled autobiographical fragment; it survives as a paper, and its boundaries and date are editorial rather than those of a publication authorized by Kierkegaard. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1844:** *Trial Sermon*. A manuscript sermon created for an ecclesiastical examination or trial setting, not issued as a publication. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1845:** *Projected Writings*. Three paper units recording book-projects rather than completed books; the intentions themselves are the surviving made objects. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1846:** *Eulogy on Autumn*. A small manuscript project preserved among the papers and never established as a lifetime publication. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1846:** *Drafts for Occasional Discourses on Death*. A group of preparatory religious texts whose unfinished, grouped status resists calling them a discourse collection. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1847:** *The Dialectic of Ethical and Ethical-Religious Communication*. A substantial manuscript investigation of communication, preserved in several paper units but not finalized for publication. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1848:** *An Apology*. A small manuscript complex defending or explaining Kierkegaard’s authorial activity; it remained unpublished. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1848:** *Invitation to Lectures and to Subscription*. Draft announcements for projects that were not realized; here the project, rather than an eventual lecture series or book, is the obra that survives. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1848:** *“Let Not the Heart Sin in Sorrow”: Seven Discourses*. Draft materials for an unrealized discourse cycle; it should not be silently promoted into a completed book. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1850:** *“The Passion History!” Christian Discourses*. Draft material for another unrealized Christian discourse project. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1851:** *“The Shepherd”—“The Hired Hand”*. A manuscript sketch or projected treatment preserved as a single paper unit. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
- **1852:** *Drafts for Two Sermons*. A manuscript pair of sermon projects, not completed publications. ([SKS 27 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))

### Correspondence and inscribed objects

- **1829–1855:** *Letters to and from Kierkegaard*. A corpus of slightly more than 300 surviving letters organized in the critical edition by correspondent groups—family, Regine Olsen/Schlegel, Emil Boesen, Rasmus Nielsen, publishers, clergy, authorities, and others. Each letter is an occasion-bound communicative act, but “the correspondence” is a posthumous archival creation rather than an obra Kierkegaard composed as a whole; moreover, some items are incoming and therefore not made by him. ([SKS 28 description](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/english/sks-table-of-contents/soeren_kierkegaards_skrifter/))
- **Dates varying:** *Dedications in Presentation Copies*. The critical edition records 119 known handwritten dedications; these are inscriptions that transform individual copies into singular objects, not independent books, and some survive only through copies or transcripts. ([SKS 28 contents](https://teol.ku.dk/skc/sks/))
