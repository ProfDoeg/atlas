## Works by David Ben-Gurion

### Historical studies and political books

- **1918:** *Eretz Yisrael in the Past and Present* (*Eretz Yisroel in fargangenheit un gegenvart*). A Yiddish historical-geographical study made jointly with Yitzhak Ben-Zvi while both men were in the United States; later Hebrew and English publications are manifestations of this collaborative obra, not new works. [A scholarly bibliographical review identifies it as their joint study.](https://www.tau.ac.il/sites/default/files/media_server/imported/577/files/2016/01/palestine1.pdf)

- **1933:** *From Class to Nation* (*Mi-Maʿamad le-ʿAm*). A programmatic collection in which labor-Zionist organization becomes a national political project; individual essays had separate lives before and after their incorporation into the book. [David Ohana’s scholarly bibliography records the work and its constituent essay “Toward the Future.”](https://pdfcoffee.com/modernism-and-david-ohana-auth-modernism-and-zionism-palgrave-macmillan-uk-2012pdf-pdf-free.html)

- **1938:** *On the Ways of Our Policy* (*Darkhei Mediniyutenu*). An edited report of the world convention of Ihud Poalei Zion, substantially associated with Ben-Gurion but not simply a single-author monograph; it is simultaneously documentary record, political program, and editorial construction. [A scholarly bibliography records Ben-Gurion as editor and gives the 1938 publication details.](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110492507-023/pdf?licenseType=open-access)

- **1950:** *When Israel Fought* (*Be-Hilahem Yisrael*). A documentary-political collection of wartime writings and speeches, assembled retrospectively but originally made as interventions directed toward an uncertain future. [The Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute describes its contents and preserves Ben-Gurion’s dated preface of 24 June 1950.](https://bgh.org.il/books/)

- **1951–1957:** *Vision and Path* (*Hazon ve-Derekh*), five volumes. A large collected obra of speeches, essays, reports, and ideological statements rather than five continuously composed books; its unity is editorial and political. [The Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute presents the five-volume collection.](https://bgh.org.il/books/)

- **1952; expanded English manifestation 1954:** *Rebirth and Destiny of Israel*. A selection of speeches and essays rather than an independently composed narrative; sources differ between 1952 and 1954 because they variously date the underlying collection and its English publication. [The Croatian Encyclopedia dates the work to 1952](https://enciklopedija.hr/clanak/ben-gurion-david), while [bibliographical records identify the New York English edition as 1954](https://iccr.gov.in/sites/default/files/English%20books_0.pdf).

- **1956:** *From Class to Nation* (*Mi-Maʿamad le-ʿAm*), enlarged collected manifestation. This later compilation should not be mistaken for an entirely new obra merely because its contents and publication form differ from the earlier collection. [Ohana’s bibliography cites the 1956 volume and identifies an essay contained in it.](https://pdfcoffee.com/modernism-and-david-ohana-auth-modernism-and-zionism-palgrave-macmillan-uk-2012pdf-pdf-free.html)

- **1957:** *Israel and the Diaspora* (*Yisrael ve-ha-Tefutzah*). A separately issued political essay on the relationship between the state and Jewish communities abroad; it also reappeared inside later collections. [A scholarly study records the 1957 Government Printer publication and its later inclusion in *Netzah Yisrael*.](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Ohana-David-Political-Theologies-in-the-Holy-Land_-Israeli-Messianism-and-its-Critics-Routledge-Jewish-Studies-Series-Routledge-2013.pdf)

- **1959:** *The Sinai Campaign* (*Maʿarekhet Sinai*). Ben-Gurion’s political-military account of the 1956 war, made by a participant who was also prime minister and defense minister; its object is therefore both history and official self-interpretation. [The work appears as *The Sinai War*, Tel Aviv, 1959, in a scholarly bibliography](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110492507-023/pdf?licenseType=open-access), while [a Hebrew cultural reference dates *Maʿarekhet Sinai* to 1960](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/lexicon/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F/); the discrepancy reflects edition/date conventions and should not be silently resolved.

- **1963:** *Israel: Years of Challenge*. An English-language historical and political synthesis assembled from Ben-Gurion’s accounts and addresses; it precedes and overlaps with his later personal history rather than constituting an unrelated obra. [The contemporary bibliographical record dates it to 1963](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ben-gurion-gruen-david), although some editions bear 1964 publication dates.

- **1963–1964:** *The Eternity of Israel* (*Netzah Yisrael*). A collection of the broad programmatic introductions Ben-Gurion wrote for Israeli government yearbooks during the 1950s and early 1960s; the institute dates its first appearance to autumn 1963, while bibliographies commonly cite 1964. [The Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute explains the collection’s origin](https://bgh.org.il/books/), and [Ohana’s bibliography gives the 1964 Tel Aviv form](https://pdfcoffee.com/modernism-and-david-ohana-auth-modernism-and-zionism-palgrave-macmillan-uk-2012pdf-pdf-free.html).

- **1966:** *The Jews in Their Land*. An illustrated historical panorama edited by Ben-Gurion and built from multiple contributors and media; its collaborative, editorial character makes it closer to a historical Gesamtkunstwerk than to a conventional single-author book. [The contemporary encyclopedia identifies Ben-Gurion as editor and dates it to 1966.](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ben-gurion-gruen-david)

- **1967:** *Meetings with Arab Leaders* (*Pegishot im Manhigim Araviyim*). A documentary account of Ben-Gurion’s conversations and attempted political accommodations with Arab leaders from the 1930s onward. The expanded English manifestation, *My Talks with Arab Leaders* (1972–1973), incorporates later material and therefore is not a mechanically identical translation. [The English edition’s publication history identifies the 1967 Hebrew source and its added material.](https://www.scribd.com/document/933303468/My-Talks-With-Arab-Leaders-David-Ben-Gurion-490c3196f230a536fa0f150702a98bd5-Anna-s-Archive)

- **1969:** *The Renewed State of Israel* (*Medinat Yisrael ha-Meḥudeshet*), two volumes. A history and political self-account of Israel’s establishment and first years, made explicitly from Ben-Gurion’s standpoint and as an attempt to shape the state’s future memory. [The Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute describes the work’s purpose](https://bgh.org.il/books/), and [a scholarly bibliography records the 1969 Tel Aviv edition](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110492507-023/pdf?licenseType=open-access).

- **1970:** *Negotiations with Nasser*. A documentary narrative of indirect efforts to initiate discussions with Egyptian president Gamal Abdel Nasser; some of its material was subsequently absorbed into the expanded English *My Talks with Arab Leaders*. [The contemporary encyclopedia lists it as a distinct 1970 publication.](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ben-gurion-gruen-david)

- **1971:** *Israel: A Personal History*. The English manifestation of Ben-Gurion’s state history combines memoir, documentation, photographs, and political argument; it is not merely a neutral chronicle. [Cambridge’s scholarly bibliography records the London 1971 publication.](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/history-of-modern-israel/bibliography/2180E080E109A7CDED255A6088D47DCB)

### Biblical studies

- **1950s–1960s:** *The Bible-study circle at the prime minister’s residence*. A continuing intellectual and performative project convened by Ben-Gurion rather than a fixed text: scholars and public figures interpreted the Bible in relation to sovereignty, ethics, and the modern state. Its resistance to being called a single “work” is essential—it existed through meetings, arguments, transcripts, and later publications. [The Hebrew cultural encyclopedia identifies the circle as a central expression of Ben-Gurion’s cultural activity.](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/lexicon/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F/)

- **1969:** *Studies in the Bible* (*Iyyunim ba-Tanakh*). A collection of Ben-Gurion’s biblical essays, letters, and interpretations, relating the ancient text to Jewish history and the modern state; later editions alter the container, not the underlying essays. [The Hebrew cultural encyclopedia dates the collection to 1969](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/lexicon/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F/), and [the Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute identifies it as a collected work](https://bgh.org.il/books/).

- **1972:** *Ben-Gurion Looks at the Bible*. An English selection and translation from Ben-Gurion’s biblical studies, not a wholly new composition. [The contemporary encyclopedia lists the 1972 manifestation.](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ben-gurion-gruen-david)

### Memoirs, diaries, and correspondence

- **c. 1900–1973:** *Diaries*. A lifelong manuscript obra begun when Ben-Gurion was about fourteen and continued, with interruptions, almost until his death. It resists reduction to the subsequently edited books: the archival diary is the made thing, while *War Diary* and other printed selections are incomplete editorial manifestations. [The Hebrew cultural encyclopedia describes approximately seventy years of diary-keeping](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/lexicon/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F/); [Oxford Bibliographies characterizes the resulting literature and archive as exceptionally extensive](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637).

- **1947–1949; published 1982:** *War Diary: The War of Independence* (*Yoman ha-Milḥamah: Milḥemet ha-ʿAtzmaʾut*). An edited publication of the contemporary diary, not a book composed in 1982; editorial selection means the printed text is not coextensive with the manuscript diary. [The Hebrew cultural encyclopedia records both the wartime span and the 1982 publication.](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/lexicon/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F/)

- **1904–1973:** *Correspondence*. A vast epistolary body preserved because Ben-Gurion retained copies of outgoing letters and incoming documents. It is an open-ended obra rather than one authored book, and printed selections cannot claim completeness. [The Hebrew cultural encyclopedia describes his systematic preservation practice](https://tarbutil.cet.ac.il/lexicon/%D7%93%D7%95%D7%93-%D7%91%D7%9F-%D7%92%D7%95%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%95%D7%9F/); [the Ben-Gurion Institute treats letters as one of the principal forms of his writing](https://bgh.org.il/books/).

- **1918–1968; published 1968:** *Letters to Paula and the Children* (*Mikhtavim el Pola ve-el ha-Yeladim*). A selected family correspondence in which intimate writing and Zionist political work continually overlap; it is an edited manifestation of a larger correspondence, not the correspondence entire. [The Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute identifies and provides the collection.](https://bgh.org.il/books/)

- **1904–1919; published 1971–1972:** *The Letters of David Ben-Gurion*, volume 1. An edited chronological corpus prepared by Yehuda Erez; the letters are Ben-Gurion’s, but the volume’s boundaries and apparatus are the editor’s creation. [David Landau’s source note gives the span, editor, publishers, and 1972 date](https://publicism.info/biography/ben-gurion/16.html), while contemporary summaries date the series from 1971.

- **1970:** *Recollections*. A memoir fashioned from remembered episodes rather than a continuous documentary history; it overlaps with the Hebrew memoir project but is not necessarily textually identical to it. [A scholarly bibliography records the London 1970 work.](https://pdfcoffee.com/modernism-and-david-ohana-auth-modernism-and-zionism-palgrave-macmillan-uk-2012pdf-pdf-free.html)

- **1971–posthumous:** *Memoirs* (*Zikhronot*), multiple volumes. Ben-Gurion began an intended eleven-volume history of Israel’s early years but did not complete that project; published volumes combine memoir with letters, diary extracts, speeches, and documents, and some appeared after his death. It is therefore both an unfinished project and a posthumously constructed textual obra. [A scholarly source records volume 1 in 1971 and volume 2 as a 1971–1986 publication](https://pdfcoffee.com/modernism-and-david-ohana-auth-modernism-and-zionism-palgrave-macmillan-uk-2012pdf-pdf-free.html); [a bibliographical review describes volume 4 as a miscellany of letters, diary portions, speeches, and other writings](https://www.tau.ac.il/sites/default/files/media_server/imported/577/files/2016/01/palestine1.pdf).

- **Undated recollections; published posthumously 1974–1975:** *My Father’s House* (*Beit Avi*). Childhood recollections edited by Peter Frye and Amos Ettinger and issued after Ben-Gurion’s death; because editors shaped the published object, it is a mediated, posthumous manifestation rather than an uncomplicated authorial book. [Landau’s source note identifies the editors and 1975 edition](https://publicism.info/biography/ben-gurion/16.html), while [a scholarly bibliography cites a 1974 Tel Aviv issue](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Ohana-David-Political-Theologies-in-the-Holy-Land_-Israeli-Messianism-and-its-Critics-Routledge-Jewish-Studies-Series-Routledge-2013.pdf).

### Speeches, articles, prefaces, and public interventions

- **1910–1959:** *Published writings, speeches, articles, and letters*. This is a body of work rather than one title: Shmuel Lachover’s standard bibliography, *The Writings of David Ben-Gurion: A Bibliography, 1910–1959*, is the controlling inventory for the period. Its entries should not be collapsed into the later books that reprint them. [The National Library of Israel identifies the bibliography, editor, and chronological coverage.](https://www.nli.org.il/he/books/NNL_ALEPH990010042100205171/NLI)

- **1949–October 1973:** *Published articles, speeches, letters, prefaces, and excerpts*. Lily Adar’s continuation bibliography identifies 3,770 published items for this period, selected from a computerized database of approximately 21,000 records; consequently, no short atlas list can honestly pretend that every intervention is individually reproduced here. The corpus itself is the obra, while each bibliographical item remains separately traceable in the standard inventory. [The Open University Library describes the bibliography’s scope, rules of inclusion, and database.](https://www.openu.ac.il/library/whatisnew/38343.html)

- **1950–early 1960s:** *Introductions to the Israel Government Yearbooks*. These annual essays were programmatic public addresses on immigration, security, Jewish history, science, and Israel’s place in the world—manifestos embedded inside administrative reference books. They later became the collection *Netzah Yisrael*. [The Ben-Gurion Heritage Institute describes their original function and later collection.](https://bgh.org.il/books/)

### Manifestos and collaboratively authored state texts

- **1942:** *Biltmore Program*. A political manifesto calling for a Jewish Commonwealth, framed collectively within the Extraordinary Zionist Conference; Ben-Gurion was one of its principal makers, not its sole author. [Oxford Bibliographies identifies him as one of the program’s framers.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)

- **14 May 1948:** *Declaration of the Establishment of the State of Israel*. A performative manifesto that helped constitute the state by being approved, signed, and proclaimed, rather than merely describing it. Authorship is irreducibly collective: drafts passed through legal and political committees and Moshe Sharett before Ben-Gurion’s decisive final revision and proclamation, so attributing the whole text to him alone would be false. [Oxford Bibliographies confirms that Ben-Gurion proclaimed independence on 14 May 1948](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637).

### Newspapers and editorial projects

- **1907–1915:** *Poale Zion periodical writing and editing*. Ben-Gurion wrote for and helped edit labor-Zionist party newspapers, including *Ha-Aḥdut*; this was collective political media work rather than a bounded personal book. Individual contributions belong in Lachover’s item-level bibliography, and anonymous or collectively signed matter resists secure attribution. [Oxford Bibliographies records that he edited the party newspaper](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637), and [Landau’s source inventory specifically cites issues of *Achdouth* from 1910](https://publicism.info/biography/ben-gurion/16.html).

### Institutional and political projects

- **1906 onward:** *Poale Zion organization in Palestine*. A collective political project that Ben-Gurion helped build and through which much of his early writing and organizing acquired force; it cannot responsibly be called his work alone. [Oxford Bibliographies identifies him as one of the party’s founders in 1906.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)

- **1920:** *Histadrut*. A labor federation co-founded and then administered by Ben-Gurion as its first secretary-general; it is an institutional obra made by many people, not a personal artifact. [Oxford Bibliographies records his role as co-founder and secretary-general.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)

- **1930:** *Mapai*. A political party formed through the merger of labor-Zionist organizations, with Ben-Gurion among its founders and its dominant leader. It is properly a collective political project whose consequences cannot be separated from its organizational medium. [Oxford Bibliographies records the founding and Ben-Gurion’s leadership.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)

- **1948:** *The State of Israel*. Ben-Gurion did not “author” a country, but state-making was his central political project: he led the provisional government and proclaimed independence. The state emphatically resists classification as his personal work because it was made collectively, amid conflict, and by institutions and populations whose agency cannot be absorbed into one biography. [Oxford Bibliographies documents his leadership of the provisional government and proclamation of independence.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)

- **1948:** *Israel Defense Forces*. An institutional consolidation of pre-state armed organizations under state authority, pursued by Ben-Gurion as prime minister and defense minister. It is a collective state institution rather than a personally authored object, although the integration project and doctrine of unified command bear his decisive agency. [Oxford Bibliographies attributes responsibility for building the IDF to Ben-Gurion during his premiership.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)

- **1953 onward:** *Sde Boker and development of the Negev*. A lived political project joining settlement, personal example, territorial planning, and public rhetoric; no single text contains it. Ben-Gurion’s move to the kibbutz and subsequent advocacy made his own life one medium of the project, but neither Sde Boker nor the Negev can be classified as his personal work. [Oxford Bibliographies identifies Sde Boker as his kibbutz and burial place](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637), while [the Hebrew cultural encyclopedia identifies “Southward—to the Negev” among his programmatic writings](https://www.lib.cet.ac.il/PAGES/articleitem.asp?item=110).

- **1965:** *Rafi*. A short-lived political party created after Ben-Gurion’s break with Mapai; it was a collective organization and electoral intervention, not a text, and its incompleteness as a durable political project is part of its status. [Oxford Bibliographies records Ben-Gurion’s creation of Rafi and its ten Knesset seats.](https://academic.oup.com/reference/62376/reference-article-abstract/554963637)
