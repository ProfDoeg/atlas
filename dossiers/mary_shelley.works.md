## Works by Mary Wollstonecraft Shelley

### Novels and novel-projects

- **1816–18; substantially revised 1831:** *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus*. A Gothic and philosophical novel first issued anonymously; Percy Bysshe Shelley supplied revisions and the 1818 preface, but scholarship treats Mary Shelley as author, and the substantially rewritten 1831 edition as a distinct textual manifestation. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1819–20:** *Mathilda*. A completed novella or short novel about incestuous desire and bereavement; William Godwin prevented publication, so it remained manuscript until 1959. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1820–22; published 1823:** *Valperga; or, The Life and Adventures of Castruccio, Prince of Lucca*. A historical novel whose surviving printed manifestation was materially shortened and altered by Godwin while preparing it for publication. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1824–26:** *The Last Man*. An apocalyptic novel presented as a reconstruction of prophetic Sibylline leaves—a deliberately unstable fiction of editorial recovery. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1827–29; published 1830:** *The Fortunes of Perkin Warbeck: A Romance*. A historical novel that treats the pretender Perkin Warbeck as the surviving Richard of York. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1833–35:** *Lodore*. A three-volume domestic and social novel. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1835–37:** *Falkner: A Novel*. A three-volume novel and Shelley’s last completed novel. [MWSN; Bib]

### Travel obras

- **1814 and 1816 journeys; published 1817:** *History of a Six Weeks’ Tour through a Part of France, Switzerland, Germany, and Holland*. A collaborative travel book by Mary and Percy Bysshe Shelley, made from Mary’s journal and letters by both writers; it also served as a publication vehicle for Percy’s “Mont Blanc.” [MWSN; Bib]
- **1840–43 journeys; published 1844:** *Rambles in Germany and Italy, in 1840, 1842, and 1843*. A two-volume travel narrative combining letters, political commentary, art writing, and memoir; it was Shelley’s last book published during her lifetime. [MWSN; Bib]

### Dramas and literature for young readers

- **1820:** *Proserpine*. A mythological verse-and-prose drama conceived for young readers; Percy Bysshe Shelley contributed two lyric poems, and the version published in 1832 was substantially cut, so it is a collaborative and textually multiple obra. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1820:** *Midas*. A mythological drama for young readers; Percy supplied two songs, and the whole remained unpublished until 1922. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1820:** *Maurice; or, The Fisher’s Cot*. A children’s story written for Laurette Tighe; long presumed lost, its presentation manuscript was rediscovered in Italy in 1997. [LL4; Bib]

### Tales and short fiction

- **1819:** *Valerius: The Reanimated Roman*. An unfinished tale-project known from manuscript, about a Roman restored to life; it resists classification as a completed story. [CTS; Bib]
- **1822:** *A Tale of the Passions; or, The Death of Despina*. A historical tale published in *The Liberal*. [CTS; Bib]
- **1823–24:** *The Bride of Modern Italy*. A tale published in *The London Magazine*. [CTS; Bib]
- **1826:** *Lacy de Vere*. An annual tale organized around loss, memory, and Wordsworthian quotation. [LL4; CTS]
- **1826; published 1863:** *Roger Dodsworth: The Reanimated Englishman*. A satirical reanimation tale prompted by a contemporary newspaper hoax; publication was posthumous. [CTS; Bib]
- **1827:** *The Convent of Chailot* [usually normalized as *Chaillot*]. A historical tale published anonymously; the modern scholarly edition places it among part-authored or attributed writings, so Mary Shelley’s precise share is not completely secure. [LL4; CTS]
- **1828:** *Ferdinando Eboli: A Tale*. A tale of divided identity published in *The Keepsake*. [CTS; Bib]
- **1829:** *The Mourner*. A framed tale of grief, guilt, and reconciliation. [CTS; Bib]
- **1829:** *The Evil Eye: A Tale*. A supernatural tale set in Greece and Albania. [CTS; Bib]
- **1829:** *The False Rhyme*. A historical tale built around minstrelsy, family conflict, and an apparently supernatural warning. [CTS; Bib]
- **1830:** *The Swiss Peasant*. A tale of revolutionary violence and its domestic consequences. [CTS; Bib]
- **1830:** *Transformation*. A supernatural tale of bodily exchange and moral reform. [CTS; Bib]
- **1831:** *The Dream: A Tale*. A historical romance structured around a revelatory dream. [CTS; Bib]
- **1832:** *The Brother and Sister: An Italian Story*. An Italian historical tale of separated siblings and political violence. [CTS; Bib]
- **1832:** *The Invisible Girl*. A Gothic tale whose apparent supernatural manifestation is ultimately explained. [CTS; Bib]
- **1833:** *The Smuggler and His Family*. A domestic adventure tale published in *Original Compositions in Prose and Verse*. [CTS; Bib]
- **1833:** *The Mortal Immortal*. A speculative tale narrated by a man who has survived for centuries after drinking an alchemical elixir. [CTS; Bib]
- **1834:** *The Elder Son*. A family and inheritance tale published in *Heath’s Book of Beauty*. [CTS; Bib]
- **1834:** *The Trial of Love*. A historical tale published in *The Keepsake*. [CTS; Bib]
- **1836:** *The Parvenue*. A social tale about class mobility, wealth, and estrangement. [CTS; Bib]
- **1837:** *The Pilgrims*. A historical tale in which pilgrimage and storytelling frame questions of love and religious identity. [CTS; Bib]
- **1838:** *Euphrasia: A Tale of Greece*. A historical tale of Greek resistance and female heroism. [CTS; Bib]
- **Before 1844; published 1877:** *The Heir of Mondolfo*. A posthumously published Italian Gothic tale; its exact composition date is uncertain. [CTS; Bib]
- **After 1844:** *Cecil*. An unfinished fictional fragment preserved in a notebook; it is a project rather than a completed tale. [LL4]

### Poems and song-texts

- **1823; revised c. 1824–25:** *The Choice*. A long autobiographical elegiac poem on Percy Shelley’s death surviving in substantially different Hunt/Forman and journal manifestations; neither was published in Shelley’s lifetime. [LL4]
- **1825:** *On Reading Wordsworth’s Lines on Peel Castle*. A manuscript poem responding to Wordsworth’s “Elegiac Stanzas.” [LL4]
- **1826:** *To Jane—with the “Last Man”*. A brief presentation-poem fragment accompanying a copy of the novel. [LL4]
- **1830:** *Absence*. A lyric published anonymously in *The Keepsake*. [LL4]
- **1827; published 1830; revised 1835:** *A Dirge*. An elegiac lyric surviving in more than one manuscript and printed manifestation. [LL4]
- **1830:** *A Night Scene*. An anonymously published lyric; attribution, once tentative, is supported by documentary evidence. [LL4]
- **1831:** *The Death of Love*. A manuscript album poem first recovered and published by Betty T. Bennett. [LL4]
- **1832:** *Stanzas (“To love in solitude and mystery”)*. An anonymously published lyric whose attribution was proposed by Emily Sunstein and confirmed through manuscript evidence. [LL4]
- **1832:** *Stanzas (“I must forget thy dark eyes’ love-fraught gaze”)*. An anonymously published lyric attributed to Shelley from manuscript and bibliographical evidence. [LL4]
- **1833:** *Stanzas (“The tide of Time was at my feet”)*. A lyric surviving in a private manuscript manifestation. [LL4]
- **1834:** *La Vida es sueño*. A revised manifestation of “The tide of Time was at my feet,” not simply an unrelated second poem. [LL4]
- **1833:** *Tempo è ben di Morire (“Sadly borne across the waves”)*. A manuscript lyric with an Italian title. [LL4]
- **1833:** *Fair Italy! Still shines thy sun as bright*. A manuscript lyric. [LL4]
- **1838:** *Stanzas (“O come to me in dreams, my love!”)*. A lyric published in *The Keepsake*. [LL4]
- **1838:** *Stanzas (“How like a star you rose upon my life”)*. A lyric published in *The Keepsake*. [LL4]
- **1838:** *Stanzas (“O listen while I sing to thee”)*. A lyric later manifested as a separately issued musical canzonet. [LL4]
- **c. 1842–43:** *O Listen While I Sing to Thee: Canzonet*. A song-text set to music by Henry Hugh Pearson for voice with harp or piano; the composed musical object is Pearson’s, while Shelley made the words. [LL4; Bib]
- **1820:** *Proserpine Draft Fragment*. A verse fragment associated with the drama rather than an autonomous, securely finished poem. [LL4]
- **1824:** *God of the best, the brightest*. A poetic fragment placed by the scholarly edition among attributed writings; attribution is not certain. [LL4]
- **1831:** *Alas, I weep my life away*. A poetic fragment of uncertain status and attribution. [LL4]
- **1831:** *Struggle No More*. A poetic fragment of uncertain status and attribution. [LL4]
- **Date uncertain; published 1830:** *Song (“When I’m no more, this harp that rings”)*. Included in W. H. Lyles’s bibliography but rejected or omitted from A. A. Markley’s later scholarly poem canon; attribution therefore remains disputed. [Bib; LL4]
- **Date uncertain; published 1834:** *Ode to Ignorance*. Assigned to Shelley by Lyles but not accepted into Markley’s later edition; it should not be treated as securely hers. [Bib; LL4]
- **Date uncertain; published 1834:** *Fame*. Assigned to Shelley by Lyles but omitted from Markley’s later edition; attribution remains doubtful. [Bib; LL4]

### Essays, reviews, memoirs, and public interventions

- **1823:** *Madame d’Houtetôt*. A biographical and critical essay published in *The Liberal*. [LL; Bib]
- **1823:** *Giovanni Villani*. A historical-biographical essay published in *The Liberal*. [LL; Bib]
- **1823:** *Narrative of a Tour round the Lake of Geneva, and of an Excursion through the Valley of Chamouni*. A periodical reworking of material from the collaborative *Six Weeks’ Tour*, making it a new manifestation rather than an entirely new journey. [Bib]
- **1824:** *Recollections of Italy*. A travel and cultural essay published in *The London Magazine*. [LL; Bib]
- **1824:** *On Ghosts*. An essay that combines anecdote, sceptical inquiry, and speculation about supernatural experience. [LL; Bib]
- **1826:** *Defence of Velluti*. A newspaper intervention defending the castrato singer Giovanni Battista Velluti. [LL; Bib]
- **1826:** *The English in Italy*. A review-essay on English travel and residence in Italy. [LL; Bib]
- **1827:** *Review of The Italian Novelists*. A review-essay whose authorship is treated as probable rather than absolutely certain in the modern scholarly edition. [LL4; Bib]
- **1829:** *Illyrian Poems—Feudal Scenes*. A review-essay on Balkan poetry and history. [LL; Bib]
- **1829:** *Modern Italy*. A political and cultural review-essay. [LL; Bib]
- **1829:** *Review of The Loves of the Poets*. A literary review. [LL; Bib]
- **1829:** *Recollections of the Lake of Geneva*. A memoir-travel essay and later manifestation of Shelley’s Genevan materials. [LL; Bib]
- **1830:** *Review of Cloudesley; a Tale*. A review of William Godwin’s novel. [LL; Bib]
- **1830:** *Review of 1572: Chronique du temps de Charles IX*. A review of Prosper Mérimée’s historical novel. [LL; Bib]
- **1831:** *Memoirs of William Godwin*. A biographical introduction printed anonymously with Godwin’s *Caleb Williams*; it is distinct from Shelley’s later, much larger unfinished biography-project. [LL; Bib]
- **1831:** *Review of Thomas Moore’s Life and Death of Lord Edward Fitzgerald*. A historical-biographical review. [LL; Bib]
- **1831:** *Living Literary Characters, No. II: The Honourable Mrs Norton*. A contemporary literary portrait. [LL; Bib]
- **1831:** *Living Literary Characters, No. IV: James Fenimore Cooper*. A contemporary literary portrait. [LL; Bib]
- **1832:** *Review of The Bravo; a Venetian Story*. A review of James Fenimore Cooper’s novel. [LL; Bib]
- **1838:** *Modern Italian Romances, I*. The first part of an extended survey of contemporary Italian fiction. [LL4; Bib]
- **1838:** *Modern Italian Romances, II*. The second part of that survey; together the two instalments form one serial essay but appeared as separate periodical objects. [LL4; Bib]

### Unfinished biographies and historical fragments

- **1814; lost:** *Life of Louvet de Couvray*. An early biographical project recorded in Shelley’s journal but no longer extant; the intention survives more securely than the made text. [LL1]
- **c. 1814–16:** *The Theseus Fragment*. A brief historical-biographical exercise drawing on Plutarch; unfinished and untitled by Shelley, its present title is editorial. [LL4]
- **c. 1814–16:** *The Cyrus Fragment*. A brief life of Cyrus combined with notes on ancient civilizations; unfinished and editorially titled. [LL4]
- **c. 1819–20:** *The Samuel Fragment*. An unfinished historical or scriptural prose piece known only from manuscript. [LL4]
- **c. 1819–20:** *The Necessity of a Belief in the Heathen Mythology to a Christian*. An unfinished argumentative fragment; its provisional character resists treatment as a completed essay. [LL4]
- **1823:** *Life of Shelley*. A fragmentary first attempt to narrate Percy Bysshe Shelley’s life. [LL4]
- **1836–40:** *Life of William Godwin*. An authorized two-volume biography-and-correspondence project left unfinished; its surviving papers remain a work in process rather than a reconstructable final text. [LL4]
- **1821:** *Cry of War to the Greeks*. A political prose fragment placed among part-authored and attributed writings; its incompleteness and uncertain authorship prevent secure classification as Shelley’s finished manifesto. [LL4]

### Translations

- **1816:** *Correspondence, Maxims and Reflections of Louis XVI*. An unfinished English translation of letters then believed to be royal but now regarded as spurious; it survives only as a fragment. [LL4]
- **1817:** *Cupid and Psyche*. An unfinished translation from Apuleius’s Latin, preserved in fragmentary manuscript form. [LL4]
- **1818; published 1839:** *Relation of the Death of the Family of the Cenci*. A translation from an Italian manuscript account; made in connection with Percy Shelley’s *The Cenci* and later printed in Mary’s edition of his works. [LL4]
- **1818; lost:** *Mirra*. A translation-project from Vittorio Alfieri’s tragedy; contemporary evidence says Shelley began it, but no translation is known to survive. [LL1]
- **c. 1847–50:** *Inez de Medina*. A late, unfinished translation from Spanish or Spanish-derived material; the date is conjectural. [LL4]

### Editorial obras and textual stewardship

- **1824:** *Posthumous Poems of Percy Bysshe Shelley*. An edited collection through which Mary Shelley selected, transcribed, arranged, and introduced her husband’s unpublished and scattered poetry; legal pressure from Sir Timothy Shelley curtailed the project. [Bib; LL]
- **1831:** *Adventures of a Younger Son*. Edward John Trelawny’s novel as prepared with Mary Shelley’s editorial assistance; the exact extent of her intervention is not fully recoverable. [Bib]
- **1831:** *Frankenstein* in Bentley’s *Standard Novels*. A heavily revised authorial edition with a new introduction, forming a distinct manifestation of the novel rather than a routine reprint. [MWSN; Bib]
- **1835:** *Transfusion; or, The Orphan of Unwalden*. William Godwin Jr.’s posthumous novel, edited for publication by Mary Shelley. [Bib]
- **1839:** *The Poetical Works of Percy Bysshe Shelley*. A four-volume critical and memorial edition with Mary Shelley’s biographical and explanatory notes; a second, one-volume manifestation followed almost immediately. [Bib; LL]
- **1839–40:** *Essays, Letters from Abroad, Translations and Fragments, by Percy Bysshe Shelley*. A two-volume prose edition assembled and annotated by Mary Shelley; the title page bears 1840 although bibliographical evidence places publication in 1839. [Bib; LL]
- **1845:** *Essays, Letters from Abroad, Translations and Fragments* and *A Six Weeks’ Tour*. A revised one-volume editorial manifestation combining Percy Shelley’s prose with the collaborative travel narrative. [Bib]

### Journals, correspondence, and documentary writing

- **1814–44:** *Journals*. A discontinuous manuscript record jointly kept with Percy Shelley in its early phases and solely by Mary later; missing volumes and collaborative entries make it a documentary corpus rather than one stable, intentionally published work. [Journals]
- **1814–51:** *Letters*. A large dispersed body of correspondence, now gathered in a three-volume scholarly edition; individual letters are made texts, but the collected “Letters” is an editorial obra Shelley herself never designed. [Letters]
- **Before 1814; lost:** *Juvenile stories and “scribble”*. Shelley recalled making childhood stories, but the papers were reportedly lost during the 1814 elopement; no titles or secure dates survive, so this is evidence of creation without recoverable works. [LL1]
- **1815–16; lost:** *Journal for May 1815–July 1816*. A missing portion of the journal corpus; its former existence is documented, but its contents cannot responsibly be reconstructed. [LL1]

### Attributions rejected or unresolved

- **c. 1815:** *History of the Jews*. A satirical scriptural fragment in Shelley’s handwriting, but handwriting proves copying rather than authorship; the scholarly edition therefore classifies it as attributed, not secure. [LL4]
- **1815 or 1816:** *Address to the Duchess of Angoulême*. An unfinished political satire in Shelley’s hand; authorship remains uncertain, and Percy Shelley may have participated. [LL4]
- **1827:** *The Convent of Chaillot*. Although traditionally collected among Shelley’s tales, the latest comprehensive edition marks it as part-authored or attributed; her involvement is strong, sole authorship less so. [LL4]
- **1827:** *Review of The Italian Novelists*. Circumstantial and internal evidence strongly support Shelley’s authorship, but the review was anonymous and remains formally attributed rather than proven. [LL4]
- **1832:** *The Pole*. Often assigned to Mary Shelley because it appeared as by “the Author of Frankenstein,” but later scholarship identifies Claire Clairmont as its author; it is therefore not a Mary Shelley obra. [Bib]
