## Works by Ezekiel son of Buzi

### Prophetic obra

- **593–571 BCE, with later editorial development:** *The Ezekiel corpus (the Book of Ezekiel)*. A collection of visions, oracles, laments, allegories, riddles, protocols, and narrated sign-acts attributed to Ezekiel, rather than a conventional single-author book; its oldest material is commonly associated with the historical prophet, while the extent of subsequent expansion by disciples or editors remains disputed. The received book’s dated framework runs from 593 to 571 BCE, but the final form may be later ([Ezekiel 1:1–3; 29:17](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.1.1-3?lang=bi); [Oxford Bible Commentary](https://www.uecf.net/books/the-oxford-bible-commentary/chapter54.html); [Oxford Handbook of the Prophets](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/28016/chapter-abstract/211810315)).

### Constructed and inscribed sign-acts

- **593–592 BCE:** *Siege of Jerusalem on a brick*. An engraved brick or clay tablet depicting Jerusalem, supplemented by representations of siegeworks and an upright iron griddle: simultaneously drawing, model, installation, and prophetic sign. Whether all the siege equipment was physically modeled or engraved on the brick is disputed ([Ezekiel 4:1–3](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.4.1-3?lang=bi); [Keil and Delitzsch commentary](https://www.stepbible.org/?q=version%3DKD%40reference%3DEze.4); [Viberg, *Prophets in Action*](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1807528/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

- **593–592 BCE:** *Siege-ration bread*. A deliberately measured loaf or batch made from mixed grains and legumes, baked over cattle dung after Ezekiel objects to the originally prescribed human excrement. It is food, material sign, and component of a prolonged bodily performance rather than an independently titled work ([Ezekiel 4:9–17](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.4.9-17?lang=bi); [“Ezekiel and the Construction of Cultural Trauma”](https://doi.org/10.1515/opth-2022-0221)).

- **593–592 BCE:** *Divided hair of Jerusalem*. Ezekiel shaves his head and beard with a sword, weighs the hair into thirds, burns, cuts, and scatters it, then binds a few hairs in his garment before burning part of that remnant. The result is not one stable object but a deliberately destroyed and dispersed assemblage whose disappearance constitutes the sign ([Ezekiel 5:1–4](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.5.1-4?lang=bi); [Viberg, *Prophets in Action*](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1807528/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

- **Before 586 BCE; exact date unstated:** *Exile baggage and the breached wall*. Ezekiel prepares an exile’s bundle, carries it out publicly, digs through a wall, and departs with the load over his shoulder and his face covered. The baggage and breach are made objects, but their significance lies in the coordinated performance; scholarship therefore classifies the episode as a sign-act rather than a sculpture or architectural work ([Ezekiel 12:1–16](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.12.1-16?lang=bi); [St Andrews, “Is he not a Riddle Monger?”](https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/is-he-not-a-riddle-monger-%D7%94%D7%9C%D7%90-%D7%9E%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%81%D7%9C-%D7%9E%D7%A9%D7%81%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%9D-%D7%94%D7%95%D7%90-ezekiels-sign-acts/)).

- **Before 588 BCE; exact date unstated:** *The two roads and their signpost*. A marked pair of routes branching toward Jerusalem and Rabbah, with a directional sign at their fork, made to stage Nebuchadnezzar’s divinatory choice. It is unclear whether the text imagines an actual roadside construction, a drawn diagram, or a miniature demonstration ([Ezekiel 21:18–23](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.21.18-23?lang=bi); [Oxford Bible Commentary](https://www.scriptureanalysis.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/The-Oxford-Bible-Commentary.pdf); [Translation Insights and Perspectives](https://tips.translation.bible/story/translation-commentary-on-ezekiel-2119/)).

- **15 January 588 BCE, approximately:** *Record of the day the siege began*. Ezekiel is commanded to write down “this very day,” thereby making a dated memorial document that fixes Nebuchadnezzar’s commencement of the siege of Jerusalem. No separate manuscript survives, and the record may exist for us only through its incorporation into the book ([Ezekiel 24:1–2](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.24.1-2?lang=bi); [Asbury Bible Commentary, “Ezekiel’s Dates”](https://classic.biblegateway.com/resources/asbury-bible-commentary/iii-ezekiels-dates)).

- **15 January 588 BCE, approximately:** *The rusted cooking pot*. Ezekiel sets a pot on the fire, fills it with water, choice pieces of meat, and bones, boils its contents, empties it, and heats the vessel itself until its corrosion is consumed. This is a culinary assemblage engineered for destruction—a prophetic sign-act and enacted parable rather than a durable object ([Ezekiel 24:3–14](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.24.3-14?lang=bi); [Viberg, *Prophets in Action*](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1807528/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

- **After 586 BCE; exact date unstated:** *The two inscribed sticks*. Two pieces of wood, one inscribed for Judah and its associates and the other for Joseph/Ephraim and its associates, are brought together in Ezekiel’s hand as a sign of political reunification. Whether they literally become one object, merely appear as one during the performance, or were already joined in the literary form of the account is debated ([Ezekiel 37:15–28](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.37.15-28?lang=bi); [Harvard Theological Review, “The Literary Expansion of Ezekiel’s ‘Two Sticks’ Sign Act”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/literary-expansion-of-ezekiels-two-sticks-sign-act-ezekiel-371528/7ADC39B5C475AF16B0F29B63FAD334D7)).

### Embodied manifestations

- **593–592 BCE:** *Bearing the guilt of Israel and Judah*. Ezekiel lies bound first on his left side and then on his right, facing the siege image with an exposed arm. This resists designation as an obra separate from the siege installation: it is a time-based bodily sign whose reported duration and literal mode of performance remain disputed ([Ezekiel 4:4–8](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.4.4-8?lang=bi); [Viberg, *Prophets in Action*](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1807528/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

- **Before 586 BCE; exact date unstated:** *The trembling meal*. Ezekiel eats bread with quaking and drinks water with anxiety, making an ordinary meal into a performed forecast of siege and desolation. It leaves no autonomous object and therefore resists being called a “work” except as an embodied prophetic manifestation ([Ezekiel 12:17–20](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.12.17-20?lang=bi); [Viberg, *Prophets in Action*](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A1807528/FULLTEXT01.pdf)).

- **Before 588 BCE; exact date unstated:** *Groaning before the people*. Ezekiel publicly groans with a breaking heart and bitter grief, turning his body and breath into the sign of approaching catastrophe. It is an ephemeral action prophecy, not a surviving text or object ([Ezekiel 21:6–7](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.21.6-7?lang=bi); [Oxford Bible Commentary](https://www.scriptureanalysis.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/The-Oxford-Bible-Commentary.pdf)).

- **588 BCE:** *The sign of unperformed mourning*. After his wife’s death, Ezekiel refrains from conventional mourning rites and eats ordinary bread; the absence of action is itself made communicative. This especially resists the category of “work”: the narrative calls Ezekiel himself, rather than an artifact, the sign ([Ezekiel 24:15–27](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.24.15-27?lang=bi); [“Ezekiel and the Construction of Cultural Trauma”](https://doi.org/10.1515/opth-2022-0221)).

### Visionary project and protocol

- **573 BCE in the book’s chronology:** *The envisioned temple, city, and redistributed land*. A measured but unbuilt sacred complex, cultic constitution, territorial scheme, and renamed city described in Ezekiel 40–48: an architectural-political-religious project approaching a Gesamtkunstwerk because building, ritual, law, landscape, and social order cannot be separated. Ezekiel is commanded to communicate and write its design, but the text never confirms completion of a separate plan, and scholars dispute which portions belong to Ezekiel and which to later redaction ([Ezekiel 40–48](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.40.1?lang=bi); [St Andrews, “Ezekiel’s Temple Vision”](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/15994/Lyons_2018_LiftedMyEyes_Envisioning_AAM.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1); [Ezekiel 43:10–12](https://www.sefaria.org/Ezekiel.43.10-12?lang=bi)).
