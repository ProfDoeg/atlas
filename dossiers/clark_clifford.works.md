## Works by Clark McAdams Clifford

### Book-length obra

- **1991:** *Counsel to the President: A Memoir*. A political memoir made jointly with Richard C. Holbrooke; Clifford supplied the recollection and first-person authority, while Holbrooke was an acknowledged collaborator, so it is not accurately classified as Clifford’s solitary book.

### Articles and public interventions

- **May 18, 1964:** *H. S. T. at Eighty*. A commemorative article published in *The Democrat* and catalogued as Clifford’s work in the Library of Congress finding aid.

- **July 1969:** *A Viet Nam Reappraisal: The Personal History of One Man’s View and How It Evolved*. A *Foreign Affairs* article combining political argument, retrospective testimony, and public recantation of Clifford’s earlier support for the war; in that sense it acts partly as a manifesto for disengagement.

- **May 22, 1970:** *Set a Date in Vietnam. Stick to It. Get Out.* A *Life* magazine article advocating a fixed American withdrawal date; its imperative title and intended political effect make “manifesto” more exact than neutral essay.

### Reports and strategic memoranda

- **September 24, 1946:** *American Relations with the Soviet Union*. A classified presidential report, commonly called the Clifford–Elsey Report, prepared by Clifford and his assistant George M. Elsey. Contemporary archival descriptions credit both men, while historical accounts often say Elsey did much of the drafting; it therefore resists attribution to Clifford alone.

- **November 19, 1947:** *Confidential Memorandum for the President* (commonly the *Clifford–Rowe Memorandum* or *The Politics of 1948*). A campaign-strategy memorandum that functioned as a political protocol for Truman’s 1948 reelection effort. The Truman Library states that James H. Rowe Jr. originally drafted it, although Clifford submitted it and it subsequently circulated under both names.

- **1967:** *Report on the Israeli Attack on the USS Liberty* (commonly the *Clifford Report*). A presidential advisory report examining the attack on the American intelligence ship; references to it survive under an informal rather than securely fixed title, and the institutional process behind it makes individual authorship less straightforward than the eponym suggests.

- **March 1, 1977:** *Report by the President’s Personal Emissary to Greece, Turkey, and Cyprus to President Carter*. A mission report on Clifford’s Eastern Mediterranean diplomacy. It speaks in Clifford’s name but explicitly acknowledges the substantive participation of Matthew Nimetz, Nelson Ledsky, Gregory Treverton, Robert Hopper, and the relevant embassy staffs, so it is best treated as a collaborative diplomatic report.

### Presidential speechwriting as collaborative obra

- **1946–1950:** *Presidential speeches, messages, statements, and addresses drafted for Harry S. Truman*. This is a body of work rather than a stable list of individually attributable Clifford texts: the Truman Library preserves drafts and working files for several State of the Union messages, the March 12, 1947 address announcing what became known as the Truman Doctrine, numerous 1948 campaign speeches, the January 20, 1949 inaugural address introducing Point Four, and other presidential communications. Clifford himself emphasized that a presidential speechwriter was never the sole writer; these texts appeared under Truman’s authority and should not be silently reassigned to Clifford.

- **1948:** *Truman campaign-speech program*. A collective political and rhetorical project encompassing prepared addresses, platform remarks, research, itineraries, and editorial coordination during the “Whistlestop” campaign. The archive summarizes 293 Truman speeches and informal remarks, but it does not establish that Clifford personally wrote each one; the project is therefore a collaborative campaign obra, not 293 securely attributable Clifford works.

### Speeches, testimony, and interviews without a fixed-work boundary

- **1946–1997:** *Speeches, lectures, interviews, and other public statements*. The Library of Congress preserves these as a chronological archival corpus rather than a scholarly collected-works edition, including drafts, outlines, transcripts, proofs, and background matter. It resists conversion into a list of discrete authored works because folders may contain preparations, variant drafts, appearances, or statements assembled with staff rather than independently composed texts.

- **1883–1992 (Clifford material principally twentieth-century):** *Congressional testimony*. The Library of Congress describes a corpus of testimony, statements, drafts, transcripts, press conferences, and preparation files, with documented appearances continuing through May 6, 1992. Testimony is a performed legal-political manifestation rather than necessarily a self-contained authored text, and the archival date span includes inherited background material predating Clifford’s birth.

- **July 26, 1971:** *Oral History Interview with Clark M. Clifford*. A recorded and transcribed act of recollection created with an interviewer for the Truman Library; it is a jointly produced historical source rather than a conventional authored work.

### Archival residue rather than individually classifiable works

- **1942–1998:** *Correspondence, memoranda, legal papers, notes, drafts, and advisory writings*. Clifford made thousands of such documents, but the Library of Congress finding aid describes them by series, office, correspondent, or subject rather than certifying each as a titled work. Treating every retained sheet as an autonomous obra would confuse documentary survival with authorship; the corpus is nevertheless part of what he made.
