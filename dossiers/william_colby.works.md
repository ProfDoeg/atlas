## Works by William Egan Colby

### Books and memoirs

- **1978:** *Honorable Men: My Life in the CIA*. An autobiographical history made with writer Peter Forbath; the shared credit matters, since the published book is not securely attributable to Colby’s pen alone. Standard biographical references identify it as the first of Colby’s two books. ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/alic/special-collections/taylor-collection.html); [Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113))

- **1989:** *Lost Victory: A Firsthand Account of America’s Sixteen-Year Involvement in Vietnam*. A memoir and historical-political argument credited to Colby “with James McCargar”; contemporary reviewers consequently describe it both as Colby’s account and as a collaboratively produced book. ([Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/entertainment/books/1989/12/24/william-colbys-vietnam/2c931967-01d7-4b20-af46-142bdb98533d/); [National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/research/alic/special-collections/taylor-collection.html))

### Essays and policy articles

- **1973:** *Security in an Open Society*. An address to National Security Agency employees subsequently manifested as an essay in *Cryptologic Spectrum* 3, no. 4; it argues publicly for the place of secret intelligence within a free society and thus lies between institutional speech and authored article. ([NSA historical citation](https://media.defense.gov/2022/May/05/2002990644/-1/-1/0/CQ2021-01_050522.PDF))

- **1976:** *Intelligence Secrecy and Security in a Free Society*. A policy essay published in *International Security* 1, no. 2, pp. 3–14. Its intervention on secrecy and democratic control makes it closer to a public argument than to a descriptive intelligence report. ([JSTOR issue record](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i323245); [article record](https://doi.org/10.2307/2538496))

- **1975–1978; published 1978:** *Food Stamps for International Neighbors*. A foreign-policy essay proposing an analogy between domestic food assistance and international economic support; Princeton preserves its drafts, statistics, and notes, while *Worldview* published the resulting article in its January–February 1978 issue. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113); [Cambridge Core](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0084255900026383))

- **1980–1981:** *Intelligence in the 1980s*. A repeatedly revised address and policy article rather than one entirely stable text: archival records preserve its revisions and correspondence, while bibliographic evidence records manifestations in the *ABA Standing Committee Intelligence Report*, *The Information Society*, and CIA’s *Studies in Intelligence*. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113); [Reagan Library finding aid](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/finding_aids_pdfs/219342.pdf?VersionId=0MfXEGc1iA00DbbNaCQ8vxgjauz.QsaZ))

- **circa 1976–1990s:** *Articles by Colby*. An archival obra comprising five folders of additional published articles and drafts. The Princeton finding aid does not supply their individual titles, so this body of writing resists conversion into a reliable title-by-title bibliography without examination of the physical papers. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113))

### Testimony, speeches, and interviews

- **1970–May 1992:** *Congressional testimony*. A three-folder body of prepared statements and testimony: public acts made jointly by witness, committees, questioning, and transcription rather than conventional sole-authored works. Individual appearances include Colby’s 15 January 1975 testimony on reported CIA domestic activity and his 23 May 1975 testimony to the Senate intelligence investigation, but the archival catalogue does not itemize every title. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113); [National Archives JFK release index](https://www.archives.gov/research/jfk/release-2017-2018?order=Doc+Date&p=&page=124&sort=desc))

- **1974–1996:** *Speeches and public appearances*. A six-folder performative corpus preserved partly as Colby’s texts and partly only through programs or press clippings. It therefore includes both works he composed and manifestations that merely document acts of speaking; the finding aid does not permit a complete speech-by-speech reconstruction. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113))

- **1974–1994:** *Interviews with Colby*. An archival corpus of dialogic manifestations: Colby helped make the recorded or transcribed answers, but interviewers and editors also shaped each object, so the series resists classification as his sole-authored work. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113))

### Correspondence

- **1943–1996:** *Correspondence*. A seven-box epistolary obra ranging from routine notes to exchanges with political, intelligence, scientific, and journalistic figures. These are made texts, but not a deliberately constituted “work,” and Princeton’s alphabetical folder-level description cannot support naming every individual letter. ([Princeton finding aid](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113))

### Institutional project

- **1972–:** *CIA Museum*. A project rather than a personally fabricated collection: while serving as CIA executive director, Colby proposed an internal museum containing historically significant intelligence artifacts. The resulting institution is collaborative and continuing, so only its initiating intention—not the entire museum or every object in it—can reasonably be attributed to him. ([CIA Museum](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/))

### Sources

- Princeton University Library, Public Policy Papers, [*William E. Colby Papers, 1935–1996*, MC113](https://findingaids.library.upenn.edu/records/PRIN_MUDD_MC113). This archival finding aid supplies the controlling description of Colby’s correspondence, testimony, speeches, articles, interviews, drafts, and dates.

- National Archives, [John E. Taylor Collection bibliography](https://www.archives.gov/research/alic/special-collections/taylor-collection.html). Its author entries identify the two books and distinguish them from books about Colby.

- “William Egan Colby,” [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/us-history-biographies/william-egan-colby). This standard biographical account independently states that Colby wrote two books in retirement.

- JSTOR, [*International Security*, vol. 1, no. 2](https://www.jstor.org/stable/i323245). Issue-level bibliographic record for *Intelligence Secrecy and Security in a Free Society*.

- Cambridge University Press, [“Food Stamps for International Neighbors”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0084255900026383). Journal record establishing authorship, form, issue, and pagination.

- National Security Agency, [*Cryptologic Quarterly* historical study](https://media.defense.gov/2022/May/05/2002990644/-1/-1/0/CQ2021-01_050522.PDF). Bibliographic documentation for *Security in an Open Society*.

- Central Intelligence Agency, [CIA Museum](https://www.cia.gov/legacy/museum/). Institutional history crediting Colby with the museum’s initiating idea.

- Arnold R. Isaacs, [“William Colby’s Vietnam,” *The Washington Post*, 24 December 1989](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/entertainment/books/1989/12/24/william-colbys-vietnam/2c931967-01d7-4b20-af46-142bdb98533d/). Contemporary review documenting the full title and James McCargar’s collaborative credit.
