## Works by Frank Herbert

### Novels and novel-length obras

- **1955–1956:** *The Dragon in the Sea*. A psychological science-fiction novel first serialized as *Under Pressure* and also issued under the unauthorized title *21st Century Sub*; these are manifestations of one obra, not three novels. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank) [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1963–1965:** *Dune*. A science-fiction novel assembled from the substantially continuous magazine serials “Dune World” and “The Prophet of Dune”; the 1965 book is a revised fix-up rather than a mere reprint of them. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1965–1966:** *The Green Brain*. An ecological science-fiction novel expanded from the shorter text “Greenslaves.” [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1965–1966; revised 1978:** *Destination: Void*. A science-fiction novel whose first book manifestation developed “Do I Wake or Dream?”; Herbert substantially revised the novel in 1978. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank) [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1966:** *The Eyes of Heisenberg*. A science-fiction novel first serialized in a shorter manifestation as “Heisenberg’s Eyes.” [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1967–1968:** *The Heaven Makers*. A science-fiction novel first manifested as a magazine serial. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1967–1968:** *The Santaroga Barrier*. A speculative novel first manifested as a three-part magazine serial. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1969:** *Dune Messiah*. The second Dune novel, first serialized in *Galaxy* during July–November 1969. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1970:** *Whipping Star*. A ConSentiency novel first serialized in *If*; it extends the world previously used in “A Matter of Traces” and “The Tactful Saboteur.” [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1972:** *Soul Catcher*. A contemporary psychological and political novel, commonly distinguished from Herbert’s science fiction as his principal “mainstream” novel. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1972:** *The Godmakers*. A fix-up novel: Herbert altered and joined “You Take the High Road,” “Missing Link,” “Operation Haystack,” and “The Priests of Psi,” adding new material. The component stories consequently remain distinct earlier texts. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1972–1973:** *Hellstrom’s Hive*. A science-fiction novel first serialized as “Project 40.” [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1976:** *Children of Dune*. The third Dune novel, first manifested as a four-part *Analog* serial. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1977:** *The Dosadi Experiment*. A ConSentiency novel first serialized in *Galaxy*. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1979:** *The Jesus Incident*. A Pandora/Ship novel co-written with Bill Ransom; joint authorship is integral to the obra. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1980:** *Direct Descent*. A short novel developed from the 1954 story “Packrat Planet”; its independent book form is an expansion, not simply another edition of the story. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1981:** *God Emperor of Dune*. The fourth Dune novel. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1982:** *The White Plague*. A biological-disaster and political science-fiction novel. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1983:** *The Lazarus Effect*. A Pandora/Ship novel co-written with Bill Ransom. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1984:** *Heretics of Dune*. The fifth Dune novel. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1985:** *Chapterhouse: Dune*. The sixth and last Dune novel completed and published by Herbert; the variant spacing *Chapter House Dune* belongs to particular manifestations. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1986:** *Man of Two Worlds*. A science-fiction novel co-written with Brian Herbert. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1988:** *The Ascension Factor*. The final Pandora/Ship novel, co-written with Bill Ransom and published after Frank Herbert’s death; the posthumous date is a publication date, not a claimed composition date. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **Before 1965; published 2012:** *High-Opp*. A completed dystopian novel left unpublished during Herbert’s life; its project was finished even though its public manifestation was posthumous. [FUP](https://books.google.com/books?id=NaarjwEACAAJ)
- **1957; published 2013:** *Angels’ Fall*. A completed survival-adventure novel left unpublished during Herbert’s life; some catalogues print the title as *Angel’s Fall*. [FUP](https://books.google.com/books?id=NaarjwEACAAJ) [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **Between 1956 and 1965; published 2013:** *A Game of Authors*. A completed Cold War thriller published posthumously; the available bibliographic evidence does not establish a more exact composition date. [FUP](https://books.google.com/books?id=NaarjwEACAAJ)
- **Before 1965; published 2014:** *A Thorn in the Bush*. A completed, Mexico-derived mainstream novel left unpublished during Herbert’s life. [FUP](https://books.google.com/books?id=NaarjwEACAAJ)

### Poetry

- **1971:** *Carthage: Reflections of a Martian*. A poem published in the anthology *Mars, We Love You*. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **Dates various; collected 1992:** *Songs of Muad’Dib: The Poetry of Frank Herbert*. A posthumously assembled collection edited by Brian Herbert, largely gathering verse embedded in or associated with the Dune fiction; as a book-object it is an editorial manifestation, not a collection designed and completed by Frank Herbert himself. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)

### Story collections and retrospective manifestations

- **1970:** *The Worlds of Frank Herbert*. Herbert’s first short-fiction collection; the expanded 1971 American manifestation contains nine stories. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank) [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1973:** *The Book of Frank Herbert*. A collection containing ten stories, including three first publications. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1975:** *The Best of Frank Herbert*. A selected collection edited by Angus Wells; its later division into *1952–1964* and *1965–1970* represents two shortened manifestations, not new Herbert obras. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1980:** *The Priests of Psi*. A five-story collection, distinct as an arranged book-object although all its component fictions had appeared earlier. [FH-CS](https://schematax.org/schemata/herbert/schematax_herbert_texts-nondune.pdf)
- **1985:** *Eye*. An illustrated collection joining previously published fiction with the new “The Road to Dune”; Jim Burns’s illustrations make the volume a collaborative visual-verbal manifestation. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **2014:** *The Collected Stories of Frank Herbert*. A posthumously assembled collection advertised as the most complete gathering of Herbert’s short fiction, but not literally complete: it omits excerpts, “Nightmare Blues,” and the novel-length *The Dragon in the Sea*. [FH-CS](https://schematax.org/schemata/herbert/schematax_herbert_texts-nondune.pdf)
- **2016:** *Four Unpublished Novels*. A posthumous omnibus joining *High-Opp*, *Angels’ Fall*, *A Game of Authors*, and *A Thorn in the Bush*; the constituent novels are Herbert’s obras, while the omnibus arrangement is a later editorial manifestation. [FUP](https://books.google.com/books?id=NaarjwEACAAJ)
- **2016:** *Unpublished Stories*. A posthumous collection of thirteen stories recovered from Herbert’s papers; its title describes their status before this manifestation, not their status afterward. [FH-CS](https://schematax.org/schemata/herbert/schematax_herbert_texts-nondune.pdf)

### Nonfiction books, edited projects, and protocols

- **Circa 1952:** *Survival and the Atom*. A twenty-page pamphlet collecting Herbert’s newspaper articles about nuclear survival; surviving copies reportedly carry no date, so 1952 is bibliographic attribution rather than a date printed in the object. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1970:** *New World or No World*. An Earth Day anthology edited and introduced by Herbert; it is best understood as an activist editorial project—a text intended to intervene in ecological politics—rather than as a solely authored book. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1973:** *Threshold: The Blue Angels Experience*. A photographic book containing Herbert’s screenplay for a documentary about the Blue Angels; its union of photographs, documentary script, and film-related publication makes it a small collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than simply prose nonfiction. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank) [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1974:** *Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow*. An anthology edited collaboratively by Herbert, Bonnie L. Heintz, Donald A. Joos, and Jane Agorn McGee; Herbert also supplied its introduction. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1980:** *Without Me, You’re Nothing: The Essential Guide to Home Computers*. A practical computing handbook co-written with Max Barnard: principally a protocol—an instructional text—rather than narrative nonfiction. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1981:** *The Home Computer Handbook*. A revised British manifestation of *Without Me, You’re Nothing*, not a wholly independent obra. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1981:** *Nebula Winners Fifteen*. An anthology edited by Herbert; the selection and framing are his editorial obra, while the collected literary works remain those of their respective authors. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1987:** *The Maker of Dune: Insights of a Master of Science Fiction*. A posthumous collection of Herbert’s essays and interviews edited by Tim O’Reilly; its individual texts are Herbert’s, but the volume as arranged is O’Reilly’s editorial manifestation. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)
- **1988:** *The Notebooks of Frank Herbert’s Dune*. A posthumous miscellany edited by Brian Herbert from Frank Herbert’s notes; the underlying fragments are Herbert’s, while their selection and book-form resist attribution as a completed Frank Herbert work. [SFE](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank)

### Essays, introductions, and public arguments

- **1973:** *Introduction to Saving Worlds*. An introduction to Roger Elwood and Virginia Kidd’s ecological anthology, later reissued as *The Wounded Planet*. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1973:** *Introduction: Tomorrow’s Alternatives?*. An essay introducing *Frontiers 1: Tomorrow’s Alternatives*; it was later reprinted as “Doll Factory, Gun Factory.” [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1973:** *Introduction to Tomorrow, and Tomorrow, and Tomorrow*. Herbert’s framing essay for the collaboratively edited anthology. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1973:** *Listening to the Left Hand*. An essay published in *Harper’s Magazine*. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1974:** *Science Fiction and a World in Crisis*. An essay in Reginald Bretnor’s *Science Fiction: Today and Tomorrow*; some bibliographies omit “a” from the title, a minor title dispute among sources. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html) [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1976:** *Men on Other Planets*. An instructional essay in *The Craft of Science Fiction*. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1976–1977:** *The Sky Is Going to Fall*. An ecological argument published in *Seriatim* in 1977; a slightly different version had appeared as Herbert’s “Overview” newspaper column on July 4, 1976. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)
- **1977:** *The ConSentiency and How It Got That Way*. A fictional-world exposition published in *Galaxy*; bibliographies dispute whether to class it as essay or fiction, and that resistance is intrinsic to the text. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1980:** *Dune Genesis*. An account of the novel’s origins published in *Omni*. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)

- **1968:** *2068 A.D.*. A speculative newspaper article for the *San Francisco Sunday Examiner & Chronicle*. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1968:** *We’re Losing the Smog War*. The first half of a two-part newspaper investigation of air pollution. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1968:** *Lying to Ourselves About Air*. The second half of Herbert’s two-part newspaper investigation of air pollution. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1970:** *You Can Go Home Again*. An autobiographical newspaper feature concerning Herbert’s Northwestern childhood. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1976:** *Overview*. A newspaper column whose text partially overlaps “The Sky Is Going to Fall.” [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **1977:** *New Lifestyle to Fit a World of Shortages*. A newspaper article on ecological adaptation. [FH-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography)
- **Dates and titles incompletely established:** *Herbert’s wider newspaper corpus*. Herbert worked for newspapers for many years, but the scholarly working bibliography explicitly says the entire corpus is “nearly impossible to track down.” It therefore resists honest item-by-item enumeration: treating the seven frequently cited articles above as a complete journalism catalogue would manufacture completeness unsupported by the sources. [TO](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html)

- **1982:** *Frank Herbert Reads His God Emperor of Dune*. A performed selection of excerpts read by the author, not a complete audiobook or an independent novel. [DFAQ](https://groups.google.com/g/alt.fan.dune/c/W4msmQLDzMs)
- **Date uncertain; released posthumously in 1994:** *Heretics of Dune*. A recording of selected passages read by Herbert; its commercial release date does not establish when the performance was recorded. [DFAQ](https://groups.google.com/g/alt.fan.dune/c/W4msmQLDzMs)
- **1994:** *The Dune Audio Collection*. A posthumous compilation drawing from the four 1977–1979 Caedmon recordings; sources disagree on whether it preserves every part of the originals, and it omits their extensive Herbert liner notes. [DA](https://www.stason.org/TULARC/education-books/frank-herbert-dune/7-3-1-The-Dune-Audio-Collection.html)

### Unfinished or attribution-resistant projects

- **1957 onward:** *They Stopped the Moving Sands*. An intended magazine article about Oregon dune stabilization that Herbert researched but did not finish; it is a project whose investigation became generative material for *Dune*, not a completed published article. [Time](https://time.com/3841458/the-dune-abides/)
- **Before 1986:** *Dune 7 outline and notes*. An unfinished continuation project surviving in notes attributed to Herbert; later novels by Brian Herbert and Kevin J. Anderson claim to draw on that material, but those completed novels are not Frank Herbert obras, and the surviving project should not be silently converted into one. [FH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert)

### Sources

- **[SFE]** John Clute and David Langford, [“Herbert, Frank,” *The Encyclopedia of Science Fiction*](https://sf-encyclopedia.com/entry/herbert_frank). Its checklist distinguishes novels, collections, nonfiction, editorial work, variants, serializations, and posthumous manifestations.
- **[TO]** Timothy O’Reilly, [“Bibliography,” in *Frank Herbert*](https://www.oreilly.com/tim/herbert/biblio.html). This scholarly working bibliography is especially useful for relationships among stories, serials, revised novels, essays, and collections; it also explicitly warns that Herbert’s journalism cannot presently be enumerated completely.
- **[FH-B]** [“Frank Herbert bibliography”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert_bibliography). This derivative checklist cites Daniel J. H. Levack and Mark Willard’s standard bibliography and was used to reconcile dates and categories absent from the earlier O’Reilly list.
- **[FH-CS]** Alexander Schmitt, [*Frank Herbert: Fictional and Non-fictional Texts (Non-Dune)*](https://schematax.org/schemata/herbert/schematax_herbert_texts-nondune.pdf), version 1.0.1, 2023. This concordance derives its story data from the bibliography in *The Collected Stories of Frank Herbert* and maps stories to their collection manifestations.
- **[FUP]** Frank Herbert, [*Four Unpublished Novels: High-Opp, Angel’s Fall, A Game of Authors, A Thorn in the Bush*](https://books.google.com/books?id=NaarjwEACAAJ), WordFire Press, 2016. Publisher metadata establishes that the four completed novels were written before *Dune* and first published posthumously.
- **[DA]** Alan Schwartz et al., [“The Dune Audio Collection,” *alt.fan.dune* FAQ](https://www.stason.org/TULARC/education-books/frank-herbert-dune/7-3-1-The-Dune-Audio-Collection.html). Used for recording dates, formats, durations, connective text, liner notes, and the compilation’s uncertain completeness.
- **[DFAQ]** [*alt.fan.dune* Frequently Asked Questions](https://groups.google.com/g/alt.fan.dune/c/W4msmQLDzMs). Used to identify Herbert’s excerpt recordings and to distinguish them from complete audiobooks.
- **[Time]** Lev Grossman, [“The Dune Abides,” *Time*](https://time.com/3841458/the-dune-abides/), 2015. Used for the unfinished Oregon-dunes article project and its relation to *Dune*.
- **[FH]** [“Frank Herbert”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Herbert). Used only for the carefully qualified existence of Herbert’s unfinished “Dune 7” notes and the later authors’ claim to have used them.
