## Works by Alexander Graham Bell

### Experimental apparatus, inventions, and technological projects

- **c. 1858–1863:** *Speaking machine*. A mechanical simulation of the human vocal tract made by Bell and his brother Melville James Bell; the surviving descriptions do not establish a single stable design or exact date.
- **1873–1875:** *Ear phonautographs*. Experimental sound-recording instruments, including one incorporating a human middle ear supplied by Clarence J. Blake; they were research apparatus rather than commercial products.
- **1873–1876:** *Harmonic or multiple telegraph*. A project for sending several signals over one wire by tuned vibrating reeds. Bell built successive experimental forms, so it resists identification as one finished object.
- **1874–1876:** *Electric speaking telephone*. A system converting vocal vibrations into corresponding electrical variations and back into sound. Bell’s priority and the relation of his patent to Elisha Gray’s work remain historically disputed, although the Bell patents survived extensive litigation.
- **1875:** *Improvement in Transmitters and Receivers for Electric Telegraphs* (U.S. Patent 161,739). A patented harmonic-telegraph system and Bell’s first U.S. patent.
- **1875:** *Gallows telephone*. Bell and Thomas A. Watson’s experimental membrane-and-armature telephone; the name belongs to a reconstructed apparatus type rather than to a commercial model.
- **1876:** *Improvement in Telegraphy* (U.S. Patent 174,465). Bell’s foundational telephone patent, drafted broadly enough to encompass the transmission of speech by undulatory electrical current.
- **1876:** *Improvement in Telephonic Telegraph Receivers* (U.S. Patent 178,399). A patented receiver for tuned and telephonic telegraph systems.
- **1876:** *Improvement in Generating Electric Currents* (U.S. Patent 181,553). A patented magneto-electric generator intended to replace batteries in telegraphic circuits.
- **1876:** *Liquid-transmitter telephone*. The experimental instrument used on 10 March 1876 to transmit intelligible speech. It is an episode-specific apparatus, not the sole object properly called “Bell’s telephone.”
- **1877:** *Improvement in Electric Telegraphy* (U.S. Patent 186,787). A permanent-magnet telephone incorporating an iron diaphragm and signaling arrangement.
- **1878:** *Speaking Telephone* (U.S. Patent 201,488). A patented telephone apparatus refining transmission and reception.
- **1879:** *Improvement in Telephone Circuits* (U.S. Patent 220,791). A metallic-circuit arrangement intended to suppress inductive interference from neighboring wires.
- **1880:** *Photophone* (U.S. Patent 235,199). A wireless speaking system that modulated reflected light and recovered sound at a distant receiver.
- **1880:** *Photophone Transmitter* (U.S. Patent 235,496). A light-beam modulator jointly patented with Charles Sumner Tainter.
- **1880:** *Selenium Cell* (U.S. Patent 235,497). A photosensitive electrical component jointly patented with Tainter for radiophonic reception.
- **1880–1881:** *Spectrophone*. An experimental instrument that generated audible effects when modulated radiant energy was absorbed by matter; it appeared through demonstrations and papers rather than as one standardized machine.
- **1881:** *Electric Call-Bell* (U.S. Patent 238,833). A patented electrical signaling bell.
- **1881:** *Telephonic Receiver* (U.S. Patent 241,184). A receiver producing sound through electrically induced changes in conducting material.
- **1881:** *Photophonic Receiver* (U.S. Patent 241,909). A radiant-energy receiver jointly patented with Tainter.
- **1881:** *Induction balance and bullet probe*. An electromagnetic detector adapted during the effort to locate the bullet in President James A. Garfield’s body. The attempted examination failed, partly because of interference from the metal bed, but Bell subsequently produced a successful detector form.
- **1881–1886:** *Graphophone project*. A collaborative body of recording experiments by Bell, Chichester A. Bell, and Tainter, replacing Edison’s tinfoil with incised wax and developing associated recording and playback mechanisms.
- **1881:** *Wax-cylinder recording apparatus deposited at the Smithsonian*. An altered Edison phonograph and copper recording sealed as evidence of the Volta Laboratory Associates’ work; its hybrid construction makes individual authorship difficult to separate.
- **1882:** *Vacuum jacket for artificial respiration*. A proposed negative-pressure respiratory apparatus. It remained principally a project and published protocol rather than a medically adopted machine.
- **1886:** *Reproducing Sounds from Phonograph Records* (U.S. Patent 341,212). A graphophone-related patent jointly credited to Bell, Chichester A. Bell, and Tainter.
- **1886:** *Transmitting and Recording Sounds by Radiant Energy* (U.S. Patent 341,213). A joint patent combining sensitive fluid jets, radiant-energy modulation, and photographic recording.
- **1886:** *Recording and Reproducing Speech and Other Sounds* (U.S. Patent 341,214). A joint graphophone patent concerning engraved sound records and their reproduction.
- **1890s:** *Audiometer*. An apparatus for measuring hearing acuity. Bell’s work contributed an experimental instrument and method rather than the only device historically known by that name.
- **1890s–1910s:** *Tetrahedral construction system*. A modular structural principle developed through thousands of cells, frames, and kites. It is best understood as a sustained project or technological language, not one object.
- **1899:** *Spool kites and kites with radial wings*. Experimental kite configurations presented to the National Academy of Sciences.
- **1903:** *Frost King*. A large tetrahedral kite built at Beinn Bhreagh; it belongs to a collaborative experimental program rather than being Bell’s unaided handiwork.
- **1904:** *Aerial Vehicle* (U.S. Patent 757,012). A patented tetrahedral flying structure.
- **1904:** *Aerial Vehicle or Other Structure* (U.S. Patent 770,626). A patent extending tetrahedral construction beyond a single aircraft form.
- **1907:** *Connection Device for the Frames of Aerial Vehicles and Other Structures* (U.S. Patent 856,838). A patented joint for assembling tetrahedral structural cells.
- **1907:** *Cygnet I*. A giant tetrahedral kite that briefly carried Thomas Selfridge above Bras d’Or Lake while under tow. It was a collective construction of Bell’s Beinn Bhreagh team.
- **1907–1909:** *Aerial Experiment Association aircraft program*. A collaborative project initiated and financed by Bell and Mabel Bell and executed with Frederick W. Baldwin, Glenn H. Curtiss, J. A. D. McCurdy, and Thomas Selfridge; its named machines were *Red Wing*, *White Wing*, *June Bug*, and *Silver Dart*, so none should be assigned to Bell alone.
- **1908:** *White Wing*. An Aerial Experiment Association biplane incorporating wheeled landing gear and lateral-control surfaces; a collective obra, not a sole Bell invention.
- **1908:** *June Bug*. An Aerial Experiment Association aircraft designed principally by Curtiss and the association, included here because it was a manifestation of Bell’s collaborative aeronautical project rather than his individual work.
- **1909:** *Silver Dart*. The Aerial Experiment Association aircraft that made the first controlled powered flight in Canada; McCurdy flew it, and its authorship is collective.
- **1908–1922:** *Hydrodrome or hydrofoil project*. Bell and Frederick W. Baldwin’s long experimental program in high-speed watercraft. Numerous models and craft appeared, making the project—not any single hull—the encompassing work.
- **c. 1911:** *Dhonnas Beag*. An early self-propelled Bell–Baldwin hydrofoil experimental craft; its Gaelic name means “little devil.”
- **1911:** *Flying Machine* (U.S. Patent 1,010,842). A collective Aerial Experiment Association patent naming Bell, Baldwin, McCurdy, Curtiss, and the estate of Selfridge.
- **1911:** *Flying Machine* (U.S. Patent 1,011,106). A second collective Aerial Experiment Association patent covering aircraft construction and control.
- **1913:** *Flying Machine* (U.S. Patent 1,050,601). Bell’s patent for an aircraft lateral-control arrangement.
- **1917–1919:** *HD-4*. Bell and Baldwin’s hydrofoil craft, which established a world water-speed record in 1919. It is a collaborative machine built by the Beinn Bhreagh laboratory organization.
- **1922:** *Hydrodrome, Hydroaeroplane, and the Like* (U.S. Patents 1,410,874–1,410,877). Four related patents jointly granted to Bell and Baldwin for hydrofoil sets, hulls, propulsion, and control arrangements.

### Pedagogical texts, protocols, and interventions concerning deaf education

- **1872:** *Visible Speech as a Means of Communicating Articulation to Deaf-Mutes*. A pedagogical article explaining the application of his father’s notation to speech instruction.
- **1872:** *Establishment for the Study of Vocal Physiology*. A sixteen-page prospectus for Bell’s teaching practice; part advertisement, part pedagogical protocol.
- **1872:** *On the Nature and Uses of Visible Speech*. An eight-page explanatory pamphlet extracted from an earlier periodical appearance.
- **1872:** *Articulation for Deaf-Mutes*. A conference paper and recorded discussion on oral instruction.
- **1874:** *Lip-Reading and the Education of Semi-Mutes*. A published abstract of a pedagogical paper.
- **1874:** *Recording the Vibrations of the Human Voice*. A reported address linking experimental inscription of sound to speech teaching.
- **1874:** *The Semi-deaf Pupil*. A short convention speech.
- **1874:** *Visible Speech*. A six-page convention speech.
- **1874:** *Upon the Education of the Sense of Hearing in the Semi-Deaf*. A short pedagogical address.
- **1877:** *Assisting the Hearing with Sound Lenses*. A published portion of a letter proposing acoustic assistance.
- **1877:** *On the Principles and Applications of the System of Visible Speech Invented by His Father, Mr. Alexander Melville Bell*. An address surviving in the proceedings only by title.
- **1883:** *Upon a Method of Teaching Language to a Very Young Congenitally Deaf Child*. A pedagogical case study.
- **1883:** *Upon the Formation of a Deaf Variety of the Human Race*. A substantial demographic memoir arguing that intermarriage among deaf people might create a hereditary “variety.” Its status as scientific work cannot be separated from its eugenic premises and harmful social program.
- **1884:** *Fallacies Concerning the Deaf, and the Influence of These Fallacies in Preventing the Amelioration of Their Condition*. A polemical paper and intervention in deaf education policy.
- **1884:** *Deafness in White Cats*. Two scientific letters on heredity and deafness.
- **1884:** *Presidential Address and Discussions at the Convention of Articulation Teachers of the Deaf*. A recorded body of convention speech rather than a single independently composed essay.
- **1884:** *Speeches at the Fifth National Conference of Principals and Superintendents of Institutions for Deaf-Mutes*. Proceedings preserve a body of interventions whose individual titles were not fixed in Osborne’s bibliography.
- **1884:** *Deaf-Mute Instruction in Relation to the Work of the Public Schools*. A policy address.
- **1884:** *Deaf Classes in Connection with the Public Schools*. A published abstract of an address and discussion.
- **1885:** *Is There a Correlation between Defects of the Senses?* A scientific article.
- **1885:** *The Wisconsin Bill Relating to the Instruction of Deaf-Mutes*. A letter intervening in proposed education law.
- **1886:** *The Deaf-Mutes of Martha’s Vineyard*. A published abstract with supplementary notes.
- **1887:** *The Value of Reading for the Deaf*. A summary of an address.
- **1888:** *California Resolution*. A conference intervention whose brief title and proceedings context resist classification as an autonomous work.
- **1888:** *On Reading as a Means of Teaching Language to the Deaf*. A pedagogical conference paper.
- **1888:** *Census of the Deaf*. A conference intervention.
- **1888:** *Address on Gallaudet*. A conference address; Osborne records no fuller title.
- **1888:** *Facts and Opinions Relating to the Deaf from America*. A 195-page evidentiary compilation prepared for the British Royal Commission, closer to a research dossier than a conventional authored book.
- **1889:** *The Census of 1890—Suggestions Concerning the Deaf, Blind, etc.* A published policy letter.
- **1890:** *Professor A. Graham Bell’s Studies of the Deaf*. A letter answering criticism of Bell’s statements about marriage among deaf people.
- **1891:** *The Training of Teachers at Gallaudet College*. Congressional testimony opposing an appropriation; a political intervention rather than a neutral pedagogical description.
- **1891:** *Marriage*. An address to deaf students concerning marriage and heredity.
- **1891:** *Reading Before Writing*. A letter on teaching sequence.
- **1891:** *Origin of the American Association to Promote the Teaching of Speech to the Deaf*. An institutional history delivered as a lecture.
- **1891:** *The Thorax and Larynx*. An anatomical lecture and instructional protocol.
- **1891:** *The Pharynx and Mouth in Their Relation to Speech*. An anatomical lecture.
- **1891:** *The Functions of the Epiglottis and Soft Palate*. A lecture-demonstration.
- **1891:** *Methods of Studying the Mechanism of Speech*. An experimental and pedagogical protocol.
- **1891:** *Visible Speech, as Taught to the Deaf*. An illustrated lecture.
- **1891:** *Consonants*. A lecture on speech production.
- **1891:** *Vowels, Glides and Combinations*. A lecture on speech production.
- **1892:** *The Condition of Articulation Teaching in American Schools for the Deaf*. A presidential address incorporating charts and tables.
- **1892:** *Condition of Articulation Teaching in America*. A conference report.
- **1892:** *Upon the Classification of Methods of Instructing the Deaf*. A classificatory paper.
- **1892:** *The Progress Made in Teaching Deaf Children to Read Lips and Talk, in the United States and Canada*. A conference paper also published in *Science*.
- **1894:** *Utility of Signs*. A paper opposing reliance on sign language in instruction; later paired with *The Question of Sign-Language*.
- **1894:** *The Question of Sign-Language*. A pedagogical polemic later republished with *Utility of Signs*.
- **1894:** *Valedictory Address*. A convention address without a more specific fixed title.
- **1894–1895:** *Growth of the Oral Method of Instructing the Deaf*. An anniversary address expanded with an appendix and statistical charts.
- **1895:** *A Few Thoughts Concerning Parents’ Associations*. An organizing address delivered at the establishment of an association of parents of deaf children.
- **1897:** *The Mystic Oral School: An Argument in Its Favor*. Legislative advocacy published with a statistical appendix.
- **1897:** *Education of the Deaf*. An address to the National Education Association.
- **1899:** *Presidential Address to the Sixth Summer Meeting of the American Association to Promote the Teaching of Speech to the Deaf*. An institutional manifesto explaining the association’s origins, purposes, and program, accompanied by statistics.
- **1900:** *A Philanthropist of the Last Century Identified as a Boston Man (Francis Green)*. A historical paper identifying an early advocate of deaf education.
- **1900:** *Sketch of the Life of Francis Green, with Extracts from His Unpublished Autobiography*. A biographical article partly assembled from another person’s manuscript.
- **1900–1905:** *Historical Notes Concerning the Teaching of Speech to the Deaf*. A serial historical study published over five years, making it one continuing project rather than several separately titled works.
- **1906:** *Special Report upon the Deaf*. An official report prepared as special agent for the United States Twelfth Census.
- **1906–1908:** *Special Report upon the Deaf: Abstract of and Comments on the Returns of the Twelfth Census*. A serialized reinterpretation of the census report.
- **1909:** *French Pronunciation in the Melville Bell Symbols*. A technical notation paper.
- **1911:** *The Mechanism of Speech*. A collected lecture-book with the appended paper *Vowel Theories*; Osborne notes that it largely remanifests material already represented elsewhere in his bibliography.
- **1912:** *What Is the Deaf Child Sent to School For?* A published educational address.
- **1917:** *The Growth of the Oral Method in America*. An expanded commemorative address.
- **1917:** *Graphical Studies of Marriages of the Deaf in America*. A 259-page statistical atlas whose charts advance Bell’s hereditarian program; it is simultaneously data work, visual argument, and eugenic intervention.

### Scientific papers and addresses on sound, telephony, and radiant energy

- **1876:** *Researches in Telephony*. Bell’s first formal scientific communication on the telephone.
- **1877:** *Telephonic Method of Transmitting Speech*. A brief published abstract.
- **1877:** *On the Telephone*. A published lecture delivered at the Essex Institute.
- **1877:** *The Telephone* (Boston Society of Arts). An address surviving in the proceedings only by title.
- **1877:** *On Recent Experiments in Telephony*. A British Association lecture surviving in its report only by title.
- **1877:** *Researches in Electric Telephony*. A substantial lecture published by the Society of Telegraph Engineers.
- **1877:** *The Telephone* (London Society of Arts). A journal article derived from Bell’s presentation.
- **1878:** *On the Determination of Equipotential Curves and Surfaces by the Telephone*. A scientific communication recorded only by title.
- **1879:** *Upon Residual Induction*. A scientific communication recorded only by title.
- **1879:** *Upon the Methods of Exploring the Field of Induction of Flat Spirals*. A scientific communication recorded only by title.
- **1879:** *Upon Binaural Audition*. A scientific communication initially recorded only by title.
- **1880:** *Binaural Audition*. An abstract with accompanying tables.
- **1880:** *On the Production and Reproduction of Sound by Light*. The principal experimental paper announcing speech transmission through modulated light.
- **1880:** *The Photophone*. A lecture-paper describing the light telephone; its appearances in different journals are manifestations of the same underlying work.
- **1881:** *The Production of Sound by Radiant Energy—The Spectrophone*. An experimental paper on audible effects produced through intermittent radiation.
- **1881:** *Upon a Modification of Wheatstone’s Microphone and Its Applicability to Radiophonic Researches*. A technical communication published in more than one venue.
- **1884:** *A Possible Method of Electrical Communication between Vessels at Sea*. A speculative technical paper proposing induction-based maritime communication.
- **1885:** *Open Letter to the Hon. A. M. Garland, Attorney-General of the United States*. A nineteen-page public defense against the federal challenge to Bell’s patents; it acts as legal advocacy rather than detached description.
- **1908:** *The Bell Telephone: The Deposition of Alexander Graham Bell in the Suit Brought by the United States to Annul the Bell Patents*. Printed legal testimony, not a book conceived as literature or a scientific monograph.
- **1911:** *Address on Early Telephone Days*. A historical address to the Telephone Pioneers of America.
- **1912:** *The Telephone*. A short popular-mechanics article.
- **1916:** *Address on Early Telephone Days*. A fortieth-anniversary address distinct from the 1911 speech despite the shared title.
- **1922:** *Prehistoric Telephone Days*. An autobiographical article on the development of Bell’s ideas before the practical telephone.

### Medical and surgical proposals

- **1882:** *Upon an Apparatus for Determining without Pain to the Patient the Position of a Projectile of Lead or Other Metal in the Human Body*. A technical paper describing Bell’s electromagnetic probe.
- **1882–1883:** *Upon the Electrical Experiments to Determine the Location of the Bullet in the Body of the Late President Garfield; and Upon a Successful Form of Induction Balance for the Painless Detection of Metallic Masses in the Human Body*. A combined experimental report and apparatus description.
- **1882:** *Upon a Proposed Method of Producing Artificial Respiration by Means of a Vacuum Jacket*. A published abstract of an unrealized or incompletely realized medical apparatus project.
- **1903:** *Radium and Cancer*. A letter proposing localized radium treatment; it is a speculative medical protocol, not evidence that Bell conducted a clinical treatment.

### Heredity, eugenics, longevity, and sheep-breeding projects

- **1899:** *On the Development by Selection of Supernumerary Mammae in Sheep*. A paper reporting Bell’s selective-breeding experiment.
- **1901:** *Conditions Affecting the Fertility of Sheep and the Sex of Their Offspring*. A scientific communication based on the Beinn Bhreagh flock.
- **1904:** *Sheep Catalogue of Beinn Bhreagh, Victoria County, Nova Scotia*. A twenty-two-page herd catalogue: part husbandry record, part experimental dataset.
- **1904:** *The Multi-Nippled Sheep of Beinn Bhreagh*. A brief scientific report.
- **1908:** *A Few Thoughts Concerning Eugenics*. A eugenic program published in *National Geographic*; its category is ideological intervention as much as scientific text.
- **1909–1922:** *Genealogical Record Office longevity project*. A data-gathering institution and unfinished research program established to investigate heredity and long life; the archive is a collective record system rather than a bounded authored work.
- **1912:** *Sheep-Breeding Experiments on Beinn Bhreagh*. A report extracted from the laboratory’s internal *Beinn Bhreagh Recorder*.
- **1914:** *How to Improve the Race*. An explicitly eugenic essay whose prescriptive social purpose makes it closer to a manifesto than a neutral research paper.
- **1914:** *Sex-Determination in Sheep*. A compilation made from Bell’s records; because an editor compiled and published it, Bell’s authorship is documentary rather than straightforwardly literary.
- **1918:** *The Duration of Life and Conditions Associated with Longevity*. A statistical study derived from the Hyde genealogy.
- **1919:** *Who Shall Inherit Long Life?* A hereditarian popular-science article arguing that natural processes could increase human vigor.

### Aeronautical and invention writings

- **1896:** *Letter on the Successful Flight of S. P. Langley’s Aerodrome*. A published eyewitness communication concerning Langley’s experimental aircraft, not an account of a machine made by Bell.
- **1899:** *Spool Kites and Kites with Radial Wings*. A scientific communication accompanying Bell’s kite experiments.
- **1903:** *The Tetrahedral Principle in Kite Structure*. An illustrated technical exposition of Bell’s modular construction system.
- **1907:** *Aerial Locomotion*. A substantial illustrated survey and statement of Bell’s aeronautical project.
- **1910:** *Historical Addresses on Presenting the Langley Medal to Wilbur and Orville Wright*. Ceremonial addresses that also construct a history of aviation.
- **1913:** *Address on Presenting the Langley Medal to Gustave Eiffel*. A ceremonial scientific address.
- **1913:** *Address on Presenting the Langley Medal to Glenn H. Curtiss*. A ceremonial scientific address.
- **1916:** *Preparedness for Aerial Defense*. A military-policy address advocating aviation readiness.
- **1885:** *Preventing Collisions with Icebergs in a Fog*. A proposed technological solution published as a short illustrated paper.
- **1912 onward:** *Simple Experiments*. A continuing series in *The Volta Review*, not a single fully delimited text; Osborne identifies its beginning but does not supply a terminal installment.
- **1914:** *Discovery and Invention*. A popular essay on inventive method.
- **1914:** *Why Does a Cream Pitcher Have a Projecting Lip?* An illustrated investigation using an ordinary object to frame a problem in fluid behavior.
- **1917:** *Prizes for the Inventor: Some of the Problems Awaiting Solution*. A popular essay and programmatic list of unsolved technical problems.

### Institutions, periodicals, archives, and collaborative bodies of work

- **1872:** *School of Vocal Physiology and Mechanics of Speech*. Bell’s Boston teaching institution: an educational project and social organization rather than a text or apparatus.
- **1877:** *Bell Telephone Company*. A corporation founded by Gardiner Hubbard, Thomas Sanders, Bell, and Watson to turn the telephone into a working communications system; it cannot be treated as Bell’s sole creation.
- **1880:** *Volta Laboratory*. A research organization established with Bell’s Volta Prize funds and developed with Chichester A. Bell and Charles Sumner Tainter.
- **1880s:** *Volta Laboratory sealed deposits*. Documentary and material ensembles placed at the Smithsonian to establish experimental priority for the photophone and graphophone; they are archival acts as well as scientific objects.
- **1883:** *Science*. Bell and Gardiner Hubbard helped establish and finance the periodical, but editorial and institutional authorship was collective; calling the journal a Bell “work” without qualification would overstate his role.
- **1887:** *Volta Bureau*. An institution founded and endowed by Bell for collecting and disseminating information concerning deaf education.
- **1890:** *American Association to Promote the Teaching of Speech to the Deaf*. Bell helped organize and sustained this advocacy body, now the Alexander Graham Bell Association for the Deaf and Hard of Hearing; it is an institutional project with many makers.
- **1898–1903:** *National Geographic Society presidency and magazine transformation*. Bell’s program enlarged membership and tied membership to receipt of the magazine, but the resulting institution and publication remain collective works.
- **1907–1909:** *Aerial Experiment Association*. A time-limited collaborative research organization proposed by Mabel Bell and led by Bell; its project joined laboratory, aircraft production, flight performance, photography, and publication.
- **1909–1922:** *Beinn Bhreagh Recorder*. A multivolume internal laboratory chronicle assembled from dictation, observations, photographs, tables, and staff contributions. It is a collaborative archival Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a conventionally authored book.
- **1879–1922:** *Scientific notebooks, journals, and experimental records*. Bell’s extensive manuscript record constitutes an open-ended body of work documenting daily investigations; the Library of Congress finding aid does not support treating every page or notebook as a separately titled obra.
