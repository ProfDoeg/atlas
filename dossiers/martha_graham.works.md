### Choreographic obras and projects, 1940–1959

- **1940:** *Letter to the World*. Dance-theatre portrait of Emily Dickinson to Hunter Johnson, with designs by Lauterer and Gilfond. Its spoken poetry is borrowed textual material, not a literary work authored by Graham. [LOC chronology]
- **1940:** *El Penitente*. Choreographic dance-drama to Horst, originally designed by Lauterer and later redesigned by Noguchi. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1941:** *Punch and the Judy*. Choreographic dance-drama to Robert McBride, using a text by Edward Gordon Craig; authorship is therefore collaborative and distributed across media. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1942:** *Land Be Bright*. Choreographic work to Arthur Kreutz, with sets and costumes by Charlotte Trowbridge. [LOC chronology]
- **1943:** *Deaths and Entrances*. Choreographic dance-drama to Johnson, inspired by the Brontë sisters. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1943:** *Salem Shore*. Choreographic work to Nordoff, with designs by Lauterer and Gilfond. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1944:** *Appalachian Spring*. Commissioned dance-drama to Copland with a Noguchi setting; dance, score, décor, costume, and dramatic scenario form a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a solely authored object. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1944:** *Hérodiade*. Choreographic duet to Hindemith with a Noguchi setting. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1944:** *Imagined Wing*. Choreographic work to Milhaud with Noguchi sets and Gilfond costumes. [LOC chronology]
- **1946:** *Dark Meadow*. Choreographic ritual to Chávez, with Noguchi sets, Gilfond costumes, and Rosenthal lighting. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1946:** *Cave of the Heart*. Choreographic treatment of Medea to Samuel Barber, with Noguchi, Gilfond, and Rosenthal as collaborators. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1947:** *Errand into the Maze*. Choreographic duet to Gian Carlo Menotti with Noguchi décor and Rosenthal lighting. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1947:** *Night Journey*. Choreographic retelling of Oedipus from Jocasta’s perspective, to William Schuman with a Noguchi setting. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1948:** *Diversion of Angels*. Abstract choreographic work to Norman Dello Joio; Noguchi’s original setting was removed after the premiere. [LOC chronology; Court]
- **1950:** *Eye of Anguish*. Choreographic work included in the legally examined Graham repertoire; documentation is much thinner than for the canonical works. [Court]
- **1950:** *Judith*. Choreographic dance-drama to Schuman with a Noguchi setting; it must be distinguished from Graham’s unrelated 1980 *Judith*. Some sources date its public premiere to 1951. [Court; Chronology]
- **1951:** *The Triumph of St. Joan*. Choreographic work to Dello Joio. [Chronology]
- **1952:** *Canticle for Innocent Comedians*. Choreographic cycle to Thomas Ribbink. It was later lost or fragmentary and has generated reinterpretations, which should not be mistaken for Graham’s intact original. [Court; Chronology]
- **1954:** *Ardent Song*. Choreographic work to Alan Hovhaness. [Court; Chronology]
- **1955:** *Seraphic Dialogue*. Choreographic reworking of the Joan of Arc material to Dello Joio, with a Noguchi setting. [Court; Chronology]
- **1958:** *Clytemnestra*. Graham’s full-evening dance-drama to Halim El-Dabh, with Noguchi sets and costumes by Graham and Helen McGehee. It is a particularly clear Gesamtkunstwerk. [Court; Chronology]
- **1958:** *Embattled Garden*. Choreographic dance-drama to Carlos Surinach with a Noguchi setting. [Court; Chronology]
- **1959:** *Episodes: Part I*. Graham’s portion of a jointly conceived program with George Balanchine, to Anton Webern; the two choreographers ultimately made separate halves, so *Episodes* is a composite event rather than a single jointly choreographed work. [Court; Chronology]

### Movement system, institutions, and collective obras

- **1926–1991:** *Graham technique*. A codified movement system organized around breath, contraction and release, spiraling, weight, and the expressive torso. It is simultaneously protocol, pedagogical practice, and embodied body of work rather than a single fixed text. [LOC collection; Movement-system chronology]
- **1926–1991:** *Martha Graham Dance Company*. The performing institution Graham founded as the Martha Graham Concert Group and developed into a durable collective obra; its dancers and collaborators materially participated in making the repertory. [LOC chronology; Company history]
- **1926–1991:** *Martha Graham School*. The teaching institution through which Graham’s movement protocol was created, transmitted, and revised. Treating it only as an organization misses its character as a continuing pedagogical project. [Company history]
- **1930:** *Dance Repertory Theatre*. Collective project formed with Doris Humphrey, Charles Weidman, and Helen Tamiris to provide an American platform for modern dance; it was an institutional intervention rather than a Graham-authored ballet. [LOC chronology]
- **1965 onward:** *Graham repertory for Batsheva Dance Company*. A directorial and transmission project begun when Graham became Batsheva’s first artistic director; individual Graham works remained distinct obras, while their Israeli staging constituted new manifestations. [Batsheva history]

### Writings and documentary texts

- **1938:** *This Modern Dance*. Article published in London’s *Dancing Times*; an expository and polemical text about modern dance, approaching manifesto without being formally titled as one. [LOC chronology]
- **1941:** *Martha Graham: Sixteen Dances in Photographs*. Collaborative photographic book with Barbara Morgan. The dances are Graham’s, the photographs Morgan’s, and the volume itself is therefore a jointly made documentary manifestation rather than a solely authored Graham book. [LOC collection]
- **1973:** *The Notebooks of Martha Graham*. Edited publication of Graham’s working notes, choreographic sketches, images, and reflections. These are project documents and generative protocols, not verbal substitutes for the dances. [WorldCat record; publisher record]
- **1985:** *Martha Graham*. Illustrated autobiographical and artistic survey credited to Graham; its status lies between artist’s book, retrospective document, and mediated self-portrait. [NYPL bibliographic record]
- **1991:** *Blood Memory*. Autobiography completed in draft shortly before Graham’s death and published posthumously with editorial assistance from Ron Protas. It is a memoir, but its mediated production and posthumous publication complicate the idea of a wholly self-contained authorial text. [NYPL archive; Britannica]

### Attribution, survival, and completeness

- **1926–1991:** *The Graham repertory as a body of work*. Authorities variously state that Graham made 180 or 181 choreographic works. The discrepancy arises partly from revisions, excerpts, alternate titles, student choreography, theatrical contributions, reconstructions, and unfinished projects; the list above therefore names documented things without pretending that every title corresponds to one autonomous, equally preserved obra. [LOC collection; Company history]
- **1926–1991:** *Lost and reconstructed dances*. Much of Graham’s early choreography was not fully notated or filmed, and Graham initially resisted recording live dance. Later reconstructions are evidence-based manifestations made with dancers, coaches, photographs, scores, programs, and archival notes; they should not be silently equated with untouched originals. [LOC collection; NYPL archive]
- **1956–1991:** *Copyright-fixed repertory*. Litigation after Graham’s death examined only seventy dances said to exist in fixed form, not her entire choreographic production. The court found discrepancies between repertoire dates and copyright registrations and assigned ownership work by work; legal fixation, authorship, ownership, and artistic creation are therefore not synonymous. [Court]
