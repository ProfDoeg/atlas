## Works by Dion Fortune

### Psychology, social questions, health, and practical life

- **1922:** *The Machinery of the Mind*. A popular exposition of psychoanalysis published under her birth name, Violet M. Firth; the Society of the Inner Light’s bibliography treats it as her first nonfiction book. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1925:** *The Psychology of the Servant Problem*. A social-psychological handbook published as Violet M. Firth, applying psychological analysis to domestic service. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1925:** *The Soya Bean*. A practical pamphlet or short handbook on cultivating and using soya; it appears in bibliographic accounts but is omitted from the Society’s formal title list, so the apparent corpus is not perfectly consistent. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Hermetic Library bibliography](https://hermetic.com/fortune/index))
- **1928:** *The Problem of Purity*. A sexual-ethics and personal-development manual published as Violet M. Firth, rather than an occult treatise in the narrower sense. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1935:** *Practical Occultism in Daily Life*. A compact protocol for applying occult principles to ordinary conduct; later editions added archival material, but that supplement should not be mistaken for a new work completed by Fortune. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Bibliography of Occult and Fantastic Beliefs](https://d-nb.info/1190102951/34))

### Esoteric teaching, doctrine, and meditation

- **1924:** *The Esoteric Philosophy of Love and Marriage*. An occult-philosophical study of sexuality, polarity, and marriage. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1928:** *The Esoteric Orders and Their Work*. An explanatory and partly initiatory study of esoteric organizations and their functions. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1929:** *Sane Occultism*. A collection derived from periodical articles, intended to distinguish disciplined occult practice from credulity and sensationalism. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1930:** *Mystical Meditations on the Collects*. A sequence of Christian esoteric meditations, first issued from material published in *The Inner Light*; later editions sometimes expand the title to *Mystical Meditations on the Christian Collects*. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1930:** *Psychic Self-Defence*. A hybrid occult casebook, autobiographical narrative, diagnostic manual, and protective protocol; its unstable mixture of asserted experience and exemplary narrative makes it resist classification as straightforward nonfiction. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1930:** *The Training and Work of an Initiate*. An account and protocol concerning esoteric initiation, discipline, and service. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1931:** *Spiritualism in the Light of Occult Science*. A critical occult interpretation of mediumship and Spiritualism, assembled from articles in *The Inner Light*. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1932:** *Through the Gates of Death*. An esoteric account of dying and post-mortem existence, functioning partly as instruction for the dying and bereaved. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1934:** *Avalon of the Heart*. A psychogeographical and devotional meditation on Glastonbury, published under Violet M. Firth; it combines place-writing, legend, mystical history, and manifesto for Glastonbury as a spiritual centre. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1935:** *The Mystical Qabalah*. A systematic exposition of the Tree of Life as a framework for Western occult philosophy and practice. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **Received before 1924; published 1949:** *The Cosmic Doctrine*. A cosmological teaching that Fortune presented as mediumistically received rather than solely authored; its status is consequently both text and claimed manifestation, and it appeared only after her death. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **Articles written during Fortune’s lifetime; collected 1962:** *Aspects of Occultism*. A posthumous editorial assemblage of previously published teachings, not a book planned in this form by Fortune. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **Articles written during Fortune’s lifetime; collected 1962:** *Applied Magic*. A posthumous collection of instructional essays; the volume is an editorial object assembled from Fortune’s texts rather than an independently completed manuscript. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1942–1943 material; collected 1999:** *Principles of Hermetic Philosophy*. Gareth Knight’s posthumous editorial collection of Fortune’s monthly teachings, including material also identified as *The Esoteric Philosophy of Astrology*. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Bibliography of Occult and Fantastic Beliefs](https://d-nb.info/1190102951/34))
- **Archival material; collected 1997:** *An Introduction to Ritual Magic*. A posthumously constructed instructional volume combining Fortune’s surviving material with Gareth Knight’s editorial contribution; its book-form authorship is therefore shared and retrospective. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **Archival material; collected 1998:** *The Circuit of Force: Occult Dynamics of the Etheric Vehicle*. A posthumous collection made from Fortune’s teachings preserved in the Society’s archive; the Society recognizes the underlying writings, although the title is absent from its shorter formal list of published works. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Bibliography of Occult and Fantastic Beliefs](https://d-nb.info/1190102951/34))

### Occult and initiatory fiction

- **1922–1926:** *The Secrets of Dr. Taverner*. A linked cycle of occult case stories first published separately from 1922 and collected in 1926; later editions added “A Son of the Night,” so the collection has no entirely stable historical contents. ([Supernatural Fiction Database](https://tartaruspress.com/f4.htm))
- **1927:** *The Demon Lover*. An occult novel in which fiction also serves as indirect instruction about magical coercion and psychic influence. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))
- **1935:** *The Winged Bull*. An occult novel organized around magical polarity, initiation, and psychological transformation. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Susan Johnston Graf](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09589230601116182))
- **1936:** *The Goat-Foot God*. An occult novel and imaginative vehicle for a modern revival of Pan; it is closely related to Fortune’s ritual work but is not itself merely a disguised ritual script. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Susan Johnston Graf](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09589230601116182))
- **1938:** *The Sea Priestess*. A self-published occult novel whose narrative functions as an imaginative initiation into lunar, priestess, and polarity symbolism. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Susan Johnston Graf](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09589230601116182))
- **Unfinished by 1939; published 1956 or 1957:** *Moon Magic*. Fortune’s final occult novel, left incomplete; a protégé supplied a conclusion while claiming mediumistic assistance, so the finished book cannot be attributed wholly and unproblematically to Fortune. Sources disagree between 1956 and 1957 for first publication. ([Supernatural Fiction Database](https://tartaruspress.com/f4.htm), [Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune))

### Non-occult fiction under the name V. M. Steele

- **1935:** *The Scarred Wrists*. A non-occult novel published under the pseudonym V. M. Steele; scholarly discussion explicitly distinguishes it from Fortune’s occult novels. ([Susan Johnston Graf](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09589230601116182), [Supernatural Fiction Database](https://tartaruspress.com/f4.htm))
- **1935 or 1936:** *Hunters of Humans*. A non-occult novel published as V. M. Steele. Bibliographies disagree on whether its publication year was 1935 or 1936, so neither date should be silently preferred. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Supernatural Fiction Database](https://tartaruspress.com/f4.htm))
- **1936 or 1937:** *Beloved of Ishmael*. A non-occult novel published as V. M. Steele. Bibliographic sources variously date it to 1936 and 1937. ([Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/dion-fortune), [Supernatural Fiction Database](https://tartaruspress.com/f4.htm))

### Rituals and operative texts

- **1930s:** *Rite of Isis*. A ritual protocol performed on some occasions before a public audience and preserved in the Society’s archive; its complete text was published only posthumously. It is an enacted work as much as a written one and underlies elements of *The Sea Priestess* and *Moon Magic*. ([Skylight Press](https://skylightpress.co.uk/products/dion-fortunes-rites-of-isis-and-of-pan))
- **1930s:** *Rite of Pan*. A ritual protocol likewise performed in the 1930s and later recovered from the archive; it supplied operative material behind *The Goat-Foot God*. ([Skylight Press](https://skylightpress.co.uk/products/dion-fortunes-rites-of-isis-and-of-pan))
- **April 1941–February 1942:** *The Arthurian Formula, Part One*. A series of trance communications attributed by Fortune to an inner-plane source and later made the foundation of the Society’s Greater Mystery work. The published *Arthurian Formula* is not wholly hers: Margaret Lumley Brown wrote Parts Two and Three, while later editors added further material. ([St Andrews doctoral thesis](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/31065/Thesis-Alexander-James-Michael-Erickson-complete-version.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=5), [published-volume description](https://dokumen.pub/the-arthurian-formula.html))
- **Date not securely fixed:** *The Glastonbury Script*. A trance or visionary script attributed to Violet M. Firth and published posthumously as an appendix to *The Arthurian Formula*; it is a claimed manifestation as well as a made text. ([The Arthurian Formula](https://dokumen.pub/the-arthurian-formula.html))
- **Date not securely fixed:** *Clairvoyant Visions of Atlantis*. A body of visionary material attributed jointly to Fortune and Margaret Lumley Brown and later excerpted in *The Arthurian Formula*; its collaborative, mediumistic, and editorial layers prevent treatment as a stable Fortune-only work. ([The Arthurian Formula](https://dokumen.pub/the-arthurian-formula.html))

### Periodical and editorial project

- **1927–1940:** *The Inner Light*. A monthly journal founded as the organ of the Fraternity of the Inner Light and edited by Fortune through its original thirteen-volume run. It is a collective periodical project rather than a single-authored text, although much of Fortune’s later nonfiction first appeared in it. ([International Association for the Preservation of Spiritualist and Occult Periodicals](https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/inner_light_fortune/index.html))
- **1939–1942:** *Letters to Members and Friends of the Fraternity of the Inner Light*, later collected as *The Magical Battle of Britain*. Weekly wartime letters coordinating meditation and visualization: simultaneously correspondence, practical protocol, and an extended magical project. Gareth Knight’s posthumous editions reshape the surviving series, and publication dates differ by edition. ([Bibliography of Occult and Fantastic Beliefs](https://d-nb.info/1190102951/34), [Open British National Bibliography](https://obnb.uk/p16015928-the-magical-battle-of-britain-the-war-letters-of-dion-fortune))

### Organizations, places, and collective projects

- **1922–1924 onward:** *Christian Mystic Lodge / Community of the Inner Light / Fraternity of the Inner Light*. Fortune’s esoteric school developed through changing names and institutional forms, making it a long-lived obra rather than one fixed text. Sources differ over whether to identify its founding with the 1922 meditation group, the Community formed in 1924, or the ritual establishment of the Fraternity in 1928. ([Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/5515/chapter-abstract/148442972), [Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/work-aims), [International Association for the Preservation of Spiritualist and Occult Periodicals](https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/inner_light_fortune/index.html))
- **1924 onward:** *Chalice Orchard*. A Glastonbury property developed by Fortune and Thomas Loveday as hostel, social centre, lecture site, sanctuary, and ritual environment. Its mixture of place, community, and spiritual programme makes it a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a building. ([Gareth Knight](https://garethknight.blogspot.com/2015/08/the-sanctuary-at-chalice-orchard.html), [Chalice Centre](https://www.chalicecentre.net/dion_fortune_holy_grail.html))
- **1920s–1940s:** *The Inner Light training system*. A staged programme of Lesser and Greater Mysteries combining degrees, lectures, meditation, ritual, and supervised practice. It is a collective and evolving protocol, not a securely bounded authored text, and surviving descriptions do not permit every component to be assigned solely to Fortune. ([International Association for the Preservation of Spiritualist and Occult Periodicals](https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/inner_light_fortune/index.html), [Society of the Inner Light](https://innerlight.org.uk/work-aims))
- **October 1939–October 1942:** *The wartime meditation campaign*. A coordinated sequence of weekly visualizations and letters intended to aid Britain through occult action. It resists being called a literary work: the made thing was the organized practice and participant network, while the surviving letters were its protocol. ([Bibliography of Occult and Fantastic Beliefs](https://d-nb.info/1190102951/34))

- Gareth Knight, *Dion Fortune and the Inner Light* (Thoth Publications, 2000). The standard biographical and bibliographical authority underlying the commonly reproduced fiction and nonfiction lists; publication details are summarized in the Society bibliography and in the bibliographical sources below.

Yeats, Arthur Machen, Algernon Blackwood, and Dion Fortune*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.2307/jj.18254351.4) (State University of New York Press, 2015). Scholarly discussion of the authorship, mediumistic origin, and later publication of *The Arthurian Formula*.
- Bruno Antonio Buike, [*Bibliography of Occult and Fantastic Beliefs*, vol. 2](https://d-nb.info/1190102951/34). Bibliographical checklist for Fortune’s organizations, wartime letters, archival collections, and collaborative posthumous publications.
