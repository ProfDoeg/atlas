## Works by Louis Braille

### Writing systems and notations

- **1825–1837:** *Système d’écriture en points saillants à six points* (the Braille system). An obra that is neither simply a text nor merely an alphabet: it is a generative writing protocol for letters, punctuation, numbers, mathematics, music, and plainchant. Its essentials existed by 1825 and its definitive arrangement appeared in 1837; it developed directly from Charles Barbier’s raised-point writing, a debt Braille expressly acknowledged, while some refinements between the two editions arose through experimentation by other blind pupils and cannot be assigned exclusively to him. [Henri, pp. 49–57](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt); [Campsie](https://www.perkins.org/braille-barbier/)

- **By 1829–1837:** *Notation musicale et notation du plain-chant en points*. A specialized protocol within the six-dot system, not a musical composition: Braille first adapted existing letter-based practices, then replaced them with a more coherent notation whose principal structures underlie later international Braille music. Its evolution was experimental and partly collective, although Braille’s contribution was considered predominant. [Henri, pp. 57–61, 81–86](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt); [Musée Louis Braille](https://www.museelouisbraille.com/en/braille-l-inventeur)

- **Before 1839:** *Planche d’impression pour la correspondance entre aveugles et voyants*. A flexible-leather printing apparatus with a ruled frame, devised jointly with an otherwise incompletely identified blind colleague named Binet; it embossed ordinary letterforms so sighted correspondents could write something readable by touch. It resists individual attribution because the surviving account presents it as a collaboration. [Musée Louis Braille](https://museelouisbraille.com/fr/ses-inventions); [Henri, p. 101 n. 3](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **1839:** *Décapoint*. A system and writing protocol that constructs ordinary letterforms, maps, geometrical figures, and musical signs on a grid up to ten points high, allowing blind writers to produce forms legible to sighted readers. Braille also contemplated a seven-point-high version but did not complete its formation code, so that variant is a project rather than a finished system. [Henri, pp. 97–102](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt); [Musée Louis Braille](https://museelouisbraille.com/fr/ses-inventions)

- **1839:** *Grille régulatrice du décapoint*. A material instrument as well as part of the protocol: a fine open grid constructed at Braille’s direction to regulate the position, separation, and dimensions of dotted ordinary letters. The surviving scholarship distinguishes Braille’s design from the subsequent machines made by Foucault. [Henri, pp. 99–100](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **1841 onward:** *Système Braille-Foucault*. A collaborative writing system manifested mechanically in François-Pierre Foucault’s “planche à pistons,” later called the raphigraphe: Foucault built the machine to execute Braille’s dotted ordinary-letter code. Whether Braille or Foucault introduced carbon paper for immediately visible writing is expressly uncertain, so the apparatus itself should not be attributed to Braille alone. [Henri, pp. 100–102](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **Before 1852:** *Machine pour tracer la notation musicale ordinaire*. An unfinished project represented by several successively improved experimental machines, intended to let blind musicians write staff notation readable by sighted people. Braille’s final model could form clefs, staves, ledger lines, notes, and words, but illness interrupted the work; it therefore resists being called a completed invention. [Henri, pp. 105–106](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt); [Musée Louis Braille](https://museelouisbraille.com/fr/ses-inventions)

### Protocols and teaching texts

- **1829:** *Procédé pour écrire les paroles, la musique et le plain-chant au moyen de points, à l’usage des aveugles et disposés pour eux*. A thirty-two-page protocol explaining the first published state of Braille’s writing and notation systems; Braille dictated it to the director Alexandre-René Pignier, and it was paradoxically printed chiefly in embossed linear type rather than in Braille. This version still used combinations of dots and lines and is not identical with the modern system. [Henri, pp. 49–55](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt); [Perkins School for the Blind](https://www.perkins.org/braille-barbier/)

- **1837:** *Procédé pour écrire les paroles, la musique et le plain-chant au moyen de points, à l’usage des aveugles et disposés pour eux*, second edition. A substantially revised manifestation of the 1829 protocol rather than a mere reprint: it eliminated the difficult linear signs, codified the essentially definitive six-dot alphabet, clarified the exposition through tables and examples, and supplied multilingual specimens to encourage international diffusion. Braille acknowledged unnamed blind collaborators’ “ingenious applications,” so not every refinement can securely be treated as his alone. [Henri, pp. 55–57, 69–76](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt); [Musée Louis Braille](https://www.museelouisbraille.com/fr/braille-l-inventeur)

- **1838:** *Petit memento d’arithmétique à l’usage des commençants, contenant les nombres entiers et les fractions décimales, suivi de 100 problèmes*. A concise elementary arithmetic manual made for Braille’s pupils and printed in embossed linear type. It is a teaching protocol rather than an exposition of his alphabet. [Henri, p. 34](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **1839:** *Nouveau procédé pour représenter par des points la forme même des lettres, les cartes de géographie, les figures de géométrie, les caractères de musique, etc., à l’usage des aveugles*. A sixteen-page printed protocol for décapoint and its grid-based representation of visual forms; unlike Braille’s other contemporary publications, it was printed in ink for sighted readers. It is simultaneously a textual explanation and the specification of a writing technology. [Henri, pp. 97–99, 114](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **1843:** *Tableau chiffré destiné à faire écrire les aveugles avec les lettres ordinaires*, first version. An embossed reference table encoding the coordinates needed to construct ordinary letterforms in dots. Pierre Henri records it as the first printed state but supplies no fuller surviving title, so it should not be expanded beyond the documented wording. [Henri, p. 101 n. 1](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **1848:** *Tableau chiffré destiné à faire écrire les aveugles avec les lettres ordinaires*. A revised embossed protocol and code table, including a description of the Foucault apparatus and reflecting Braille’s eventual preference for visible carbon-paper output. It is a new manifestation of the 1843 table, not an unrelated work. [Henri, pp. 101–102](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

### Correspondence, legal acts, and unrecovered texts

- **1831–1851:** *Correspondance*. A surviving but incomplete body of letters, chiefly addressed to Alexandre-René Pignier and later issued in facsimile by the Institut national des jeunes aveugles; the collected edition does not transform the individual letters into a single work, so this entry names a corpus rather than an obra. Earlier scholarship believed almost nothing survived, and letters mentioned by Pignier remain lost, making completeness impossible to claim. [BnF notice for the INJA facsimile edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb370828507); [Henri, pp. 96–97, 113–114](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **3 December 1840:** *Letter written in décapoint*. A surviving demonstration-letter produced with Braille’s 1839 grid; it is both correspondence and a material proof of the writing protocol. Its addressee is not identified in Pierre Henri’s caption, so none should be supplied. [Henri, plate V](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **21 May 1842:** *Letter to Abbé Charles-Louis Carton*. A raphigraphic demonstration-letter announcing and exemplifying Foucault’s first piston-board; it was also an act of dissemination, since Braille sent it to the founder of the Bruges school to introduce the apparatus abroad. [Henri, pp. 100–101 and plate VI](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **1850:** *Letter requesting retirement*. A raphigraphic administrative letter to the director of the Institution, asking him to obtain Braille’s retirement from the minister because of failing health. The request was unsuccessful; this is an operative document rather than a literary work. [Henri, p. 107](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **26 December 1851:** *Testament*. A dictated legal instrument that disposed of Braille’s property, forgave loans made before that December, and made charitable and personal bequests. It acts rather than describes, but it resists ordinary authorship language because a notary formally composed the document from Braille’s declaration and Braille was recorded as unable to sign. [Henri, pp. 110–112](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **Undated, before 1852:** *Observations supplied to Alexandre-René Pignier*. Notes used by Pignier in defending himself against attacks by Pierre-Armand Dufau and Joseph Guadet. Pignier printed extracts, but the originals have not been found; they are therefore documented texts without recoverable independent form or secure dates, and they resist treatment as finished works. [Henri, p. 114 n. 1](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)

- **School years, before 1828:** *Literary and scientific compositions*. Pignier remembered a body of clear, concise student compositions, but no titles, dates, or surviving manuscripts are identified. They are evidence of textual making, not a reconstructible oeuvre, and should not be converted into invented individual works. [Henri, pp. 29–30](https://archive.org/stream/lavieetloeuvrede00pier/lavieetloeuvrede00pier_djvu.txt)
