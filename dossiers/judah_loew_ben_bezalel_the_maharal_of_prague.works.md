## Works by Judah Loew ben Bezalel

### Commentaries and systematic works published in his lifetime

- **1578:** *Gur Aryeh*. A supercommentary on Rashi’s commentary on the Pentateuch—more precisely, a sustained defense and interpretation of the biblical, midrashic, and aggadic material Rashi selected. It was Loew’s first printed work. ([YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **1582:** *Gevurot ha-Shem*. A theological and exegetical treatise on the Exodus, miracles, Passover, and the Passover Haggadah; it incorporates both speculative interpretation and discussion of ritual law. The first edition appeared anonymously, although Loew’s authorship is not disputed. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel))

- **1589:** *Derekh Ḥayyim*. A commentary on *Pirkei Avot* that turns a commentary on individual maxims into a systematic work of ethics, anthropology, education, and metaphysics. Loew subsequently described *Netivot Olam* as its second part. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **1595–1596:** *Netivot Olam*. A large ethical-theological work organized as thirty-three “paths,” each devoted to a virtue, vice, religious practice, or form of human conduct. The publication year is variously given as 1595 or 1596 because the Hebrew and civil-year conversion and the production of its two parts are reported differently. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel))

- **1598:** *Be’er ha-Golah*. A defense and interpretation of difficult rabbinic aggadot, structured as seven “wells”; it is simultaneously commentary, theological argument, and polemic, notably against Azariah de’ Rossi’s historical criticism. Loew presented it as a continuation or second part of *Tif’eret Yisrael*, despite its appearing first. ([YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **1599:** *Tif’eret Yisrael*. A systematic theological treatise on Torah, revelation, commandments, and Israel’s reception of the Torah. Some reference lists incorrectly or confusingly give 1593; the recognized first edition is Venice, 1599, while the 1593 Prague printing belongs to *Derush ‘al ha-Torah ve-ha-Mitzvot*. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10046-liwa-ben-bezaleel); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **1599–1600:** *Netzaḥ Yisrael*. A theological philosophy of exile, national existence, repentance, and messianic redemption. Bibliographies normally date it 1599, while surviving first-edition descriptions sometimes give 1599–1600. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel); [YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel))

- **1600:** *Or Ḥadash*. A metaphysical and homiletical commentary on the Book of Esther and Purim, first printed together with *Ner Mitzvah* but conceptually a separate work. ([Sotheby’s, first-edition description](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2014/important-judaica-n09239/lot.66.html); [Christie’s, first-edition description](https://www.christies.com/en/lot/lot-352744))

- **1600:** *Ner Mitzvah*. A treatise on Hanukkah, the successive empires, Greek power, and Israel’s spiritual survival. Although physically issued after *Or Ḥadash* in the same first-edition volume, it is a distinct obra rather than merely a chapter of that commentary. ([Sotheby’s, first-edition description](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2014/important-judaica-n09239/lot.66.html); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

### Sermons that became printed texts

- **1584:** *Derush ‘al ha-Teshuvah*. A sermon on repentance, delivered for Shabbat Shuvah and subsequently transmitted as a discrete text. It resists a simple “book” classification: the obra is both an oral communal event and its printed textual afterlife. ([Torat Har Etzion, “The Maharal’s Books—An Overview”](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/sifrei-hamaharal-sekira-clalit))

- **1589:** *Derush le-Shabbat ha-Gadol*. A pre-Passover sermon combining theology, interpretation, and practical discussion of Passover. Later bibliographies sometimes call the resulting publication a *Haggadah shel Pesaḥ*, but the authentic text should not be confused with the much later *Divrei Negidim* Haggadah falsely attributed to Loew. ([Torat Har Etzion](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/sifrei-hamaharal-sekira-clalit); [Schechter Institutes](https://schechter.edu/pesah-potpourri-on-the-origin-and-development-of-some-lesser-known-pesah-customs/))

- **1593:** *Derush ‘al ha-Torah ve-ha-Mitzvot*. A published discourse on Torah and commandments, functioning less as a neutral description than as a programmatic intervention in religious education and practice. ([Torat Har Etzion](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/sifrei-hamaharal-sekira-clalit); [Hebrew Printing in Bohemia and Moravia reference](https://mameloshn.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2020/01/Yiddish_language_and_literature_with_spe.pdf))

- **Delivered 1597; printed 1598:** *Hesped ‘al Petirat Ḥakham*. A funeral sermon for the Prague scholar Akiva Günzburg, transformed from an occasional oral act into a printed memorial text. ([Olga Sixtová, ed., *Hebrew Printing in Bohemia and Moravia*](https://dokumen.pub/hebrew-printing-in-bohemia-and-moravia-8020022201-9788020022202.html))

### Protocol and communal institution

- **After 1573; exact date uncertain:** *Regulations of the Prague Ḥevra Kadisha*. Communal statutes governing Prague’s burial society: a protocol in the strict sense, because the text organized conduct rather than merely describing it. The surviving corporate text belongs to the society as well as to Loew, so his role is more safely described as drafting or codifying its regulations than as sole literary authorship. ([YIVO Encyclopedia, “Death and the Dead”](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article/2092); [Posen Library](https://www.posenlibrary.com/author/hevra-kadisha-prague); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.jevzajcg.me/enciklopedia/Encyclopaedia%20Judaica%2C%20v.%2009%20%28Her-Int%29.pdf))

### Manuscript and posthumously assembled works

- **Sixteenth–early seventeenth century; published 1958–1961:** *Perushei Maharal mi-Prag le-Aggadot ha-Shas* / *Ḥiddushei Aggadot*. Loew’s extensive commentaries on the nonlegal narratives and sayings of the Babylonian Talmud, preserved in manuscripts and first assembled into a multivolume printed corpus in the twentieth century. Calling it one “book” conceals its manuscript plurality and later editorial construction. ([YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **Sixteenth–early seventeenth century; first printed 1863:** *Ḥiddushei Gur Aryeh ‘al ha-Shas*. Talmudic novellae associated especially with tractates Shabbat, Eruvin, and Pesaḥim. This posthumous editorial object is distinct from the 1578 Pentateuchal *Gur Aryeh*, despite sharing its title. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **Sixteenth–early seventeenth century; first printed 1775:** *Ḥiddushei Maharal mi-Prag ‘al Tur Yoreh De‘ah*. Halakhic novellae on the ritual-law division of Jacob ben Asher’s *Tur*, preserved and published well after Loew’s death. The original composition date is not fixed. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel); [Guttel, “The Affinity of Halakhah, Thought and Education in Maharal’s Teaching”](https://lif.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/guttel.pdf))

- **1594:** *Pesak be-‘Inyan ‘Agunah* (“Ruling concerning an agunah”). A case-specific halakhic decision concerning a woman unable to remarry; it is a juridical act and responsum rather than a treatise. Other rulings and responsa attributed to Loew survive dispersed through manuscripts and later responsa collections, so they cannot responsibly be converted into a complete list of separately titled works. ([Guttel](https://lif.ac.il/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/guttel.pdf); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel))

- **Sixteenth–early seventeenth century; various later publications:** *Uncollected sermons, novellae, responsa, and annotations*. This is a remainder corpus, not a single work: the standard bibliography records additional pieces as partly printed and partly manuscript, without supplying stable titles or dates for every item. Their resistance to itemization reflects their documentary condition rather than their nonexistence. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel); [*Path of Life* contents](https://www.cz-museums.cz/UserFiles/file/Deni%20v%20oboru/Publikace/eng01.pdf))

### Announced projects and lost or unrealized works

- **Announced from 1582 onward; completion unknown:** *Sefer ha-Gedulah*. A projected work on creation and the Sabbath within Loew’s architectonic series named from 1 Chronicles 29:11. His references speak prospectively, so it should be catalogued as a project, not silently promoted to a lost completed book. ([Torat Har Etzion](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/sifrei-hamaharal-sekira-clalit))

- **Announced in 1582; completion unknown:** *Sefer ha-Hod*. A projected work on Sukkot. No securely identified text survives, and the evidence does not decide whether it was completed and lost or never written. ([Torat Har Etzion](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/sifrei-hamaharal-sekira-clalit))

- **Announced in 1582; completion unknown:** *Shamayim va-Aretz*. A projected work on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur. Like *Sefer ha-Hod*, it survives as an intention documented inside another work, not as an extant obra. ([Torat Har Etzion](https://etzion.org.il/en/philosophy/great-thinkers/maharal/sifrei-hamaharal-sekira-clalit))

### Things traditionally made by him that are not securely his works

- **No contemporary date or evidence:** *The Prague Golem*. This is a legendary creation or manifestation, not a documented obra by Loew: the developed story connecting him to a golem is late and unsupported by his writings or contemporary sources. It therefore belongs to the history of his reception, not to his catalogue of works. ([YIVO Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.yivo.org/article.aspx/Yehudah_Leib_ben_Betsalel); [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/judah-loew-liwa-loeb-ben-bezalel); [Sid Z. Leiman, “The Adventure of the Maharal of Prague in London”](https://leimanlibrary.com/wp-content/texts_of_publications/93.%20The%20Adventure%20of%20the%20Maharal%20of%20Prague%20in%20London%20R.%20Yudel%20Rosenberg%20and%20the%20Golem%20of%20Prague.pdf))

- **Published 1905:** *Divrei Negidim: Haggadah shel Pesaḥ*. A Haggadah commentary issued by Yudl Rosenberg as though recovered from a manuscript by Loew. Scholarship identifies it as Rosenberg’s own pseudepigraphic creation; it is consequently not a work by Loew, despite its long inclusion in popular lists. ([Schechter Institutes](https://schechter.edu/pesah-potpourri-on-the-origin-and-development-of-some-lesser-known-pesah-customs/); [Sid Z. Leiman](https://leimanlibrary.com/wp-content/texts_of_publications/93.%20The%20Adventure%20of%20the%20Maharal%20of%20Prague%20in%20London%20R.%20Yudel%20Rosenberg%20and%20the%20Golem%20of%20Prague.pdf))

- **Published 1909:** *Nifla’ot Maharal*. Yudl Rosenberg’s collection of golem narratives, presented as an old manuscript chronicle but rejected by scholarship as a modern literary fabrication. It made the modern Maharal–golem legend; Loew did not make the text or, on the available historical evidence, its golem. ([Sid Z. Leiman](https://leimanlibrary.com/wp-content/texts_of_publications/93.%20The%20Adventure%20of%20the%20Maharal%20of%20Prague%20in%20London%20R.%20Yudel%20Rosenberg%20and%20the%20Golem%20of%20Prague.pdf); [Schechter Institutes](https://schechter.edu/pesah-potpourri-on-the-origin-and-development-of-some-lesser-known-pesah-customs/))
