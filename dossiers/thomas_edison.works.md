## Works by Thomas Alva Edison

### Patented obra: the complete surviving register

- **1868–1931:** *The 1,093 successful United States patent applications*. This is the only complete catalogue of Edison’s formally attributed inventions: legal-technical texts embodying apparatus, processes, systems, and designs, rather than 1,093 necessarily separate physical objects. The Thomas A. Edison Papers provides every title, patent number, execution date, filing date, issue date, subject, and patent document; its register—not a selection—is the controlling item-level list for this corpus. [Complete patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1868–1931:** *Approximately 500–600 unsuccessful or abandoned United States patent applications*. These are projects in the strict sense: intended inventions that did not become issued patents. The Edison Papers gives only an estimate, so neither their number nor their titles can presently be treated as a complete work-list. [Edison Papers, “Edison’s Patents.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents)

- **1872–1931:** *Foreign patents*. Dyer and Martin compiled 1,239 grants in thirty-four countries, but the Edison Papers warns that no complete list exists; many are foreign legal manifestations of inventions already represented in the U.S. register rather than additional made things. [Edison Papers, “Edison’s Patents.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents)

### Telegraphic, telephonic, and inscription systems

- **1868–1869:** *Electrographic Vote-Recorder*. Edison’s first successful patented apparatus recorded legislative votes electrically; it survives as patent no. 90,646, executed in 1868 and issued in 1869. [Patent register and document.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1869–1873:** *Printing-telegraph and stock-printer systems*. A family of transmitters, type wheels, synchronized receivers, switches, and printing mechanisms rather than one immutable machine; several patents were jointly attributed to Franklin L. Pope. [Patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1871–1876:** *Automatic and chemical telegraph system*. This obra joined paper perforators, high-speed transmitters, receiving instruments, chemically prepared recording paper, and circuits; its distributed form resists treatment as a single invention. [Patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1873–1876:** *Duplex and quadruplex telegraph systems*. These arrangements sent two and ultimately four messages simultaneously over one wire. They were cumulative system-projects represented by numerous patents, not a solitary object with one definitive date. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1874–1878:** *Electric pen and autographic-printing system*. A motor-driven stencil-making pen, duplicating press, ink, and prepared paper formed a copying system later adapted by others into mimeograph and tattooing technologies. The later products are derivative manifestations, not Edison works. [Patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1876–1878:** *Acoustic and harmonic multiplex telegraphs*. A group of tuned transmitters, receivers, relays, and circuits intended to place multiple signals on one wire; these experiments also supplied parts and concepts for Edison’s telephone work. [Patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1877–1878:** *Carbon telephone transmitter*. A variable-resistance microphone that made Bell’s telephone commercially practical over longer distances. It was developed through laboratory experiments and multiple patent claims, and therefore should not be reduced to one surviving specimen. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1885–1887:** *Phonoplex telegraph system*. A method and apparatus for sending telegraph signals over circuits already used for other purposes, especially railways; it was both an invention and an operating communications system. [Edison Papers chronology.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/chronology/1881-1890)

### Recorded-sound Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1877–1878:** *Tinfoil phonograph*. The first machine to record and reproduce sound, joining mechanical apparatus, recording surface, inscription, and performed voice. The famous claim that Edison’s first recorded words were “Mary Had a Little Lamb” rests chiefly on later recollection; the earliest experiments are incompletely documented and their recordings do not survive. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

- **1887–1889:** *Improved cylinder phonograph*. Edison’s West Orange project combined a wax cylinder, electric motor, recording and reproducing mechanisms, and methods of duplicating records. It was collective laboratory work divided among teams, so “Edison’s phonograph” names a directed research obra rather than unaided manufacture by one person. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1896:** *Edison Home Phonograph*. An inexpensive spring-motor cylinder machine introduced as a domestic product; it is a commercial manifestation of the continuing phonograph project rather than an entirely new medium. [Edison Papers chronology, 1891–1900.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/chronology/1891-1900)

- **1912–1929:** *Blue Amberol cylinder system*. A celluloid-cylinder format and its associated recording and playback machinery, made by Edison’s companies. Individual catalogue recordings were performances by artists and productions by company personnel, not compositions or personal performances by Edison. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park recorded-sound catalogue.](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/search-the-catalog.htm)

- **1912–1929:** *Edison Diamond Disc system*. A vertically cut disc, reproducer, phonograph, recording process, and catalogue conceived as an integrated high-fidelity system. The thousands of Diamond Disc masters resist attribution as Edison’s individual works: he supervised tests and repertoire policy, but performers, composers, conductors, arrangers, and recording staff made the particular recordings. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park recorded-sound catalogue.](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/search-the-catalog.htm)

- **1888–1929:** *Edison-company sound-recording catalogue*. This is a corporate archive and body of manifestations, not a personal Edison discography; the National Park Service states that most of its surviving Edison recordings date from 1888–1929. It belongs here only with that resistance to individual authorship made explicit. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park recorded-sound catalogue.](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/search-the-catalog.htm)

### Electric-light and power system

- **1878–1882:** *Incandescent electric-lighting system*. Edison’s project encompassed a durable high-resistance lamp, generators, parallel circuits, underground conductors, sockets, switches, fuses, meters, and an economic plan for central-station supply. Edison did not invent electric light as such; the obra was the practical integrated system, developed collaboratively at Menlo Park and represented by many patents. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1879–1880:** *High-resistance carbon-filament incandescent lamp*. An improved practical lamp within the larger lighting system, not “the invention of the light bulb” without qualification: incandescent lamps predated Edison, while his laboratory pursued filament materials, vacuum, resistance, manufacture, and system compatibility. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

- **1880–1882:** *“Jumbo” dynamo and direct-current generating apparatus*. Large generators and associated regulating equipment made central-station lighting possible. Their status is systemic and collaborative: individual machines embodied designs developed and revised by Edison’s laboratory and manufacturing staff. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1881:** *Three-wire electric-lighting system*. A distribution arrangement reducing the copper required for a direct-current network; it is an infrastructural protocol embodied in wiring and generating practice rather than a freestanding consumer object. [Patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

- **1882:** *Pearl Street central-station system*. A working urban electric utility in lower Manhattan integrating generation, feeders, mains, metering, lamps, and commercial administration. It is best classified as a technological and organizational Gesamtkunstwerk, made by Edison’s companies and staff under his direction rather than by Edison alone. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1883:** *The Edison effect*. A one-way current observed between a hot lamp filament and an added electrode. It was a discovery and experimental manifestation, not a finished invention; Edison patented an application but did not develop the later thermionic valve made from the phenomenon. [Patent register.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/edison-s-patents/patent-listing)

### Motion-picture system and corporate manifestations

- **1888–1892:** *Kinetograph*. A motion-picture camera project developed at West Orange. Edison supplied the project, resources, and electromechanical direction, while W. K. L. Dickson contributed most of the photographic knowledge and led much experimental work; sole authorship by Edison would therefore be misleading. [Edison Papers, “Motion Pictures.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/component/content/article/motion-pictures?Itemid=101&catid=91%3Ainventions)

- **1888–1892:** *Kinetoscope*. A peephole motion-picture viewing machine intended to do for sight what the phonograph did for hearing. Like the Kinetograph, it is a laboratory collaboration especially associated with Dickson, although patents and corporate promotion placed Edison’s name foremost. [Edison Papers, “Motion Pictures.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/component/content/article/motion-pictures?Itemid=101&catid=91%3Ainventions)

- **1892–1893:** *Black Maria*. Edison’s tar-paper-covered revolving motion-picture studio at West Orange, designed to follow sunlight and supply subjects for the Kinetoscope. It was an apparatus-building-studio hybrid made for a collective production enterprise. [Library of Congress, “Inventing Entertainment.”](https://www.loc.gov/collections/edison-company-motion-pictures-and-sound-recordings/about-this-collection/)

- **1894:** *Edison Kinetoscope film programme*. The earliest commercially exhibited Edison-company motion pictures formed a programme of brief recorded actions rather than authored narrative films. Dickson and other employees photographed and performed many of them, so they are company works associated with Edison, not secure individual works by him. [Library of Congress, “Inventing Entertainment.”](https://www.loc.gov/collections/edison-company-motion-pictures-and-sound-recordings/about-this-collection/)

- **1896:** *Vitascope*. A projected-motion-picture system marketed under Edison’s name but substantially derived from the Phantoscope of C. Francis Jenkins and Thomas Armat. It resists inclusion as an Edison invention: his company acquired and promoted it, while Armat’s technical authorship must not be erased. [Edison Papers motion-picture catalogue history.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/motion-picture-catalogs)

- **1896:** *Projecting Kinetoscope*. Edison’s company’s own projector, commercially exhibited after the Vitascope. It belongs to the laboratory and corporate equipment programme rather than constituting a film. [Edison Papers chronology, 1891–1900.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/chronology/1891-1900)

- **1896–1918:** *Edison Manufacturing Company motion pictures*. A very large corporate filmography embracing actualities, trick films, comedies, dramas, news subjects, and educational films. These films were made by camera operators, directors, performers, writers, and production staff—including Dickson, Edwin S. Porter, and many others—and must not be silently converted into personal works by Edison merely because his name appeared on the company or copyright deposit. [Edison Papers, motion-picture catalogues.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/motion-picture-catalogs)

- **1894–1913:** *Kinetophone and synchronized sound-picture projects*. Several attempts joined phonograph reproduction to Kinetoscope or projected film. They are audiovisual systems—genuine Gesamtkunstwerke—but synchronization remained imperfect, and the individual films and recordings were collaborative company productions rather than Edison-authored texts. [Library of Congress, “Inventing Entertainment.”](https://www.loc.gov/collections/edison-company-motion-pictures-and-sound-recordings/about-this-collection/)

### Mining, materials, batteries, and industrial processes

- **1880–1900:** *Magnetic ore-separation and low-grade iron-ore milling system*. A long project involving magnetic separators, crushers, conveyors, dryers, concentrating machinery, and the New Jersey ore works. Technically extensive but commercially unsuccessful, it is still an obra because the project and industrial plant—not only a profitable product—were the made thing. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

- **1894–1900:** *Three-high crushing rolls*. Heavy ore-crushing machinery developed within the milling project and covered by patent no. 644,746. It later informed Edison’s cement-processing machinery. [Edison Papers chronology, 1891–1900.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/chronology/1891-1900)

- **1896–1898:** *Fluoroscope*. A fluorescent-screen X-ray viewing apparatus improved in Edison’s laboratory. Clarence Dally performed much of the experimental work and suffered fatal radiation injury; the apparatus therefore cannot responsibly be narrated as Edison’s solitary manufacture. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

- **1899–1920s:** *Edison Portland-cement manufacturing system*. A project joining improved long rotary kilns, crushing and grinding machinery, plant organization, and concrete-product experiments. It was an industrial process and factory complex rather than merely a patented object. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

- **1900s:** *Single-pour concrete house project*. Edison proposed reusable iron molds into which an entire reinforced-concrete house—including structural and ornamental elements—would be poured. Only limited manifestations followed; the project’s ambition, expense, and practical difficulties are part of the work rather than reasons to omit it. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park biography.](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/edison-biography.htm?simple=True)

- **1899–1910:** *Nickel–iron alkaline storage battery*. A rechargeable battery system developed through nearly a decade of repeated chemical and manufacturing experiments, withdrawn and redesigned after early failures, then reintroduced. It was collective laboratory work and became one of Edison’s most profitable products. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park biography.](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/edison-biography.htm?simple=True)

- **1914–1918:** *Domestic chemical-production programme*. After wartime imports were disrupted, Edison established processes and plants for chemicals needed in storage batteries, phonograph records, and other industries. This was a coordinated manufacturing project, not one invention with a stable title. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park, “Edison and World War I.”](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/thomas-edison-in-world-war-i.htm)

- **1915–1918:** *Naval research projects*. As head of the Naval Consulting Board, Edison pursued submarine-detection, camouflage, torpedo, communications, and defensive-research problems. Many remained experiments or recommendations; their status is project rather than completed apparatus, and their surviving documentary record does not justify pretending that each became an operational invention. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park, “Edison and World War I.”](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/thomas-edison-in-world-war-i.htm)

- **1927–1931:** *Domestic-rubber research project*. Edison screened thousands of plants and developed methods intended to obtain an American source of natural rubber, ultimately concentrating on goldenrod. It remained an experimental agricultural, chemical, and industrial project at his death rather than a completed replacement for commercial rubber. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

### Laboratories and invention as an institutional obra

- **1876–1882:** *Menlo Park research laboratory*. A purpose-built complex integrating machine shop, chemical laboratory, library, testing, drafting, patenting, and manufacture. Edison’s most consequential made thing may have been this repeatable institution of collaborative invention; the buildings themselves were constructed by many hands. [Edison Papers biography.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/biography/detailed-biography)

- **1887–1931:** *West Orange laboratory complex*. A much larger research-and-development institution supporting simultaneous projects in sound, motion pictures, batteries, ore milling, cement, chemicals, and rubber. It is both architecture and a continuing social-technical protocol, not simply the room in which individual inventions happened. [Thomas Edison National Historical Park biography.](https://www.nps.gov/edis/learn/historyculture/edison-biography.htm?simple=True)

- **1870s–1920s:** *The industrial research laboratory as method*. Edison did not patent “team research” as one object, but his organization of machinists, chemists, draftsmen, experimenters, patent lawyers, factories, and markets was itself an institutional creation. It also makes individual attribution difficult: an Edison patent may document Edison’s direction, his own experiment, an employee’s contribution, or several of these together. [Edison Papers, “Inventions.”](https://edison.rutgers.edu/life-of-edison/inventions)

### Documentary corpus

- **c. 1860s–1931:** *Laboratory notebooks, experimental notes, drawings, correspondence, account books, legal papers, and business records*. Approximately five million pages survive in the archival corpus associated with Edison and his organizations. These are texts and working protocols, but not all were written by Edison, and the documentary edition is explicitly selective; the corpus therefore resists conversion into a conventional authorial bibliography. [Thomas A. Edison Papers, digital and book editions.](https://edison.rutgers.edu/research/book-edition)

- **1870s–1931:** *Public statements, interviews, articles, speeches, testimony, and dictated reminiscences*. These dispersed texts helped create the public figure “Edison,” but many passed through reporters, stenographers, editors, or company publicity staff. Authorship and wording must consequently be established document by document from the Edison Papers rather than inferred from appearance under his name. [Thomas A. Edison Papers digital edition.](https://edisondigital.rutgers.edu/)
