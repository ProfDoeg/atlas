## Works by Captain Robert Falcon Scott

### Expeditionary projects

- **1901–1904:** *British National Antarctic Expedition (“Discovery” Expedition)*. A collective scientific and geographical project commanded by Scott, not a work of sole authorship: its material results included surveys, soundings, specimen collections, observations, photographs, maps, journals, and reports produced by many expedition members. Scott led its principal southern and western journeys, but the expedition itself was jointly organized by the Royal Society and Royal Geographical Society. ([Oxford Dictionary of National Biography text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Dictionary_of_National_Biography%2C_1927_supplement/Scott%2C_Robert_Falcon))

- **1910–1913:** *British Antarctic Expedition (“Terra Nova” Expedition)*. Scott conceived, organized, and commanded this combined geographical, scientific, and South-Pole project, but it remained a collective undertaking and continued after his death; calling the whole expedition his obra therefore describes his directing intention, not sole manufacture of everything it produced. The surviving expedition archive includes his diaries, memoranda, orders, correspondence, and records alongside work by other members. ([British Library, Add MS 51024–51042](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001958841))

### Book-length narrative

- **1905:** *The Voyage of the “Discovery”*. Scott’s two-volume narrative of the 1901–1904 expedition, combining travel narrative, expedition history, geographical argument, and material derived from shipboard and sledging records. Max Jones’s scholarly edition of Scott’s journals identifies this as the only book Scott prepared and published himself; later abridgments and retellings are manifestations of it, not additional works. ([Jones, “Principal Editions of the Writings of Robert Falcon Scott”](https://www.scribd.com/document/672616345/Robert-Falcon-Scott-Max-Jones-Journals-Scott-s-Last-Expedition))

### Reports and scientific-geographical texts

- **1903:** Report of proceedings sent home by the relief ship *Morning*. This was an official expedition report rather than a literary work; Scott’s subsequent report says that it had been intended to amplify a telegram, itself altered in transmission by Reuters. Its exact bibliographical independence is consequently unstable: report, telegram, and published rendering are related manifestations rather than securely identical texts. ([Scott, “The National Antarctic Expedition”](https://zenodo.org/record/2508573/files/article.pdf))

- **23 March 1904:** *The National Antarctic Expedition: Summary of Proceedings*. An official report from *Discovery* at Auckland Island, addressed to the presidents of the Royal and Royal Geographical societies and subsequently published in *The Geographical Journal*. Scott expressly treated it as an outline of the expedition’s movements and work, leaving scientific conclusions to the responsible specialists. ([published text](https://zenodo.org/record/2508573/files/article.pdf))

- **April 1905:** *Results of the National Antarctic Expedition, I: Geographical*. Scott’s contribution to a six-part preliminary scientific report in *The Geographical Journal*, accompanied by photographs and charts. It is a geographical paper within a collectively authored report, not another version of his expedition book. ([Nature’s contemporary account](https://www.nature.com/articles/072057a0); [Geographical Journal contents index](https://pahar.in/pahar/Journals/Royal%20Geographical%20Society%201830-1954/Geographical%20Journal%201893-1954/1%20Table%20of%20Contents%20for%20Geographical%20Journal%201893-1937%20s.pdf))

- **7 June 1911:** *Lecture on the Ross Ice Barrier*. An oral scientific exposition delivered for the expedition’s “Universitas Antarctica”; the surviving published text is explicitly “from notes by Griffith Taylor,” so it is evidence of Scott’s lecture but not a verbatim authorial manuscript. ([*Scott’s Last Expedition*, vol. II](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Scott%27s_Last_Expedition/Volume_2/Universitas))

### Expedition periodical contributions

- **1902–1903:** Contributions to *The South Polar Times*. Scott wrote miscellaneous humorous, scientific, editorial, and puzzle material for the handwritten-and-typescript expedition magazine, using at least the pseudonyms “Experto Crede,” “Ignotus,” “Glossopteris Flora,” and “Cross Stick.” Because anonymity was part of the magazine’s protocol and individual attributions are not uniformly secure, this is best treated as a partially attributable body of contributions rather than a confidently titled list of separate works. ([Anne Fadiman, “South Polar Times”](https://www.laphamsquarterly.org/roundtable/south-polar-times); [Royal Collection Trust catalogue](https://www.rct.uk/collection/exhibitions/the-heart-of-the-great-alone/the-queens-gallery-buckingham-palace/the-south-polar-times-volume-1-april-to-august-1902))

- **1907:** Preface to the published facsimile of *The South Polar Times*, volumes I–II. Scott’s prefatory text explains the circumstances and intended fidelity of the London reproduction; the magazine itself remains a collective Gesamtkunstwerk of typescript, manuscript, watercolour, drawing, photography, scientific observation, jokes, and verse, edited principally by Ernest Shackleton and Louis Bernacchi rather than authored by Scott alone. ([Royal Collection Trust catalogue](https://www.rct.uk/collection/exhibitions/the-heart-of-the-great-alone/the-queens-gallery-buckingham-palace/the-south-polar-times-volume-1-april-to-august-1902); [publication description](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Scott%2C+Robert+Falcon%2C+1868-1912))

- **1911:** Contributions to the revived *South Polar Times*. Scott again contributed to the expedition magazine edited by Apsley Cherry-Garrard. The periodical is securely collective, while a complete title-by-title attribution of its anonymous matter to Scott is not; the third volume appeared publicly only in 1914. ([University of Cambridge](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/final-newspaper-of-captain-scotts-doomed-expedition-reproduced); [*South Polar Times* publication record](https://natlib.govt.nz/records/21206312))

### Diaries, memoranda, and working records

- **18 October 1910–29 March 1912:** Antarctic base and sledging diaries. Twelve manuscript volumes form Scott’s principal surviving journal obra, moving between daily record, scientific observation, command document, private reflection, and an account increasingly written for possible public readers. Portions covering 24 January–18 June 1911 were rewritten extensively, later sections were carbon-copied, and the 1913 *Scott’s Last Expedition* omitted criticisms and other material; thus manuscript, rewritten text, typescript copies, and published edition are distinct manifestations. ([British Library, Add MS 51024–51035](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-001958842); [“Captain Scott rewrote his story”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/polar-record/article/abs/captain-scott-rewrote-his-story-januaryjune-1911/186F9C4289805194BDB8EF1716BD99B0))

- **1910–1912:** Expedition memoranda, inventories, lists, observations, and related records. These are working instruments rather than a single titled work: some occur inside the diary volumes, while others survive separately in the British Library’s nineteen-volume group of expedition records. Their resistance to “work” status is functional—they document and organize an undertaking rather than present a finished composition. ([British Library, Add MS 51024–51042](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/032-001958841); [Add MS 51024–51035](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-001958842))

### Protocols and commands

- **1910–1912:** Sledging orders and instructions to expedition parties. These are protocols: texts intended to cause and regulate action, including orders in letter form to the geologist Thomas Griffith Taylor. They belong to Scott’s command archive but have no single fixed collective title or composition date. ([British Library, Add MS 51024–51035](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-001958842))

### Manifesto and final messages

- **March 1912:** *Message to the Public*. A signed text written at the end of Scott’s last sledging diary, explaining the expedition’s disaster and implicitly soliciting judgment and support for the dependants of the dead. It acts as well as describes and is therefore properly a manifesto; its first publication, in the editorial construction *Scott’s Last Expedition*, is a later manifestation. ([British Library, Add MS 51035 description](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-001958842))

- **March 1912:** Final letters to named relatives, friends, and public figures. These individual letters—including those to Kathleen Scott, Hannah Scott, J. M. Barrie, Leonard Darwin, J. J. Kinsey, and others—were detached from the last diary and delivered according to Scott’s instructions. They are distinct acts of correspondence rather than chapters of a book; the letter to Barrie was initially printed only in abridged form, making its manuscript, abridgment, and later full publication materially different manifestations. ([British Library, Add MS 51035 description](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-001958842))

### Posthumous editorial constructions

- **1913:** *Scott’s Last Expedition, vol. I: Being the Journals of Captain R. F. Scott*. A posthumous editorial construction arranged by Leonard Huxley from Scott’s diaries, letters, and final message, not a book completed by Scott. The original edition suppressed some criticism, and volume II chiefly contains reports by surviving expedition members, so neither the two-volume whole nor its exact published text should be attributed to Scott without qualification. ([British Library catalogue](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/036-001958842); [Jones, principal-editions bibliography](https://www.scribd.com/document/672616345/Robert-Falcon-Scott-Max-Jones-Journals-Scott-s-Last-Expedition))

- **1914:** *The Voyages of Captain Scott*. Charles Turley’s posthumous retelling from *The Voyage of the “Discovery”* and *Scott’s Last Expedition*, supplemented by J. M. Barrie and Clements Markham. It is an adaptation made from Scott’s texts, not another work made by Scott himself. ([Jones, principal-editions bibliography](https://www.scribd.com/document/672616345/Robert-Falcon-Scott-Max-Jones-Journals-Scott-s-Last-Expedition))
