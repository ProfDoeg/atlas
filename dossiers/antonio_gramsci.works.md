## Works by Antonio Francesco Gramsci

### Scholarly notes and unfinished studies

- **1910–1926:** *Scritti 1910–1926*. The comprehensive corpus of Gramsci’s surviving pre-prison writing: school compositions, university work, journalism, criticism, translations, speeches, party documents, and political essays. It is an **obra** rather than an author-designed book; many newspaper pieces were unsigned or pseudonymous, so attribution remains a philological question, and the National Edition is still incomplete. ([National Edition plan](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/bibliografia-gramsciana-detail?id_s=IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955); [International Gramsci Society](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/resources/pre-prison-index/index.html))

- **1912–1913:** *Appunti di glottologia*. University notes made while attending Matteo Bartoli’s linguistics course. They resist simple authorship: the physical and verbal record is Gramsci’s, but the course and much of its intellectual substance were Bartoli’s; the National Edition consequently classifies them as “documents.” ([Casa Museo Antonio Gramsci](https://www.casamuseogramsci.it/en/antonio-gramsci-eng/works/); [National Edition plan](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/bibliografia-gramsciana-detail?id_s=IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955))

- **1927:** *Prison study plan*. A **project**, communicated in letters to Tatiana Schucht, for studies of Italian intellectuals, comparative linguistics, Pirandello’s theatre, and popular serial literature. It was not itself a finished text, but it helped generate the later prison notebooks. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

### Journalism and editorial creations

- **1914–1926:** *Journalistic writings*. An extensive **body of work**—articles, unsigned editorials, theatre reviews, polemics, reports, translations, and recurring columns—made for such periodicals as *Il Grido del Popolo*, *Avanti!*, *L’Ordine Nuovo*, *L’Unità*, and *La Correspondance internationale*. It cannot responsibly be reduced to a short list of “books”: the principal Italian collected edition occupies six volumes and more than 4,500 pages, while authorship of some unsigned collaborative copy remains uncertain. ([International Gramsci Society pre-prison index](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/resources/pre-prison-index/index.html); [National Edition plan](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/bibliografia-gramsciana-detail?id_s=IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955))

- **1916–1920:** *Sotto la Mole*. A recurring journalistic column in the Turin edition of *Avanti!* comprising urban observation, cultural criticism, theatre reviewing, and political intervention. It is a serial form rather than a single autonomous book. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **11 February 1917:** *La Città futura*. A one-issue socialist newspaper edited by Gramsci and largely written by him, containing four signed articles alongside excerpts selected from other writers. As edited assemblage, political intervention, and printed object, it is simultaneously an **obra** and a small **Gesamtkunstwerk** of militant journalism. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **September 1917–October 1918:** *Il Grido del Popolo* under Gramsci’s editorship. An editorial **project** and collective weekly manifestation, not wholly an individually authored work; Gramsci wrote for it, selected contributions, and shaped entire issues, including one devoted to free trade. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1919–1920:** *L’Ordine Nuovo: Rassegna settimanale di cultura socialista*. A collectively founded weekly—Gramsci, Angelo Tasca, Umberto Terracini, and Palmiro Togliatti were its makers—with Gramsci as editorial secretary. It acted as workshop, manifesto, and organizing instrument for the factory-council movement, so authorship belongs partly to Gramsci and partly to an editorial collective. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1921–1922:** *L’Ordine Nuovo* daily. The Piedmont edition of *Avanti!* was remade under this title with Gramsci in editorial control; it was a collective newspaper and political instrument rather than one continuous authored text. Its articles form the corpus later edited as *Socialismo e fascismo: L’Ordine Nuovo 1921–1922*. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html); [pre-prison corpus description](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/resources/pre-prison-index/index.html))

- **1923–1924:** *Articles signed “G. Masci”*. Pseudonymous reports and analyses of Italian politics and fascism written for *La Correspondance internationale*. They belong to Gramsci’s journalistic corpus, but the pseudonym and editorial circumstances make attribution an essential part of identifying the objects. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1923–1924:** *L’Unità*. Gramsci proposed the newspaper, its title, and its worker-and-peasant political orientation; its first issue appeared in February 1924 under a collective editorial staff. This is best classified as an institutional **project** he initiated, not as a text he individually authored. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1924:** *Critica Proletaria*. An unrealized project for a quarterly of Marxist studies and political culture. No finished periodical appeared, but the intention and editorial design constitute the work that survives. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

### Manifestos, protocols, speeches, and political interventions

- **1919:** *Ai commissari di reparto delle officine Fiat-Centro e Brevetti*. A collective political **manifesto** addressed to factory workshop commissioners and published by *L’Ordine Nuovo*. Its precise individual authorship should not be detached from the journal’s collective factory-council practice. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1924:** *La crisi italiana*. A political report delivered to the Communist Party central committee and then published under this title. It is both analysis and intervention: a text meant to direct collective action rather than merely describe the Matteotti crisis. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1925:** *Dispense della scuola per corrispondenza del Partito comunista d’Italia*. Printed study materials prepared for the party’s correspondence school. These are pedagogical **protocols**—texts designed to form political cadres—and survive as part of the pre-prison corpus rather than as a single authorially published book. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **16 May 1925:** *Speech against the bill on secret associations*. Gramsci’s only speech in the Italian Chamber of Deputies, attacking legislation used against opposition organizations. It is an oral parliamentary act preserved textually, not originally a book or essay. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1925–1926:** *Tesi di Lione*. Programmatic theses prepared principally by Gramsci in collaboration with Palmiro Togliatti and adopted at the Communist Party of Italy’s Third Congress. They are a collective **manifesto and protocol** for remaking the party; sole authorship should therefore not be asserted. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html); [Fondazione Gramsci bibliographical record](https://bg.fondazionegramsci.org/biblio-gramsci/detail/IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955/Edizione-nazionale-degli-scritti-di-Antonio-Gramsci.html))

- **1926:** *Cinque anni di vita del partito*. A dictated report on the Lyon Congress, transcribed by Riccardo Ravagnan and published in *L’Unità*. Its material form is therefore collaborative—Gramsci’s dictated discourse mediated through another writer’s hand and newspaper production. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1926:** *Tesi sul lavoro contadino*. Agrarian theses approved by the party congress at Bari and described by the standard chronology as directly inspired by Gramsci. “Inspired by” is not secure individual authorship, so this is best recorded as a collective political protocol with Gramscian intellectual responsibility. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **14 October 1926:** *Lettera al Comitato centrale del Partito comunista sovietico*. A political letter composed on behalf of the Italian party leadership concerning the conflict inside the Soviet party. Togliatti withheld it rather than transmitting it, so it is simultaneously correspondence, an institutional intervention, and a failed political act. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html); [bibliography of first publications](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/igsn/bibliographies/b05_3.shtml))

- **October 1926:** *Alcuni temi della quistione meridionale*. An unfinished political essay intended for a projected party periodical and first published posthumously in 1930. Its first editors explicitly reported that it was incomplete and likely unrevised; later titles such as *La questione meridionale* name editorial presentations, not a finished book issued by Gramsci. ([International Gramsci Society bibliography](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/igsn/bibliographies/b05_3.shtml); [chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

### Organizations, schools, and collective political forms

- **1917:** *Club di vita morale*. A small proletarian cultural discussion circle formed by Gramsci with Carlo Boccardo, Attilio Carena, and Andrea Viglongo. It is an organizational **creation**, not a text, conceived to join socialist politics with sustained intellectual formation. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1919–1920:** *Factory-council project*. Through *L’Ordine Nuovo*, manifestos, translations, programs, and direct organizing, Gramsci and his collaborators helped make the Turin factory councils into an institutional experiment in workers’ democracy. It resists designation as a discrete “work”: it was a collective political form enacted through organizations and texts. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1925:** *Scuola per corrispondenza del Partito comunista d’Italia*. A cadre-education system that Gramsci helped establish and supplied with lessons. The school—not merely its printed notes—was a pedagogical and organizational creation. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

- **1926–1927:** *School for political prisoners at Ustica*. A collectively organized prison school in which Gramsci taught history while Amadeo Bordiga taught science. Its classes and social relations were the made thing; no single stable text exhausts it. ([International Gramsci Society chronology](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/about_gramsci/chronology.html))

### Prison notebooks: manuscript obra and unfinished project

- **1929–1932:** *Quaderni di traduzioni*. Translation notebooks containing Gramsci’s exercises and translations from several languages. The National Edition separates them from his theoretical notebooks because translation here is a scholarly practice and material manuscript object, not simply an original treatise. ([Digital Library Antonio Gramsci](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/en/prison-notebooks); [Casa Museo Antonio Gramsci](https://www.casamuseogramsci.it/en/antonio-gramsci-eng/works/))

- **1929–1935:** *Quaderni miscellanei*. Notebooks in which reading notes, preliminary formulations, source extracts, and first-draft reflections coexist. They are working instruments and “texts in motion,” not finished books; Gramsci repeatedly revised and transferred material from them. ([National Edition plan](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/bibliografia-gramsciana-detail?id_s=IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955); [Digital Library Antonio Gramsci](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/en/prison-notebooks))

- **1932–1935:** *Quaderni speciali*. Thematic notebooks in which Gramsci regrouped and rewrote earlier notes on subjects including philosophy, intellectuals, Machiavelli, literature, journalism, folklore, Americanism, and subaltern groups. They represent movement toward books never completed, so each is both manuscript **obra** and unfinished **project**. ([National Edition plan](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/bibliografia-gramsciana-detail?id_s=IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955); [International Gramsci Society concordance](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/resources/concordance_table/))

- **1929–1935:** *Quaderni del carcere*. The complete material creation consists of thirty-three manuscript notebooks, later classified as translation, miscellaneous, and special notebooks; Tatiana Schucht’s Roman-numeral cataloguing and later editors’ numbering are not Gramsci’s final book plan. The whole is an unfinished intellectual workshop—2,848 manuscript pages rather than an author-completed publication—and the familiar thematic volumes issued after his death are editorial manifestations of it. ([Digital Library Antonio Gramsci](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/en/prison-notebooks); [Fondazione Gramsci archive](https://fondazionegramsci.org/archivi/); [International Gramsci Society concordance](https://www.internationalgramscisociety.org/resources/concordance_table/))

### Correspondence

- **1906–1937:** *Epistolario*. The surviving correspondence encompasses family letters, political communications, prison letters, applications, drafts, and messages written before and during confinement. It is a relational **body of work**, not one book: later collections entitled *Lettere dal carcere* are editorial selections, while the National Edition plans nine chronological and parallel-correspondence volumes. ([National Edition plan](https://gramsci.digital-library.it/bibliografia-gramsciana-detail?id_s=IT-GRAMSCI-BIB00001-0018955); [Casa Museo Antonio Gramsci](https://www.casamuseogramsci.it/en/antonio-gramsci-eng/works/))
