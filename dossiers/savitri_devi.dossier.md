# Savitri Devi: Research Dossier

## Scope, Method, and Evidentiary Labels

This dossier concerns **Savitri Devi Mukherji**, born **Maximiani Julia Portas** (1905–1982), not the numerous other South Asian women named Savitri Devi and not the yoga teacher **Indra Devi**.

The reconstruction draws principally on:

- Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke’s archival biography *Hitler’s Priestess*;
- Savitri Devi’s books, memoirs, recorded interviews, surviving correspondence, passports, and manuscript chronologies;
- a declassified British military-intelligence file;
- records and scholarship concerning Hindu nationalism, postwar neo-Nazism, the World Union of National Socialists, Holocaust denial, and ecofascism;
- contemporaneous reporting on her 1949 arrest and trial;
- recent academic and journalistic discussions of her reception.

The Savitri Devi Archive preserves valuable primary material but is operated by ideological admirers. Its editorial interpretations are therefore distinguished from the underlying documents.

Labels used:

- **Documented fact:** supported by a contemporaneous document, official record, publication, dated correspondence, photograph, or multiple independent sources.
- **Reported fact:** stated in a generally reliable secondary source but not independently verified here from an original record.
- **Self-report:** Savitri Devi’s own retrospective account.
- **Allegation or theory:** a claim for which some evidence exists but which is not established.
- **Rumor or myth:** a narrative unsupported by adequate evidence, or a supernatural claim not susceptible to ordinary historical verification.
- **Propaganda:** material consciously created to advance a political or religious cause.

## Names, Titles, and Self-Presentation

**Maximiani Julia Portas** was the name recorded by her principal biographer. “Portaz” appears in passports and publications, while **Maximine Portaz** was used for her English translations of Diderot and Voltaire.

At Santiniketan in 1935, women in her dormitory reportedly called her **Savitri**, interpreted in her later account as “solar energy.” She probably added **Devi** herself. She rejected the mistaken form “Mrs. Devi,” since Devi was not her husband’s surname.

She used **Savitri Devi Mukherji** after marrying Asit Krishna Mukherji. Admirers subsequently called her “Hitler’s priestess,” a “prophetess of Hitlerism,” or, in publicity issued by Ernst Zündel’s Samisdat Publishers, “Hitler’s Guru.” These were promotional or retrospective titles, not offices she formally held.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and grandparents

Nicholas Goodrick-Clarke reconstructs the family as follows:

- Her mother was **Julia Portas, née Nash**, from an English family connected with Cornwall. Julia was one of two surviving daughters of **William Nash**, who had married a first cousin.
- Her father, whose given name is not consistently supplied in accessible sources, belonged to Lyon’s Greek community. His father was Greek; his mother was an Italian woman from London. He had acquired French nationality through residence.
- Maximiani therefore described herself as of Greek, Italian, and English ancestry.

Her father was reportedly a respected member of Lyon’s Greek community. Maximiani’s attachment to Greece developed early and became central to her identity. She came to regard modern French republican nationalism as alien and the ancient Greek world as her spiritual ancestry.

Her father died in Lyon on **24 February 1932**, reportedly of “paralysis,” probably a stroke. Her mother died in Lyon on **25 March 1960**.

### Siblings and extended family

No sibling of Maximiani is securely identified in the principal published biography. Her later correspondence mentions Mukherji in-laws, including a Mrs. Banerjee/Bannerjee in New Delhi. The journalist **Sumanta Banerjee** has been described in later reporting as her nephew by marriage and as politically opposed to her views.

### Marriage

She met the Bengali editor **Asit Krishna Mukherji** no later than 1938, although the exact first meeting is uncertain. They married civilly in Calcutta on **29 September 1939** and underwent a Hindu religious ceremony on **9 June 1940**.

The marriage combined practical and ideological considerations:

- **Documented fact:** marriage made her the wife of a British Indian subject and enabled her to obtain a British Indian passport.
- **Reported fact/self-report:** she feared wartime internment or removal as a Greek foreign national.
- **Documented ideological affinity:** Mukherji had edited the pro-Axis *New Mercury* and was sympathetic to Germany and Japan.
- **Reported fact:** the union was largely companionate and political rather than conventionally domestic or sexual.
- They lived apart for long periods after 1945 but remained married and maintained contact.
- No children are documented.

Mukherji died in Savitri Devi’s New Delhi apartment on **21 March 1977**. The archive chronology says he suffered heat stroke while fasting to death. The exact medical and intentional circumstances are not independently established.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1905–1923

Maximiani was born in Lyon on 30 September 1905 and grew up during the French Third Republic. Her education exposed her to republican civic ideology, which she rejected. She later remembered refusing to honor the French Revolution and its egalitarian ideals.

She completed the **Certificat d’études** in 1917 and entered the Lycée Anatole France, studying Latin and Greek. In 1918 financial limitations led her family to transfer her to a modern lycée.

A formative encounter occurred in 1918 when she met a German prisoner of war in Lyon. She later made this part of her self-created narrative of lifelong sympathy for Germany.

Around 1920–1921 she won a bicycle in an essay contest based on a sanitized biography of Louis Pasteur. On learning that Pasteur had experimented on animals, she returned the prize. The story comes through her own recollection and sympathetic chronology, but it is consistent with her later uncompromising opposition to animal experimentation.

She received a secondary-school diploma in 1921 and a superior diploma in 1923. In February or March 1922 she visited an asylum at Laforce in southwestern France. Her retrospective description expressed revulsion toward intellectually disabled and mentally ill inmates. This episode prefigures the combination of animal protection, eugenic hierarchy, and indifference or hostility toward vulnerable humans that later appeared in *Impeachment of Man*.

## Education and Intellectual Formation

### University of Lyon

She began philosophy studies at the University of Lyon in January 1924.

Her recorded qualifications included:

- psychology examination, June 1924;
- general philosophy and logic, March 1925;
- ethics and sociology, June 1925;
- *Diplôme d’études supérieures de philosophie*, November 1925, with work on belief and William James’s “The Will to Believe”;
- final philosophy examination, February 1927;
- master’s degree in philosophy, approved July 1928;
- science studies including physical chemistry, general chemistry, biochemistry, and mineralogy, 1930–1931;
- master’s or equivalent science degree, approved in 1932;
- two doctoral theses approved in 1934;
- doctorate in philosophy defended at Lyon on **1 April 1935**.

Her principal doctoral work concerned simplicity in mathematical thought or the philosophy of science. Her complementary thesis concerned the Greek religious reformer **Theophilos Kaïris**. The precise original titles vary across bibliographical accounts and should be checked against the Lyon university copy before quotation.

### Formative reading

Documented or self-acknowledged influences included:

- ancient Greek religion and history;
- Friedrich Nietzsche, especially *The Will to Power*;
- the French Parnassian poet **Leconte de Lisle**, whose racialized poetic images of ancient India impressed her as a teenager;
- Greek poet **Kostis Palamas**;
- Ernest Renan and currents of French racial and antisemitic thought;
- William James;
- Indo-European philology and nineteenth-century “Aryan” theories;
- Hindu texts, particularly the *Bhagavad Gita*, Puranic time cycles, and the doctrine of divine avatars;
- later, René Guénon and, to a lesser degree, Julius Evola;
- Nazi racial ideology and Adolf Hitler’s political mythology.

### Greece and Palestine

She first landed in Greece on **9 August 1923**. On 9 November she visited the Athenian Acropolis while Hitler’s Beer Hall Putsch was taking place in Munich—a coincidence she later endowed with symbolic meaning.

She returned repeatedly, traveled through the Peloponnese on foot and horseback, supported herself by tutoring French, and researched Kaïris. On **28 May 1928** she renounced French citizenship in favor of Greek nationality.

In spring 1929 she made an Orthodox Easter pilgrimage from Athens through Rhodes to Palestine, returning through Cyprus and perhaps Egypt. She later claimed that observing Jewish settlement and Jewish–Arab tensions in Palestine crystallized her antisemitism and made her realize that she was, in spirit, a National Socialist. This is a **self-report**, not proof of membership in any Nazi organization. Goodrick-Clarke dates her explicit admiration for National Socialism to the late 1920s.

## Migration to India and Hindu Formation, 1935–1939

Contrary to accounts dating her Indian migration to 1932, passport and educational chronology place her departure after completing her doctorate in 1935.

She applied for an Indian visa on 2 April 1935, sailed through Ceylon, arrived there on 13 May, and entered India at Dhanushkodi on **17 May 1935**. She visited Rameswaram during Vaishakha Purnima, Pondicherry, Madras, Calcutta, Dacca, and Gaudiya Math institutions.

At **Rabindranath Tagore’s Visva-Bharati University at Santiniketan**, she studied Hindi and improved her Bengali. She met Tagore, Amiya Chakravarty, and Margaret Spiegel. The encounter with Tagore is recorded in her chronology, but no close intellectual or personal relationship is established.

During 1935–1937 she traveled widely:

- Lucknow;
- Jalandhar, where she taught English and Indian history;
- Lahore, where she studied hatha yoga with an unnamed South Indian Brahmin museum curator;
- Haridwar and Gangotri, reportedly on foot;
- Kashmir and the Amarnath ice lingam;
- Mathura during Janmashtami;
- the Khyber Pass;
- Baghdad, Babylon, Syria, Basra, and possibly Egypt.

She stopped intensive hatha yoga after experiencing optic-nerve pain and emphasized **karma yoga** and **bhakti yoga** instead.

She met **Swami Satyananda**, probably in 1937, and worked as an itinerant lecturer for the **Hindu Mission**. Her lectures and pamphlets advocated Hindu consolidation against Christian missionary activity and Islam, while treating Hindu caste and religious diversity as survivals of an ancient Aryan order.

Her first important political pamphlet, *A Warning to the Hindus*, was composed in 1938–1939 and published in Calcutta in 1939 under Hindu Mission patronage. It urged the formation of a Hindu political unity or “Hindudom.” Academic analysis places the book within late-colonial Bengali Hindu nationalism, rather than treating it simply as a foreign import.

### Relationship to Hinduism

Her “conversion” was unconventional. She adopted Hindu dress, names, pilgrimage, devotional practice, and political identification, but there is no universally recognized Hindu conversion authority and no single, securely documented initiation ceremony. Her Hinduism was filtered through European racial theory and her belief that India preserved living “Aryan” paganism.

She did not regard all Indians as racially equal. Her conception connected caste hierarchy, skin color, heredity, culture, and spiritual rank. At the same time, *The Non-Hindu Indians and Indian Unity* proposed political absorption of non-Hindu Indians into a territorial-cultural Hindu nation.

## Marriage, Axis Politics, and Wartime Activity, 1938–1945

### Asit Krishna Mukherji and pro-Axis publishing

Mukherji began publishing *The New Mercury* in Calcutta in 1935. A declassified British file records that the German consul-general praised the paper in April 1936 and intended to recommend it to German firms for advertising. British authorities closed the paper in 1937.

Mukherji later published *The Eastern Economist*, reportedly with Japanese support, and operated the Susil Gupta imprint. His publishing structures printed or distributed several of Savitri Devi’s books and translations.

### Intelligence suspicions and espionage claims

A declassified MI7 file, **KW 93/39-Poll(W), “Potentially hostile foreigners,”** lists:

- “Mukherjee, A. K., Editor, ‘New Mercury’, Calcutta,” noting German consular interest;
- “Savitri Devi, Miss Mazimiani M. Portaz,” identifying her as Greek, giving a Calcutta address, and stating that she was connected with Indian propaganda.

This establishes that British intelligence **suspected** the couple. It does not, by itself, prove specific espionage operations.

Savitri Devi later claimed that she and Mukherji entertained Allied officers at weekly gatherings, extracted information, and passed reports through Indian couriers crossing into Japanese-controlled Burma. Goodrick-Clarke treats the Mukherjis as operating a Japanese intelligence cell. A Japanese-related document cited by the Savitri Devi Archive appears to show concern for her family and offers circumstantial corroboration.

The correct evidentiary formulation is:

- **Documented fact:** British intelligence listed both as potential Axis agents.
- **Documented fact:** Mukherji’s publications had German and reportedly Japanese contacts.
- **Self-report, partially corroborated:** the couple gathered and transmitted Allied military information to Japanese agents.
- **Unresolved:** the operational value, volume, recipients, and consequences of that intelligence.
- **Not established:** claims that she was formally an officer or employee of the German Sicherheitsdienst. Later summaries calling her an “SD agent” are stronger than the accessible documentary record supports.

### Political connections

Through the Hindu Mission she reportedly met **Subhas Chandra Bose**. Goodrick-Clarke says the Mukherjis had political contact with Bose, but the extent of Savitri Devi’s direct relationship is unclear.

In September 1941 she received a thirty-minute interview with **Mohandas K. Gandhi** at Sevagram. Her later portrayal of Gandhi categorized him as a “man against time”—a moral figure whose universalism and nonviolence she considered politically opposed to her own hierarchical worldview.

### Akhnaton studies

During the war she turned increasingly to the Egyptian pharaoh Akhnaton/Akhenaten, whom she presented as a solar monotheist or pantheist who anticipated a scientific religion of cosmic order.

Works from this period included:

- *L’Étang aux lotus*;
- *The Non-Hindu Indians and Indian Unity*;
- *Akhnaton’s Eternal Message*;
- *Joy of the Sun*;
- *A Son of God*;
- the possibly lost or bibliographically uncertain *A Perfect Man*.

### Psychological crisis in 1945

Following Germany’s defeat, she spent weeks traveling in India in an effort to avoid war news. In June 1945 she attempted suicide at Varkala on the Malabar Coast. This is reported in her biographical chronology and is consistent with her own accounts of despair after the Third Reich’s collapse. She survived, returned to Calcutta, began *Impeachment of Man*, and during the Durga and Kali festivals consecrated herself to Kali.

## Europe, Iceland, and the Formation of Postwar Hitlerism, 1945–1948

She left Bombay for Britain on 2 November 1945 and arrived at Southampton on 15 November using her status as the wife of a British Indian subject. She moved among London, Lyon, Paris, Jersey, and other locations.

In London she:

- lectured about Akhnaton;
- met Muriel Gantry and Veronica Vassar;
- corresponded with **Aldous Huxley** about Akhnaton;
- worked as a dresser for Indian dancer **Ram Gopal**;
- met the poet and printer Geoffrey Potocki de Montalk.

She completed *Impeachment of Man* in Lyon on **29 March 1946**.

From November 1946 to July 1947 she lived in Iceland. She visited the erupting Mount Hekla, spent a night on its slopes, visited Goðafoss, and completed *Akhnaton: A Play* in Reykjavík. Her attraction to volcanoes and solar landscapes became part of her nature mysticism and self-image as a pilgrim to sites of elemental power.

In 1948 she traveled with Ram Gopal through Scandinavia. In Stockholm she met explorer **Sven Hedin**, a public admirer of Germany. During a train journey through occupied Germany on 15–16 June she distributed food, cigarettes, and pro-Nazi material.

She sold or used personal jewelry to finance thousands of leaflets and posters. Potocki printed material bearing slogans such as “Germany, arise and fight!” She exploited travel permits obtained through acquaintances, including Georgette Soustelle, to enter different occupation zones.

## Propaganda Mission, Arrest, Trial, and Imprisonment, 1948–1949

Between 1948 and February 1949 she made repeated journeys in occupied Germany, distributing Nazi propaganda and seeking sympathetic former Nazis.

She was arrested in Cologne on **20 February 1949**, transferred through Düsseldorf, and imprisoned at Werl. A preliminary hearing occurred in Essen; her trial took place in Düsseldorf on **5 April 1949** before a British occupation court.

### Legal outcome

- **Documented fact:** she was convicted for distributing National Socialist propaganda and for conduct described as maintaining or reviving the military and Nazi spirit in Germany.
- **Reported sentence:** three years’ imprisonment.
- She served only several months and was released on **18 August 1949**, reportedly following intervention or clemency associated with the British authorities and her status as the wife of an Indian/British subject.

Contemporaneous German newspaper reporting depicted her attempting to turn the proceedings into a political platform. Her memoir *Defiance* likewise presents arrest and imprisonment as a consciously embraced martyrdom.

At Werl she met **Hertha Ehlert**, a former SS concentration-camp guard convicted at the Belsen trial. Devi treated Ehlert and other imprisoned Nazi women as comrades and later maintained contacts with them.

A prison search on 30 May confiscated manuscripts of *Impeachment of Man*, *Gold in the Furnace*, and *The Lightning and the Sun*. They were returned on 17 June. She completed *Gold in the Furnace* in prison on 16 July.

Colonel Vickers, the commandant, reportedly called her “the most objectionable Nazi” he had ever met. This became a favored part of her self-presentation and later supplied the subtitle of *Long-Whiskers and the Two-Legged Goddess*.

## Writing and Neo-Nazi Pilgrimage, 1949–1960

After release she returned to Lyon and wrote *Defiance*. Mukherji published it in Calcutta in 1951, followed by *Gold in the Furnace* in 1952.

In 1953 she undertook an ideological pilgrimage through sites associated with Hitler and National Socialism:

- Linz;
- Braunau am Inn;
- Berchtesgaden and the Obersalzberg;
- Munich;
- Landsberg;
- Nuremberg;
- the Teutoburg Forest and Hermannsdenkmal;
- Externsteine.

She visited the widow and grave of executed Einsatzgruppen commander **Otto Ohlendorf**. This was not incidental historical tourism: *Pilgrimage* presents such places as sacred geography.

She lived with a German associate identified pseudonymously as **Katja U.** in Emsdetten. German police searched her room in December 1954, confiscated *Pilgrimage*, and interrogated her for approximately ten hours. The manuscript was later returned.

In 1955 she moved near Hanover and taught in a language school. She completed *The Lightning and the Sun* on **21 March 1956**.

### The “men in time” system

Her central historical theory classified figures into three types:

- **Men in Time:** successful agents of historical decline, represented principally by Genghis Khan.
- **Men above Time:** spiritually elevated but politically ineffective figures, represented by Akhnaton.
- **Men against Time:** violent figures who resist decline and prepare a new golden age, represented by Hitler.

Hitler was presented not simply as a politician but as a divine avatar or precursor to **Kalki**, Vishnu’s final avatar. His defeat became, in her scheme, evidence that he had appeared too early in the Kali Yuga, not evidence against his mission.

### Journey back to India, 1957

In 1957 she traveled from Germany through:

- Athens;
- Alexandria and Cairo;
- Akhetaten/Tell el-Amarna;
- Beirut;
- Damascus;
- Baghdad;
- Tehran and Mashhad;
- Zahedan;
- Quetta;
- Lahore;
- New Delhi;
- Calcutta.

Near Cairo she met **Johannes von Leers**, a former Nazi propagandist then living in Egypt as Omar Amin, and Mahmoud Saleh. She suffered illness from contaminated water and another attack of elephantiasis.

On a train after Mathura she was assaulted and robbed. In Orissa she briefly worked as a translator for East German engineers at Joda. Back in Calcutta she taught at a French school.

In 1958 she published *The Lightning and the Sun*, *Pilgrimage*, and *Paul de Tarse*. *Impeachment of Man* followed in 1959.

## Neo-Nazi International, 1960–1971

After her mother’s death she returned to Europe in 1960. She spent approximately six weeks in Spain with former SS commando **Otto Skorzeny** and met Belgian collaborator **Léon Degrelle**.

She taught in Montbrison beginning in January 1961. That May she attended a British National Party camp in Norfolk, meeting Beryl Cheetham and appearing with Bruno Ludtke, Roland Kerr-Ritchie, and Robert Lyons.

### World Union of National Socialists

In August 1962 she attended Colin Jordan’s camp near Guiting Power in the Cotswolds. There she met **George Lincoln Rockwell**, leader of the American Nazi Party.

Photographs and scholarship on transnational neo-Nazism establish that she participated in the conference leading to the **Cotswold Agreement** and the World Union of National Socialists. She was one of the agreement’s signatories.

The camp attracted police and press attention. On 8 August Special Branch officers stopped her, and her passport was marked to bar further entry into Britain. She nevertheless entered on at least one later occasion and was repeatedly refused entry in 1963, 1964, and 1966.

Her relationship with Rockwell was political and ideological. She corresponded with him, and her religious interpretation of Hitlerism supplied ideas for *National Socialist World*. Claims that she was the principal intellectual power behind Rockwell are interpretive rather than measurable facts.

### Teaching and dismissal

She taught at:

- Montbrison;
- Saint-Étienne;
- Firminy;
- correspondence courses administered in France.

The Ligue internationale contre l’antisémitisme, or LICA, campaigned against her in 1964 and 1966. Her sympathetic chronology calls this “persecution.” The documentary core is that her publicly Nazi and antisemitic activity became the subject of complaints and that in October 1966 she was removed from classroom teaching and relegated to correcting correspondence papers. That employment ended in 1969.

### Françoise Dior and other associates

She became close to **Françoise Dior**, niece of fashion designer Christian Dior and then a prominent French-British neo-Nazi. Devi stayed for months at Dior’s homes, including Ducey in Normandy, though the relationship reportedly became strained by domestic incompatibility.

Other associates included:

- Colin Jordan;
- John Tyndall;
- Hans-Ulrich Rudel;
- Einar Åberg;
- Bruno Ludtke;
- Beryl Cheetham;
- Gaston-Armand Amaudruz;
- Saint-Loup/Marc Augier;
- Matt Koehl;
- Ernst Zündel;
- Revilo P. Oliver;
- Miguel Serrano;
- Johannes von Leers;
- Léon Degrelle;
- Otto Skorzeny.

## Return to India and Final Decade, 1971–1982

She left Europe in June 1971, lived briefly in Poona, and moved to New Delhi. The Hindu Mahasabha initially supplied a guest room. She completed *Souvenirs et réflexions d’une Aryenne* there on 12 September 1971.

She subsequently lived in modest rented rooms in South Extension, New Delhi. Accounts describe sparse furniture, numerous cats and birds, failing eyesight, financial insecurity, and intermittent work teaching French.

### Assault and illnesses

- On **1 March 1974**, she was assaulted and robbed of most of her jewelry in New Delhi and reportedly left for dead.
- She suffered severe dysentery in 1974.
- She experienced malaria and dysentery in 1976.
- She underwent surgery for glaucoma on 9 October 1976.
- Cataract operations followed in May 1980 and March 1981.
- Recurrent elephantiasis affected her from the 1930s; she counted at least 27 attacks by 1980.
- On **30 March 1981**, she suffered a stroke causing partial right-side paralysis.

Her work at the Alliance Française ended in August 1977. In June 1980 she was dismissed from teaching French to United Nations personnel. Accessible sources do not establish whether these dismissals were principally political, medical, age-related, or performance-related.

In November 1978 she recorded extensive English-language interviews later edited as *And Time Rolls On*. In 1982 she recorded a further seven hours in French.

### Final migrations

After the 1981 stroke she stayed with friends in Jaipur and New Delhi. On 4 October 1981 she flew to Germany, stayed in an old-age home near Munich and with supporters, but German authorities ordered her to leave by January 1982.

During 1982 she moved among:

- Lyon hospitals and hospices;
- Genas-Vurey;
- Alix;
- Steinen near Lörrach;
- Kaltenbach near Traunstein;
- Prien;
- Munich;
- Lausanne;
- Paris;
- Nantes;
- Sible Hedingham.

Her late life was sustained largely by a transnational network of ideological associates rather than by accumulated wealth.

## Employment, Institutions, Publishing, and Finances

### Employment

Known work included:

- private French tutoring in Greece;
- English and Indian-history teaching at Jalandhar;
- Hindu Mission lecturer;
- dresser for Ram Gopal’s dance company;
- language-school teaching near Hanover;
- interpreter for East German engineers in Orissa;
- French-school and Alliance Française teaching in Calcutta and New Delhi;
- French public-school supply teaching in Montbrison, Saint-Étienne, and Firminy;
- correction of correspondence-course papers;
- private instruction to United Nations personnel.

### Publishing structures

Most of her books were self-financed, published by her husband, or issued through small sympathetic presses:

- **Hindu Mission**, Calcutta;
- **A. K. Mukherji**, Calcutta;
- **Susil Gupta**, an imprint controlled by Mukherji;
- **Thacker, Spink and Co.**;
- **Philosophical Publishing House**, London;
- later neo-Nazi presses including **Noontide Press**, Samisdat, and retrospective archive publishers.

There is no evidence that her writings generated substantial lifetime income. She sold jewelry to finance propaganda, traveled cheaply, lived in rented rooms or with supporters, and depended on teaching and friends.

### Property, wealth, businesses, and investments

No business ownership, patents, substantial investments, real-estate portfolio, or significant inherited fortune is documented. Her jewelry functioned as portable savings and was repeatedly sold or stolen. Mukherji controlled small publishing imprints but no reliable financial accounts survive in the consulted material.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### Greek pagan nationalism

Her earliest mature identity centered on Greece, classical paganism, ancestry, and rejection of French republican universalism.

### Antisemitism and National Socialism

By her own chronology, the Palestine journey of 1929 crystallized an existing hostility toward Judaism and egalitarian monotheism. She embraced Nazi racial hierarchy before settling in India and remained committed after the Holocaust and Germany’s defeat.

Her writings contain explicit antisemitic conspiracy theories, approval of racial hierarchy, admiration for Hitler, and hostility toward racial mixing. These are not allegations by opponents; they are documented positions in her own publications.

### Hindu nationalism

She interpreted Hindu traditions as the last living form of ancient Indo-European pagan civilization. Her prewar pamphlets urged Hindu political organization and resistance to Islam and Christianity.

This relationship remains contested:

- Some scholars treat her as an active participant in late-colonial Hindu-nationalist politics.
- Critics describe her Hinduism as racialized European appropriation.
- Her contemporary Hindu associates nevertheless employed her as a lecturer, published her work, accommodated her, and gave her access to nationalist networks.
- No evidence shows that she commanded a mass constituency in India.

### Akhnaton and solar religion

She portrayed Akhnaton as a “man above time”: an advocate of truth, sunlight, beauty, and cosmic order whose political failure reflected excessive gentleness.

### Biocentrism and animal advocacy

*Impeachment of Man* rejects human-centered ethics and calls for protection of animals, plants, species, and ecosystems. She opposed vivisection, slaughter, hunting, circuses, fur, and industrial exploitation of nature.

Her position was not modern egalitarian animal-rights philosophy. It was integrated with hierarchy, eugenics, racial selection, coercive population policy, and approval of severe punishment. She considered some animals and ecologically ordered civilizations more valuable than many humans.

### Esoteric Hitlerism

Her distinctive synthesis joined:

- Hindu cyclic time;
- Vishnu’s avatars;
- Nazi racial mythology;
- Hitler devotion;
- cosmic struggle between order and decay;
- expectation of a future golden age;
- sacred geography and relic-like pilgrimage;
- reincarnation and supernatural destiny.

This was primarily a post-1945 religious reconstruction of political defeat. It permitted followers to interpret Nazism as a timeless revelation rather than a defeated state ideology.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Asit Krishna Mukherji

Husband, publisher, ideological collaborator, and alleged intelligence partner. He provided legal status, publishing infrastructure, and access to Bengali pro-Axis circles.

### Swami Satyananda and the Hindu Mission

Mentor and institutional patron during her early Hindu-nationalist activity. The mission sponsored lectures and *A Warning to the Hindus*.

### Subhas Chandra Bose

Reported political contact through Hindu Mission and Mukherji networks. The depth of direct collaboration is unresolved.

### Mohandas Gandhi

One documented half-hour meeting at Sevagram in September 1941. She rejected his universalism and nonviolence while treating him as personally principled.

### Rabindranath Tagore

Met at Santiniketan in 1935. No continuing collaboration is established.

### Aldous Huxley

Corresponded about Akhnaton in 1944–1946. Surviving letters establish an intellectual contact, not endorsement by Huxley of her Nazism.

### Muriel Gantry

Long-term British friend and host. Devi died at Gantry’s home in Sible Hedingham.

### Françoise Dior

Close friend, host, and political associate in European neo-Nazi circles.

### George Lincoln Rockwell and Colin Jordan

Organizational associates in the 1962 creation of the World Union of National Socialists. She influenced the religious and philosophical side of their international movement.

### Miguel Serrano

Documented correspondent in 1980 and fellow theorist of esoteric Hitlerism. The surviving letters suggest that their systems developed partly independently; simple claims that one wholly derived from the other are overstated.

### Ernst Zündel

Later publisher and interviewer. Goodrick-Clarke credited her with influence on his Holocaust denial, but archive editors dispute the chronology. Zündel was already developing within denialist networks, so direct causal priority remains unresolved.

### Miriam/Myriam Hirn

Friend, caregiver, correspondent, and eventual literary heir. Hirn preserved material and authorized archive reproduction.

## Controversies, Crimes, Allegations, and Evidentiary Assessment

### Nazi propaganda conviction

**Court finding/documented fact:** convicted in Düsseldorf in April 1949 for illegal National Socialist propaganda in occupied Germany. She did not deny distributing the material and treated conviction as a political honor.

### Wartime espionage

**Documented suspicion:** British intelligence listed her and Mukherji as potentially hostile Axis-connected persons.

**Self-report with circumstantial corroboration:** she described collecting information from Allied officers and passing it to Japanese intelligence through couriers.

**Unresolved:** no consulted operational file establishes the precise intelligence transmitted, whether German agencies formally directed her, or whether any mission affected military operations.

### Holocaust denial

**Documented fact:** her postwar writings minimized, denied, rationalized, or displaced Nazi mass murder. Goodrick-Clarke describes her as one of the early postwar Holocaust deniers.

Her procedure included:

- treating Allied accounts as victor propaganda;
- comparing or subordinating Nazi crimes to Allied bombing and postwar expulsions;
- describing Nazi defendants and convicted perpetrators as martyrs;
- maintaining Hitler’s innocence or salvific status regardless of documentary evidence.

These positions are established by her own texts, not merely allegations.

### Antisemitism and racial supremacy

**Documented fact:** she promoted antisemitic conspiracy theories, biological hierarchy, caste-like racial separation, eugenics, and an “Aryan” civilizational ideal.

### Association with convicted perpetrators and collaborators

**Documented or strongly reported:**

- Hertha Ehlert;
- Otto Ohlendorf’s widow and grave;
- Johannes von Leers;
- Otto Skorzeny;
- Léon Degrelle;
- Hans-Ulrich Rudel.

Association does not prove participation in their crimes, but she approached them precisely as admired representatives of the defeated Nazi order.

### “ODESSA” and Nazi escape networks

Goodrick-Clarke places a chapter called “The Odessa Connection” within her postwar milieu. She met figures commonly associated with fugitive or expatriate Nazi networks.

**Unresolved/allegation:** no consulted evidence shows that she personally ran an escape route, laundered funds, forged travel papers, or transported wanted war criminals. Stronger internet claims that she “helped smuggle Nazis out of Europe” should not be treated as established without additional archival proof.

### Advocacy of violence and coercion

Her writings endorsed severe state violence, racial selection, eugenic exclusion, and in some passages killing or allowing the death of people she considered destructive, degenerate, or ecologically harmful.

This is **documented ideological advocacy**. There is no evidence that she personally committed homicide or commanded an armed organization.

### Employment controversies

French antiracist activists campaigned against her teaching in the 1960s. She lost classroom duties in 1966.

**Documented outcome:** removal from ordinary teaching.

**Disputed characterization:** sympathetic sources call the campaign “persecution”; opponents regarded it as opposition to an openly Nazi teacher.

### No substantiated financial or sexual scandal

The adversarial search produced no relevant evidence of fraud, embezzlement, money laundering, corrupt business dealings, sexual abuse, or civil litigation involving this historical Savitri Devi. Modern Indian court cases bearing the common name “Savitri Devi” concern unrelated persons.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, and Propaganda

### “Hitler’s guru”

**Story:** Savitri Devi supposedly taught or spiritually guided Hitler.

**Origin and circulation:** postwar promotional copy, especially Samisdat advertising such as “Discovered Alive in India: Hitler’s Guru!”

**Evidence:** none. There is no evidence that she met Hitler, corresponded with him, or influenced Third Reich policy.

**Effect:** the slogan transformed an obscure postwar devotee into a seemingly central occult authority.

**Label:** propaganda/myth.

### Hitler as an avatar of Vishnu

**Story:** Hitler was a divine avatar, partial avatar, or precursor of Kalki sent to combat Kali-Yuga decline.

**Origin:** most fully systematized in *The Lightning and the Sun* (1958), with antecedents in her late-1940s writing.

**Evidence:** this is a theological assertion, not a verifiable biographical fact.

**Circulation:** neo-Nazi, esoteric-Hitlerist, occult, internet, and countercultural networks.

**Effect:** it became the single most recognizable feature of her reputation and enabled a religious cult of Hitler after the Third Reich’s destruction.

**Label:** documented belief; religious myth.

### Prophetic dream of Hermann Göring’s death

**Story:** on 15–16 October 1946 she dreamed or astrally perceived Göring’s death in London.

**Evidence:** retrospective self-report; no independent evidence of paranormal perception.

**Effect:** reinforced her self-presentation as spiritually linked to Nazi leaders.

**Label:** supernatural claim/self-created mythology.

### Sacred coincidence of the Acropolis and Beer Hall Putsch

**Story:** her 9 November 1923 visit to the Acropolis coincided providentially with Hitler’s Munich putsch.

**Evidence:** the dates may coincide; the spiritual meaning is retrospective.

**Effect:** supplied a symbolic bridge between ancient Greece and Hitlerism.

**Label:** factual coincidence embedded in self-created mythology.

### “Nazi priestess” and “most objectionable Nazi”

“Hitler’s Priestess” was a later biographical title. “Most objectionable Nazi” reportedly originated with Werl commandant Colonel Vickers and was proudly appropriated by Devi. Both shaped her cultic persona, although neither was a formal Nazi title.

### Ashes beside Rockwell

A memorial service was held on 20 February 1983 at the American New Order headquarters, and her ashes were reportedly placed in its “Valhalla” beside those of George Lincoln Rockwell.

The ceremonial placement is well reported within movement sources. The present location of either set of ashes is uncertain, and later stories of scattering or burial at Rockwell’s grave are inconsistent.

### Supposed mainstream ecological influence

Some commentary portrays her as a founder of modern ecology or a major influence on animal-rights movements.

The evidence supports a narrower conclusion:

- she independently articulated an extreme biocentric argument;
- radical-right environmentalists and ecofascists later cited her;
- *Impeachment of Man* also circulated outside explicitly Nazi milieus;
- no evidence establishes her as a central founder of mainstream environmentalism, animal-rights law, or deep ecology.

### Confusion with Indra Devi

The two women were European-born contemporaries who adopted Indian religious identities and the name Devi. **Indra Devi** became a yoga teacher associated with international popular yoga; Savitri Devi became a Nazi theorist. No documented personal connection was found.

## Death

Savitri Devi arrived at Muriel Gantry’s home in Sible Hedingham on **17 October 1982**. She was awaiting a planned journey to the United States, where Matt Koehl had arranged funds, a visa, and a speaking engagement.

She died shortly after midnight on **22 October 1982**, aged 77. The cause was recorded as heart attack and coronary thrombosis.

Her cremation took place at Colchester Crematorium on **7 December 1982**. A neo-Nazi memorial service was held in the United States on 20 February 1983.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### Neo-Nazi canonization

Her books were reprinted by Noontide Press, Samisdat, and later online archives. She acquired the status of a movement ancestor or prophetess, particularly among groups seeking to turn Hitlerism into a religion independent of the historical German state.

### Esoteric Hitlerism

Her principal successor in public reputation was Miguel Serrano, though their correspondence suggests parallel development as well as mutual recognition. Later esoteric Nazis combined her time-cycle theory with Hyperborean, UFO, occult, and conspiracy narratives that were not necessarily hers.

### Holocaust denial

Her denialist and apologetic treatment of Nazi crimes circulated through postwar revisionist networks. The exact degree of her personal influence on Ernst Zündel is disputed.

### Ecofascism

Recent terrorism and extremism scholarship identifies her synthesis of racial supremacy, animal advocacy, population control, and nature worship as an important antecedent for sections of ecofascism. Her actual readership remained marginal, but digital circulation increased her availability.

### Alt-right revival

A 2017 BBC investigation reported renewed interest among parts of the online alt-right. Claims that prominent right-wing figures directly followed Devi should be separated from documented citation. Richard Spencer discussed related traditionalist and far-right ideas; broad claims connecting Steve Bannon directly to Devi often rely on association through Julius Evola or Traditionalism rather than a demonstrated reading relationship.

A 2025 doctoral study of “Aryan connections” between Europe and India treats her as an illustrative bridge between European white supremacy and Hindu upper-caste nationalism. Recent scholarship continues to use her less as evidence of a large personal following than as a case study in the international circulation of “Aryan” ideology.

## Atlas Connections

Only connections supported by consulted sources are included.

### Friedrich Nietzsche — documented influence

She read Nietzsche’s *The Will to Power* during her 1923 Greek journey and repeatedly acknowledged Nietzsche as a formative philosophical influence. No personal connection was possible; Nietzsche died before her birth.

### Benito Mussolini — documented political admiration

On a 1926 visit to Italy she admired Mussolini’s regime. No meeting or correspondence is documented.

### Rabindranath Tagore — reported/documented meeting

She visited Visva-Bharati at Santiniketan in July 1935, studied Hindi and Bengali, and met Tagore. No sustained correspondence, mentorship, or ideological agreement has been established.

### Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi — documented meeting

She met Gandhi at Sevagram in September 1941 for an approximately thirty-minute interview. She later classified him as a morally consistent “man against time” while rejecting his universalism and nonviolence.

### Julius Evola — documented intellectual influence; no documented meeting

Her later Traditionalist framework drew to some degree on Evola, though René Guénon was the more important Traditionalist influence. No reliable evidence of a personal meeting or correspondence was found.

### René Guénon — documented intellectual influence; no meeting established

Her late memoir and theory of cyclic decline used Traditionalist ideas associated with Guénon. This is an intellectual reception, not a personal connection.

### Miguel Serrano — documented correspondence and shared ideological network

Three surviving letters from March 1980 document direct correspondence. Both developed forms of esoteric Hitlerism. The letters indicate mutual recognition but also suggest that their principal systems emerged partly independently.

### Savitri Devi — subject entry

The atlas roster itself includes Savitri Devi; this is the present subject rather than an external connection.

### Indra Devi — no documented connection

Despite their similar adopted names and European-to-Indian migrations, no meeting, correspondence, collaboration, or influence was found.

### Other roster names

No adequately documented personal crossing was found with the remaining roster members. Shared interests, common geography, or indirect inclusion in broad ideological traditions were not treated as connections.
