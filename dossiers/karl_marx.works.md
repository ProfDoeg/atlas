## Works by Karl Marx

### Literary experiments

- **1835:** *Reflections of a Young Man on the Choice of a Profession*. A school-leaving essay: juvenile prose rather than a mature political work. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1836–1837:** *Book of Love, Part I; Book of Love, Part II; Book of Songs*. Three manuscript albums of poems presented to Jenny von Westphalen; their status is private gift, literary experiment, and autobiographical object rather than published œuvre. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1837:** *Scorpion and Felix*. An unfinished comic novel surviving only in fragments. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1837:** *Oulanem*. An unfinished verse tragedy. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1837:** *Cleanthes, or the Starting Point and Necessary Continuation of Philosophy*. A philosophical dialogue reported by Marx in a letter but subsequently destroyed; it is therefore a lost project, not an extant text. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)

### Philosophical and political works

- **1840–1841:** *Difference Between the Democritean and Epicurean Philosophy of Nature*. Marx’s doctoral dissertation, accompanied by preparatory notebooks; portions of the planned treatment are lost. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1842–1843:** *Rheinische Zeitung journalism*. A body of editorials, reports, polemics, and unsigned newspaper interventions, including writings on press freedom, the wood-theft law, the Moselle winegrowers, divorce, and the Prussian state. Authorship of particular unsigned articles sometimes depends on editorial reconstruction rather than a signature. [MEGA I/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1843:** *Critique of Hegel’s Philosophy of Right*. An unfinished manuscript commentary on Hegel’s theory of the state; the familiar title is editorial, and only part of the projected critique survives. [MEGA I/2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1843:** *On the Jewish Question*. A two-part polemical essay responding to Bruno Bauer, published in the *Deutsch-Französische Jahrbücher*. [MEGA I/2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1843–1844:** *A Contribution to the Critique of Hegel’s Philosophy of Right: Introduction*. A published introduction to an uncompleted larger project; it became an autonomous essay although conceived as a threshold to another work. [MEGA I/2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1844:** *Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts*. A group of incomplete Paris manuscripts on political economy, alienation, communism, and human emancipation. “The 1844 Manuscripts” is a posthumous editorial construction rather than a book Marx completed or titled as such. [MEGA I/2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1844:** *Comments on James Mill*. Notes and extended reflections embedded in Marx’s Paris excerpt notebooks; they resist classification as an independent treatise because quotation, summary, and original argument are materially intertwined. [MEGA IV/3](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1844:** *Critical Marginal Notes on the Article “The King of Prussia and Social Reform. By a Prussian”*. A newspaper polemic published in two installments. [MEGA I/2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1844–1845:** *The Holy Family, or Critique of Critical Criticism*. A polemical book made jointly with Friedrich Engels, though Marx wrote the greater portion; its collective authorship should not be collapsed into a Marx-only work. [MEGA I/4](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung/i/4-m/e)
- **1845:** *Theses on Feuerbach*. Eleven compressed manuscript notes intended for Marx’s own use, not a finished publication or manifesto. Engels later edited and published them under a supplied title, altering some wording. [MEGA IV/3](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1845–1846:** *The German Ideology manuscript complex*. Seventeen manuscripts and two contemporary printed texts produced principally by Marx and Engels, with contributions or copying by Joseph Weydemeyer and others. It was neither a completed two-volume book nor a stable text called *The German Ideology* in Marx’s lifetime; the apparent unified work is substantially an editorial creation. [MEGA I/5](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1845–1847:** *Draft article on Friedrich List’s “National System of Political Economy”*. An incomplete critical manuscript and abandoned publication project. [MEGA I/4](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i/4-m/e)
- **1846:** *Peuchet: On Suicide*. A translation-adaptation of passages from Jacques Peuchet, reorganized and supplemented by Marx; it resists simple designation as either Marx’s original essay or merely a translation. [MEGA I/4](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i/4-m/e)
- **1846:** *Circular Against Kriege*. A collective political intervention drafted chiefly by Marx and Engels and adopted by the Brussels Communist Correspondence Committee. [MEGA I/5](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1846–1847:** *The Poverty of Philosophy*. A French-language polemical book against Pierre-Joseph Proudhon and Marx’s first published book devoted substantially to political economy. [MEGA I/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1847:** *Moralizing Criticism and Critical Morality*. A serial polemic against Karl Heinzen. [MEGA I/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1847:** *Wage Labour and Capital*. Lectures delivered to the German Workers’ Society in Brussels and published as newspaper articles in 1849; the familiar later pamphlet was edited by Engels, who revised its terminology. [MEGA I/6 and I/9](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1847:** *The Communists and Karl Heinzen*. A political polemic published as a newspaper article. [MEGA I/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1847:** *Speech on the Question of Free Trade*. A public speech subsequently issued as a pamphlet; it is simultaneously an event, an oral intervention, and a printed text. [MEGA I/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1847–1848:** *Manifesto of the Communist Party*. A manifesto in the strong sense: a jointly written, commissioned program intended to organize and act politically, not merely to describe. Engels’s earlier *Principles of Communism* was a precursor, not part of Marx’s authorship. [MEGA I/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1848:** *Demands of the Communist Party in Germany*. A revolutionary program issued collectively in the name of the Communist Party; Marx and Engels participated in its making, but it is an organizational document rather than proprietary authorship. [MEGA I/7](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1848:** *Speech on Poland*. A public political address preserved through its printed report. [MEGA I/7](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1848:** *Speech on the Question of Free Trade* and related Brussels speeches in printed versions. These manifestations demonstrate that Marx’s spoken and printed interventions do not always constitute the same textual object. [MEGA I/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)

### Newspapers, journalism, and serial political interventions

- **1848–1849:** *Neue Rheinische Zeitung*. A collectively produced revolutionary newspaper edited by Marx, containing hundreds of articles, notices, reports, and editorial interventions by Marx, Engels, and collaborators. Marx made both individual texts and the newspaper’s editorial-political form; unsigned authorship remains uncertain for some pieces. [MEGA I/7–I/9](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1848–1849:** *Revolutionary Spain and other European revolutionary reporting*. Newspaper analyses formed within Marx’s larger journalistic practice rather than as books. Later collections can make autonomous “works” out of texts that originally belonged to serial publication. [MEGA I/7–I/10](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1849:** *Wage Labour and Capital*. The *Neue Rheinische Zeitung* article series derived from Marx’s 1847 lectures; its interruption when the newspaper closed left the exposition incomplete. [MEGA I/9](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1850:** *The Class Struggles in France, 1848 to 1850*. A series written for the *Neue Rheinische Zeitung. Politisch-ökonomische Revue*. The familiar book title and arrangement derive partly from Engels’s later editorial republication. [MEGA I/10](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1850:** *Address of the Central Authority to the League*. Two organizational addresses, made jointly with Engels for the Communist League; they function as political protocols and directives rather than detached essays. [MEGA I/10](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1850:** *Reviews from the Neue Rheinische Zeitung. Politisch-ökonomische Revue*. Joint Marx–Engels assessments of politics and the economy; division of authorship is not always recoverable at sentence level. [MEGA I/10](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1851–1862:** *New-York Tribune correspondence*. A large transatlantic body of reports, leading articles, and political-economic analysis. Many pieces appeared anonymously or under Marx’s byline after substantial work by Engels, and editors sometimes altered them, so the corpus resists clean individual attribution. [MEGA I/11–I/19](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1853:** *The British Rule in India* and *The Future Results of British Rule in India*. Two connected newspaper articles, not chapters of a book, whose later pairing is reinforced by anthologization. [MEGA I/12](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1853–1856:** *Crimean War journalism*. A corpus of dispatches and leading articles made with Engels for the *New-York Tribune*, *Neue Oder-Zeitung*, and other papers; unsigned publication and editorial alteration complicate attribution. [MEGA I/12–I/15](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1854:** *Revolutionary Spain*. A sequence of *New-York Tribune* articles on Spanish history and contemporary revolution; it is a serial historical intervention rather than a book authored in that form. [MEGA I/13](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1855:** *Lord John Russell*. A journalistic-political study published serially; its later existence as a separate title is an editorial manifestation of newspaper work. [MEGA I/14](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1856:** *Speech at the Anniversary of the People’s Paper*. A public address known through a newspaper report and therefore mediated rather than preserved as a complete authorial manuscript. [MEGA I/15](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1856–1857:** *Revelations of the Diplomatic History of the Eighteenth Century*. A historical investigation published serially in the *Free Press* and later as a pamphlet; Marx did not complete the larger history projected around it. [MEGA I/15–I/16](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1857–1858:** *New American Cyclopædia articles*. Reference articles produced by Marx and Engels, often through divided research and drafting labor; individual attribution and the editors’ intervention require case-by-case treatment. [MEGA I/16](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1858–1862:** *Journalism on the opium trade, colonialism, slavery, the financial crisis, and the American Civil War*. A transnational article corpus rather than a single titled work; some Civil War pieces were drafted or substantially supplied by Engels. [MEGA I/16–I/19](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1861–1862:** *Die Presse correspondence*. Political journalism for the Vienna newspaper, including writings on the American Civil War, Mexico, and European diplomacy. Editorial alteration and unsigned publication sometimes obscure the exact textual boundary of Marx’s contribution. [MEGA I/19](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)

### Political histories and polemical books

- **1852:** *The Eighteenth Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte*. A historical-political essay first published in the periodical *Die Revolution*; Marx revised it for the second edition of 1869. [MEGA I/11](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1852:** *The Great Men of the Exile*. A satirical manuscript composed jointly with Engels but unpublished in their lifetimes; allocation of particular passages between the collaborators is partly reconstructed. [MEGA I/11](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1852–1853:** *Revelations Concerning the Communist Trial in Cologne*. A documentary-polemical pamphlet exposing police fabrication and the prosecution of Communist League members. [MEGA I/11–I/12](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1853:** *The Knight of the Noble Consciousness*. A polemical pamphlet against August Willich, written by Marx but issued under a deliberately theatrical title and occasion. [MEGA I/12](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1859–1860:** *Herr Vogt*. A book-length polemic assembled from political argument, documentary evidence, satire, and personal defense; its hybrid construction resists the category of theoretical treatise. [MEGA I/18](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)

### The critique of political economy: project, manuscripts, and books

- **1844–1883:** *Critique of political economy*. Marx’s overarching, repeatedly redesigned research and publication project. Its made form is not one completed work but a constellation of notebooks, drafts, lectures, articles, books, revisions, and abandoned plans. [MEGA Sections II and IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende)
- **1850–1853:** *London Notebooks*. Research notebooks containing excerpts, calculations, bibliographical records, and original observations on political economy. Because Marx’s voice is interleaved with copied material, these are working instruments and material traces of a project rather than conventional authored texts. [MEGA IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1857–1858:** *Crisis Notebooks*. Newspaper clippings, extracts, tables, and comments assembled to follow the world economic crisis; they are an analytical apparatus or research installation more than a finished literary work. [MEGA IV/14](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1857:** *Introduction to the Critique of Political Economy*. An unfinished methodological introduction that Marx withheld because he decided against anticipating his results; it survives as a fragment of the larger economic project. [MEGA II/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1857–1858:** *Economic Manuscripts of 1857–1858*. Seven connected notebooks later published as the *Grundrisse*. “Grundrisse” is a posthumous editorial title: the manuscripts are a research draft, not a book Marx prepared for publication. [MEGA II/1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1858:** *Urtext of “A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy”*. A surviving fragment of an earlier draft whose boundaries and relation to the published book are established editorially. [MEGA II/2 overview](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1859:** *A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy*. A published book presenting the opening portion of Marx’s planned economic critique; the famous preface is part of this specific manifestation. [MEGA II](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1861–1863:** *Economic Manuscript of 1861–1863*. Twenty-three notebooks constituting a vast, unfinished continuation and reconstruction of the critique of political economy. Parts were later edited as *Theories of Surplus Value*, but Marx did not make or title a finished “fourth volume of Capital” under that name. [MEGA II/3](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1863–1865:** *Economic Manuscripts of 1863–1865*. Drafts for the three books of *Capital*, including the principal surviving manuscript for Book III. They are components of a project in formation, not the subsequently printed volumes as edited by Engels. [MEGA II/4.1–II/4.2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1863–1868:** *Supplementary manuscripts for Books II and III of Capital*. Fifteen drafts, sketches, and source extracts through which Marx revised categories and attempted new presentations; their plurality resists treatment as a single finished text. [MEGA II/4.3](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1865:** *Value, Price and Profit*. A report delivered orally to the General Council of the International Working Men’s Association and preserved in Marx’s manuscript; the familiar title was supplied at posthumous publication. [MEGA II/4.1](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1867:** *Capital: A Critique of Political Economy, Volume I*. The first published German manifestation of Marx’s principal economic work, devoted to the production process of capital. [MEGA II/5](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1872–1873:** *Capital, Volume I, second German edition*. A substantially revised authorial manifestation, not merely a reprint. [MEGA II/6](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1872–1875:** *Le Capital*. The French serial edition, translated by Joseph Roy but extensively revised and authorized by Marx; Marx gave it an independent scientific value, so it is more than a translation while remaining collaborative in textual production. [MEGA II/7](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1873–1883:** *Revisions and materials for later editions of Capital, Volume I*. Authorial corrections, additions, and instructions continued after the German second edition. The 1883 third German edition appeared after Marx’s death under Engels’s editorship and is consequently not an entirely author-made final text. [MEGA II/8](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1868–1881:** *Manuscripts for Book II of Capital*. Ten surviving drafts, including a large second full draft, through which Marx repeatedly attempted to present circulation, turnover, and social reproduction. Marx never completed Book II for publication. [MEGA II/11](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1871–1882:** *Late manuscripts for Book III of Capital*. Notes and drafts on surplus value, profit, interest, and rent; they form a bridge between the 1864–1865 manuscript and Engels’s later edition but not a completed third volume. [MEGA II/14](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1885:** *Capital, Volume II*. A posthumous editorial creation by Engels using seven of Marx’s ten surviving Book II manuscript groups. It is indispensable to Marx’s œuvre but resists being called a book Marx himself made in its printed form. [MEGA II/12–II/13](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)
- **1894:** *Capital, Volume III*. A posthumous book constructed by Engels chiefly from Marx’s 1864–1865 manuscript together with later materials and extensive editorial organization. Its textual authenticity and the extent of Engels’s transformation remain matters of scholarly dispute. [MEGA II/14–II/15](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/ii-abteilung)

### Manifestos, protocols, and collective political texts

- **1864:** *Inaugural Address of the International Working Men’s Association*. A founding manifesto drafted by Marx and adopted by the Association: an institutional speech-act issued collectively rather than a private essay. [MEGA I/20](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1864:** *Provisional Rules of the International Working Men’s Association*. A governing protocol drafted by Marx and authorized by the organization. Its authorial identity is inseparable from collective adoption. [MEGA I/20](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1864–1872:** *International Working Men’s Association documents*. Addresses, resolutions, reports, mandates, circulars, declarations, minutes, and drafts made by Marx alone or in committees for the General Council. Many act in the organization’s name, so “authorship” and institutional agency do not coincide. [MEGA I/20–I/24](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1866:** *Instructions for the Delegates of the Provisional General Council*. A practical and political protocol prepared for the Geneva Congress of the International. [MEGA I/20](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1869:** *The General Council of the First International to the Federal Council of Romance Switzerland*. An organizational intervention associated with conflicts inside the International and issued through collective authority. [MEGA I/21](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1870:** *First Address on the Franco-Prussian War*. A manifesto drafted by Marx for and adopted by the International’s General Council. [MEGA I/21](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1870:** *Second Address on the Franco-Prussian War*. A second collective manifesto warning against annexation and addressing the altered political situation after the fall of the French Empire. [MEGA I/21](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1871:** *The Civil War in France*. An address drafted by Marx and issued by the International’s General Council, interpreting and defending the Paris Commune. Its genre is manifesto-address, even though it subsequently circulated as a book. [MEGA I/22](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1871:** *First and Second Drafts of The Civil War in France*. Substantial preparatory manuscripts whose arguments and organization differ from the adopted address; they are distinct stages of the project, not alternate editions authorized for publication. [MEGA I/22](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1871–1872:** *The Alleged Splits in the International*. A polemical circular made jointly by Marx and Engels for the General Council. [MEGA I/23 overview](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1872–1873:** *The Alliance of Socialist Democracy and the International Working Men’s Association*. A documentary polemic prepared by Marx and Engels, with Paul Lafargue’s participation, for institutional publication; it is a collective dossier rather than a purely individual book. [MEGA I/24](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1872–1875:** *Late International Working Men’s Association documents*. Resolutions, statements, reports, and correspondence made during the organization’s transfer to New York and decline; many resist separation from the institutional acts they performed. [MEGA I/24](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)

### Late critiques, questionnaires, and research works

- **1875:** *Marginal Notes on the Programme of the German Workers’ Party*. A private critical letter-document later published as the *Critique of the Gotha Programme*. It was not written as a public book, and the familiar title is posthumous. [MEGA I/25](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1877:** *Notes on Adolph Wagner*. Critical notes written in and around Wagner’s textbook; their form is interrupted commentary rather than a completed treatise. [MEGA I/25](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1877:** *From Critical History*. Marx’s contribution to Engels’s *Anti-Dühring*, principally the chapter on the history of political economy; the enclosing book is Engels’s work, not a Marx–Engels coauthored whole in equal measure. [MEGA I/27 overview](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1878–1880:** *Mathematical Manuscripts*. Working studies of algebra, differential calculus, and the history of mathematical methods. They are research notebooks and exploratory demonstrations, not a completed mathematical book. [MEGA I/28](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1880:** *A Workers’ Inquiry*. A questionnaire comprising one hundred questions about working and living conditions; it is a research protocol designed to elicit collective knowledge and political organization. [MEGA I/25](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1880:** *Preamble to the Programme of the French Workers’ Party*. A concise programmatic text made through collaboration with Jules Guesde, Paul Lafargue, and Engels; the precise division between Marx’s formulation and collective negotiation should not be erased. [MEGA I/25](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1880–1882:** *Chronological Extracts*. Extensive chronological tables and historical excerpts compiled from world histories; they function as a research apparatus rather than a narrative history authored for publication. [MEGA IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1879–1882:** *Ethnological Notebooks*. Excerpts and commentary on Lewis Henry Morgan, Henry Sumner Maine, John Lubbock, John Budd Phear, and related writers. The title is editorial, and quotation, translation, abbreviation, and original reflection are inseparable within the notebooks. [MEGA IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1881:** *Drafts of a Letter to Vera Zasulich*. Four drafts and a short final letter examining whether the Russian rural commune might avoid capitalist dissolution. The drafts are a branching argumentative project rather than discarded copies of one stable essay. [MEGA I/25](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)
- **1881–1882:** *Notes on Russian agrarian relations*. Excerpts, statistics, and analytical comments connected with Marx’s study of the peasant commune; these working materials resist treatment as a single titled work. [MEGA IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1882:** *Preface to the Russian Edition of the Communist Manifesto*. A jointly signed Marx–Engels intervention reconsidering the manifesto’s argument in relation to Russia; it is a new paratext and political act, not simply part of the 1848 manifestation. [MEGA I/25](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/i-abteilung)

### Notebooks, marginalia, and correspondence

- **1839–1882:** *Excerpt notebooks, notes, chronological tables, bibliographies, and marginalia*. A lifelong body of reading-work covering philosophy, art, religion, politics, economics, diplomacy, agriculture, chemistry, geology, technology, mathematics, anthropology, and history. MEGA treats these as a distinct section of the legacy, but individual items often resist being called “works” because they combine other writers’ words with Marx’s selection, arrangement, calculation, and commentary. [MEGA IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1843:** *Kreuznach Notebooks*. Excerpts and notes on political history and theories of the state made during Marx’s critique of Hegelian political philosophy; they are research instruments, not a completed history. [MEGA IV/2](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1844:** *Paris Notebooks*. Excerpts from political economists and socialists interwoven with Marx’s own reflections; the subsequently isolated “Comments on James Mill” arose inside this material object. [MEGA IV/2–IV/3](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1845:** *Manchester Notebooks*. Reading notes made by Marx and Engels during their study of British political economy and social conditions; joint use complicates individual ownership of the notebooks as works. [MEGA IV/4](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1853–1857:** *Notebooks on diplomacy and Spanish history*. Excerpts, chronologies, and notes supporting Marx’s historical journalism and his unfinished diplomatic-history project. [MEGA IV/12–IV/13](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1858–1863:** *Notebooks on political economy, the Polish question, and the Vogt controversy*. Research and documentary materials made for several simultaneous projects rather than a unified authored text. [MEGA IV/15–IV/17](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1864–1870:** *Agricultural, financial-crisis, and Irish-history notebooks*. Excerpts and notes feeding the later *Capital* manuscripts and Marx’s political work on Ireland; their operative unity lies in research practice, not publication. [MEGA IV/18–IV/20](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1870s–1882:** *Agronomy, geology, chemistry, physiology, technology, anthropology, and Russian-history notebooks*. A late interdisciplinary research corpus whose incompleteness is intrinsic: it records investigation in motion, not abandoned chapters of one identifiable book. [MEGA IV](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende/iv-abteilung)
- **1835–1883:** *Letters*. Thousands of surviving letters, drafts, enclosures, and jointly written communications constitute a major epistolary œuvre, ranging from family correspondence to theoretical argument and political organization. Some letters were coauthored, copied by secretaries or family members, sent institutionally, or survive only as drafts or extracts, so “one letter, one author, one text” cannot always be assumed. [MEGA III](https://mega.bbaw.de/de/mega-baende)
