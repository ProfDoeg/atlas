## Works by André Breton

### Poetry, automatic writing, and lyric books

- **1919:** *Mont de piété*. Breton’s first poetry collection, assembling poems written from 1913 to 1919; the original book included two drawings by André Derain. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1919–1920:** *Les Champs magnétiques*. A collaborative experiment in automatic writing made with Philippe Soupault—not conventionally a single-author book and often treated as the foundational manifestation of literary Surrealism. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1920:** *S’il vous plaît*. An automatic-writing play made with Philippe Soupault; its fourth act was suppressed from the original publication and recovered later. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1920:** *Vous m’oublierez*. A short Dada theatrical sketch made with Philippe Soupault. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1923:** *Clair de terre*. A poetry collection incorporating earlier poems, automatic texts, and verbal experiments; Breton substantially reorganized it in later editions. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1924:** *Poisson soluble*. A sequence of automatic prose texts originally issued with the first *Manifeste du surréalisme* as its theoretical preface. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1930:** *Ralentir travaux*. A collectively written book of poems by Breton, René Char, and Paul Éluard; individual authorship of its lines is intentionally unstable. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1930:** *L’Immaculée Conception*. A collaborative sequence by Breton and Paul Éluard combining automatic prose, simulated pathological states, and propositions for human conduct. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1931:** *L’Union libre*. A separately issued lyric poem whose successive images construct a woman’s body; it later entered Breton’s collected poetry. [Mélusine concordance](https://www.melusine-surrealisme.fr/wp/index-et-concordances-des-oeuvres-completes-dandre-breton)

- **1932:** *Le Revolver à cheveux blancs*. A poetry collection containing work written during the preceding decade, including *L’Union libre*. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1934:** *L’Air de l’eau*. A lyric sequence associated with Breton’s relationship with Jacqueline Lamba; it is poetry, but also an amorous itinerary. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1936:** *Au lavoir noir*. A short poetic book first made with an engraving by Pablo Picasso. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1940:** *Pleine marge*. A long poem written on the threshold of Breton’s wartime exile. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1940:** *Fata Morgana*. A long poem written in Marseille and first published with drawings by Wifredo Lam; Vichy censorship obstructed its initial distribution. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1943:** *Poèmes*. A wartime gathering of Breton’s poems rather than a wholly new poetic sequence. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1944:** *Les États généraux*. A small poetic sequence first published during Breton’s American exile. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1947:** *Ode à Charles Fourier*. A typographically and verbally discontinuous long poem addressed to the utopian socialist; it is simultaneously homage, political reconsideration, and poetic construction. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1947:** *Le Déshonneur des poètes*. A polemical pamphlet against patriotic and propagandistic Resistance poetry; although written as criticism, its title and form deliberately answer the collective volume *L’Honneur des poètes*. [Mélusine concordance](https://www.melusine-surrealisme.fr/wp/index-et-concordances-des-oeuvres-completes-dandre-breton)

- **1947:** *La Lampe dans l’horloge*. A short poetic-political meditation on revolutionary possibility in the atomic age. [Mélusine concordance](https://www.melusine-surrealisme.fr/wp/index-et-concordances-des-oeuvres-completes-dandre-breton)

- **1948:** *Poèmes*. A retrospective selection rather than a single newly composed work; its status is therefore that of a self-curated poetic corpus. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1948:** *La Brebis galante*. A poem-and-image book made with Max Ernst, whose etchings prevent the object from separating illustration cleanly from text. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1949:** *L’Aigle sexuel*. A separately published poem made in dialogue with a drawing by Joan Miró. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1953:** *Alouette du parloir*. A small poetic book first accompanied by etchings by Joan Miró. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1960:** *Le La*. A compact poetic text whose title invokes both a musical pitch and an act of setting poetry in motion. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

### Narratives that resist the novel

- **1928; revised 1963:** *Nadja*. A photographically documented narrative of encounter, Parisian wandering, desire, and institutional violence; Breton expressly positioned it against the conventional novel and extensively revised its text and images. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1932; revised 1955:** *Les Vases communicants*. A hybrid of dream record, psychoanalytic dispute, autobiography, political reflection, and documentary evidence; calling it either essay or narrative alone is inadequate. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1937:** *L’Amour fou*. A discontinuous, photographically articulated inquiry into desire, objective chance, and lived experience rather than a novel. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1944; enlarged 1947:** *Arcane 17 enté d’Ajours*. A book-length poetic meditation made from travel narrative, love address, political allegory, myth, and occult symbolism; the 1947 *Ajours* were grafted onto the earlier book rather than simply appended as a new edition. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1948:** *Martinique, charmeuse de serpents*. A travel-and-encounter book made with André Masson, combining Breton’s prose and poetry with Masson’s texts and images; its shared construction resists single authorship. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

### Manifestos, declarations, protocols, and polemics

- **1924:** *Manifeste du surréalisme*. A manifesto in the strict sense: it defines, names, recruits, authorizes practices, and attempts to bring a movement into being rather than merely describe one. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1926:** *Légitime défense*. A political pamphlet defending Surrealist autonomy against Communist Party discipline while maintaining revolutionary commitment. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1929:** *Le Trésor des jésuites*. A theatrical work made with Louis Aragon for the “procès Barrès” milieu; surviving documentation does not make it a conventional, independently staged play. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1929–1930:** *Second manifeste du surréalisme*. A manifesto and disciplinary intervention that redefines Surrealism through exclusions, reconciliations, and political demands. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1932:** *Misère de la poésie: “L’Affaire Aragon” devant l’opinion publique*. A polemical dossier defending poetic expression during the prosecution of Louis Aragon while disputing the Communist handling of the case. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1934:** *Qu’est-ce que le surréalisme ?* A lecture-manifestation delivered in Brussels and then published, combining historical account, definition, and political positioning. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1935:** *Position politique du surréalisme*. A constructed dossier of speeches, declarations, and polemical texts rather than a continuous treatise. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1938:** *Pour un art révolutionnaire indépendant*. A manifesto made with Leon Trotsky in Mexico and signed by Breton and Diego Rivera; scholarship generally credits Trotsky with substantial participation, so the printed signatures do not settle authorship. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1942:** *Prolégomènes à un troisième manifeste du surréalisme ou non*. A deliberately provisional anti-manifesto: its title withholds the institutional certainty normally claimed by the genre. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1949:** *Flagrant délit*. A forensic literary polemic demonstrating that *La Chasse spirituelle*, promoted as a newly found Rimbaud manuscript, was a forgery. [Mélusine concordance](https://www.melusine-surrealisme.fr/wp/index-et-concordances-des-oeuvres-completes-dandre-breton)

- **1953:** *Du surréalisme en ses œuvres vives*. A late manifesto-like restatement arguing that Surrealism remains an active operation rather than a closed historical style. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1955:** *Éphémérides surréalistes*. A compressed chronology added to *Manifestes du surréalisme*; it makes the movement’s history into an editorial and legitimating instrument. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1955:** *Pour un nouvel humanisme*. An unfinished or abandoned project preserved only in fragmentary form; its incompletion is part of the work’s status. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

### Essay collections, conversations, dictionaries, and anthologies

- **1924:** *Les Pas perdus*. A collection of essays, portraits, and interventions whose arrangement retrospectively makes Breton’s passage from Dada to Surrealism into an intellectual itinerary. [Pléiade, vol. I](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1934:** *Point du jour*. A constructed collection of essays and interventions on dreams, art, politics, and Surrealist practice. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1938:** *Dictionnaire abrégé du surréalisme*. A dictionary-exhibition catalogue made with Paul Éluard: its definitions, names, images, and quotations collectively manifest Surrealism rather than neutrally document it. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1938:** *Trajectoire du rêve*. An anthology of dream narratives selected and introduced by Breton; it is an editorial construction whose constituent dreams belong to multiple contributors. [Mélusine concordance](https://www.melusine-surrealisme.fr/wp/index-et-concordances-des-oeuvres-completes-dandre-breton)

- **1940; suppressed, then issued 1945; enlarged 1950 and 1966:** *Anthologie de l’humour noir*. A polemical anthology made through selection, ordering, biographical notices, and commentary; censorship and repeated revision make it a changing editorial obra rather than a fixed compilation. [Pléiade, vol. II](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1952:** *Entretiens 1913–1952*. A sequence of radio conversations with André Parinaud, revised for publication by Breton; it is simultaneously oral performance, autobiography, and authored editorial reconstruction. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1953:** *La Clé des champs*. A retrospectively arranged collection of political, poetic, and artistic interventions from the 1930s through the early 1950s. [Pléiade, vol. III](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1957:** *L’Art magique*. A historical and theoretical inquiry made with Gérard Legrand and accompanied by the results of a questionnaire; the extent of each collaborator’s contribution makes simple single authorship misleading. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1958:** *Constellations*. Twenty-two prose pieces written in response to Joan Miró’s gouache series of the same name; neither ordinary art criticism nor illustration, it is a paired verbal-visual construction. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1966:** *Le Surréalisme et la peinture*. The final state of a continually enlarged work begun in the 1920s; its successive essays and reproduced artworks make it an evolving verbal-visual atlas rather than a stable treatise. José Pierre assisted with the later edition, and the work’s boundaries changed repeatedly. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1970, posthumous:** *Perspective cavalière*. A posthumously assembled collection of Breton’s later essays edited by Marguerite Bonnet; Breton made the component texts, but not the finished book in this form. It therefore resists attribution as a lifetime obra. [Pléiade, vol. IV](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

### Periodicals as collective projects

- **1919–1924:** *Littérature*. A review founded by Breton, Louis Aragon, and Philippe Soupault; its changing editorship and Dada-to-Surrealism trajectory make the periodical a collective project rather than a Breton text. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1924–1929:** *La Révolution surréaliste*. The movement’s review, initially directed by Pierre Naville and Benjamin Péret and subsequently by Breton; each issue is a collective manifestation, so the run cannot properly be called his individual work. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1930–1933:** *Le Surréalisme au service de la révolution*. A six-issue review directed by Breton that recast the movement’s relation to revolutionary politics. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1942–1944:** *VVV*. A New York Surrealist review edited through a changing collaboration involving Breton, Max Ernst, Marcel Duchamp, and David Hare; its typography, foldouts, games, and images make each number a multimedia manifestation. [Centre Pompidou chronology](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/archive/uL4sNnW)

- **1948–1951:** *Néon*. A collective Surrealist review in whose direction Breton participated; it is a group platform, not an individually authored serial work. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1952–1955:** *Médium: Communication surréaliste*. A review and collective communication project associated with Breton’s postwar group; editorial responsibility was shared and changed over its run. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1956–1959:** *Le Surréalisme, même*. A five-number review directed by Breton with Jean Schuster as managing editor; its title asserts continuation rather than retrospective commemoration. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

- **1961–1965:** *La Brèche: Action surréaliste*. The final review project sustained by Breton during his lifetime, made collectively across eight numbers. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)

### Exhibitions and collective manifestations

- **1925:** *La Peinture surréaliste*. The first group exhibition of Surrealist painting, organized at Galerie Pierre with Breton’s participation; it made the movement spatially and visually manifest rather than merely illustrating its texts. [Metropolitan Museum exhibition documentation](https://libmma.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p16028coll12/id/18919/download)

- **1936:** *Exposition surréaliste d’objets*. An exhibition project at Galerie Charles Ratton that placed found, natural, mathematical, ethnographic, interpreted, and Surrealist objects in a deliberately dehierarchized field. [Centre Pompidou catalogue](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/media/document/d2/b2/d2b227153d784eae3f74a28ee5e36ef7/normal.pdf)

- **1938:** *Exposition internationale du surréalisme*. A collective environment organized principally by Breton and Paul Éluard, with Marcel Duchamp responsible for major aspects of its installation; because rooms, darkness, sound, performance, objects, and catalogue acted together, it approaches a Surrealist Gesamtkunstwerk. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/media/document/f9/57/f9573cfb6562214f1a786d02cc336b2d/normal.pdf)

- **1942:** *First Papers of Surrealism*. A New York exhibition organized by Breton and Marcel Duchamp for the Coordinating Council of French Relief Societies; Duchamp’s obstructing string installation dominated the physical manifestation, making authorship irreducibly collaborative. [Duchamp Research Portal](https://www.duchamparchives.org/cp/archive/collection/fonds-andre-breton/)

- **1947:** *Le Surréalisme en 1947*. An international exhibition conceived by Breton and Marcel Duchamp around initiatory passage, myth, and twelve altars; exhibition, catalogue, architecture, and ritualized itinerary together form a collective Gesamtkunstwerk. [Paris Musées](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/en/node/319189)

- **1959–1960:** *Exposition inteRnatiOnale du Surréalisme (EROS)*. An exhibition organized by Breton and Duchamp with José Pierre at Galerie Daniel Cordier around eroticism; it was a multisensory collective manifestation rather than a separable Breton artwork. [Déméter](https://www.peren-revues.fr/demeter/942)

- **1965–1966:** *L’Écart absolu*. The last international Surrealist exhibition organized under Breton’s direction, its title taken from Fourier; the show functioned as a collective critique of consumer society. [Metropolitan Museum exhibition documentation](https://libmma.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p16028coll12/id/18919/download)

### Poem-objects, collages, assemblages, and found objects

- **1931:** *Objet à fonctionnement symbolique* (also catalogued under variants including *Objet* and *Main gantée et main rouge*). An assemblage whose title and date vary across contemporary reproductions; that instability should not be silently resolved. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/ca9XzK)

- **1934:** *Rêve-Objet* / untitled *Page-Objet*. A multipart construction converting a recorded dream into pages, attached things, and images; the museum catalogues the surviving sheet both as part of the ensemble and as an untitled page-object. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/ressources/oeuvre/cn7rEqy)

- **1935:** *Je vois, j’imagine*. A poem-object documented photographically by Man Ray; writing, image, and attached matter are constitutive rather than separable media. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/recherche/oeuvres?ensemble=150000000002173)

- **1937:** *Madame, vous m’êtes apparue…*. A poem-object joining handwritten language to found and constructed visual material. [Centre Pompidou discussion](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/es/ressources/oeuvre/cdjj86)

- **1941:** *Portrait de l’acteur A. B.* A poem-object and self-portrait by displaced signs and things rather than mimetic likeness. [Centre Pompidou discussion](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/es/ressources/oeuvre/cdjj86)

- **1941:** *Ces terrains vagues où j’erre…*. An object-poem combining language, relief, found imagery, and suspended material; its words are physical components, not merely a caption. [MoMA assemblage catalogue](https://assets.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_1880_300062228.pdf)

- **December 1941:** *Poem-Object*. An untitled or generically titled construction in MoMA’s catalogue; forcing a descriptive phrase into the position of a fixed title would falsify its status. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/81121)

- **1942–1943:** *J’ai salué à six pas le Commandant Lefebvre des Noëttes*. A sewn and collaged poem-object produced for the deluxe portfolio of *VVV*; it is at once text, image, handwork, and serial publication component. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/es/ressources/oeuvre/cdjj86)

- **c. 1942:** *Poème-Objet from VVV Portfolio*. A printed/collaged contribution catalogued by MoMA under a generic title; its membership in a collective portfolio complicates its status as a unique autonomous work. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/33217)

- **1953:** *Souvenir du paradis terrestre*. A found stone interpreted and installed by Breton; finding, naming, and placement—not fabrication of the stone—constitute his act of making. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/cpjERL)

- **1959:** *Le Hasard objectif*. A cork object installed between mannequin arms in Breton’s studio; it operates as object, emblem, and spatial definition of one of his central concepts. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/cpjERL)

- **c. 1959:** *La Galvanisation*. A poem-object from Breton’s studio; the museum dates it approximately, so no exact year should be imposed. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/cpjERL)

### The studio as continuing obra

- **1922–1966:** *The arrangement of the atelier at 42 rue Fontaine*, especially the wall behind Breton’s desk. Breton continually selected, juxtaposed, and rearranged artworks, Indigenous objects, popular and natural objects, curiosities, and personal finds; it resists being called a conventional artwork, yet its deliberate relations and final 1966 state make it an environmental atlas and perhaps his most sustained Gesamtkunstwerk. The present museum installation preserves only a substantial portion of a changing collection, not an uncontested complete original. [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fr/pompidou-plus/magazine/article/lhistoire-secrete-du-mur-breton-oeuvre-emblematique-de-la-collection-du-centre-pompidou)

### Dispersed and incompletely bounded work

- **1911–1966:** *Alentours, inédits, tracts, declarations, prefaces, exhibition texts, questionnaires, interviews, and recovered texts*. These are hundreds of distinct interventions rather than one work, but the standard critical edition itself groups many under editorial headings such as *Alentours*, *Inédits*, and *Supplément*; those headings are not titles invented by Breton, and treating each grouping as a single obra would misdescribe the corpus. The four-volume edition explicitly includes scattered, unpublished, and later recovered texts, while its own twenty-year process of discovery makes absolute completeness a claim that should be resisted. [Pléiade catalogue](https://www.catalogue-pleiade.fr/Breton.jF.htm)

- **1915–1966; largely published posthumously:** *Correspondence*. Breton made a vast body of letters, but most named volumes—such as the editions of letters to Simone Kahn or correspondence with Paul Éluard—were assembled and titled by later editors. The letters are texts and material acts of relation; the posthumous books are not lifetime works in the same sense. [BnF bibliography](https://www.bnf.fr/fr/andre-breton-1896-1966-bibliographie)
