## Works by Sir Ernest Henry Shackleton

### Expeditionary books and collaborative publications

- **April–August 1902:** *The South Polar Times, volume I*. A manuscript shipboard periodical created during the *Discovery* expedition, edited by Shackleton and filled with contributions from the wintering party; it is a collective creation rather than a solely authored book. The later printed volumes are manifestations of the original periodical, not new Shackleton works.

- **April–July 1908:** *Aurora Australis*. An edited, illustrated and hand-produced collective book—the first printed and bound in Antarctica—made at Cape Royds by members of the British Antarctic Expedition. Shackleton devised and edited the whole, contributed two poems under “Nemo,” and depended on George Marston for its images, Ernest Joyce and Frank Wild for printing, and Bernard Day for its packing-case bindings; it is consequently closer to a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk than to a conventionally authored book.

- **1909:** *The Heart of the Antarctic: Being the Story of the British Antarctic Expedition, 1907–1909*. A two-volume expedition narrative issued under Shackleton’s name, incorporating scientific and first-person contributions by expedition members. Its sole-authorship label is unstable: journalist Edward Saunders shaped the book from Shackleton’s notes and dictation and is properly described as his amanuensis or uncredited literary collaborator.

- **1909:** *The Antarctic Book: Winter Quarters, 1907–1909*. A separately bound supplement made for the deluxe issue of *The Heart of the Antarctic*, limited to 300 copies and signed by the shore party. Edited by Shackleton, it combines his poems, Douglas Mawson’s fiction, George Marston’s images and the expedition members’ signatures; some copies differ because an erroneous contents reference and part of *Erebus* were reset, so its two states are manifestations rather than separate works.

- **1919:** *South: The Story of Shackleton’s Last Expedition, 1914–1917*. Shackleton’s narrative of the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition, including the *Endurance* party, the Ross Sea party and scientific appendices by expedition specialists. It is an authored book assembled from a collective documentary record—including other participants’ diaries—and should not be mistaken for a wholly solitary composition; editions dated 1920 are later manifestations of the 1919 work.

### Poems

- **1908:** *Midwinter Night*. A comic poem contributed to *Aurora Australis* under “Nemo”; the contents page attributes it to Nemo while the poem itself is signed “Veritas,” but Eric Marshall’s contemporary diary identifies Shackleton as its author.

- **1908:** *Erebus*. A long descriptive expedition poem contributed under “Nemo” to *Aurora Australis* and reprinted under Shackleton’s name in *The Antarctic Book*. The first state of the latter accidentally displaced part of the poem, a bibliographical fault rather than evidence of another version intended by Shackleton.

- **1909:** *Aurora Australis*. A poem by Shackleton printed in *The Antarctic Book*; despite sharing its title with the 1908 collective volume, it is a distinct text, not another edition of that book.

### Recorded speech

- **1909–1910:** *My South Polar Expedition*. A spoken-word account recorded on an Edison Amberol wax cylinder, with Shackleton describing the achievements of the 1907–09 expedition. It is a performed and mechanically fixed oral text—not a book—and catalogues differ between the likely recording date and the record’s 1910 issue date.

- **1921:** *Farewell message before the Shackleton–Rowett Expedition*. A brief speech reportedly recorded by Harry Grindell Matthews with an experimental sound-on-film apparatus immediately before *Quest* sailed. The manifestation is lost and surviving descriptions do not establish a stable title or complete text, so it resists treatment as an extant work.

### Expedition diaries

- **1901–1903:** *Discovery-expedition diary*. Shackleton’s manuscript record of his service in Scott’s British National Antarctic Expedition. It survives as an archival text rather than a book prepared for publication.

- **1907–1909:** *Nimrod-expedition diary*. Shackleton’s manuscript diary of the British Antarctic Expedition, subsequently used in constructing *The Heart of the Antarctic*. Its archival continuity should not be confused with the later edited expedition narrative.

- **1914–1917:** *Endurance-expedition diary*. Shackleton’s manuscript record of the Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition. It is one component of the collective documentary substrate of *South*, not merely an early edition of that book.

- **1921–1922:** *Quest-expedition diary*. The incomplete manuscript diary of the Shackleton–Rowett Expedition, ending with entries immediately before Shackleton’s death. It is an unfinished life-record, not a completed publication; reproductions and later transcriptions are manifestations of the surviving manuscript.

### Projects enacted as journeys

- **1907–1909:** *British Antarctic Expedition (Nimrod Expedition)*. Shackleton’s organized project to explore the Ross Sea sector and reach the geographical and magnetic South Poles. Neither principal objective was wholly achieved, but the expedition—as logistical design, scientific programme, journey and collective cultural production—is an obra that cannot be reduced to its published narrative.

- **1914–1917:** *Imperial Trans-Antarctic Expedition*. A two-party project intended to make the first crossing of Antarctica from the Weddell Sea to the Ross Sea by way of the Pole. The destruction of *Endurance* prevented the crossing, leaving the intended traverse unfinished, while the improvised survival and rescue journeys became an unplanned creation distinct from the original project.

- **1921–1922:** *Shackleton–Rowett Expedition (Quest Expedition)*. Shackleton’s final expeditionary project, whose announced objectives changed during planning and remained diffuse—oceanographical and geographical work, island exploration and an intended Antarctic voyage. His death at South Georgia left it incomplete under his authorship; Frank Wild continued the expedition, so its later course cannot simply be called Shackleton’s work.

### Doubtful and resistant attributions

- *“Men wanted for hazardous journey” recruitment advertisement*. No contemporary copy has been found, and researchers generally regard the famous wording as apocryphal. It is therefore not included as a Shackleton manifesto: its resistance to work-status begins with the absence of evidence that he made it.
