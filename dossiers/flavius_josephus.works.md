## Works by Flavius Josephus

### Historiographical works

- **c. 75–79 CE, with possible later revision:** *The Judean War* (*Bellum Judaicum*). A seven-book work of contemporary historiography, extending from the Hasmonean period through the revolt against Rome and its aftermath. Josephus says that he first composed an account in his “paternal language” for eastern readers and then produced the surviving Greek work; whether that lost precursor was Aramaic or Hebrew, how closely the Greek corresponds to it, and whether Book 7 was added later remain disputed. ([Brill’s *Flavius Josephus Online*](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/fjo/); [University of Southampton discussion](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/465574/1/973930.pdf); [overview of the Aramaic-version dispute](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/file/448697))

- **93/94 CE:** *Judean Antiquities* (*Jewish Antiquities*, *Antiquitates Judaicae*). A twenty-book universal and national history, recasting Jewish traditions and history from creation to Josephus’s own generation for Greek-reading audiences; Josephus himself dates its completion to Domitian’s thirteenth regnal year and his own fifty-sixth year. Its transmitted text contains passages whose exact wording or authenticity is disputed—most famously the notice concerning Jesus—so the surviving manifestation cannot everywhere be identified confidently with what Josephus wrote. ([Cambridge study of its publication date](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/autobiography-of-josephus-and-the-hypothesis-of-a-second-edition-of-his-antiquities1/87A6A70F6D90CB8DB00508ED4704FB7D); [Brill’s *Flavius Josephus Online*](https://scholarlyeditions.brill.com/fjo/); [Wiley, “The Text of the Josephan Corpus”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118325162.ch20))

### Autobiographical appendix

- **93/94 CE or later:** *Life of Josephus* (*Vita*). A compact autobiographical and self-defensive narrative, especially concerned with Josephus’s command in Galilee; materially it behaves less like an independent “book” than an appendix to *Judean Antiquities*, with which the manuscript tradition transmits it. Its date is contested: the traditional post-100 dating depends on a late report about Agrippa II’s death, whereas Steve Mason argues for composition alongside the *Antiquities* in 93/94; hypotheses of later revision also remain in play. ([Brill Josephus Project review of Mason’s commentary](https://josephus.org/FlJosephus2/brillReview2.htm); [Cambridge study of the autobiography and second-edition hypothesis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/autobiography-of-josephus-and-the-hypothesis-of-a-second-edition-of-his-antiquities1/87A6A70F6D90CB8DB00508ED4704FB7D); [Wiley corpus survey](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118325162.ch20))

### Apologetic and polemical work

- **After 93/94 CE, possibly after 96:** *On the Antiquity of the Judeans*, conventionally *Against Apion* (*Contra Apionem*). A two-book apologetic and polemical treatise that acts publicly in defence of Jewish antiquity, historiography, law, and collective character; “Against Apion” is a later, overly narrow title because Apion is only one of several opponents. It necessarily postdates *Judean Antiquities*, which it cites, but no secure completion date survives, and the relative order of this work and *Life* is uncertain; portions of its Greek text are lost and recoverable only through ancient translation or epitome. ([John M. G. Barclay’s Brill commentary](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/1126948/flavius-josephus-translation-and-commentary-volume-10-against-apion); [Cambridge discussion of the terminus post quem](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/transactions-of-the-royal-historical-society/article/abs/cleopatras-and-the-jews/5E7FC716D0D2280FADC588B6FD26910C); [Wiley corpus survey](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118325162.ch20))

### Lost or uncertain manifestations

- **Before the surviving Greek *Judean War*, probably c. 70–79 CE:** *Account of the war in Josephus’s “paternal language”* (title unknown). Josephus presents this as an earlier account addressed to Parthians, Babylonians, Arabians, Jews beyond the Euphrates, and the people of Adiabene; it does not survive as an independently identifiable text. Its status resists classification: it may have been a separate first edition, a source-text or draft behind the Greek *War*, or a literary claim, and neither “Aramaic” nor “Hebrew” can be imposed as certain. ([Cambridge, “The Composition of Josephus’ Antiquities”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/classical-quarterly/article/abs/composition-of-josephus-antiquities/1F20569FB6F70566233D8CF881BA6894); [discussion of scholarly disagreement](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/file/448697))

### Announced projects

- **Announced in 93/94 CE:** *A summary of the war and subsequent events down to Josephus’s present* (no fixed title). This is a project promised at the end of *Judean Antiquities*, not a securely attested completed obra. It has variously been related to *Life*, to a contemplated revision of *The Judean War*, or to a separate continuation; the evidence does not establish that Josephus completed an independent text. ([H. St. J. Thackeray’s survey of Josephus’s works](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64837/old/64837-h/64837-h.htm); [Cambridge study of *Life* and the proposed second edition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/abs/autobiography-of-josephus-and-the-hypothesis-of-a-second-edition-of-his-antiquities1/87A6A70F6D90CB8DB00508ED4704FB7D))

- **Announced by 93/94 CE:** *On God and His Being and on the Laws* / *On Customs and Causes* (provisional scholarly titles). Josephus promises a four-book explanatory treatment of God, Jewish laws, and the reasons some actions are permitted or forbidden, and elsewhere defers relevant discussions to such a treatment. It is therefore securely a project, perhaps one already taking shape, but not securely a completed work: no independent text survives, and scholarship disagrees about how far it was written or whether material intended for it entered *Against Apion*. ([David Altshuler, “The Treatise ‘On Customs and Causes’ by Flavius Josephus”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1454314); [Thackeray’s survey](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64837/old/64837-h/64837-h.htm))

### Rejected or doubtful attributions

- **Date unknown; attribution rejected:** *A Discourse to the Greeks Concerning Hades*. This homiletic-theological text circulated under Josephus’s name but is not accepted as his obra; it is generally identified with, or derived from, a work associated with Hippolytus, *Against Plato, on the Cause of the Universe*. It belongs to the history of Josephus’s attributed corpus, not to the scholarly corpus of things he made. ([Perseus catalogue of the four accepted works](https://catalog.perseus.tufts.edu/catalog/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0526); [Wiley corpus survey](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/9781118325162.ch20))

- **Probably first century CE; attribution rejected:** *Fourth Maccabees*. Older editions sometimes printed this philosophical martyr narrative among Josephus’s writings, but modern scholarship does not regard it as his work. Its former attribution is therefore a catalogue-history phenomenon rather than evidence that Josephus made it. ([Thackeray’s survey](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64837/old/64837-h/64837-h.htm); [Perseus catalogue of the accepted Josephan corpus](https://catalog.perseus.tufts.edu/catalog/urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0526))

- **No securely identifiable date or title:** *Supposed history of Syria or the Seleucids*. Some first-person cross-references in *Judean Antiquities* have been interpreted as traces of a lost monograph, but variations in the wording suggest that Josephus was reproducing references from his sources. This resists being called a work at all: the standard corpus does not recognize an independently attested Josephan composition behind those phrases. ([Thackeray’s survey](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/64837/old/64837-h/64837-h.htm))
