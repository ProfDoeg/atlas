## Works by Stephen Edwin King

### Novels and book-length fiction

- **1974:** *Carrie*. Novel.
- **1975:** *’Salem’s Lot*. Novel.
- **1977:** *The Shining*. Novel.
- **1977:** *Rage*. Novel published as Richard Bachman; King later permitted it to fall out of print, so its continuing status as a marketable book is intentionally resisted.
- **1978:** *The Stand*. Novel; substantially restored and revised as *The Stand: The Complete & Uncut Edition* in 1990.
- **1979:** *The Long Walk*. Novel published as Richard Bachman.
- **1979:** *The Dead Zone*. Novel.
- **1980:** *Firestarter*. Novel.
- **1981:** *Roadwork*. Novel published as Richard Bachman.
- **1981:** *Cujo*. Novel.
- **1982:** *The Running Man*. Novel published as Richard Bachman.
- **1982:** *The Dark Tower: The Gunslinger*. Novel assembled from five previously published linked narratives; King produced a materially revised version in 2003.
- **1983:** *Christine*. Novel.
- **1983:** *Pet Sematary*. Novel.
- **1984:** *Cycle of the Werewolf*. Illustrated calendar-novel created with artist Bernie Wrightson; its monthly architecture and integration of image and prose make it a small Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1984:** *The Talisman*. Novel co-created with Peter Straub.
- **1984:** *Thinner*. Novel published as Richard Bachman.
- **1986:** *It*. Novel.
- **1987:** *The Eyes of the Dragon*. Fantasy novel, first manifested in a limited 1984 edition as *The Eyes of the Dragon: A Story*.
- **1987:** *Misery*. Novel.
- **1987:** *The Dark Tower II: The Drawing of the Three*. Novel.
- **1987:** *The Tommyknockers*. Novel.
- **1989:** *The Dark Half*. Novel.
- **1990:** *The Stand: The Complete & Uncut Edition*. Restored and revised novel; not simply a reprint of the 1978 text.
- **1991:** *The Dark Tower III: The Waste Lands*. Novel.
- **1991:** *Needful Things*. Novel.
- **1992:** *Gerald’s Game*. Novel.
- **1992:** *Dolores Claiborne*. Novel whose eclipse scene intersects with *Gerald’s Game*.
- **1994:** *Insomnia*. Novel.
- **1995:** *Rose Madder*. Novel.
- **1996:** *The Green Mile*. Serial novel first manifested in six monthly parts—*The Two Dead Girls*, *The Mouse on the Mile*, *Coffey’s Hands*, *The Bad Death of Eduard Delacroix*, *Night Journey*, and *Coffey on the Mile*—before appearing as a single volume.
- **1996:** *Desperation*. Novel issued simultaneously with the related Bachman novel *The Regulators*.
- **1996:** *The Regulators*. Novel published as Richard Bachman; it is a parallel-world counterpart rather than a conventional sequel to *Desperation*.
- **1997:** *The Dark Tower IV: Wizard and Glass*. Novel.
- **1998:** *Bag of Bones*. Novel.
- **1999:** *The Girl Who Loved Tom Gordon*. Novel.
- **2001:** *Dreamcatcher*. Novel.
- **2001:** *Black House*. Novel co-created with Peter Straub and a sequel to *The Talisman*.
- **2002:** *From a Buick 8*. Novel.
- **2003:** *The Dark Tower I: The Gunslinger—Revised and Expanded*. King’s substantial rewriting of the 1982 novel; it is a distinct authorial text, not merely an edition.
- **2003:** *The Dark Tower V: Wolves of the Calla*. Novel.
- **2004:** *The Dark Tower VI: Song of Susannah*. Novel.
- **2004:** *The Dark Tower VII: The Dark Tower*. Novel completing the principal seven-volume sequence while deliberately unsettling the idea of completion.
- **2005:** *The Colorado Kid*. Mystery novel whose unresolved mystery resists the genre’s customary closure.
- **2006:** *Cell*. Novel.
- **2006:** *Lisey’s Story*. Novel.
- **2007:** *Blaze*. Early novel published as Richard Bachman after extensive revision by King.
- **2008:** *Duma Key*. Novel.
- **2009:** *Under the Dome*. Novel developed from, but not identical with, the abandoned project *The Cannibals*.
- **2011:** *11/22/63*. Novel.
- **2012:** *The Dark Tower: The Wind Through the Keyhole*. Novel positioned chronologically between the fourth and fifth Dark Tower books.
- **2013:** *Joyland*. Novel.
- **2013:** *Doctor Sleep*. Novel and sequel to *The Shining*.
- **2014:** *Mr. Mercedes*. Novel and first Bill Hodges book.
- **2014:** *Revival*. Novel.
- **2015:** *Finders Keepers*. Novel and second Bill Hodges book.
- **2016:** *End of Watch*. Novel and third Bill Hodges book.
- **2017:** *Gwendy’s Button Box*. Novella co-created with Richard Chizmar.
- **2017:** *Sleeping Beauties*. Novel co-created with Owen King.
- **2018:** *The Outsider*. Novel connected to the Bill Hodges/Holly Gibney sequence.
- **2018:** *Elevation*. Novella issued as a standalone book.
- **2019:** *The Institute*. Novel.
- **2021:** *Later*. Novel.
- **2022:** *Gwendy’s Final Task*. Novel co-created with Richard Chizmar; King did not co-author the intervening *Gwendy’s Magic Feather*.
- **2022:** *Fairy Tale*. Novel.
- **2023:** *Holly*. Novel.
- **2025:** *Never Flinch*. Novel.
- **2026:** *Other Worlds Than These*. Announced novel; at the date of this list it is a forthcoming project rather than a completed public manifestation.

### Author-constructed collections and composite books

- **1978:** *Night Shift*. Story collection; its contents are individual obras as well as components of this later authorial arrangement.
- **1982:** *Different Seasons*. Four-novella collection structured by seasonal subtitles.
- **1985:** *Skeleton Crew*. Collection of stories, the novella *The Mist*, and poems.
- **1990:** *Four Past Midnight*. Four-novella collection.
- **1993:** *Nightmares & Dreamscapes*. Collection mixing short fiction, a teleplay, an essay, a poem, and other forms; it resists classification as merely a “story collection.”
- **1999:** *Hearts in Atlantis*. Composite fiction of two novellas and three stories linked by recurring characters, history, and the Vietnam era; simultaneously a collection and a larger made thing.
- **2002:** *Everything’s Eventual: 14 Dark Tales*. Story-and-novella collection.
- **2008:** *Just After Sunset*. Story-and-novella collection.
- **2009:** *Stephen King Goes to the Movies*. Retrospective selection of five previously collected stories with new authorial commentary; principally a curatorial manifestation rather than five new fictions.
- **2010:** *Full Dark, No Stars*. Collection of four novellas; later editions add “Under the Weather,” but that addition should not be mistaken for part of the original arrangement.
- **2015:** *The Bazaar of Bad Dreams*. Collection whose introductions make it partly an anthology of fiction and partly a commentary on making fiction.
- **2020:** *If It Bleeds*. Collection of four novellas.
- **2024:** *You Like It Darker*. Collection of twelve fictions, including stories, novellas, and previously unpublished work.

### Nonfiction books, memoirs, and collaborative documents

- **1981:** *Danse Macabre*. Critical and autobiographical study of horror in twentieth-century popular culture.
- **1988:** *Nightmares in the Sky: Gargoyles and Grotesques*. Image-and-essay book made with photographer f-stop Fitzgerald; the photographs and King’s text form a collaborative visual-verbal object.
- **1994:** *Mid-Life Confidential: The Rock Bottom Remainders Tour America with Three Chords and an Attitude*. Collaborative tour chronicle by King, Dave Barry, Amy Tan, and other members of the Rock Bottom Remainders.
- **2000:** *On Writing: A Memoir of the Craft*. Memoir joined to a writing protocol: it narrates a life and instructs writers.
- **2000:** *Secret Windows: Essays and Fiction on the Craft of Writing*. A limited-edition retrospective compilation, not an entirely new integral text.
- **2004:** *Faithful: Two Diehard Boston Red Sox Fans Chronicle the Historic 2004 Season*. Season-long documentary correspondence co-created with Stewart O’Nan; published in 2004, with subsequent trade manifestations in 2005.
- **2013:** *Guns*. Digital essay responding to American gun violence and King’s decision concerning *Rage*; it operates partly as polemic or manifesto rather than detached description.

### Screen, stage, radio, and multimedia obras

- **1982:** *Creepshow*. Original feature-film screenplay by King, designed with director George A. Romero as a live-action imitation of a horror comic; film and tie-in graphic adaptation together approach a Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1985:** *Cat’s Eye*. Anthology-film screenplay combining adaptations of two earlier stories with the original linking story “General.”
- **1985:** *Silver Bullet*. Feature-film screenplay adapted by King from his own *Cycle of the Werewolf*.
- **1986:** *Maximum Overdrive*. Feature film written and directed by King from his story “Trucks”; it is his sole feature-directing obra.
- **1987:** *Sorry, Right Number*. Original teleplay, later printed in *Nightmares & Dreamscapes*.
- **1991:** *Golden Years*. Television-series project created by King; only seven episodes were broadcast, leaving its narrative deliberately or industrially unfinished.
- **1992:** *Sleepwalkers*. Original feature-film screenplay rather than an adaptation of a prior King story.
- **1993:** *The X-Files: “Chinga”*. Teleplay credited to King, substantially rewritten by series creator Chris Carter; the final episode therefore has disputed or layered textual authorship.
- **1997:** *The Shining*. Television-miniseries teleplay adapting King’s novel more closely than Stanley Kubrick’s film.
- **1997:** *Ghosts*. Long-form musical-film screenplay/story co-created with Michael Jackson, Stan Winston, and Mick Garris; authorship is collaborative and the object crosses music video, short film, and horror narrative.
- **1999:** *Storm of the Century*. Original television-miniseries screenplay, simultaneously published as a “novel for television.”
- **2002:** *Rose Red*. Original television-miniseries screenplay.
- **2004:** *Kingdom Hospital*. Television-series project developed by King from Lars von Trier’s *Riget*; adaptation and reinvention complicate sole authorship.
- **2006:** *Desperation*. Television-film teleplay adapted by King from his novel.
- **2012:** *Ghost Brothers of Darkland County*. Southern-gothic stage musical made with composer and lyricist John Mellencamp and musical director T Bone Burnett; a Gesamtkunstwerk joining King’s book, songs, performance, and recorded manifestation.
- **2018:** *Castle Rock*. Television project associated with King as executive producer and source-world creator, but chiefly made by Sam Shaw and Dustin Thomason; it resists inclusion as a King-authored work and belongs here only as a derivative collaborative manifestation.
- **2021:** *Lisey’s Story*. Eight-episode television-miniseries teleplay written by King from his novel.
- **2021:** *Strawberry Spring*. Scripted podcast adaptation produced with King’s authorization, but not written solely by him; it is a manifestation of his story rather than securely his new obra.

### Comics, illustrated narratives, and transmedia projects

- **1982:** *Creepshow*. Graphic story album adapted from King’s screenplay and illustrated by Bernie Wrightson; because the comics object was executed collaboratively, “King’s comic” overstates sole authorship.
- **1999–2000:** *The Plant: Zenith Rising*. Unfinished epistolary novel released online in installments under an honor-payment experiment; both project and publication protocol are part of the obra.
- **2003:** *The Girl Who Loved Tom Gordon: A Pop-Up Book*. Sculptural paper-engineered adaptation made with Kees Moerbeek and illustrator Alan Dingman; a book-object rather than simply another edition of the novel.
- **2007–2016:** *The Dark Tower* comics. Licensed, collaboratively scripted and illustrated expansions and adaptations overseen by King but chiefly written by Robin Furth and Peter David; they are manifestations of King’s fictional project, not straightforwardly works authored by him.
- **2008:** *Stephen King’s N.*. Animated web-series manifestation of the novella, developed as sequential online episodes.
- **2008–2012:** *The Stand* comics. Licensed Marvel adaptation written by Roberto Aguirre-Sacasa and illustrated by Mike Perkins; derivative manifestation rather than a new King-authored text.
- **2009–2010:** *The Talisman: The Road of Trials*. Comic adaptation of King and Peter Straub’s novel, scripted and drawn by other creators; the planned series was not completed.
- **2010–2011:** *American Vampire*. Comic series created by Scott Snyder and Rafael Albuquerque; King wrote the parallel origin narrative in the first five issues, making that arc collaborative rather than the entire continuing series his obra.
- **2012:** *The Little Green God of Agony*. Free online comic adaptation scripted by Dennis Calero from King’s story; a derivative manifestation.
- **2012:** *Road Rage*. Comic miniseries adapting “Throttle” and Richard Matheson’s “Duel”; King is a source author, while Chris Ryall scripted the comic.
- **2019–2021:** *Sleeping Beauties*. Comic adaptation of the novel, scripted by Rio Youers and illustrated by Alison Sampson; derivative manifestation of Stephen and Owen King’s collaborative fiction.
- **2025:** *Hansel and Gretel*. Retelling written by King for Maurice Sendak’s previously unpublished designs; a posthumous visual-verbal collaboration and therefore not comfortably classified as King’s work alone.

### Early, unfinished, unpublished, and unstable projects

- **c. 1960:** *People, Places and Things*. Self-produced collection made with Chris Chesley in two reportedly surviving copies; the juvenilia’s contents and textual state are imperfectly documented.
- **1963:** *The Aftermath*. Unpublished juvenile novel; it exists as a manuscript rather than an authorized public work.
- **1966–1970:** *The Long Walk*. Early novel project written before King’s professional debut and only manifested publicly in revised form in 1979.
- **1970:** *The House on Value Street*. Abandoned novel project about the kidnapping of an heiress; parts of its intention and research were transformed into *The Stand*, so calling it a completed “work” would be misleading.
- **1972–1973:** *Blaze*. Unpublished early novel until King revised and published it under the Bachman name in 2007.
- **1973:** *The Dark Tower*. Long-form project begun with “The Gunslinger” and extended, revised, and recursively reopened over decades; its seven-volume “completion” does not exhaust the project.
- **1974–1985:** *The Plant*. Unfinished epistolary novel privately issued in three installments as Christmas chapbooks, then expanded and released online in 2000; neither manifestation completes it.
- **1977:** *The Cannibals*. Abandoned novel project, portions and central circumstances of which were later reworked into *Under the Dome*; it survives as a fragment, not a finished novel.
- **1983:** *The Leprechaun*. Unproduced screenplay project; its status is intention and script rather than realized film.
- **1985:** *Cat’s Eye*. The official bibliography also labels a screenplay manuscript “unpublished,” despite the released film; this is a bibliographic distinction between the script as text and the film as manifestation.
- **1998:** *Chinga*. The official bibliography similarly records King’s submitted teleplay as unpublished because the broadcast text was rewritten; the manuscript and aired episode should not be silently conflated.
- **2000:** *The Shotgunners*. Unfinished or unpublished screenplay associated with filmmaker Sam Peckinpah; accounts of its completion and textual survival vary.
- **2008–present:** *The Talisman III*. Repeatedly discussed third Jack Sawyer novel planned with Peter Straub. Straub’s death in 2022 leaves it a disputed or unrealized collaborative project rather than an extant completed book.
