## Works by Federico García Lorca

### Poetry books and cycles

- **1917–1920; published 1921:** *Libro de poemas*. His first poetry book, assembled from youthful poems with help from his brother Francisco; composition and publication are separately dated in the scholarly chronology. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1920–1923; reconstructed posthumously:** *Suites*. A large, repeatedly reorganized poetic project rather than a text Lorca fixed for publication; dispersed manuscripts produced competing reconstructions, beginning with André Belamich’s 1983 critical edition, and new discoveries continue to alter its boundaries. [Centro Federico García Lorca, *Suites* exhibition catalogue](https://www.centrofedericogarcialorca.es/descargas/LORCA_SUITES_cat.pdf)

- **1921–1924; published 1927:** *Canciones*. A deliberately organized poetry book, completed in 1924 and first issued by the Málaga review *Litoral*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1921; published 1931:** *Poema del cante jondo*. A poetic cycle conceived in relation to Andalusian deep song and largely written before the 1922 Concurso de Cante Jondo; it is poetry about and shaped by music, not a transcription of flamenco songs. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1923–1927; published 1928:** *Primer romancero gitano* (usually *Romancero gitano*). A book-length modern transformation of the traditional romance form, not a collection of folk ballads. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1925–1926; published 1926:** *Oda a Salvador Dalí*. A long poem first published separately in the *Revista de Occidente*; it can function as an autonomous poem or as a small publication-object. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928; published 1928:** *Oda al Santísimo Sacramento del Altar*. A long devotional and avant-garde ode dedicated to Manuel de Falla and published separately in the *Revista de Occidente*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928; unfinished:** *Oda a Sesostris*. A projected long poem on which Lorca worked but did not complete; its fragmentary survival resists treatment as a finished poetry book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1929–1930; prepared for publication in 1936; published 1940:** *Poeta en Nueva York*. A multimedia-minded poetry project whose intended arrangement involved poems, photographs and a lecture-recital; Lorca left José Bergamín a manuscript in July 1936, but the two rival first editions and missing editorial materials make its final form disputed. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1931–1934; published 1940:** *Diván del Tamarit*. A book of gacelas and casidas, completed in 1934 and prepared for an edition by the University of Granada that the Civil War prevented; its first publication was therefore posthumous. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932–1934; published 1935:** *Seis poemas galegos*. A six-poem Galician-language book, made with linguistic assistance from Galician friends and edited for publication by Eduardo Blanco-Amor; the degree of that assistance has generated discussion but not serious rejection of Lorca’s authorship. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1934; published 1935:** *Llanto por Ignacio Sánchez Mejías*. A four-part elegiac poem-cycle for the bullfighter and friend, first read publicly before appearing as a separate book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1935–1936; published as a cycle in 1984:** *Sonetos del amor oscuro*. Eleven surviving love sonnets, several written in Valencia in 1935; Lorca did not publish or definitively title the sequence, and editors have also used *Sonetos de amor*, so its received title and completeness remain unsettled. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1917–1936:** *Poemas sueltos*. The collected editions necessarily use this editorial category for autonomous poems and fragments not securely assignable to Lorca’s books; it is a posthumously constituted corpus, not a work he designed under that title. [Bibliografía lorquiana, Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)

### Theatre, puppet theatre and dramatic projects

- **1918:** *La historia del tesoro*. A collaboratively improvised “juego teatral,” staged with Ángel Barrios, Miguel Pizarro and Manuel Ángeles Ortiz; because no stable script is known, it survives more as a theatrical event than as a text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1919–1920:** *El maleficio de la mariposa*. Lorca’s first professionally staged play, a symbolist verse drama about insects; its 1920 production incorporated music by Grieg and designs by Rafael Barradas, making the premiere a collaborative manifestation rather than Lorca’s work alone. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1921–1922:** *Tragicomedia de don Cristóbal y la señá Rosita*. A puppet farce, also transmitted within the unstable grouping *Los títeres de Cachiporra*; manuscripts, rewritings and later performance forms prevent a single uncontested definitive text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1922–1923; lost:** *La niña que riega la albahaca y el príncipe preguntón*. Lorca’s adaptation of a traditional Andalusian tale for the 1923 Three Kings’ Day puppet performance, with music by Manuel de Falla and puppets, scenery and realization by Hermenegildo Lanz and Lorca; the text is lost, so the obra survives principally as documentation of a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1923; unfinished:** *Lola la comedianta*. A projected comic stage work with music by Manuel de Falla; work began, but the collaboration never became a complete performable piece. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1923–1925:** *Mariana Pineda*. A “romance popular en tres estampas,” completed in 1925 and premiered in 1927 with scenery by Salvador Dalí; the dramatic text is Lorca’s, while the first production was an intentionally collaborative visual and theatrical object. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1924–1930:** *La zapatera prodigiosa*. A farce developed through more than one version, begun as a first act in 1924, completed in 1928 and premiered in 1930; Lorca continued revising it for later productions. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1925–1928:** *Amor de don Perlimplín con Belisa en su jardín*. An “aleluya erótica” or chamber farce combining dialogue, music and puppet-like theatricality; an early version was finished in 1926, but Lorca continued revising it before its delayed 1933 premiere. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1929–1930; incomplete surviving text:** *El público*. An experimental drama written chiefly in Cuba and completed in some authorial sense in 1930, but known from an incomplete draft because the fuller manuscript is lost; it therefore resists classification as either simply finished or unfinished. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1931:** *Así que pasen cinco años*. A “legend of time” completed in August 1931; rehearsals begun in 1936 were interrupted by war and Lorca’s murder, so the play existed as a finished text but not as a realized production during his life. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932:** *Bodas de sangre*. A tragedy completed in August 1932 and premiered in 1933; its mixture of prose, verse, song, movement and ritual makes the theatrical object more than a purely literary text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1933–1934:** *Yerma*. A “poema trágico” in prose and verse, completed and premiered in 1934; its songs and choral passages are constitutive dramatic materials, not detachable lyric ornament. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1934:** *Retablillo de don Cristóbal y doña Rosita*. A short puppet farce premiered in Buenos Aires; it reworks the Don Cristóbal material but is a distinct performance-text rather than merely another edition of the earlier *Tragicomedia*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1934–1935:** *Doña Rosita la soltera o El lenguaje de las flores*. A play begun in late 1934, completed and premiered in 1935; Lorca termed it a “poema granadino del novecientos,” deliberately troubling the boundary between drama and poem. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1935–1936; unfinished:** *Comedia sin título*. The surviving first act of an unnamed political and metatheatrical drama, published posthumously under an editorially descriptive title; incompleteness is integral to the object now read. [Instituto Cervantes, *Teatro incompleto–teatro abierto*](https://cervantes.org/es/sobre-nosotros/publicaciones/federico-garcia-lorca-teatr-nie-dokonczony-teatr-otwarty-teatro)

- **1936:** *La casa de Bernarda Alba*. A three-act “drama de mujeres en los pueblos de España,” completed in June and read by Lorca in July 1936 but neither published nor staged during his lifetime. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1936; unfinished:** *Los sueños de mi prima Aurelia*. A dramatic project of which Lorca completed only a first act; it should be catalogued as an unfinished project, not silently promoted to a finished play. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **By 1936; fragmentary project:** *La bola negra* / *La piedra oscura*. A prose or dramatic project about homosexual persecution surviving in only a few manuscript pages; evidence that Lorca changed its title to *La piedra oscura* is recent, and neither genre nor intended final form is secure. [Account of the manuscript and its collected-works publication](https://www.huffingtonpost.es/life/cultura/como-leer-la-bola-negra-lorca-donde-cuatro-paginas-que-edicion-publican-f202609.html)

- **Undated or lost:** *La destrucción de Sodoma*. A dramatic project known from testimony and catalogues but not from a complete surviving text; it resists being called a finished work because its extent and final form cannot be established. [Bibliografía lorquiana, Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)

### Adaptations, direction and theatrical manifestations

- **1923:** *Fiesta de Reyes Magos*. A domestic puppet-theatre Gesamtkunstwerk devised with Manuel de Falla and Hermenegildo Lanz, combining medieval and early-modern texts, Lorca’s lost folk-tale adaptation, music, scenery and puppets; authorship belongs to the event’s collaborators rather than to Lorca alone. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932–1935:** *La Barraca*. A state-sponsored itinerant university-theatre project co-directed artistically by Lorca and Eduardo Ugarte; its productions, tours, costumes, music and public pedagogy form a collective body of work rather than a Lorca-authored book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932:** *La vida es sueño*. Lorca’s La Barraca staging of Calderón’s auto sacramental; the text is Calderón’s, while Lorca’s made thing was an adaptation and production, so it should not be mistaken for an authored Lorca play. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1933:** *Fuenteovejuna*. A La Barraca production of Lope de Vega, adapted and directed under Lorca and Ugarte with scenery and costumes by Alberto Sánchez; authorship is distributed across text, adaptation, performance and design. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1934:** *La dama boba* (advertised as *La niña boba*). Lorca’s stage adaptation of Lope de Vega, premiered in Buenos Aires; it is an adaptation and directing object, not an original Lorca drama. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1934:** *El burlador de Sevilla*. A La Barraca production of the play attributed to Tirso de Molina; Lorca’s contribution lies in theatrical selection, adaptation and staging, not authorship of the source text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1935:** *Peribáñez y el comendador de Ocaña*. A Club Anfistora production of Lope de Vega on which Lorca collaborated; it is evidence of theatrical making, but not securely a wholly Lorca-directed or Lorca-authored work. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

### Prose, screenplay and editorial creations

- **1916:** *Mi pueblo*. An autobiographical prose piece about Fuente Vaqueros, preserved among Lorca’s juvenile prose rather than designed as an independent book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1917:** *Fantasía simbólica*. An early prose text published for the centenary of José Zorrilla; it belongs to Lorca’s juvenilia rather than to a later author-shaped collection. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1917–1918; published 1918:** *Impresiones y paisajes*. A book of travel impressions developed from educational journeys and newspaper prose; it is Lorca’s first published book and combines travel writing, meditation and lyrical prose. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1927:** *Santa Lucía y San Lázaro*. A short surrealizing prose piece published in the *Revista de Occidente*, autonomous but later collected among Lorca’s prose. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1927–1928:** *Nadadora sumergida*. A surrealist prose-poem; its genre is intentionally unstable between narrative, image and lyric text. [Bibliografía lorquiana, Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)

- **1927–1928:** *Suicidio en Alejandría*. A short surrealist prose-poem rather than a conventional story, preserved within the collected prose. [Bibliografía lorquiana, Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)

- **1927–1928:** *Amantes asesinados por una perdiz*. A surrealist prose-poem whose title and image-logic resist ordinary narrative classification. [Bibliografía lorquiana, Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)

- **1928–1929; published 1929:** *Degollación de los inocentes*. A surrealist prose piece first published with an illustration by Salvador Dalí; publication joins writing and image without making the illustration Lorca’s. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1929:** *Viaje a la luna*. A screenplay written in New York for possible realization with Emilio Amero; it is a film-project and sequence of visual instructions, not a film Lorca completed. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928:** *Gallo*. A two-number Granada avant-garde review conceived and edited by Lorca with collaborators; the magazine as collective publication-object is the obra, while its individual contents have separate authors. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928:** *Pavo*. A comic counter-review made by the same circle as *Gallo*; its parodic action makes it closer to a collective manifestation than to a conventional authored text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1924; unrealized:** *Libro de los putrefactos*. A projected collaboration initiated by Salvador Dalí, to which Lorca was to contribute a prologue; because the book never appeared and Lorca’s intended contribution is not securely extant, it remains a project rather than a completed joint work. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

### Lectures, lecture-recitals and spoken-text protocols

- **1922; revised thereafter:** *El cante jondo. Primitivo canto andaluz* / *La arquitectura del cante jondo*. A lecture first delivered with live guitar illustration and later revised and retitled; its performed, musical form is part of the work, and the titles do not necessarily identify two wholly separate texts. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1926:** *La imagen poética de don Luis de Góngora*. A critical lecture delivered in Granada and later Madrid; it is an oral-performance text as well as an essay. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1926:** *Paraíso cerrado para muchos, jardines abiertos para pocos*. A lecture on the Granada poet Pedro Soto de Rojas, repeatedly delivered in revised performance contexts. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928–1930:** *Imaginación, inspiración, evasión* / *La mecánica de la poesía*. A lecture repeatedly revised and renamed; the variant titles mark an evolving discourse rather than securely separate works. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928:** *Sketch de la nueva pintura*. A lecture on modern painting, conceived as a spoken intervention in contemporary visual art rather than as a book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928–1930:** *El patetismo de la canción de cuna española* / *Canciones de cuna españolas*. A lecture-recital using discussion and sung examples; its live conjunction of speech and music makes it a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely an essay. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932–1933:** *Un poeta en Nueva York*. A lecture-recital made to present and order the New York poems, often with projected photographs; it is both commentary on *Poeta en Nueva York* and a distinct performed manifestation of that project. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932:** *Elegía a María Blanchard*. A memorial address for the painter, delivered on several occasions; it belongs to Lorca’s oratorical prose rather than to his poetic elegy-books. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1933:** *Juego y teoría del duende*. A performative aesthetic lecture developed during the voyage to and stay in Argentina; it proposes an artistic theory while also enacting the quality it describes. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1933:** *Cómo canta una ciudad de noviembre a noviembre*. A lecture-recital assembled from songs, memories and piano performance; its inseparability of spoken text and performed music makes “lecture” only a partial classification. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1933:** *Al alimón sobre Rubén Darío*. A jointly delivered lecture with Pablo Neruda; it is a dialogic performance whose authorship cannot properly be assigned to Lorca alone. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1936:** *Alocución sobre la Semana Santa granadina*. A radio address made for Unión Radio; its primary object was a broadcast performance, although it survives through textual transcription. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

### Music and sound objects

- **Before 1916:** *Juvenile piano compositions*. Lorca wrote early instrumental pieces, including compositions known as *Canción de invierno* and *Madrigal de verano*, but the musical corpus is small, partly unpublished and textually uncertain; it should not be conflated with later musical settings of his poetry by other composers. [Roger D. Tinnell, *Federico García Lorca y la música: catálogo y discografía anotados*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Federico_Garc%C3%ADa_Lorca_y_la_m%C3%BAsica.html?id=i01LAAAAYAAJ)

- **1930–1931:** *Colección de canciones populares españolas* (also *Canciones populares antiguas*). Lorca collected, selected and harmonized traditional melodies, supplied piano accompaniments and recorded them with Encarnación López “La Argentinita”; the melodies and many words are traditional, so this is an arrangement-and-performance obra rather than original song composition. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1931:** *Five gramophone discs with La Argentinita*. Ten recorded performances issued by La Voz de su Amo, with La Argentinita singing and Lorca playing piano; these sound recordings are the surviving manifestation of the song project and the only generally recognized commercial recordings of Lorca as performer. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1931:** *Zorongo gitano*. A traditional song arranged and accompanied by Lorca for the recorded collection; Lorca made the harmonization and performance, not the underlying folk song.

- **1931:** *Anda jaleo*. A traditional song arranged and accompanied by Lorca; attribution should be to him as arranger and pianist, not as author of the traditional melody or lyric.

- **1931:** *Los cuatro muleros*. A traditional song in Lorca’s piano arrangement and recorded performance with La Argentinita.

- **1931:** *En el Café de Chinitas*. A traditional song arranged and accompanied by Lorca for the recording project.

- **1931:** *Las tres hojas*. A traditional song arranged and accompanied by Lorca for the recording project.

- **1931:** *Los mozos de Monleón*. A traditional ballad arranged and accompanied by Lorca.

- **1931:** *Los pelegrinitos*. A traditional song arranged and accompanied by Lorca.

- **1931:** *Romance de Don Boyso*. A traditional romance arranged and accompanied by Lorca.

- **1931:** *Las morillas de Jaén*. Lorca’s arrangement and piano performance of a song derived from older Iberian tradition.

- **1931:** *Sevillanas del siglo XVIII*. Lorca’s arrangement and piano performance of traditional sevillanas. The ten recorded titles and Lorca’s role are catalogued as a single discographic project rather than ten claims of original composition. [Roger D. Tinnell, *Federico García Lorca y la música: catálogo y discografía anotados*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Federico_Garc%C3%ADa_Lorca_y_la_m%C3%BAsica.html?id=i01LAAAAYAAJ)

### Drawings and visual projects

- **Circa 1915–1936:** *Drawings*. Lorca made hundreds of autonomous drawings, illustrated manuscripts, dedications, theatre sketches and hybrid word-images, many untitled or multiply titled by later cataloguers; this corpus is a body of visual work, not one “book,” and item-level titles and dates are often editorial or approximate. Mario Hernández’s catalogue is the standard point of reference. [Spanish State Archives catalogue record for *Libro de los dibujos de Federico García Lorca*](https://www.mcu.es/ccbae/es/consulta/registro.do?id=54701)

- **1927:** *Galeries Dalmau exhibition*. Lorca’s Barcelona exhibition constituted a selected public arrangement of his drawings and therefore a distinct visual manifestation, even though its constituent sheets also belong to the larger drawing corpus. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1928; unrealized:** *Libro de dibujos*. A projected book of Lorca’s drawings, intended to carry a prologue by Sebastià Gasch and an epilogue by Salvador Dalí; it remained an editorial project rather than a finished book. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1932:** *Eight drawings shown at the Ateneo Popular de Huelva*. A small exhibited group whose selection and display constituted a public manifestation; surviving sheets must be identified through the drawing catalogues rather than assumed from the exhibition notice alone. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

### Manifestos, correspondence and unstable corpora

- **1933:** *Manifesto against Hitler*. Lorca was one of its signatories; this is a manifesto in the strict sense—a collective text that acts publicly—but not a text attributable to Lorca as sole author. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **1936:** *Manifesto against António de Oliveira Salazar*. Another collective political act signed by Lorca; the signature is his intervention, while authorship of the wording is collective or uncertain. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/cronologia/)

- **Circa 1910s–1936:** *Correspondence*. Hundreds of letters, postcards, dedications and notes survive and are catalogued separately as Lorca’s outgoing correspondence; they are made texts, but not a single author-designed work, and many remain privately held, lost or known only through copies. [Fundación Federico García Lorca, *Catálogo general de los fondos documentales*, vols. V–VI, described here](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)

- **1917–1936:** *Juvenilia, fragments and untitled manuscripts*. The archive and critical collected editions preserve additional poems, dramatic fragments, prose drafts, lists and unrealized titles. Treating every archival leaf as a separately titled “work” would invent boundaries Lorca did not establish; the appropriate obra is therefore the documented, open corpus, with uncertainty retained at item level. [Fundación Federico García Lorca manuscript catalogues, described by the Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/portales/federico_garcia_lorca/bibliografia/)
