## Works by Erik Satie

### Piano works and keyboard projects

- **9 September 1884:** *Allegro*. Satie’s earliest surviving piano piece, based on Frédéric Bérat’s song “Ma Normandie”; it remained unpublished until 1995.
- **1885:** *Valse-ballet*. A piano waltz, privately published by Satie’s father in 1887.
- **1885:** *Fantaisie-valse*. A piano waltz, likewise issued by Alfred Satie in 1887.
- **1886:** *Deux quatuors à cordes*. An unfinished project for two string quartets, surviving only in sketches.
- **1886–89:** *Quatre Ogives*. Four austere pieces for piano or organ; sources disagree slightly over whether to date the set wholly to 1886 or through its 1889 publication.
- **September 1887:** *Trois Sarabandes*. A cycle of three piano sarabandes.
- **1888:** *Trois Gymnopédies*. Three piano dances; Debussy later orchestrated Nos. 1 and 3, but those orchestrations are Debussy’s manifestations of Satie’s work.
- **1889:** *Chanson hongroise*. A piano fragment or exercise, published only posthumously.
- **1889–97:** *Gnossiennes*. Six separately composed piano pieces later treated as a series; only Nos. 1–3 were grouped and published by Satie, while the numbering and association of Nos. 4–6 are posthumous editorial decisions.
- **1890:** *Danse*. A piece for small orchestra whose music was subsequently reused in *Trois morceaux en forme de poire*.
- **1891:** *[Untitled Rose+Croix piece]*. An untitled piano composition later published by Robert Caby as *Première pensée Rose+Croix*; that supplied title is not Satie’s.
- **1891:** *Leit-motiv du “Panthée”*. A short, incompletely specified musical object without surviving instrumentation.
- **1892:** *Fête donnée par des chevaliers normands en l’honneur d’une jeune demoiselle*. A ceremonial piano piece associated with Satie’s Rose+Croix period.
- **1892–93:** *Prélude d’Eginhard*. A short piano prelude, published posthumously.
- **21–23 March 1893:** *Danses gothiques*. Nine linked piano dances presented as prayers or acts of spiritual discipline.
- **1893:** *Vexations*. A short, enigmatic keyboard text bearing an instruction that its motif be played 840 times. Whether that instruction makes one performance the “work,” a protocol, or an unrealizable project remains disputed.
- **1893:** *Prière*. A brief keyboard or organ piece associated with the religious works.
- **1893:** *Modéré*. An untitled-in-substance piano or organ piece identified by its tempo marking; it may belong to *Messe des pauvres*.
- **1897:** *Pièces froides*. Six piano pieces divided into *Trois airs à faire fuir* and *Trois danses de travers*.
- **1897:** *Petite ouverture à danser*. A small piano piece, preserved among Satie’s manuscripts and published posthumously.
- **1897:** *Caresse*. An unfinished or fragmentary piano piece, published posthumously.
- **1897–1914:** *Carnet d’esquisses et de croquis*. An editorially assembled body of sketches, exercises, fragments, and such manuscript headings as *Coucher de soleil du sommeil*, *Eaux troublées*, *Deuil*, *Lignes dans le sable*, *Ondulation*, *Champ*, *Transparence*, and *Le Mur de verre*. It resists classification as a work because Satie did not constitute the surviving leaves as a finished collection.
- **1899:** *Jack in the Box*. Three piano pieces for an unrealized pantomime by Jules Dépaquit; Satie’s manuscript was recovered after his death and orchestrated by Darius Milhaud.
- **1899–1900:** *Aline-Polka*. An incomplete piano transcription of a polka by Henry Pacory, so its musical authorship is shared or derivative.
- **c. March 1900:** *Rêverie du pauvre*. A piano adaptation or transposition of music attributed to Jules Massenet, rather than a wholly original composition.
- **1900:** *Verset laïque et somptueux*. A piano piece whose title recasts a liturgical form in secular terms.
- **1901:** *Le Poisson rêveur* or *The Dreamy Fish*. Incidental piano music for a tale by “Lord Cheminot,” the pseudonym of Contamine de Latour; it survives incompletely.
- **1901:** *Le Bœuf Angora*. An unfinished orchestral project whose surviving music was reconstructed for piano by Johny Fritz; later completions are not wholly Satie’s.
- **1901:** *Poudre d’or*. A waltz made in piano and orchestral forms.
- **1902:** *Illusion*. A piano and orchestral instrumentalization derived from *Tendrement*.
- **1903:** *Trois morceaux en forme de poire*. Seven pieces for piano four hands—despite the title’s “three”—assembling new and reused music into a deliberately anti-formal suite.
- **1906:** *Fugue-valse*. A piano exercise from Satie’s Schola Cantorum period.
- **1906:** *Passacaille*. A piano passacaglia composed during his formal contrapuntal studies.
- **1906:** *Prélude en tapisserie*. A piano prelude conceived in the language of decorative art.
- **1906:** *Nun ruhen alle Wälder*. Satie’s keyboard harmonization of a Lutheran chorale, therefore an arrangement rather than an entirely original obra.
- **1906–09:** *Douze petits chorals*. Twelve student exercises for piano; the familiar collection and its text were established posthumously by Robert Caby.
- **1907:** *Nouvelles pièces froides*. Three piano pieces, *Sur un mur*, *Sur un arbre*, and *Sur un pont*.
- **1908:** *Fâcheux exemple*. A counterpoint exercise later extracted and published as a piano piece.
- **1908:** *Désespoir agréable*. A counterpoint exercise later made to function as an independent piano miniature.
- **1908–12:** *Aperçus désagréables*. A three-part work—*Pastorale*, *Choral*, and *Fugue*—for piano four hands.
- **1908–09:** *Petite sonate*. An unfinished piano sonata project, represented by only a first movement.
- **c. 1909:** *Deux choses*. Two piano pieces, *Effronterie* and *Poésie*, assembled and published posthumously.
- **1909:** *Profondeur*. A minuet exercise for piano.
- **1909:** *Menuet lent*. An unfinished Schola Cantorum exercise.
- **1909:** *Menuet basque*. An unfinished piano minuet surviving in sketches.
- **1909:** *Le Conteur magique*. An unfinished minuet project surviving on a single sketch leaf.
- **1909:** *Songe-creux*. A piano exercise later published as an independent miniature.
- **1909:** *Le Prisonnier maussade*. A piano exercise later published posthumously.
- **1909:** *Le Grand Singe*. A small minuet exercise for piano.
- **1909:** *Le Dîner de Pierrot*. Arrangements of cabaret songs for a children’s performance; the music is lost.
- **1911:** *En habit de cheval*. Two chorales alternating with two fugues, made both for piano four hands and for orchestra.
- **c. 1912:** *Deux rêveries nocturnes*. Two posthumously published piano pieces identified principally by their tempo indications.
- **1912:** *Deux préludes pour un chien*. A rejected two-piece project; the first prelude remains untitled and unpublished, while the second was issued posthumously as *Prélude canin*.
- **1912:** *Préludes flasques (pour un chien)*. Four piano pieces rejected by the publisher Durand: *Voix d’intérieur*, *Idylle cynique*, *Chanson canine*, and *Avec camaraderie*.
- **1912:** *Véritables préludes flasques (pour un chien)*. A replacement set of three piano pieces: *Sévère réprimande*, *Seul à la maison*, and *On joue*.
- **1913:** *Descriptions automatiques*. Three text-bearing piano pieces: *Sur un vaisseau*, *Sur une lanterne*, and *Sur un casque*.
- **1913:** *Embryons desséchés*. Three comic piano pieces named for marine organisms.
- **2 August 1913:** *San Bernardo*. The first version of the piano piece later revised as *Españaña*; published only in 2002.
- **1913:** *Croquis et agaceries d’un gros bonhomme en bois*. Three piano pieces: *Tyrolienne turque*, *Danse maigre*, and *Españaña*.
- **1913:** *Chapitres tournés en tous sens*. Three piano pieces: *Celle qui parle trop*, *Le Porteur de grosses pierres*, and *Regrets des enfermés*.
- **1913:** *Vieux sequins et vieilles cuirasses*. Three mock-historical piano pieces.
- **1913:** *L’Enfance de Ko-Quo*. Three children’s piano pieces withheld after the publisher objected to their accompanying texts; first published in 1999.
- **1913:** *Trois nouvelles enfantines*. Three children’s piano pieces—*Le Vilain Petit Vaurien*, *Berceuse*, and *La Gentille Toute Petite Fille*—whose collective title is posthumous.
- **1913:** *Menus propos enfantins*. Three illustrated or verbally annotated children’s piano pieces.
- **1913:** *Enfantillages pittoresques*. Three children’s piano pieces.
- **1913:** *Peccadilles importunes*. Three children’s piano pieces whose long titles act as miniature moral narratives.
- **1913:** *As-tu vu la casquette du père Bugeaud?* An incomplete harmonization of a military tune, rather than an autonomous original melody.
- **1913:** *Le Veau d’or*. An unrealized parody project drawing on Gounod and Offenbach; its intended medium is uncertain.
- **1914:** *Sports et divertissements*. A Gesamtkunstwerk-like album of twenty-one miniature piano pieces, calligraphic prose-poems, and Satie’s drawings; music, word, image, and book design are inseparable manifestations.
- **1914:** *Heures séculaires et instantanées*. Three piano pieces with private prose texts that Satie explicitly warned performers not to read aloud.
- **1914:** *Sous les catalpas*. An unrealized project for three piano pieces; titles survive, but the projected music does not.
- **1914:** *Le Cheval est un animal hippique, équestre & domestique*. An unrealized three-piece piano project, known from its plan and titles.
- **July 1914:** *Les Trois Valses distinguées du précieux dégoûté*. Three piano waltzes forming a musical and verbal portrait.
- **November 1914:** *Soupirs fanés*. An abandoned three-piece piano project; one projected piece is untitled.
- **November 1914:** *Souvenirs fadasses*. An abandoned project for *Barbouillage*, *Poil*, and *Recrudescence*.
- **1915:** *Avant-dernières pensées*. Three piano pieces—*Idylle*, *Aubade*, and *Méditation*—dedicated respectively to Debussy, Dukas, and Roussel.
- **1917:** *Sonatine bureaucratique*. A piano parody or bureaucratic “adaptation” of Clementi’s Sonatina Op. 36 No. 1; its derivative construction is the point of the work.
- **1917:** *Rag-time Parade*. Hans Ourdine’s solo-piano arrangement of music from *Parade*; the composition is Satie’s, but this manifestation is not solely his making.
- **1919:** *Cinq Nocturnes*. Five completed piano nocturnes; sketches for a sixth survive, so “six nocturnes” describes an unfinished project rather than a completed set.
- **1919:** *Petite danse*. A brief piano piece published posthumously.
- **1919:** *Rêverie de l’enfance de Pantagruel*. A piano manifestation of the first of the *Trois petites pièces montées*.
- **1920:** *Premier Menuet*. Satie’s final completed solo-piano work.

### Songs, mélodies, and vocal works

- **possibly February 1887:** *Élégie*. A song for voice and piano to words by Contamine de Latour; the precise date is uncertain.
- **April–May 1887:** *Trois mélodies*. The songs *Les Anges*, *Les Fleurs*, and *Sylvie*, to poems by Contamine de Latour; later publications regrouped some of them differently.
- **December 1887:** *Chanson*. A song to words by Contamine de Latour, later incorporated editorially into *Trois autres mélodies*.
- **1893:** *Bonjour Biqui, bonjour!*. A very short song and graphic love-object addressed to Suzanne Valadon.
- **1893–95:** *Messe des pauvres*. A mass-like work for voices and organ assembled from separately conceived religious numbers. Its original order, completeness, and relationship to the lost *Messe de la foi* remain uncertain.
- **1899–1901:** *Trois chansons de cabaret*. Sketches for *Les Complots*, *Une séance à la Haute Cour*, and *Les Dépêches anglaises*; all three are unfinished.
- **12 August 1899:** *Un dîner à l’Élysée*. A cabaret song for voice and piano to words by Vincent Hyspa.
- **1899–1900:** *Le Veuf*. A cabaret song to words by Hyspa, surviving in two musical versions.
- **1900:** *Geneviève de Brabant*. A comic lyric work for voices, chorus, and piano, probably to a text by Contamine de Latour; its authorship was initially obscured by pseudonyms and its surviving form is incomplete.
- **1900:** *Chœur des marins*. A vocal number later associated with the unfinished opera *Paul & Virginie*.
- **1901:** *Poudre d’or*. Besides its piano form, Satie made an orchestral dance manifestation.
- **1902:** *Tendrement*. A sung waltz to words by Vincent Hyspa, also transformed into piano and orchestral versions.
- **published 1903:** *Je te veux*. A waltz-song to words by Henry Pacory, also existing in piano and orchestral manifestations; its exact composition date precedes but is less secure than its publication date.
- **1903–04:** *Petit recueil des fêtes*. Four cabaret songs—*Le Picador est mort*, *Sorcière*, *Enfant-martyre*, and *Air fantôme*—to words by Vincent Hyspa.
- **1904:** *La Diva de l’Empire*. A café-concert song to words by Dominique Bonnaud and Numa Blès, also orchestrated; the piano *Intermezzo américain* is Hans Ourdine’s arrangement.
- **1904:** *J’avais un ami*. A cabaret song to words by Vincent Hyspa.
- **1904:** *Douceur d’oublier*. A cabaret song to words by Hyspa.
- **1904–05:** *Les Bons Mouvements*. A cabaret song to words by Hyspa.
- **1905:** *Chez le docteur*. A comic song to words by Hyspa.
- **18 August 1905:** *Impérial-Oxford*. A cabaret song whose music survives but whose text by Contamine de Latour is lost.
- **18 August 1905:** *Légende californienne*. A cabaret song whose text is lost; its music was later reused in *La Belle Excentrique*.
- **September 1905:** *L’Omnibus automobile*. A cabaret song to words by Hyspa.
- **1905:** *Allons-y Chochotte*. A café-concert song to words by D. Durante.
- **1905–06:** *Pousse l’amour*. An operetta project, apparently abandoned and now lost.
- **1906:** *Chanson médiévale*. A song to a text by Catulle Mendès, later grouped with two earlier songs as *Trois autres mélodies*.
- **1907:** *Rambouillet (Une réception à Rambouillet)*. A cabaret song whose words are lost.
- **1907:** *Les Oiseaux (Ils nous prêtent leurs noms)*. A cabaret song surviving without its text.
- **1907:** *Marienbad (Il portait un gilet)*. A cabaret song surviving without its text.
- **1907:** *Psitt! Psitt!*. A cabaret song.
- **1909:** *La Chemise*. A song to words by Jules Dépaquit, surviving in three versions.
- **1914:** *Trois poèmes d’amour*. Three songs for voice and piano for which Satie wrote both words and music; the deliberately numbered love-poems fuse literary and musical objects.
- **1916:** *Trois mélodies*. Settings of Léon-Paul Fargue’s *La Statue de bronze*, Mimi Godebska’s *Daphénéo*, and René Chalupt’s *Le Chapelier*.
- **1917–18:** *Socrate*. A “symphonic drama” for voices and chamber orchestra using Victor Cousin’s French translation of Plato; Satie also made the voice-and-piano version.
- **1920:** *Quatre petites mélodies*. Four songs—*Élégie*, *Danseuse*, *Chanson*, and *Adieu*—setting Lamartine, Cocteau, and anonymous eighteenth-century verse.
- **1920–23:** *Paul & Virginie*. An abandoned three-act comic-opera project with Cocteau and André Derain; only two vocal numbers survive, so the intention rather than a complete opera is the work.
- **1923:** *Ludions*. Five songs to poems by Léon-Paul Fargue: *Air du rat*, *Spleen*, *La Grenouille américaine*, *Air du poète*, and *Chanson du chat*.

### Chamber, orchestral, and environmental music

- **1891–92:** *Trois sonneries de la Rose+Croix*. Three ceremonial fanfares—*Air de l’Ordre*, *Air du Grand Maître*, and *Air du Grand Prieur*—made for brass and harps and also in piano form; the original ensemble specification is not wholly secure.
- **1904:** *Le Piccadilly*, also called *La Transatlantique*. A march or cakewalk existing for piano and for piano with strings.
- **1914:** *Choses vues à droite et à gauche (sans lunettes)*. Three pieces for violin and piano: *Choral hypocrite*, *Fugue à tâtons*, and *Fantaisie musculaire*.
- **1914:** *Autre choral*. A violin-and-piano piece rejected from or left unused in *Choses vues à droite et à gauche*.
- **1917:** *Embarquement pour Cythère*. An unfinished work for violin and piano; modern performing completions, including Robert Orledge’s, necessarily add another authorial hand.
- **1918:** *Musique d’ameublement*. The initial furniture-music project, comprising *Tapisserie en fer forgé* and *Carrelage phonique*. It resists the autonomous-work category: Satie intended it as environmental sound not to be listened to.
- **1919:** *Marche de Cocagne*. A miniature for two trumpets, subsequently reused in *Trois petites pièces montées*.
- **1919–20:** *Trois petites pièces montées*. An orchestral triptych—*De l’enfance de Pantagruel*, *Marche de Cocagne*, and *Jeux de Gargantua*—also issued for piano four hands.
- **1920:** *Sons industriels*, also called *Deux entr’actes de musique d’ameublement*. Two pieces, *Chez un “bistrot”* and *Un salon*, for clarinets, trombone, and piano four hands. They are protocols for socially ambient sound as much as concert pieces.
- **1921:** *Sonnerie pour réveiller le bon gros Roi des Singes*. A tiny ceremonial fanfare for two trumpets.
- **1923:** *Tenture de cabinet préfectoral*. Furniture music for small orchestra; like the earlier pieces, it deliberately refuses attentive concert listening.

### Theatre, ballet, spectacle, and cinema

- **1891:** *Salut drapeau!* A hymn for Joséphin Péladan’s drama *Le Prince de Byzance*, surviving in a form whose intended vocal and instrumental forces are uncertain.
- **1891:** *Le Fils des étoiles*. Music for Péladan’s “Chaldean pastoral,” originally conceived for flutes and harps and surviving chiefly through Satie’s three piano preludes.
- **1892:** *Deux préludes du Nazaréen*. Two piano preludes for Henri Mazel’s esoteric drama; the projected complete dramatic collaboration did not materialize.
- **December 1892:** *Uspud*. A “Christian ballet” made with Contamine de Latour. Its piano score, scenario, typography, and fanciful orchestral indications form a multimedia project closer to a paper Gesamtkunstwerk than to a conventionally staged ballet.
- **December 1892:** *Noël*. Incidental piano music for a shadow-theatre spectacle; it is lost apart from partial reconstruction.
- **1894:** *Prélude de La Porte héroïque du ciel*. A piano prelude for Jules Bois’s esoteric drama.
- **1900:** *Petit prélude de “La Mort de Monsieur Mouche”*. A surviving piano prelude for a lost or unrealized play by Contamine de Latour.
- **1913:** *Le Piège de Méduse*. A one-act “lyric comedy” for which Satie made both the dramatic text and seven dances for prepared piano, later orchestrated. As play, score, performance instruction, and visual publication with Braque’s woodcuts, it exceeds a single-medium classification.
- **1913:** *Les Pantins dansent*. Music for a “poème dansé,” made in piano and small-orchestra forms.
- **1915:** *Cinq grimaces pour Le Songe d’une nuit d’été*. Incidental orchestral music for an unrealized circus-style production of Shakespeare planned with Jean Cocteau; the surviving suite was published posthumously.
- **1916–17; additions 1919:** *Parade*. A “realist ballet” created with Cocteau, Picasso, Massine, and the Ballets Russes. Its score incorporates noise-making instruments, while scenario, choreography, décor, and music make the staged event a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than Satie’s property alone.
- **1920–21:** *La Belle Excentrique*. A four-part “fantaisie sérieuse” for dancer and orchestra or piano four hands, incorporating earlier material from *Légende californienne*.
- **1923:** *La Statue retrouvée*. Music for a collaborative divertissement by Cocteau and Picasso, surviving as a brief trumpet-and-organ piece.
- **July–December 1923:** *Scènes nouvelles pour “Le Médecin malgré lui”*. Nine recitatives and a finale inserted into Gounod’s opera; it is an intervention in another composer’s obra rather than a freestanding opera.
- **1924:** *Concurrence*. An unrealized ballet project; no completed score survives.
- **1924:** *Quadrille*. An unrealized ballet project, known as an intention rather than a completed musical object.
- **February 1924:** *Deux petites choses*. An abandoned ballet project; the title and proposal survive without a completed score.
- **1924:** *Mercure, poses plastiques en trois tableaux*. A ballet made with Picasso and Léonide Massine; its poses, décor, choreography, and orchestral music constitute a collaborative spectacle.
- **1924:** *Relâche*. An “instantaneist ballet” created with Francis Picabia and Jean Börlin. Its provocatively advertised cancellation, stage action, score, and film entr’acte make the event a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1924:** *Cinéma*. The orchestral score synchronized to René Clair’s film *Entr’acte*, performed between the acts of *Relâche*; it is both film music and a component of the larger spectacle.

### Literary works, manifestos, protocols, and institutional fictions

- **1892:** *Uspud*. In addition to its music, Satie and Contamine de Latour made a printed ballet argument or scenario; the exact division of literary authorship between them is not fully recoverable.
- **1893–95:** *Église métropolitaine d’art de Jésus-Conducteur*. A church founded, designed, administered, and effectively populated by Satie alone. It is an institutional artwork or sustained project rather than a conventional text.
- **1893–95:** *Cartulaire de l’Église métropolitaine d’art de Jésus-Conducteur*. Satie’s self-published church bulletin, containing pronouncements, statutes, polemics, blessings, and anathemas. These writings act on their imagined institution and therefore function as manifestos and performative decrees, not merely descriptions.
- **1895:** *Intende votis supplicum*. A separately printed church polemic or anathema directed against figures Satie considered enemies of art and religion.
- **1890s–1925:** *Private advertisements, prospectuses, visiting cards, and institutional stationery*. Elaborately calligraphed notices found among Satie’s papers, many apparently never circulated. They resist work-status: some are jokes, fictional bureaucracy, graphic objects, or rehearsals of an imagined public identity.
- **1900–25:** *Critical articles, lectures, aphorisms, and musical polemics*. A dispersed textual body collected posthumously by Ornella Volta and Nigel Wilkins; it includes writings commonly known as *Mémoires d’un amnésique*, *Cahiers d’un mammifère*, and *Observations d’un imbécile (Moi)*. These are editorial groupings, not books issued by Satie in those collected forms.
- **1912:** *Mémoires d’un amnésique*. A sequence of short prose pieces published in the *Revue musicale S.I.M.*; it parodies autobiography rather than supplying one.
- **February–March 1913:** *Le Piège de Méduse*. The dramatic text of Satie’s lyric comedy, later published as a book in 1921; it is also inseparable from its dances and stage protocol.
- **1913–14:** *Texts and drawings for Sports et divertissements*. Twenty prose-poems and drawings plus a prefatory chorale, integrated calligraphically with the music; treating them as mere “programme notes” would misdescribe the made object.
- **1914:** *Trois poèmes d’amour*. Three deliberately artless love-poems written to be sung in Satie’s own settings; words and music are coequal parts of the obra.
- **1917:** *Le Cubisme et la musique*. A published article connecting contemporary musical practice with Cubism.
- **1918–20:** *Musique d’ameublement declarations and performance instructions*. Short manifestos and protocols defining music meant to merge with a room’s noises and suppress conventional listening.
- **1921:** *La Journée du musicien*. A comic prose self-portrait describing the composer’s supposedly exact daily routine; it is literary persona-making, not reliable biography.
- **various dates:** *Three poems without fixed collective title*. Six-line poems preserved in manuscript and published posthumously by Nigel Wilkins; the later grouping is editorial, and their individual dating is uncertain.
- **various dates:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters, postcards, petitions, and unsent communications survive, edited as *Correspondance presque complète*. They are things Satie made, sometimes with conspicuous graphic and performative design, but they resist wholesale classification as an authored “work.”

### Graphic objects and embodied manifestations

- **1890s–1920s:** *Calligraphic scores*. Satie’s manuscript scores frequently combine ornamental handwriting, drawings, private performance directions, and music. They are distinct visual manifestations even where the underlying composition also exists as conventional print.
- **1913–14:** *Original album of Sports et divertissements*. A set of hand-lettered musical and pictorial panels commissioned by Lucien Vogel; the facsimile album is a visual-musical object, not simply an edition of piano pieces.
- **c. 1893–95:** *Insignia, seals, vestments, hierarchy, and proposed abbatial apparatus of the Église métropolitaine*. Designs and descriptions survive unevenly. Their status ranges from graphic artefact to unrealized institutional project, and they should not be silently promoted into completed physical works.
- **1890s–1925:** *Self-fashioned public personae*. The “Velvet Gentleman,” church dignitary, functionary in identical suits, and later bowler-hatted composer were embodied, durational manifestations constructed through dress, stationery, photographs, and public conduct. They resist attribution as discrete works, but their resistance is essential to Satie’s practice.
