## Works by Abd al-Qadir al-Jilani

### Composed protocol

- **Undated; probably late in life:** *al-Ghunya li-ṭālibī ṭarīq al-ḥaqq* (“Sufficient Provision for Seekers of the Path of Truth”). A substantial protocol for Muslim life, combining ritual law, everyday conduct, creed, ethics, and instruction for Sufi novices; it is the only extant work generally accepted as writing composed by al-Jilani himself, although its precise date is unknown. ([Malik, pp. 16–19](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781463244392_A46705757/preview-9781463244392_A46705757.pdf))

### Spoken works preserved as sermon collections

- **Probably early 1140s–early 1160s; compiled during or after his lifetime:** *Futūḥ al-ghayb* (“Openings/Insights into the Unseen”). Seventy-eight short discourses or lectures on spiritual discipline, probably assembled by one of al-Jilani’s sons; sources disagree whether the compiler was ʿĪsā or ʿAbd al-Razzāq, and one transmission adds two further discourses supplied by ʿAbd al-Wahhāb. Most scholars accept an authentic core, but Jacqueline Chabbi questioned the attribution; it is thus an oral obra mediated by posthumous compilation, not straightforwardly an authored book. ([Malik, pp. 19–21](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781463244392_A46705757/preview-9781463244392_A46705757.pdf); [Munjid, pp. 25, 30 n. 51](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

- **545–546 AH / 1150–1152 CE:** *al-Fatḥ al-rabbānī* (“The Divine Opening”; also circulated as *al-Fatḥ al-rabbānī wa-l-fayḍ al-raḥmānī*). A collection of sixty-two dated preaching sessions: the made acts were sermons, while the surviving textual object is their compilation. Braune, Demeerseman, Malik, and much modern scholarship accept it, but Chabbi rejected every attributed work except the *Ghunya*, and early biographical literature does not securely attest this title. ([Malik, pp. 8–9, 21](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781463244392_A46705757/preview-9781463244392_A46705757.pdf); [Munjid, p. 27](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

### Dubious or composite textual attributions

- **Undated; transmitted later:** *Malfūẓāt* (“Utterances”). A collection of sayings attributed to al-Jilani and commonly appended to editions of *al-Fatḥ al-rabbānī*; its compiler, date, and textual boundaries are uncertain, and stylistic and historical objections prevent treating it securely as his obra. ([Munjid, pp. 27–28](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

- **Undated; transmitted later:** *Khamsata ʿashara maktūban* (“Fifteen Letters”). Fifteen letters said to have been written to a disciple; their attribution is traditional rather than securely documented, and the collection contains material judged stylistically or historically unreliable. ([Munjid, pp. 27–28](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

- **Undated; transmitted later:** *Jalāʾ al-khawāṭir* (“Clarification/Purification of Thoughts,” also “Removal of Cares”). A further collection of forty-five discourses attributed to al-Jilani. Because its transmission is late and its contents include anachronistic material, it resists classification as either his book or an authenticated record of his spoken obra. ([Munjid, pp. 27–28](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

- **Undated; transmitted later:** *Sirr al-asrār wa-maẓhar al-anwār* (“The Secret of Secrets and Manifestation of Lights”). A protocol-like mystical manual treating creation, classes of saints, the divisions of sacred knowledge, remembrance, and litanies; historical anachronisms and its omission of the Hanbali position where that would be expected make al-Jilani’s authorship highly doubtful. ([Munjid, pp. 27–28](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041); [Post, p. 65 n. 137](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/71/82/14/2020718214/2020718214.pdf))

### Rejected attribution

- **Published under his name in 2009; underlying text earlier and undated here:** *Tafsīr al-Jīlānī* / *al-Fawātiḥ al-ilāhiyya wa-l-mafātiḥ al-ghaybiyya*. A Qurʾanic commentary marketed as al-Jilani’s work but rejected by textual scholarship: its theology conflicts with positions securely associated with the Hanbali author, and the text itself has been identified with another authorial tradition. It belongs to the posthumous manifestation of “al-Jilani” as an author, not to the recoverable corpus of things he made. ([Post, p. 65 n. 137](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/71/82/14/2020718214/2020718214.pdf); [Kaya, “A Study on the Attribution and Authenticity Problems…”](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/1083234))

### Pedagogical and institutional obra

- **From 521 AH / 1127 CE:** Public preaching, teaching, and legal responses in Baghdad. This was an ongoing performative obra rather than a single text: al-Jilani preached regularly, taught Qurʾanic exegesis, hadith, and jurisprudence, and issued legal and ethical answers; the sermon collections preserve only portions mediated by listeners and compilers. ([Munjid, pp. 20–21](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

- **From 528 AH / 1133–34 CE:** The teaching community centered on al-Mukharrimī’s madrasa and an associated *ribāṭ* in Baghdad. Al-Jilani directed and animated this institutional project, but the buildings were constructed or enlarged for him through donors and communal labor, so they resist attribution as architecture he personally made. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/abd-al-qader-jilani/); [Munjid, p. 20](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))

- **Posthumous formation:** *The Qādiriyya*. The order bearing his name is a historical manifestation of his teaching and reputation, not securely his deliberate foundation: Fazlur Rahman, Trimingham, and later scholarship doubt that he designed a formal order or left a system called the Qādiriyya. It should therefore not be catalogued without qualification as an organization he made. ([Munjid, pp. 20–21](https://journal.umy.ac.id/index.php/afkaruna/article/download/43/2161/6041))
