## Works by Dihya

### Political and military projects

- **c. 688–703:** *Resistance to the Umayyad conquest of Ifrīqiya*. A political-military project in which Dihya led forces associated with the Aurès and opposed Ḥassān ibn al-Nuʿmān; the resistance and Ḥassān’s temporary retreat are accepted in broad outline, but the surviving narratives were written centuries later and do not securely define her polity, coalition, or precise chronology. ([Modéran](https://doi.org/10.4000/encyclopedieberbere.1306); [Kaegi](https://books.google.com/books?id=zexq5Hl42mQC))

- **late 690s, date disputed:** *Victory over Ḥassān ibn al-Nuʿmān*. A military outcome—sometimes located at Wādī Nīnī or Meskiana—rather than a separable authored “work.” The victory and Ḥassān’s withdrawal toward Cyrenaica belong to the narrative’s credible core, while its date, location, scale, and reported five-year interval before his return depend on conflicting late sources. ([Modéran](https://doi.org/10.4000/encyclopedieberbere.1306); [Kaegi](https://books.google.com/books?id=zexq5Hl42mQC))

### Destructive projects attributed to her

- **before Ḥassān’s final campaign, conventionally c. 698–703:** *Dismantling of the fortress of Baghāya*. This is an attributed act of unmaking: al-Mālikī’s tradition says that Dihya had the fortress dismantled so that it could not serve Ḥassān. It has no original title, its date is not secure, and archaeology has not supplied an identifiable structure that can confidently be presented as her work. ([Modéran](https://doi.org/10.4000/encyclopedieberbere.1306))

- **late 690s–early 700s, attribution disputed:** *Scorched-earth campaign*. Later medieval narratives make this a deliberate project—destroying settlements, crops, and orchards to deprive the invaders of wealth—but modern source criticism treats the story as a progressively enlarged hostile motif rather than a securely recoverable program. It therefore resists classification as Dihya’s obra: the devastation may reflect prolonged warfare, later moralizing legend, or acts wrongly concentrated under her name. ([Hannoum](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ahess_0395-2649_1999_num_54_3_279771); [Modéran](https://doi.org/10.4000/encyclopedieberbere.1306))

### No securely attributable material or textual works

- **seventh century:** *No surviving authored corpus*. No securely attributable book, manifesto, protocol, inscription, coinage, building, monument, or portable object by Dihya is identified in the standard modern scholarly treatments. Palaces, fortresses, wells, and other places subsequently bearing her name belong to her afterlife in legend and commemoration, not to a demonstrable catalogue of things she made. ([Modéran](https://doi.org/10.4000/encyclopedieberbere.1306); [Hannoum](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ahess_0395-2649_1999_num_54_3_279771))
