## Works by Jakob Fugger 'the Rich'

### Foundations and institutional obras

- **1509–1521:** *Fugger Chapel in St Anne’s, Augsburg*. A family funerary chapel and memorial initiated jointly by Jakob and Ulrich Fugger, built in 1509–1512 and secured as a permanent foundation by Jakob in 1521 on behalf of himself and his deceased brothers. Because architecture, sculpture, painting, furnishings, liturgy, burial, and dynastic representation form a single programme—executed by artists including Hans Daucher and Jörg Breu the Elder after designs associated with Albrecht Dürer—it is properly a commissioned **Gesamtkunstwerk**, not an object personally fabricated by Fugger; individual artistic attributions remain a specialist question. ([Scheller](https://doi.org/10.18452/24508); [Fugger Foundations](https://www.fugger.de/stiftungen/repraesentation-zur-ehre-der-verstorbenen); [KHM exhibition](https://renaissance-im-norden.khm.at/en/emperor-merchants-clergy/))

- **1514–1521:** *Fuggerei*. A project for an endowed settlement of inexpensive dwellings for impoverished Augsburg Catholics, planned by 1514, under construction from 1516, and legally constituted in 1521; the name “Fuggerei” is documented only from 1531. It is simultaneously architecture, a continuing social protocol, and an institution, and was founded by Jakob in the names of his deceased brothers Georg and Ulrich rather than as his solitary work. ([Scheller](https://doi.org/10.18452/24508); [Fugger history timeline](https://www.fugger.de/en/history/history-of-the-fugger-family-since-1367); [Fugger Foundations](https://www.fugger.de/en/foundations/merchant-benefactor-visionary))

- **1517–1521:** *Preaching Endowment at St Moritz, Augsburg* (*Prädikatur bei St. Moritz*). A permanently financed preaching office—an institutional and liturgical obra rather than a building—established through a papal grant of patronage in 1517 and secured with the other principal foundations in the charter of 1521. It too was constituted in the names of Jakob, Georg, and Ulrich Fugger. ([Scheller](https://doi.org/10.18452/24508); [Fugger Foundations](https://www.fugger.de/geschichte/vielseitig-und-seit-500-jahren-aktiv))

- **23 August 1521:** *Foundation complex of the Fugger Chapel, Fuggerei, and St Moritz preaching post*. The three foundations were legally joined, financed, and protected through one charter and a dedicated capital fund, while remaining three distinct institutions. As an enduring administrative design, this is a corporate obra and a **protocol** for perpetuity; it should not be conflated with any one of its physical manifestations. ([Scheller](https://doi.org/10.18452/24508); [Fugger Foundations](https://www.fugger.de/en/history/jakob-fugger-the-worlds-most-successful-philanthropist))

### Buildings and commissioned environments

- **1500–1514:** *Rebuilding of the Chapel of St Mark in Santa Maria dell’Anima, Rome* (now the *Cappella del Crocifisso*). The chapel originated as a foundation of Jakob’s brother Markus, but its rebuilding was carried through under Jakob; it therefore resists attribution as Jakob’s independent creation and is better classified as his continuation of a familial commemorative project. ([Aloys Schulte, *Die Fugger in Rom 1495–1523*, listed in the specialist bibliography](https://books.google.com/books?id=9xLfyZ7qB4EC); [summary with attribution](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Fugger))

- **1512–1515; enlarged 1523:** *Fugger Houses at the Weinmarkt, Augsburg*. A residence, warehouse, representational palace, and headquarters of the family firm assembled from existing properties rather than created as an entirely new freestanding building. The architect is disputed between Hans Hieber and Jakob Zwitzel; much of the original interior and façade painting was subsequently lost, and the complex was heavily damaged in 1944 and rebuilt in simplified form. ([Augsburg City Lexicon](https://www.stadtlexikon-augsburg.de/stadtlexikon-augsburg/artikel/stadtlexikon/fuggerhauser/3841); [Augsburg architectural-history brochure](https://www.augsburg.de/fileadmin/user_upload/footer/presse/downloads/2021/21_09_03_TdoD21_Broschuere_RZ_web_kompr.pdf))

- **1512–1515:** *Great Court, Small Court, and Ladies’ Court of the Fugger Houses*. A sequence of architectural interiors combining the upper-German urban house with forms derived from Italian palace architecture; the Ladies’ Court is the most concentrated Renaissance manifestation of the ensemble. These courts are separable components but not wholly independent works, and their designer shares the unresolved Hieber-or-Zwitzel attribution of the larger complex. ([Augsburg City Lexicon](https://www.stadtlexikon-augsburg.de/stadtlexikon-augsburg/artikel/stadtlexikon/fuggerhauser/3841))

- **1512–1515:** *Painted and furnished programme of the Fugger Houses*. A now largely lost ensemble of façade frescoes attributed to Hans Burgkmair the Elder and interiors containing painted decoration, leather hangings, fireplaces, majolica stoves, and gilded ceilings. Fugger made the environment as patron and organizer, not as executing artist; later destruction makes its original completeness impossible to reconstruct securely. ([Augsburg City Lexicon](https://www.stadtlexikon-augsburg.de/stadtlexikon-augsburg/artikel/stadtlexikon/fuggerhauser/3841); [Lieb](https://books.google.de/books?id=xd5NAQAAIAAJ))

### Commercial and organizational projects

- **1494 onward:** *Fugger–Thurzo mining enterprise*. A transregional commercial project joining Fugger capital and distribution to the Thurzos’ mining and metallurgical operations in Hungary. It was a jointly made organization rather than a single authored object: agreements were concluded in 1494, renewed and expanded in 1495, and followed by permissions, declarations of common ownership, service contracts, and revised arrangements through 1499. ([Jansen, documentary appendix](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche))

- **18 August 1494:** *First partnership agreement of the Fugger brothers*. A corporate **protocol** constituting and regulating a family trading company; Jakob was one participant, so the document and the organization it made cannot be assigned to him alone. ([Jansen, contents and documentary edition](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche))

- **23–24 December 1502:** *Renewed partnership agreement and special commercial provisions*. A group of corporate protocols: renewal of the Fugger partnership, special rules for the Hungarian mines and the Carinthian and Thuringian smelting works, and separate provisions for real property. These are collective legal acts preserved by scholarship as several documents, not a unitary literary work by Jakob. ([Jansen, contents and documentary edition](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche))

- **30 December 1512:** *Extension of the partnership agreement and special provisions for the Hungarian mines and landed property*. These instruments reorganized the business around Jakob and his nephews, helping make the firm customarily known as “Jakob Fugger and Nephews.” They are joint commercial protocols whose practical authorship includes legal and scribal labor that the surviving titles do not disentangle. ([Jansen, contents and documentary edition](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche))

- **by 1525:** *International Fugger trading, mining, and finance organization under Jakob’s direction*. A distributed organizational obra comprising mines, smelting works, trading posts, credit arrangements, information routes, agents, accounts, and contracts rather than one stable artifact. It resists enumeration as a conventional “work”: ownership and decision-making were corporate, its instruments survive unevenly, and the specialist histories catalogue transactions rather than establish a closed œuvre. ([Jansen](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche); [Fugger Archive publication series](https://www.fugger.de/archiv/veroeffentlichungen-des-fuggerarchivs))

### Legal protocols and testamentary projects

- **27 August 1521:** *First testament*. A dispositive legal **protocol** for succession, property, family obligations, and the preservation of Fugger institutions. The surviving document is Fugger’s testamentary act, although—as with early-modern legal instruments—its written form should not automatically be treated as wholly autograph composition. ([Jansen, documentary edition](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche); [Konrad Verlag, Fugger-testament edition](https://www.konrad-verlag.de/programm/titel/die-fuggertestamente-des-16-jahrhunderts-1.html))

- **22 December 1525:** *Second testament*. Fugger’s final succession protocol, made eight days before his death and replacing or revising the earlier testamentary settlement. It is both a text and an operative project for what should happen after its maker could no longer act; authorship of its legal wording is not identical to authorship in the literary sense. ([Jansen, documentary edition](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche); [Konrad Verlag, Fugger-testament edition](https://www.konrad-verlag.de/programm/titel/die-fuggertestamente-des-16-jahrhunderts-1.html))

### Letters and interventions

- **24 April 1523:** *Letter to Emperor Charles V*. A dictated or scribally transmitted demand for settlement of imperial obligations and a reminder of Fugger’s role in financing Charles’s election. Because it sought repayment and political protection for large commercial companies, it functions less as descriptive correspondence than as a forceful intervention—almost a private **manifesto** of financial power. ([University of Augsburg scholarly study](https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/40104); [Fugger Archive presentation of the document](https://www.fugger.de/veranstaltungen/fugger-forum/jakob-fugger-nicht-zu-fassen/jakob-fugger-und-karl-v-wer-hat-hier-wen-in-der-hand))

- **January 1525:** *Letters to Duke George of Saxony concerning Luther and the Peasants’ War*. Political correspondence preserved in the edited records of Duke George’s church policy and discussed in Fugger scholarship. These are individual interventions rather than a composed collection; the surviving documentary edition, not Fugger himself, supplies their corpus-like form. ([Saxon Academy edition guide](https://www.saw-leipzig.de/de/publikationen/digitale-publikationen/Akten-und-Briefe-zur-Kirchenpolitik-Herzog-Georgs-von-Sachsen); [SLUB record for vol. 2, 1525–1527](https://slub.qucosa.de/landing-page/https%3A%2F%2Fslub.qucosa.de%2Fapi%2Fqucosa%253A33966%2Fmets/?L=1))

- **various dates:** *Surviving business letters, instructions, accounts, contracts, and financial instruments*. This archival residue is not a fixed-titled work or complete correspondence: many texts were produced by secretaries, factors, partners, and counterparties in the firm’s name, and the standard editions print selections rather than a complete Jakob-Fugger corpus. It should therefore be acknowledged as a distributed documentary practice while resisting false item-by-item attribution to Fugger personally. ([Jansen](https://elibrary.duncker-humblot.com/book/34580/jakob-fugger-der-reiche); [Fugger Archive publication programme](https://www.fugger.de/archiv/veroeffentlichungen-des-fuggerarchivs))
