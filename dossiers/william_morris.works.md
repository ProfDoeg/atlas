## Works by William Morris

### Poetry, verse narratives, and drama

- **c. 1854–1857:** *Early Poems*. A corpus of manuscript and periodical poems preceding Morris’s first collection; the boundaries and dates of individual pieces remain uncertain. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1856:** *The Oxford and Cambridge Magazine contributions*. Eight poems, five prose romances, reviews, and essays written for the short-lived periodical Morris founded and financed; this is a collaborative publishing project as well as a body of texts. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1858:** *The Defence of Guenevere, and Other Poems*. A collection of thirty poems, chiefly dramatic medievalist lyrics. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1867:** *The Life and Death of Jason*. A book-length narrative poem retelling the Argonautic legend. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1868–1870:** *The Earthly Paradise*. A four-part cycle of twenty-four verse tales framed by the journey of medieval wanderers; several drafted or projected tales were omitted, so the published poem is also the surviving form of a larger project. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1868–1870:** *Tales Omitted from The Earthly Paradise*. Unpublished, abandoned, or excluded verse narratives belonging to the project rather than to its completed manifestation. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1872:** *Love Is Enough; or, The Freeing of Pharamond*. A morality play in verse, written for reading rather than conventional theatrical production and framed with lyrics intended for music. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1876:** *The Story of Sigurd the Volsung and the Fall of the Niblungs*. A book-length epic poem made from Norse legendary material Morris had previously translated. [The Collected Works of William Morris](https://marxists.architexturez.net/archive/morris/collected/)
- **1885:** *Chants for Socialists*. A sequence of political songs and poems meant for collective use: less a conventional poetry book than an agitational and performative protocol. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1885–1886:** *The Pilgrims of Hope*. A socialist narrative poem first serialized in *Commonweal* and left formally incomplete; its incompletion is part of its status as a project. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1887:** *The Tables Turned; or, Nupkins Awakened*. A socialist stage play and satirical intervention written for performance by activists. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1891:** *Poems by the Way*. A collection bringing together recent lyrics, political songs, translations, and earlier pieces. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Various dates:** *Poems from the Prose Romances*. Lyrics embedded in Morris’s late romances; they resist treatment as autonomous works because their primary form is architectural—songs placed inside larger narratives. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)

### Prose romances and fiction

- **1856:** *The Story of the Unknown Church*. An early prose romance published in *The Oxford and Cambridge Magazine*. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1856:** *The Lindenborg Pool*. An early supernatural prose romance. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1856:** *A Dream*. An early prose dream-vision. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1856:** *The Hollow Land*. A prose romance whose three-part dream structure resists the later category of the fantasy novel. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1856:** *Golden Wings*. An early prose romance published in *The Oxford and Cambridge Magazine*. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **c. 1872:** *The Novel on Blue Paper*. An unfinished contemporary novel preserved in manuscript; it is a project rather than a completed work, and its familiar title is editorial. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1886–1887; book 1888:** *A Dream of John Ball*. A historical-socialist dream vision first serialized in *Commonweal*. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1886; book 1888:** *A King’s Lesson*. A brief historical parable originally published beside *A Dream of John Ball*. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1889:** *A Tale of the House of the Wolfings and All the Kindreds of the Mark*. A prose-and-verse historical fantasy; its mixed form deliberately refuses the separation of epic, song, and novel. [The Collected Works of William Morris](https://marxists.architexturez.net/archive/morris/collected/)
- **1889:** *The Roots of the Mountains*. A prose romance set in an invented Germanic society. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1890:** *News from Nowhere; or, An Epoch of Rest*. A utopian romance first serialized in *Commonweal*; fiction, socialist argument, and designed social world operate as one obra. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1890; revised book 1891:** *The Story of the Glittering Plain*. A prose romance issued first in conventional form and then as the first book Morris printed at the Kelmscott Press; the two manifestations are materially different objects. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1894:** *The Wood Beyond the World*. A prose romance important to the formation of modern secondary-world fantasy. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *Child Christopher and Goldilind the Fair*. A prose-and-verse romance adapted from the medieval tale of Havelok. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Well at the World’s End*. A four-part prose romance and one of Morris’s last completed books. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895–1896; published 1897:** *The Water of the Wondrous Isles*. A completed prose romance published after Morris’s death. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896; published 1897:** *The Sundering Flood*. Morris dictated the ending during his final illness; its posthumous text is complete in narrative terms but materially mediated by amanuensis and editor. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Various dates:** *Unfinished Romances*. A manuscript corpus of abandoned narratives rather than a work Morris titled or prepared as a book; its resistance to singular “work” status is essential. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)

### Translations and collaborative retellings

- **1869:** *The Story of Grettir the Strong*. A translation of *Grettis saga*, made collaboratively with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1870:** *The Story of the Volsungs and Niblungs, with Certain Songs from the Elder Edda*. A collaborative translation with Eiríkr Magnússon, distinct from Morris’s later verse epic on the same material. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1871:** *Three Northern Love Stories, and Other Tales*. A collection of Icelandic saga translations made with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1871:** *The Story of Gunnlaug the Worm-Tongue and Raven the Skald*. A saga translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon and included among the northern love stories. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1871:** *The Story of Frithiof the Bold*. A saga translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1871:** *The Story of Viglund the Fair*. A saga translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1875:** *The Aeneids of Virgil*. Morris’s deliberately archaizing verse translation of Virgil’s *Aeneid*. [The Collected Works of William Morris](https://marxists.architexturez.net/archive/morris/collected/)
- **1887:** *The Odyssey of Homer Done into English Verse*. A verse translation in Morris’s idiosyncratically Germanic English. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *The Tale of Beowulf*. A verse translation made with the philologist A. J. Wyatt; the extent and character of each collaborator’s contribution make sole authorship inappropriate. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Old French Romance of the Tale of King Coustans the Emperor*. A translation printed by the Kelmscott Press. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The History of Over Sea*. A translation of an Old French romance printed with *King Coustans*. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Ordination of Knighthood*. A translation of an Old French didactic text; both narrative and protocol for chivalric conduct. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Order of Chivalry*. Morris’s translated manifestation of Ramon Llull’s chivalric treatise, published with *The Ordination of Knighthood*. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *Lancelot of the Lake*. An incomplete translation project from the Old French prose romance. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *Tristram*. An incomplete Old French translation project, not a finished book. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *Egil’s Saga*. A translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon whose publication history is posthumous. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *The Tale of Haldor*. A short Icelandic translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *The Story of Kormak*. A saga translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *The Story of the Men of Weaponfirth*. A saga translation made with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unpublished in Morris’s lifetime:** *The Laxdæla Saga*. A collaborative translation with Eiríkr Magnússon. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1891–1895:** *The Saga Library*. A six-volume collaborative translation project by Morris and Eiríkr Magnússon; individual sagas remain distinct texts inside a deliberately constructed corpus. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **Unfinished:** *The Iliad of Homer*. An incomplete verse-translation project; Morris translated only part of the poem. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)

### Travel writing, criticism, lectures, and socialist texts

- **1871 and 1873:** *Journals of Travel in Iceland*. Two manuscript travel journals, not originally made as a public book; their later collected form is editorial. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1877:** *The Lesser Arts*. A lecture arguing that everyday objects and environments belong within art; later collected in *Hopes and Fears for Art*. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1877:** *Manifesto of the Society for the Protection of Ancient Buildings*. A collaborative manifesto conventionally associated with Morris and Philip Webb; it acts by founding a preservation ethic rather than merely describing one. [Society for the Protection of Ancient Buildings](https://www.spab.org.uk/about-us/spab-manifesto)
- **1877–1896:** *Addresses, reports, letters, and interventions for the Society for the Protection of Ancient Buildings*. A practical preservation corpus whose “work” lies partly in attempted prevention; several speeches are lost or survive only through reports. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1878:** *The Decorative Arts: Their Relation to Modern Life and Progress*. A public lecture and design polemic. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1879:** *The Art of the People*. A lecture joining artistic production to social conditions. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1880:** *The Beauty of Life*. A lecture arguing for a unified art of dwelling and later incorporated into *Hopes and Fears for Art*. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1881:** *Art and the Beauty of the Earth*. A lecture on built environments, ornament, and common life. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1882:** *Hopes and Fears for Art*. A collection of five lectures; the book is a later manifestation of texts first made as public speech. [The Collected Works of William Morris](https://marxists.architexturez.net/archive/morris/collected/)
- **1883:** *Art, Wealth and Riches*. A socialist lecture connecting artistic deprivation to class society. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1883:** *Art under Plutocracy*. A lecture and socialist polemic. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1883:** *Useful Work versus Useless Toil*. A lecture subsequently issued as a pamphlet; its distinction between kinds of labour functions as political argument and practical criterion. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1884:** *Art and Socialism*. A lecture joining Morris’s artistic and revolutionary projects. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1884:** *How We Live and How We Might Live*. A lecture that moves from diagnosis into a projected social design. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1884:** *The Manifesto of the Socialist League*. A collectively issued manifesto drafted principally by Morris and revised through the League; its corporate authorship should not be reduced to Morris alone. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1884:** *A Summary of the Principles of Socialism*. A pamphlet written with H. M. Hyndman; joint authorship is explicit. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1885:** *The Worker’s Share of Art*. An article treating access to beauty as a social claim. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1885:** *The Socialist League Manifesto, second edition*. A revised manifestation annotated by Morris and E. Belfort Bax rather than a simple reprint of the first manifesto. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1885:** *For Whom Shall We Vote?* A Socialist League election leaflet; a tactical manifesto rather than an explanatory essay. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1886–1888:** *Socialism from the Root Up*. A sequence written with E. Belfort Bax and serialized in *Commonweal*; the joint text was later manifested as a book. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1886:** *A Short Account of the Commune of Paris of 1871*. A Socialist League pamphlet credited jointly to Morris, E. Belfort Bax, and Victor Dave. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1886:** *True and False Society*. A lecture and socialist pamphlet. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1886:** *Socialism: The Ends and the Means*. A strategic political lecture. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1886:** *Home Rule and Humbug*. A Socialist League pamphlet arguing against parliamentary substitution for social revolution. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1887:** *The Policy of Abstention*. A statement of Morris’s anti-parliamentary socialist strategy. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1887:** *The Society of the Future*. A speculative political lecture whose imagined society overlaps with, but is not identical to, *News from Nowhere*. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1887–1888:** *The Socialist Diary*. A private political diary, unfinished and not made for publication; calling it a “work” risks converting working notes into a designed public text. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/nonfictiondiariesessays/socialistdiariesessays)
- **1888:** *Signs of Change*. A collection of seven socialist lectures and essays. [The Collected Works of William Morris](https://marxists.architexturez.net/archive/morris/collected/)
- **1890:** *Gothic Architecture*. An essay developed from Morris’s introduction to John Henry Parker’s edition of a portion of Ruskin’s *The Nature of Gothic*. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1891:** *The Socialist Ideal: Art*. A late statement of Morris’s insistence that art is inseparable from free social labour. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1892:** *The Preface to The Nature of Gothic*. A framing essay for the Kelmscott Press edition of Ruskin; simultaneously criticism and a declaration of the press’s artistic politics. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1893:** *Communism*. A late lecture defining Morris’s revolutionary ideal. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/nonfictiondiariesessays/socialistdiariesessays)
- **1893:** *Textiles*. An essay classifying and evaluating textile techniques from the standpoint of a practicing designer. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1893:** *Printing*. An essay on typography, materials, spacing, and the construction of the ideal book. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1893:** *The Ideal Book*. A lecture articulating the book as an integrated visual and material object. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1894:** *How I Became a Socialist*. An autobiographical political essay. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1896:** *The Present Outlook of Socialism in England*. A late assessment of socialist organization and historical development. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/nonfictiondiariesessays/socialistdiariesessays)
- **1870–1896:** *Reviews, letters to editors, Commonweal journalism, political resolutions, speeches, and reported interventions*. A corpus of hundreds of occasion-specific texts, including collaborative, unattributed, reconstructed, and explicitly lost items; it resists conversion into a stable list of authored “works.” Nicholas Salmon’s chronological register records both surviving and lost manifestations rather than silently treating the corpus as complete. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)

### Painting, drawing, calligraphy, and furniture

- **1856:** *La Belle Iseult*. Morris’s only known completed easel painting, long called *Queen Guenevere*; the model is generally identified as Jane Burden, but the title and subject were reassessed by Tate. [Tate](https://www.tate.org.uk/art/artworks/morris-la-belle-iseult-n04999)
- **c. 1856–1858:** *King Arthur and Sir Lancelot*. Figurative designs later used in the collaborative Tristram-and-Isoude stained-glass scheme; Morris’s cartoons are components of a larger ensemble rather than autonomous windows. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1857:** *The Backgammon Players*. A stained-glass cartoon and medievalizing composition associated with the Red Lion Square period. [Birmingham Museums](https://www.birminghammuseums.org.uk/)
- **1857–1858:** *The Prioress’s Tale wardrobe*. A painted piece of furniture designed by Philip Webb and decorated collaboratively by Morris and Edward Burne-Jones; individual hands and responsibilities are not entirely separable. [Ashmolean Museum](https://www.ashmolean.org/)
- **c. 1857–1860:** *Red Lion Square furniture*. Heavy medievalizing chairs, tables, settle, and cabinet pieces made for Morris and Burne-Jones’s rooms, usually to Philip Webb’s structural designs with painted decoration by the circle; attribution to Morris alone would be misleading. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-philip-webb-red-house)
- **1856–1896:** *Calligraphic manuscripts*. A body of decorated handwritten books and leaves—literary, liturgical, and experimental—in which script, initials, borders, and page architecture form a single obra; some remained unfinished. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **c. 1870:** *A Book of Verse*. An illuminated manuscript made for Georgiana Burne-Jones, combining Morris’s poetry, calligraphy, painted ornament, and book design. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1870s:** *The Odes of Horace manuscript*. An illuminated calligraphic manuscript; a private handmade book rather than a publication. [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/collection-items/the-odes-of-horace-illuminated-by-william-morris)
- **1870s:** *The Story of Frithiof manuscript*. A decorated calligraphic manuscript joining translation, lettering, and painted ornament. [Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/William-Morris)
- **c. 1872:** *Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám manuscript*. A collaborative illuminated book written and ornamented by Morris, with figurative work by Edward Burne-Jones and Charles Fairfax Murray; it is a Gesamtkunstwerk, not solely Morris’s artifact. [British Library](https://www.bl.uk/collection-items/the-rubaiyat-of-omar-khayyam)
- **1874:** *A Book of Verse manuscript for Louisa Baldwin*. An illuminated manuscript whose calligraphy and ornament make the text into a singular material object. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)

### Houses, rooms, and decorative ensembles

- **1859–1860:** *Red House*. A domestic Gesamtkunstwerk commissioned by Morris and architecturally designed by Philip Webb, with furniture, stained glass, painting, textiles, and garden shaped collaboratively by their circle; calling it a building “by Morris” alone obscures that authorship. [National Trust](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/london/red-house/history-of-red-house)
- **1866–1868:** *The Green Dining Room*. A complete interior for the South Kensington Museum, designed and executed by Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co.; Morris supplied its organizing decorative scheme while Webb, Burne-Jones, and others contributed distinct elements. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-green-dining-room)
- **1866–1867:** *Armoury and Tapestry Rooms, St James’s Palace*. A royal decorative commission involving painted ceilings, wallpapers, and fittings; an ensemble made by the Firm under Morris’s direction rather than a single portable object. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1867:** *Dining room at 1 Holland Park for Aleco Ionides*. A collaborative interior by the Firm, with Morris responsible for ornamental design within an ensemble involving Philip Webb and others. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-his-circle)
- **1876–1878:** *Red House decoration revisited*. Morris & Co. supplied later wallpapers and textiles to the house; these are manifestations of independent patterns, not a second architectural authorship. [National Trust](https://www.nationaltrust.org.uk/visit/london/red-house/history-of-red-house)
- **1878–1896:** *Kelmscott House interiors and workshops*. A lived, repeatedly altered domestic-production environment rather than a fixed completed work; Morris used it for weaving, meetings, printing experiments, and socialist organization. [William Morris Society](https://williammorrissociety.org/about-william-morris/kelmscott-house/)
- **1881–1896:** *Merton Abbey Works*. A production system for dyeing, block printing, weaving, stained glass, tapestry, and carpets; Morris adapted rather than architecturally authored the site, so its status is best understood as an industrial-artistic project. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)

### Wallpaper and surface-pattern designs

- **1862; issued 1864:** *Trellis*. Morris’s first wallpaper design, inspired by the rose trellis at Red House; Philip Webb drew the birds, making the repeat collaborative in detail. [William Morris Gallery](https://www.wmgallery.org.uk/object/trellis-wallpaper/)
- **1862; issued 1864:** *Daisy*. An early wallpaper of meadow flowers; sources differ over whether to date it by design or first printing. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1864–1866:** *Fruit*, also called *Pomegranate*. An early fruit-and-foliage wallpaper whose two established names refer to the same pattern. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1864–1866:** *Diaper*. A wallpaper adapted from one of Morris’s tile patterns rather than conceived independently for paper. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1868–1871:** *Indian*. A wallpaper adapted from an earlier paper; attribution is confused, and the surviving business correspondence has led to a possible association with George Gilbert Scott Jr. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1868–1871:** *Queen Anne*. An adaptation of an older wallpaper sample supplied by Charles Harrison Townsend; “Morris design” here means redesign and manufacture, not wholly original invention. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1868–1871:** *Carnation*. One of the Firm’s wallpapers adapted from an eighteenth- or early nineteenth-century model; exact creative responsibility is not secure. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1868–1871:** *Daffodil*. An adapted historical wallpaper whose attribution should remain with the Firm’s collaborative production rather than unqualifiedly with Morris. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1871:** *Scroll*. A complex wallpaper whose separately usable underpattern was also issued as *Branch*. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1871:** *Branch*. The underprint extracted from *Scroll* and issued independently; both component and autonomous commercial pattern. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1872:** *Larkspur*. A wallpaper repeat built from vertically climbing flowers and foliage. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1872:** *Jasmine*. A layered wallpaper integrating surface foliage with a secondary underpattern. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1874:** *Willow*. A wallpaper built from overlapping willow leaves, later reworked more naturalistically as *Willow Bough*. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1875:** *Marigold*. A compact repeat designed for both wallpaper and printed textile, so no single medium exhausts the obra. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1876:** *Pimpernel*. A large-scale wallpaper dominated visually by tulip forms despite its title. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1876:** *Wreath*. A wallpaper pattern of curving floral garlands. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1876:** *Chrysanthemum*. A wallpaper design later manifested in multiple colourways. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1877:** *Acanthus*. A monumental foliage wallpaper using deeply layered scrolling leaves. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1877:** *Apple*. A fruit-and-foliage wallpaper repeat. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1877:** *Bower*. A dense floral wallpaper design. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1878:** *Peacock*. A wallpaper whose title and attribution appear in Morris & Co. design records; surviving variants complicate identification of a single definitive manifestation. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1879:** *Sunflower*. A large floral wallpaper preserved through both original designs and manufactured papers. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1880:** *Pink and Poppy*. A wallpaper for which multiple original design sheets survive; design stages should not be mistaken for separate finished patterns. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1881:** *Grafton*. A scrolling floral wallpaper named for Grafton Street, where Morris & Co. had premises. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1881:** *Brer Rabbit*. A wallpaper adapted from the animals in the medieval “Rabbit and Vine” silk Morris admired; also produced as textile. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1883:** *Rose*. A wallpaper combining roses with scrolling foliage. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1883:** *Bird and Anemone*. A repeat design used for wallpaper and furnishing fabric. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1884:** *Garden Tulip*. A floral wallpaper repeat. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1887:** *Willow Bough*. A more naturalistic reworking of the earlier *Willow*, later transferred to textile. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1887:** *Balmoral*. A special wallpaper incorporating Queen Victoria’s “VRI” monogram; a site- and patron-specific commission rather than an ordinary retail pattern. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-wallpaper-design)
- **1892:** *Blackthorn*. A wallpaper and textile pattern attributed in Morris & Co. records to John Henry Dearle, although it has repeatedly been marketed under Morris’s name; it should not be silently absorbed into Morris’s oeuvre. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object-type/wallpaper/)
- **1862–1896:** *Morris wallpaper designs*. The full catalogue is a body of interrelated repeats, colourways, adaptations, borders, and trial designs rather than hundreds of cleanly separable authored pictures; attribution is especially unstable where Morris & Co. records assign work to assistants such as Dearle or May Morris. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)

### Printed and woven textiles

- **1868:** *Two reconstructed 1830s chintzes*. Experimental block-printed fabrics made by the Firm from historical models; they were technical trials, not original Morris patterns. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1868–1870:** *Jasmine Trellis*. Morris’s first original furnishing-textile design, simplified to accommodate the manufacturer’s chemical-dye palette. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1873:** *Tulip and Willow*. Morris’s second purpose-designed printed textile and an early experiment whose Prussian-blue result dissatisfied him. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1875:** *Marigold*. Probably the first Morris textile printed by Thomas Wardle; also manifested as wallpaper. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1876:** *Honeysuckle*. A block-printed furnishing textile of intertwined flowering stems. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1876:** *Tulip and Rose*. Morris’s first securely dateable design for a woven furnishing textile. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1876:** *Rose and Thistle*. A textile repeat using ordinary garden plants as monumental ornament. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1876:** *Bluebell*. A floral furnishing-textile design. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1876:** *Columbine*. A furnishing-textile pattern based on the garden flower. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1878:** *Bird*. A jacquard-woven wool furnishing textile used in Morris’s own interiors. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1878:** *Peacock and Dragon*. A large figured woven-wool textile drawing on medieval and Islamic precedents. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1879:** *Dove and Rose*. A silk-and-wool double-cloth furnishing textile. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1879:** *Flowerpot*. A woven furnishing textile organized around paired vases and branching plants. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1880–1881:** *Oak*. A silk furnishing textile designed for the Throne Room at St James’s Palace. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1881:** *Brother Rabbit*, also called *Brer Rabbit*. A printed textile adapted from a medieval silk and closely related to the wallpaper pattern. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1881:** *Bird and Vine*. A woven textile repeat of confronted birds among foliage. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1883:** *Strawberry Thief*. An indigo-discharge printed cotton inspired by birds taking fruit at Kelmscott Manor; technically and commercially one of Morris & Co.’s defining textiles. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1883:** *Kennet*. An indigo-discharge printed furnishing textile named after a Thames tributary. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1883:** *Evenlode*. A printed cotton named after an Oxfordshire river. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1883:** *Windrush*. A printed textile named after another Cotswold river. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1883:** *Medway*. A printed floral textile and one of several river-named patterns. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1884:** *Wandle*. Morris’s largest repeating printed-textile design, named for the river beside Merton Abbey. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1884:** *Cray*. Morris’s last major identifiable design for a printed textile. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1884:** *Granada*. A figured woven textile emulating the structure and richness of late-medieval Flemish velvet. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1888:** *Last identifiable woven-textile designs*. A small terminal group in the Morris & Co. records; after this point John Henry Dearle generally designed the Firm’s new woven patterns, making later blanket attribution to Morris untenable. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1860s–1890s:** *Embroidery designs*. A corpus of hangings, curtains, bedcovers, cushion panels, altar cloths, and kits, frequently designed by William but stitched, adapted, or independently designed by Jane Morris, May Morris, Bessie Burden, and workshop embroiderers; “Morris” may indicate firm, pattern, or designer and is not a safe synonym for William’s authorship. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)

### Carpets and tapestries

- **1876:** *Hammersmith carpet*. An early hand-knotted carpet design associated with Morris’s revival of traditional carpet making. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1878–1879:** *Tulip and Lily carpet*. A hand-knotted carpet design combining strongly outlined flowers with a flat field. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1879:** *Cabbage and Vine*. Morris’s first experimental tapestry weaving, made substantially by his own hand; both technical exercise and autonomous textile. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **c. 1879:** *Acanthus and Vine tapestry*. An early foliage tapestry design emerging from Morris’s self-directed weaving experiments. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1881–1885:** *The Woodpecker*. A tapestry designed by Morris, including its verse and ornamental setting; unlike many Firm tapestries, its principal design is his rather than Burne-Jones’s. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/)
- **1883:** *The Forest*. A tapestry designed collaboratively: Morris supplied foliage and overall ornamental structure, Philip Webb the animals, and John Henry Dearle worked on floral detail. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1887:** *The Bullerswood Carpet*. A large hand-knotted carpet designed for John Sanderson’s house; workshop execution makes it a collective manufactured object. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1887:** *The Clouds Carpet*. A monumental carpet commissioned for Percy Wyndham’s house *Clouds*; attribution includes Morris’s design authority and workshop manufacture. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **c. 1888:** *Ispahan carpet*. Traditionally associated with Morris & Co. but attributed in modern museum cataloguing to John Henry Dearle; it resists inclusion as a William Morris work. [Los Angeles County Museum of Art](https://collections.lacma.org/)
- **1890:** *The Orchard*. A figurative tapestry designed by Morris with John Henry Dearle; its collaborative authorship should remain visible. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/)
- **1890–1894:** *The Holy Grail tapestries*. A seven-piece Gesamtkunstwerk for Stanmore Hall: Burne-Jones designed the figures, Morris the heraldry and much ornamental structure, Dearle the flowers, and Morris & Co. wove the panels. No single panel is properly “by Morris” alone. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-quest-for-the-holy-grail-tapestries)
- **1880s–1890s:** *Morris & Co. tapestry and carpet corpus*. A workshop body of designs and woven objects in which conception, cartoons, enlargement, colouring, weaving, and later reweaving were distributed among Morris, Webb, Burne-Jones, Dearle, and unnamed artisans; the firm’s output resists modern single-author cataloguing. [William Morris Society, textiles catalogue review](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SU81.4.3.Payne_.pdf)

### Stained glass, tiles, and church decoration

- **1862:** *Tristram and Isoude stained-glass panels*. A thirteen-panel suite for Harden Grange: Morris designed four figures and the unifying architecture, inscriptions, leading, and colour, while Burne-Jones, Ford Madox Brown, Val Prinsep, and others designed remaining figures. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/)
- **1860s–1890s:** *Stained-glass foliage, diaper, quarry, canopy, and border designs*. A reusable pattern repertory by Morris that appeared across many windows; these are designs with multiple manifestations rather than one-off site works. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object/)
- **1860s–1890s:** *Morris & Co. ecclesiastical windows*. More than 150 window schemes were made by the Firm from cartoons by Morris, Burne-Jones, Ford Madox Brown, Webb, and later Dearle; corporate manufacture and recycled cartoons mean the installed windows cannot responsibly be catalogued as William’s sole works. [Stained Glass in Wales](https://catalogue.stainedglass.wales/person/21)
- **1873:** *Quarry designs for St Mary’s, Edge Hill*. Geometric and vegetal stained-glass filling patterns by Morris for the church’s second window. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object/)
- **1876:** *Quarry designs for the Duke of Richmond’s chapel, Fochabers*. Stained-glass ornamental patterns made for a specific architectural commission. [William Morris Gallery](https://wmgallery.org.uk/object/)
- **c. 1862–1876:** *Tile designs*. A corpus of floral, animal, heraldic, biblical, and literary tiles made for repetition and combination; many figure subjects were designed by other members of the Firm, while Morris often supplied patterns and overall schemes. [William Morris Society catalogue](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **1860s–1880s:** *Painted furniture, ceilings, walls, metalwork, and domestic glass by Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co.* A corporate decorative corpus documented through designs, account books, and surviving objects; because “made by Morris & Co.” does not establish personal design by William Morris, it resists honest conversion into a list of his individual works. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-his-circle)

### The Kelmscott Press and the book as Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1891–1898:** *The Kelmscott Press*. A publishing and manufacturing project that produced fifty-three books in sixty-six volumes, designed through the conjunction of type, paper, ink, spacing, initials, borders, illustration, binding, and hand presswork; eleven titles appeared after Morris’s death, so the press corpus exceeds what he personally completed. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1889–1890:** *Golden type*. A roman typeface designed by Morris after fifteenth-century Venetian models. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-the-kelmscott-press)
- **1891–1892:** *Troy type*. A heavy gothic typeface designed for the Kelmscott Press. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-the-kelmscott-press)
- **1892:** *Chaucer type*. A smaller adaptation of Troy made for the projected *Works of Geoffrey Chaucer*. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-the-kelmscott-press)
- **1891–1896:** *Kelmscott initials, borders, title pages, printer’s marks, and ornaments*. A corpus of roughly 650 woodcut decorations designed principally by Morris and cut by professional engravers; the individual blocks are components, while their page-specific combinations are manifestations. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-the-kelmscott-press)
- **1891:** *The Story of the Glittering Plain*. The first Kelmscott Press book; a new material manifestation of Morris’s romance, set in Golden type. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1891:** *Poems by the Way*. A Kelmscott publication in which Morris’s poems and his book design coincide. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1892:** *The Defence of Guenevere, and Other Poems*. A materially redesigned Kelmscott manifestation of the 1858 collection. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1892:** *The Golden Legend*. A three-volume Kelmscott edition of William Caxton’s translation; Morris made the book-object but did not author or translate its text. [Morgan Library & Museum](https://www.themorgan.org/collection/printed-books-and-bindings)
- **1892:** *The Recuyell of the Historyes of Troye*. A Kelmscott revival of Caxton’s English text; its authorship, translation, typography, and manufacture belong to different historical makers. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1892:** *The History of Reynard the Foxe*. A Kelmscott edition of Caxton’s translation and therefore Morris’s book design, not Morris’s literary composition. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1892:** *The Nature of Gothic*. A Kelmscott edition of a chapter by John Ruskin, framed by Morris’s preface; text, manifesto, and book-object have distinct authorship. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1893:** *The History of Godefrey of Boloyne*. A Kelmscott edition of Caxton’s translation. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1893:** *The Order of Chivalry*. A Kelmscott volume combining Morris’s translations of two medieval instructional texts. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1893:** *The History of Reynard the Foxe* and related Caxton revivals. These books constitute a sustained project to reactivate early English printing rather than merely reproduce old texts. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1893–1894:** *The Poems of William Shakespeare*. A compact Kelmscott book whose editorial text is Shakespeare’s and whose material authorship is Morris’s press. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1894:** *Atalanta in Calydon*. A Kelmscott edition of Algernon Charles Swinburne’s poem; Morris designed its typographic manifestation but did not write the text. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1894:** *The Tale of the Emperor Coustans and of Over Sea*. A Kelmscott book joining two Morris translations. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1894:** *The Wood Beyond the World*. A Kelmscott book in which Morris was author, typographer, ornament designer, publisher, and production director. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *Child Christopher and Goldilind the Fair*. A two-volume Kelmscott manifestation of Morris’s romance. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *The Book of Wisdom and Lies*. A Kelmscott edition of Horace Shipp’s translation of Sandor Petőfi; Morris made its visual-material form, not its verbal content. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *Poems Chosen Out of the Works of Robert Herrick*. A Kelmscott selection and designed book-object. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *The Poetical Works of Percy Bysshe Shelley*. A three-volume Kelmscott edition. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *The Life and Death of Jason*. A Kelmscott manifestation of Morris’s 1867 poem. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1895:** *The Tale of Beowulf*. A Kelmscott manifestation of the Morris–Wyatt translation. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Works of Geoffrey Chaucer*. The Kelmscott Chaucer, designed by Morris with eighty-seven illustrations by Edward Burne-Jones and wood engraving principally by W. H. Hooper; an exemplary Gesamtkunstwerk whose celebrated unity is collaborative. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-kelmscott-chaucer)
- **1896:** *The Earthly Paradise*. An eight-volume Kelmscott manifestation of Morris’s poem. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Floure and the Leafe and the Boke of Cupide*. A Kelmscott edition of poems then attributed to Chaucer but no longer accepted as his; the historical misattribution belongs to the book’s record. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Shepheardes Calender*. A Kelmscott edition of Edmund Spenser with illustrations by Arthur Gaskin; Morris’s role was designer-publisher. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896:** *The Well at the World’s End*. A two-volume Kelmscott manifestation of Morris’s romance, completed shortly before his death. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1896–1898:** *Posthumous Kelmscott books*. Eleven press books were completed after Morris’s death from projects, texts, and designs already in production; they are manifestations of his press project but not objects whose final making he supervised. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
- **1898:** *A Note by William Morris on His Aims in Founding the Kelmscott Press*. Morris’s account of the project, printed posthumously with a chronological list of the press’s books; simultaneously retrospect, manifesto, and primary catalogue. [University of Maryland, William Morris Collection](https://www.lib.umd.edu/collections/special/art/morris)

### Organizational and collective projects

- **1861–1875:** *Morris, Marshall, Faulkner & Co.* A collaborative firm founded to reunite design and skilled making across media; the organization itself was Morris’s central practical project, but its products are not automatically his personal works. [Victoria and Albert Museum](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/william-morris-and-his-circle)
- **1875–1896:** *Morris & Co.* The reorganized decorating and manufacturing firm owned and directed by Morris; it is an obra and production protocol encompassing many makers, not a pseudonym for a solitary artist. [V&A, “William Morris textiles”](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/willam-morris-textiles)
- **1877–1896:** *Society for the Protection of Ancient Buildings*. Morris co-founded and continually worked through the organization; its preservation campaigns are collective acts whose “made thing” is partly the survival of buildings not restored. [Society for the Protection of Ancient Buildings](https://www.spab.org.uk/about-us/history)
- **1883–1884:** *Social Democratic Federation work*. Speeches, programs, articles, and organizing produced within a collective political body; corporate texts cannot always be apportioned securely among Morris and his comrades. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1884–1890:** *Socialist League*. Morris helped found, financed, spoke for, and drafted texts for the League; the organization and its practices are a political project rather than a single authored work. [William Morris Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/archive/morris/works/)
- **1885–1890:** *Commonweal*. A socialist newspaper edited, financed, typeset in part, and extensively written for by Morris; it is both collective periodical and sustained political manifestation. [William Morris Archive](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/exhibits/show/titles)
