## Works by Manuela Sáenz de Vergara y Aizpuru

### Political print

- **1830:** *La Torre de Babel*. A polemical printed sheet condemned by Colombian authorities as seditious and defamatory. The surviving official record calls Sáenz responsible for its printing and distribution, but does not securely establish that she composed the text; it is therefore best classified as a political manifestation she sponsored rather than an unquestionably authored obra. ([Chambers, pp. 105–06](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8801231.pdf))

### Correspondence

- **1820–1853:** *Correspondence*. This is Sáenz’s principal surviving textual obra: individual letters concerning love, war, intelligence, money, exile, friendship, and republican politics, not a book planned by her. Vicente Lecuna transcribed forty-one letters and documents dated 1820–1832, while Jorge Villalba’s standard *Manuela Sáenz: epistolario* gathers later material, especially the correspondence with Juan José Flores; losses, editorial selection, scattered manuscripts, and doubtful letters prevent the corpus from being called complete. ([Astudillo Figueroa, pp. 128–29](https://repositorio.uasb.edu.ec/bitstream/10644/2819/1/TD014-DECLA-Astudillo-La%20emergencia.pdf); [Villalba edition record](https://biblioteca.casadelacultura.gob.ec/bib/66713))

- **1822–1830:** *Letters to Simón Bolívar*. An epistolary relationship and political-intelligence channel extending across their periods of separation; it survives only fragmentarily and has repeatedly been rearranged into later “love-letter” books. Some letters circulated as Sáenz’s have insecure provenance, so neither the familiar published sequence nor every item attributed to her should be treated as an uncontested authorial corpus. ([López](https://repositorio.ufsc.br/bitstream/id/9bcb0e67-c778-45ce-bce6-37c0283e05df/14.%20OKLANDA%2020181%20DOSSIER%207%20Silvia%20L%20Lopez%20Del%20discurso%20amoroso%20a%20correspondencia%20de%20Simon%20Bolivar%20y%20Manuela%20Saenz.pdf); [Vilalta](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/307665121_Historia_de_las_mujeres_y_memoria_historica_Manuela_Saenz_interpela_a_Simon_Bolivar_1822-1830))

- **October 1823, date assigned by Vicente Lecuna:** *Letter to James Thorne*. A surviving draft of Sáenz’s rejection of marital reconciliation, and thus a personal letter that also acts as a declaration of autonomy. Daniel O’Leary’s account raises the possibility that Bolívar retouched the copy associated with him, so its exact textual authorship is not wholly secure. ([Antología del pensamiento feminista nuestroamericano, vol. I, p. 140](https://fundacionmisangre.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Antolog%C3%ADa-del-pensamiento-feminista-nuestroamericano.-Tomo-I.pdf))

- **1829–1845:** *Letters to Juan José Flores*. Fifty-four surviving letters form an extended political correspondence rather than a deliberately unified book: Sáenz reported intelligence from exile, advised and warned Flores, and wrote about debts, inheritance, health, and everyday life. They constitute a sustained political project conducted through private letters. ([Cordero de Espinosa, pp. 65–66](https://www.udlaediciones.com.ec/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/BICENTENARIO.pdf); [Villalba edition record](https://biblioteca.casadelacultura.gob.ec/bib/66713))

- **10 August 1850:** *Descripción del 25 de septiembre*. A letter to Daniel Florencio O’Leary recounting the attempted assassination of Bolívar on 25 September 1828; although formally correspondence, it is also a commissioned eyewitness narrative made for O’Leary’s historical archive. ([Chambers, pp. 101–02](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/8801231.pdf); [Hennes, reference list](https://revistas.uasb.edu.ec/index.php/kipus/en/article/view/928))

### Disputed diaries

- **Dates represented as spanning the Bolívar years; first published in modern editions:** *Diario de Quito*. A purported personal diary attributed to Sáenz and published from papers promoted as “lost”; its manuscript history and authenticity have not been established to the standard of her documented correspondence. It resists classification as her obra and should be recorded as an attributed text whose modern editorial manifestation helped construct the later cultural figure of “Manuelita.” ([Hennes](https://revistas.uasb.edu.ec/index.php/kipus/en/article/view/928))

- **Dates represented as belonging to the Paita exile; published in 1995:** *Diario de Paita*. Another purported diary issued by Carlos Álvarez Saá in *Manuela: sus diarios perdidos y otros papeles*. Scholars discuss it as a supposed or attributed original rather than an authenticated manuscript, and its demonstrable cultural afterlife does not resolve whether Sáenz made the text. ([Hennes](https://revistas.uasb.edu.ec/index.php/kipus/en/article/view/928); [Triviño](https://cvc.cervantes.es/literatura/mujer_independencias/trivino02.htm))

### Work made for livelihood in Paita

- **circa 1835–1856:** *Letters and translations for visiting whalers*. Sáenz wrote or translated private correspondence for North American sailors; this was practical linguistic labor rather than a literary project, and no defined corpus or individual titles have been identified. Its resistance to being called a “work” is essential: these were service texts made for other people’s use. ([University of Texas Press, Murray biography](https://utpress.utexas.edu/9780292778719/))

- **circa 1835–1856:** *Commissioned embroidery*. Sáenz produced embroidered objects for income during exile. No catalogue raisonné, surviving-object list, individual title, or secure date is known, so this is an attested body of material production rather than a recoverable series of named obras. ([University of Texas Press, Murray biography](https://utpress.utexas.edu/9780292778719/))

- **circa 1835–1856:** *Confectionery made in Paita*. Ricardo Palma remembered Sáenz serving sweets she had made herself, while later accounts describe her producing them for sale. These perishable, repeat-made objects survive only in testimony and resist treatment as individually nameable works. ([Martos Carrera, pp. 231–32](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331316238_Paita_en_una_tradicion_de_Ricardo_Palma))
