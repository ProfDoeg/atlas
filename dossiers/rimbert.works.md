## Works by Rimbert of Hamburg-Bremen

### Hagiographic obra

- **865–876:** *Vita Anskarii* (*Life of Ansgar*). A Latin saint’s life that is also formally a collective letter to the monks of Corbie; Rimbert was its principal author, but the *Vita Rimberti* credits an unnamed fellow disciple of Ansgar as co-author, and Ansgar’s own account of his visions may have supplied material. The authentic longer recension must be distinguished from a shortened, ideologically altered version produced around 1100. ([Medieval Nordic Literature in Latin](https://medieval.wiki.uib.no/Rimbertus); [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz56229.html); [Geschichtsquellen des deutschen Mittelalters](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4204?s=en))

### Letters and lost correspondence

- **Before 865:** *Untitled inquiry concerning the cynocephali*. A lost letter or epistolary report in which Rimbert supplied information about the purported dog-headed people and asked whether they were human and therefore proper objects of Christian mission; its existence and substance survive only through Ratramnus of Corbie’s reply, *De cynocephalis*. It resists treatment as an extant work because none of Rimbert’s wording survives. ([Geschichtsquellen des deutschen Mittelalters](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4093); [Ratramnus, *Epistola de Cynocephalis*](https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Epistola_de_Cynocephalis_%28Ratramnus_Corbeiensis%29))

- **868–870:** *Untitled inquiry concerning marriages between relatives*. A lost request for a canonical opinion, apparently sent by Rimbert and possibly jointly with Abbot Adalgar after the Synod of Worms; Ratramnus’s fragmentary reply, *De propinquorum coniugiis*, is addressed to both men. Because the initiating document is lost, its precise authorship, wording, date, and whether it constituted one letter or a less formal consultation remain uncertain. ([Geschichtsquellen des deutschen Mittelalters](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/4097); [James T. Palmer, “Rimbert’s *Vita Anskarii* and Scandinavian Mission in the Ninth Century”](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/1554/Palmer2004-JnlEcclHist55-Rimbert.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1))

- **865–888:** *Untitled letter on virginity to a nun at Neuenheerse*. A pastoral letter, addressed according to later tradition to a nun or abbess associated with Neuenheerse, preserved only by insertion into the posthumous *Vita Rimberti*. Its embedded transmission makes it a surviving textual fragment rather than an independently preserved letter, and the transmitted *Vita* itself contains apparent later interpolations. ([Geschichtsquellen des deutschen Mittelalters](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/5791); [Palmer](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/handle/10023/1554/Palmer2004-JnlEcclHist55-Rimbert.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1))

- **884:** *Untitled report to Archbishop Liutbert concerning the Frisian victory over the Northmen*. A lost letter reporting the battle in the district called Norditi; the *Annales Fuldenses* attest that Rimbert wrote it, but the text was omitted from the surviving annal manuscript. Later accounts may preserve knowledge related to the event, but they do not securely preserve Rimbert’s letter itself. ([Wilhelm Wattenbach, *Deutschlands Geschichtsquellen im Mittelalter*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/54949/54949-h/54949-h.htm); [Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Rimbert))

- **Ninth century, dates unknown:** *Other correspondence*. Medieval and older scholarly testimony describes Rimbert as conducting a substantial correspondence, but no inventory, titles, recipients, or textual remains allow its constituent letters to be reconstructed. This therefore resists classification as a determinate body of works: it is evidence of textual activity, not a recoverable corpus. ([Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Rimbert))

### Institutional project

- **Traditionally 882:** *Collegiate foundation at Bücken*. An ecclesiastical institution—traditionally described as a collegiate monastery or chapter and missionary center—founded under Rimbert’s episcopal authority, rather than a text or a securely attributable surviving building. The conventional date is 882, but research proposing an earlier local ecclesiastical nucleus makes it safer to distinguish Rimbert’s institutional foundation from the origin of the church site and from the much later fabric of the present collegiate church. ([Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Rimbert); [Evangelical parish of Bücken](https://www.kirchenkreis-syke-hoya.de/kirchengemeinden/buecken); [Kreiszeitung report on revised dating](https://www.kreiszeitung.de/lokales/nienburg/grafschaft-hoya-ort120424/buecken-wirklich-7096622.html))
