## Works by Rosa Luxemburg

### Monographs, pamphlets, and sustained arguments

- **1893:** *Bericht an den III. Internationalen Sozialistischen Arbeiterkongreß in Zürich über den Stand und Verlauf der sozialdemokratischen Bewegung in Russisch-Polen 1889–1893*. A political report and credentialing document: it describes a movement but also acts by claiming international recognition for Luxemburg’s tendency. It is the opening work in the critical *Gesammelte Werke*. [[GW 1.1](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-1)]

- **1896:** *Die polnische Frage auf dem Internationalen Kongreß in London*. A polemical pamphlet against making restoration of an independent Poland a socialist-programmatic demand; its interventionist purpose makes it closer to a counter-manifesto than to detached historical writing. [[GW 1.1](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-1)]

- **1897–1898:** *Die industrielle Entwickelung Polens*. An economic-historical dissertation, accepted at Zürich and published in 1898, arguing that Russian Poland’s industrial integration with the empire made national economic separation untenable; this was Luxemburg’s first monograph. [[GW 1.1](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-1)]

- **1898–1900:** *Sozialreform oder Revolution?* A two-part polemic first made serially against Eduard Bernstein’s revisionism, then reworked as a book; because its successive states answer a live controversy, it is both theoretical obra and political intervention rather than one immutably fixed text. [[GW 1.1](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-1)]

- **1900–1901:** *Die sozialistische Krise in Frankreich*. A sustained series, subsequently treated as a pamphlet-length work, opposing socialist participation in Pierre Waldeck-Rousseau’s bourgeois government. Its original object was an unfolding controversy, so the “work” is a sequence of interventions before it is a book. [[GW 1.2](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-2)]

- **1904:** *Organisationsfragen der russischen Sozialdemokratie*. A two-part review-essay responding to Lenin’s *One Step Forward, Two Steps Back* and disputing centralized conceptions of party organization; later editions have often retitled it *Leninism or Marxism?*, a title Luxemburg did not give it. [[GW 1.2](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-2)]

- **1905–1906:** *In revolutionärer Stunde: Was weiter?* A three-part Polish revolutionary intervention asking what organization and action should follow the upheaval of 1905; it is strategic writing made inside the event it seeks to advance. [[GW 2](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-2)]

- **1906:** *Was wollen wir? Kommentar zum Programm der Sozialdemokratie des Königreichs Polen und Litauens*. A programmatic exposition of the SDKPiL’s aims: simultaneously commentary, manifesto, and practical instrument of party formation. [[GW 2](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-2)]

- **1906:** *Massenstreik, Partei und Gewerkschaften*. A pamphlet drawing strategic consequences from the 1905 Russian Revolution for the German workers’ movement. It describes the mass strike while deliberately trying to alter how parties and unions act, so it belongs partly to the manifesto form. [[GW 2](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-2)]

- **1908–1909:** *Nationalitätenfrage und Autonomie*. A five-part Polish-language theoretical series published in *Przegląd Socjaldemokratyczny*, not originally a freestanding book; treating it as one obra preserves its serial construction without mistaking later collected editions for the original object. [[Complete Works project](https://www.versobooks.com/collections/the-complete-works-of-rosa-luxemburg)]

- **1907–1914, revised through 1918; published 1925:** *Einführung in die Nationalökonomie*. An unfinished textbook-project developed from Luxemburg’s teaching at the SPD party school and continued during imprisonment. Paul Levi’s posthumous edition made a book from surviving portions, but the obra properly remains an incomplete pedagogical project. [[GW 5](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-5)]

- **1913:** *Die Akkumulation des Kapitals: Ein Beitrag zur ökonomischen Erklärung des Imperialismus*. A completed monograph extending and criticizing Marx’s reproduction schemas in order to explain imperial expansion; it generated an international controversy that Luxemburg answered in a separate work. [[GW 5](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-5)]

- **1915; published 1921:** *Die Akkumulation des Kapitals oder Was die Epigonen aus der Marxschen Theorie gemacht haben: Eine Antikritik*. A prison-written reply to critics of *The Accumulation of Capital*. It was complete as a rebuttal but appeared only after Luxemburg’s death, so 1921 is publication history, not its date of composition. [[GW 5](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-5)]

- **1915; published 1916:** *Die Krise der Sozialdemokratie*. An anti-war pamphlet issued illegally under the pseudonym “Junius” and therefore commonly called the *Junius-Broschüre*. It is analysis functioning as manifesto: an attempt to remake socialist opposition after the SPD’s approval of war credits. [[GW 4](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-4)]

- **1918; published 1922:** *Zur russischen Revolution*. An unfinished prison manuscript, left untitled by Luxemburg and supplied with its familiar title by posthumous editors. Paul Levi’s publication converted abandoned working material into an apparent book; its incompleteness and Luxemburg’s failure to publish it are essential parts of what the object is. [[GW editorial history](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/editionsgeschichte)]

### Manifestos, programmes, protocols, and last interventions

- **1915:** *Entwurf zu den Junius-Thesen*. A draft set of internationalist theses intended to orient socialist opposition to the First World War; as a proposal for collective adoption it is a protocol-manifesto, not merely an essay. [[GW 4](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-4)]

- **1916–1918:** *Spartakusbriefe* and associated illegal anti-war leaflets. A clandestine, collectively produced serial manifestation of the Gruppe Internationale/Spartacus current, to which Luxemburg contributed from prison under censorship; individual pieces were often anonymous or pseudonymous, and the collective vehicle should not be assigned wholly to her. [[GW 4](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-4)]

- **14 December 1918:** *Was will der Spartakusbund?* A programme published in *Die Rote Fahne* that sets out immediate political, social, and economic measures and the league’s conception of revolutionary power. With minor changes it became the founding programme of the KPD, making it both manifesto and operational protocol. [[Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung](https://rosaluxemburg.org/en/decisions/)]

- **31 December 1918:** *Unser Programm und die politische Situation*. Luxemburg’s programmatic speech to the founding congress of the KPD; it is an oral political act preserved through a printed report rather than a book authored for publication. [[GW 4](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-4)]

- **14 January 1919:** *Die Ordnung herrscht in Berlin*. Luxemburg’s final published newspaper article, written during the suppression of the January struggle and printed one day before her murder. Its recurring closing declaration makes it an act of political defiance as much as reportage. [[GW 4](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-4)]

### Journalism, speeches, teaching, and correspondence

- **1893–1919:** *Articles, reports, reviews, resolutions, appeals, corrections, speeches, and congress interventions*. This is not a single work and resists being made into one: it is a multilingual body of hundreds of situated objects in Polish, German, Russian, and French, often first printed anonymously or reconstructed from stenographic reports. The most comprehensive German critical corpus currently occupies seven volumes in nine books; Volumes 1–4 arrange the principal writings chronologically, while Volumes 6 and 7 add later-recovered material, so their tables of contents—not later library editions—are the item-level inventory. [[Digital *Gesammelte Werke*](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher)]

- **1905:** *Vorwärts articles on the Russian Revolution*. A body of rapid newspaper reportage and political interpretation produced during Luxemburg’s brief period directing the paper; authorship of newspaper matter can be editorial or unsigned, and the recovered texts are therefore a journalistic corpus rather than a clean series of individually signed works. [[GW 1.2](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-1-2)]

- **1907–1914:** *SPD Parteischule lectures and economic-history manuscripts*. A pedagogical project comprising spoken courses, notes, preparatory manuscripts, and studies of slavery, pre-capitalist societies, wage labour, and economic crises. Some material fed into *Einführung in die Nationalökonomie*, but the surviving pieces do not form a finished book and should not be forced into one. [[Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung, “Rosa in Translation”](https://www.rosalux.de/en/news/id/39819)]

- **1912:** *Frauenwahlrecht und Klassenkampf*. A speech made for the second Social Democratic Women’s Day and subsequently printed; it is an oratorical intervention linking women’s suffrage to class struggle, not a “book” despite later pamphlet editions. [[GW 3](https://rosaluxemburgwerke.de/buecher/band-3)]

- **From adolescence to January 1919:** *Letters*. Thousands of private, political, editorial, and prison communications survive unevenly and constitute a distributed epistolary body rather than a work Luxemburg designed for publication. *Briefe aus dem Gefängnis* and other named letter-books are posthumous editorial selections, whereas the scholarly base is the six-volume *Gesammelte Briefe*. [[Karl Dietz Verlag](https://dietzberlin.de/reihe/luxemburg-gesammelte-briefe/)]

### Translation and literary criticism

- **1918; published 1919:** *Wladimir Korolenko, Die Geschichte meines Zeitgenossen*. Luxemburg’s German translation from Russian, made in Breslau prison, with her own substantial introduction on Korolenko and Russian literature. The translated language is Luxemburg’s making, but the underlying autobiography remains Korolenko’s obra; the introduction is independently hers. [[Translator bibliography](https://uelex.de/uebersetzer/luxemburg-rosa/)]

### Botanical and material work

- **May 1913–October 1918:** *Herbarium*. Eighteen school notebooks—two containing botanical notes and the others preserving 377 dried specimens—assembled during travel, residence, and imprisonment. It is a material research-and-memory object whose authorship consists in collecting, identifying, arranging, annotating, and preserving living matter; scholarship describes the notebooks as attributed to Luxemburg, while archival and biographical evidence strongly supports that attribution. [[Zych et al., “A plant love story”](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ppp3.10396)]

### Collective political creations

- **1893–1918:** *Social Democracy of the Kingdom of Poland, later of Poland and Lithuania (SDKP/SDKPiL)*. A party co-founded by Luxemburg with Leo Jogiches, Julian Marchlewski, and others: a collective institutional project, not her sole obra, but one she helped formulate, publicize, and sustain. Its changing name and collective agency resist attribution to any single maker. [[Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung biography](https://www.rosalux.de/en/foundation/historical-centre-for-democratic-socialism-1/rosa-luxemburg/the-life-of-rosa-luxemburg)]

- **1893–1896:** *Sprawa Robotnicza*. A clandestine Polish socialist periodical co-created and edited by Luxemburg and collaborators, with Luxemburg formally assuming editorship under a pseudonym in 1894. The made thing is both a run of issues and an organizational instrument; unsigned matter prevents every component from being securely individualized. [[Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung](https://www.rosalux.de/news/id/7005/rosa-luxemburgs-erste-sozialdemokratie)]

- **1914–1918:** *Gruppe Internationale / Spartakusgruppe / Spartakusbund*. A changing anti-war political formation built collectively by Luxemburg, Karl Liebknecht, Franz Mehring, Clara Zetkin, Leo Jogiches, and others. It is best classified as an organizational project and political manifestation, not a personal work by Luxemburg.

- **November 1918–January 1919:** *Die Rote Fahne*. A revolutionary newspaper jointly edited by Luxemburg and Liebknecht, combining serial publication, political organization, and daily intervention. Luxemburg made many of its leading articles and helped shape the whole vehicle, but neither the newspaper nor all its contents can be attributed to her alone. [[Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung biography](https://www.rosalux.de/en/foundation/historical-centre-for-democratic-socialism-1/rosa-luxemburg/the-life-of-rosa-luxemburg)]

- **30 December 1918–1 January 1919:** *Communist Party of Germany (KPD)*. A collective institutional creation in whose founding Luxemburg participated and whose initial programme derived from her Spartacus text. It resists classification as her “work”: congress delegates, allied organizations, and co-founders made the party, and they rejected some of her tactical recommendations. [[Rosa-Luxemburg-Stiftung biography](https://www.rosalux.de/en/foundation/historical-centre-for-democratic-socialism-1/rosa-luxemburg/the-life-of-rosa-luxemburg)]
