## Works by Salomon Oppenheim jr.

### Commercial obra

- **1789–1828:** *The commission, exchange, and trading house later named Sal. Oppenheim jr. & Cie.* An enduring commercial institution founded in Bonn when Oppenheim began trading on his own account; the date rests partly on family tradition, and his relative Samuel Wolff participated as a silent capital partner. Oppenheim moved the enterprise to Cologne in 1798, while the surviving name is first documented only in 1816; it therefore resists treatment as a single, wholly individual creation. [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz73651.html); [Portal Rheinische Geschichte](https://www.rheinische-geschichte.lvr.de/Persoenlichkeiten/familie-oppenheim/DE-2086/lido/57c956cd98b2c7.39961812)

### Collective institutional creations

- **1818:** *Rheinschifffahrts-Assecuranz-Gesellschaft*. A transport-insurance company in whose constitution Sal. Oppenheim jr. & Cie. participated by subscribing fifteen shares. Calling it Oppenheim’s “work” would overstate the evidence: it was a collective corporate creation involving numerous Cologne and Mainz investors, and the identifiable actor in the shareholder record is his firm rather than Oppenheim alone. [Peter Koch, “Von der Agrippina und Colonia zur AXA AG,” in Hans Pohl (ed.), *Geschichte des Versicherungswesens in Deutschland*](https://media.dav-medien.de/toc/9783515098786_i.pdf)

- **1825:** *Preußisch-Rheinische Dampfschiffahrtsgesellschaft*. A joint-stock steam-navigation enterprise, now historically continuous with Köln-Düsseldorfer Deutsche Rheinschiffahrt, which Oppenheim co-initiated and co-founded. It is an institutional and infrastructural project rather than a personally authored object, and contemporary scholarship consequently describes him variously as investor, co-initiator, or co-founder. [Portal Rheinische Geschichte](https://www.rheinische-geschichte.lvr.de/Persoenlichkeiten/familie-oppenheim/DE-2086/lido/57c956cd98b2c7.39961812); [Historische Gesellschaft der Deutschen Bank, *Bank und Geschichte*, no. 32](https://www.bankgeschichte.de/files/documents/publications/historical-review/Folge_2015_1.pdf?language_id=3)

### Financial operation

- **1818–1822:** *Transfer of the French war indemnity to Prussia*. A large-scale financial operation organized jointly by Oppenheim and the Berlin banking house Mendelssohn after the settlement of 1818. This is not comfortably a “work” at all: it was an executed commission and network of transactions rather than a persistent object, although the resulting service was sufficiently consequential that Oppenheim received the title of Royal Prussian Senior Court Agent in 1822. [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz73651.html); [Portal Rheinische Geschichte](https://www.rheinische-geschichte.lvr.de/Persoenlichkeiten/familie-oppenheim/DE-2086/lido/57c956cd98b2c7.39961812)
