## Works by Narendranath Datta

### Authored and author-shaped books

- **1896:** *Karma-Yoga*. An obra made from eight New York lectures: Vivekananda delivered and revised the material, while J. J. Goodwin stenographically recorded it. Its spoken and collaborative production makes it more than a conventionally written book. [Complete Works, vol. 1](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/volume_1/complete_works_v1_contents.htm)

- **1896:** *Raja-Yoga*. A book combining Vivekananda’s lectures and exposition with his translation and commentary on Patañjali’s *Yoga Aphorisms*; he actively prepared it for publication, so it is both authored treatise and pedagogical protocol. [Complete Works, vol. 1](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/volume_1/complete_works_v1_contents.htm)

- **1896:** *Vedanta Philosophy: An Address Before the Graduate Philosophical Society*. The published form of Vivekananda’s Harvard address, “The Vedanta Philosophy”; an oration turned into a pamphlet rather than a book composed independently on the page. [Complete Works master index](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *Bhakti-Yoga*. An exposition assembled from Vivekananda’s classes and writings on devotion. The title names a coherent obra, but its familiar book form is editorially stabilized from separately delivered material. [Complete Works, vol. 3, “Bhakti-Yoga”](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *Para-Bhakti, or Supreme Devotion*. A continuation of *Bhakti-Yoga*, concerned with devotion in its highest form; later editions often bind it to *Bhakti-Yoga*, so its independence as a separate work is unstable. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *Lectures from Colombo to Almora*. A deliberately shaped sequence of public speeches and replies produced during Vivekananda’s return through Ceylon and India. It is a campaign in oratory—part manifesto, part national itinerary—rather than a book originally written as continuous prose. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899–1900:** *The East and the West* (*Prāchya o Pāścātya*). A Bengali comparative-cultural essay first serialized in *Udbodhan* and only subsequently constituted as a book; the English text in the *Complete Works* is a translation. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *Modern India* / *Present-Day India* (*Bartamān Bhārat*). A Bengali historical and social essay first serialized in *Udbodhan* and published as a separate book only after Vivekananda’s death; its later English title varies by translation. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1902:** *Vedanta Philosophy: Lectures on Jnana Yoga*. A lifetime book publication drawn from lectures on knowledge, māyā, the self, and liberation. The larger work now called *Jnana-Yoga* was consolidated editorially from such lecture texts rather than completed by Vivekananda as a single manuscript. [Contemporary bibliography](https://www.vivekananda.net/BooksBySwami.html)

- **1895; first published 1909:** *Inspired Talks*. Notes made by S. E. Waldo from Vivekananda’s classes at Thousand Island Park. This resists attribution as a written work by Vivekananda: the teaching performance is his, but the surviving verbal object is a disciple’s record, selected and published posthumously. [Complete Works master index](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Public-address cycles and spoken manifestations

- **11–27 September 1893:** *Addresses at the Parliament of Religions*. A six-address oratorical cycle comprising “Response to Welcome,” “Why We Disagree,” “Paper on Hinduism,” “Religion Not the Crying Need of India,” “Buddhism, the Fulfilment of Hinduism,” and the final-session address. These are performative interventions—manifestos of religious universality as much as descriptive texts—and their printed forms derive from manuscripts, transcripts, and reports. [Complete Works, vol. 1](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/volume_1/complete_works_v1_contents.htm)

- **1894–1896:** *Lectures and Discourses in America*. The collected obra includes “Soul, God and Religion,” “The Hindu Religion,” “What Is Religion?,” “Vedic Religious Ideals,” “Reason and Religion,” “Vedanta as a Factor in Civilisation,” “The Spirit and Influence of Vedanta,” “Steps of Hindu Philosophic Thought,” “Vedanta and Privilege,” “Krishna,” three Gita lectures, “Mohammed,” “The Soul and God,” and practical instruction on breathing and meditation. Their boundaries and titles are frequently editorial, and some survive only through shorthand or auditors’ notes. [Complete Works, vol. 1](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1895–1896:** *Jnana-Yoga lectures*. Sixteen connected lectures, from “The Necessity of Religion” through “The Real and the Apparent Man,” later presented as a single book. They are a lecture cycle and posthumously stabilized obra, not one continuously authored manuscript. [Complete Works, vol. 2](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *Practical Vedanta*. Four London lectures bearing that collective title. The series functions as a manifesto for applying non-dual philosophy to ordinary life; later books enlarge it with other lectures not originally part of the four. [Complete Works, vol. 2](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *The Ideal of a Universal Religion*. A lecture advancing unity without the erasure of religious difference; its printed existence is the documentary residue of a spoken event. [Complete Works, vol. 2](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *The Way to the Realisation of a Universal Religion*. A companion lecture and practical religious manifesto, distinct from “The Ideal of a Universal Religion.” [Complete Works, vol. 2](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *Practical Vedanta and Other Lectures*. An editorial constellation containing the four “Practical Vedanta” lectures alongside “The Open Secret,” “The Way to Blessedness,” “Yajnavalkya and Maitreyi,” “Soul, Nature and God,” “Cosmology,” two lectures on Sāṃkhya, and “The Goal.” It is useful as an obra but should not be mistaken for a collection arranged under that title by Vivekananda himself. [Complete Works, vol. 2](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *Addresses on Bhakti-Yoga*. Six lectures—“The Preparation,” “The First Steps,” “The Teacher of Spirituality,” “The Need of Symbols,” “The Chief Symbols,” and “The Ishta.” They form a pedagogical sequence, but the collective book title is editorial. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *My Master*. A lecture-portrait of Ramakrishna and a public act of lineage-making; different deliveries and printed witnesses complicate the idea of one final text. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *Christ, the Messenger*. A lecture interpreting Jesus through Vivekananda’s Vedantic universalism; it survives as a reported discourse rather than an autograph treatise. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *The Great Teachers of the World*. A comparative-religious discourse and manifesto of plural spiritual authority. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *My Plan of Campaign*. A Madras lecture whose title accurately signals its form: a manifesto and program of education, organization, and service. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *The Work Before Us*. A public program for collective social and religious action; it acts rhetorically by recruiting its hearers and is therefore closer to manifesto than neutral essay. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *The Future of India*. A national lecture-manifesto delivered in Madras, preserved as part of the Colombo-to-Almora sequence. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *Vedanta in Its Application to Indian Life*. A lecture sequence joining philosophy to social practice; the printed object is a record of public teaching. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *The Common Bases of Hinduism*. A Lahore lecture attempting to articulate shared foundations across Hindu traditions; it is a classificatory intervention and public manifesto. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897:** *Sannyasa: Its Ideal and Practice*. A lecture defining renunciation as a lived discipline; it is partly description and partly protocol. [Complete Works, vol. 3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899–1900:** *Discourses on Jnana-Yoga*. Nine numbered discourses preserved in the *Complete Works*. Their absence of individual authorial titles is significant: they survive as an editorially numbered spoken sequence. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899–1900:** *Six Lessons on Raja-Yoga*. Six numbered classes functioning as a practical contemplative protocol; their text depends on note-taking and later editing. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1900:** *My Life and Mission*. An autobiographical public lecture rather than an autobiography written as a book. Its “text” is the surviving representation of a performed act of self-definition. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1900:** *Buddha’s Message to the World*. A comparative-religious lecture and interpretation of Buddhism, surviving through the apparatus of transcription. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1900:** *Is Vedanta the Future Religion?*. A lecture whose interrogative form resists reduction to a fixed creed; it is both diagnosis and prospective manifesto. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Prose essays, articles, appeals, and prefaces

- **1890s:** *Is the Soul Immortal?*. A prose essay arguing for immortality; the collected edition supplies the work but does not securely establish a date in its title apparatus. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Reincarnation*. A prose exposition distinct from newspaper reports and lectures published under the same title. The recurrence of the title across different documentary witnesses makes its textual identity easy to blur. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *On Professor Max Müller*. An essay responding to Müller’s treatment of Ramakrishna and acknowledging his scholarship. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *On Dr. Paul Deussen*. A short intellectual portrait occasioned by Vivekananda’s association with the German philosopher. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Sketch of the Life of Pavhari Baba*. A biographical-spiritual sketch; it is neither full biography nor merely doctrinal essay. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Aryans and Tamilians*. A historical and philological intervention whose claims belong to its nineteenth-century intellectual setting. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Stray Remarks on Theosophy*. A polemical prose piece rather than a systematic treatise. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1894:** *Reply to the Madras Address*. A written public reply that acts by consolidating a transnational circle of supporters; both letter and manifesto. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *What We Believe In*. A concise declaration of principles—manifesto is more exact than essay. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Our Duty to the Masses*. A programmatic social appeal whose imperative function makes it a manifesto. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *To My Brave Boys*. An exhortatory public message, situated between letter, appeal, and manifesto. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *A Plan of Work for India*. An organizational project expressed as text: its intention and proposed action are the work even where every proposed institution was not realized. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Fundamentals of Religion*. A compact doctrinal essay, preserved among miscellaneous prose rather than as an independently designed book. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1897–1899:** *The Belur Math: An Appeal*. A fundraising and institution-building appeal: a text intended to produce a place and community, not merely describe them. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *The Advaita Ashrama, Himalayas*. A statement defining the purpose of the Mayavati institution; simultaneously manifesto, institutional protocol, and part of the project it announces. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1900s:** *The Ramakrishna Home of Service, Varanasi: An Appeal*. An appeal incorporated into the collected works; its exact share of individual composition and institutional editing is not made explicit by the collected edition. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Historical Evolution of India*. A historical essay surviving in the miscellaneous writings, without a securely fixed date in the collected-edition heading. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *The Bengali Language*. A prose intervention on language and style, itself part of Vivekananda’s effort to reshape modern Bengali prose. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Matter for Serious Thought*. A Bengali social essay preserved in translation; the English title is therefore not necessarily an authorial title. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Hinduism and Shri Ramakrishna*. A Bengali theological-historical essay joining an account of Hinduism to an interpretation of Ramakrishna. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *The Story of the Boy Gopala*. A devotional narrative or retelling, not an original plot in the modern fictional sense. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Shiva’s Demon*. A short narrative or parable preserved among prose and poetry; the collected edition does not make its generic status entirely stable. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Struggle for Expansion*. A prose essay in the posthumously assembled miscellaneous writings. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *The Birth of Religion*. A prose essay or lecture-text whose mode of first composition is not securely identified by the collected-edition heading. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Four Paths of Yoga*. A synthetic essay presenting karma, bhakti, raja, and jnana as complementary disciplines; its date is not fixed in the standard contents. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Cyclic Rest and Change*. A short philosophical prose work preserved posthumously. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *A Preface to The Imitation of Christ*. A prefatory essay attached to another author’s work; its paratextual status is part of what it is, not a reason to omit it. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Travel writing and memoir

- **1899–1900:** *Memoirs of European Travel* (*Bilātyātrīr Patra*). Bengali travel letters or memoir-pieces first associated with *Udbodhan* and later translated into English. The work moves among letter, serialized travelogue, social criticism, and memoir. [Complete Works, vol. 7](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899–1900:** *Memoirs of European Travel II*. A separately presented continuation in the collected edition; the division into parts belongs partly to publication history rather than to an unequivocal authorial book-plan. [Complete Works, vol. 7](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Notes of Some Wanderings with the Swami Vivekananda*. This should not be counted as a work by Vivekananda: it is Sister Nivedita’s record of journeys and conversations. Its presence near his corpus documents a manifestation of his speech, not his authorship. [Complete Works master index](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Translations, adaptations, and scriptural protocols

- **1896:** *Patanjali’s Yoga Aphorisms*. Vivekananda’s English translation and commentary, issued within *Raja-Yoga*. It is a transformative translation and practical protocol, not an original sutra-text. [Complete Works, vol. 1](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/volume_1/complete_works_v1_contents.htm)

- **1890s:** *Narada-Bhakti-Sutras*. An English translation or adaptation associated with Vivekananda’s teaching on devotion. Its published form is posthumous, and the degree of editorial reconstruction should not be concealed. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *The Imitation of Christ* selections. Vivekananda translated or adapted selected portions into Bengali and supplied a preface; this is a devotional transmission project rather than an authored original. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *The Hymn of Creation*. A rendering of the *Rig Veda*’s Nāsadīya hymn. The collected works contain more than one placement/version, so “The Hymn of Creation” is not necessarily one uniquely fixed English text. [Complete Works, vols. 4 and 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Nirvanashatkam, or Six Stanzas on Nirvana*. A translation or recasting of the Sanskrit hymn traditionally attributed to Śaṅkara, not an original poem by Vivekananda. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *To a Friend*. An English translation of Vivekananda’s own Bengali poem, traditionally known as *Sakhār Prati*; original and translation should not be mistaken for two independently conceived poems. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *The Hymn of Samadhi*. A translated devotional or mystical poem; the English object is mediated from an Indic-language source. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *A Hymn to the Divine Mother*. A translation or adaptation of devotional verse, not securely classifiable as wholly original English poetry. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *A Hymn to Shiva*. A translated or adapted hymn whose source-language and English manifestations should be distinguished. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *A Hymn to the Divinity of Shri Ramakrishna*. A devotional translation or bilingual manifestation rather than an ordinary English lyric. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *“And Let Shyama Dance There”*. An English rendering of a Bengali devotional poem associated with Vivekananda; the English title is translational. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *A Song I Sing to Thee*. An English rendering of Vivekananda’s Bengali devotional verse; song, poem, prayer, and translation overlap here. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Poems, hymns, and songs

- **1880s:** *In Search of God*. An early poem traditionally associated with Vivekananda’s period of spiritual searching; exact dating varies in later presentations and should not be forced more narrowly. [Complete Works master index](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1895:** *The Song of the Sannyasin*. A long English poem and manifesto of renunciation, composed during the Thousand Island Park period. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1895:** *Hold On Yet a While, Brave Heart*. An occasional English poem of consolation and endurance. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1895:** *The Cup*. A symbolic devotional poem; the “cup” is an existential vocation rather than a narrative object. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1895:** *The Story of the Boy Gopala*. Also transmitted in proximity to Vivekananda’s verse, though the collected edition classifies it among mixed prose and poetry; its unstable genre should be retained. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1896:** *A Benediction*. An occasional poem made as a blessing, so its social act is as important as its literary form. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *Kali the Mother*. An English poem composed in Kashmir, at once visionary lyric, hymn, and invocation. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *To the Fourth of July*. An occasional English poem written for American Independence Day; Vivekananda’s death on the same date four years later gave it a retrospective significance not part of its original making. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *Peace*. A meditative English poem associated with the Kashmir period. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *To the Awakened India*. A poem addressed to the journal *Prabuddha Bharata* and, through its title’s double meaning, to an “awakened India”; poem and public manifesto coincide. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *Requiescat in Pace*. An elegiac occasional poem. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *Angels Unawares*. A three-part poem; the numbered divisions belong to one composite work, not three unrelated titles. [Complete Works, vol. 4](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1898:** *On the Sea’s Bosom*. A Bengali poem associated with sea travel and later presented in English translation; the translated title is not the original verbal object. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *The Living God*. A poem-manifesto locating divinity in living human beings and service rather than solely in ritual images. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *To an Early Violet*. A short occasional lyric addressed to a flower. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *To My Own Soul*. An introspective English lyric. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *Thou Blessed Dream*. A philosophical lyric treating manifested life as dream. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *Light*. A short English spiritual poem. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899:** *Who Knows How Mother Plays!*. A devotional poem on the inscrutable play of the Divine Mother. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1900:** *My Play Is Done*. A poem of completion and release; its title should not be retroactively treated as a precisely dated prediction of Vivekananda’s death. [Complete Works, vol. 6](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1900:** *No One to Blame*. A short philosophical poem stressing responsibility and causation. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *The Dance of Shiva*. A devotional poem or hymn; the standard contents do not supply a secure date. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *Shiva in Ecstasy*. A companion devotional poem, distinct in the collected edition from “The Dance of Shiva.” [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *To Shri Krishna*. A devotional poem; the collected index prints the name as “Khrishna,” an evident editorial spelling rather than grounds for inventing another work. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *A Hymn to Shri Ramakrishna* (two collected texts). The *Complete Works* lists two distinct hymns under the same English title; they should not be silently collapsed, but the title alone cannot distinguish them. [Complete Works, vol. 8](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *Reason, Faith and Love*. A short poem or aphoristic composition preserved among “prose and poems”; its genre is not firmly separated by the collected edition. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *The Message of Divine Wisdom*. A poetic or devotional composition preserved in a mixed generic section. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Undated:** *Six Sanskrit Mottoes*. Six compact Sanskrit verbal creations, including institutional mottoes; they are inscriptions or formulas designed to guide collective action rather than poems or essays in the ordinary sense. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **Late nineteenth century:** *Khandana Bhava-Bandhana*. A Bengali ārati hymn to Ramakrishna, made to be sung in communal worship. It is simultaneously song, liturgical protocol, and recurring performance, not merely a printed poem.

- **Late nineteenth century:** *Om Hrim Ritam*. A Sanskrit hymn to Ramakrishna; its made form includes the sung liturgical manifestation as well as the written words.

### Letters and documentary acts

- **1888–1902:** *Letters of Swami Vivekananda*. More than nine hundred surviving letters are presented across five “Epistle” series in the later nine-volume *Complete Works*. They are individual acts addressed to particular recipients—not a book Vivekananda planned—and dates, recipients, translations, and even readings remain editorial matters in particular cases. [Complete Works master index](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1894–1901:** *Public letters, replies, and addresses*. The collected works distinguish open letters and formal replies—among them communications to Madras, Calcutta, Khetri, brother-disciples, supporters, and journals—from private correspondence. These texts often function as manifestos, fundraising instruments, instructions, or institutional decisions rather than personal literature. [Complete Works, vols. 4–7](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Institutions, periodicals, and collective projects

- **1886 onward:** *The Ramakrishna monastic order*. Vivekananda helped form the brotherhood at Baranagar from Ramakrishna’s young disciples and subsequently reshaped it into an organized monastic body. It is a collective obra rather than his sole creation, and its institutional form continued to change after his death. [Belur Math biography](https://belurmath.org/swami-vivekananda/)

- **1 May 1897:** *Ramakrishna Mission*. An organization founded by Vivekananda for religious teaching and organized social service by monastics and laypeople. It is an institutional Gesamtkunstwerk—rule, community, pedagogy, relief work, publication, and built places—whose making was collective and remains ongoing. [Belur Math biography](https://belurmath.org/swami-vivekananda/)

- **1897–1901:** *Belur Math*. The permanent monastery and headquarters envisioned by Vivekananda, for which land was acquired in 1898 and the institutional body subsequently registered. He made the project and directed its religious conception, but the buildings and later campus were collective works rather than objects constructed by him alone. [Belur Math biography](https://belurmath.org/swami-vivekananda/)

- **1896:** *Prabuddha Bharata*. An English-language journal brought into being under Vivekananda’s inspiration and guidance. He was neither its routine editor nor the sole maker of its issues, so it belongs to his oeuvre as a publishing project rather than an authored periodical. [Complete Works master index](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **14 January 1899:** *Udbodhan*. The Bengali journal of the Ramakrishna movement, initiated at Vivekananda’s direction with Swami Trigunatitananda as founding editor. It is a collective publishing project and platform for new Bengali prose, not a book by Vivekananda. [Udbodhan history](https://udbodhan.org/patrika)

- **19 March 1899:** *Advaita Ashrama, Mayavati*. A Himalayan monastery conceived as a center for non-dualistic practice and publication, deliberately minimizing sectarian ritual and imagery. Its founding was collaborative, especially with the Seviers, while Vivekananda supplied the governing project and manifesto. [Complete Works, “The Advaita Ashrama, Himalayas”](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1899–1902:** *Ramakrishna Home of Service, Varanasi*. A service institution encouraged, named, and supported by Vivekananda but founded and operated collectively by younger workers. It therefore belongs to his institutional project without being exclusively his creation. [Complete Works, “The Ramakrishna Home of Service, Varanasi: An Appeal”](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

### Mediated records that resist being called his works

- **1893–1900:** *Reports in American newspapers*. The *Complete Works* preserves dozens of reports under titles such as “Divinity of Man,” “Religious Harmony,” “The Religions of India,” “True Buddhism,” “India’s Gift to the World,” and “The Women of India.” These testify that lectures occurred, but their wording and headlines were made by reporters and editors; they are manifestations of Vivekananda’s public speech, not securely authored texts. [Complete Works, vols. 2–3](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1895–1902:** *Notes of class talks and lectures*. Auditor-made records include “Religion and Science,” “Notes on Vedanta,” “Thoughts on the Vedas and Upanishads,” “Lessons on Raja-Yoga,” “Lessons on Bhakti-Yoga,” “Mother-Worship,” and many fragmentary notes. Their intellectual performance is Vivekananda’s, but the exact textual objects were made jointly by speaker, note-taker, translator, and editor. [Complete Works, vols. 5–9](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Questions and Answers*. Records of discussions at Harvard, the Twentieth Century Club, the Brooklyn Ethical Society, the Math, and with disciples. They are dialogic events: isolating Vivekananda as sole “author” suppresses the questions and the recorder’s mediation. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s–1902:** *Conversations and Dialogues*. Recollections and diary records by Surendra Nath Das Gupta, Surendra Nath Sen, Priya Nath Sinha, Sarat Chandra Chakravarty, and others. These resist classification as works by Vivekananda because disciples created the surviving texts, sometimes through translation and retrospective recollection. [Complete Works, vols. 5–7](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s–1902:** *Sayings and Utterances*. An editorial anthology of remembered oral statements. It documents a body of speech but is neither a manuscript nor a collection designed by Vivekananda. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)

- **1890s:** *Interviews*. Newspaper and periodical interviews on India, England, women, religious mission, miracles, and Hindu identity. They are co-produced documentary forms: interviewer, editor, and publication participate in making the “text,” and verbatim accuracy cannot simply be assumed. [Complete Works, vol. 5](https://www.ramakrishnavivekananda.info/vivekananda/master_index.htm)
