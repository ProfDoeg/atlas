## Works by Sabino Policarpo Arana Goiri

### Philological works and language projects

- **1887:** *Etimologías euskéricas*. A twenty-one-page philological pamphlet and Arana’s first separately published linguistic work; its etymologies belong to a prescientific phase of Basque studies and should not be mistaken for modern historical linguistics. [The collected edition’s bibliography records the title, date, and extent](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1888:** *Pliegos euskeráfilos*. A short pamphlet advocating the study and preservation of Basque. Some bibliographies confuse it with, or omit it beside, the similarly named *Pliegos euskeralógicos*; the fourteen-work count used by the collected edition treats them as separate things. [The distinction is discussed in this account of the collected corpus](https://latribunadelpaisvasco.com/art/21459/inigo-urkullu-premio-sabino-arana-2025).

- **1888:** *Gramática elemental del Euskera Bizkaino*. A roughly two-hundred-page grammar of the Biscayan variety of Basque, conceived both as linguistic description and as a protocol for teaching and national language-building. [It appears as an independent book in the collected edition’s bibliography](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1892:** *Pliegos euskeralógicos*. A twenty-four-page philological pamphlet, distinct from the earlier *Pliegos euskeráfilos*. [The collected edition dates and describes it separately](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1895:** *Tratado etimológico de los apellidos euskéricos*. A 128-page etymological study of Basque surnames that also functioned as an instrument for defining national and genealogical belonging. [The collected edition’s bibliography supplies its date and extent](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1896:** *Lecciones de Ortografía del Euskera Bizkaino*. A 306-page orthographic treatise and prescriptive language protocol rather than merely a descriptive book: it attempts to regulate how Biscayan Basque should be written. [It is catalogued as an independent book in the collected edition](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1897:** *Umiaren lenengo aizkidia*. An approximately eighty-page elementary Basque reading primer for children; it is a pedagogical object and language-normalization tool, not simply a literary text. [The collected edition lists it independently](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **c. 1886–1903:** *Basque lexical and onomastic coinages*. Arana made a dispersed body of proposed spellings, personal names, political terms, and neologisms—most famously *Euzkadi*—across his linguistic works and journalism. This resists treatment as one fixed work: it is a continuing language project whose individual manifestations are embedded in other texts. [The Sabino Arana archive describes its holdings of manuscripts, lists, and philological documentation](https://www.sabinoaranagoiri.eus/archivo.php).

### Historical-political pamphlets, speeches, and manifestos

- **1888:** *Pliegos histórico-políticos I*. A fourteen-page historical-political pamphlet in which antiquarian argument begins to become a nationalist political project. The first volume’s title is duplicated in the printed bibliography of the collected edition, an apparent bibliographical error rather than evidence of two separate works. [See the edition’s bibliography](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1889:** *Pliegos histórico-políticos II*. A twenty-four-page continuation of the preceding pamphlet. [It is separately dated and described by the collected edition](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1892:** *Bizkaya por su independencia: cuatro glorias patrias*. A book assembled from four historical-legendary narratives first published in *La Abeja* in 1890; it is simultaneously historiographical fiction, political argument, and a foundational nationalist manifesto. Its narratives rewrite received legendary battles rather than constitute reliable modern historiography. [The collected edition records the 141-page book](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf), while a scholarly study traces the four pieces to their 1890 periodical versions and the 1892 book](https://addi.ehu.es/bitstream/handle/10810/38240/14141-53192-1-PB.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).

- **1893:** *Discurso de Larrazabal*. A political speech delivered on 3 June 1893 and subsequently issued as a sixteen-page pamphlet. It is properly a manifesto—an utterance intended to inaugurate organized political action—as well as a printed text. [The collected edition lists the pamphlet](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf), and the institutional chronology identifies the speech as Arana’s first public exposition of his ideas](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/en/historias-vascas/efemerides/8).

- **1897:** *El Partido Carlista y los Fueros Vasko-Nabarros*. A polemical political leaflet or pamphlet directed against the Carlist understanding of the fueros; contemporary description calls it a “hoja,” so its later treatment as a book can misstate the original object. [The collected edition dates it to 1897](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf), and an archival account describes the original form and ensuing controversy](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/7095.pdf).

- **1902–1903:** *Spanish autonomist or “evolution” project*. In his final political phase Arana announced an intention to create a new organization pursuing extensive Basque autonomy within Spain. It resists being called a completed work: it was a strategic project expressed in articles and correspondence, remained programmatically unsettled, and was interrupted by his death.

### Calendars and instructional ephemera

- **1896:** *Egutegi Bizkattarra: egutegui de bolsillo para 1897*. A forty-eight-page pocket calendar combining practical calendrical information with language and nationalist pedagogy. Its object-date is 1896 although the calendar was made for 1897. [The collected edition gives the publication year, intended year, and extent](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1897:** *Lenengo Egutegi Bizkaitarra* (calendar for 1898). A wall calendar rather than a conventional book; it uses everyday timekeeping as a vehicle for normalized Basque vocabulary and national culture. [A study of the collected works identifies it specifically as a wall calendar for 1898](https://jakin.eus/show/6a9918c2949e58151a85ba089ea6b39dc0838d33).

### Theatre and unfinished dramatic projects

- **1897–1898:** *De fuera vendrá…: comedia en tres actos*. A three-act contemporary comedy written in manuscript but not published during Arana’s lifetime; it remained outside both editions of his *Obras completas* and was recovered and critically edited by José Luis de la Granja in 1982. Thus its status is not that of a lifetime publication but of a completed dramatic manuscript with a posthumous editorial history. [The Musée Basque catalogue dates the composition to 1897–1898](https://bibliotheque.musee-basque.fr/index.php?id=1219&l_typdoc=&lvl=author_see&nb_per_page_custom=25&nbr_lignes=32&page=1), and scholarship confirms that it remained unpublished until later](https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/REVHISTO/en/article/download/7690/6715/16884).

- **1902–1903:** *Libe*. A historical melodrama published under the pseudonym “Jelalde,” set in a legendary medieval Bizkaia and conceived as nationalist theatre. The 1934 stage version was developed and adapted by Manuel de la Sota, so that adaptation must not be attributed wholly to Arana; the underlying dramatic text was Arana’s. [A scholarly legal-historical bibliography records the 1903 publication](https://www.iuravasconiae.eus/voz/arana-goiri-sabino-550/), while scholarship dates composition to 1902 and distinguishes the later adaptation](https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/REVHISTO/en/article/download/7690/6715/16884).

- **Before November 1903:** *Labozko*. An unfinished and unpublished dramatic project set in antiquity, concerning Roman contact with the Basques. Because the surviving piece breaks off before its resolution, it is an incomplete project rather than a finished play, and its precise date has not been securely established. [Jonatan Pérez Mostazo identifies the manuscript and stresses that its incompleteness prevents recovery of the ending](https://e-revistas.uc3m.es/index.php/REVHISTO/en/article/download/7690/6715/16884).

- **Date uncertain, 1890s:** *La bandera fenicia*. A dramatic work or project attributed to Arana in recent theatre scholarship, but absent from the standard fourteen-item bibliography and from accounts that recognize only *De fuera vendrá…* and *Libe* as his completed plays. Its survival, completeness, and exact dating therefore remain disputed and it should not silently be promoted to the status of an established finished play. [The attribution appears in a recent study of Basque theatrical networks](https://roderic.uv.es/bitstreams/448bf06c-3faf-4b39-a56e-6de65af4ccd6/download), whereas other scholarship describes only two completed plays](https://webs.ucm.es/centros/cont/descargas/documento28343.pdf).

### Periodical projects and journalism

- **1893–1895:** *Bizkaitarra*. A newspaper project founded, edited, and largely written by Arana; thirty-two numbers appeared. It is both a collective periodical object and a sequence containing many individually titled articles, so treating every issue or article as a separate “book” would obscure what Arana actually made. [The collected edition identifies the run and number of issues](https://www.eusko-ikaskuntza.eus/PDFFondo/irujo/13767.pdf).

- **1897:** *Baserritarra* (also printed *Baseritaŕa*). An eighteen-number nationalist weekly initiated by Arana after the suppression of *Bizkaitarra*. Teófilo Guiard initially appeared as director, partly shielding Arana from censorship, and soon resigned; consequently “Arana’s newspaper” describes the project but not sole authorship of every component. [Euskariana supplies the complete run and explains the direction and attribution](https://www.euskariana.euskadi.eus/euskadibib/es/bib/1643916.do?format=ficha).

- **1899:** *El Correo Vasco*. A short-lived daily newspaper founded as a nationalist organ and containing Arana’s journalism; it was a collaborative editorial enterprise, not a single-authored text. [The Sabino Arana Foundation dates its first issue to 4 June 1899](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/en/historias-vascas/efemerides/8).

- **1901–1903:** *La Patria*. A nationalist weekly founded as an unofficial organ of EAJ-PNV and a vehicle for Arana’s late political writing. Arana did not author every item, and the paper continued independently of any single text; eighty-eight issues appeared under this title before it was closed. [The Sabino Arana Foundation gives the run, function, and issue count](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/es/historias-vascas/efemerides/el-27-de-octubre-de-1901-se-publico-el-primer-numero-del-semanario-nacionalista-vasco-la-patria-20201027).

- **1901–1902:** *Euzkadi*. A cultural magazine devoted to science, arts, and letters, created within Arana’s publishing campaign and containing his work. It must not be confused with the later daily newspaper *Euzkadi*, founded in 1913, ten years after his death. [The Foundation’s survey distinguishes Arana’s magazine from the later daily](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/storage/report/es/arabarra.pdf).

- **1903:** *Patria*. The renamed continuation of *La Patria*, launched after censorship closed the former title. Only its lifetime portion belongs to Arana’s activity: the periodical continued until 1906, so the whole 130-number run cannot be described as something he personally made. [The institutional history explains the change of title and continuation after Arana’s death](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/es/historias-vascas/efemerides/el-27-de-octubre-de-1901-se-publico-el-primer-numero-del-semanario-nacionalista-vasco-la-patria-20201027).

- **1886–1903:** *Articles in other newspapers and journals*. Arana made a dispersed body of more than six hundred articles, including early linguistic pieces and later political interventions in publications he did not control. This body resists classification as one work, while the articles themselves cannot safely be reconstructed as a definitive set of independent obras without reproducing the collected edition’s article-level index; the archival edition explicitly distinguishes “other publications” from his six principal periodical projects. [The specialized bibliographical profile reports more than six hundred articles](https://www.bvfe.es/es/component/mtree/autor/9224-arana-goiri-sabino.html), and the Sabino Arana archive supplies the corpus structure](https://www.sabinoaranagoiri.eus/obra.php).

### Poetry, songs, and an anthem

- **c. 1880s–1903:** *Poems, hymns, and songs*. The collected and archival tradition attributes thirty-three poems, hymns, and songs to Arana. This is a body of work rather than one fixed obra, and some pieces survive as manuscripts or occasional publications rather than authorially constituted books; the archival descriptions therefore provide a more honest boundary than later anthologies. [The bibliographical profile gives the total of thirty-three](https://www.bvfe.es/es/component/mtree/autor/9224-arana-goiri-sabino.html), while the personal archive separately identifies poetry and song materials](https://www.sabinoaranagoiri.eus/archivo.php).

- **1895; revised 1902:** *Euzko Abendaren Ereserkia*. A hymn text first drafted in prison in 1895 and rewritten definitively in 1902, intended for an existing *ezpata-dantza* melody. Arana made the words and the hymn-project, not the later musical arrangement: Cleto/Kelda Zabala’s posthumous adaptation helped create the form subsequently used as the official wordless anthem. [The Sabino Arana Foundation documents both textual stages and the later musical intervention](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/eu/euskadiko-istorioak/efemerideak/euzko-abendaren-ereserkia-euskal-ereserkia).

### Graphic and political-symbolic creations

- **1894:** *Ikurriña*. A flag designed jointly by Sabino and Luis Arana for Bizkaia and first raised at Euskeldun Batzokija on 14 July 1894; it later became the general Basque flag. Joint authorship is essential here: attributing the design to Sabino alone silently erases Luis Arana’s documented role. [The Sabino Arana Foundation identifies both brothers as designers and its original territorial purpose](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/es/historias-vascas/efemerides/el-nacimiento-de-la-ikurrina-20200714).

- **1893–1895:** *Jaun-Goikua eta Lagi-Zarra* / *JEL* as emblematic formula. A political-religious motto and condensed ideological protocol repeatedly materialized in print, flags, and organizational rules. It resists being treated as a single authored text: its obra is the formula’s designed conjunction and repeated public use.

- **c. 1896–1897:** *Euzkadi*. A coined name for the projected federation or nation of the Basques, later also applied to publications and institutions. Like a flag or motto, it is not a book but a lexical-political creation whose dating varies with whether one means private formation, first printed use, or subsequent adoption.

### Organizations and constitutional projects

- **1894:** *Euskeldun Batzokija*. A political and social association created as a meeting place and practical embodiment of Arana’s program; the first ikurriña was raised at its Bilbao premises. It is an institutional obra rather than a text, although statutes, membership practices, and symbols constituted it. [The institutional history records its 1894 opening and Arana’s presidency](https://www.sabinoarana.eus/eu/euskadiko-istorioak/efemerideak/la-ikurrina-la-bandera-vasca-20250714).

- **1895:** *Bizkai Buru Batzar / Euzko Alderdi Jeltzalea (EAJ-PNV)*. The political organization conventionally described as founded by Arana on 31 July 1895 grew out of Euskeldun Batzokija. It is a collective institutional creation rather than his sole personal work, and its later forms cannot be projected unchanged back onto the clandestine founding organization.

- **1890s–1903:** *The federal Euzkadi project*. Arana formulated a prospective confederation of the seven Basque territories, each retaining internal autonomy. It is properly a project—an intended political constitution that did not come into being during his lifetime—not a completed state or a single bounded text.

### Archival writings that resist work-status

- **1876–1903:** *Correspondence, drafts, notebooks, lists, reports, annotations, and drawings*. Arana generated a large documentary residue extending from childhood papers through political correspondence and unfinished manuscripts. These objects exist, but they resist being called a catalogue of discrete “works”: many are private acts of writing, working documents, fragments, or stages of other obras, and their dates and titles are often archival descriptions rather than authorial titles. [The Sabino Arana Foundation’s personal archive explicitly describes the unpublished drafts, correspondence, annotations, drawings, lists, reports, and manuscripts](https://www.sabinoaranagoiri.eus/archivo.php).
