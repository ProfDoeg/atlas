## Works by Augustus Henry Julian Le Plongeon

### Books, manuals, and book-length polemics

- **1867:** *La religión de Jesús comparada con las enseñanzas de la Iglesia, ó La vida de Jesús auténtica del M. R. P. Fr. Pedro Gual, ante el tribunal de la razón y de la ciencia*. A Spanish-language anti-clerical polemic published in Boston; its copyright deposit gives *La vida de Jesús auténtica* as the title, so the title’s extent and hierarchy vary among records. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 19–20)

- **1868:** *El R. fray Pedro Gual y sus doctrinas en trasparencia*. A further Spanish-language attack on Pedro Gual, sometimes catalogued as though it were merely another title or state of the 1867 polemic; the relationship between the two texts is therefore bibliographically unstable. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Le+Plongeon%2C+Augustus%2C+1826-1908))

- **1869:** *Los Jesuitas y el Perú, o, El R. fray Pedro Gual y sus doctrinas en transparencia*. A book-length continuation or enlargement of the Peruvian religious controversy; because it incorporates the earlier Pedro Gual title, it resists a clean distinction between new obra and substantially revised manifestation. ([Augustus Le Plongeon bibliography](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9B%D0%B5-%D0%9F%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B6%D0%BE%D0%BD%2C_%D0%9E%D0%B3%D1%8E%D1%81%D1%82))

- **1873:** *Manual de fotografía*. A Spanish-language technical handbook and protocol for photographic practice. Le Plongeon’s working copy became a continuing technical object through tipped-in recipes, instructions, and handwritten additions. ([Smithsonian Libraries](https://collections.si.edu/search/results.htm?q=%22Le+Plongeon%2C+Augustus%2C+1826-1908%22); [Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 20)

- **1881:** *Vestiges of the Mayas; or, Facts Tending to Prove That Communications and Intimate Relations Must Have Existed, in Very Remote Times, Between the Inhabitants of Mayab and Those of Asia and Africa*. A speculative archaeological book that turns field observations into a diffusionist history connecting the Maya with Africa and Asia. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/05006265/))

- **1886:** *Sacred Mysteries among the Mayas and the Quiches, 11,500 Years Ago: Their Relation to the Sacred Mysteries of Egypt, Greece, Chaldea and India; Freemasonry in Times Anterior to the Temple of Solomon*. An illustrated comparative-religion and occult-archaeological treatise; its historical and linguistic conclusions were rejected by later scholarship. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 20; [selected bibliography](https://hatch.kookscience.com/wiki/Augustus_Le_Plongeon))

- **1896:** *Queen Móo and the Egyptian Sphinx*. An illustrated archaeological-romance and speculative history in which photographs, drawings, purported decipherments, autobiography, and myth form something close to a Gesamtkunstwerk. The surviving 388-page typescript lacks its title page, while publication records and editions vary between “Sphinx” and “Sphynx.” ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 17–19; [Smithsonian Libraries](https://collections.si.edu/search/results.htm?q=%22Le+Plongeon%2C+Augustus%2C+1826-1908%22))

### Articles, communications, memorials, and public interventions

- **1866:** *Cólera epidémico*. A medical article published in the *Gaceta Médica de Lima*; it belongs to Le Plongeon’s medical rather than archaeological production. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 20)

- **1872:** *The Causes of Earthquakes*. A scientific article, related to lectures he had delivered in California, published in *Van Nostrand’s Eclectic Engineering Magazine*. ([bibliographic listing](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9B%D0%B5-%D0%9F%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B6%D0%BE%D0%BD%2C_%D0%9E%D0%B3%D1%8E%D1%81%D1%82))

- **1876:** *Finaliza el memorial de Dr. Augustus Le Plongeon […], Mérida, January 27, 1876*. A published memorial or petition concerning his Yucatán work; the archival copy’s title is incomplete, so no missing words should be supplied. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 20)

- **1876:** *Remitido: C. Presidente de la República Mexicana, Sr. D. Sebastián Lerdo de Tejada*. A public address to Mexico’s president, printed in *La Razón del Pueblo*; it is an operative petition—a text that seeks governmental action, and thus approaches a manifesto rather than merely describing research. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 20–21)

- **1876:** *Las ruinas de Yucatán*. A letter to newspaper editors, later reprinted in *El Pensamiento*; it is a distinct public communication rather than a book, despite later reuse of the same discoveries. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 21)

- **1876:** *Ruinas de Yucatán*. Another published letter to the editors, also reprinted in *El Pensamiento*. The archive treats it separately from *Las ruinas de Yucatán*, although their near-identical titles make their textual relationship uncertain. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 21)

- **1877:** *Account of Discoveries in Yucatan*. A sequence of Le Plongeon’s reports and letters incorporated by Stephen Salisbury Jr. into *The Mayas, the Sources of Their History: Dr. Le Plongeon in Yucatan, His Account of Discoveries*. Authorship is composite: Salisbury compiled and framed the publication, while Le Plongeon made the embedded account. ([digitized volume](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/4/47/The_Mayas%2C_the_source_of_their_history._Dr._Le_Plongeon_in_Yucatan%2C_his_account_of_discoveries_%28IA_mayassourceofthe00salirich%29.pdf))

- **1879:** *Archaeological Communication on Yucatan*. A report printed in the *Proceedings of the American Antiquarian Society* and in a composite pamphlet with works by Philipp J. J. Valentini, Stephen Salisbury Jr., and Alice Dixon Le Plongeon; the communication is his, but the containing publication is not a single-author obra. ([Wellcome Collection](https://content.www.wellcomecollection.org/concepts/gndpsm96); [contemporary bibliography](https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/508369/MRHL1912013001001.pdf))

- **1879:** *Letter on the Maya Language and Related Matters*. A letter published in the *Proceedings of the American Antiquarian Society*; its conventional title is descriptive rather than necessarily authorial. ([contemporary bibliography](https://repository.naturalis.nl/pub/508369/MRHL1912013001001.pdf))

- **1885:** *Photography Not an Art*. A brief polemical article in *Photographic Times and American Photographer*; its form is an intervention in a professional debate rather than a technical protocol. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 21)

- **between 1886 and 1908:** *Xochicalco Pyramid: History of the Lost Continent of Atlantis*. A newspaper article published in the New York *Times* according to the archive; the finding aid cannot supply a closer date. It is related to, but not necessarily textually identical with, the longer posthumous *Pyramid of Xochicalco*. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 21)

- **1901:** *A Monument to the Memory of Atlantis*. A magazine article presenting Le Plongeon’s interpretation of the Pyramid of Xochicalco as a monument to the destruction of Mu; it is a public manifestation of his larger Xochicalco project. ([*Mind*, vol. 8](https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/mind/mind_v8_1901.pdf))

### Lectures and lecture-projects

- **1870:** *A Sketch of the Ancient Inhabitants of Peru and Their Civilization*. An illustrated series of lectures delivered before the California Academy of Sciences. The surviving three-notebook manuscript is incomplete and incorporates photographs, a site plan, and loose annotations, so it is both performed discourse and multimedia working object. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 15–16)

- **between 1886 and 1908:** *Syllabus of Ten Lectures on Ancient American Civilization…*. A three-page protocol for a lecture course; its title is physically incomplete, and the archive preserves the syllabus rather than evidence that all ten lectures were delivered. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 19)

- **1896:** *The Monuments of Mayach and Their Historical Teachings*. A lecture read before the Albany Institute on 22 December 1896. This is sometimes confused with Alice Dixon Le Plongeon’s similarly titled illustrated publication; the archival lecture record attributes this item to Augustus, while the published photographic work is cited elsewhere as Alice’s. ([Getty Research Institute record](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/component/10QT94); [Cambridge Archaeological Journal](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-archaeological-journal/article/photographic-insights-from-engaged-archaeology-yucatan-and-beyond/7A10017D91E6D913CE1AD2AB96C834D0))

### Unpublished and unfinished texts

- **1860s:** *Manuscript concerning the temperature of the earth’s core*. An untitled eight-page French manuscript; it survives as a scientific text but not as a finished or published work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **1860s:** *Alerta Peruanos*. A four-page Spanish manuscript whose genre is uncertain; its imperative title suggests a political warning or manifesto. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **1860s:** *Alegoría*. A two-page Spanish manuscript; the finding aid establishes its material existence but not whether it is literary, political, or fragmentary. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **1860s:** *Carta de Plinio el Joven al Emperador Trajano*. A three-page Spanish manuscript, apparently a translation, transcription, or adaptation rather than an original composition; the archive does not resolve which. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **1860s:** *Reglamento interior del comité central de la provincia*. A four-page Spanish protocol for an organization: a text meant to govern conduct rather than describe it. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **between 1866 and 1869:** *El Rev. P. Fleury*. A two-page Spanish manuscript associated with Le Plongeon’s Peruvian religious controversies; its intended genre and completion status are not specified. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **between 1866 and 1869:** *El Rev. Gual y sus doctrinas au transparencia*. A one-page unfinished Spanish manuscript, probably part of the Pedro Gual polemical project; it resists treatment as an independent completed work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 14)

- **1868:** *Documents for the formation of a philanthropic society for victims of the Lima earthquake*. Three jointly signed Spanish manuscripts establishing an organization. They are collective protocols, so Le Plongeon’s authorship is participatory rather than exclusive. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 15)

- **1869:** *Letter to the editor of El Comercio*. An eight-page Spanish manuscript, dated uncertainly to 3 February; the archive does not establish whether it was published. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 15)

- **between 1866 and 1869:** *Biografía del fraile Pisco, Obispo de Chachapoyas*. An eight-page Spanish biographical manuscript with an inserted note, belonging to the larger Peruvian religious-controversy portfolio. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 15)

- **1860s:** *1847 à 1848*. A nine-page Spanish manuscript known only by this date-like title; its subject and intended status remain unidentified. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 15)

- **1860s:** *Untitled partial manuscript concerning comparative religions*. A nine-page Spanish fragment. Its incompleteness is intrinsic evidence: it is a surviving project-material, not securely a finished obra. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 15)

- **1860s:** *Untitled partial manuscript concerning religious practices in Peru*. An eight-page Spanish fragment whose original title and intended extent are unknown. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 15)

- **1870s:** *Untitled book on the Inca*. A thirty-seven-page English manuscript containing two chapters of an unfinished book, on the foundation and religion of the Inca empire. It survives as a project rather than a completed book. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 16)

- **circa 1870s:** *Untitled book on the Yucatán peninsula*. A 167-page, heavily composite manuscript with chapters on Yucatán’s name, geography, and Maya grammar, plus pasted photographs, drawings, and extensive additions. Pages are missing throughout, making it an unfinished photographic-textual assemblage rather than a recoverable complete book. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 16)

- **1872:** *Apology for the American Race*. A signed and dated seven-page English manuscript; the archive does not record publication. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 16)

- **1880s:** *Untitled manuscript concerning ancient religions*. A thirty-six-page English comparative-theology manuscript; without a fixed title or evidence of publication, it remains a research text rather than a formally issued work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 16)

- **1880s:** *Untitled manuscript concerning the meaning of Maya architecture*. An English manuscript missing its first four pages. Its physical loss prevents determination of the original title and possibly of the full argument. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 16–17)

- **between 1886 and 1908:** *Untitled manuscript concerning Egyptian and Maya languages as cognates*. A three-page typescript expressing part of Le Plongeon’s comparative-language project; it may be a fragment or précis rather than an autonomous work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 19)

- **between 1886 and 1908:** *Experiences of Augustus Le Plongeon*. The surviving first nine pages of a typescript about his psychic experiences. Since only an opening fragment remains, it cannot securely be classified as memoir, occult testimony, or completed work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 19)

- **between 1886 and 1908; published 1913–1914:** *The Origin of the Egyptians*. A posthumously serialized treatise connecting Egyptian origins with the Maya; later issued as a 1983 book, that volume is a posthumous manifestation rather than a book published by Le Plongeon himself. ([*The Word*, vol. 17](https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/word_percival/word_v17_apr_1913-sep_1913.pdf); [Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 20)

- **between 1886 and 1908; published 1913–1914:** *Pyramid of Xochicalco*. A sixty-one-page manuscript, missing page 4, posthumously serialized in *The Word*. Its relationship to the shorter newspaper article on Xochicalco is close but not demonstrated to be identity. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 19; [scholarly bibliography](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348062283_2021_THE_END_OF_XOCHICALCO_A_RITUAL_DESTRUCTION))

### Photographic obras and documentation projects

- **1862–1870:** *Photographs of Peru*. A body of albumen and collodion photographs of landscapes, archaeological sites, pottery, architecture, and people, made during Le Plongeon’s Peruvian years. It is an open corpus rather than a single fixed work, dispersed among notebooks, prints, negatives, and later reproductions. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 12, 31–40)

- **1873–1884:** *Photographic survey of Yucatán and neighboring Maya sites*. A large documentary project encompassing Chichén Itzá, Uxmal, Aké, Mayapán, Izamal, Isla Mujeres, Cozumel, and other locations, with approximately 500 images reported for Chichén Itzá alone. Augustus and Alice Dixon Le Plongeon worked collaboratively, and attribution of many individual exposures cannot safely be separated; the corpus therefore resists a single-author catalogue of “works.” ([Getty digital collection](https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/digital_collections/notable/plongeon.html); [Cambridge Archaeological Journal](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-archaeological-journal/article/photographic-insights-from-engaged-archaeology-yucatan-and-beyond/7A10017D91E6D913CE1AD2AB96C834D0))

- **1875–1876:** *Photographic sequence of the excavation and removal of the Chacmool*. A staged documentary sequence showing excavation, discovery, handling, and transport of the sculpture at Chichén Itzá. Some images were made by Alice—one surviving negative explicitly credits her—so this is best understood as a collaborative expeditionary obra, not exclusively Augustus’s photography. ([Getty digital collection](https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/digital_collections/notable/plongeon.html); [Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 32–35, 67)

- **1870s–1880s:** *Stereoscopic record of Maya architecture and sculpture*. A substantial body of paired wet-collodion negatives and albumen stereographs intended to produce spatially convincing views of ruins, reliefs, and excavation contexts. Individual negatives, prints, half-stereos, and lantern-slide derivatives are manifestations of an unfixed documentary system rather than separate titled obras. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 31–71)

- **1862–1900:** *Lantern-slide lecture collection*. A visual apparatus assembled from Peruvian and Yucatecan photographs for projection and public teaching. Because slides reproduce earlier negatives and served changing lecture sequences, the collection is neither simply a publication nor a stable single work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), pp. 71–85)

### Drawings, maps, plans, and composite working objects

- **1862–1883:** *Pocket notebooks of Peruvian, Middle Eastern, Mexican, and Maya antiquities*. Two composite notebooks containing pencil and watercolor drawings, photographs, engravings, site notes, and copied codex figures. They are research assemblages or miniature Gesamtkunstwerke rather than conventional texts, and some inserted images were made by others. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 12)

- **1862–1870:** *Drawings of Peru*. Six pen-and-ink drawings of Peruvian landscapes and sites accompanied by two drawings of ancient Middle Eastern subjects. The archive groups them as eight objects but does not supply individual authorial titles. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 12)

- **circa 1883:** *Section drawing of the Platform of Venus*. An archaeological cross-section showing an excavation trench and artifacts, credited jointly to Augustus and Alice Le Plongeon. It is an analytical field record rather than a freestanding artwork. ([Lawrence G. Desmond, “Augustus Le Plongeon: Early Mayanist, Archeologist, Photographer”](https://archaeoplanet.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/2001-augustus-le-plongeon-early-mayanist-archeologist-photographer.pdf))

- **circa 1896:** *Original Maya-motif drawing for Queen Móo and the Egyptian Sphinx*. A pencil illustration made for incorporation into the book; it is simultaneously an individual drawing and a component of the larger illustrated obra. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 18)

- **after 1890:** *Mock-up illustrations for a publication on Xochicalco*. Eight plates taken from Antonio Peñafiel’s *Monumentos del arte mexicano antiguo* and arranged for possible use in Le Plongeon’s publication project. Because the underlying images are Peñafiel’s and the intended publication remained unresolved, Le Plongeon’s made thing here is the editorial mock-up, not the plates themselves. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 21)

### Archaeological and practical projects

- **1864:** *Electro-hydropathic baths in Peru*. A proposed or established therapeutic enterprise for which Le Plongeon received an official grant. The surviving evidence establishes the authorized project, but not confidently the completed building or its duration, so it should not be silently converted into an architectural work. ([Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 21)

- **1873–1884:** *Archaeological survey and excavation of Maya sites*. A long-duration expeditionary project, undertaken with Alice and local Maya workers, joining excavation, measurement, photography, drawing, collecting, linguistic study, and speculative interpretation. Its made object is a distributed body of records and interventions rather than a single text. ([Getty digital collection](https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/digital_collections/notable/plongeon.html); [Desmond and Messenger study description](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA41702046))

- **1875:** *Excavation of the Chacmool at Chichén Itzá*. An archaeological intervention that uncovered and repositioned the reclining sculpture from the Platform of the Eagles and Jaguars. The sculpture itself was not made by Le Plongeon; what he made was the excavation, interpretive identification, and documentary event, collaboratively produced with Alice and Maya laborers. ([Getty digital collection](https://www.getty.edu/research/tools/digital_collections/notable/plongeon.html); [Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 67)

- **circa 1883:** *Excavation and reconstruction study of the Platform of Venus*. A field project preserved through excavation records, a section drawing, photographs, and a later reconstruction image. The “work” is therefore not the ancient platform but Le Plongeon’s intervention and representational reconstruction of it. ([Desmond, “Augustus Le Plongeon: Early Mayanist, Archeologist, Photographer”](https://archaeoplanet.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/08/2001-augustus-le-plongeon-early-mayanist-archeologist-photographer.pdf); [Getty finding aid](https://www.getty.edu/research/collections/static/pdf/2004.M.18.pdf), p. 35)
