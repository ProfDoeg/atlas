## Works by Abraham ben Samuel Abulafia

### Philosophical and esoteric commentaries

- **1273:** *Sefer ha-Ge’ulah* (“Book of Redemption”). The first of Abulafia’s three commentaries on Maimonides’ *Guide of the Perplexed*; only portions survive. [The standard account dates and identifies it as the first commentary](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110599978/html).
- **1270s:** *Mafteaḥ ha-Ra‘ayon* (“Key of the Idea”). An early linguistic-kabbalistic treatise or commentary, closely related to *Get ha-Shemot*; its precise date and place within the sequence of Abulafia’s writings remain uncertain. It is included in Amnon Gross’s collected edition and in Idel’s primary-source bibliography. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1270s:** *Get ha-Shemot* (“Writ/Divorce of the Names”). A theoretical exposition of names, letters, language, and intellect rather than a conventional biblical commentary; Abulafia presents it as groundwork for the “Kabbalah of Names.” [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **before or about 1280:** *Ḥayyei ha-Nefesh* (“Life of the Soul”). The second commentary on the *Guide of the Perplexed*, joining Maimonidean psychology and prophecy to Abulafia’s linguistic Kabbalah. Its exact date is not securely established. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1280:** *Sitrei Torah* (“Secrets of the Torah”). The third commentary on the *Guide of the Perplexed* and a deliberately esoteric reading of its philosophical secrets. [Idel, *Abraham Abulafia’s Esotericism*](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/document/70561/1/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf)
- **before 1285–1286:** *Gan Na‘ul* (“Locked Garden”). A commentary on *Sefer Yeṣirah* concerned with letters, divine names, and creation; its exact date is uncertain. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1285–1286:** *Oṣar ‘Eden Ganuz* (“Treasury of the Hidden Eden”). Abulafia’s large, partly autobiographical commentary on *Sefer Yeṣirah*; it is simultaneously exegesis, intellectual autobiography, and an account of his prophetic project. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **date uncertain:** *Untitled commentary on Sefer Yeṣirah*. Scholarship counts a third commentary in addition to *Gan Na‘ul* and *Oṣar ‘Eden Ganuz*. Its title and textual boundaries are not fixed, so it resists treatment as an securely bounded book. [Survey of Abulafia’s writings](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/philosophy-and-religion/judaism-biographies/abraham-ben-samuel-abulafia)
- **date uncertain:** *Perush Sefer Yeṣirah* (“Commentary on the Book of Formation”). Israel Weinstock published an anonymous commentary “from the school/foundation of Abraham Abulafia,” formerly attributed to Abraham ibn Ezra; attribution to Abulafia himself is disputed and should not be treated as settled. [Idel’s primary-source bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/1/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf)
- **1289:** *Sefer Mafteḥot ha-Torah* (“Book of the Keys of the Torah”). A five-part Pentateuch commentary conceived as a single exegetical project but transmitted as five separately titled “keys.” [Hames’s discussion of the five books](https://dgagaeta.cultura.gov.it/public/uploads/documents/Saggi/Saggi_32.pdf)
- **1289:** *Mafteaḥ ha-Ḥokhmot* (“Key of the Wisdoms”). The Genesis portion of *Mafteḥot ha-Torah*, interpreting scripture through philosophical and combinatory-kabbalistic techniques. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1289:** *Mafteaḥ ha-Shemot* (“Key of Names”). The Exodus portion of the five-part Torah commentary; “names” refers both to the Hebrew title of Exodus and to Abulafia’s theology of divine names. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1289:** *Mafteaḥ ha-Qorbanot* (“Key of the Sacrifices”). The Leviticus portion of the project. It appears to be lost or unidentified, and consequently resists description as a surviving text. [Hames’s enumeration of the five “keys”](https://dgagaeta.cultura.gov.it/public/uploads/documents/Saggi/Saggi_32.pdf)
- **1289:** *Mafteaḥ ha-Sefirot* (“Key of the Sefirot”). The Numbers portion of *Mafteḥot ha-Torah*, using “sefirot” in Abulafia’s distinctive philosophical-linguistic framework. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1289:** *Mafteaḥ ha-Tokhaḥot* (“Key of the Admonitions”). The Deuteronomy portion of the Pentateuch project. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

### Protocols of prophetic practice

- **1280:** *Ḥayyei ha-‘Olam ha-Ba* (“Life of the World to Come”). A practical contemplative protocol: it instructs the practitioner in preparation, breathing, vocalization, bodily movement, and permutations of divine letters intended to induce prophetic experience. [Idel, *The Mystical Experience in Abraham Abulafia*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.18253228)
- **c. 1285:** *Or ha-Sekhel* (“Light of the Intellect”). A systematic instructional treatise dedicated to disciples Abraham and Nathan; it explains letter-combination, pronunciation, and intellectual conjunction, making it both philosophical exposition and protocol. [Idel on its dedication and contents](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/3/273)
- **1280s:** *Sefer ha-Ḥesheq* (“Book of Desire”). A handbook of combinations and pronunciations of divine names, dedicated to Jacob ben Abraham; its operative sequences make it more accurately a protocol than merely a theoretical book. [Study locating the dedication in 1289](https://dgagaeta.cultura.gov.it/public/uploads/documents/Saggi/Saggi_32.pdf)
- **1280s:** *Sefer ha-Melammed* (“Book of the Teacher”). A pedagogical treatise on the divine name, intellect, prophecy, and letter combination; its exact date is uncertain. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)
- **1291:** *Imrei Shefer* (“Words of Beauty”). Abulafia’s last securely dated work and one of his clearest meditation manuals, compressing his combinatory practice into a late systematic statement. [Idel, *Abraham Abulafia’s Esotericism*](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/document/70561/1/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf)

### Prophetic books and self-commentaries

- **1279:** *Sefer ha-Yashar* (“Book of the Upright”). The first named prophetic book, composed at Patras. The revelation itself is lost, while material identified as its commentary survives; the “work” therefore persists principally through its author’s act of interpreting an absent text. [Idel on its place and date in the prophetic sequence](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/1/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf)
- **c. 1280:** *Sefer ha-‘Edut* (“Book of Testimony”). A prophetic testimony to Abulafia’s messianic mission. The underlying revelation is not independently extant, but Abulafia’s commentary survives in the manuscript compilation later printed as *Maṣref ha-Sekhel*. [Idel’s identification of the surviving commentary](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/3/273)
- **c. 1280:** *Sefer ha-Ḥayyim* (“Book of Life”). A prophetic book or self-commentary belonging to the early revelatory sequence; portions survive in *Maṣref ha-Sekhel*. It should not be confused with *Ḥayyei ha-Nefesh* or *Ḥayyei ha-‘Olam ha-Ba*. [Idel’s references to the text and its manuscript compilation](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/3/273)
- **1281:** *Sefer Ish Adam* (“Book of the Man Adam”). A prophetic text with commentary, composed in Messina and preserving information about Abulafia’s circle there. Its surviving state is bound up with the later manuscript compilation *Maṣref ha-Sekhel*. [Survey of Sicilian Jewish sources](https://morasha.it/bibliografia-sullebraismo-siciliano/)
- **c. 1281:** *Sefer ha-Berit* (“Book of the Covenant”). One unit in the surviving prophetic-commentary corpus assembled as *Maṣref ha-Sekhel*. Whether it circulated as a fully independent book, rather than as part of that sequence, is not entirely clear. [Description of Gross’s collected edition and its contents](https://researchmap.jp/mshrshd/presentations/16502900/attachment_file.pdf)
- **c. 1281–1282:** *Sefer ha-Haftarah* (“Book of the Dismissal/Conclusion”). A prophetic book known mainly through Abulafia’s commentary. Its wording and status overlap with the following *Ḥotam ha-Haftarah*, and catalogues do not always distinguish the layers consistently. [Idel on the surviving prophetic commentaries](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/1/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf)
- **c. 1281–1282:** *Ḥotam ha-Haftarah* (“Seal of the Haftarah”). The concluding prophetic book or section in the *Maṣref ha-Sekhel* complex. It includes visual and spatially arranged letter-poetry, so it is not merely discursive prose but a small textual-graphic Gesamtkunstwerk. [Ayin Press’s manuscript-based description](https://ayinpress.org/lightdarkness/)
- **1285–1288, with disputed internal chronology:** *Sefer ha-Ot* (“Book of the Sign”). The only prophetic book substantially preserved as such, an apocalyptic and autobiographical revelation composed around Abulafia’s exile on Comino. Harvey Hames argues that what is called one book may actually comprise three sections from different periods. [Hames, “Three in One or One that Is Three”](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rjuiv_0484-8616_2006_num_165_1_2882)
- **1279–1288:** *The remaining unnamed prophetic books*. Abulafia variously reports a large prophetic corpus—scholarly accounts differ between twenty-two and twenty-six books—but most titles and texts are lost. These are known projects or claimed manifestations rather than individually recoverable works, and forcing them into twenty-odd invented entries would conceal rather than repair the evidence. [Encyclopaedia Judaica’s account of twenty-six prophetic books](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0011_0_10514.html)

### Letters, polemics, and shorter treatises

- **before 1280:** *Sheva‘ Netivot ha-Torah* (“Seven Paths of the Torah”). A short hermeneutical manifesto setting out seven ascending modes of scriptural interpretation, from literal reading to prophetic letter-combination. [Gross-edition contents documented here](https://researchmap.jp/mshrshd/presentations/16502900/attachment_file.pdf)
- **1280s:** *Ve-Zot li-Yehudah* (“And This Is for Judah”). An epistle to Judah Salomon defending Abulafia’s Kabbalah and prophetic claims amid opposition associated with Solomon ibn Adret; it acts polemically as well as explaining doctrine. [Early publication and identification in the *Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/699-abulafia-abraham-ben-samuel)
- **1280s:** *Maṣref la-Kesef ve-Kur la-Zahav* (“A Crucible for Silver and a Furnace for Gold”). An epistle employing metallurgical language for intellectual purification and allegorical interpretation. It is sometimes shortened to *Maṣref la-Kesef*. [Idel identifies it explicitly as an epistle](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/16/3/273)
- **1280s:** *Shomer Miṣwah* (“Guardian of the Commandment”). A short instructional-dedicatory work written for Solomon ha-Kohen; its genre lies between letter, ethical exhortation, and kabbalistic protocol. [Documentary discussion of its dedicatee](https://dgagaeta.cultura.gov.it/public/uploads/documents/Saggi/Saggi_32.pdf)
- **date uncertain:** *Sefer ha-Meliṣ* (“Book of the Interpreter/Advocate”). A short work surviving in the prophetic manuscript complex printed under the editorial title *Maṣref ha-Sekhel*. Its independent textual boundaries are not entirely secure. [Idel, bibliography](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/handle/document/70561/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1)

### Composite, anonymous, and disputed objects

- **late thirteenth century; compilation date uncertain:** *Maṣref ha-Sekhel* (“Crucible of the Intellect”). This is best treated not as one straightforward authorial book but as a manuscript compilation containing Abulafia’s prophetic books or, more often, his commentaries on them: *Sefer ha-Meliṣ*, *Sefer Ish Adam*, *Sefer ha-Berit*, *Sefer ha-‘Edut*, *Sefer ha-Ḥayyim*, *Sefer ha-Yashar*, and *Ḥotam ha-Haftarah*. Its unity is codicological and editorial as much as authorial, so it resists being counted as an additional work beside all its components. [Contents of Gross’s edition](https://researchmap.jp/mshrshd/presentations/16502900/attachment_file.pdf)
- **late thirteenth or early fourteenth century:** *Sefer ha-Ṣeruf* (“Book of Combination”). Amnon Gross printed this in the collected-work series, but modern scholarship generally treats it as an anonymous work of the Abulafian school or of a disciple, not securely as Abulafia’s own creation. It belongs in the attribution history, not in an undisputed canon. [Discussion of its anonymous, school-derived status](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/document/70561/1/ssoar-2020-idel-Abraham_Abulafias_Esotericism_Secrets_and.pdf)
- **late thirteenth century:** *Ner Elohim* (“Lamp of God”). Gross paired this text with *Get ha-Shemot* in the collected series, but it is attributed to Abulafia’s disciple Nathan ben Sa‘adyah Harar rather than to Abulafia. Its inclusion in the edition makes it part of the material reception of his project, not something he himself made. [Gross-series contents and attribution warning](https://researchmap.jp/mshrshd/presentations/16502900/attachment_file.pdf)
