## Works by Igor Fyodorovich Stravinsky

### Music-theatre works and Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1908–1914:** *Le Rossignol (The Nightingale)*. Opera in three acts after Hans Christian Andersen; its long gestation leaves its first act stylistically distinct from the later acts. [K018]
- **1910:** *L’Oiseau de feu (The Firebird)*. Ballet score for Diaghilev’s Ballets Russes, subsequently revised and remade as concert suites in 1911, 1919 and 1945. [K010]
- **1910–1911; revised 1947:** *Pétrouchka*. Ballet whose music, choreography and stage action form a Gesamtkunstwerk; Stravinsky later made the independent piano work *Trois mouvements de Pétrouchka* and other reductions. [K012]
- **1911–1913; repeatedly revised:** *Le Sacre du printemps (The Rite of Spring)*. Ballet subtitled “Pictures of Pagan Russia,” conceived with Nicholas Roerich; the score also became an autonomous concert obra and survives in several revised states. [K015]
- **1914–1917; orchestration completed 1923:** *Les Noces*. Dance-cantata or choreographic cantata for voices, four pianos and percussion; its succession of abandoned orchestrations makes the completed object inseparable from a prolonged project. [K040]
- **1915–1916:** *Renard*. Burlesque “tale sung and played,” combining singers, dancers or mimes and chamber ensemble; it deliberately resists classification as either opera or ballet. [K023]
- **1918:** *L’Histoire du soldat (The Soldier’s Tale)*. Travelling music-theatre work for three speakers, dancers and seven instrumentalists, made with C. F. Ramuz; it is a deliberately portable Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an opera in the conventional sense. [K029]
- **1919–1920:** *Pulcinella*. Ballet with voices, based on eighteenth-century music then attributed largely to Pergolesi; its transformative authorship lies between composition and recomposition. [K034]
- **1921–1922:** *Mavra*. One-act comic opera after Pushkin, with libretto by Boris Kochno. [K039]
- **1925–1927; revised 1948:** *Oedipus rex*. Opera-oratorio with Jean Cocteau’s text translated into Latin by Jean Daniélou; its static ritual presentation deliberately crosses opera, concert work and spoken theatre. [K047]
- **1927–1928; revised 1947:** *Apollon musagète (Apollo)*. Ballet in two scenes for string orchestra, created for choreography by George Balanchine. [K048]
- **1928; revised 1950:** *Le Baiser de la fée (The Fairy’s Kiss)*. Ballet after Andersen, recomposing and orchestrating music by Tchaikovsky; it is simultaneously homage, arrangement and new dramatic obra. [K049]
- **1933–1934:** *Perséphone*. Mélodrame in three parts for speaker, tenor, chorus, children’s chorus, dancers and orchestra, made with André Gide; it resists separation into theatre, ballet and oratorio. [K056]
- **1936–1937:** *Jeu de cartes (Card Game)*. Ballet in three “deals,” written for Balanchine’s American Ballet. [K059]
- **1941–1942:** *Danses concertantes*. Concert dances for chamber orchestra, conceived without a fixed stage action but subsequently choreographed; its status shifts between concert suite and ballet. [K063]
- **1942:** *Circus Polka*. Piano piece commissioned for George Balanchine’s elephant ballet and immediately orchestrated; the peculiar original spectacle makes it a theatrical project as well as a concert miniature. [K064]
- **1944:** *Scènes de ballet*. Ballet score commissioned for a Broadway revue, later established as an autonomous orchestral work. [K069]
- **1947:** *Orpheus*. Ballet in three scenes for chamber orchestra, created with Balanchine. [K076]
- **1947–1951:** *The Rake’s Progress*. Opera in three acts with libretto by W. H. Auden and Chester Kallman, after William Hogarth’s engravings. [K078]
- **1953–1957:** *Agon*. Ballet for twelve dancers and orchestra; its mixture of historical dance forms and serial procedures is integral to Balanchine’s choreography but also functions as concert music. [K088]
- **1961–1962:** *The Flood*. Biblical musical play made for television, combining narration, acting, dance, chorus and orchestra; “television opera” only partly captures its mixed-media character. [K098]

### Orchestral and concertante obras

- **1905–1907; revised 1913 and 1947:** *Symphony in E-flat major, Op. 1*. Large orchestral symphony and Stravinsky’s first completed orchestral obra. [K003]
- **1907–1908:** *Scherzo fantastique, Op. 3*. Orchestral scherzo; a ballet was later mounted to it without Stravinsky’s initial authorization. [K005]
- **1908:** *Feu d’artifice (Fireworks), Op. 4*. Short orchestral fantasy composed as a wedding gift. [K007]
- **1908:** *Chant funèbre (Funeral Song), Op. 5*. Orchestral memorial to Rimsky-Korsakov, long treated as lost until the orchestral parts were rediscovered in 2015. [K008]
- **1917:** *Le Chant du rossignol (The Song of the Nightingale)*. Symphonic poem drawn from the second and third acts of *Le Rossignol* but reshaped as a separate orchestral obra. [K026]
- **1921:** *Suite No. 2 for Small Orchestra*. Orchestral transformation of movements from *Three Easy Pieces* and *Five Easy Pieces*. [K038]
- **1925:** *Suite No. 1 for Small Orchestra*. Orchestral transformation of four pieces from *Five Easy Pieces*. [K045]
- **1923–1924; revised 1950:** *Concerto for Piano and Wind Instruments*. Concertante obra written for Stravinsky to perform as soloist. [K042]
- **1928:** *Four Studies for Orchestra*. Orchestral recomposition of the *Three Pieces for String Quartet* and *Étude pour pianola*. [K051]
- **1929; revised 1949:** *Capriccio for Piano and Orchestra*. Three-movement concertante work composed for Stravinsky’s own performances. [K050]
- **1931:** *Violin Concerto in D*. Four-movement concertante obra written with advice from violinist Samuel Dushkin. [K053]
- **1935:** *Concerto for Two Solo Pianos*. Concert work without orchestra, made for Stravinsky and his son Soulima to perform. [K058]
- **1937–1938:** *Concerto in E-flat, “Dumbarton Oaks”*. Chamber-orchestra concerto modelled in part on the Brandenburg Concertos. [K060]
- **1938–1940:** *Symphony in C*. Four-movement orchestral symphony composed across Stravinsky’s emigration to the United States. [K061]
- **1942:** *Four Norwegian Moods*. Orchestral suite drawing on Norwegian folk materials. [K065]
- **1943:** *Ode*. Orchestral triptych written in memory of Natalie Koussevitzky; the first performance exposed copying errors later corrected. [K066]
- **1945:** *Symphony in Three Movements*. Orchestral symphony whose materials include abandoned film projects, transformed into a new concert obra. [K073]
- **1945:** *Ebony Concerto*. Concerto for clarinet and jazz band, written for Woody Herman’s ensemble. [K074]
- **1946:** *Concerto in D (“Basle Concerto”)*. Concerto for string orchestra commissioned by Paul Sacher. [K075]
- **1958–1959:** *Movements for Piano and Orchestra*. Five compressed serial movements for piano and orchestra. [K091]
- **1963–1964:** *Variations: Aldous Huxley in Memoriam*. Serial orchestral memorial; Balanchine later choreographed it, but the original object is a concert work. [K103]

### Chamber, keyboard and mechanically performed obras

- **1898:** *Tarantella*. Juvenile piano piece; it entered the catalogue only after the principal numbering had been established. [KN01]
- **circa 1902:** *Scherzo*. Early piano composition, surviving in manuscript and therefore not merely a reported lost work. [KN02]
- **1903–1904:** *Piano Sonata in F-sharp minor*. Four-movement student sonata, rediscovered among archival materials after having been considered lost. [K001]
- **1908:** *Four Studies, Op. 7*. Four piano études later partly transformed into orchestral music. [K009]
- **1914:** *Three Pieces for String Quartet*. Three sharply differentiated miniatures, later orchestrated within *Four Studies*. [K019]
- **1914:** *Valse des fleurs*. Piece for two pianos, written for Stravinsky’s children. [KN12]
- **1915:** *Three Easy Pieces*. Piano-duet miniatures in which the lower part was designed for a less advanced player. [K021]
- **1915:** *Souvenir d’une marche boche*. Piano miniature and wartime caricature; its occasional character makes it closer to a musical intervention than a major concert work. [KN14]
- **1917:** *Five Easy Pieces*. Piano-duet miniatures with the easier part placed in the upper register. [K025]
- **1917:** *A Waltz for Children*. Very short piano piece published for young readers. [KN16]
- **1917:** *Study for Pianola*. Composition for player piano rather than a transcription of a human performance; its medium is a programmed mechanical manifestation. [K027]
- **1917:** *Pour Pablo Picasso*. Unpublished clarinet miniature written in Picasso’s album; it resists the status of a public work and survives as an inscribed occasional object. [KN18]
- **1917:** *Canon for Two Horns*. Brief unpublished canon, catalogued among the later supplements. [KN17]
- **1918:** *Lied ohne Name*. Duet for two bassoons; a small manuscript piece rather than a formally published opus. [KN19]
- **1917–1918:** *Ragtime for Eleven Instruments*. Chamber obra engaging with ragtime through an idiosyncratic European ensemble. [K030]
- **1919:** *Piano-Rag-Music*. Solo-piano obra written for Arthur Rubinstein but conceived with intentionally unstable notation and rhythm. [K032]
- **1919:** *Three Pieces for Clarinet Solo*. Three unaccompanied miniatures written for Werner Reinhart. [K033]
- **1920; rescored 1952:** *Concertino*. Single-movement work for string quartet, later transformed for twelve instruments. [K035]
- **1920; revised 1947:** *Symphonies of Wind Instruments*. Ritual one-movement composition for winds, dedicated to Debussy; the 1947 revision is substantially rescored. [K036]
- **1921:** *Les cinq doigts (The Five Fingers)*. Eight easy piano pieces later orchestrated as *Eight Instrumental Miniatures*. [K037]
- **1921:** *Trois mouvements de Pétrouchka*. Virtuoso piano recomposition of three scenes from *Pétrouchka*, not a simple reduction. [K012]
- **1923:** *Octet for Wind Instruments*. Neoclassical chamber work for flute, clarinet, two bassoons, two trumpets and two trombones. [K041]
- **1924:** *Piano Sonata*. Three-movement neoclassical sonata. [K043]
- **1925:** *Serenade in A*. Four-movement piano work conceived around the duration and sides of a gramophone record. [K044]
- **1932:** *Duo concertant*. Five-movement concert work for violin and piano, written for Stravinsky and Dushkin. [K054]
- **1936–1937:** *Preludium for Jazz Ensemble*. Short band piece, later revised for concert band. [K082]
- **1940:** *Tango*. Piano dance subsequently arranged in several instrumental forms; the compact dance is an autonomous obra, not a book-like “text.” [K062]
- **1943–1944:** *Sonata for Two Pianos*. Four-movement keyboard work originally begun as a solo-piano project. [K067]
- **1944:** *Elegy*. Unaccompanied viola memorial for Alphonse Onnou, playable with an optional divided-string realization. [K072]
- **1947:** *Little Canon*. Tiny contrapuntal memorial for pianist Alexandre Cingria. [K071]
- **1953:** *Septet*. Serially organized chamber work for clarinet, horn, bassoon, piano and string trio. [K080]
- **1955:** *Greeting Prelude*. Orchestral birthday miniature based on the familiar birthday tune; it is an occasional transformation rather than an entirely independent thematic invention. [K085]
- **1959:** *Epitaphium*. Memorial chamber piece for flute, clarinet and harp. [K090]
- **1959:** *Double Canon: Raoul Dufy in Memoriam*. Brief string-quartet memorial. [K092]
- **1964:** *Fanfare for a New Theatre*. Twelve-tone miniature for two trumpets, composed for the opening of New York’s State Theater. [K102]
- **1965:** *Canon on a Popular Russian Tune*. Chamber canon derived from a tune also used in *The Firebird*, but explicitly claimed by Stravinsky as a separate work. [K105]
- **1968:** *Sketches for a Piano Sonata*. Two serial fragments for an unrealized sonata; the project is unfinished and should not be treated as a completed sonata. [K109]

### Vocal, choral and liturgical obras

- **1902:** *The Storm Cloud (Le Nuage)*. Early song for voice and piano after Pushkin; sources disagree over whether an even earlier piano piece bearing this title was a separate work, and the latter is lost. [KN03]
- **1904:** *Cantata for Rimsky-Korsakov’s Sixtieth Birthday*. Cantata for chorus and piano, performed privately and subsequently lost; its existence is documented, but the musical object cannot presently be reconstructed. [KN04]
- **1904:** *The Mushrooms Going to War*. Juvenile song for bass and piano. [KN05]
- **1906:** *The Driver and the Tarantula*. Song for voice and piano; the score is lost. [KN06]
- **1906–1907:** *Faun and Shepherdess, Op. 2*. Song cycle for mezzo-soprano and orchestra after Pushkin. [K002]
- **1907; arranged 1923 and 1933:** *Pastorale*. Wordless song for soprano and piano, later remade for voice or violin with wind instruments and for violin and piano. [K006]
- **1907–1908:** *Two Melodies, Op. 6*. Two songs for mezzo-soprano and piano on poems by Sergey Gorodetsky. [K004]
- **1910; orchestrated 1951:** *Two Poems of Paul Verlaine, Op. 9*. Two songs for baritone and piano, later made into an orchestral version. [K011]
- **1911; arranged 1954:** *Two Poems of Konstantin Balmont*. Two songs for voice and piano, later rescored for voice and chamber ensemble. [K013]
- **1911–1912:** *Zvezdoliki (The Star-Faced One)*. Cantata for male chorus and very large orchestra after Balmont. [K014]
- **1912–1913:** *Three Japanese Lyrics*. Three songs for voice and chamber ensemble, also performable with piano. [K016]
- **1906–1913; arranged 1930:** *Three Little Songs: Recollections of My Childhood*. Songs for voice and piano, later orchestrated. [K017]
- **1914:** *Pribaoutki*. Four Russian songs for voice and eight instruments, translating folk speech into a highly constructed musical object. [K020]
- **1915–1916:** *Berceuses du chat (Cat’s Cradle Songs)*. Four songs for contralto and three clarinets. [K022]
- **1915–1917:** *Three Tales for Children*. Three songs for voice and piano. [K024]
- **1914–1917:** *Four Russian Peasant Songs*. Four unaccompanied women’s-voice settings, with a later version using four horns. [K028]
- **1917–1918:** *Berceuse*. Short song for voice and piano. [K097]
- **1918–1919:** *Four Russian Songs*. Four songs for voice and piano; two were later incorporated into the chamber-instrument arrangement *Four Songs*. [K031]
- **1926; revised 1949:** *Pater Noster (Otche Nash)*. A cappella Orthodox prayer setting, later adapted from Church Slavonic to Latin. [K046]
- **1930; revised 1948:** *Symphony of Psalms*. Choral symphony for chorus and orchestra, using Latin psalm texts; it is neither conventional symphony nor liturgical service music alone. [K052]
- **1932; revised 1949 and 1964:** *Credo*. A cappella liturgical setting, first in Church Slavonic and subsequently adapted to Latin. [K055]
- **1934; revised 1949:** *Ave Maria*. A cappella prayer setting, likewise recast from Slavonic into Latin. [K057]
- **1937–1938:** *Petit ramusianum harmonique*. Brief vocal tribute to C. F. Ramuz; it is an occasional inscription-like composition rather than a substantial concert work. [KN24]
- **1944:** *Babel*. Cantata for narrator, male chorus and orchestra, written for the collaborative biblical project *Genesis Suite*. [K068]
- **1944–1948:** *Mass*. Latin Mass for mixed chorus and double wind quintet, conceived for liturgical rather than theatrical use. [K077]
- **1951–1952:** *Cantata*. Chamber cantata on anonymous English texts for two soloists, women’s chorus and instrumental ensemble. [K079]
- **1953:** *Three Songs from William Shakespeare*. Song set for mezzo-soprano, flute, clarinet and viola. [K081]
- **1954:** *Four Songs*. Chamber rescoring for voice, flute, harp and guitar of selected earlier Russian songs and children’s tales; it is a new manifestation assembled from older obras. [K083]
- **1954:** *In Memoriam Dylan Thomas*. Dirge-canons and song for tenor, string quartet and four trombones. [K084]
- **1955:** *Canticum Sacrum ad honorem Sancti Marci nominis*. Latin sacred cantata for soloists, chorus and orchestra, structured for performance in St Mark’s, Venice. [K086]
- **1957–1958:** *Threni: id est Lamentationes Jeremiae Prophetae*. Serial setting of the Latin Lamentations for soloists, chorus and orchestra. [K089]
- **1960–1961:** *A Sermon, a Narrative and a Prayer*. Sacred cantata for speakers, soloists, chorus and orchestra. [K095]
- **1962:** *Anthem: The Dove Descending Breaks the Air*. A cappella choral setting of T. S. Eliot. [K096]
- **1962–1963:** *Abraham and Isaac*. Sacred ballad in Hebrew for baritone and orchestra. [K101]
- **1964:** *Elegy for J.F.K.*. Memorial song for low voice and three clarinets on a text by W. H. Auden. [K100]
- **1965:** *Introitus: T. S. Eliot in Memoriam*. Liturgical memorial for male chorus and chamber ensemble. [K104]
- **1965–1966:** *Requiem Canticles*. Condensed serial setting of portions of the Requiem Mass; it is not a complete liturgical requiem. [K106]
- **1966:** *The Owl and the Pussy-Cat*. Song for soprano and piano after Edward Lear, Stravinsky’s final completed original composition. [K107]

### Transformations, orchestrations and arrangements of other makers’ music

- **1908:** *Kobold*. Orchestration of a piano piece by Edvard Grieg; the act is arrangement, not original thematic composition. [KN07]
- **1908:** *Nocturne and Valse brillante*. Orchestrations of piano music by Frédéric Chopin for a projected ballet context. [KN08]
- **1908:** *Mephisto’s Song of the Flea*. Orchestration of Beethoven’s setting. [KN09]
- **1908:** *Mephisto’s Song of the Flea*. Separate orchestration of Mussorgsky’s setting. [KN10]
- **1908:** *Shaklovity’s Aria and Choral Finale from Khovanshchina*. Orchestral realization of music by Mussorgsky. [KN11]
- **1908:** *Arrangement of a Prelude by Anatoly Lyadov*. Early orchestral arrangement; it should not be mistaken for an independently composed Stravinsky prelude. [KN13]
- **1909:** *Les Sylphides*. Orchestrations of Chopin piano pieces for Diaghilev’s ballet; Stravinsky supplied only part of the collaborative orchestration, so authorship of the complete ballet score is divided. [KN08/associated catalogue supplement]
- **1917:** *Song of the Volga Boatmen*. Arrangement of the Russian folk song for winds and percussion, initially made as a replacement anthem; its function was ceremonial and political as much as compositional. [KN15]
- **1918:** *Choral Prologue from Boris Godunov*. Arrangement of Mussorgsky for chorus and orchestra. [KN18]
- **1919:** *La Marseillaise*. Arrangement for solo violin; a functional national-anthem realization rather than a claim to the underlying work. [KN20]
- **1921:** *Two Movements from The Sleeping Beauty*. Arrangement of Tchaikovsky made for Diaghilev’s production. [KN21]
- **1925:** *Suite on Themes, Fragments and Pieces by Pergolesi*. Violin-and-piano transformation of music from *Pulcinella*, made with Samuel Dushkin. [K034]
- **1932–1933:** *Suite italienne*. Cello-and-piano transformation of *Pulcinella*, made with Gregor Piatigorsky; collaborative attribution should be retained. [K034]
- **1934:** *Suite italienne*. Violin-and-piano transformation of *Pulcinella*, made with Samuel Dushkin. [K034]
- **1934:** *Divertimento from The Fairy’s Kiss*. Concert suite drawn from the Tchaikovsky-based ballet and later also arranged for violin and piano. [K049]
- **1941:** *The Star-Spangled Banner*. Orchestral and choral arrangement of the United States anthem; contemporary police action against a performance generated a persistent but inaccurate story that Stravinsky was arrested for the harmonization. [KN26]
- **1941:** *Bluebird Pas-de-deux*. Orchestration and adaptation of music from Tchaikovsky’s *The Sleeping Beauty*. [KN27]
- **1959–1960:** *Monumentum pro Gesualdo di Venosa ad CD annum*. Instrumental recomposition of three madrigals by Carlo Gesualdo; its intervention goes beyond neutral transcription. [K094]
- **1959:** *Tres Sacrae Cantiones*. Completion and realization of three incomplete Gesualdo motets; authorship is necessarily shared and reconstructive. [K093]
- **1960:** *Berceuse*. Arrangement for two recorders of Anne’s lullaby from *The Rake’s Progress*. [K097]
- **1963:** *Canzonetta*. Orchestral arrangement of a piece by Jean Sibelius. [K099]
- **1963:** *Eight Instrumental Miniatures*. Orchestration of the eight piano pieces in *Les cinq doigts*. [K037]
- **1968:** *Two Sacred Songs from Hugo Wolf’s Spanisches Liederbuch*. Instrumental and vocal arrangement of two Wolf songs. [K108]
- **1969:** *Four Preludes and Fugues from The Well-Tempered Clavier*. Orchestral arrangement of four Bach keyboard pieces. [K110]
- **1956:** *Chorale Variations on “Vom Himmel hoch da komm’ ich her.”* Orchestral realization of Bach’s canonical variations; Stravinsky altered textures and supplied additional counterpoint, making it more than literal transcription. [K087]

### Occasional compositions, inscriptions and objects that resist work-status

- **1917:** *Church Prayer for Chorus*. Brief liturgical or occasional setting preserved outside the original numbered catalogue. [KN22]
- **1917:** *Dialogue between Joy and Reason*. Small occasional vocal composition; its private or album-like function makes “work” an uneasy label. [KN23]
- **1939:** *To Mister Paul*. Musical greeting for Paul Sachs, preserved as an occasional inscription rather than a public composition. [KN25]
- **1945:** *Welcome Richard*. Musical greeting for Richard Rodzinski. [KN28]
- **1949:** *Birthday Choral for Balanchine*. Collaborative or occasional birthday composition associated with Balanchine and Stravinsky; its precise work-status is marginal. [KN29]
- **1951:** *Happy Birthday for Mary Curtis*. Private occasional composition based on a pre-existing birthday tune. [KN30]
- **1957:** *To Paul Hindemith*. Brief musical tribute or inscription, not a conventionally published concert obra. [KN31]
- **1959:** *À Marcel Dupré*. Musical greeting associated with Bernard Gavoty and Marcel Dupré. [KN32]
- **1960:** *Succès, triomphe au Roi David, mon cher Milhaud*. Musical congratulation to Darius Milhaud; it is an inscribed act of friendship more readily than an autonomous work. [KN33]

### Spoken and written obras

- **1935–1936:** *Chroniques de ma vie*. Two-volume autobiography produced with substantial editorial and drafting help from Walter Nouvel; Stravinsky signed it, but scholarship disputes any simple model of solitary authorship.
- **1939–1940; published 1942:** *Poétique musicale sous forme de six leçons*. Six Charles Eliot Norton lectures, composed with assistance from Pierre Souvtchinsky and Alexis Roland-Manuel; it is a poetics or aesthetic manifesto as much as a descriptive book.
- **1959:** *Conversations with Igor Stravinsky*. Dialogic book with Robert Craft; Stravinsky acknowledged that Craft shaped the language, so it should be catalogued as collaborative conversation rather than unqualified sole authorship.
- **1960:** *Memories and Commentaries*. Collaborative memoir-and-interview volume with Robert Craft.
- **1962:** *Expositions and Developments*. Collaborative volume of recollection, criticism and position-taking with Robert Craft.
- **1963:** *Dialogues and a Diary*. Collaborative book with Robert Craft; later editions titled *Dialogues* omit the diary, an editorial change rather than a new Stravinsky obra.
- **1966:** *Themes and Episodes*. Collaborative collection with Robert Craft.
- **1969:** *Retrospectives and Conclusions*. Collaborative collection with Robert Craft.
- **1959–1969; revised 1971, published 1972:** *Themes and Conclusions*. Stravinsky-authorized revision and consolidation of *Themes and Episodes* and *Retrospectives and Conclusions*; published posthumously, it is simultaneously a new editorial object and a remanifestation of earlier collaborative texts.
- **Letters written throughout his life; selected editions published posthumously:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters are made texts but not a single self-defined obra; editions such as Robert Craft’s *Selected Correspondence* are editorial creations whose completeness and selection belong to the editor, not Stravinsky.

### Recorded manifestations and performance-made objects

- **1920s–1971:** *Commercial recordings conducted or played by Stravinsky*. The standard discography records his commercially released performances as conductor or pianist, together with some Robert Craft performances made under his supervision. These are manifestations and interpretive acts—not additional compositions—and individual releases frequently repackage the same sessions.
- **1950s–1960s:** *Stravinsky Conducts Stravinsky / The Recorded Legacy*. Columbia’s systematic recording project, later issued in multi-disc collections, sought to preserve composer-supervised readings of most of the oeuvre; it is best treated as a body of recorded manifestations rather than one musical work.
- **1921–1929:** *Composer-made and composer-rewritten pianola rolls*. Stravinsky prepared rolls of *Étude pour pianola*, *Pulcinella*, *The Rite of Spring*, *Piano-Rag-Music*, *Ragtime*, *Pétrouchka*, *Le Chant du rossignol*, *Three Tales for Children*, *Four Russian Songs*, *Concertino*, *Les Noces* and *The Firebird*. Each is a mechanically encoded performance-version whose status lies between arrangement, recording and executable protocol.
