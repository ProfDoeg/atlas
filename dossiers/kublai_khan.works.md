## Works by Kublai Khan

### Authored poem

- **Date unknown, before 1294:** *陟玩春山紀興 (Inspiration Recorded while Ascending and Enjoying Spring Mountain).* A regulated Chinese verse (*qilü*) attributed to Kublai and the sole poem by him preserved in the standard anthology *Yuan shi xuan*; its occasion is traditionally associated with a spring visit to a Buddhist sanctuary, but its date is not securely established. ([study of Yuan imperial poetry](https://www.sinoss.net/uploadfile/2010/1130/5061.pdf))

### Edicts and manifesto-like acts

- **1261:** *Edict of Qubilai Khan (Qub1).* A Mongolian imperial command in Uyghur-Mongolian script. It is a chancery production issued in Kublai’s voice rather than an autograph composition, so its “authorship” is institutional and performative. ([Historical Corpus of the Mongolian Language](https://mongoliancorpus.org/sources))

- **1268:** *Edict of Qubilai Khan (Qub2), commonly called the Shaolin Edict.* A Mongolian imperial command preserved as an inscription, granting protections or privileges to Buddhist clerics; as a *jarliq*, it acts rather than merely describes and therefore fits “manifesto” or legal instrument better than “literary work.” ([Historical Corpus](https://mongoliancorpus.org/sources); [Vogel and Theobald, showing and discussing the inscription](https://publikationen.uni-tuebingen.de/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10900/151266/Vogel-Theobald_%20Marco-Polo_OA.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1))

- **1269:** *Edict promulgating the new state script.* A manifesto-like decree that made the writing system designed by the Tibetan preceptor ’Phags-pa an imperial script intended for the empire’s several languages. The script itself was Phagpa’s creation at Kublai’s commission; Kublai made the operative decree, not the letterforms. ([Max Planck Research Library](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/studies/10/18/index.html))

- **18 December 1271:** *Proclamation of the dynastic name “Great Yuan” (大元).* A constitutive imperial proclamation: it did not describe an existing object so much as bring a named polity into official existence, and thus is best classified as a manifesto or sovereign speech act. The transmitted wording is chancery text, not demonstrably Kublai’s autograph prose. ([*Yuan shi*, Shizu annals, via Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/wiki.pl?if=en&res=966494); [Morris Rossabi, *Khubilai Khan: His Life and Times*](https://www.fulcrum.org/concern/monographs/r781wg644?locale=en))

- **1274:** *興師征南詔 (Edict Mobilizing the Army for the Southern Campaign).* An imperial war proclamation against the Southern Song, preserved in the official administrative compilation *Yuan dianzhang* and, with textual differences, in the *Yuan shi*. Those variants make it unsafe to treat either transmitted version as Kublai’s own fixed wording. ([*Yuan dianzhang* record and textual comparison](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%BF%BD%E5%BF%85%E7%83%88))

- **1277 or 1289:** *Edicts of Khubilai Khan I–III (Qub5, Qub3, Qub6).* Three catalogued ’Phags-pa-script imperial documents whose dating remains unresolved between the corresponding cyclical years 1277 and 1289. They resist treatment as three personally written “works”: they are separate chancery monuments issued under Kublai’s authority, and the corpus presently lists their editions as forthcoming. ([Historical Corpus](https://www.mongoliancorpus.org/sources); [Tumurtogoo and Cecegdari, *Mongolian Monuments in ’Phags-pa Script*](https://altaica.ru/LIBRARY/Tumurtogoo_phags_pa2010.pdf))

- **1280 or 1292:** *Edict of Khubilai Khan IV (TumP5).* A Sino-Mongolian inscription in ’Phags-pa script, conventionally dated to one of two possible repetitions of its cyclical date. Ligeti catalogued the monument as an edict of Kublai but retained the same chronological uncertainty. ([Historical Corpus](https://www.mongoliancorpus.org/sources); [Louis Ligeti, *Monuments en écriture ’phags-pa*](https://real-eod.mtak.hu/18085/1/AkademiaiKiado_002847.pdf))

### Commissioned scripts, protocols, and scientific systems

- **1260–1269:** *’Phags-pa or Mongolian Square Script.* A projected universal imperial writing system commissioned by Kublai and designed by Drogön Chögyal Phagpa. It is not properly Kublai’s authored text: his obra here is the project and its official implementation, while the graphic system is Phagpa’s design. ([Max Planck Research Library](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/studies/10/18/index.html); [Tumurtogoo and Cecegdari](https://altaica.ru/LIBRARY/Tumurtogoo_phags_pa2010.pdf))

- **1276–1281:** *Shoushi Calendar (授時曆, “Season-Granting Calendar”).* A calendrical and astronomical protocol ordered by Kublai, calculated principally by Guo Shoujing and Wang Xun, and formally adopted in 1281. It belongs to Kublai’s commissioned state programme, not to his personal authorship. ([Columbia University, “Mongol Support for Science”](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/mongols/pop/china/astro_pop.htm); [Guo Shoujing biographical account](https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/news-wires-white-papers-and-books/guo-shoujing))

### Cities and architectural projects

- **1256–1259:** *Kaiping, later Shangdu (Xanadu).* A new capital-city project commissioned by Kublai and designed by his adviser Liu Bingzhong, combining a Chinese axial walled city with an adjoining Mongolian pastoral and encampment landscape. It is an imperial obra approaching a *Gesamtkunstwerk*, but not a building personally designed by Kublai. ([UNESCO, Site of Xanadu](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1389); [UNESCO nomination assessment](https://whc.unesco.org/document/152541))

- **1260–1264:** *Golden memorial stupa for Sakya Pandita at Sakya Monastery.* Kublai ordered Phagpa to build this reliquary monument; the young Newar artist Anige directed its execution. The attribution is consequently divided among commissioner, religious designer, and maker, and the object should not be assigned to Kublai without that qualification. ([Project Himalayan Art, Anige and Kublai’s stupa commissions](https://rubinmuseum.org/projecthimalayanart/essays/white-stupa-attributed-to-nepalese-artist-anige/?relatedview=all))

- **1264–1293:** *Dadu or Khanbaliq (Great Capital).* Kublai ordered Liu Bingzhong to plan a new capital northeast of the ruined Jin capital; construction began in the 1260s and continued through 1293, with Liu, Guo Shoujing, Ikhtiyar al-Din, and numerous artisans responsible for its actual design and execution. The city is therefore Kublai’s project and political manifestation, not his sole architectural work. ([“Ancient Chinese Capital Models,” *PLOS ONE*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5743849/); [Nancy Shatzman Steinhardt, *Chinese Imperial City Planning*](https://upcommons.upc.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0649bdcd-5b69-4a97-b0b6-b887df3bc3c9/content))

- **1270:** *Temple to the Northern Peak, Quyang.* An imperially sponsored temple construction executed to established Chinese architectural standards during Kublai’s reign. Evidence places it within his building programme, but does not support calling him its designer. ([Nancy Shatzman Steinhardt, “Eurasian Impacts on the Yuan Observatory in Haocheng”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/eurasian-influences-on-yuan-china/eurasian-impacts-on-the-yuan-observatory-in-haocheng/020F577B0F3AE4FE330E3EBEAE0570F8))

- **1271–1279:** *White Stupa of the Great Sage Longevity and Myriad Peace Monastery, Dadu.* A monumental Tibetan-style reliquary commissioned under Kublai’s patronage, designed and supervised by Anige, with its iconographic and relic programme probably devised by an imperial Tibetan preceptor. It survives, but its status as “made by Kublai” is strictly that of commissioned political-religious project. ([Project Himalayan Art](https://rubinmuseum.org/projecthimalayanart/essays/white-stupa-attributed-to-nepalese-artist-anige/?relatedview=all); [study of Kublai and the White Stupa](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArtiId=ART001384795))

- **1279 onward:** *Guanxingtai Observatory at Haocheng.* A brick astronomical observation platform begun under Kublai as part of the state calendar programme and associated with Guo Shoujing. It is an instrument-building and architectural project rather than a personally designed work. ([Steinhardt, “Eurasian Impacts on the Yuan Observatory in Haocheng”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/eurasian-influences-on-yuan-china/eurasian-impacts-on-the-yuan-observatory-in-haocheng/020F577B0F3AE4FE330E3EBEAE0570F8))

- **During Kublai’s reign, date uncertain:** *Yuan astronomical observatory at Dadu.* Kublai ordered an observatory for instruments brought by the Persian astronomer Jamal al-Din; the original complex is lost and the familiar Beijing structure is a later reconstruction. This is best treated as a commissioned scientific institution and architectural project, not as Kublai’s handiwork. ([Columbia University, “The Beijing Observatory”](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/mongols/pop/china/astro_pop.htm))

### Hydraulic and transport projects

- **1281–1283:** *Jizhou Canal.* A state waterway project constructed during Kublai’s reign as part of the reconfiguration of the north–south grain-transport network. Its inclusion here records imperial authorization, not personal engineering authorship. ([Pierre-Louis Viollet, *Water Engineering in Ancient Civilizations*](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/48060/1/159.pdf))

- **1289:** *Huitong Canal.* A canal shortcut ordered by Kublai to improve grain transport toward Dadu and executed through the Yuan engineering administration. It is an imperial infrastructure project whose practical design is associated with officials and engineers rather than the khan himself. ([“The Controversy over Grain Conveyance during the Reign of Qubilai Qaqan,” *Journal of Asian Studies*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/abs/controversy-over-grain-conveyance-during-the-reign-of-qubilai-qaqan-126094/D055D2105C693953BD91B89707C290B8); [Viollet](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/48060/1/159.pdf))

- **1291–1293:** *Tonghui Canal.* A canal and lock system proposed and engineered by Guo Shoujing, ordered and mobilized by Kublai, linking Dadu with Tongzhou and the Grand Canal. Kublai named the completed waterway, but Guo was its designer; the work therefore belongs to Kublai’s programme only in the patronal and sovereign sense. ([“The Controversy over Grain Conveyance”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-asian-studies/article/abs/controversy-over-grain-conveyance-during-the-reign-of-qubilai-qaqan-126094/D055D2105C693953BD91B89707C290B8); [Viollet](https://ndl.ethernet.edu.et/bitstream/123456789/48060/1/159.pdf))
