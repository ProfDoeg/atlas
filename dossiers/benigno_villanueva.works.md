## Works by Benigno Benjamín Villanueva

### Correspondence of uncertain survival and attribution

- *Three untitled letters to General Juan Prim y Prats*. Claudio Morales Gorleri reports receiving copies of three letters in which Villanueva expressed sympathy for Russia during the Crimean War, but supplies neither dates, archival shelf marks, facsimiles, nor a critical transcription; they are therefore reported private correspondence, not securely established works. ([Morales Gorleri, 2015](https://revistacruzdelsur.ar/Numeros_011-020/RHCZDS-01101-Morales_Gorleri-El_condor.pdf))

- **1858:** Untitled letter to his mother, Rafaela Lozada y Reyes de Villanueva. Morales Gorleri reports a copy dated in Moscow but does not reproduce or identify the original; Pastor Obligado had earlier asserted more generally that Villanueva wrote to his mother from Moscow. Its survival, contents, and attribution remain unverified. ([Morales Gorleri, 2015](https://revistacruzdelsur.ar/Numeros_011-020/RHCZDS-01101-Morales_Gorleri-El_condor.pdf); [Obligado, 1903, pp. 142–150](https://archive.org/details/tradicionesargen00obli))

- **1859:** Untitled letter to his mother, Rafaela Lozada y Reyes de Villanueva. Like the 1858 letter, this is known only from Morales Gorleri’s statement that he received a copy; without an identified original or critical edition, it resists treatment as a securely extant text. ([Morales Gorleri, 2015](https://revistacruzdelsur.ar/Numeros_011-020/RHCZDS-01101-Morales_Gorleri-El_condor.pdf))

### Commercial project

- **After 1847:** Grocery business in California. The received biography says that Villanueva opened a provisions shop for Gold Rush migrants and accumulated a modest fortune; this is a commercial project rather than a titled obra, and its documentation is entangled with the larger disputed life story. ([Obligado, 1903, pp. 142–150](https://archive.org/details/tradicionesargen00obli); [Morales Gorleri, 2015](https://revistacruzdelsur.ar/Numeros_011-020/RHCZDS-01101-Morales_Gorleri-El_condor.pdf))

### Attributed military practice

- **Crimean War, 1853–1856:** Adaptation of the lasso, bolas, ambushes, and Río de la Plata cavalry practices for Russian troops. This would be an embodied tactical protocol taught through practice, not a book or known written manual; it is attributed by Obligado and elaborated by Morales Gorleri, but the Russian phase of Villanueva’s career has not been independently substantiated. ([Obligado, 1903, pp. 142–150](https://archive.org/details/tradicionesargen00obli); [Morales Gorleri, 2015](https://revistacruzdelsur.ar/Numeros_011-020/RHCZDS-01101-Morales_Gorleri-El_condor.pdf); [Sizonenko, 1995, bibliographic record and summary](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%B8%D0%BB%D1%8C%D1%8F%D0%BD%D1%83%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%B0%2C_%D0%91%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BD%D0%BE_%D0%91%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8%D0%BD))

### No securely established oeuvre

- No standard bibliography, catalogue raisonné, or collected-works edition for Villanueva has been identified, and the biographical literature attributes no books, articles, manifestos, protocols, artworks, buildings, musical compositions, or formally titled creations to him. Even his Russian identity and career are disputed: Isidoro Gilbert treated the story as legend, while historian Aleksandr I. Sizonenko reported finding no documentary confirmation of the Russian episode; consequently, the items above constitute reported traces rather than a verified corpus. ([Gilbert, *El oro de Moscú*](https://books.google.com/books?id=8tB7AAAAMAAJ); [Sizonenko, “Сто лет одиночества аргентинского генерала, который воевал в России,” 1995](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%B8%D0%BB%D1%8C%D1%8F%D0%BD%D1%83%D1%8D%D0%B2%D0%B0%2C_%D0%91%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B3%D0%BD%D0%BE_%D0%91%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%B8%D0%BD))
