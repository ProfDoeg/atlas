# Joshua Milton Blahyi: Research Dossier

## Scope and Evidentiary Method

Joshua Milton Blahyi’s biography is unusually dependent on his own testimony. Several central elements—childhood priesthood, ritual sacrifice, cannibalism, supernatural protection, intimacy with President Samuel Doe, the number of people killed, and a visionary conversion—were publicized by Blahyi himself after he became an evangelist.

This dossier uses the following labels:

- **Documented fact:** established by official records, contemporaneous photographs or recordings, institutional documentation, or mutually corroborating sources.
- **Reported fact:** reported by an identifiable journalist, witness, participant, or institution but not independently established here.
- **Self-reported claim:** stated by Blahyi in testimony, interviews, sermons, or books.
- **Allegation:** accusation by an identified person or source that has not resulted in a judicial finding.
- **Disputed claim:** materially contradicted by another identifiable source or rendered doubtful by contrary evidence.
- **Unverified claim:** repeated in available sources without adequate independent confirmation.
- **Myth or legend:** supernatural, folkloric, propagandistic, or symbolic material historically important to his reputation but not empirically established.
- **Finding:** conclusion formally reached by an official body.
- **Unresolved:** evidence is inadequate to choose among competing versions.

No Liberian or international criminal court is known to have convicted Blahyi of a war crime or crime against humanity. His admissions are evidence that he made the statements, not independent proof of every act, victim count, or supernatural explanation in them.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and the circumstances of his birth

Blahyi has said both parents belonged to the Sarpo-Krahn community of Kabedeh District, Sinoe County. His ministry’s former biographical material asserted that his birth was “traditionally arranged” to produce a successor to his father’s priestly position.

At his 2008 TRC hearing, Blahyi offered a more elaborate genealogy:

- His father was supposedly the intended successor to a priesthood but had become educated and wealthy and was therefore considered unsuitable.
- An older son, Benedict, was allegedly rejected as insufficiently grounded in traditional culture.
- Elders consequently required Blahyi’s father to have a son with a woman from the relevant tribal community.
- Blahyi said that both his future parents left existing marriages to marry one another and conceive him.

**Status:** self-reported claim. No birth record, parental testimony, or independent ethnographic documentation was located that confirms this succession narrative.

A more recent sympathetic profile says Blahyi’s father was a powerful traditional priest known, among other things, for helping infertile women. This account says Blahyi therefore had many siblings. It substantially relies on interviews with Blahyi and should not be treated as independent verification.

### Siblings and extended family

Identified relatives include:

- **Benedict:** described by Blahyi as an older son of his father who was rejected for priestly succession.
- **Harrison Challar:** identified in later accounts as a half-brother who disputed Blahyi’s childhood-priest narrative.
- **Ernest Nelson:** one of several half-brothers; photographed or observed driving Blahyi in Monrovia during Damon Tabor’s reporting.
- **Emmanuel:** a teenage nephew who served as a general assistant or factotum.
- Numerous unnamed distant relatives in Sinoe County who reportedly donated land to Blahyi’s missionary project.

Harrison Challar’s reported counter-account is important. He said Blahyi was never a priest; instead, after leaving school he sold Kool-Aid and chicken soup in a market, then became involved in drug trafficking and robbery.

**Status:** reported family contradiction. The public record available here does not resolve the dispute.

### Marriage and children

Blahyi is reported to be married to a woman described as a pastor. Public biographical accounts name four children:

- Michaela
- Joshua Milton Jr.
- Janice Marva
- Jackie MaryBeth

At the 2008 hearing he said he was living in Ghana “with my family.” His wife and family, he told the TRC, had agreed that proceeds from a book would be placed in an account to help people seeking rehabilitation.

No reliable public documentation was located concerning:

- his wife’s full name;
- the date or place of marriage;
- previous marriages or partners;
- the children’s birth dates;
- legal ownership of family property.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1971–1989

### Birth and education

Blahyi was born on 30 September 1971, reportedly in Monrovia. He told the TRC that he left formal schooling after the **third grade**. His ministry’s biography says he was removed from primary school at seven to undergo five years of religious training.

No school records have been located. He had little formal education and later developed his public career primarily through oral preaching, testimony, charisma, religious patronage, and autobiographical narration.

### Claimed initiation as a priest

Blahyi’s principal version is that:

1. At approximately seven, his father surrendered him to Krahn elders.
2. He underwent several years of training and initiation.
3. At eleven, in 1982, he became priest for the Krahn people.
4. He was allegedly the youngest person ever to hold the office; the next-youngest incumbent, he told the TRC, had been fifty-seven.
5. As priest he provided tribal protection, divination, ritual markings, and sacrifice.
6. A deity identified in various spellings as **Nyanbe-un-weh**, **nya-na-o-weh**, or a similar form selected sacrificial victims through visions.
7. Elders supposedly kidnapped children after he identified their family names.
8. Sacrifice occurred monthly during his priesthood.

He told the TRC that his period of ritual activity ran from **1982 to 1996**.

**Status:** self-reported claim, strongly disputed.

### Evidence against or limiting the priesthood narrative

Damon Tabor interviewed specialists for *The New Yorker*:

- Social anthropologist **David Brown**, who had worked in Liberia since the 1970s, said he knew of no secret society corresponding to Blahyi’s description.
- Other unnamed experts reportedly found the account implausible; one called it “ludicrous.”
- Half-brother Harrison Challar reportedly denied that Blahyi had ever been a priest.

The public record does establish the importance of ritual protection, secret societies, sacred objects, charms, and spiritual explanations during Liberia’s wars. It does **not** independently establish that an eleven-year-old Blahyi was high priest of the entire Krahn population or that he conducted thousands of prewar sacrifices.

A further problem is arithmetic. Blahyi said monthly sacrifices began at eleven and that his killings over the entire 1982–1996 period exceeded 20,000. Monthly sacrifice by itself could not remotely yield that number; his estimate therefore also attributed deaths to military operations, orders, subordinates, or a broader sphere of responsibility. He never supplied a verifiable victim list.

### Early criminality

The alternative family account holds that, after leaving school, Blahyi worked in a market before entering drug dealing and robbery.

**Status:** reported claim by a relative; no court or police records located.

## Education and Formation

### Formal education

Blahyi’s formal education apparently ended in the third grade. No later accredited schooling is documented.

### Claimed traditional teachers and associates

At the TRC he named:

- **Old Man Jaswen/Jeswin:** another traditional priest who allegedly returned to perform fortification rites in 1990 and did not return.
- **Uzeo Barway**
- **Yonplu Tele:** described with Barway as traditional warriors of different rank.
- Unnamed Krahn elders who allegedly initiated and trained him.

These relationships are known principally through Blahyi’s testimony.

### Christian mentors

The most consequential Christian relationship was with **Bishop John Kun Kun**—also rendered Konkon—of the City of Light Church of God in Monrovia.

In the conversion narrative recorded in *The Redemption of General Butt Naked*:

- Kun Kun visited Blahyi at his barracks during the 1996 fighting.
- Blahyi was disturbed that the bishop reached him without being detected.
- Blahyi blamed his bodyguard David Johnson and shot Johnson in the legs.
- Kun Kun nevertheless continued the religious intervention.
- Blahyi later identified Kun Kun as his “spiritual father.”

Blahyi also credited prayer warriors from **Soul Winning/Soul Leading Ministry** and a crusade called **Operation Destroy Egypt**. He said sixty-four days of fasting and prayer freed him from the evil spirit controlling him.

**Status:** the conversion relationship with Kun Kun is reported by Blahyi, Kun Kun, the filmmakers, and observers; supernatural elements remain matters of belief.

## Political and Military Background

Blahyi’s career arose from the breakdown of Samuel Doe’s regime and the First Liberian Civil War.

### Samuel Doe connection

Doe, a Krahn master sergeant, seized power in the coup of 12 April 1980. Blahyi later claimed:

- he served Doe as a traditional protector or spiritual adviser;
- Doe was one of his “sub-priests”;
- Blahyi and others installed a sacred altar, pot, or ritual object in the Executive Mansion;
- cabinet officials paid homage knowingly or unknowingly;
- ritualized food placed officials under the deity’s influence;
- he attempted to protect Doe spiritually when Charles Taylor’s rebellion began.

At the TRC, Blahyi said that he, Jaswen, Barway, and Tele were summoned in 1989 and came to Monrovia in 1990 to support Doe because Doe was Krahn.

**Status:** self-reported claim. The ethnic-political alignment is historically plausible, but no Doe-regime document or independent witness located here verifies Blahyi’s alleged office, altar, rituals, or influence over the cabinet.

### After Doe’s death

Doe was captured and killed by Prince Johnson’s INPFL forces in September 1990. Blahyi said Doe’s death threw the Krahn community into disarray and exile. He described his ensuing mission as protecting Krahn people and marking Krahn soldiers spiritually.

Krahn and Mandingo opponents of Charles Taylor later organized the **United Liberation Movement of Liberia for Democracy (ULIMO)**. Blahyi said his influence was initially limited because the organization was multiethnic and Mandingo leaders held authority.

When ULIMO split:

- **ULIMO-K** aligned with Alhaji Kromah and was predominantly Mandingo.
- **ULIMO-J** aligned with Roosevelt Johnson and drew much of its support from Krahn communities.

Blahyi joined ULIMO-J, where his claimed religious and military authority became more important.

## Chronological Life History

## 1989–1993: Entry into the Civil War

Charles Taylor’s National Patriotic Front of Liberia invaded from Côte d’Ivoire in December 1989. Blahyi’s exact location and conduct during the first months are not independently reconstructed.

He said that in 1990 he came to Monrovia with traditional specialists to support Doe and provide protective markings for Krahn soldiers. After Doe’s death he remained engaged in protecting Krahn interests and ultimately became associated with ULIMO.

No reliable evidence was located that he held a conventional commissioned rank. “General” was a militia title and nom de guerre rather than proof of appointment in a regular army.

## 1993–1995: ULIMO-J Commander

Blahyi became a field commander within ULIMO-J. At the TRC he said political instructions came directly from **Roosevelt Johnson**, while a commander he called **General Lincoln** provided military direction. He also named Aaron Kiede, Michael Kooler, and Abraham Tooted as superiors, although he emphasized that operationally he was often “a commander on my own.”

Independent scholar Nicholai Lidow later characterized him as a **platoon-size commander**, probably commanding no more than approximately forty fighters and active for roughly three years. Former commander Mohammed Toure likewise described Blahyi as a notorious killer but rejected the idea that he could have caused 20,000 deaths.

### Naked warfare

Blahyi said clothing interfered with immediate access to spiritual power. During house-to-house fighting in Monrovia he therefore fought naked, normally retaining shoes and weapons. Combat photographs document him and naked or minimally dressed fighters in 1996.

The nakedness served several possible functions:

- belief in magical protection;
- ritual preparation;
- intimidation;
- removal of ordinary identity and inhibitions;
- unit branding;
- production of a fearsome public reputation.

Blahyi’s fighters have been described as children or adolescents, although not every member was a child. Some accounts say fighters wore women’s clothing or wigs; such dress occurred among several Liberian militias and should not automatically be attributed to Blahyi’s unit in every photograph.

### Drugs and conditioning

Blahyi has described intoxicating fighters and training children not to fear death. In the documentary account, he said he used Hollywood films to persuade child soldiers that death was not final: an actor killed in one film could appear alive in another.

**Status:** self-reported and reported by filmmakers. The practice is consistent with broader documentation of coercion, drugs, theatrical identity, and magical protection among Liberian armed groups, but individual incidents remain incompletely documented.

### Human sacrifice and cannibalism

Blahyi repeatedly stated that before or during operations he:

- selected and killed victims, including children;
- divided or consumed hearts;
- drank blood;
- used body parts in rituals;
- allowed or ordered followers to rape;
- performed sacrifices to maintain supernatural power.

At the TRC he said faction leaders sometimes negotiated the number of victims and demanded secrecy because public knowledge would damage ULIMO-J politically. He named Roosevelt Johnson and General Lincoln in this context.

**Status:** admissions/self-reported claims. Their general gravity is consistent across his statements, but the scale, frequency, particular victims, chain of command, and ritual mechanics are unverified. Roosevelt Johnson and other accused leaders did not receive a judicial trial on these specific assertions.

## 1995: Bomi, Cape Mount, ECOMOG and ULIMO Conflict

Blahyi testified about an episode in which ECOMOG personnel or agents allegedly induced children to steal ULIMO-J weapons in exchange for rice and money. He said ULIMO-K later disarmed ECOMOG personnel near Lofa Bridge, after which fighting broke out between ULIMO-J and ECOMOG.

He described ULIMO-J fighters:

- ambushing an ECOMOG truck;
- taking uniforms;
- disguising themselves as ECOMOG troops;
- using the disguise to gain access to personnel concealed in a women’s sacred bush;
- taking captives toward the Guthrie area.

He referred to fighting or massacres around Robertsport, Madoma, Cape Mount and a settlement near the Guthrie bridge. His answers were often unclear about which force committed which killings. He denied or deflected responsibility for some massacre scenes, saying enemy forces had struck and withdrawn before his arrival.

**Status:** self-reported operational narrative with unresolved attribution. The transcript alone is insufficient to reconstruct the casualties.

## March–April 1996: Roosevelt Johnson Crisis and the Battle of Monrovia

In March 1996, transitional authorities attempted to arrest or disarm ULIMO-J leader Roosevelt Johnson after a murder accusation. Blahyi said:

- rival fighters tried to divide Krahn support away from Johnson;
- Johnson’s house was searched;
- Blahyi and associates refused to surrender him;
- they locked Johnson and General Dugbah in a room to prevent voluntary surrender;
- violence escalated after an ambush and the death of a man called Dweh;
- Blahyi travelled by canoe from West Point to Robertsport and returned overnight with arms;
- full fighting began on **6 April 1996**.

The April 6 crisis brought factional fighting into Monrovia. Blahyi’s naked combat and armed command are contemporaneously documented. An Associated Press photograph dated **15 May 1996** shows him outside Barclay Training Center threatening another combatant with a knife.

Blahyi said he continued human sacrifices during the battle. His command became popularly known as the Naked Base Commandos.

### Strength and responsibility

Later publicity often called Blahyi one of Liberia’s major warlords. Lidow’s research suggests a smaller position: a localized commander with perhaps forty men, subordinate to Roosevelt Johnson’s faction.

This does not negate participation in serious crimes. It does undermine descriptions portraying him as the commander of an army large enough to kill one-tenth of all Liberian war dead.

## 1996: Conversion and Abandonment of Fighting

Blahyi dates his conversion to 1996, shortly after or during the April fighting.

### Vision narrative

He told the TRC that, after killing a child and dividing the victim’s heart, he saw a light brighter than the sun. Jesus allegedly addressed him as “my son” and asked why he lived as a slave when he was meant to be a king.

He said that afterward:

- his pistol would not fire;
- a rocket launcher would not fire;
- three AK-47 bullets struck or approached him without wounding him;
- fear displaced his confidence;
- he abandoned the battlefield;
- Christian prayer workers came to him;
- sixty-four days of fasting and prayer ended the possessing spirit’s control.

Other versions emphasize Bishop John Kun Kun’s visit to the barracks. The precise sequence varies between tellings.

**Status:** documented conversion claim; supernatural explanation is religious testimony, not independently verifiable.

### Shooting of David “Senegalese” Johnson

Blahyi’s former bodyguard **David Johnson**, nicknamed “Senegalese,” reportedly failed to detect Bishop Kun Kun’s approach. Blahyi shot him in both legs and prevented timely treatment. Johnson’s legs were amputated. Years later Blahyi sought his forgiveness. Johnson died of tuberculosis, and the documentary ends with Blahyi at his grave.

**Status:** reported fact based on the documentary’s interviews with participants.

### Disbandment

Blahyi’s fighters were left without the food, shelter, or command structure the unit had provided. Former members Emmette and Eugene Gray later described becoming refugees, joining opposing forces during the second civil war, undergoing incomplete rehabilitation, homelessness, addiction, and crime.

## 1997–2003: Early Evangelism, Ghana and Renewed War

By August 1997 journalists were reporting that Blahyi had become a preacher. He said he remained out of subsequent fighting.

At the TRC he described:

- worship or work with the Ark of Jesus Christ in 1998;
- a false radio report that he had resumed fighting;
- refusal to leave Liberia in a UN vehicle;
- going on radio and to Kiss FM to demand a correction;
- an approach in Ghana from “Seckou Sacker,” who allegedly offered money, a jeep, and a position with LURD; Blahyi said he refused.

**Status:** self-reported. No independent documentation of the alleged LURD offer was located.

### End Time Train Evangelistic Ministries

Blahyi founded or helped found **End Time Train Evangelistic Ministries Inc.** in Ghana in 1999. His former ministry material described evangelistic travel in:

- Ghana
- Togo
- Benin
- Nigeria
- Cameroon
- Equatorial Guinea
- Gabon
- Côte d’Ivoire
- Guinea
- Sierra Leone
- Kenya
- Liberia
- Chile

The Chile claim is unusual and not independently corroborated. At the TRC he personally named Cameroon, Nigeria, Equatorial Guinea, Guinea, Sierra Leone, Ghana and Liberia.

He did not initially describe himself as a settled congregational pastor. In 2008 he told commissioners: “I do not have a congregation, I am a crusader.”

### Sacred objects and national peace

Blahyi claimed that sacred flags and seals had been planted on Putu Mountain and that he uprooted or transferred them to a church in Ghana in 2003, after which Liberia experienced peace.

**Status:** mythic/self-reported causal claim. Liberia’s peace followed military exhaustion, rebel pressure on Charles Taylor, international intervention, Taylor’s departure, and the Accra peace process; no evidence establishes that ritual objects caused it.

## 2004–2007: Return, Public Ministry and Rehabilitation Work

Blahyi said he had not visited Liberia’s Executive Mansion after 2004. He returned to Liberia regularly for preaching and reconciliation work, though periods of residence in Ghana continued.

In **2007** he founded **Journeys Against Violence (JAV)**, intended to rehabilitate former fighters, street youth and people with drug addiction. One residence was established in Chocolate City, a Monrovia suburb.

JAV imposed rules including:

- no drugs;
- no alcohol;
- no sex;
- no fighting;
- daily prayer;
- participation in work or training.

Programs reportedly included driving instruction, bricklaying, farming, and residential support.

## 15 January 2008: Truth and Reconciliation Commission Testimony

Blahyi travelled from Ghana to testify publicly at the Centennial Memorial Pavilion in Monrovia. The hearing was broadcast on Liberian radio and television.

### Admissions

He admitted or claimed:

- priestly activity from 1982 to 1996;
- spiritual support for Doe;
- participation in ULIMO-J;
- command responsibility during the April 6 fighting;
- human sacrifices, including children;
- mass killing;
- ritual activity associated with military operations;
- participation in fighting against ECOMOG;
- knowledge of factional massacres;
- a total responsibility of “not less than” 20,000 deaths;
- willingness, in principle, to face prosecution.

The transcript contains an apparent moment in which “two hundred thousand” was spoken or transcribed and immediately clarified as “20,000.” Subsequent reporting and Blahyi’s own usage consistently cite **20,000**.

He broke down crying and apologized to Liberia. He invoked John 8:32—“the truth shall make you free”—as a reason for testifying.

### Limits of the hearing

The commissioners challenged few of his assertions. They did not require:

- victim names;
- dates for most killings;
- burial locations;
- operational records;
- testimony from subordinates;
- forensic corroboration;
- a coherent reconciliation of his small unit with the death estimate.

Blahyi also said he had been told his TRC statements could not be used to initiate legal action against him. Nevertheless, he stated that he would repeat them if brought before a court.

### TRC finding and recommendation

The TRC’s 2009 final report:

- listed “Joshua Milton Blayir” among persons it regarded as responsible;
- nevertheless placed him among those **not recommended for prosecution**;
- said the exempted individuals had cooperated, admitted crimes, spoken truthfully and expressed remorse.

This was a commission recommendation, not a criminal judgment establishing every confession, not an acquittal, and not necessarily a legally operative amnesty. A 2009 US diplomatic cable noted that Blahyi’s exemption, despite the atrocities attributed to him, produced questions about the commission’s consistency.

## 2008–2011: Forgiveness Campaign and Documentary Production

Filmmakers **Eric Strauss** and **Daniele Anastasion** followed Blahyi for approximately five years. Their film documented encounters with victims and former followers.

Identified encounters included:

- **Praise**, a girl blinded in one eye after being injured by Blahyi when she was a baby;
- David “Senegalese” Johnson, whose legs had been amputated after Blahyi shot him;
- former child soldiers and homeless veterans;
- people who forgave him;
- people who were unable or unwilling to forgive him.

Some encounters were emotionally reciprocal. Others showed visible distress at Blahyi’s arrival or pressure to embrace and forgive him.

Following death threats, Blahyi reportedly fled again to Ghana, at one stage living in or near a refugee community. He later returned to Liberia.

### *The Vice Guide to Liberia*

VICE filmed Blahyi touring territory he once controlled and recounting ritual killing, cannibalism, child-soldier recruitment, and naked warfare. Producer Andy Capper said he remained in contact with Blahyi and occasionally sent him small sums for food because Blahyi was moving in response to threats.

The film greatly expanded Blahyi’s international notoriety, but its sensational framing also amplified unverified claims.

### *The Redemption of General Butt Naked*

The documentary premiered in the US Documentary Competition at the **2011 Sundance Film Festival**. Cinematographers Eric Strauss, Ryan Hill and Peter Hutchens received the festival’s documentary cinematography award.

The film later appeared in broadcast and festival settings, including PBS/Economist Film Project coverage and the Documentary Channel.

## 2012–2016: International Patronage, Books and JAV

### Brenda Weber and American donors

After seeing the documentary, **Brenda Weber**, a Christian pharmacy manager from Illinois, contacted Blahyi through Facebook. In 2012 she created a small nonprofit to support his work.

According to *The New Yorker*:

- Weber sent approximately **US$800 per month** beginning in 2012.
- Roughly half was intended for JAV food and half for Blahyi.
- She later sent another approximately US$300 monthly after hearing residents were missing breakfast.
- She exhausted approximately **US$40,000** in family savings.
- She opened a **US$50,000 line of credit** and sold personal possessions to continue funding.
- Other American Christians also donated.
- Weber, Beverly and Dan Jakubek visited Liberia.
- JAV residents called Weber “Mama Brenda.”

These figures were reported from interviews and observed arrangements, not audited accounts.

### JAV governance and financial allegations

JAV’s administration included several of Blahyi’s relatives. A resident alleged that:

- Blahyi misappropriated money;
- his family controlled the organization;
- beneficiaries were sometimes underfed;
- residents were positioned as props when foreign journalists visited;
- promised employment had not materialized.

Driving-course graduates reportedly lacked the commercial licenses and vehicles needed to convert training into jobs.

Blahyi denied misappropriation. He said JAV was supported by book proceeds, preaching donations and benefactors. He acknowledged failing to fulfill some promises but said the young men lacked judgment and that he knew what was good for them.

**Status:** allegation and denial; no audit, lawsuit, criminal charge, or judicial finding was located.

### Program scale

In 2016 Blahyi told Reuters that JAV had trained approximately **1,000** former combatants and street children since 2007. The organization’s residential population was far smaller: reporting described approximately eighteen residents in 2016 and later accounts gave forty-eight participants in 2017.

The difference may reflect cumulative short-term training versus residential enrollment, but no independently audited totals were found.

Blahyi sought approximately **US$500,000** for further farming and construction training. Liberian reactions ranged from support to suspicion that the program was a publicity or fundraising vehicle.

### Sinoe land project

Distant relatives reportedly donated approximately **3,000 acres** of forest in Sinoe County to Blahyi’s missionary organization. He proposed:

- a medical clinic;
- schools;
- a computer center;
- running water and generators;
- vocational training;
- a Bible college;
- dormitories for 480 former child soldiers;
- accommodation for foreign missionaries.

During Tabor’s visit, the project remained largely forest and village land. No evidence located here establishes that the proposed city, college or large residential complex was completed.

### Personal residence and finances

In the mid-2010s Blahyi lived in a modest mustard-colored house in New Georgia Estate, Monrovia, reportedly without running water and with unreliable electricity. He travelled locally in an SUV driven by a half-brother but did not appear to possess major independently documented wealth.

His financial network included:

- American evangelical donors;
- Facebook contacts;
- preaching donations;
- asserted book income;
- donated or pledged land in Sinoe;
- family members employed by JAV.

No company filings, tax records, bank statements, audited charity accounts, investment portfolios, patents, or substantial commercial businesses were found.

## 2013: Published Memoir

Destiny Image published *The Redemption of an African Warlord: The Joshua Blahyi Story: A Modern Day Conversion from Saul to Paul* in 2013. It repackaged his life as an evangelical conversion testimony.

A foreword by New York pastor and supporter **Bojan Jancic** compared the conversion to that of Saul of Tarsus.

The book’s authorship should be described cautiously. It is published under Blahyi’s name and presents his story in the first person, but available catalogues do not clarify whether editorial or ghostwriting assistance shaped the English text.

## 2016–2024: Public Debate over Redemption and Accountability

Blahyi repeatedly said he was prepared to appear before a future war-crimes court. At other moments he questioned the usefulness and cost of international prosecutions, arguing after Charles Taylor’s conviction that funds spent on trials could instead assist victims.

These positions are not strictly incompatible—he could accept personal trial while criticizing the international model—but they reveal tension between restorative and punitive conceptions of justice.

Victims and justice advocates continued to argue that Christian conversion could not substitute for criminal accountability. Other Liberians regarded public confession, rehabilitation work and forgiveness as meaningful in a country where many more powerful commanders had not admitted responsibility.

In May 2024 President Joseph Boakai issued an executive order supporting institutions for a Liberian war- and economic-crimes court. As of the latest materials consulted, the court had not tried Blahyi.

## 2025: Kidney Illness and Transfer to Kenya

In February 2025 Liberian reporting said Blahyi was critically ill with kidney complications. He had reportedly received treatment at John F. Kennedy Medical Center in Liberia without sufficient improvement and was transferred to Kidney Solutions Hospital in Kenya.

His family appealed for prayer. Some public commentary speculated that the illness or foreign transfer could help him evade a future tribunal.

**Status:**

- Kidney illness and medical transfer: reported fact based on family and local press.
- Claim that illness was fabricated or the transfer designed to evade justice: allegation/conspiracy narrative; no supporting evidence located.
- Death: no death was established. Later public reporting confirms survival into 2026.

## 2026: Return to Public Political Commentary

On 12 March 2026, Blahyi reportedly appeared on a podcast from the Mount Barclay area and declared war between Liberia and Guinea “inevitable” amid a border dispute. On 23 March another Liberian outlet reported him saying Guinea had “more to lose” and presenting combat experience as more decisive than weaponry.

The *Liberian Investigator* characterized him as a supporter of President Joseph Boakai’s Unity Party administration.

**Status:** reported fact concerning his remarks and political alignment. No evidence showed that he held government or military office or that his comments reflected official Liberian policy.

## Companies, Institutions, Projects and Financial Interests

### End Time Train Evangelistic Ministries Inc.

- Founded in Ghana, reportedly in 1999.
- Purpose: evangelical crusades and missionary work.
- Promoted Blahyi as a converted war commander and former traditional priest.
- Claimed activity across West and Central Africa and beyond.
- Legal incorporation details, annual reports and audited accounts were not located.

### Journeys Against Violence

- Founded in 2007.
- Operated a residential facility in Chocolate City.
- Target populations: ex-combatants, former child soldiers, unhoused youth and drug users.
- Activities: prayer, abstinence rules, driving classes, farming and construction or bricklaying.
- Funding: foreign evangelical donors, book proceeds and preaching donations according to Blahyi.
- Financial concern: allegations of family control, diversion of funds, inadequate food and incomplete training outcomes.
- No formal finding of fraud or embezzlement was located.

### God Bless Liberia Home

Academic reporting on Pentecostalism and transitional justice referred to a God Bless Liberia Home in which Blahyi and another former combatant-turned-evangelist housed approximately fifty men. Its precise organizational relationship to JAV is unclear.

### Proposed Sinoe development

- Approximately 3,000 acres reportedly donated by relatives.
- Proposed clinic, schools, vocational center, Bible college and accommodation.
- Completion and land-title status unresolved.

### Drug rehabilitation

By 2025 sympathetic profiles described Blahyi as engaged in rehabilitation work involving users of “kush,” the synthetic-drug mixture associated with a severe West African addiction crisis. Houses of Joseph announced him as an ambassador connected to work with children and addiction.

**Status:** reported organizational role; program outcomes and finances have not been independently audited in the sources consulted.

## Political, Religious and Ideological Development

### Ethnic-protection ideology

Blahyi described his wartime allegiance as primarily tribal rather than national. His claimed priestly protection originally applied only to Krahn people. He said Roosevelt Johnson deserved protection despite being Grebo because Johnson represented Krahn political interests and was married to a Krahn woman.

At the TRC, Blahyi later argued that human unity should transcend factional, tribal and sectional bonds.

### Traditional religion and demonization

Blahyi retrospectively described his former deity and religious practices as Satanic. He also claimed to have led a “coastal line division of the Black Witches of West Africa.”

These statements belong to an evangelical conversion framework that reinterprets African traditional religion as literal demonic bondage. Scholars and journalists have warned that this language may distort actual Krahn religious structures and encourage conspiratorial witchcraft accusations.

It is therefore necessary to distinguish:

- historically documented use of charms, divination and ritual protection in Liberia;
- Blahyi’s personal theological interpretation of those practices as Satanism;
- his unverified claims about a continent-wide witch hierarchy.

### Evangelical Christianity

His Christianity emphasizes:

- dramatic rebirth;
- the power of confession;
- forgiveness of enemies;
- spiritual warfare;
- deliverance from demonic possession;
- rehabilitation of former fighters;
- the Pauline model of a persecutor transformed into a preacher.

His favorite cited passage is John 8:32. His public identity depends on the scale of the contrast between “General Butt Naked” and “Evangelist Joshua Blahyi.”

### Criminal justice

Blahyi has variously:

- submitted to the TRC;
- said he would accept a court’s decision;
- supported creation of a Liberian war-crimes court;
- criticized the expense and cultural distance of Charles Taylor’s international prosecution;
- emphasized forgiveness and practical support for victims.

## Crimes, Allegations, Investigations and Accountability

### Conduct Blahyi admitted

Blahyi has admitted or claimed responsibility for:

- murder;
- human sacrifice;
- killing children;
- cannibalism;
- mutilation;
- command of child soldiers;
- administering or encouraging intoxicants;
- wartime looting and seizure of property;
- ordering or permitting rape;
- fighting naked under claimed magical protection;
- attacks during the April 1996 Monrovia conflict;
- shooting his bodyguard David Johnson and denying him treatment.

These admissions are documented statements. Individual crimes were not separately adjudicated.

### Twenty-thousand-death claim

**Claim:** Blahyi and his forces or decisions caused no fewer than 20,000 deaths between his childhood and 1996.

**Evidence for:**

- repeated first-person admissions;
- TRC testimony;
- undisputed participation in violent conflict;
- testimony from former commander Mohammed Toure that Blahyi was a notorious killer;
- contemporaneous evidence that he commanded armed fighters.

**Evidence against the scale:**

- no victim list;
- no matching set of massacre records;
- no graves or forensic evidence tied to such a total;
- no command documentation;
- probable command strength of only about forty;
- active combat career of roughly three years;
- Toure’s estimate that even 1,000 was implausibly high;
- the number would amount to an extraordinary share of all Liberian war deaths.

**Assessment:** participation in killings is strongly supported; the exact **20,000** figure is an unverified and probably inflated estimate. It may combine personal killings, ordered killings, casualties in operations, ritual claims, and deaths symbolically assumed as command responsibility.

### Rape

News reports stated that Blahyi told the TRC he ordered fighters to rape women. The surviving online transcript is incomplete or awkwardly transcribed in places and does not give a full incident inventory.

**Status:** documented admission as reported contemporaneously; no individual victim case adjudicated.

### Cannibalism and sacrifice

The consistency of Blahyi’s admissions gives them evidentiary weight as admissions against interest. Their post-conversion usefulness also created incentives for exaggeration: the greater the former evil, the more dramatic the conversion and the larger the international audience.

**Assessment:** plausible and repeatedly admitted, but the number, frequency and precise ritual system remain unresolved.

### No conviction

Blahyi has not been shown to have been:

- indicted by a Liberian war-crimes tribunal;
- tried for crimes against humanity;
- convicted;
- imprisoned for wartime offenses.

The TRC’s recommendation against prosecution was based on cooperation and remorse, not innocence. Critics argue that exempting an admitted mass killer while recommending lesser political actors for sanction revealed inconsistency.

### JAV financial controversy

A participant alleged misappropriation and nepotism. Blahyi denied diversion of funds. The available record lacks an audit, formal investigation, indictment, civil suit or judgment.

**Classification:** allegation and denial; unresolved.

### Possible future prosecution

Liberia’s movement toward a war- and economic-crimes court revived the possibility of legal scrutiny. Whether the TRC recommendation, limitation issues, evidentiary weaknesses, immunities attached to testimony, or future enabling legislation would permit Blahyi’s prosecution remains unresolved.

## Health, Psychological Crises and Substance Use

### Wartime substance use

Blahyi and reports about his command describe heavy wartime intoxication among combatants. Accounts commonly mention drugs and alcohol, but no medical record establishes a specific diagnosis or addiction history for Blahyi.

### Religious interpretation of mental state

Blahyi describes his former conduct as demonic possession or enslavement. This is a theological explanation, not a clinical psychiatric diagnosis.

The 2011 documentary reportedly captured episodes of emotional collapse, including crying in a hotel bathroom and asking when his ordeal would end. Such footage shows distress but does not establish a particular disorder.

### Kidney disease

In early 2025 he reportedly developed serious kidney complications, was treated in Monrovia, and transferred to Kenya in critical condition. The underlying diagnosis, treatment, prognosis and recovery course were not publicly documented in reliable medical detail.

His subsequent public appearance or recorded remarks in March 2026 establish that reports of imminent death in 2025 did not culminate in a documented death.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda and Disputed Narratives

## The invulnerable naked general

**Story:** Blahyi fought completely naked except for shoes and weapons because clothing blocked magical power. Bullets could not harm him.

**Appearance and circulation:** wartime fighters and civilians; Blahyi’s testimony; later newspaper, documentary and internet retellings.

**Evidence:** photographs confirm naked or nearly naked combat. No evidence establishes magical invulnerability. Survival without a recorded bullet wound can result from chance, tactical circumstances, selective memory or storytelling.

**Reputational effect:** produced one of the most memorable noms de guerre of the Liberian wars and transformed a small-unit commander into a global symbol of “warlord Africa.”

## High priest at eleven

**Story:** tribal elders selected Blahyi as high priest of the entire Krahn people after elaborate arrangements surrounding his birth.

**Appearance:** his ministry biography, TRC testimony and autobiography.

**Evidence for:** Blahyi’s repeated account.

**Evidence against:** his half-brother’s denial; ethnographers’ inability to identify the described institution; extraordinary scale of the claimed office.

**Effect:** foundational to the Saul-to-Paul conversion story. It makes the wartime commander a captive of inherited spiritual evil rather than merely a criminal entrepreneur.

## Monthly child sacrifice from 1982

**Story:** visions identified children to be kidnapped and sacrificed monthly from the time Blahyi was eleven.

**Evidence:** his own account only in the material consulted.

**Problems:** no identified prewar victim, family testimony, contemporaneous investigation or independent ritual witness.

**Classification:** grave self-accusation; unverified.

## Twenty thousand victims

**Story:** Blahyi was personally or command-responsible for at least 20,000 deaths.

**Circulation:** TRC broadcast, Associated Press and BBC reports, documentaries, Christian publicity and internet summaries.

**Evidence:** confession and acknowledged violent command.

**Counterevidence:** small command, short operational period, absence of victim-level evidence, rejection by a fellow commander and an academic specialist.

**Effect:** converted him from a localized militia commander into one of the world’s most notorious self-described killers; strengthened both condemnatory and redemptive narratives.

## “Most evil man in the world”

This journalistic label circulated in tabloid and documentary publicity. It is rhetoric, not an evidentiary category or comparative historical finding.

## Satanic control of Liberia

**Story:** Blahyi’s deity controlled political leaders; Doe was a sub-priest; an altar in the Executive Mansion bound cabinet members; a sacred national flag and seal at Putu Mountain prevented peace.

**Evidence:** Blahyi’s testimony.

**Counterevidence:** no corroborating Doe-era document, cabinet testimony, physical examination or ethnographic confirmation.

**Effect:** provides a supernatural theory of Liberian state violence and reinforces Pentecostal narratives of national deliverance.

## Uprooting the objects brought peace

**Story:** Blahyi surrendered or uprooted occult objects in 2003, allowing Liberia to become peaceful.

**Classification:** religious myth/self-created causal narrative. The documented peace process had political, military and international causes.

## Steven Spielberg offer

**Story:** Steven Spielberg met Blahyi in Monrovia and offered **US$900,000** for film rights, but Blahyi rejected the offer because Spielberg wanted to weaken the Christian message.

**Circulation:** told by Blahyi to patrons and reported by *The New Yorker* in 2016.

**Evidence against:** Spielberg spokesman Marvin Levy said Spielberg had never heard of the proposal.

**Classification:** disputed claim, effectively denied by the supposed counterparty.

**Effect:** increased Blahyi’s prestige among donors by portraying him as refusing Hollywood wealth for religious principle.

## Illness as flight from justice

**Story:** Blahyi’s 2025 kidney crisis or transfer to Kenya was staged or strategically used to escape a coming war-crimes court.

**Evidence:** public suspicion only.

**Evidence against:** reports of treatment at two named medical institutions and family appeals; no proof of fabrication.

**Classification:** allegation or conspiracy narrative.

## The redeemed Saul

**Story:** Blahyi’s life repeats the conversion of Saul of Tarsus: persecutor, visionary encounter, then Christian evangelist.

**Circulation:** his memoir subtitle, sermons, supporters and Bojan Jancic’s foreword.

**Classification:** hagiographic typology rather than literal historical identity.

**Effect:** gave international Christian audiences a familiar interpretive frame and attracted patrons.

## Popular-culture afterlife

A warlord named **General Butt-Fucking Naked** appears in the musical *The Book of Mormon*. The name and broad premise clearly echo Blahyi’s public persona, although the stage character is fictional.

Internet retellings often flatten the record into a spectacle: “naked cannibal warlord kills 20,000 and finds Jesus.” These versions generally erase doubts about the death toll, the limited size of his command, the political structure of ULIMO-J, victims’ resistance to forgiveness and unresolved financial questions.

## Awards, Honors and Cultural Recognition

Blahyi himself is not known to have received a major state honor or internationally recognized humanitarian award.

## Later Life and Present Status

As of the latest dated evidence consulted:

- Blahyi remained alive in **March 2026**.
- He continued to be described as an evangelist and former warlord.
- He remained involved, at least publicly, in youth or drug-rehabilitation activity.
- He had survived or stabilized following serious kidney illness reported in February 2025.
- He remained outside prison and had not been convicted for wartime conduct.
- Liberia’s proposed war-crimes institutions had not yet produced a case against him.
- His public rhetoric had shifted beyond reconciliation ministry into commentary on border security and national preparedness.
- His precise residence was not firmly established, though the March 2026 reporting placed his public activity in or around Mount Barclay/Monrovia.

No reliable death report was found.

## Reputation and Legacy

Blahyi occupies several simultaneous historical roles:

- a documented ULIMO-J militia commander in the 1996 Monrovia fighting;
- a self-confessed perpetrator of murder, sacrifice, cannibalism, rape-related command abuses and recruitment of children;
- a probable exaggerator of the numerical scale and institutional grandeur of his past;
- one of the few Liberian commanders to provide an expansive public confession to the TRC;
- an evangelical preacher whose conversion may be sincerely held while also providing status, income, protection and international patrons;
- a rehabilitation organizer credited by some participants with housing or training them;
- the head of an organization accused by a participant of nepotism, poor provision and misuse of donor money;
- a test case in the unresolved conflict between confession, forgiveness, restorative justice and criminal accountability;
- an international media archetype whose nickname frequently displaced the political history of the faction he served.

His reputation differs by audience:

- **Some victims:** unrepentant skepticism, anger and demand for prosecution.
- **Some former fighters:** gratitude for shelter, religious community or vocational training.
- **Some Liberian Christians:** evidence of divine transformation.
- **Some scholars and journalists:** a small-time commander who successfully converted sensational confession into global prominence.
- **International popular culture:** the archetypal naked, cannibalistic African warlord.
- **Justice advocates:** an emblem of impunity.
- **Supporters:** a uniquely candid former perpetrator doing work more powerful commanders avoided.

The unresolved historical question is not simply whether he changed. It is how much of the identity he says he renounced was retrospectively enlarged to make the new identity compelling.
