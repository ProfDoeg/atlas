## Works by Hilma af Klint

### Academic, naturalistic, and commissioned works

- **1877–1941:** *Landscapes, Portraits and Miscellaneous Works*. Nearly 400 landscapes, portraits, botanical studies, anatomical and scientific illustrations, commissioned pictures, and other representational works constitute the seventh volume of the catalogue raisonné; many have no fixed individual title. ([Catalogue raisonné, vol. VII](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Stolpe_2022_EN_LO_u-kopia.pdf))
- **1880s–1910s:** *Botanical and scientific studies*. Drawings and paintings made from observed plants and other natural forms—part professional illustration, part artistic study—which resist separation from the spiritual “research” into which af Klint later incorporated botany. ([Moderna Museet](https://www.modernamuseet.se/en/malmo/exhibitions/hilma-af-klint/in-depth/); [Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf))

### Collective spiritualistic drawings and records

- **1895–1910:** *The Five (De Fem): fifteen spiritualistic sketchbooks*. Collaborative séance records combining automatic drawings, writing, diagrams, prayers, and recorded communications. They cannot securely be called works by af Klint alone: Anna Cassel, Cornelia Cederberg, Sigrid Hedman, and Mathilda Nilsson also participated, and individual hands are not always separable. ([Catalogue raisonné, vol. I](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/bocker/hilma-af-klint-spiritualistic-drawings-1896-1910/); [Grand Palais dossier](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/fileadmin/user_upload/DP_Hilma_af_Klint.pdf))
- **ca. 1903–04:** *Spiritual Drawings of The Five*. A body of mediumistic drawings within the collective notebooks, traditionally exhibited under af Klint’s name but more accurately treated as manifestations of a group practice rather than securely individual obras. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/N/bo29582814.html))

### Gesamtkunstwerk and project: The Temple

- **1904–31:** *The Temple*. An unrealized project for a circular or spiral building through which visitors would encounter paintings in an ordained spiritual progression. Because the building was never constructed, the surviving obra is dispersed among paintings, notebooks, diagrams, and late architectural sketches; its intended unity makes it a projected Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely an architectural design. ([MAVCOR/Yale](https://mavcor.yale.edu/mavcor-journal/object-narratives/hilma-af-klints-temple-paintings); [Grand Palais](https://www.grandpalais.fr/en/magazine/hilma-af-klints-paintings-temple-mission-guided-angels))
- **1906–15:** *The Paintings for the Temple*. The central cycle comprises 193 paintings and drawings arranged in eleven series, made in two phases separated by a 1908–12 interruption. The catalogue attributes the cycle to af Klint, but her notes assign Anna Cassel a crucial role, and recent scholarship disputes the older image of wholly solitary authorship. ([Catalogue raisonné, vol. II](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/en/bocker/hilma-af-klint-catalogue-raisonne-2/); [Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/teaching-materials/hilma-af-klint-paintings-for-thefuture/paintings-for-the-temple); [Tate guide](https://fix-hswy-tk-label-inheritance.up.railway.app/documents/1844/TMEXH_0086_AfKlint_Mondrian_LargePrintGuide_web_aw_.pdf))
- **1906–07:** *Group I, Primordial Chaos*. The opening Temple series: small oils whose diagrammatic letters, spirals, organic forms, and blue-yellow polarity try to make an otherwise invisible cosmology perceptible. “Primordial Chaos” appears to be a posthumously standardized title, and Anna Cassel’s participation has been specifically identified. ([Grand Palais](https://www.grandpalais.fr/en/magazine/hilma-af-klints-paintings-temple-mission-guided-angels); [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou-plus/magazine/article/hilma-af-klint-the-unsung-pioneer-of-abstraction))
- **1907:** *The Eros Series*. A pink-dominated component of the Temple cycle exploring attraction, polarity, and union through biomorphic and geometric signs. ([Moderna Museet](https://www.modernamuseet.se/en/malmo/exhibitions/hilma-af-klint/in-depth/))
- **1907:** *The Large Figure Paintings*. A Temple group of symbolic figure paintings, including *The Key to All Work to Date*; they operate as pictorial diagrams or summaries rather than conventional figure painting. ([Wikimedia Commons catalogue grouping](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category%3AThe_Paintings_for_the_Temple_%28Hilma_af_Klint%29); [Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf))
- **1907:** *Group IV, The Ten Largest*. Ten monumental tempera paintings on paper, organized as Childhood, Youth, Adulthood, and Old Age. They are both individual panels and one architecturally conceived cycle, so treating them as ten independent easel pictures loses part of the obra. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [The Fifth Desk](https://www.thefifthdesk.org/work/the-ten-largest/))
- **1908:** *Group V, The Seven-Pointed Star*. A serial component of the Temple project in which geometry functions as a diagrammatic spiritual language. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou-plus/magazine/article/hilma-af-klint-the-unsung-pioneer-of-abstraction))
- **1908:** *Group VI, Evolution*. Sixteen paintings combining human and animal figures, written signs, circles, rosettes, and ovals into a speculative account of creation and spiritual development. They are neither illustrations of established science nor pure abstraction, but painted research diagrams. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Grand Palais](https://www.grandpalais.fr/en/magazine/hilma-af-klints-paintings-temple-mission-guided-angels))
- **1912:** *The US Series*. The principal series made when af Klint resumed the Temple project; “US” is her classificatory designation, not a reference to the United States. ([Grand Palais](https://www.grandpalais.fr/en/magazine/hilma-af-klints-paintings-temple-mission-guided-angels); [Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf))
- **1913–15:** *Tree of Knowledge, the W Series*. A sequence of diagrammatic trees joining botanical structure, spiritual ascent, and cosmology; it belongs to the Temple complex rather than being botanical illustration in the ordinary sense. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Guggenheim Bilbao](https://prensa.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/src/uploads/2024/07/Dosier-de-prensa-Hilma-af-Klint_EN.pdf))
- **1914–15:** *Group IX/SUW, The Swan*. A serial transformation of paired swans into increasingly geometric configurations, concerned with polarity and reconciliation. It is a pictorial process rather than a single composition. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou-plus/magazine/article/hilma-af-klint-the-unsung-pioneer-of-abstraction))
- **1915:** *Group IX/UW, The Dove*. A Temple series combining the dove, Christian imagery, diagrams, and abstraction to address conflict, descent, and reconciliation. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/pompidou-plus/magazine/article/hilma-af-klint-the-unsung-pioneer-of-abstraction))
- **1915:** *Group X, The Altarpieces*. Three monumental paintings conceived as the culminating destination of the unrealized Temple installation. They behave as an altar ensemble and architectural terminus, not simply as three detachable pictures. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Serpentine extended labels](https://d37zoqglehb9o7.cloudfront.net/uploads/2020/03/hilma_af_klint_wall_texts.pdf))
- **1915:** *Closing Painting*. The concluding picture associated with the Altarpieces and the closure of the Temple cycle. Its identity depends upon its place in the sequence, making it closer to a final movement than an autonomous easel painting. ([Guggenheim Bilbao](https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/exhibitions/hilma-af-klint))

### Portable archive of the Temple

- **1917–20:** *The Blue Books*. Ten blue-bound volumes containing photographs and small watercolor copies of the 193 Temple works, with annotations. They are simultaneously catalogue, portable surrogate, presentation device, and secondary manifestation of an otherwise immovable Gesamtkunstwerk—not merely sketchbooks or later reproductions. ([Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf); [University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/N/bo29582814.html))

### Post-Temple serial investigations

- **1916:** *The Parsifal Series*. A sequence of 144 works on paper divided into groups, and af Klint’s first major body after completing the Temple paintings. Its serial, investigative construction matters more than any single sheet. ([Guggenheim Bilbao](https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/exhibition/the-parsifal-series-and-the-atom-series-1916-17); [Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf))
- **1917:** *The Atom Series*. A sequence treating the atom as both physical structure and spiritual microcosm; it belongs with *Parsifal* in volume IV of the catalogue raisonné. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf))
- **1917–20:** *Geometric Series and Other Works*. Several groups of small paintings constructed with ruler and compass from triangles, circles, squares, and ellipses. Af Klint understood these as “spiritual-scientific” research; the catalogue does not supply a distinct stable title for every constituent work. ([Catalogue raisonné, vol. V](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/en/bocker/hilma-af-klint-geometric-series-and-other-works-1917-1920/))
- **1917–20:** *Geometric Series I–VI*. Numbered serial experiments within the preceding corpus. Their generic numbers are archival designations rather than descriptive titles, and their methodical recurrence makes the series—not the isolated sheet—the most meaningful unit. ([Catalogue raisonné, vol. V](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/en/bocker/hilma-af-klint-geometric-series-and-other-works-1917-1920/); [publisher catalogue](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Var_2024_ENG_lo_s.pdf))
- **1919–20:** *Flowers, Mosses and Lichens*. A portfolio of forty-six botanical drawings pairing observed plants with dates, names, spiritual qualities, and small pictograms. It is at once a herbarium, symbolic lexicon, and research protocol, and thus resists classification as either botanical illustration or autonomous art alone. ([MoMA checklist](https://press.moma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Hilma-af-Klint-FINAL-Checklist.pdf); [Moderna Museet](https://www.modernamuseet.se/en/malmo/exhibitions/hilma-af-klint/in-depth/))
- **1920:** *Series II, The Teachings of Buddhism*. A group of diagrammatic paintings, individually numbered rather than consistently titled, translating religious and philosophical study into geometric form. ([Guggenheim](https://www.guggenheim.org/exhibition/hilma-af-klint); [Catalogue raisonné, vol. V](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/en/bocker/hilma-af-klint-geometric-series-and-other-works-1917-1920/))

### Late watercolors

- **1922:** *On the Viewing of Flowers and Trees*. A watercolor series in which pools and veils of color register an encounter with plant life rather than describe botanical appearances. It is better understood as a phenomenological or spiritual investigation than as conventional flower painting. ([Serpentine extended labels](https://d37zoqglehb9o7.cloudfront.net/uploads/2020/03/hilma_af_klint_wall_texts.pdf); [Hilma af Klint Foundation](https://hilmaafklint.se/hilma-af-klint-catalogue-raisonne/))
- **1922–41:** *Late Watercolours*. The catalogue raisonné gathers 468 illustrations from this period, largely watercolor and graphite works; most constituent series have no titles, so inventing individual names would falsify the record. These untitled sequences are manifestations of sustained color research rather than a single titled project. ([Catalogue raisonné overview](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Var_2024_ENG_lo_s.pdf); [Catalogue raisonné description](https://twelve-books.com/collections/hilma-af-klint/products/hilma-af-klint-late-watercolours-1922-1941-by-hilma-af-klint))
- **1922–41:** *Untitled late watercolor series*. Numerous separately catalogued sequences of organic, atmospheric, botanical, and energetic forms belong here, but neither af Klint nor the catalogue supplies titles for most of them. Their resistance to naming is part of their documentary status and should not be repaired with descriptive inventions. ([Catalogue raisonné, vol. VI](https://bokforlagetstolpe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Hilma-folder-sidvis.pdf))

### Notebooks, texts, diagrams, and protocols

- **1890s–1944:** *Notebooks and written archive*. Roughly one hundred notebooks and some 26,000 manuscript pages combine séance minutes, received messages, diaries, glossaries, diagrams, preparatory studies, cataloguing, spiritual speculation, and retrospective interpretation. This is not one “book”: it is an evolving research archive in which text and image repeatedly exchange functions. ([University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/N/bo29582814.html); [Guggenheim Bilbao](https://www.guggenheim-bilbao.eus/en/exhibitions/hilma-af-klint))
- **1906–07:** *Notes on Letters and Words Pertaining to Works by Hilma af Klint*. A notebook functioning as a glossary or decoding protocol for recurrent letters, words, and signs in the paintings. It instructs interpretation without conclusively stabilizing the symbols’ meanings. ([National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo, checklist](https://www.momat.go.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/561-list-en.pdf))
- **date not securely established:** *Studies on the Life of the Soul*. A substantial philosophical manuscript associated with af Klint’s spiritual and anthroposophical research. It is a study-text rather than a conventionally published book, and its date should not be narrowed without manuscript-level evidence. ([Grand Palais](https://www.grandpalais.fr/en/magazine/hilma-af-klints-paintings-temple-mission-guided-angels))
- **1930–31:** *Notebook containing plans for the Temple*. Written notes and architectural sketches specifying a circular building and spiral ascent. These sheets are the surviving design-stage manifestation of an unbuilt project, not evidence that af Klint constructed a building. ([MAVCOR/Yale](https://mavcor.yale.edu/material-objects/planning-drawing-hilma-af-klimts-temple); [MAVCOR Journal](https://mavcor.yale.edu/sites/default/files/article_pdf/johnson_paul_0.pdf))
- **1932:** *Notebook*. A late manuscript volume containing writing, drawing, and watercolor, part of af Klint’s retrospective ordering and interpretation of her production rather than a separately published text. ([National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo, checklist](https://www.momat.go.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/561-list-en.pdf))
- **1934:** *Notebook*. A late notebook in pencil and watercolor, likewise combining record, interpretation, and continuing pictorial research. ([National Museum of Modern Art, Tokyo, checklist](https://www.momat.go.jp/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/561-list-en.pdf))
