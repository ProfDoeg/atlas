## Works by Francis of Assisi

### Protocols, rules, and forms of life

- **1209–1221:** *Earlier Rule (Regula non bullata)*. A collaboratively accumulated protocol for the life of the Friars Minor, finalized at the chapter of 1221; it contains prayers and praise as well as legislation, and therefore resists classification as a rule authored by Francis alone. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Paolazzi critical edition](https://www.istcap.org/en/publications/editiones-archivi-franciscani-historici/))
- **Possibly c. 1216:** *Fragments of Another Earlier Rule*. Textual fragments reconstructed by Kajetan Esser as remains of an otherwise lost stage in the evolving Franciscan protocol; their independence, date, and exact relationship to the 1221 Rule remain disputed. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **1212–1213, approximately:** *Form of Life Given to Clare and Her Sisters*. A short protocol for the women of San Damiano, preserved only because Clare incorporated it into her own Rule; both its early date and its precise original wording require caution. ([Kajetan Esser’s study](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/esser2.html); [Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated, probably before 1221:** *Rule for Hermitages*. A compact spatial and behavioral protocol assigning rotating roles to brothers living in hermitages. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **1223:** *Later Rule (Regula bullata)*. The definitive protocol for the Friars Minor, confirmed by Honorius III; Francis is its principal voice, but its juridical form emerged through collaboration with the fraternity and Roman Church and is not simply a solitary-authored text. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Early Documents](https://books.google.com/books?id=vwVsEM8mXWYC))
- **1226:** *Last Will Written for Clare and Her Sisters*. A final poverty protocol preserved as a quotation in Clare’s Rule rather than as an independent manuscript; its transmission makes it both Francis’s text and Clare’s documentary act. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Esser’s study](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/esser2.html))

### Admonitions, testaments, and enacted instruction

- **1220s:** *Admonitions*. A collection of twenty-eight short pieces of religious instruction for the friars; whether Francis designed it as one work, and how much its ordering belongs to transmitters, cannot be established. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *True and Perfect Joy*. A dictated exemplary dialogue or oral performance preserved through later narrative transmission; its Franciscan substance is accepted in modern collections, but it resists being called a securely recoverable authorial text. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Early Documents corpus](https://books.google.com/books?id=vwVsEM8mXWYC))
- **April–May 1226:** *Testament Made at Siena*. A short dictated instruction reportedly made during grave illness and embedded in the *Assisi Compilation*, not independently transmitted; even its status as a discrete work has been questioned. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html))
- **1226:** *Testament*. A remembrance, admonition, and interpretive manifesto intended to govern how the friars enacted their Rule, although Francis expressly denied that it was another rule. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Early Documents](https://books.google.com/books?id=vwVsEM8mXWYC))
- **1226:** *Blessing for Brother Bernard*. A deathbed blessing preserved only inside a later narrative source; Esser printed it among Francis’s dictated works, but its lack of independent manuscript transmission makes its textual status insecure. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html))

### Letters and circular manifestos

- **Before or shortly after 1220:** *Letter to the Clergy, Earlier Redaction*. A circular manifesto demanding material reverence for the Eucharist and sacred writing. Esser dated this version before 1219, whereas Carlo Paolazzi places both redactions after the papal decree of November 1219. ([dating and redaction dispute](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carta_a_todos_los_cl%C3%A9rigos); [Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **After 1219, probably 1220:** *Letter to the Clergy, Later Redaction*. A revised manifestation of the same Eucharistic campaign, altered to reflect ecclesiastical constitutions; some editors print two works, while others regard the differences as insufficient to warrant separate publication. ([dating and redaction dispute](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carta_a_todos_los_cl%C3%A9rigos))
- **1220:** *First Letter to the Custodians*. A circular protocol directing Franciscan officials to promote penance, Eucharistic reverence, and public praise, and to reproduce and circulate the document. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Early Documents index](https://ofsaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/UNIT-3.-TABLE-OF-CONTENTS-AND-INTRODUCTION.pdf))
- **1220 or later:** *Second Letter to the Custodians*. A shorter follow-up circular concerned with distributing Francis’s message; it survives as a distinct letter in the critical corpus. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Earlier Exhortation to the Brothers and Sisters of Penance (First Letter to the Faithful)*. A two-part exhortation to penitents rather than an ordinary private letter; its audience and its relationship to a formal “Third Order” remain disputed. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated, later than the preceding exhortation according to Esser:** *Later Letter to the Faithful*. A much-expanded circular manifesto for Christians of every social condition; although Esser treated it as a later redaction, its scale and organization allow it to stand as a separate work. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Letter to Brother Leo*. A private autograph note granting Leo freedom to discern how to live their shared religious project; the surviving material object is one of the exceptionally rare texts in Francis’s own hand. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Bartoli Langeli, autograph edition record](https://www.brepols.net/products/IS-9782503509648-1))
- **Undated, probably 1220s:** *Letter to a Minister*. A pastoral protocol instructing an unnamed minister how to receive troublesome or sinful brothers mercifully; its addressee is unidentified. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Late 1225–1226, probably:** *Letter to the Entire Order*. A circular admonition to the whole fraternity, incorporating the prayer conventionally titled *Almighty, Eternal, Just, and Merciful God*; the late dating depends partly on Ubertino da Casale’s later testimony. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Probably 1220:** *Letter to the Rulers of the Peoples*. A public manifesto asking political rulers to institute communal praise and Eucharistic observance; it survives through a comparatively late witness, so its transmission is less secure than that of the principal letters. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [historical edition](https://www.capdox.capuchin.org.au/francescanea/the-writings-of-saint-francis-of-assisi/))
- **1223–1224, approximately:** *Letter to Anthony of Padua*. A short authorization allowing Anthony to teach theology provided that study does not extinguish prayer and devotion; its authenticity was once challenged but is generally accepted in the critical corpus. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))

### Prayers, praises, songs, and liturgical constructions

- **1205–1206, traditionally:** *Prayer Before the Crucifix*. A vernacular prayer associated with Francis’s conversion at San Damiano; its attribution is not completely secure, its surviving form may be a translation from Italian, and no evidence proves that these were the words spoken during the reported vision. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Exhortation to the Praise of God*. A brief praise-construction assembled principally from biblical phrases, possibly meant for communal recitation. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Praises to Be Said at All the Hours*. A liturgical protocol and song of praise designed for repetition before the canonical hours by day and night. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Office of the Passion*. A private liturgical Gesamtkunstwerk assembled from selected and recomposed scriptural psalms, an antiphon, rubrics, and prayer for performance across the hours and seasons; it is neither simply an original poem nor merely a copied office. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Salutation of the Blessed Virgin Mary*. A devotional praise-prayer recognized in the critical corpus. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Salutation of the Virtues*. A personifying praise-poem closely related to the medieval *lauda* tradition. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Undated:** *Prayer Inspired by the Our Father*. A prayer-like expansion rather than a doctrinal commentary. Earlier editors doubted the attribution; Esser accepted it, but the earliest known explicit attribution to Francis is late-medieval, so authorship remains insecure. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [authorship history](https://www.assisijourney.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/C-Knowles-PhD-thesis.pdf))
- **September 1224, traditionally:** *Praises of God Most High*. A praise-prayer written on one side of a parchment for Brother Leo after Francis’s experience at La Verna; the autograph survives, making the sheet simultaneously text, relic, and personal object. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **September 1224, traditionally:** *Blessing for Brother Leo*. A scriptural blessing written with a tau sign on the reverse of the same parchment as the *Praises of God Most High*; materially the two “works” are one double-sided object. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **1225–1226:** *Canticle of Brother Sun, or Praises of the Creatures*. An Umbrian vernacular song first composed during Francis’s illness at San Damiano in spring 1225 and enlarged with stanzas on reconciliation and death; its layered composition resists a single-date label. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **After the initial composition of the Canticle, probably 1225:** *Hear, Little Poor Ones (Audite, poverelle)*. A sung vernacular exhortation for the women of San Damiano, rediscovered in a fourteenth-century manuscript in 1976; it is probably the “holy words with melody” mentioned by early narrative sources, but that identification is inferential. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm); [Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html))

### Lost or textually resistant works

- **Before 1226:** *Blessing and Absolution for Clare and Her Sisters*. A lost written blessing known only through early narrative testimony; because its words do not survive, the obra is an attested documentary act rather than a recoverable text. ([Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html); [Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Before 1226:** *Writing to Clare Concerning Fasting*. A lost protocol mentioned by Clare herself; no title, date, or wording can safely be supplied. ([Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html); [Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Before 1226:** *Several Writings to Clare and Her Sisters Concerning Poverty*. Clare reports receiving multiple writings from Francis, but their number, dates, titles, and relationship to the surviving *Form of Life* and *Last Will* are unknown. They resist enumeration as individual works because the evidence preserves only a plural trace. ([Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html))
- **1221:** *Letter to the Provincial Minister and Brothers of France*. A lost autograph letter reportedly sent around the 1221 general chapter; only the report of its existence survives. ([Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html); [Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **After Christmas 1222:** *Letter to the Students at Bologna*. A lost letter reportedly announcing the Christmas 1222 earthquake and remembered for its faulty Latin; attribution rests on Thomas of Eccleston’s testimony. ([Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html); [Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **1226:** *Letter to Lady Jacoba*. A lost summons reportedly dictated shortly before Francis’s death; later narrative tradition says Jacoba arrived before it could be sent, making the work inseparable from the story that transmits it. ([Franciscan Authors](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
- **Before 1226:** *Other dictated sayings and instructions*. Early sources attribute many spoken formulations to Francis, but the boundary between a made text, remembered speech, and a hagiographer’s literary construction cannot consistently be recovered; the critical corpus therefore does not treat every reported utterance as an independent work. ([Giovanni Miccoli](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/Miccoli-LosescritosdeFrancisco.html))

### Built, social, and performative projects

- **c. 1206:** *Repair of San Damiano*. A hands-on architectural project undertaken during Francis’s conversion; the surviving church has subsequently changed, so it cannot be treated straightforwardly as an intact building “by” Francis. ([Cambridge Companion excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/60430/excerpt/9780521760430_excerpt.pdf); [Franciscan chronology](https://www.franciscanmedia.org/franciscan-tradition-and-resources/chronology-of-the-life-of-st-francis/))
- **c. 1206–1208:** *Repairs of San Pietro della Spina and the Porziuncola*. Two further church-restoration projects attributed by the early biographical tradition to Francis; they were repairs rather than designs for new buildings, and their material extent is difficult to isolate from later reconstruction. ([Cambridge Companion excerpt](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805217/60430/excerpt/9780521760430_excerpt.pdf))
- **1208/1209 onward:** *The Fraternity of Lesser Brothers*. A social and religious project formed around a lived Gospel practice before it became a juridically stable “order”; its collaborative, continually changing human medium makes it an obra without a single material boundary. ([Early Documents](https://books.google.com/books?id=vwVsEM8mXWYC); [Franciscan chronology](https://www.franciscanmedia.org/franciscan-tradition-and-resources/chronology-of-the-life-of-st-francis/))
- **1212 onward:** *The community of Poor Women at San Damiano*. A collaborative religious project made by Clare, Francis, and their companions rather than a foundation attributable to Francis alone; Clare became its principal legislator and author. ([Associated Press overview](https://apnews.com/article/43baff1f86243ed8d9cde6a6fdcf3359); [Esser’s study](https://www.franciscanos.org/estudios/esser2.html))
- **Undated development, traditionally c. 1221:** *A Franciscan order of lay penitents*. The traditional “Third Order” is better classified as Francis’s influence upon an existing penitential movement than as a securely dated institution he single-handedly made; historians explicitly dispute the foundation claim. ([Cambridge Companion chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-francis-of-assisi/third-order-of-francis/8B4FE3632366017E86AB89354D109FD2); [historical qualification](https://www.secularfranciscansusa.org/wp-content/uploads/CIOFS_SA_Manual_Chapter_3_English.pdf))
- **24–25 December 1223:** *Christmas at Greccio*. An ephemeral devotional Gesamtkunstwerk combining place, manger, animals, gathered bodies, preaching, light, song, and Eucharistic liturgy. Francis organized a commemorative manifestation of the Nativity, not the first sculptural “crib” in the modern sense; claims that he invented every form of Christmas crèche overstate the evidence. ([Vatican account](https://www.vatican.va/content/francesco/en/apost_letters/documents/papa-francesco-lettera-ap_20191201_admirabile-signum.html); [historical question about earlier crèches](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rscir_0035-2217_1922_num_2_1_1163))

### Bodily manifestation

- **September 1224:** *The stigmata*. Early witnesses present the wounds that appeared on Francis’s body at La Verna as a divine manifestation, not something Francis made; it belongs in an atlas of his obra only if “work” is allowed to include a body transformed into a sign. Its theological meaning, physical character, and the development of its accounts remain subjects of historical interpretation. ([Early Documents](https://books.google.com/books?id=vwVsEM8mXWYC); [Franciscan Authors bibliography](https://franciscanauthors.rich.ru.nl/franautf.htm))
