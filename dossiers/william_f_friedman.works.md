## Works by William Frederick Friedman

### Cryptanalytic monographs and protocols

- **1917:** *A Method of Reconstructing the Primary Alphabet from a Single One of the Series of Secondary Alphabets*. Riverbank Publication No. 15, a protocol for recovering a cipher alphabet; although Riverbank often omitted bylines, the series bibliography attributes this monograph to Friedman. [Riverbank publications list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbank_Publications)

- **1918:** *Methods for the Solution of Running-Key Ciphers*. Riverbank Publication No. 16, a cryptanalytic protocol rather than a general book. [Riverbank publications list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbank_Publications)

- **1918:** *An Introduction to Methods for the Solution of Ciphers*. Riverbank Publication No. 17, a training monograph systematizing cipher-solving procedures. [Riverbank publications list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbank_Publications)

- **1918:** *Synoptic Tables for the Solution of Ciphers and a Bibliography of Cryptographic Literature*. Riverbank Publication No. 18, simultaneously a reference instrument, tabular problem-solving aid, and bibliography. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1918:** *Formulae for the Solution of Geometrical Transposition Ciphers*. Riverbank Publication No. 19, a technical protocol made jointly with Lenox R. Lohr; it should not be assigned to Friedman alone. [Riverbank publications list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbank_Publications)

- **1918:** *Several Machine Ciphers and Methods for Their Solution*. Riverbank Publication No. 20, a study and set of procedures for attacking machine-generated ciphers. [Riverbank publications list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbank_Publications)

- **1918:** *Methods for the Reconstruction of Primary Alphabets*. Riverbank Publication No. 21, made jointly with Elizebeth Smith Friedman; its collective authorship matters because the Riverbank pamphlets were frequently issued without explicit bylines. [Riverbank publications list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverbank_Publications)

- **1922:** *The Index of Coincidence and Its Applications in Cryptography*. Riverbank Publication No. 22, a research monograph and mathematical instrument introducing the index of coincidence. Its French printing bears a Paris imprint, and sources differ between 1921 and 1922 when distinguishing preparation from publication. [Friedman Collection analytical-guide record](https://www.scribd.com/document/217961334/Friedman-Collection-Guide)

- **1923:** *Elements of Cryptanalysis*. War Department Training Pamphlet No. 3, a classified instructional protocol that reorganized Friedman's earlier work into the Army’s first systematic cryptanalytic manual. [NSA scan and publication record](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/publications/folder_237/41760269079943.pdf)

- **1931:** *Advanced Military Cryptography*. Army Extension Courses Special Text No. 166, an advanced instructional manual on making and operating military cryptosystems. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1935:** *Field Codes Used by the German Army During the World War*. A War Department technical-historical study of German field-code systems in the First World War. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1936–1942:** *Military Cryptanalysis, Part I: Monoalphabetic Substitution Systems*. The first component of a four-part classified training work, repeatedly revised rather than a succession of wholly independent obras. Lambros D. Callimahos later enlarged the fourth edition, so the postwar form is collaborative. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1937–1943:** *Military Cryptanalysis, Part II: Simpler Varieties of Polyalphabetic Substitution Systems*. The second component of the Army course, surviving in preliminary, extension-course, revised, and third-edition manifestations. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1938:** *Military Cryptanalysis, Part III: Aperiodic Substitution Systems*. The third component of the classified textbook project, a protocol for solving nonperiodic substitution systems. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1939–1941:** *Military Cryptanalysis, Part IV: Transposition and Fractionating Systems*. The fourth component, first appearing in preliminary form in 1939 and in a later War Department manifestation in 1941. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1938:** *Use of Codes and Ciphers in the World War and Lessons to Be Learned Therefrom*. A historical-technical article that turns First World War experience into guidance for future communications security. [Friedman bibliography](https://mu-internal.net/~rclark/cryptography_folder/friedman.html)

- **1938:** *The Zimmermann Telegram of January 16, 1917, and Its Cryptographic Background*. A documentary cryptologic history made with Charles J. Mendelsohn; classification delayed its public circulation, and the joint authorship should be retained. [Friedman bibliography](https://mu-internal.net/~rclark/cryptography_folder/friedman.html)

- **1942:** *American Army Field Codes in the American Expeditionary Forces During the First World War*. A retrospective technical history of American field-code practice, prepared for the War Department. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1952–1958:** *Military Cryptanalytics*. A postwar reconstruction and enlargement of the *Military Cryptanalysis* course made with Lambros D. Callimahos. Parts and sections appeared at different dates, including Part I in 1952, the first section of Part II in 1955, and further sections in 1958; it is therefore a continuing instructional project as much as a fixed text. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

- **1965:** *The Friedman Lectures on Cryptology*. A six-lecture retrospective course: an oral performance, typescript, and training work whose textual manifestation was subsequently declassified. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

### Essays, polemics, and historical investigations

- **1920:** *“Saying It” in Cipher*. A popular magazine article published under “Cora J. Jensen”; the attribution to Friedman is probable rather than secure and should remain marked as such. [Bibliographic attribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Friedman)

- **1957:** *The Shakespearean Ciphers Examined: An Analysis of Cryptographic Systems Used as Evidence That Some Author Other Than William Shakespeare Wrote the Plays Commonly Attributed to Him*. A collaborative scholarly polemic by William and Elizebeth Friedman, using cryptanalysis to test—and reject—Baconian cipher claims. [Bibliographic record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Friedman)

- **1959:** *“Acrostics, Anagrams, and Chaucer.”* A philological-cryptanalytic article made jointly with Elizebeth Friedman, extending the couple’s examination of alleged literary ciphers. [Voynich and Friedman bibliography](https://www.voynich.nu/hist/reeds/reeds_biblio.html)

- **1961:** *“Codes and Ciphers (Cryptology).”* An encyclopaedia article and concise public exposition of the field, later circulated by NSA as a separate reprint. [NSA publications inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Publications/)

### Long investigations and collective cryptanalytic projects

- **1923:** *Solution of the Hebern wired-rotor cipher machine*. A cryptanalytic result rather than a conventional text: Friedman reconstructed the system from message evidence and developed coincidence techniques in doing so. It resists treatment as a solitary “work” because the surviving obra consists of analysis, methods, and institutional records rather than one stable publication. [NSA historical assessment](https://www.nsa.gov/Portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/reports-research/FOLDER_506/41788439082745.pdf)

- **1930s–1940:** *PURPLE cryptanalytic project*. Friedman organized and directed the SIS effort that reconstructed Japan’s diplomatic cipher system before Pearl Harbor. The result was a collective institutional creation—not Friedman's unaided obra—and individual technical contributions by Frank Rowlett, Solomon Kullback, Abraham Sinkov, Genevieve Grotjan, and others must not be absorbed into his name. [NSA Friedman collection overview](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/)

- **1944–1969:** *Voynich Manuscript study group and artificial-language hypothesis*. A prolonged, unfinished research project involving Friedman, Elizebeth Friedman, John Tiltman, and other cryptanalysts. It produced working files, conferences, and hypotheses but no decipherment, so its resistance to being called a completed work is essential to its classification. [Annotated Voynich bibliography](https://www.voynich.nu/hist/reeds/reeds_biblio.html)

- **1946:** *Bletchley Park Diary*. A private documentary record of Friedman’s postwar British mission rather than a publication; its later editing and circulation are manifestations distinct from the original diary. [NSA supplemental collection inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Supplemental-Correspondence/)

### Cryptographic machines and technical systems

- **Riverbank period, date not fixed:** *Polyalphabet wheel*. A mechanical cryptanalytic aid consisting initially of a rubber-faced alphabet wheel and later multiple wheels; the surviving description establishes the invention but not a secure single completion date. [NSA historical account](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/cryptologic-spectrum/legendary_william_friedman.pdf)

- **1932–1936:** *Converter M-134 and M-134A*. Rotor-cipher systems using electrical or tape control of rotor displacement. They are engineered manifestations of Friedman's “Cryptographic System” project, but later machines incorporated work by George A. Graham, Frank Rowlett, and others. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1935–1944:** *SIGABA / Converter M-134C*. A secure rotor-machine system emerging from Friedman's concept of irregular electrical rotor stepping and subsequent work by Frank Rowlett and Navy collaborators. It is a collective technical Gesamtkunstwerk—mechanism, circuitry, operating protocol, and secret patent history—and exclusive attribution to Friedman would be misleading. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1941:** *SISDE-30 authentication device*. A message-authentication apparatus credited in the archive to Friedman and Rosen; it survives as description and diagram rather than as an independently published work. [NSA patent and equipment inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Patent-Equipment/)

- **1942:** *Converter M-228 / SIGCUM cryptographic system*. A machine project developed with Frank B. Rowlett; the patent record calls it the basic invention embodied in M-228, making joint attribution necessary. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1944:** *Converter M-325 / SIGFOY*. An electrical rotor cryptograph associated with Friedman's patent application of August 1944. The project became a machine and a patent, two related but nonidentical manifestations. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

### Patented inventions and legal manifestations

- **1924:** *Secret Signaling System—Apparatus for Automatically Enciphering and Deciphering Messages* (U.S. Patent 1,516,180). A teleprinter cipher mechanism made with Louis M. Evans, introducing an intermittent third key-tape transmitter. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1925:** *Secret-Signaling Apparatus for Automatically Enciphering and Deciphering Messages* (U.S. Patent 1,522,775). A related automatic teleprinter-cipher apparatus using a connection-changing switchboard. [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1522775)

- **1925:** *Printing Telegraph System* (U.S. Patent 1,530,660). A printing-telegraph invention using carrier frequencies; it is a communications apparatus, not primarily a written cryptologic work. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1926:** *Alphabetical Chart* (U.S. Patent 1,608,590). A coding instrument for composing two-letter differential symbols. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1928:** *Printing Telegraph System* (U.S. Patent 1,694,874). A second, technically distinct printing-telegraph invention employing a modulated carrier; identical titles should not obscure that the patents claim different mechanisms. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1932:** *Cryptograph Enciphering and Deciphering Device* (U.S. Patent 1,857,374). A Baudot-based cryptographic apparatus made with George A. Graham and Louis M. Evans and designed to work with a typewriter keyboard. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1936:** *Cryptographic System* (U.S. Patent 2,028,772). Made with George A. Graham, this patent covers a key-tape-controlled rotor system associated with the early M-134. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1937:** *Message Authentication System* (U.S. Patent 2,080,416). An apparatus combining keyboard, punched-card control, and adding-machine mechanisms; the NSA patent index describes it as an early use of punched cards for cryptographic keying. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1938:** *Cryptographic Apparatus* (U.S. Patent 2,139,676). A single-rotor apparatus with mechanical cam-wheel keying and a translator mechanism. [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2139676A/en)

- **1938:** *Cryptographic Device* (U.S. Patent 2,140,424). A separate cryptographic-device patent granted the same year; it must not be silently merged with Patent 2,139,676 merely because their titles are similar. [NSA patent and equipment inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Patent-Equipment/)

- **1939:** *Electrical Switching Mechanism* (U.S. Patent 2,166,137). Made with Frank B. Rowlett, this mechanism adapted electrical sorting equipment to randomize punched cards. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1940:** *Electric Control System for Tabulating Cards, Documents, and the Like* (U.S. Patent 2,224,646). Made with V. E. Whitman, this is a punched-card control apparatus whose relation to cryptologic practice is functional rather than literary. [Friedman patent list](https://www.cryptome.hope-tindall.org/wff-patents.htm)

- **1946:** *Cryptographic Device* (U.S. Patent 2,395,863). The legal manifestation of the M-138 strip-cipher device; the patent’s title is generic, while the made object is a particular manual cipher apparatus. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1949:** *System for Enciphering Facsimiles* (U.S. Patent 2,465,367). A facsimile-encryption system made with Joseph O. Mauborgne; the joint patent embodies one branch of Friedman's wartime telephoto project. [NSA patent and equipment inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Patent-Equipment/)

- **1950:** *Authenticating Device* (U.S. Patent 2,518,458). An electrical mechanism for authenticating messages, documented through an unusually extensive sequence of drafts, secrecy orders, amendments, and patent-office actions. [NSA patent and equipment inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Patent-Equipment/)

- **1951:** *Facsimile Enciphering System* (U.S. Patent 2,552,548). A system for encrypting facsimile or telephoto transmissions by polarity reversal. [NSA patent and equipment inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Patent-Equipment/)

- **1959:** *Electrical Cryptograph* (U.S. Patent 2,877,565). The patent manifestation of the M-325/SIGFOY project, filed in 1944 but granted fifteen years later. [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2877565A/en)

- **1933/2000:** *Cryptographic System* (U.S. Patent 6,097,812). Filed in 1933 for electrically controlled aperiodic rotor stepping but kept secret until its grant in 2000; the extraordinary gap makes filing date and public manifestation equally necessary dates. [NSA patent index](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/friedman-documents/patent-equipment/FOLDER_076/41709289074860.pdf)

- **1936/2000:** *Cryptographs* (U.S. Patent 6,130,946). Filed in 1936 and withheld under secrecy until 2000, this patent concerns differential cam-wheel control of cryptographic rotors. [Friedman patent list](https://www.cryptome.hope-tindall.org/wff-patents.htm)

- **1944/2001:** *Cryptographic System* (U.S. Patent 6,175,625). The delayed patent manifestation associated with SIGABA, made jointly with Frank B. Rowlett and granted decades after both the wartime machine and Friedman's death; neither the late patent nor Friedman’s senior position justifies erasing the system’s collective authorship. [NSA patent and equipment inventory](https://www.nsa.gov/Helpful-Links/NSA-FOIA/Declassification-Transparency-Initiatives/Historical-Releases/Friedman-Documents/Patent-Equipment/)
