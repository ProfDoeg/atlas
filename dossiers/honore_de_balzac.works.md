## Works by Honoré de Balzac

### The encompassing project: *La Comédie humaine*

- **1833–1850:** *La Comédie humaine*. An unfinished Gesamtkunstwerk of interlocking fiction, social taxonomy, philosophy, and analysis: Balzac repeatedly renamed, regrouped, and revised earlier texts for it. The title emerged in 1841; the 1842–1848 Furne edition established its principal architecture, while Balzac’s catalogue retained many unwritten works.
- **1842:** *Avant-propos de La Comédie humaine*. A manifesto for the cycle: it explains the returning characters, the classification of social “species,” and the ambition to write the history omitted by historians—the history of manners.

- **1839–1841:** *Le Curé de village*. A novel first serialized, then expanded and revised.

### Letters and autobiographical records

- **1809–1850:** *Correspondance*. Thousands of letters form a documentary corpus rather than a single work designed by Balzac; the modern Pléiade edition prints both his letters and, where needed, those of correspondents, so the editorial corpus is not wholly authored by him.
- **1832–1848:** *Lettres à Madame Hanska*. A vast epistolary sequence, historically published as a separate corpus; it records love, business, travel, composition, and self-fashioning but was not made as a public book.
- **1830–1848:** *Album de voyage* and travel notebooks. Working records of journeys and observations rather than finished travel books; titles and boundaries are editorial.
- **1836:** *Voyage en Sardaigne*. Letters and notes connected with Balzac’s speculative journey to investigate mining slag; they constitute a travel-and-business dossier more readily than a stable literary work.
- **1847–1848:** *Lettre sur Kiew*. A travel account in epistolary form, left incomplete and published posthumously.
- **Various dates:** Dedications, prefaces, prospectuses, publishing contracts, petitions, and corrected proofs. These are real acts of authorship and book-making, but not securely enumerable as independent works: many are manifestations within other obras, while the proofs of the “Furne corrigé” materially revise *La Comédie humaine* itself.

### Material and entrepreneurial creations

- **1826–1828:** Balzac’s printing house and type-foundry enterprise. This was a made commercial and technological undertaking—not a text—and it produced printed matter for other authors as well as severe debts; individual impressions are products of the shop, not automatically works authored by Balzac.
- **1835–1836:** *La Chronique de Paris*. A periodical enterprise directed and partly written by Balzac with numerous collaborators; authorship belongs article by article, while the journal as a whole is a collective manifestation.
- **1836–1840:** *Société des gens de lettres* interventions. Balzac helped make institutional protocols, petitions, and claims for literary property; these acts resist classification as individual literary works even where his drafting can be documented.
- **1846–1850:** The house at 18 rue Fortunée. Balzac bought, furnished, and repeatedly redesigned this domestic environment for life with Éveline Hanska. It is a material project and self-fashioned interior, not solely an architectural work authored by him.
