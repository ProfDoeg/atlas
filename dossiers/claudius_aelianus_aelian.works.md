## Works by Claudius Aelianus

### Animal miscellany

- **Date unknown; early third century CE:** *On the Characteristics of Animals* (*Περὶ ζῴων ἰδιότητος*; conventionally *De natura animalium*). An obra in seventeen books: a deliberately varied prose compilation of animal lore, marvels, moral examples, and religious observations, rather than a systematic zoological treatise; it survives substantially intact and is established in the modern Teubner edition of García Valdés, Llera Fueyo, and Rodríguez-Noriega Guillén ([De Gruyter](https://www.degruyter.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110973570/html)).

### Historical and cultural miscellany

- **Date unknown; early third century CE:** *Various History* or *Historical Miscellany* (*Ποικίλη ἱστορία*; *Varia historia*). An obra in fourteen books comprising anecdotes, biographies, customs, marvels, sayings, and moral exempla; the transmitted text is not the whole thing Aelian made, since Books 1–2 are relatively full while the archetype appears to have excerpted Books 3–14, and additional material survives as quotations and fragments ([Dilts’s critical edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35228657c); [transmission analysis](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/77755/1/Heraclides_epitome_of_Aristotle_V4.pdf)).

### Fictional epistolary cycle

- **Date unknown; late second or early third century CE:** *From Aelian’s Rustic Letters* (*Ἐκ τῶν Αἰλιανοῦ ἀγροικικῶν ἐπιστολῶν*). A surviving cycle of twenty literary letters spoken by fictional inhabitants of an idealized Attic countryside—not documentary correspondence but an interconnected epistolary creation. Attribution to Aelian, once disputed, is now generally accepted; the manuscript title’s “from” may mean that the twenty letters are only a selection from a larger lost collection, so the boundaries of the original obra remain uncertain ([Drago edition review](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/2026/2026.02.06/); [manuscript-tradition study](https://clahresearch.wordpress.com/2018/10/22/aelian-and-philostratus-contrasting-manuscript-traditions/)).

### Fragmentary theological-historical works

- **Date unknown:** *On Providence* (*Περὶ προνοίας*). A lost obra known through excerpts and citations, especially in the *Suda*; its surviving manifestations present historical and marvelous examples of divine governance rather than a continuously recoverable treatise ([Digital Suda witness catalogue](https://www.lagl.org/tools/suda/works_catalog.php?what=urn%3Acts%3AgreekLit%3Atlg0545.suda001); [Domingo-Forasté edition review](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/1995/1995.09.06/)).

- **Date unknown:** *On Divine Manifestations* (*Περὶ θείων ἐναργειῶν*). A lost collection or treatise about perceptible divine interventions, now represented by only a few assigned fragments. Because neither its original scale nor its organization can be reconstructed, it resists classification beyond “fragmentarily witnessed obra” ([fragment-edition assessment](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/1995/1995.09.06/); [discussion of the assigned fragments](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/77755/1/Heraclides_epitome_of_Aristotle_V4.pdf)).

### Political invective

- **Shortly after 222 CE:** *Indictment of Gynnis* (*Κατηγορία τοῦ γύννιδος*; title also rendered *Indictment of the Effeminate Man*). A written political invective, cast as an accusation against the recently killed tyrant generally identified as Emperor Elagabalus; it is wholly lost and known only because Philostratus describes Aelian reading it aloud, so it was an accusatory literary performance rather than an actual judicial indictment ([Philostratus, *Lives of the Sophists* 2.31.2](https://topostext.org/work/224); [Society for Classical Studies](https://classicalstudies.org/annual-meeting/573smith)).

### Material that resists work-status

- **Date unknown:** Unassigned Aelianic fragments. The standard fragment edition prints hundreds of passages attributed simply to Aelian, chiefly through the *Suda*, without securely attaching them to a named work; many may derive from the fuller *Various History*, while some attributions or fragment boundaries are doubtful. These are textual manifestations of lost writing, not demonstrably separate obras, and therefore cannot responsibly be inflated into additional titles ([Domingo-Forasté, *Epistulae et fragmenta*](https://catalogo.upsa.es/cgi-bin/koha/opac-detail.pl?biblionumber=131060); [Nesselrath’s critical review](https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/1995/1995.09.06/)).
