## Works by Robert Hooke

### Printed scientific obras

- **1661:** *An Attempt for the Explication of the Phaenomena, Observable in an Experiment Published by the Honourable Robert Boyle*. A scientific pamphlet on capillary action and Hooke’s first separately printed work; its experiment originated in Boyle’s circle, but the explanatory text is Hooke’s. [Keynes’s bibliography identifies it as his earliest publication](https://archive.org/details/b3045684x).

- **1665:** *Micrographia: or Some Physiological Descriptions of Minute Bodies Made by Magnifying Glasses*. A Gesamtkunstwerk of experimental report, natural philosophy, instrument-making and monumental images; Hooke composed the observations and drew the subjects, although the translation of his drawings into engraved plates involved unnamed craftsmen. [The Royal Society’s complete first-edition copy](https://ttp.royalsociety.org/ttp/ttp.html?id=HookeMicrographia) preserves the text and plates.

- **1667:** *A Method for Making a History of the Weather*. A protocol prescribing coordinated, standardized meteorological observations and the instruments and tabular forms required to record them; Thomas Sprat printed it in his *History of the Royal-Society*. [The Royal Society catalogue attributes the paper to Hooke](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/clp_20/classified-papers-volume-20-robert-hookes-papers).

- **1674:** *Animadversions on the First Part of the Machina Coelestis of the Honourable, Learned, and Deservedly Famous Astronomer Johannes Hevelius … Together with an Explication of Some Instruments*. A polemical astronomical treatise and instrument description defending telescopic sights against Hevelius’s naked-eye practice. It therefore acts partly as a manifesto for instrument-mediated observation. [Hooke bibliography and digital copy](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1674:** *An Attempt to Prove the Motion of the Earth from Observations*. An astronomical report on Hooke’s zenith observations of γ Draconis, intended to demonstrate annual parallax; the measurements did not furnish a conclusive proof, so the title records a project as much as an achievement. [Max Planck Institute digital edition](https://echo.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/ECHOdocuView?url=/permanent/library/XX5BK5U4/pageimg).

- **1676:** *A Description of Helioscopes, and Some Other Instruments*. An illustrated instrument treatise describing apparatus for observing the Sun and measuring angular dimensions. [Keynes-based list and digital copy](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1677:** *Lampas: or, Descriptions of Some Mechanical Improvements of Lamps & Waterpoises*. A compound technological obra: descriptions of improved lamps and hydrostatic instruments, accompanied by claims about combustion and air. [Keynes-based bibliography and scan](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1678:** *Lectures de Potentia Restitutiva, or of Spring*. A printed lecture-treatise on elastic bodies that states the relation now called Hooke’s law; it also includes material on gravity and mechanical invention. [Keynes-based bibliography and scan](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1678:** *Cometa: Containing a Discourse of the Comet Which Appeared in the Years 1677 and 1678*. An observational and theoretical astronomical discourse, later incorporated into *Lectiones Cutlerianae*. Its identity is bibliographically awkward because it circulated as a separately titled component as well as part of the collection. [The collected volume is catalogued and digitized here](https://archive.org/details/LectionesCutler00Hook).

- **1679:** *Lectiones Cutlerianae, or, A Collection of Lectures: Physical, Mechanical, Geographical & Astronomical*. A collected obra assembling six previously issued or separately titled Cutlerian discourses—*Animadversions*, *An Attempt to Prove the Motion of the Earth*, *A Description of Helioscopes*, *Lampas*, *Lectures de Potentia Restitutiva* and *Cometa*—with further material. It is one made book but not six new works. [Complete digital copy](https://archive.org/details/LectionesCutler00Hook).

- **1679–1682:** *Philosophical Collections*. Seven numbers of a scientific periodical edited by Hooke during the interruption of the *Philosophical Transactions*. This is both editorial project and collective manifestation: Hooke selected, arranged and contributed material, but did not author everything it contains. [The Biodiversity Heritage Library describes Hooke’s seven-number series](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/62536).

- **1681:** *Preface to An Historical Relation of the Island Ceylon in the East-Indies*. A paratext to Robert Knox’s narrative; Hooke also appears to have edited and shepherded Knox’s book through the press, but the narrative itself remains Knox’s obra rather than Hooke’s. [Hooke bibliography and digitized edition](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1680–1683:** *The English Atlas*. An eleven-volume universal atlas projected by Moses Pitt, of which only four volumes appeared before Pitt’s bankruptcy. Hooke examined maps and copy and wrote extensive directions for regional descriptions, making this an unfinished collaborative project rather than a Hooke-authored book; he was reportedly never fully paid. [Account of Hooke’s role and the surviving volumes](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html#moses-pitts-atlas).

### Papers, lectures and posthumously constituted texts

- **1662–1699:** *Royal Society papers*. A large body of experiment reports, protocols, observations, lectures, memoranda, drawings and instrument descriptions rather than a single fixed work. The Royal Society’s classified-paper catalogue preserves separately described items—including papers on air resistance, weather history, astronomy, light, mechanics, fossils and earthquakes—but the archive is the residue of Hooke’s work as Curator of Experiments, not an authorially constituted “collected works.” [Royal Society, *Classified Papers*, volume 20](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/clp_20/classified-papers-volume-20-robert-hookes-papers).

- **1665–1703:** *Contributions to the Philosophical Transactions*. Numerous observations, letters, apparatus descriptions and experiment reports appeared under Hooke’s name or were reported by the journal’s editor. The boundaries of authorship are unstable because some items are editorial summaries of oral demonstrations, some joint communications, and some reports about Hooke rather than texts written by him. [Gunther’s collected documentary volumes and the standard bibliography are identified in the *Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Hooke.pdf).

- **1670s–1690s:** *Cutlerian lectures not separately printed during Hooke’s lifetime*. Discourses on earthquakes, memory, light, gravity, navigation, mechanical invention and natural history survive through manuscripts and later editorial selections. Their titles and textual boundaries often belong partly to Richard Waller’s posthumous arrangement, so they resist treatment as securely fixed lifetime works. [Waller’s 1705 edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=6xVTAAAAcAAJ).

- **1672–1683:** *Memorandum book or diary*. A working manuscript combining appointments, accounts, health notes, experiments, building business and compressed records of conversation. It is a documentary practice rather than a polished literary work; the traditional Robinson–Adams edition covers only 1672–1680 and omits material subsequently published by Felicity Henderson. [London Archives manuscript and editorial history](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html#the-diaries).

- **1688–1690; 1692–1693:** *Later diaries*. Intermittent manuscript diary sequences known chiefly through R. T. Gunther’s transcription. They are incomplete survivals, and the published transcription is acknowledged to contain inaccuracies. [Bibliographical account](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html#the-diaries).

- **c. 1690s:** *An account of his own life*. An unfinished autobiographical project surviving among Hooke’s papers and printed by Waller. It resists classification as a completed autobiography because Hooke neither finished nor published it. [Waller’s *Posthumous Works*](https://books.google.com/books?id=6xVTAAAAcAAJ).

- **1705:** *The Posthumous Works of Robert Hooke*. A posthumous editorial construction by Richard Waller containing Hooke’s lectures, discourses and biographical materials. Its constituent papers are Hooke’s, but the selection, titles, sequence and book as book are Waller’s manifestation, not an obra completed by Hooke. [Digitized edition and bibliographical description](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1726:** *Philosophical Experiments and Observations of the Late Eminent Dr Robert Hooke*. William Derham’s posthumous collection of Hooke’s experimental papers. Like Waller’s volume, it witnesses otherwise dispersed works but should not be silently treated as a book Hooke himself designed. [Digitized edition and bibliographical description](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

### Drawings, maps and visual works

- **1665:** *Drawings for Micrographia*. A corpus of microscopic, telescopic and material studies—including the flea, louse, ant, cork, moulds, nettle, needle point, lunar surface and patterned textiles—made as scientific evidence and translated into engraved plates. Individual sheets are not all securely extant, and responsibility for engraving is distinct from Hooke’s authorship of the observations and drawings. [Royal Society digital *Micrographia*](https://ttp.royalsociety.org/ttp/ttp.html?id=HookeMicrographia).

- **1666:** *Plan for rebuilding the City of London*. An unexecuted urban project proposing a substantially regularized post-Fire street plan. It is an obra as project, although property rights, compensation and cost prevented its realization. [Westminster School Archives’ architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s–1690s:** *Architectural and mechanical drawings*. Surviving and recorded plans, elevations, instrument sketches and working diagrams form a dispersed graphic practice rather than a closed oeuvre; some sheets formerly assigned to Wren have been connected with Hooke, while other attributions remain disputed. [Batten’s architectural study and illustrations](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

### Instruments, machines and experimental constructions

- **c. 1658–1659:** *Boyle–Hooke air pump*. A pneumatic machine designed and constructed by Hooke for Robert Boyle’s experiments. It is a collaborative experimental object conventionally attached to “Boyle’s” investigations, and no securely identified original survives. [Westminster School Archives’ account of Hooke’s achievements](https://www.roberthooke.org/achievements-h2.html).

- **1660s:** *Compound microscope and illumination apparatus*. Hooke improved and used a compound microscope with a lamp, globe and condensing system for the observations published in *Micrographia*. The instrument was a working assemblage rather than a wholly unprecedented invention, and its surviving identity is principally textual and pictorial. [Royal Society digital *Micrographia*](https://ttp.royalsociety.org/ttp/ttp.html?id=HookeMicrographia).

- **1660s:** *Wheel barometer*. A barometric instrument translating changes in mercury level into rotary pointer movement. Sources describe Hooke as its inventor or improver; that ambiguity should not be converted into an uncontested claim of sole invention. [Westminster School Archives’ instrument list](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s:** *Anemometer*. An instrument for measuring wind strength, devised for Hooke’s coordinated weather-observation project. It is both a machine and part of the meteorological protocol. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s:** *Oat-beard hygrometer*. A humidity indicator exploiting the movement of an oat awn as atmospheric moisture changed. Hooke devised or improved it as one component of his weather-history apparatus. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Weather clock*. A mechanism intended to record meteorological variables automatically. It is best understood as an evolving experimental project; descriptions and reconstructions survive more securely than an original complete machine. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Universal joint*. A coupling permitting rotary movement between angled shafts, described and applied by Hooke. “Hooke’s joint” names his manifestation of a mechanism with antecedents, so invention should not be understood as creation without precedent. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Screw-divided quadrant*. A precision astronomical quadrant using a screw mechanism to subdivide angular readings. Hooke’s claim concerns a new method of division and reading rather than the invention of the quadrant itself. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Reflecting quadrant*. An astronomical measuring instrument employing reflection. It belongs to a field of competing and overlapping designs, so Hooke’s construction should not be conflated with every later reflecting quadrant. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Marine telescope*. A telescope adapted for observations at sea. It survives chiefly as a described design or project, not as a securely identified extant object. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Gregorian reflecting telescope*. Hooke constructed a working telescope from James Gregory’s optical design. The design is Gregory’s; Hooke’s obra is its technically successful material realization. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Odometer*. A geared device for recording the distance travelled by a wheeled vehicle. Hooke’s contribution was a particular construction or improvement, not the invention of distance-counting mechanisms as a class. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1660s–1670s:** *Otocousticon*. An acoustic hearing aid intended to augment the ear. It is known principally through Hooke’s experimental and documentary record and resists identification with one stable surviving object. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **c. 1660s–1670s:** *Anchor escapement*. A clock escapement associated with Hooke and central to improved pendulum clocks. Priority and exact authorship have long been contested, particularly in relation to William Clement, so it is safer to identify Hooke’s design work than to declare an undisputed sole invention. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **c. 1658–1675:** *Spring-regulated watch*. A portable timekeeper governed by a balance spring. Hooke pursued the project over many years, but priority was bitterly disputed with Christiaan Huygens and the surviving record does not support silently awarding exclusive invention to either man. [Instrument catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- **1670s:** *Marine barometer*. A barometer adapted to operate aboard ship. It belongs to Hooke’s continuing program of making unstable natural quantities mechanically legible. [Royal Society catalogue of Hooke papers](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/clp_20/classified-papers-volume-20-robert-hookes-papers).

- **1670s:** *Helioscopes*. Several instruments for viewing and measuring the Sun safely, described rather than reducible to one standardized artifact. They are a family of experimental constructions as well as the subject of Hooke’s 1676 book. [Bibliographical description](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1670s:** *Improved lamps and “waterpoises”*. Experimental lamps and hydrostatic balances described in *Lampas*. The printed descriptions preserve a technological project whose individual physical specimens are not securely identified. [Bibliographical description](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- **1670s–1680s:** *Artificial-muscle, flying-machine and diving-machine projects*. Hooke proposed and tested mechanisms inspired by animal motion, flight and underwater work. These resist being called completed inventions: much of the obra consists precisely in experimental models, trials and unrealized designs recorded in lectures and papers. [Royal Society catalogue of Hooke’s papers](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/clp_20/classified-papers-volume-20-robert-hookes-papers).

### Architecture and urban works

- **1666 onward:** *Survey and reconstruction of the City of London*. A vast body of boundary-setting, foundation-laying, regulation, valuation and supervision undertaken as City Surveyor after the Great Fire. It is collective civic production rather than one building or wholly personal obra; Hooke’s exact share in many structures cannot be separated cleanly from that of Wren, fellow surveyors, builders and owners. [Architecture catalogue and attribution warning](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **late 1660s–1670s:** *Cheapside and Holborn conduits*. Civic water structures constructed under Hooke’s direction. They belong to the assimilated infrastructure of the rebuilt City and are consequently less bounded than a titled architectural monument. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **late 1660s–1670s:** *North-bank Thames improvements*. Proposed and supervised works involving quays, paving and riverfront infrastructure. This is an urban project without a single fixed title or object boundary. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1667–1670s:** *Fleet River canalization*. A civic-engineering work undertaken with Christopher Wren, transforming the polluted open Fleet into a canal. It was a public-health and urban-infrastructure manifestation but proved commercially unsuccessful; authorship is explicitly collaborative. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1671 onward:** *Reconstruction of Bridewell*. An institutional rebuilding for which Hooke acted as architect. The complex was altered and lost, and the documentary record does not reduce neatly to one surviving design. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1672:** *Repairs to Barbers’ Hall*. Architectural alterations in which Hooke was involved; “involved” is the warranted category, since the record does not establish sole design authorship. The building was later demolished. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1673:** *Screen for Merchant Taylors’ Hall*. A designed architectural furnishing rather than a building. The screen was destroyed during the Second World War. [Batten’s catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **1673:** *Navy Victualling Office, Seething Lane*. A rebuilding project with which Hooke was involved, although the specialist catalogue cautions that he probably had no part in its construction. It therefore resists inclusion as a securely authored building. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1674–1676:** *Montagu House, Bloomsbury*. A great house designed by Hooke for Ralph Montagu and documented by roughly 150 diary references. Fire destroyed it in 1686; whether Hooke participated in the similar reconstruction, and how much of his exterior survived into that rebuilding, remain disputed. [Batten’s documentary catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **1674–1677:** *Bethlehem Hospital, Moorfields*. A monumental hospital designed by Hooke and later demolished in 1814. Its institutional scale and collaborative construction make it more than a drawing but less than an exclusively handmade personal object. [Batten’s catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **1675:** *Setting-out of the Royal Observatory, Greenwich*. Hooke laid out or “set out” the building, but whether its architectural design was his, Wren’s or another person’s remains uncertain. It should therefore be named as a documented intervention, not confidently as “Hooke’s observatory.” [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s:** *House for Sir Walter Yonge in Devon*. A house design recorded in Hooke’s architectural corpus, but its location and realization have not been securely identified. It survives primarily as a project. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s:** *House for Lord Oxford, Privy Gardens, Whitehall*. A domestic architectural work attributed to Hooke in the specialist lists; its exact chronology and surviving fabric are uncertain. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s:** *House for Sir Richard Edgcumbe, Cornwall*. A house design associated with the Mount Edgcumbe family. The source lists a design rather than an unequivocally executed Hooke building. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s:** *Church for Sir John Lowther, Westmorland*. An architectural design whose execution and present identification are uncertain. It is consequently an obra as project. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s:** *Sherwood Place, Brentwood*. A house design listed in Hooke’s architectural corpus, with no securely established surviving building. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s:** *House for Lord Burlington, probably Londesborough House*. A design whose identification as Londesborough House is itself qualified as probable. The uncertainty belongs in the work’s name rather than being silently removed. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1670s–1680s:** *Building at Magdalene College, Cambridge, possibly the Pepys Library*. Hooke produced a design for the college, but identification of the designed building with the Pepys Library is uncertain. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1676; 1688; 1693:** *Alterations at Westminster Abbey*. Work encompassing repaving of the choir and lantern, the north window in collaboration with Wren, Henry VII’s Chapel and precinct buildings. This is a sequence of interventions rather than a single independent building. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1677:** *The Monument to the Great Fire of London*. A commemorative column, surveying instrument and potential astronomical apparatus. Convention long credited Wren, but Hooke’s diary and contemporary documentation support a major or principal Hooke design role; because the work emerged from their official collaboration, attribution remains historically entangled. [Architecture catalogue and attribution discussion](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- **1678:** *Royal College of Physicians, Warwick Lane*. A purpose-designed institutional building attributed definitively to Hooke by Batten’s architectural study. It was demolished in the nineteenth century. [Batten’s catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **c. 1678–1680:** *St Mary Magdalene, Willen*. A church designed for Richard Busby and one of the few buildings firmly assigned to Hooke. Nineteenth-century alteration added an apse and removed the tower cupola, so the surviving church is not an intact manifestation of his design. [Batten’s catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **1679 onward:** *Ragley Hall and gardens*. A country house and landscape design for the Earl of Conway. The east front substantially preserves Hooke’s work, while the west front was altered around 1750 and the present gardens are nineteenth-century replacements. [Ragley’s institutional history](https://www.ragley.co.uk/the-estate/) and [Batten’s catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **1693:** *Alderman Aske’s Hospital or Almshouses, Hoxton*. An institutional complex designed by Hooke and demolished in the nineteenth century. [Batten’s catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- **1666–1703:** *Supervision of Wren’s City churches*. Hooke supervised construction at more than thirty churches and worked as Wren’s junior collaborator at St Paul’s Cathedral. These activities made the buildings materially possible but do not turn every church into a sole-authored Hooke obra; the category is supervision and collaborative realization. [Architecture catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

### Sources

- Geoffrey Keynes, *A Bibliography of Dr Robert Hooke* (Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1960), the standard enumerative bibliography of Hooke’s printed writings; [Wellcome Collection record](https://content.www.wellcomecollection.org/works/bnqky2tw) and [digital loan copy](https://archive.org/details/b3045684x).

- M. I. Batten, “The Architecture of Dr Robert Hooke, F.R.S.,” *The Walpole Society* 25 (1936–1937), pp. 83–113, the foundational specialist catalogue of Hooke’s architecture; [transcription with illustrations](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/margaret-battens-the-architecture-of-dr-robert-hooke/).

- The Royal Society, *Science in the Making: Classified Papers, Volume 20, Robert Hooke’s Papers, 1662–1699*, archival item-level catalogue; [collection description](https://makingscience.royalsociety.org/items/clp_20/classified-papers-volume-20-robert-hookes-papers).

- Robert Hooke Compendium, “Bibliography,” a synthesis explicitly based on Keynes and documenting the diaries, Pitt atlas and digital copies; [online bibliography](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- Westminster School Archives, “Architecture,” a work list derived from Margaret ’Espinasse and qualified against Batten; [online catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-architecture/).

- Westminster School Archives, “Inventions,” a categorized list of instruments Hooke invented or improved; [online catalogue](https://www.roberthooke.org.uk/hookes-inventions/).

- R. T. Gunther, *Early Science in Oxford*, especially volumes VI, VII, VIII, X and XIII, which collect Hooke’s papers, lectures, diaries and printed works; [volume X](https://archive.org/details/earlyscienceinox10gunt).

- Richard Waller, ed., *The Posthumous Works of Robert Hooke* (London, 1705), the principal early collection of Hooke’s unpublished lectures and autobiographical material; [digital copy](https://books.google.com/books?id=6xVTAAAAcAAJ).

- William Derham, ed., *Philosophical Experiments and Observations of the Late Eminent Dr Robert Hooke* (London, 1726), a second major posthumous collection; [bibliographical link and digital copy](https://hookiana.uk/bibliography.html).

- Charles Coulston Gillispie, ed., *Dictionary of Scientific Biography*, “Robert Hooke,” for the relationship among Keynes’s bibliography, Gunther’s documentary volumes and the posthumous collections; [article PDF](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/DSB/Hooke.pdf).
