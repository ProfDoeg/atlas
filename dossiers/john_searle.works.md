## Works by John Rogers Searle

### Monographs: sustained philosophical obras

- **1969:** *Speech Acts: An Essay in the Philosophy of Language*. A monograph developing the theory of speech acts from Searle’s Oxford dissertation; it treats speaking as rule-governed action rather than merely the transmission of propositions. [Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/searle-john-1932-2025/v-1/bibliography/searle-john-1932-bib)

- **1971:** *The Campus War: A Sympathetic Look at the University in Agony*. Political and institutional commentary on American university protest, distinct from Searle’s systematic philosophy. Some bibliographies date it 1972 because that is the year of the British Penguin manifestation; the American World Publishing edition appeared in 1971. [Philosophica bibliography](https://www.philosophica.info/voces/searle/Searle.html)

- **1979:** *Expression and Meaning: Studies in the Theory of Speech Acts*. A linked collection of philosophical essays extending and revising the project of *Speech Acts*, especially through accounts of indirect speech, metaphor, fiction, and classifications of illocutionary acts. [Cambridge University Press](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805213/13933/excerpt/9780521313933_excerpt.pdf)

- **1983:** *Intentionality: An Essay in the Philosophy of Mind*. A systematic monograph transferring the apparatus of conditions of satisfaction from speech acts to perception, belief, intention, and action. [Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/searle-john-1932-2025/v-1/bibliography/searle-john-1932-bib)

- **1992:** *The Rediscovery of the Mind*. A polemical monograph rejecting both dualism and reductive or computational accounts of mind while making consciousness central to its ontology. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262691543/the-rediscovery-of-the-mind/)

- **1995:** *The Construction of Social Reality*. A work of social ontology explaining institutional facts through collective intentionality, constitutive rules, and status functions. [Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/searle-john-1932-2025/v-1/bibliography/searle-john-1932-bib)

- **1998:** *Mind, Language and Society: Philosophy in the Real World*. A synthetic introductory obra joining Searle’s projects in mind, language, and social reality. Bibliographies also give 1999, reflecting the date of a British manifestation rather than a separate work. [Philosophica bibliography](https://www.philosophica.info/voces/searle/Searle.html)

- **2001:** *Rationality in Action*. A philosophical monograph, originating in the Jean Nicod Lectures, on practical reason, reasons for action, desire-independent motivation, and the “gap” between reasons and decisions. [MIT Press, Jean Nicod Lectures](https://mitpress.mit.edu/series/jean-nicod-lectures/)

- **2004:** *Mind: A Brief Introduction*. A compact survey and restatement of Searle’s philosophy of mind, written as an introduction but also arguing for his biological naturalism. [Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/49752)

- **2010:** *Making the Social World: The Structure of Human Civilization*. A sequel and substantial reconstruction of the project begun in *The Construction of Social Reality*, emphasizing declarations, deontic powers, political authority, and human rights. [Oxford University Press](https://academic.oup.com/book/5336)

- **2015:** *Seeing Things as They Are: A Theory of Perception*. A monograph defending direct realism and attacking the “Bad Argument” that perceptual experience places an intermediary between perceiver and world. [Oxford University Press](https://blog.oup.com/2015/01/perception-experience-intentionality-philosophy/)

### Lecture works and remade public texts

- **1984:** *Minds, Brains and Science*. The book manifestation of Searle’s six 1984 BBC Reith Lectures; it is therefore both a performed lecture series and a revised written work on mind, artificial intelligence, action, and the social sciences. [Open British National Bibliography](https://obnb.uk/a00237069-john-r-searle)

- **1997:** *The Mystery of Consciousness*. A book assembled by revising and connecting Searle’s reviews and exchanges about contemporary theories of consciousness; it resists classification as a wholly new monograph because much of its textual substance had already appeared in *The New York Review of Books*. [Berkeley consciousness bibliography](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~jfkihlstrom/ConsciousnessWeb/current_scene.htm)

- **2002:** *Consciousness and Language*. A thematically organized collection of Searle’s previously published essays, with the book functioning as a retrospective construction rather than a single continuously composed argument. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/consciousness-and-language/problem-of-consciousness/C5F7133F7282CF2F797AC1ECDD4C4EAD)

- **2004/2006:** *Freedom and Neurobiology: Reflections on Free Will, Language, and Political Power*. A two-lecture work first manifested in French in 2004 and issued in English by Columbia University Press in 2006; paperback and electronic manifestations carry later dates and are not new obras. [Columbia University Press](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/freedom-and-neurobiology/9780231137539/)

- **2008:** *Philosophy in a New Century: Selected Essays*. A curated collection of essays on language, mind, social ontology, and philosophical method; its unity is retrospective and editorial rather than that of a newly composed monograph. [Cambridge University Press](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/15917/excerpt/9780521515917_excerpt.pdf)

### Collaborative obra

- **1985:** *Foundations of Illocutionary Logic*. A jointly authored formal treatise by Searle and Daniel Vanderveken, converting speech-act theory into a systematic illocutionary logic; sole attribution to Searle would therefore be incorrect. [Encyclopedia of Philosophy bibliography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/searle-john-1932)

### Editorial projects

- **1971:** *The Philosophy of Language*. An anthology edited by Searle for the Oxford Readings in Philosophy series, not a sole-authored monograph; its made form is the selection and arrangement of other philosophers’ texts together with editorial framing. [CiNii Books author record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/author/DA00242054)

- **1980:** *Speech Act Theory and Pragmatics*. A scholarly collection edited by Searle, Ferenc Kiefer, and Manfred Bierwisch. Its authorship is distributed among editors and contributors, so the volume is an editorial project rather than a Searle-authored book. [Open British National Bibliography](https://obnb.uk/a00237069-john-r-searle)

### Arguments and philosophical devices that became works in their own right

- **1958:** *Proper Names*. An article proposing that ordinary proper names work through an open-ended cluster of descriptions rather than one defining description; later bibliographies treat it as an independent early work, not merely preparatory material for *Speech Acts*. [Encyclopedia of Philosophy bibliography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/humanities/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/searle-john-1932)

- **1977:** *Reiterating the Differences: A Reply to Derrida*. A polemical article answering Jacques Derrida’s criticism of speech-act theory. Searle later withheld permission for its reproduction in Derrida’s *Limited Inc*, so the absent reprint is not another manifestation and the resulting “Searle–Derrida debate” is a relation among texts, not a jointly made work. [John Searle bibliography and debate record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Searle)

- **1980:** *Minds, Brains, and Programs*. A journal article and argumentative construction introducing the Chinese-room thought experiment against “strong AI.” The Chinese room itself is not straightforwardly a text or independently published obra: it is an imaginary protocol—rules for manipulating symbols—made to perform a philosophical counterexample inside the article. [Cambridge DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00005756)

- **1987:** *Indeterminacy, Empiricism, and the First Person*. A journal article criticizing Quinean indeterminacy by appealing to first-person knowledge of meaning and intentional content. [Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://www.rep.routledge.com/articles/biographical/searle-john-1932-2025/v-1/bibliography/searle-john-1932-bib)

- **2014:** *What Your Computer Can’t Know*. An extended review essay on books by Luciano Floridi and Nick Bostrom that restates Searle’s opposition to attributing intrinsic understanding to digital computation; it is criticism and philosophical intervention rather than a book. [Bibliographic record](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2014/10/09/what-your-computer-cant-know/)
