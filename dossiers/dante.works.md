## Works by Dante Alighieri

### Composite poetic works

- **c. 1292–1295:** *Vita nuova* (also transmitted as *Vita nova*). A vernacular prosimetrum—31 lyric poems reorganized within prose narrative and self-commentary—rather than a simple poetry collection; its date and exact title-form remain debated. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)
- **c. 1307–1321:** *Comedìa* (later conventionally *Divina Commedia*). A three-part vernacular narrative poem—*Inferno*, *Purgatorio*, and *Paradiso*—whose hundred cantos form one architectonic obra rather than three independent books; its precise starting date and stages of circulation are uncertain. [Società Dantesca Italiana](https://www.dantesca.it/pubblicazioni/edizionenazionale/titoli.html)

### Lyric poems and editorial corpora

- **c. 1283–1308:** *Rime*. This title denotes the surviving vernacular lyrics attributed to Dante outside—or extracted from—the author-made structures of the *Vita nuova* and *Convivio*: it is a posthumous scholarly corpus, not a book assembled by Dante, and its boundaries change with editorial judgments about attribution and duplication. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html) [Enciclopedia Dantesca](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/rime_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/)
- **c. 1283–1289; lost:** *Sirventese naming sixty Florentine women*. Dante reports making this ranked occasional poem in the *Vita nuova*, but no independent text survives; it therefore resists treatment as an extant work and is known only through the later obra that mentions it. [Princeton Dante Project: *Vita nuova*](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)
- **c. 1305:** *Io sono stato con Amore insieme*. A vernacular sonnet answering Cino da Pistoia; it is also materially linked to Epistle III, so poem and letter constitute a small correspondence rather than an author-made “collection.” [Enciclopedia Dantesca: “Epistole”](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/epistole_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/)
- **c. 1307–1309:** *Amor, da che convien pur ch’io mi doglia*. A vernacular canzone associated with Dante’s stay in the Casentino and sent to Moroello Malaspina with Epistle IV; the poem is often called the “montanina.” [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)

### Unfinished intellectual projects

- **c. 1303–1307:** *Convivio*. An unfinished vernacular philosophical and encyclopedic prosimetrum: Dante projected fifteen treatises organized around fourteen canzoni but completed only four, making the unrealized plan part of the work’s identity. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)
- **c. 1304–1305:** *De vulgari eloquentia*. An unfinished Latin treatise and intervention in poetic practice, defending and theorizing an illustrious Italian vernacular; it breaks off in the second of four projected books. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)

### Political treatise

- **uncertain, probably c. 1310–1318:** *Monarchia*. A complete Latin treatise arguing for universal temporal monarchy and distinguishing imperial from papal authority; proposed dates range across Dante’s exile, so no single year is secure. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html) [Società Dantesca Italiana](https://www.dantesca.it/pubblicazioni/edizionenazionale/titoli.html)

### Letters, manifestos, and commissioned voices

- **spring 1304:** *Epistle I, to Cardinal Niccolò da Prato*. A collective political letter written in the name of the captain, council, and community of the exiled Florentine Whites; Dante’s authorship is traditional but the plural institutional voice makes it a commissioned intervention rather than an uncomplicated personal letter. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/cgi-bin/dante/DispMinorWork.pl?REF=I+1-9&TITLE=Epist)
- **1304:** *Epistle II, to Oberto and Guido, counts of Romena*. A Latin consolatory letter on the death of their uncle Alessandro; its dating is circumstantial rather than supplied by an authorial manuscript. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **c. 1305–1306:** *Epistle III, to Cino da Pistoia*. A Latin response on whether the soul can pass from one passion to another, sent with the sonnet *Io sono stato con Amore insieme*; its letter-and-poem form makes it a composite act of correspondence. [Enciclopedia Dantesca](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/epistole_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/)
- **c. 1307–1309:** *Epistle IV, to Moroello Malaspina*. A Latin accompanying letter for *Amor, da che convien pur ch’io mi doglia*; recipient, dating, and circumstances have required scholarly reconstruction. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **September–October 1310:** *Epistle V, to the princes and peoples of Italy*. A public Latin manifesto announcing Henry VII’s advent and urging political submission; it acts upon its audience rather than merely describing imperial theory. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **31 March 1311:** *Epistle VI, to the Florentines*. A public denunciation and prophetic warning addressed to Dante’s native city during its resistance to Henry VII—again closer to manifesto than private correspondence. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **17 April 1311:** *Epistle VII, to Emperor Henry VII*. A Latin political exhortation pressing Henry to attack Florence; it is a direct attempt to redirect imperial action. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **April 1311:** *Epistle VIII, to Empress Margaret of Brabant*. A formal Latin letter composed in the voice of Gherardesca, countess of Battifolle; Dante is understood as its ghostwriter, so the made thing bears another person’s public identity. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **April–May 1311:** *Epistle IX, to Empress Margaret of Brabant*. A second commissioned Latin letter in Countess Gherardesca’s voice, not straightforwardly a personal manifestation of Dante. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **18 May 1311:** *Epistle X, to Empress Margaret of Brabant*. The third surviving Latin letter made for Countess Gherardesca to send under her own name. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/b26.html)
- **after 20 April 1314:** *Epistle XI, to the Italian cardinals*. A Latin manifesto written after Clement V’s death, rebuking the cardinals and urging the election of an Italian pope. [Enciclopedia Dantesca](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/epistole_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/)
- **May 1315:** *Epistle XII, to a Florentine friend*. A Latin refusal of Florence’s humiliating terms for recalling exiles; because the addressee is unnamed, the conventional title describes rather than identifies the object. [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/eng/print/b26.html)
- **uncertain, c. 1316–1320:** *Epistle XIII, to Cangrande della Scala*. A dedication of *Paradiso* followed by an interpretive accessus and exposition; both the date and Dante’s authorship—especially of the extended exegetical portion—remain disputed, so it cannot safely be treated as an uncontested work. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html) [Italian Internet Culturale](https://www.internetculturale.it/directories/ViaggiNelTesto/dante/eng/print/b26.html)
- **c. 1290; lost:** *Letter to the leading citizens of Florence after Beatrice’s death*. The *Vita nuova* reports a Latin letter beginning with a biblical lament, but only its opening words and circumstances survive; its status is that of an attested lost communicative act, not an extant text. [Princeton Dante Project: *Vita nuova*](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)
- **date unknown; lost:** *Letter to Guido da Polenta*. Early biographical and manuscript testimony records other Dantean letters, including one associated with Guido, but the text is lost and its exact nature cannot now be securely classified. [Enciclopedia Dantesca](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/epistole_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/)

### Latin pastoral correspondence

- **1319–1320:** *Vidimus in nigris albo patiente lituris* (Dante’s first eclogue; conventionally *Egloga II* in the four-poem exchange). A Latin hexameter pastoral replying to Giovanni del Virgilio’s invitation to abandon vernacular poetry; numbering varies because editions sometimes count Giovanni’s initiating poem as the first eclogue. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html) [Enciclopedia Dantesca](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/egloghe_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/)
- **1320–1321:** *Velleribus Colchis prepes detectus Eous* (Dante’s second eclogue; conventionally *Egloga IV*). A second Latin pastoral response to Giovanni del Virgilio; the transmitted *Egloghe* are a four-poem exchange, but only two of its poems were made by Dante. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)

### Lecture made into a text

- **20 January 1320:** *Quaestio de aqua et terra*. The transmitted Latin text presents itself as Dante’s written redaction of a public cosmological disputation delivered at Verona; its authenticity has repeatedly been challenged, so it may be a record, reconstruction, or later pseudonymous manifestation rather than securely Dante’s obra. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html)

### Works of disputed attribution

- **probably 1280s:** *Il Fiore*. An anonymous reworking of the *Roman de la Rose* as a corona of 232 Italian sonnets; Gianfranco Contini and other scholars attribute it to Dante, while others reject or suspend that attribution, so it belongs at the contested edge of the corpus. [Società Dantesca Italiana](https://www.dantesca.it/pubblicazioni/edizionenazionale/titoli.html) [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/il-fiore/)
- **probably 1280s; incomplete transmission:** *Detto d’Amore*. A vernacular didactic poem in rhyming couplets, preserved without its beginning and apparently by the same anonymous author as *Il Fiore*; attribution to Dante therefore rises or falls with the disputed attribution of that work. [Princeton Dante Project](https://dante.princeton.edu/dante/pdp/minor.html) [Società Dantesca Italiana](https://www.dantesca.it/pubblicazioni/edizionenazionale/titoli.html)
