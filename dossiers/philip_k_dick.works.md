### Lost, unfinished, and unrealized novel-projects

- **Early 1950s:** *Return to Lilliput*. Reported early novel-project whose manuscript is lost; its survival even as a “work” is limited to bibliographical testimony. [S8]
- **1950s:** *Nicholas and the Higs*. Completed or substantially completed realist novel whose manuscript was lost or destroyed; it resists treatment as an extant text. [S5]
- **1956:** *A Time for George Stavros*. Lost realist novel manuscript; surviving descriptions disagree about how complete a recoverable work it ever was. [S5]
- **1956:** *Pilgrim on the Hill*. Lost novel manuscript; no readable text is known to survive. [S5]
- **1970s:** *The Kneeling Legless Man*. Abandoned collaborative novel-project with Roger Zelazny, represented in the archive by a rough, incomplete draft. [S4]
- **1970s:** *The Man in the High Castle* sequel. Project discussed and partially attempted but never completed; surviving material belongs more securely to an unrealized intention than to a finished novel. [S9]
- **1981–1982:** *The Owl in Daylight*. Dick’s final projected novel, left unwritten or only fragmentarily conceived at his death; accounts of its intended form vary. [S8]
- **1981:** Untitled interstellar-war novel. Unrealized project known from notes or an outline rather than a completed literary text. [S9]

### The Exegesis and related hybrid works

- **1974–1982:** *The Exegesis*. An approximately eight-year assemblage of journals, letters, dreams, diagrams, philosophical arguments, self-interpretations, and fictional possibilities prompted by the experiences Dick called “2-3-74.” It is a continuing project rather than a finished book and approaches a textual Gesamtkunstwerk by refusing stable separation among autobiography, theology, criticism, correspondence, and fiction. [S1][S7]
- **Published 1991:** *In Pursuit of VALIS: Selections from the Exegesis*. Lawrence Sutin’s posthumous editorial selection; it is a manifestation of the larger project, not a book completed in this form by Dick. [S6][S7]
- **Published 2011:** *The Exegesis of Philip K. Dick*. Jonathan Lethem and Pamela Jackson’s much larger posthumous selection; again, the edited volume should not be mistaken for the total manuscript project. [S1][S7]
- **Published 1988:** *The Dark-Haired Girl*. Posthumously assembled hybrid of letters, essays, fiction, and poetry; the constituent texts are Dick’s, but the book-object as such is editorial. [S1][S6]

### Correspondence as an open corpus

- **1938–1982:** *Letters*. A large, dispersed body of correspondence forming an epistolary œuvre rather than one fixed text; only selections have been published, and neither their surviving total nor their editorial corpus is complete. [S1][S4]
- **Published 1991–2010:** *The Selected Letters of Philip K. Dick*, six chronological volumes. Posthumous edited manifestations covering 1938–1971, 1972–1973, 1974, 1975–1976, 1977–1979, and 1980–1982; publication dates vary between bibliographies and editions, so these volumes should not be treated as works newly authored on their publication dates. [S1][S7]
