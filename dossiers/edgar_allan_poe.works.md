## Works by Edgar Allan Poe

### Poetry collections: books as assembled objects

- **1827:** *Tamerlane and Other Poems*. Poe’s first book, published anonymously “By a Bostonian”; it assembles ten poems, several of which he later revised radically. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1829:** *Al Aaraaf, Tamerlane, and Minor Poems*. Poe’s second poetry collection and a new arrangement of substantially revised and new verse. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1831:** *Poems*. A deliberately reconstructed collection, dedicated to the United States Corps of Cadets; it introduced several major poems while omitting much earlier material. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1845:** *The Raven and Other Poems*. Poe’s final authorized poetry collection; its contents are manifestations of frequently revised poems rather than a stable “complete poems.” [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)

- **1827:** *Stanzas (“In Youth I Have Known One”)*. A lyric sometimes indexed by its opening words. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)

- **1829:** *To M—*. A lyric first printed as “Alone,” but distinct from the poem now commonly bearing that title. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)

- **1829:** *Elizabeth*. An acrostic-like address to Elizabeth Rebecca Herring. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)

- **c. 1831:** *Lines on Joe Locke*. Comic or satirical verse also called “Lines to Joe Locke.” [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)
- **1831:** *Mysterious Star!*. A new introduction written for the 1831 form of *Al Aaraaf*, functioning as part of another poem rather than securely as an independent work. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1831:** *To Helen*. The lyric beginning “Helen, thy beauty is to me,” distinct from the 1848 poem addressed to Sarah Helen Whitman. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)

- **1831–1845:** *The Valley Nis / The Valley of Unrest*. Successive titles and states of one revised lyric. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1831–1845:** *The Doomed City / The City of Sin / The City in the Sea*. Three titles for evolving manifestations of one poem. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)
- **1831–1845:** *A Paean / Lenore*. The later poem is a radical reconstruction of the earlier lament, making the boundary between revision and new work unusually unstable. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)
- **c. 1832:** *Epigram from Pulci*. A translated or adapted epigram that later scholarship rejects as Poe’s work. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)
- **1833–1834:** *To One in Paradise*. A lyric first associated with the tale *The Visionary* and also titled “To One Beloved” and “To Ianthe in Heaven.” [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)
- **1833:** *Hymn*. A lyric from “Morella,” initially called “Catholic Hymn.” [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)
- **1833:** *Latin Hymn*. Verse embedded in the tale first called “Epimanes”; it is a textual component rather than an independently published poem. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1833:** *Song of Triumph*. Embedded verse associated with “Epimanes” and therefore resistant to classification as a freestanding work. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1833:** *Enigma [On Shakespeare]*. A verse riddle. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1833:** *Serenade*. A lyric sometimes associated with Poe’s projected “Tales of the Folio Club.” [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)
- **1833:** *To —— (“Sleep On”)*. A short address poem identified by its opening words. [Mabbott](https://www.eapoe.org/works/mabbott/tom1p000.htm)

- **1835:** *To Elizabeth / Lines Written in an Album / To F——s S. O——d*. Successive titles for an album poem later redirected to Frances Sargent Osgood. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/poems/index.htm)

- **1837–1839:** *Why the Little Frenchman Wears His Hand in a Sling*. A comic tale whose precise composition and first-publication history remain uncertain. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)

- **1842–1843:** *The Gold-Bug*. A cryptographic adventure tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1842–1843:** *The Tell-Tale Heart*. A confessional tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1842–1843:** *The Black Cat*. A confessional Gothic tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1843:** *Diddling Considered as One of the Exact Sciences*. A comic taxonomy of fraud first titled “Raising the Wind”; it behaves partly as tale and partly as satirical protocol. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **1843–1844:** *A Tale of the Ragged Mountains*. A tale combining mesmerism, doubling, and pseudo-documentary narration. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Spectacles*. A comic tale of mistaken identity and defective vision. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Premature Burial*. A documentary-like compilation of cases surrounding a fictional confession, blurring essay and tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Balloon-Hoax*. A newspaper hoax masquerading as factual reportage; it is a textual action as much as a story. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Oblong Box*. A maritime mystery tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Purloined Letter*. The third Dupin tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *Mesmeric Revelation*. A philosophical dialogue presented as a mesmeric case report. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844–1845:** *The System of Doctor Tarr and Professor Fether*. A satirical asylum tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *Thou Art the Man*. A comic-detective tale. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Angel of the Odd*. A comic fantasy about improbable events. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844:** *The Literary Life of Thingum Bob, Esq.*. A satire on plagiarism, reviewing, and magazine culture. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844–1845:** *Some Words with a Mummy*. A satirical dialogue with a revived Egyptian body. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1844–1845:** *The Thousand-and-Second Tale of Scheherazade*. A continuation and parody of the Scheherazade frame. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1845:** *The Power of Words*. A cosmological dialogue. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1845:** *The Imp of the Perverse*. A philosophical essay that turns into a confessional tale, deliberately resisting the division between exposition and fiction. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1845:** *The Facts in the Case of M. Valdemar*. A pseudo-scientific report widely received as possible fact; like a manifesto or hoax, it acts upon readers’ belief. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1845–1846:** *The Sphinx*. A brief tale of optical misapprehension. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1846:** *The Cask of Amontillado*. A revenge tale in confessional form. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1847–1849:** *Mellonta Tauta*. A satirical future letter and imaginary voyage. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1849:** *Landor’s Cottage*. An architectural and landscape sketch with minimal narrative action. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1849:** *Hop-Frog*. A revenge tale also published as “Hop-Frog; or, The Eight Chained Ourang-Outangs.” [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1849:** *Von Kempelen and His Discovery*. A newspaper-style hoax about alchemy and gold. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1849:** *X-ing a Paragrab*. A typographical and journalistic burlesque. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **1849:** *The Light-House*. An unfinished diary-fiction, possibly the beginning of a tale or novel; no secure intended extent or final title can be established. [Chronology](https://www.eapoe.org/works/info/chltales.htm)
- **Undated:** *Old English Tale*. A reported project that Mabbott inferred was a tale; neither a text nor a secure title survives. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **Undated:** *Summer and Winter*. A prose fragment classified by Mabbott as a tale, though its status as an independent work is uncertain. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **Date uncertain:** *A Dream*. A very brief prose piece once attributed to Poe; the attribution is highly doubtful. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)

### Tale collections and unrealized collection-projects

- **1832–1836:** *Tales of the Folio Club*. A projected framed collection in which members of an invented literary club read parodic tales; Poe never published the designed whole, and the lost or abandoned frame makes it a project rather than a complete book. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **1839:** *Tales of the Grotesque and Arabesque*. A two-volume authorized collection whose arrangement makes “grotesque” and “arabesque” interacting modes rather than merely a heap of stories. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **1842:** *Phantasy-Pieces*. A proposed revised edition of *Tales of the Grotesque and Arabesque*; the intended collection was not published. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **1843–1845:** *Phantasy Pieces*. A further reconstructed collection-project represented by revised printed pages Poe supplied to Evert Duyckinck; the assembled object has not survived intact and is partly conjectural. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **1843:** *The Prose Romances of Edgar A. Poe*. A pamphlet containing “The Murders in the Rue Morgue” and “The Man That Was Used Up,” apparently intended as the first number of a series that did not continue. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
- **1845:** *Tales*. An authorized selection of twelve tales, shaped with editor Evert Duyckinck; it is a made collection rather than a complete fiction corpus. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)

### Treatise, textbook, cosmology, and lecture-manifestations

- **1839:** *The Conchologist’s First Book*. A textbook compiled largely from Thomas Wyatt’s conchological manual, with Poe’s exact contribution disputed; it should not be represented straightforwardly as an independently authored book. [Poe Society canon](https://www.eapoe.org/works/canon/poecanon.htm)
- **1843:** *A Lecture on American Poetry / The Poets and Poetry of America*. A lecture-performance known through notices and related textual materials; its manifestations varied, and no single fully authoritative text survives. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1848:** *The Universe*. A public lecture delivered in New York and subsequently transformed into *Eureka*; the spoken event and printed book are related manifestations, not identical objects. [Levine edition](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1848:** *Eureka: A Prose Poem*. A book-length cosmological argument that Poe called a prose poem and offered as truth and art; it deliberately refuses stable separation among poem, scientific hypothesis, metaphysics, essay, and manifesto. [Levine edition](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1848–1849:** *The Poetic Principle*. A lecture-essay delivered in changing forms and published posthumously; the surviving text is therefore a manifestation of a performance practice as well as an essay. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)

### Essays, sketches, protocols, and literary manifestos

- **1831:** *Letter to B——*. A critical letter first prefacing *Poems*; it functions as an early manifesto of Poe’s poetics. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1836:** *Maelzel’s Chess-Player*. An analytical essay exposing the supposed automaton; part investigation and part protocol of inference. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1837–1838:** *The Philosophy of Furniture*. A satirical design essay, also titled “House Furniture,” prescribing the arrangement of an ideal room and thus behaving partly as a protocol. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1839:** *The Capitol at Washington*. A sketch formerly attributed to Poe but rejected from the canon; it is not securely something he made. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1839:** *Some Account of Stonehenge*. A descriptive-antiquarian article attributed to Poe. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1839:** *A Chapter in the History of Vivum-Ovo*. An article once assigned to Poe but now rejected. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1839:** *An Opinion on Dreams*. A piece once attributed to Poe but rejected from the canon. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1840:** *The Philosophy of Animal Magnetism*. A piece formerly attributed to Poe but rejected; its presence in older “complete works” is an editorial artifact. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1840:** *Instinct versus Reason—A Black Cat*. A natural-history sketch that Mabbott placed among imaginative prose, though it is neither straightforward fiction nor formal essay. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1840–1842:** *Secret Writing*. A series of magazine articles on cryptography, also called “Cryptography”; because it solicited and solved readers’ ciphers, it was an interactive protocol and public performance as well as an essay. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1842:** *A Few Words on Secret Writing*. A cryptographic exposition associated with Poe’s public cipher challenges. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1842:** *Some Secrets of the Magazine Prison-House*. A satirical exposé of magazine economics and authors’ dependence. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1843:** *Morning on the Wissahiccon*. A landscape sketch later titled “The Elk”; editors differ over whether to classify it as tale, sketch, or essay. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1843:** *Street-Paving*. An urban-technology article. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1844:** *The Pay for American Authors*. A journalistic essay on literary labor and compensation. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1844:** *A Prediction*. A brief untitled or editorially titled article whose descriptive title appears in brackets because Poe supplied no secure title. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1845:** *Magazine Writing—Peter Snook*. A critical notice of James Dalton’s “Peter Snook,” later detached and retitled as though it were an independent essay. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1845:** *Our Magazine Literature*. A periodical essay only possibly by Poe; authorship remains disputed. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1846:** *The Philosophy of Composition*. A procedural account of poetic construction using *The Raven* as its example; whether it records actual composition or performs an ideal method is disputed, and it operates as both manifesto and protocol. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1848:** *The Rationale of Verse*. A prosodic treatise first called “Notes upon English Verse”; it advances a prescriptive system and therefore functions as a protocol as well as criticism. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **Date uncertain:** *Palaestine*. A geographical or historical article attributed to Poe in the essay canon. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **Date uncertain:** *Harpers Ferry*. A piece once attributed to Poe but rejected. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **Date uncertain:** *English Notes for Extensive Circulation*. A piece formerly attributed to Poe but rejected. [Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)

### Composite critical forms and editorial corpora

- **1835–1837:** *Southern Literary Messenger criticism and editorial writing*. A large corpus of signed and unsigned reviews, notices, essays, fillers, and editorial interventions established item by item by Burton R. Pollin; uncertain attributions prevent its honest reduction to a simple list of securely authored titles. As editorial labor it is a body of work—an obra dispersed across issues—rather than one book. [Pollin/Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1836–1837:** *The Literati of New York City*. A projected series of literary portraits; portions appeared later under related headings, but the projected totality was never completed in its initially imagined form. [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/91-essays-amp-reviews/)
- **1841–1842:** *Autography*. A composite series reproducing and interpreting writers’ handwriting; it combines facsimile, biographical notice, criticism, and visual taxonomy, so it is neither merely essay nor merely image collection. [Poe Society works](https://www.eapoe.org/works/index.htm)
- **1841–1842:** *A Chapter on Autography*. The expanded periodical manifestation of Poe’s handwriting project, containing numerous individual author portraits and facsimiles. [Poe Society canon](https://www.eapoe.org/works/canon/poecanon.htm)
- **1842–1843:** *Philadelphia Saturday Museum biographical and critical material*. A partly recoverable group of contributions whose unsigned authorship has been debated; entries should not be silently absorbed into Poe’s canon. [Poe Society canon](https://www.eapoe.org/works/canon/poecanon.htm)
- **1844–1849:** *Marginalia*. A continuing numbered series of brief observations, quotations, criticism, jokes, and speculative notes; it is a serial notebook-form and body of fragments rather than a single conventional essay. [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/91-essays-amp-reviews/)
- **1845:** *Broadway Journal nonfictional prose*. A corpus of reviews, notices, essays, paragraphs, and editorial matter written or revised while Poe served as critic, editor, and eventually proprietor. Pollin’s two-volume scholarly edition establishes the corpus while retaining attribution problems inherent in unsigned journalism. [Pollin/Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)
- **1846:** *The Literati of New York City: Some Honest Opinions at Random Respecting Their Authorial Merits, with Occasional Words of Personality*. A serial set of literary portraits; each installment is separable, but the sequence is also a designed critical-social panorama. [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/91-essays-amp-reviews/)
- **1835–1849:** *Reviews of British and Continental authors*. A dispersed critical corpus covering, among others, Coleridge, Dickens, Elizabeth Barrett, Shelley, and Tennyson; individual notices range from unsigned editorial copy to substantial essays, with some attributions disputed. [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/91-essays-amp-reviews/)
- **1835–1849:** *Reviews of American authors and American literature*. A dispersed corpus addressing writers including Hawthorne, Irving, Longfellow, and many now-obscure contemporaries; it is an editorial body of work, not one authored book, and its boundaries depend on bibliographical attribution. [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/91-essays-amp-reviews/)

### Magazine and institutional projects

- **1840–1849:** *The Penn / The Stylus*. Poe’s repeatedly announced but unrealized ideal magazine: initially *The Penn Magazine*, later *The Stylus*. Prospectuses, correspondence, a motto, proposed illustrations, financing efforts, and editorial plans survive, but no issue appeared; the intention and sustained work constitute the project.
- **1845:** *The Broadway Journal under Poe’s proprietorship*. Poe did not create the periodical, but his late-1845 ownership and editorial shaping produced a short-lived composite object joining criticism, fiction, poetry, typography, selection, and polemic; authorship belongs only partly to him. [Pollin/Poe Society](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/index.htm)

### Correspondence and working documents

- **1824–1849:** *Letters*. Poe’s surviving correspondence forms an extensive corpus of made texts—personal, professional, editorial, financial, and promotional—rather than a work he assembled for publication. The corpus is incomplete, and attribution, dating, missing enclosures, drafts, and posthumous editorial alteration must be handled letter by letter. [Poe Society works](https://www.eapoe.org/works/index.htm)
- **1827–1849:** *Manuscript revisions, presentation copies, and annotated books*. Poe repeatedly altered printed poems and tales by hand, including copies of *Tales*, *The Raven and Other Poems*, and the *Broadway Journal*; these are authorial manifestations and working objects, not merely later editions. [Poe Society tales](https://www.eapoe.org/works/tales/)
