## Works by Ted Shawn

### Choreographic obras: early solos, ballets, and Denishawn works

- **1913:** *Dances of the Ages*. A dance pageant filmed under J. Searle Dawley’s direction; Shawn devised and performed dances for it, but the surviving records do not cleanly separate his choreography from the motion-picture work as a whole. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1914:** *The Dagger Dance*. A solo portraying an Indigenous American sacrificial subject through the period’s Orientalist and primitivist theatrical conventions; sources also call it an Aztec dance. ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/denishawn-dance-film/))
- **1915:** *Nature Rhythms*. A choreographic suite documented in Christena L. Schlundt’s performance chronology; its constituent dances and versions varied between programs. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Hawaiian Ballet*. A theatrical dance attributed to Shawn in the Schlundt-derived performance database. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Danse Javanese*. A solo or short theatrical dance attributed to Shawn; the title names a staged American representation of Javanese dance, not an ethnographic record. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Japanese Sword Dance*. An early version of the Japanese-themed weapon dance; programs and later sources also use *Japanese Spear Dance*, so the relation between the two titles or versions should not be silently resolved. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873), [CriticalDance](https://criticaldance.org/denishawn-everything-old-is-new-again-again/))
- **1915:** *Aloha Waltz*. A theatrical social dance attributed to Shawn in the Schlundt chronology. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Dog Dance*. A short character dance attributed to Shawn; the surviving index establishes performance and authorship but not a stable independent text. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *La Gitanette*. A theatrical character dance attributed to Shawn. Its title participates in the period’s generalized “Gypsy” stage imagery rather than naming a documented community dance. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Columbine*. A commedia-dell’arte character dance attributed to Shawn. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Canton Canter*. A comic or character dance attributed to Shawn; the index does not establish whether it circulated as a fixed choreography apart from the program containing it. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Baseball Dance*. A comic dance-mime built from the actions of an American baseball player; later programs also connect this material with *Danse Américaine*. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *Dance of the Directoire*. A period-style theatrical dance to music by Riccardo Drigo, attributed to Shawn. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873))
- **1915:** *The Twenty-Third Psalm*. A religious solo and recurring liturgical choreography. Shawn planned it as part of an incompletely realized three-dance *Dances of David* project—*The Boy*, *The Shepherd*, and *The King*—and repeatedly revised or recontextualized it. ([Performance Philosophy](https://www.performancephilosophy.org/journal/issue/download/4/4))
- **1917:** *Dance Church Service*. A Gesamtkunstwerk-like Protestant service in rhythmic movement, incorporating prayer, doxology, Gloria, psalm, music, and dance rather than functioning simply as a staged ballet. Its contents changed between performances. ([Performance Philosophy](https://www.performancephilosophy.org/journal/issue/download/4/4))
- **1917:** *Invocation to the Thunderbird*. A solo drawing upon Shawn’s theatrical interpretation of Indigenous American imagery; sources disagree between 1916 and 1917 for its date, probably reflecting creation, first performance, or variant chronology. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001), [Denishawn School history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denishawn_school))
- **1919:** *Japanese Spear Dance*. A dramatic solo to music by Louis Horst, made from Shawn’s study and theatrical adaptation of Japanese movement. It may be a development or retitling of the earlier *Japanese Sword Dance*. ([CriticalDance](https://criticaldance.org/denishawn-everything-old-is-new-again-again/), [Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **1919:** *Julnar of the Sea*. A fantasy ballet or dance-drama associated with the Denishawn repertory. The work is attributed to Shawn, but surviving summaries do not consistently distinguish his choreography from the company’s collective theatrical realization. ([Larousse, “Ted Shawn”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/392))
- **1919:** *Palace Dance*. A theatrical dance to music credited to L. Halvorsen and choreography attributed to Shawn. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/1999))
- **1920:** *Xochitl*. A Toltec-themed ballet or “extravaganza,” created for Martha Graham with a commissioned score and designs; it is a composite stage work, although Shawn is credited with its choreography. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001), [archival exhibition caption](https://www.reaganlibrary.gov/public/2021-11/40-654-12019885-IV087-510445-2021.pdf))
- **circa 1920:** *Abduction of Sita*. A Denishawn dance-drama based on the *Ramayana*, represented in archival performance photographs. The cataloguing evidence establishes the title and Shawn’s performance context more securely than sole choreographic authorship. ([Library of Congress, Cesi Kellinger Collection](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/mu020018/mu020018.pdf))
- **1921:** *Les Mystères Dionysiaques*. A Greek-themed choreographic spectacle attributed to Shawn; it resists classification as a single dance because it joined movement, music, costume, procession, and dramatic ritual. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1922:** *Death of Adonis*. A mythological solo or dance-drama; later reconstructions identify it as Shawn’s choreography, but the transmission survives through performance memory and archival documentation rather than a definitive authorial score. ([Denishawn School history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denishawn_school))
- **1923:** *Danse Américaine*. A comic dance-mime made for Charles Weidman, frequently described through its baseball actions. Its relationship to Shawn’s earlier *Baseball Dance* is close but not definitively documented as simple identity. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **by 1923:** *Revolutionary Étude*. A music visualization to Chopin, attributed to Shawn in Denishawn programs. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *Betty’s Music Box*. A character dance to music by Carrie Jacobs-Bond, attributed to Shawn. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *Valse in A Major*. A music visualization to Mischa Levitzki, attributed to Shawn. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *Siamese Ballet*. A multi-part theatrical ballet attributed to Shawn, made from Western theatrical representations of Siamese court dance. Individual program components sometimes circulated separately. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/4507))
- **by 1923:** *Lantern Dance*. A short dance within Shawn’s East Asian-themed repertory; programs do not establish a single permanent version. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *Servant with Parasol*. A character dance or episode attributed to Shawn, probably belonging to a larger Asian-themed suite rather than constituting an autonomous obra in every performance. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *The Princess and the Demon*. A narrative dance episode attributed to Shawn. It appears in programs alongside components of the *Siamese Ballet*, making its independent status uncertain. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *Egyptian Ballet*. A multi-part theatrical ballet attributed to Shawn, combining dance, costume, scenic imagery, and music by L. Halvorsen and Walter Meyrowitz. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **by 1923:** *Thoth and Horus*. An Egyptian-themed dance episode attributed to Shawn; it may be a constituent part of *Egyptian Ballet* rather than a consistently autonomous work. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/3888))
- **1924:** *Boston Fancy: 1854*. A comic dance-farce using pantomime and New England contra-dance material, set at a Beacon Hill party; Jane Sherman reconstructed it in 1982. ([Vanaver Caravan](https://vanaver-caravan-draft.squarespace.com/denishawn))
- **by 1926:** *Gnossienne: A Priest of Knossos*. A solo music visualization to Erik Satie, attributed to Shawn; programs sometimes shorten the title to *Gnossienne*. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/4507))
- **by 1926:** *Bailerina Real*. A Spanish-inflected theatrical solo or character dance to music by Mario Tarenghi, attributed to Shawn; the source preserves the program’s spelling. ([Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/4507))
- **1920s, date not securely fixed:** *Color Study of the Madonna*, also catalogued as *The Masque*. A dance work surviving in photographic and archival description under variant titles; the instability of its title and extent is part of its status. ([Library of Congress, Cesi Kellinger Collection](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/mu020018/mu020018.pdf))
- **1931:** *Four Dances Based on American Folk Music*. A suite of solos interpreting contrasting American musical traditions; “Nobody Knows the Trouble I’ve Seen” is the best-documented surviving section. The suite’s performed forms and accompaniments changed, and later sound was added to silent film. ([Jacob’s Pillow Dance Interactive](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/ted-shawn/nobody-knows-the-trouble-ive-seen/))

### Choreographic obras for Ted Shawn and His Men Dancers

- **1933:** *Sixth Prelude from The Well-Tempered Clavichord*. An ensemble “music visualization” to Bach, filmed during the Men Dancers’ first Jacob’s Pillow season. ([Jacob’s Pillow Dance Interactive](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/playlists/men-dancers/))
- **1933:** *The French Sailor*. A character solo to music by Darius Milhaud, choreographed by Shawn for Barton Mumaw. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1933:** *Cutting the Sugar Cane*. A labor-themed ensemble dance to Ernesto Lecuona, choreographed by Shawn; it later entered larger American and labor suites. ([NYPL, Grant Hyde Code negatives](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **1933:** *Polonaise*. An ensemble music visualization, also printed as *Polonais* in some secondary accounts; the spelling and exact musical identification vary. ([Study on the Male Dancer’s Position](https://journal.kci.go.kr/krsds/archive/articleView?artiId=ART001545667))
- **1934:** *Labor Symphony*. An ensemble dance cycle about physical and mechanized work; individual movements, including *Mechanized Labor* or *Dynamo*, circulated separately, so the cycle is both one obra and a container for smaller ones. ([DanceTabs](https://dancetabs.com/2021/04/an-introduction-and-rummage-through-the-treasure-trove-that-is-the-jacobs-pillow-dance-interactive-archive/), [NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **1935:** *Kinetic Molpai*. A ritualized ensemble dance for eight men to music by Jess Meeker, stressing patterned energy and male athleticism. The title denotes the full suite rather than merely its frequently excerpted finale. ([Alvin Ailey repertory](https://ailey.org/repertory/kinetic-molpai), [Jacob’s Pillow Dance Interactive](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/ted-shawn-and-his-men-dancers/kinetic-molpai/))
- **1935:** *Pierrot in the Dead City*. A solo made for Barton Mumaw to music by Erich Wolfgang Korngold; it places a commedia figure inside an expressionist dramatic miniature. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1935:** *Dance of the Ages*. A large ensemble cycle organized around Fire, Air, Water, and Earth. Some performers contributed material under Shawn’s direction, so it resists uncomplicated sole-authorship even though programs credit Shawn as choreographer. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/), [Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1936:** *Olympiad*. A suite of sports dances, including basketball, fencing, cheerleading, and a *Decathlon* section; it converts athletic actions into staged choreography and therefore straddles dance, pageant, and physical-culture demonstration. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/), [NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **1936:** *Finale from The New World*. An ensemble music visualization to Dvořák, documented on film at Jacob’s Pillow; the archival title identifies an excerpt rather than necessarily an independently conceived work. ([Jacob’s Pillow, Past Performers](https://www.jacobspillow.org/about/past-performers/))
- **1937:** *O, Libertad!*. A three-act “American saga” in dance—Past, Present, and Future—combining Indigenous-themed historical spectacle, war episodes, jazz, sport, and material from earlier Shawn works. It is a Gesamtkunstwerk-like compilation rather than wholly new choreography: *Kinetic Molpai*, *Olympiad*, and other dances were absorbed or reframed within it. ([Springfield College Archives](https://springfield.as.atlas-sys.com/repositories/2/resources/381/digitized), [NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *Ponca Indian Dance*. An Indigenous-themed ensemble dance attributed to Shawn; programs and costume records establish it, but titles vary between *Ponca Indian Dance* and *Ponca Indian*. ([NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *Osage-Pawnee Dance of Greeting*. A staged Indigenous-themed ensemble dance preserved in the Men Dancers’ costume repertory; some sources date a later version or revival to 1942. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *Hopi Indian Eagle Dance*. A staged interpretation attributed to Shawn, not an Indigenous community’s protocol or an ethnographic document. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *Maori War Haka*. A Men Dancers ensemble adaptation based on Māori performance. It is documented as Shawn repertory, but calling the theatrical adaptation an authored “work” risks obscuring the source tradition from which it appropriated movement and title. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *Sinhalese Devil Dance*. A theatrical adaptation of Sri Lankan ritual imagery attributed to Shawn; its status as an autonomous choreography and the spelling “Sinhalse” in some sources vary. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *Danza Afro-Cubana*. A Men Dancers repertory piece documented through surviving costumes. The archival evidence does not by itself settle how much choreography was Shawn’s rather than adapted or collaboratively developed. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *The Forty-Niners*. An American historical ensemble work represented in the Men Dancers’ costume collection. Its surviving material manifestation is fragmentary rather than a complete performance text. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *The Jazz Decade*. An ensemble dance or suite represented by surviving costumes; it may also overlap with the jazz episodes of *O, Libertad!*. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/))
- **late 1930s, date uncertain:** *Credo*. A solo or dance-mime documented in Grant Hyde Code’s photographs. The photographic finding aid establishes performance but does not supply a secure creation date or complete choreographic form. ([NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **late 1930s, date uncertain:** *Movement Naïf*. A solo documented photographically; its exact date and status as an independent work rather than a program item remain uncertain. ([NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **late 1930s, date uncertain:** *Fencing*. A sports dance documented in performance photographs and closely associated with *Olympiad*; it resists counting cleanly as both an independent work and a suite movement. ([NYPL](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **1938:** *Dance of the Ages* (revised or expanded form). Sources date the work both 1935 and 1938, apparently distinguishing its original material from the later four-element production; the discrepancy should be retained rather than collapsed. ([Museum Metaphysics documentation](https://scalar.usc.edu/works/caa/media/zee-fig-3.1))
- **1930s, date uncertain:** *The Dome*. A filmed or filmable Men Dancers work catalogued by the New York Public Library; its archival manifestation is a motion picture of choreography, not the choreography itself. ([ArchiveGrid](https://researchworks.oclc.org/archivegrid/collection/data/1323066367))

### Later solos, revivals, and liturgical choreography

- **1940s, date uncertain:** *O Brother Sun and Sister Moon (A Study of St. Francis)*. A religious solo to music by Respighi, choreographed by Shawn and later transmitted through Barton Mumaw. Sources often identify later revivals rather than an uncontested premiere date. ([Jacob’s Pillow Dance Interactive](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/playlists/men-dancers/))
- **1940s–1950s, date disputed:** *The Mevlevi Dervish* or *The Whirling Dervish*. Shawn’s theatrical dervish solo, repeatedly revived and retitled; one source dates a version to 1949, while programs continued to advertise it in 1953. It is an adaptation of a living religious practice and therefore resists treatment as wholly Shawn’s invented object. ([Los Angeles Times](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1993-02-13-ca-1191-story.html), [University of Florida archival record](https://findingaids.uflib.ufl.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/158207))
- **1940s–1950s, date uncertain:** *The Divine Idiot*. A spiritual or mystical solo to music by Scriabin, choreographed and performed by Shawn; references sometimes attach the date of a late performance rather than the choreography’s creation. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1950s, date uncertain:** *Three Prayers*. A choreographic triptych whose 1960 Labanotation was prepared by Shawn, Ann Hutchinson, and Jess Meeker. The notation is a protocol for performance and a distinct manifestation of the dances, not the dances themselves. ([Springfield College Archives](https://springfield.as.atlas-sys.com/repositories/2/resources/381/digitized))
- **1965:** *Siddhas of the Upper Air*. A late duet or ceremonial reunion performed by Shawn and Ruth St. Denis for their fiftieth anniversary. Authorship is not consistently assigned in accessible summaries, so it should not be claimed unqualifiedly as Shawn’s solo choreography. ([Jacob’s Pillow Archives](https://www.jacobspillow.org/visit/archives/))

### Books and book-length textual obras

- **1920:** *Ruth St. Denis: Pioneer and Prophet; Being a History of Her Cycle of Oriental Dances*. A critical-biographical book and repertory history; it also acts as advocacy for St. Denis’s artistic project. ([NYPL, Ted Shawn collection](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19667), [Scolieri bibliography](https://academic.oup.com/book/34993/chapter-abstract/298679610))
- **1926:** *The American Ballet*. A programmatic book arguing for an American national dance and theatrical institution; it approaches a manifesto because it seeks to bring that institution into being. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1929:** *Gods Who Dance*. A travel-based study and interpretation of Asian dance and ritual, written from the cultural assumptions of Denishawn’s 1925–26 tour. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1935:** *Fundamentals of a Dance Education*. A pedagogical protocol setting out principles and exercises for dance training; bibliographies also give 1937, reflecting publication or edition history rather than necessarily a different work. ([Larousse, “Ted Shawn”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/392))
- **1940:** *Dance We Must*. A collection and reformulation of Shawn’s lectures and arguments about dance; later publication dates refer to editions, not new obras. Its exhortative title and institutional argument give parts of it the force of a manifesto. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))
- **1944:** *How Beautiful upon the Mountain: A History of Jacob’s Pillow*. An institutional history written by its founder and therefore also an act of self-archiving and project-making. ([NYPL, Ted Shawn collection](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19667))
- **1954:** *Every Little Movement: A Book about François Delsarte*. A historical, theoretical, and pedagogical reconstruction of Delsarte’s system and its influence on American dance. ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=AA6xbGWvgScC))
- **1959:** *Thirty-Three Years of American Dance*. A historical and autobiographical survey assembled from Shawn’s records; the NYPL preserves his working notes and a chronology extending from 1927 to 1956. ([NYPL, Ted Shawn collection](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19667))
- **1960:** *One Thousand and One Night Stands*. An autobiography coauthored with Gray Poole; joint authorship must be retained rather than subsuming Poole under Shawn’s name. ([Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))

### Short writings, scenarios, and serial texts

- **1910–1911:** *Student short stories, poems, and motion-picture scenarios*. The NYPL identifies this corpus but its public finding aid does not enumerate every title; it is therefore a body of works, not one securely titled obra, and completeness cannot be claimed at item level without consulting the manuscripts. ([NYPL, Ted Shawn collection](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19667))
- **date not supplied in the finding aid:** *Theatre as a Triune Art*. An article published in *Direction* presenting theater as the integration of multiple arts—a textual argument with affinities to the Gesamtkunstwerk. ([NYPL, Ted Shawn collection](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19667))
- **1936:** *Men Must Dance*. An article in *Magazine Dance* that argues publicly for the legitimacy of the male dancer; its polemical and profession-making action gives it the character of a manifesto. ([NYPL, Grant Hyde Code negatives](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/generated_finding_aids/dan185541.pdf))
- **date range not fully specified:** *Twenty-seven Boston Herald columns on modern dance*. A serial critical corpus rather than one book; the Library of Congress collection preserves material including Shawn’s final column, but the accessible finding aid does not warrant inventing titles or dates for the other twenty-six. ([Library of Congress, Denishawn Legacy Collection](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/mu021021/mu021021.pdf))
- **1910–1941:** *Annual newsletters, lectures, articles, poems, fiction, and scenarios*. The archive preserves these as Shawn’s written production, but many items are unpublished, variant, or untitled. This corpus resists conversion into a falsely complete list of discrete “works” from the public folder-level description alone. ([NYPL, Ted Shawn collection](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19667))

### Pedagogical and institutional projects

- **1915–1931/1932:** *Denishawn School of Dancing and Related Arts and the Denishawn company*. A joint project with Ruth St. Denis: simultaneously a school, touring company, repertory, technique, and artistic ideology. Its authorship is institutional and collaborative, not Shawn’s alone. ([Library of Congress, Denishawn Legacy Collection](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/15/resources/1563))
- **1933–1940:** *Ted Shawn and His Men Dancers*. A company, pedagogical experiment, touring repertory, and manifesto enacted through male bodies; it is a sustained project rather than a single choreography. The company performed more than a thousand times in over 750 cities. ([Jacob’s Pillow Dance Interactive](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/ted-shawn-and-his-men-dancers/kinetic-molpai/))
- **1933 onward:** *Jacob’s Pillow*. A continuing institutional Gesamtkunstwerk encompassing farm, school, theaters, festival, archives, repertory, and communal life. Shawn founded it and directed its development, but its buildings and programs were made collectively, including substantial labor by the Men Dancers. ([Jacob’s Pillow history](https://www.jacobspillow.org/about/the-history-of-jacobs-pillow/))
- **1937:** *School of Dance for Men*. A pedagogical and social project formalizing Shawn’s program for male dance training; the surviving brochures are manifestations of the school, not the school itself. ([Springfield College Archives](https://springfield.as.atlas-sys.com/repositories/2/resources/381/digitized))
- **1940s–1972:** *Jacob’s Pillow Dance Festival and University of the Dance*. Shawn’s continuing curatorial and educational project after the Men Dancers disbanded. It cannot be treated as his sole-authored obra because its programming, teaching, administration, and performances were collectively produced. ([Jacob’s Pillow history](https://www.jacobspillow.org/about/the-history-of-jacobs-pillow/), [Springfield College Archives](https://springfield.as.atlas-sys.com/repositories/2/resources/381/digitized))
- **1930s–1972:** *The Jacob’s Pillow archive assembled by Shawn*. A deliberately accumulated body of programs, films, photographs, correspondence, books, costumes, and records. It is not conventionally a “work,” but Shawn’s sustained self-archiving was an intentional project designed to preserve and shape the historical meaning of his dances. ([Jacob’s Pillow Archives](https://www.jacobspillow.org/visit/archives/), [Scolieri](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780199331062.001.0001))

### Performance records and material manifestations that are not the choreography itself

- **circa 1916:** *Denishawn dance film*. Silent moving-image fragments recording Shawn and St. Denis material. These films are manifestations and evidence of dances—not substitutes for the embodied obras—and camera direction or production should not automatically be attributed to Shawn. ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/denishawn-dance-film/))
- **1933–1940:** *Films of Ted Shawn and His Men Dancers*. Silent records include *Kinetic Molpai*, *Labor Symphony*, *Sixth Prelude from The Well-Tempered Clavichord*, *Finale from The New World*, and Shawn’s “Nobody Knows the Trouble I’ve Seen.” They preserve particular performances, while added soundtracks and later restorations are distinct manifestations. ([Jacob’s Pillow Dance Interactive](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/playlists/men-dancers/))
- **1915–1940s:** *Costumes, properties, and touring trunks for Denishawn and the Men Dancers*. These are collectively made material components of performances, only partly attributable to Shawn; the surviving collection is fragmentary and does not reproduce complete productions. ([Jacob’s Pillow Costume Collection](https://danceinteractive.jacobspillow.org/themes-essays/what-is-dance/costume-collection/))
- **1930s onward:** *Jacob’s Pillow studios, theater spaces, and farm structures*. Shawn initiated and helped shape this built environment, while members of the Men Dancers and later collaborators supplied design and labor. The campus is an institutional creation rather than a set of securely sole-authored architectural obras. ([Jacob’s Pillow self-guided history](https://www.jacobspillow.org/visit/self-guided-tour/))
