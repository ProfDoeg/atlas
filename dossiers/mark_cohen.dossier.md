## Scope and Evidentiary Conventions

This dossier reconstructs the publicly documentable life and career of **Mark Stewart Cohen**, the New York attorney associated with Cohen & Gresser LLP. Because “Mark Cohen” is a common name, identity control is important. Material was attributed to the subject only where the professional biography, court record, company filing, institutional publication, or surrounding facts made the identification reasonably secure.

The following distinctions are used throughout:

- **Documented fact:** supported by a court record, government filing, company filing, institutional record, or other direct documentary source.
- **Court finding / conviction:** a determination by a court or jury, not merely an accusation.
- **Firm-reported:** stated by Cohen & Gresser in its own biographies, matter descriptions, or announcements; useful as a professional record but not treated as independent adjudication.
- **Press-reported:** reported by a named news organization and attributed accordingly.
- **Not established:** no reliable source consulted for this dossier established the proposition.
- **Inference:** a conclusion drawn from documented facts but not itself explicitly stated in the source.
- **Rumor / misidentification:** a circulating claim not supported by the underlying records, or based on confusion with another person.

No reliable evidence located in the sources used here establishes that Cohen has been personally accused, charged, convicted, or found liable for fraud, corruption, money laundering, criminal activity, or professional misconduct. Controversial material in this dossier primarily concerns the clients he represented, the outcomes of those cases, litigation positions he advanced, and public narratives about his professional relationships.

## Education and Formation

### Cornell University

Cohen received a **B.A. from Cornell University in 1984** and was elected **Phi Beta Kappa**, according to his professional biography.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

The public sources reviewed do not identify his undergraduate major, thesis, teachers, or extracurricular activities.

### University of Michigan Law School

Cohen received his **J.D., magna cum laude, from the University of Michigan Law School in 1987**. He served as a **Note Editor of the Michigan Law Review**.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

His Michigan Law Review work provides the earliest direct evidence of his intellectual and legal interests.

#### 1986 — William Hastie review

The Michigan Law Review’s 1986 volume lists Cohen as author of a review titled:

**“William Hastie: Grace Under Pressure,”** 84 *Michigan Law Review* 861 (1986).

The piece reviewed scholarship on **William H. Hastie**, the civil-rights lawyer, federal judge, educator, and public official.  
Source: [Michigan Law Review, Volume 84 issue page](https://repository.law.umich.edu/mlr/vol84/iss4/).

#### 1987 — Native American sacred-sites note

Cohen authored:

**“American Indian Sacred Religious Sites and Government Development: A Conventional Analysis in an Unconventional Setting,”** 85 *Michigan Law Review* 771 (1987).

The Michigan repository describes the note as addressing the free-exercise problems produced when government development affects Native American sacred sites. Cohen argued that courts had mishandled conventional Free Exercise Clause analysis in this setting and proposed a restructured doctrinal approach.  
Source: [University of Michigan Law School repository](https://repository.law.umich.edu/mlr/vol85/iss4/5/).

This is evidence of an early scholarly interest in constitutional religious-liberty doctrine and the legal treatment of Indigenous sacred places. It does **not** establish a personal religious or political ideology.

### Later connection to Michigan Law

A 2018 University of Michigan Law School publication reported that Cohen ’87 and his firm co-founder **Lawrence T. Gresser ’86** made gifts totaling **$100,000** to establish or support the **Cohen & Gresser Scholarship Fund**.  
Source: [Michigan Law Quadrangle](https://quadrangle.michigan.law.umich.edu/issues/springsummer-2018/recent-gifts-springsummer-2018).

## Early Career

### Fried, Frank, Harris, Shriver & Jacobson

Cohen’s firm biography states that before founding Cohen & Gresser he practiced at **Fried, Frank, Harris, Shriver & Jacobson**. The biography does not supply exact dates or a complete description of his work there.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

The available sources do not establish with certainty whether his Fried Frank service fell entirely before his federal prosecutorial period, entirely after it, or on both sides of it.

### Assistant U.S. Attorney — Eastern District of New York

Cohen served as an **Assistant United States Attorney in the Eastern District of New York (EDNY)**. His current biography says he conducted numerous jury trials as a federal prosecutor.

A March 27, 1995 federal decision in **United States v. Gangi** identifies **Mark Cohen** and Elisa Liang as Assistant U.S. Attorneys for the Eastern District of New York, independently confirming Cohen’s service by that date.  
Source: [United States v. Gangi](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59148451add7b049344b50d5).

A Second Circuit decision from 1996 refers to **“Former AUSA Cohen,”** showing that he had left the office by that point.  
Source: [Second Circuit opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/101/683/595700/).

The exact beginning date of his prosecutorial service was not established from a primary personnel record in the sources used here.

### United States v. Pitera

Cohen & Gresser’s biography identifies Cohen as one of three trial attorneys in **United States v. Thomas Pitera**, a major organized-crime prosecution that the firm describes as one of the first federal death-penalty cases in New York. The case included a seven-week trial. Cohen is described as a junior prosecutor on the team.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

Thomas Pitera was convicted of multiple murders and other crimes and received life imprisonment rather than a death sentence. The importance for Cohen’s biography is that early in his career he had already acquired extended federal jury-trial experience in a high-profile organized-crime case.

## Chronological Life History

### 1963–1984: Early life and Cornell

- **January 1963:** Birth, according to Companies House. Exact date and birthplace not established.
- **1984:** B.A., Cornell University; Phi Beta Kappa.

### 1984–1987: Michigan Law

- Entered the University of Michigan Law School.
- Served on the *Michigan Law Review* as a Note Editor.
- **1986:** Published review “William Hastie: Grace Under Pressure.”
- **1987:** Published the sacred-sites / Free Exercise Clause note.
- **1987:** J.D., magna cum laude.

### Late 1980s–mid-1990s: Large-firm practice and federal prosecution

Cohen practiced at Fried Frank and served as an Assistant U.S. Attorney in EDNY. Exact sequencing is incomplete in the public sources used here.

His prosecutorial period included major jury-trial work and participation in the Pitera prosecution. He was still an AUSA in March 1995 and was described by the Second Circuit as a former AUSA in 1996.

### 1990s–2002: Return to private practice

The available sources place Cohen in private practice before the creation of Cohen & Gresser, but do not provide a complete year-by-year employment history for the latter 1990s.

### 2002: Founding Cohen & Gresser

Cohen and **Lawrence T. Gresser** founded **Cohen & Gresser LLP in New York in 2002**.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser New York office history](https://www.cohengresser.com/office/new-york/).

The firm developed into an international litigation and corporate practice. Firm materials identify later expansions into:
- **Paris — 2014**
- **Washington, D.C. — 2016**
- **London — 2018**
- **Dubai — 2025**

Source: [Cohen & Gresser corporate practice / firm history](https://www.cohengresser.com/practice_area/corporate/).

Cohen remained based in New York while building a substantial cross-border practice.

### 2005 onward: Professional writing and litigation practice

Firm records begin listing a series of professional publications by Cohen on depositions, antitrust, banking regulation, insider trading, the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act, SEC enforcement, mergers, securities law, federal sentencing, and cross-border investigations.

This body of work marks a movement from his law-school constitutional scholarship toward his mature practice areas: trial advocacy, financial regulation, securities enforcement, white-collar criminal law, and corporate investigations.

### 2008–2009: Litigation directly adverse to JPMorgan

One of the clearest direct professional connections between Cohen and **JPMorgan Chase** appears in litigation arising from the failed **Inspirada** real-estate development in Henderson, Nevada.

In **JP Morgan Chase Bank, N.A. v. Coleman-Toll Limited Partnership**, Cohen, along with Cohen & Gresser lawyers Oliver Scott Haker and Sandra C. McCallion, represented **Meritage Homes of Nevada, Inc. and Meritage Homes Corporation**, defendants in litigation brought by JPMorgan Chase and related lenders.  
Source: [JP Morgan Chase Bank v. Coleman-Toll](https://www.casemine.com/judgement/us/59146733add7b049342adb92/amp).

The case arose from a large planned Nevada development and a complex group of lender/developer disputes. The New York federal proceedings were transferred to Nevada.

**Nature of the JPMorgan connection:** direct but **adversarial**. Cohen was not shown in this matter as JPMorgan’s lawyer; he represented parties opposed to JPMorgan.

### 2009 onward: Ghislaine Maxwell becomes a Cohen & Gresser client

A 2016 federal-court privilege ruling in **Giuffre v. Maxwell** provides unusually important evidence about the chronology of Cohen’s relationship with **Ghislaine Maxwell**.

The opinion recounts Maxwell’s declaration that she hired attorney **Brett Jaffe**, then at Cohen & Gresser, at an unspecified point in **2009** to represent her concerning United States legal matters and a deposition. When Jaffe later left Cohen & Gresser, Maxwell stated that **Mark Cohen continued as her U.S. counsel**. The court found that an email’s contents supported Maxwell’s assertion that Cohen represented her United States interests while attorney Philip Barden represented her in the United Kingdom.  
Source: [Giuffre v. Maxwell, 2016 privilege opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/new-york/nysdce/1%3A2015cv07433/447706/135/).

This record is important because it establishes that Cohen’s Maxwell relationship did **not** begin with her 2020 criminal arrest. It reaches back to the Epstein-related civil-litigation era.

### 2011: Court monitor for FDNY

Cohen’s biography records that the Eastern District of New York appointed him as **Court Monitor for the New York City Fire Department** following litigation over racially discriminatory hiring practices. His responsibilities involved monitoring implementation of remedial orders. Firm materials date the appointment to November 2011 by Judge Nicholas Garaufis.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser biography](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

This was a court-appointed oversight role, not a representation of the city or FDNY as an ordinary client.

### 2011–2014: Maxwell, Epstein, and JPMorgan temporal overlap

Separate banking records reported years later establish that **Ghislaine Maxwell and Jeffrey Epstein had banking relationships with JPMorgan Chase**. Reuters reported in 2026, based on subsequently disclosed records, that JPMorgan had internally identified Maxwell as a **“High Risk Client” in 2011** because of her relationship to Epstein; JPMorgan later moved to end Epstein’s banking relationship, and Maxwell began transitioning from JPMorgan to UBS in 2013–2014.  
Source: [Reuters, 8 February 2026](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/ubs-banked-ghislaine-maxwell-years-moving-her-money-after-epsteins-arrest-2026-02-08/).

Because the 2016 federal opinion establishes that Cohen & Gresser’s Maxwell representation began in 2009 and that Cohen later continued as her U.S. counsel, there was a **temporal overlap** between Cohen’s legal representation of Maxwell and Maxwell’s documented JPMorgan banking relationship.

However:

- No source consulted establishes that Cohen arranged Maxwell’s JPMorgan accounts.
- No source consulted establishes that he advised her concerning JPMorgan or UBS.
- No source consulted establishes that he knew of JPMorgan’s internal risk classification.
- No source consulted establishes that he participated in her transition from JPMorgan to UBS.
- Temporal overlap is therefore **not evidence of involvement in the banking relationship**.

### 2013–2014: Securities and financial litigation

Cohen’s practice increasingly centered on SEC enforcement and financial cases.

On **30 May 2014**, a Cohen & Gresser team led by Cohen and Jonathan Abernethy won a trial victory for **Peter Black**, a former Wynnefield Capital analyst, in an SEC insider-trading case. The firm describes the result as a complete defense verdict.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser biography](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

### 2014–2018: International expansion and teaching

Cohen & Gresser opened in Paris in 2014. Cohen became a longtime visiting lecturer at **Université Paris I Panthéon-Sorbonne**. His professional biography lists instruction and lectures in U.S. white-collar criminal law, anti-corruption, and related subjects.

In 2017 he taught a course identified as **“U.S. White Collar Criminal Law”** with Muriel Goldberg-Darmon, with C&G lawyers Christian Everdell and Jonathan Abernethy participating as visiting lecturers.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser biography](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

This is evidence of repeated professional travel and teaching in Paris, but not evidence that Cohen ever became resident in France.

### 2017: Former JPMorgan senior lawyer joins Cohen & Gresser

On **18 April 2017**, Cohen & Gresser announced that **Kwaku Andoh** had joined the firm’s New York office. The firm stated that Andoh had spent **14 years at JPMorgan Chase**, where he had been a **Managing Director and Associate General Counsel** and had advised on more than **$10 billion** in investments, mergers and acquisitions, and venture-capital transactions across JPMorgan business lines.  
Sources: [Cohen & Gresser announcement](https://www.cohengresser.com/news_item/former-jpmorgan-chase-corporate-lawyer-joins-cg/); [Kwaku Andoh biography](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/kwaku-andoh/).

**Nature of the JPMorgan connection:** firm-level personnel/network connection. It shows that Cohen’s firm recruited a senior former JPMorgan lawyer. It does not establish that Cohen was employed by JPMorgan, represented JPMorgan, or had a personal financial relationship with the bank.

### 2018: Och-Ziff / Vanja Baros

Cohen & Gresser reports that Cohen and Jonathan Abernethy won dismissal of an SEC complaint against **Vanja Baros**, a former analyst associated with Och-Ziff, in July 2018. The SEC did not appeal.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser biography](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

### 2018: Michigan scholarship gift

The University of Michigan Law School reported gifts totaling $100,000 from Cohen and Lawrence Gresser supporting the Cohen & Gresser Scholarship Fund.  
Source: [Michigan Law Quadrangle](https://quadrangle.michigan.law.umich.edu/issues/springsummer-2018/recent-gifts-springsummer-2018).

### 2020: Ghislaine Maxwell criminal case

Maxwell was arrested in July 2020 and charged in the Southern District of New York.

The federal docket records **Mark Stewart Cohen** entering an appearance for Maxwell shortly after her arrest. The Department of Justice’s Maxwell case page preserves the federal prosecution’s charging and case history.  
Sources: [CourtListener — United States v. Maxwell](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/17318376/united-states-v-maxwell/); [DOJ Maxwell case page](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/united-states-v-ghislaine-maxwell).

Cohen & Gresser’s current biography describes his Maxwell role carefully: he **handled the initial proceedings and served as a strategic adviser to the trial team**. That description is more precise than calling him simply the sole or lead courtroom trial lawyer. The defense team also included Christian Everdell, Bobbi Sternheim, Laura Menninger, and Jeffrey Pagliuca at various stages.

### 2021: JPMorgan Chase records produced to Maxwell defense team

A Department of Justice discovery letter dated **2 February 2021** was addressed in part to **Christian Everdell and Mark Cohen at Cohen & Gresser** in the Maxwell prosecution.

The attached production index explicitly listed:

**“REPRODUCTION, JPMorgan Chase materials”**

with a Bates range of approximately 794 pages.  
Source: [DOJ discovery letter / Epstein files production](https://media-cdn.rollcall.com/epstein-files/EFTA00011267.pdf).

This is a direct documentary link between Cohen’s work in the Maxwell defense and **JPMorgan Chase material**.

What it establishes:
- Cohen was one of Maxwell’s defense lawyers receiving discovery from federal prosecutors.
- JPMorgan Chase material was among the discovery produced to that defense team.

What it does **not** establish:
- that Cohen represented JPMorgan;
- that Cohen had a personal account at JPMorgan;
- that JPMorgan paid Cohen or his firm;
- that Cohen was involved in JPMorgan’s banking relationship with Maxwell or Epstein;
- that the produced documents implicated Cohen personally.

### 2021–2022: Maxwell conviction and sentence

On **29 December 2021**, after a month-long federal jury trial, Maxwell was convicted on five counts relating to the sexual abuse of minors with Jeffrey Epstein.  
Source: [DOJ sentencing announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/ghislaine-maxwell-sentenced-20-years-prison-conspiring-jeffrey-epstein-sexually-abuse).

On **28 June 2022**, Judge Alison Nathan sentenced Maxwell to:
- **240 months / 20 years in prison**
- **five years of supervised release**
- a **$750,000 fine**

Source: [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/ghislaine-maxwell-sentenced-20-years-prison-conspiring-jeffrey-epstein-sexually-abuse).

This was a major loss for the defense team. It is not evidence of misconduct by Cohen.

### December 2022: Sam Bankman-Fried hires Cohen

After the collapse of FTX and Alameda Research and before Bankman-Fried’s extradition to the United States, Reuters reported that **Sam Bankman-Fried had hired Mark S. Cohen of Cohen & Gresser**. Paul Weiss had ceased representing Bankman-Fried, citing conflicts.  
Source: [Reuters, 6 December 2022](https://newslink.reuters.com/public/29906096).

Cohen and his longtime colleague **Christian Everdell** became the principal lawyers on Bankman-Fried’s trial defense.

### 2023: Bankman-Fried pretrial litigation

Bankman-Fried was extradited from the Bahamas under an indictment that was later supplemented with additional charges.

His defense argued that several charges added after extradition could not proceed without the Bahamas’ consent under principles associated with the extradition treaty and the doctrine of specialty. Reuters reported in June 2023 that prosecutors agreed that five later-added counts would be tried separately in March 2024 while the Bahamas issue remained unresolved.  
Source: [Reuters, 15 June 2023](https://www.reuters.com/legal/us-prosecutors-drop-new-charges-against-bankman-fried-oct-trial-2023-06-15/).

Cohen & Gresser’s biography characterizes this phase as a successful challenge to six counts on extradition-treaty grounds. That firm description should be read together with the procedural history: the disputed charges were separated from the first trial, and after Bankman-Fried was convicted on all seven counts tried in 2023, prosecutors later decided not to conduct the planned second trial.  
Sources: [Cohen & Gresser](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/); [Reuters, 29 December 2023](https://www.reuters.com/legal/sam-bankman-fried-will-not-face-second-trial-us-prosecutors-say-2023-12-29/).

### October–November 2023: Bankman-Fried trial

Cohen served as **lead trial counsel** during Bankman-Fried’s approximately five-week federal jury trial.

The prosecution presented testimony from multiple former FTX/Alameda insiders, including Caroline Ellison, Gary Wang, and Nishad Singh, as well as documentary and financial evidence.

On **2 November 2023**, the jury convicted Bankman-Fried on **all seven counts** submitted in the first trial.  
Source: [DOJ FTX prosecution page](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/prosecuting-fraud).

The defense’s strategy and courtroom performance became subjects of extensive commentary. A WIRED analysis documented criticism of the defense’s cross-examinations and strategic choices but also quoted experienced defense lawyers who argued that the evidentiary record was exceptionally difficult and that the defense had pursued the limited avenues realistically available.  
Source: [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/sam-bankman-frieds-lawyers-are-doing-just-fine-actually/).

This disagreement is best treated as professional interpretation, not an objective finding that Cohen’s representation was either competent or incompetent.

### December 2023: No second SBF trial

Federal prosecutors announced that they would not conduct a second trial on the remaining charges, emphasizing the public interest in prompt resolution and the evidence already aired at the first trial.  
Source: [Reuters, 29 December 2023](https://www.reuters.com/legal/sam-bankman-fried-will-not-face-second-trial-us-prosecutors-say-2023-12-29/).

### March 2024: SBF sentencing

On **28 March 2024**, Judge Lewis A. Kaplan sentenced Bankman-Fried to **25 years in federal prison**.  
Source: [DOJ sentencing announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/samuel-bankman-fried-sentenced-25-years-prison).

Cohen was principal trial counsel but did not remain the principal appellate advocate.

### September 2024: Justice William Strong Lecture

Cohen delivered the **Justice William Strong Lecture** at Widener Commonwealth Law School on **19 September 2024**, discussing ethical challenges confronting prosecutors and defense counsel in white-collar cases. His firm biography later listed a published article adapted from that lecture.  
Source: [Cohen & Gresser biography](https://www.cohengresser.com/attorney/mark-s-cohen/).

### November 2024 onward: UK company-control record

Companies House lists **Mark Stewart Cohen** as a person with significant control of **Cohen & Gresser (UK) LLP**, with notification dated **19 November 2024**. The recorded nature of control is **“has significant influence or control.”**

The filing does not disclose a specific equity percentage for Cohen.  
Source: [Companies House](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/OC421038/persons-with-significant-control).

### 2025: Adam Kaplan defense and conviction

Cohen’s firm biography describes an eight-week federal trial involving a former investment-adviser representative. A Department of Justice announcement identifies the relevant defendant as **Adam Kaplan**, who was tried with his twin brother Daniel Kaplan.

On **13 November 2025**, a federal jury convicted the brothers in a fraud prosecution. The DOJ stated that Adam Kaplan was additionally convicted on conspiracy, bank- and wire-fraud conspiracy, money laundering, and attempted obstruction counts.  
Source: [EDNY DOJ announcement](https://www.justice.gov/usao-edny/pr/long-island-investment-advisors-convicted-defrauding-clients-out-millions-dollars).

This was another significant defense loss, but again not evidence of misconduct by counsel.

### Late 2025: Cohen & Gresser explores private-equity / MSO financing structure

Reuters reported in December 2025 that Cohen & Gresser, then described as having roughly 70 lawyers, had been discussing outside investment structures with private-equity firms.

According to Reuters:
- one structure under consideration involved the sale of a **$40 million convertible note**;
- the note could later convert into equity in a prospective **management services organization (MSO)**;
- the MSO structure was intended to separate non-legal back-office services from the law partnership in order to navigate restrictions on non-lawyer ownership of law firms;
- co-founder Lawrence Gresser confirmed that the firm was exploring structures with investment firms.

Source: [Reuters, 18 December 2025](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/law-firms-chart-back-office-deals-investors-seek-bigger-legal-market-foothold-2025-12-18/).

The Financial Times separately reported that Cohen & Gresser was in talks to sell a stake to private equity.  
Source: [Financial Times](https://www.ft.com/content/9487e94f-fa82-4cea-aec3-ebc10ef44d43).

No source consulted for this dossier establishes that a transaction had closed by 19 August 2026.

### 2026: SBF appeal affirmed

Source: [Second Circuit decision](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca2/24-961/24-961-2026-06-12.html).

Cohen & Gresser’s biography lists continuing 2026 professional rankings, including inclusion in an inaugural Lawdragon global investigations list dated **10 August 2026**. He remained a partner and managing partner of the New York office at the research cutoff.

## Ghislaine Maxwell: Detailed Relationship Reconstruction

### 2009 civil representation

The strongest early source is the 2016 privilege ruling in *Giuffre v. Maxwell*. Maxwell stated that she retained Brett Jaffe of Cohen & Gresser in 2009. After Jaffe’s departure, Cohen continued as U.S. counsel.

This places Cohen inside Maxwell’s legal affairs during the long interval between Epstein’s 2008 Florida plea and Maxwell’s 2020 federal arrest.

### Nature of early work

The federal opinion refers to United States legal matters and deposition-related representation. The surviving public record does not provide a complete retainer agreement, billing history, or month-by-month scope of Cohen’s work.

### 2020 criminal representation

After Maxwell’s arrest, Cohen formally appeared in the Southern District of New York criminal proceeding.

His role during the pretrial period included strategic criminal-defense work. His later firm biography distinguishes his role from that of lawyers who carried more of the courtroom trial presentation.

### 2021 JPMorgan discovery

The DOJ’s discovery index is direct evidence that JPMorgan records were among the materials supplied to Cohen and the Maxwell defense.

This matters for network reconstruction because it establishes not simply that Cohen knew Maxwell, or that Maxwell banked with JPMorgan, but that **JPMorgan-source material entered Cohen’s professional case file in the federal prosecution**.

There is still no evidence in the consulted material of a preexisting Cohen–JPMorgan relationship connected to those records.

### Conviction and aftermath

Maxwell was convicted and sentenced to 20 years. Cohen’s public professional biography continues to list the representation as a major matter.

The Maxwell relationship has become part of Cohen’s public identity despite the unfavorable verdict, much as the Bankman-Fried case later did.

## Sam Bankman-Fried: Detailed Relationship Reconstruction

### Hiring

Reuters reported Cohen’s hiring on 6 December 2022, before Bankman-Fried was brought to the United States.

### Defense team

Cohen and Christian Everdell became the principal trial lawyers. Other lawyers joined or handled specialized aspects over time.

### Pretrial bail and litigation

The case generated extraordinary pretrial litigation over bail, communications, discovery, superseding indictments, and extradition-treaty issues.

### Treaty / specialty litigation

The defense’s extradition challenge materially affected which counts were tried in October 2023.

This episode is one of Cohen’s strongest technical successes in the case even though it did not prevent conviction on the seven counts that proceeded.

### Trial

The defense faced cooperating testimony from three central former insiders and extensive business records.

Cohen cross-examined major prosecution witnesses and delivered significant portions of the defense case.

### Verdict

All seven counts tried resulted in convictions.

### Second-trial decision

The remaining charges were never tried after prosecutors abandoned the planned second proceeding.

### Sentence

25 years.

### Appeal

The Second Circuit affirmed in June 2026. Cohen was no longer the primary appellate advocate, so the affirmance should not be treated as a direct assessment of his trial strategy except insofar as trial rulings were reviewed.
