## Works by Ronald Richter

### Research projects and unrealized proposals

- **c. 1934–1935:** Project to detect supposed terrestrial “delta radiation.” A proposed doctoral investigation rather than a completed work; Heinrich Rausch von Traubenberg reportedly rejected the subject, and surviving recollections disagree over how far Richter pursued it.

- **1935:** Unpublished doctoral dissertation on barrier-layer photocells. No securely documented title has been recovered, and accounts dispute Richter’s academic history; the German University in Prague’s doctoral register records his graduation, while later testimony sometimes conflates this dissertation with the rejected “delta rays” proposal.

- **1948–1952:** *Proyecto Huemul*. A state-financed project intended to produce controlled thermonuclear energy, conceived and directed by Richter. It is best treated as a failed scientific-technological Gesamtkunstwerk—proposal, experimental program, architecture, machinery, public performance, and political promise—rather than as a single experiment; the 1952 technical commission found no evidence that Richter had produced, sustained, or controlled a thermonuclear reaction.

- **By 1984–1985:** Untitled book about Richter’s European career, scientific theories, and the Huemul project. Richter told an interviewer that he was preparing this book, but no completed or published work has been located; it therefore survives only as a reported project, not as an obra with a recoverable title.

### Experimental apparatus and installations

- **1948–1949:** Untitled experimental apparatus at the Instituto Aerotécnico, Córdoba. Richter conducted arc-discharge experiments there while proposing nuclear-powered aircraft; a fire destroyed equipment, and the surviving accounts do not permit a reliable inventory or establish that the apparatus produced nuclear effects.

- **1949–1952:** Huemul Island experimental complex. Built by government personnel and contractors under Richter’s direction, it comprised laboratories, workshops, a power station, control facilities, and reactor structures. Authorship is therefore collective and administrative as well as Richter’s; the complex resists treatment as a wholly personal “work.”

- **1949–1950:** Untitled first large reactor enclosure in Laboratory 1. A roughly twelve-metre concrete cylinder designed to house Richter’s proposed reactor; Richter ordered it demolished after completion, variously because of incorrectly placed radial pipes, cracking, or alleged sabotage, so it was an abandoned and partly destroyed prototype rather than an operative reactor.

- **1950–1951:** Untitled small reactor in Laboratory 2, later described as the *thermotron*. An electric-arc apparatus into which hydrogen and lithium compounds were introduced, surrounded by magnetic, optical, and radiation-detection equipment. Richter presented its signals as evidence of fusion, but the official inspection identified ordinary arc oscillations and instrumental interference rather than nuclear reactions.

- **1951:** Untitled magnetic-confinement redesign of the Huemul reactor. After the disputed February experiment, Richter ordered the smaller apparatus dismantled and planned a replacement incorporating magnetic confinement; the design changed during construction, and a fourteen-metre excavation was ultimately filled with concrete. It is an unfinished project embodied chiefly in alterations, excavation, and abandoned construction.

- **1951–1952:** Untitled heavy-water separation installation. Shown by Richter as part of the Huemul program but operated by Dr. Ehrenberg, its authorship is not solely Richter’s. The inspectors found that no enrichment measurements had initially been made, so even its status as a functioning separation plant was disputed.

### Scientific and performative texts

- **1951:** *Reacciones termonucleares*. A written conference text read by Richter at the presidential residence after Perón’s Huemul announcement and subsequently printed in *Mundo Atómico*, year 2, vol. 1, no. 4, pp. 6–8, 10, 63–67, 71. It is simultaneously a scientific claim and a public manifesto for the Huemul project; it appears to be Richter’s only identifiable formally published scientific text.

- **1952:** Untitled replies or refutation addressed to the Huemul technical commission. A memorandum defending Richter’s theories, apparatus, and experimental claims against the inspectors’ reports; it survives indirectly through the commission’s point-by-point response and archival references. Its exact title, extent, and present completeness are uncertain.

### Claimed creation that does not securely qualify as a work

- **16 February 1951:** Alleged controlled thermonuclear reaction at Huemul. Richter treated the event as an experimental creation and the foundation of his public claim, but the result could not be reproduced or independently verified; contemporary technical examination concluded that no evidence of a nuclear reaction existed. It should therefore be recorded as a claimed manifestation, not as an accomplished reactor process.
