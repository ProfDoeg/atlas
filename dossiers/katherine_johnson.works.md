## Works by Katherine Johnson

### Collective technical compendium

- **1958:** *Notes on Space Technology*. An internally produced NACA course text compiled from seventeen lectures; Johnson supplied some descriptive material and mathematics, but the surviving document does not identify her sections, so it resists treatment as a separately authored work. ([NASA biography](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/katherine-g-johnson/); [NASA Technical Reports Server](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19740074640))

### NASA technical reports

- **September 1960:** *Determination of Azimuth Angle at Burnout for Placing a Satellite Over a Selected Earth Position* (NASA TN D-233). A coauthored technical note with Theodore H. Skopinski deriving orbital-position and launch-azimuth relationships; Johnson reportedly completed most of the work, but the published authorship remains joint. ([NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/archive/nasa/casi.ntrs.nasa.gov/19980227091.pdf); [NASA biography](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/katherine-g-johnson/))

- **June 1962:** *The Orbital Behavior of the Echo I Satellite and Its Rocket Casing During the First 500 Days* (NASA TN D-1366). A technical note coauthored with Gertrude C. Westrick, studying orbital perturbation by solar radiation pressure. NTRS metadata reverses the author order printed in the original report, which places Westrick first. ([NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19620003290); [original report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19620003290/downloads/19620003290.pdf?attachment=true))

- **July 1964:** *Approximate Solutions for Flight-Path Angle of a Reentry Vehicle in the Upper Atmosphere* (NASA TN D-2379). A technical note with Jack A. White comparing analytical approximations with numerical solutions of reentry equations; the original report places White first although NTRS metadata has at times placed Johnson first. ([NASA record](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19640016000); [NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **July 1965:** *A Study of Solar-System Geometric Parameters for Use as Interplanetary Navigation Aids* (NASA TN D-2890). A coauthored technical note with W. Thomas Blackshear treating celestial geometry as an onboard navigation resource. ([NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **December 1967:** *Midcourse-Guidance Procedure with Single Position Fix Obtained from Onboard Optical Measurements* (NASA TN D-4246). A technical protocol-like study, coauthored with Harold A. Hamer and W. Thomas Blackshear, for correcting a spacecraft’s course from one optical position fix. ([NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf); [NASA citation in a subsequent report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19690001041/downloads/19690001041.pdf?attachment=true))

- **March 1969:** *Effect of Gravitational-Model Selection on Accuracy of Lunar Orbit Determination from Short Data Arcs* (NASA TN D-5105). A coauthored technical note with Harold A. Hamer about how lunar-gravity models affect orbit determination; it concerns Langley’s unmanned Lunar Orbiter work rather than serving as an Apollo flight plan. ([NTRS-derived bibliography and discussion](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **October 1970:** *An Approach-Guidance Method Using a Single Onboard Optical Measurement* (NASA TN D-5963). A coauthored technical note with Harold A. Hamer presenting an optical approach-guidance method—part analytical study, part operational protocol. ([NASA report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700031427/downloads/19700031427.pdf); [NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **July 1971:** *Midcourse and Approach Guidance Requirements for Simplified Onboard Control of Moon-to-Earth Trajectories* (NASA TN D-6343). A technical study with Harold A. Hamer defining simplified guidance requirements for return trajectories. ([NASA report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19710021047/downloads/19710021047.pdf?attachment=true); [NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **September 1971:** *Fixed-Angle Translunar Guidance Procedures Using Onboard Optical Measurements* (NASA TN D-6461). A coauthored guidance study with Harold A. Hamer; as “procedures,” it is also a protocol prescribing how optical measurements can support translunar navigation. ([NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **May 1972:** *Simplified Interplanetary Guidance Procedures Using Onboard Optical Measurements* (NASA TN D-6752). A coauthored technical note with Harold A. Hamer extending their optical-navigation procedures to interplanetary flight. ([NASA report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19720016015/downloads/19720016015.pdf); [NTRS-derived bibliography](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **July 1978:** *Effects of Errors on Decoupled Control Systems* (NASA TP-1184). A technical paper with Harold A. Hamer analyzing how modeling, measurement, flight-condition, and control-lag errors affect a simulated STOL-aircraft control system. ([NASA technical-publications abstract](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19790004826/downloads/19790004826.pdf))

### Computational work-products that resist the category “work”

- **1953–1957:** *Flight-test reductions and wake-turbulence accident analysis*. These were calculations and analyzed data made collectively inside NACA’s workflow, not stable, individually titled publications; NASA confirms the activity but does not supply a catalogue of surviving sheets or assign Johnson sole authorship. ([NASA biography](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/katherine-g-johnson/))

- **1961:** *Trajectory and recovery calculations for Alan Shepard’s Freedom 7 flight*. A mission-specific body of hand calculation, including launch-window and recovery-position work, rather than a formally published, fixed-title text. Its exact documentary boundaries and division among Johnson and her colleagues are not established by the institutional bibliography. ([Katherine Johnson Foundation chronology](https://www.katherinejohnsonfoundation.org/her-work/); [NASA biography](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/katherine-g-johnson/))

- **1962:** *Independent verification calculations for John Glenn’s Friendship 7 flight*. Johnson checked the electronic computer’s orbital results at Glenn’s request; this is an enacted mathematical verification rather than a separately titled report, and popular accounts differ about when and how extensively she performed it. ([NASA account](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/ivv/nasa-langleys-katherine-johnson-computational-research-facility-officially-opens/); [contemporary-record dispute](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf))

- **1960s:** *Apollo lunar-module/command-module rendezvous calculations*. Johnson identified the synchronization calculations for lunar-orbit rendezvous as her greatest contribution, but NASA does not attach a discrete title or surviving sole-authored document to them. Their status is therefore collaborative mission work rather than a securely bounded obra. ([NASA biography](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/katherine-g-johnson/))

- **1970:** *Apollo 13 backup calculations or charts*. This attribution is disputed: some institutional and journalistic accounts associate Johnson with Apollo 13, while her posthumous memoir disclaims a role in that mission; no separately titled Johnson document has been identified. It should not be treated as a secure work attribution. ([NTRS-derived critical discussion](https://www.andrew-brundle.com/pdf/hidden-figures-critique.pdf); [Guardian obituary](https://www.theguardian.com/science/2020/feb/24/katherine-johnson-obituary))

- **1970s–1986:** *Space Shuttle, Earth-resources satellite, Mars-mission, and large-flexible-structure analyses*. NASA records these as Johnson’s later projects, but not as one named work or a complete set of individually attributable texts; they are projects and collaborative analytical production whose surviving documentary limits remain unclear. ([NASA biography](https://www.nasa.gov/people-of-nasa/katherine-g-johnson/); [Katherine Johnson Foundation chronology](https://www.katherinejohnsonfoundation.org/her-work/))

### Autobiographical books

- **2019:** *Reaching for the Moon: The Autobiography of NASA Mathematician Katherine Johnson*. A first-person autobiography written for younger readers and published during Johnson’s lifetime. ([Simon & Schuster publisher record](https://www.simonandschuster.com/books/Reaching-for-the-Moon/Katherine-Johnson/9781534440838))

- **2021, posthumous:** *One Step Further: My Story of Math, the Moon, and a Lifelong Mission*. A picture-book family autobiography made with Johnson’s daughters Joylette Hylick and Katherine Moore and illustrated by Charnelle Pinkney Barlow; its posthumous manifestation combines Johnson’s narrative with her daughters’ recollections rather than constituting a wholly solitary text. ([Disney/National Geographic publisher record](https://books.disney.com/book/one-step-further/))

- **2021, posthumous:** *My Remarkable Journey: A Memoir*. An adult memoir credited to Katherine Johnson with Joylette Hylick and Katherine Moore and with Lisa Frazier Page. It was issued after Johnson’s death from collaborative work and a surviving manuscript, so its publication date is not a date of solitary composition. ([Katherine Johnson Foundation bibliography](https://www.katherinejohnsonfoundation.org/books-resources/); [Katherine G. Johnson Papers finding aid](https://arvasarchive.org/download/pdf?id=wvmturhc_repositories_2_resources_6907))
