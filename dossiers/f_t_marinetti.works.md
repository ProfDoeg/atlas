## Works by Filippo Tommaso Emilio Marinetti

### Poetic books, free-word objects, and aeropoems

- **1902:** *La Conquête des Étoiles*. French-language book-length poem and Marinetti’s first published book; its cosmic assault already anticipates Futurist imagery. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1904:** *La Momie sanglante*. French-language poem issued through the newspaper *Verde e Azzurro*; later Italian publication under another title should not be mistaken for a newly composed work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1904:** *Destruction*. French-language poem; the Italian *Distruzione* is a later authorial version rather than an independent originating work. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1908:** *La Ville charnelle*. Collection of French prose-poems/poems whose city is simultaneously setting, erotic body, and modern sensory apparatus. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1911:** *Distruzione*. Italian reworking of *Destruction*, materially a new book but textually derived from the 1904 French poem. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1912:** *I poeti futuristi*. Collective poetic anthology organized and substantially framed by Marinetti; it is a movement-making platform, not wholly his authored work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1914:** *Zang Tumb Tuuum: Adrianopoli ottobre 1912*. Typographic “words-in-freedom” book made from Marinetti’s Balkan War reporting and performance pieces; text, page design, noise notation, and imagined declamation are inseparable. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1918:** *Poesie scelte*. Authorial selection of earlier poetry; a collection rather than a single newly composed poem. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1918:** *Antologia della Diana*. Collective anthology containing Marinetti’s poetry and prose; his contribution is an obra within a book he did not make alone. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1919:** *Les mots en liberté futuristes*. French-language anthology, theory-book, and typographic demonstration of words-in-freedom—a Gesamtkunstwerk-like fusion of poem, manifesto, and graphic object. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1920:** *La conquista delle stelle*. Italian version of *La Conquête des Étoiles*; a distinct publication object, but not a wholly independent composition. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1921:** *Lussuria–Velocità*. Collection of poems joining erotic and mechanical intensities; some contents derive from earlier French work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1925:** *I nuovi poeti futuristi*. Movement anthology assembled under Marinetti’s direction; collective manifestation rather than sole-authored poetry book. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1931:** *Spagna veloce e toro futurista*. Travel-poem/poetic reportage in which bullfighting, speed, landscape, and political spectacle are deliberately hybridized. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1932:** *Parole in libertà futuriste olfattive tattili-termiche*. Collection and multisensory program: poems are conceived as visual, olfactory, tactile, and thermal events, resisting classification as verbal texts alone. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1933:** *Poemi simultanei futuristi*. Collection of simultaneous poems designed to collapse separate places, sensations, and voices into one textual event. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1935:** *L’aeropoema del Golfo della Spezia*. Aeropoem: aerial perception, patriotic geography, and lyric sequence form a single work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1937:** *Il poema africano della Divisione “28 ottobre”*. Colonial-war aeropoem based on Marinetti’s Ethiopian campaign experience; poem and fascist propaganda are inseparable aspects of the object. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1937:** *Il poema del vestito di latte*. Promotional-industrial poem celebrating artificial milk fibre; part poetry, part commissioned advertising manifestation. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1938:** *Il poema di Torre Viscosa*. Industrial poem concerning the newly built company town and synthetic-fibre works; its patronage and propagandistic function resist the category of autonomous lyric. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1939:** *Il poema dei sansepolcristi*. Commemorative political poem about the founding Fascists; poem and party memorial are the same made thing. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1940:** *Il poema non umano dei tecnicismi*. Poetic project built from specialized technical vocabularies and directed against conventionally “human” lyric expression. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1942:** *Gerardo Dottori aeropittore futurista umbro*. Poem, critical tribute, and promotional monograph on Dottori’s aeropainting. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1942:** *Canto eroi e macchine della guerra mussoliniana*. Wartime poem-cycle combining technological exaltation with Fascist propaganda. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1942:** *L’esercito italiano: poesia armata*. Anthology of poems and prose assembled as a wartime patriotic object rather than a single continuous poem. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1943:** *Canzoniere futurista amoroso guerriero*. Retrospective anthology combining love poetry and war poetry. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1943:** *Lo riprenderemo*. Illustrated wartime poem; words, images, and irredentist propaganda constitute the object together. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1944:** *L’aeropoema di Cozzarini*. Aeropoem published during Marinetti’s final year. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 1945:** *Quarto d’ora di poesia della X MAS*. Final wartime poem, published posthumously; its title also appears with typographic variants such as “X Mas.” [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Undated manuscript; published 1971:** *Poesie a Beny*. Intimate poems addressed to Benedetta Cappa Marinetti; the publication date is not their secure composition date. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Uncertain composition date; published 1991:** *L’aeropoema di Gesù*. Posthumously recovered religious aeropoem; its late publication must not be used as a composition date. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 2019:** *Poema di Fiume*. Posthumous poem concerning Fiume; the surviving project belongs to Marinetti’s lifetime, while the named book-object is editorially posthumous. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 2023:** *Aeropoema vissuto della Rivoluzione Fascista*. Posthumously issued poem whose exact composition history requires manuscript study; 2023 is the edition date, not the work’s date. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; announced for 2026:** *La Palma Italia*. Posthumous poem/project listed by the Marinetti chronology as forthcoming; until publication and manuscript description, its textual completeness remains uncertain. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)

### Novels, narratives, and hybrid prose

- **1910:** *Mafarka le futuriste*. French novel and myth of self-generated mechanical progeny; prosecuted for obscenity after its Italian appearance. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/filippo-tommaso-marinetti_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1910:** *Mafarka il futurista*. Italian authorial version of *Mafarka le futuriste*; a distinct textual and legal event, though not an unrelated second novel. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1912:** *Le Monoplan du pape*. French satirical novel of aviation, nationalism, and anticlerical fantasy. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1914:** *L’Aeroplano del Papa*. Italian version of *Le Monoplan du pape*; materially a separate book and linguistically reworked, but derivative of the French novel. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1918:** *L’isola dei baci*. Collaborative novel by Marinetti and Bruno Corra; sole attribution to Marinetti would be false. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1919:** *8 anime in una bomba*. Experimental war novel/autobiographical fantasy whose eight internal personae make the “novel” partly a performed theory of divided identity. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1919:** *Un ventre di donna*. Collaborative epistolary and narrative book by Marinetti and Enif Robert; it combines letters, fiction, polemic, and Robert’s account of illness and surgery. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1920:** *Il delizioso pericolo*. Collection of short narratives, not a novel despite later cataloguing that sometimes flattens Marinetti’s prose into that category. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1921:** *L’alcova d’acciaio*. War narrative blending memoir, novel, machine-erotic fantasy, and propaganda; it resists a clean fiction/nonfiction division. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1922:** *Gli indomabili*. Allegorical novel/fable about bodily discipline and revolt. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1922:** *Gli amori futuristi*. “Programma di vita” made from short narratives and prescriptions about erotic conduct; neither ordinary fiction nor simple manifesto. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1927:** *Scatole d’amore in conserva*. Collection of compressed love stories conceived metaphorically as preserved, packaged objects. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1930:** *Novelle colle labbra tinte*. Collection of stories rather than a single narrative work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1929:** *Lo Zar non è morto*. Collective novel written by the “Gruppo dei Dieci”; Marinetti is one contributor, so the work cannot responsibly be assigned to him alone. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1931:** *Il club dei simpatici*. Novel; its social satire and episodic construction also approach the collective portrait. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1938:** *Amanti simultanei*. “Romanzo discato,” issued as a recorded novel: voice, phonographic disc, packaging, and narrative constitute the work, making it neither merely a book nor merely a sound recording. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1939:** *Patriottismo insetticida*. “Romanzo legislativo,” deliberately crossing novel, political prescription, and manifesto; sources also print the title as *Patriotismo insetticida*. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 1969:** *La grande Milano tradizionale e futurista*. Memoir/topographical portrait of Milan, published posthumously and shaped into book form by later editors. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Uncertain composition date; published 1992:** *Firenze biondazzurra sposerebbe futurista morigerato*. Posthumous novel; 1992 dates its edition, not its writing. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Uncertain composition date; published 1996:** *Originalità russa di masse distanze radiocuori*. Posthumous Russian travel-narrative/novelistic project; its hybrid reportage and fiction resist a settled genre. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 2013:** *Venezianella e studentaccio*. Posthumous novel whose surviving manuscript was turned into a book decades later. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; announced for 2026:** *Vita futurista di Capri*. Posthumous narrative project listed as forthcoming; until its manuscript and edition are described, completion and final title remain provisional. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)

### Theatre, dramatic poems, and performance scores

- **1905:** *Le Roi Bombance*. French satirical tragedy; political grotesque, banquet spectacle, and drama are integral to one another. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1909:** *Poupées électriques*. French drama featuring artificial humanoid doubles; later Italian adaptation under the title *Elettricità sessuale* materially and textually transformed it. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1910:** *Re Baldoria*. Italian version/recomposition of *Le Roi Bombance*, not an unrelated second play. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1915:** *Teatro futurista sintetico, volume I*. Collective collection of ultra-short theatrical syntheses; Marinetti authored or co-authored only part of it, while the anthology itself manifests the group project. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-drammaturgia_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/)
- **1916:** *Teatro futurista sintetico, volume II*. Second collective collection of theatrical syntheses; attribution must remain distributed among its named contributors. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1920:** *Elettricità sessuale*. Italian adaptation of *Poupées électriques*; the retitling and revision make it a distinct stage-text but not a wholly independent originating drama. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1922:** *Il tamburo di fuoco*. African-set drama combining words, movement, sound, colour, and music; it aspires to a theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1927:** *Prigionieri e vulcani*. Volume containing dramatic works rather than one indivisible play. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1941:** *Il teatro futurista*. Retrospective anthology of dramatic texts and theatrical principles; a constructed corpus rather than one new play. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 1960:** *Teatro*. Three-volume posthumous collected theatre; indispensable as a corpus, but an editorial construction rather than a single obra made by Marinetti in 1960. [Complete-theatre description](https://ftmarinetti.it/teatro/)
- **Written by 1944; published 2024:** *Velocità*. Posthumously published Futurist screenplay; its status as an unproduced screen project is more accurate than calling it a completed film. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)

### Manifestos and other texts that act

- **1909:** *Fondazione e Manifesto del Futurismo / Manifeste du futurisme*. Founding manifesto and performative act that names and launches Futurism; the Italian leaflet preceded the famous French publication in *Le Figaro*, so “20 February 1909” is not its sole first-publication date. [Marinetti archive chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)
- **1909:** *Uccidiamo il chiaro di luna! / Tuons le clair de lune!*. Manifesto-poem extending Futurism through a fictional collective expedition; later book publication does not mark its first composition. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1910:** *Contro Venezia passatista*. Anti-passatist manifesto/action associated with Futurist interventions in Venice; its public distribution and declamation are part of the work. [Manifesto archive](https://www.memofonte.it/ricerche/futurismo/archivio-manifesti-futuristi/)
- **1910:** *Discorso futurista ai Veneziani*. Speech-manifesto made for public delivery; it exists as event and printed text, neither form exhausting the work. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1911–1915:** *La guerra, sola igiene del mondo*. Political manifesto assembled through several texts and publication states, culminating in a 1915 book; it is better treated as an evolving campaign than as one fixed utterance. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1912:** *Manifesto tecnico della letteratura futurista*. Protocol for destroying syntax and reorganizing literary language. [Accademia della Crusca archive](https://futurismo.accademiadellacrusca.org/manifesti.asp)
- **1912:** *Risposte alle obiezioni*. Supplement to the technical literary manifesto; it clarifies and extends the protocol rather than merely commenting on it. [Manifesto archive](https://www.memofonte.it/ricerche/futurismo/archivio-manifesti-futuristi/)
- **1913:** *Distruzione della sintassi – Immaginazione senza fili – Parole in libertà*. Literary manifesto/protocol prescribing the free-word page. [Accademia della Crusca archive](https://futurismo.accademiadellacrusca.org/manifesti.asp)
- **1913:** *Il teatro di varietà*. Manifesto that treats popular variety performance as a model for participatory, antagonistic Futurist theatre. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-drammaturgia_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/)
- **1913:** *Nascita di un’estetica futurista*. Aesthetic manifesto/argument answering objections to Futurism and defining creation through modern forces. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)
- **1914:** *Lo splendore geometrico e meccanico e la sensibilità numerica*. Literary-aesthetic manifesto extending words-in-freedom toward mathematical, typographic, and mechanical form. [Manifesto archive](https://www.memofonte.it/ricerche/futurismo/archivio-manifesti-futuristi/)
- **1914:** *Contro l’arte inglese*. Manifesto co-authored with C. R. W. Nevinson; joint authorship must be retained. [MoMA](https://www.moma.org/interactives/exhibitions/2009/futurism/)
- **1915:** *Il teatro futurista sintetico*. Manifesto co-authored with Emilio Settimelli and Bruno Corra; a protocol for compressed, simultaneous, alogical theatrical action. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-drammaturgia_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/)
- **1915:** *La declamazione dinamica e sinottica*. Performance protocol governing the speaker’s body, voice, props, spatial movement, and visualized language. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1916:** *La cinematografia futurista*. Manifesto co-authored with Bruno Corra, Emilio Settimelli, Arnaldo Ginna, Giacomo Balla, and Remo Chiti; it proposes cinema as a synthesis of media rather than merely filmed theatre. [Manifesto archive](https://www.memofonte.it/ricerche/futurismo/archivio-manifesti-futuristi/)
- **1916:** *La nuova religione-morale della velocità*. Manifesto turning speed into an ethical and quasi-religious system. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1918:** *Manifesto del Partito Politico Futurista*. Political program attempting to convert an artistic movement into an organized political project. [Time](https://time.com/10217/the-guggenheim-goes-back-to-the-futurists/)
- **1919:** *Alfabeto a sorpresa*. Manifesto-like critical presentation of Francesco Cangiullo’s visual alphabet; Marinetti interprets and legislates another artist’s creation rather than claiming to have invented the alphabet itself. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)
- **1920:** *Al di là del comunismo*. Political manifesto proposing a Futurist alternative after Marinetti’s break with party politics. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1921:** *Il Tattilismo: manifesto futurista*. Manifesto and protocol for an art of organized touch, first presented publicly in Paris and subsequently issued in print; the date is sometimes given according to presentation, Roman performance, or publication. [Museo Omero](https://museoomero.it/opere/manifesto-futurista-sul-tattilismo/)
- **1923:** *Il teatro della sorpresa*. Theatrical manifesto co-authored with Francesco Cangiullo; surprise is prescribed as structure, not merely dramatic effect. [Arengario catalogue](https://www.arengario.it/futurismo/_pdf/catalogo-2009-marinetti.pdf)
- **1929:** *Manifesto dell’aeropittura futurista*. Collective manifesto signed by Marinetti, Balla, Benedetta, Depero, Dottori, Fillia, Prampolini, Somenzi, Tato, and others across publication states; Marinetti’s earlier article *Prospettive di volo* helped initiate it, but sole authorship is disputed and should not be imposed. [Aeropittura history](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeropittura)
- **1930:** *Manifesto della cucina futurista*. Manifesto co-authored with Fillìa; culinary protocol, publicity campaign, and social provocation rather than an ordinary essay about food. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)
- **1931:** *Manifesto dell’arte sacra futurista*. Co-authored with Fillìa; attempts to reconcile Futurist aesthetics and Catholic sacred art. [Accademia della Crusca archive](https://futurismo.accademiadellacrusca.org/manifesti.asp)
- **1932:** *Manifesto futurista della radio / La Radia*. Co-authored with Pino Masnata; a protocol for an art native to wireless transmission, rejecting radio as mere relay for theatre or literature. [Manifesto archive](https://www.memofonte.it/ricerche/futurismo/archivio-manifesti-futuristi/)
- **1933:** *La guerra futura*. Predictive and prescriptive war manifesto, not neutral military analysis. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)
- **1933:** *Il teatro totale futurista*. Project for a theatre integrating architecture, spectators, light, sound, action, and machinery—a proposed Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a completed building or production. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)
- **1933:** *Futurismo e marinettismo*. Collective declaration signed by Marinetti and other Futurists; surviving records differ in presentation and signer order, so it should not be reduced to a sole-authored text. [Accademia della Crusca archive](https://futurismo.accademiadellacrusca.org/manifesti.asp)
- **1938:** *Manifesto del teatro futurista aeroradiotelevisivo*. Late theatrical-media project proposing a synthesis of aviation, radio, television, and performance; co-authorship and title forms vary among bibliographies. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/filippo-tommaso-marinetti_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1939:** *Manifesto del romanzo sintetico*. Co-authored with Piero Bellanova and Fernando Cervelli Scrivo; a protocol for compressed narrative, dated 25 December 1939 in the scholarly biography. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/filippo-tommaso-marinetti_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1944; unpublished until 1981:** *Manifesto futurista della Patriarte*. Final manifesto, co-authored with Giovanni Acquaviva; its 11 February 1944 drafting and much later publication make it simultaneously a lifetime project and posthumous manifestation. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)

### Essays, inquiries, criticism, reportage, and programs

- **1903:** *D’Annunzio intime*. French critical essay on Gabriele D’Annunzio; a later Italian publication is a translation/posthumous edition, not a new 2020 work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1908:** *Les dieux s’en vont, D’Annunzio reste*. Polemical critical essay/book on D’Annunzio and contemporary culture. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1909:** *Enquête internationale sur le Vers libre*. International inquiry assembled from responses solicited by Marinetti; it is an editorial-social project and documentary corpus, not solely his authored essay. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1911:** *Le Futurisme*. French anthology of Marinetti’s polemical and programmatic texts; a self-curated presentation of the movement. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1912:** *La battaglia di Tripoli*. Collection of Italian newspaper dispatches from the Italo-Turkish War; reportage, eyewitness performance, and propaganda overlap. [Marinetti profile](https://ftmarinetti.it/poliedrico/)
- **1912:** *La Bataille de Tripoli*. French version of the Tripoli dispatches; a separate publication object, though textually dependent on the Italian reportage. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1913:** *Almanacco purgativo 1914*. Polemical almanac and collective Futurist publication; its printed date names the year of use, while it was issued in December 1913. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1914:** *I Manifesti del futurismo*. Collected manifestos by multiple Futurists; Marinetti shaped the movement and contributed texts, but did not author every item. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1917:** *Come si seducono le donne*. Provocative conduct essay/program, later expanded and retitled. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1917:** *Noi futuristi: teorie essenziali e chiarificazioni*. Self-anthology of Futurist theory and clarification. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1919:** *Manifesti del Futurismo*. Collective manifesto anthology; bibliographically distinct from the 1914 collection but not a newly authored unitary text. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1919:** *Democrazia futurista*. Political essay and program collecting Marinetti’s proposals for a Futurist democracy. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1920:** *Come si seducono le donne e si tradiscono gli uomini*. Expanded/reframed form of the 1917 conduct essay; materially a new book, partly a revision rather than an entirely new work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1922:** *Enrico Caviglia*. Biographical-political essay on the Italian general. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1924:** *Futurismo e Fascismo*. Anthology and political argument aligning—and distinguishing—Futurism and Fascism; it is a constructed dossier rather than one continuous essay. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1928:** *Il novissimo segretario galante*. Model-letter book and ironic social protocol; “letters” here are composed examples, not simply an archival correspondence. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1929:** *Marinetti e il futurismo*. Self-anthology of literary, political, and programmatic texts. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1929:** *Primo dizionario aereo italiano*. Aviation dictionary, generally associated with Marinetti and the aviator Fedele Azari; shortening it to *Primo dizionario aereo* can conceal its collaborative lexicographical character. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1930:** *Futurismo e novecentismo*. Polemical anthology defining Futurism against the Novecento movement. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1931:** *Il paesaggio e l’estetica futurista della macchina*. Aesthetic essay treating machinery as a producer of landscape and perception. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1932:** *La nuova architettura*. Edited collection of architectural essays and studies, not an architectural structure designed by Marinetti. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1932:** *La cucina futurista*. Book made with Fillìa from manifestos, polemics, menus, formulas, event reports, and recipes; it is simultaneously protocol, documentary archive, publicity campaign, and prospective Gesamtkunstwerk. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1933:** *Antonio Sant’Elia, architetto futurista*. Critical-biographical study and canonizing act for Sant’Elia. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1933:** *Il fascino dell’Egitto*. Collection of articles and travel-memory concerning Egypt, not a single freshly written travel narrative. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1937:** *La tecnica della nuova poesia*. Poetic theory and protocol explaining Marinetti’s later practice. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1937:** *Mario Carli*. Memorial anthology of writings concerning Carli; editorial commemoration rather than a conventional sole-authored monograph. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1941:** *Mario Menin, camicia nera futurista*. Commemorative political-biographical monograph. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1898 reports; published as a book in 1998 and again in 2024:** *Les émeutes milanaises de mai 1898 / I moti milanesi del 1898*. Early French reportage on the Milan disturbances; the Italian title denotes a later translation/editorial manifestation, not a second lifetime work. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 1990:** *Misurazioni*. Posthumous collection of literary criticism; as a volume it is an editorially assembled corpus, not a book Marinetti completed in 1990. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Written by 1944; published 2021:** *Necessità e bellezza della violenza*. Posthumously issued speech; its title and book form are editorial manifestations of a lifetime oratorical text. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)

### Translation and editorial transformations

- **1916:** *Mallarmé: Versi e prose*. Marinetti’s Italian translation/selection of Stéphane Mallarmé; the translated wording is his making, while the underlying works remain Mallarmé’s. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **1928:** *La Germania*. Italian translation of a work by Jacques Rivière; it is Marinetti’s linguistic artefact but not his originating subject or plot. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)

### Journals, organizations, campaigns, and collective manifestations

- **1894:** *Le Papyrus*. School literary journal founded and edited by the young Marinetti in Alexandria; a platform and collective publication rather than a single authored text. [Marinetti archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/)
- **1905–1909:** *Poesia*. International literary review founded, financed, and directed by Marinetti with Sem Benelli and Vitaliano Ponti involved at its origin; the periodical is an ongoing editorial obra whose contents have many authors. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/filippo-tommaso-marinetti_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/)
- **1909–1944:** *Futurism*. Movement, network, publishing apparatus, and continuing project founded and repeatedly remade by Marinetti; it resists treatment as his sole work because thousands of its manifestations were made by others. [Library of Congress guide](https://guides.loc.gov/futurist-collections/secondary-sources)
- **1910 onward:** *Serate futuriste*. Repeated collective performance-events combining speeches, manifestos, noise, poetry, theatre, provocation, and audience conflict; there is no single fixed text or complete stable list of performances. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/la-drammaturgia_%28Storia-della-civilt%C3%A0-europea-a-cura-di-Umberto-Eco%29/)
- **1918–1920:** *Partito Politico Futurista*. Political organization and social project initiated by Marinetti; it is something made, but neither a text nor solely an individual artwork. [Time](https://time.com/10217/the-guggenheim-goes-back-to-the-futurists/)
- **1921 onward:** *Tactile boards and tactile journeys*. Material realizations proposed for Tattilism; surviving examples and attributions are incomplete, so the project is more securely enumerable than a supposed definitive set of objects. [Museo Omero](https://museoomero.it/opere/manifesto-futurista-sul-tattilismo/)
- **1930–1932:** *Futurist cuisine banquets and “aerobanquets”*. Designed meals, environments, scents, sounds, tactile interruptions, publicity, and diner actions realized the culinary manifestos as collective manifestations; individual events varied and resist reduction to recipes or books. [Marinetti manifesto archive](https://ftmarinetti.it/futurismo/manifesti/)

### Diaries, archives, and works whose boundaries are editorial

- **1905–1944:** *Libroni*. Seven large scrapbooks assembled by Marinetti from clippings, photographs, correspondence, and Futurist ephemera; they are simultaneously archive, self-portrait, working apparatus, and unfinished lifetime Gesamtkunstwerk rather than conventional books. [Yale/Beinecke description noted by the Library of Congress guide](https://guides.loc.gov/futurist-collections/secondary-sources)
- **1915–1921; published 1987:** *Taccuini 1915–1921*. Wartime and postwar notebooks; the manuscripts are lifetime documents, while the named printed corpus is a posthumous scholarly construction. [Chronology](https://ftmarinetti.it/cronologia-delle-opere/)
- **Various dates:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters constitute actions, relationships, instructions, and movement administration rather than one bounded work; no complete authorial “book of letters” exists, and selected editions should not be mistaken for the whole. [MART Archivio del ’900](https://cim.mart.tn.it/cim/pages/documenti_r.jsp?lang=en&method=st&sid=2619)
