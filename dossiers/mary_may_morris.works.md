## Works by Mary "May" Morris

### Wallpaper and repeating-pattern designs

- **c. 1883:** *Honeysuckle*. A repeating wallpaper pattern for Morris & Co.; long attributed to William Morris, it is now generally attributed to May, although the surviving evidence warrants “probably” rather than absolute certainty. [[1]](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/SP99.13.2.RodgersCatalogue.pdf)
- **c. 1885:** *Horn Poppy*. A block-printed wallpaper pattern designed for Morris & Co.; surviving pattern books document several colourways. [[2]](https://www.williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/morris-co-wallpaper-pattern-book-2/)
- **c. 1886:** *Arcadia*. A block-printed wallpaper pattern and the last of the three wallpapers presently attributed to May Morris. [[2]](https://www.williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/morris-co-wallpaper-pattern-book-2/)

### Embroidery designs and embroidered objects

- **early 1880s:** *Westward Ho!*. A pictorial embroidery designed by May Morris and worked by Jane Morris; it is therefore a collaborative obra whose design and execution have different authors. [[3]](https://www.pascal-theatre.com/biographies/may-morris/)
- **c. 1885–1890:** *Flowerpot*. May adapted William Morris’s earlier design and embroidered surviving panels herself; other examples were sold as Morris & Co. kits and may have been worked by customers, so “the work” comprises both her individual embroideries and a reproducible protocol for making them. [[4]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/)
- **c. 1885–1890:** *Anemone*. A screen panel embroidered by May Morris from John Henry Dearle’s design; it is her workmanship, but not her pictorial design. [[5]](https://www.ashmolean.org/article/many-talents-of-may-morris-art-and-crafts-designer-and-embroidery-legend)
- **1880s–1890s:** *Circular floral embroidery design*. An untitled design of flowers and leaves arranged in a circle within a square, made for the Morris workshop; its generic catalogue description, rather than a fixed historical title, is retained here. [[4]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/)
- **c. 1890:** *Maids of Honour*. A square panel or cushion/fire-screen design, both designed and worked in surviving examples by May; because Morris & Co. also marketed it as a pattern, other manifestations were executed by different hands. [[6]](https://www.williammorrisgallery.co.uk/collection/explore-the-collection/)
- **c. 1890:** *Rose Wreath cushion cover*. May’s embroidery of a design by William Morris, made for Amy Carruthers Tozer; the object is hers as needlework but not as original pattern design. [[7]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object-type/textile/page/3/)
- **before 1890:** *Fruit Garden*. A pair of large embroidered portières designed by May and commissioned in a four-panel ensemble by Mary Monroe Longyear; the surviving execution belongs to the Morris & Co. workshop and cannot safely be assigned wholly to May’s hand. [[4]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/)
- **c. 1891:** *Embroidered hanging*. A hanging worked collaboratively by May Morris and Theodosia Mackay-Middlemore; the collaborative execution resists treatment as a wholly autograph object.
- **c. 1892:** *Flower Garden*. A pair of embroidered portières designed by May for the Longyear commission and executed through Morris & Co.; the design drawing survives separately from the finished Boston panels. [[4]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/)
- **early 1890s:** *Minstrel with Cymbals*. A figurative embroidery associated with May Morris and preserved by the William Morris Society; its imagery closely resembles another Morris-workshop figure, making workshop influence part of its identity. [[8]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/page/3/)
- **c. 1894:** *Autumn and Winter*. An embroidered silk-damask panel designed and probably worked by May; it forms one half of a seasonal pair rather than a fully independent decorative scheme. [[5]](https://www.ashmolean.org/article/many-talents-of-may-morris-art-and-crafts-designer-and-embroidery-legend)
- **1895–1900:** *Spring and Summer*. A large embroidered silk-damask panel designed and probably embroidered by May, paired with *Autumn and Winter*. [[9]](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2018WMSSpring.pdf)
- **1895–1900:** *Autumn and Winter*. The companion seasonal panel; sources differ slightly over whether to date it c. 1894 or 1895–1900 and over whether May’s execution is certain or merely probable. [[5]](https://www.ashmolean.org/article/many-talents-of-may-morris-art-and-crafts-designer-and-embroidery-legend) [[9]](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2018WMSSpring.pdf)
- **c. 1897:** *Cloak*. A garment designed and made by May, combining dress, embroidery, and personal artistic presentation rather than fitting neatly into “textile panel” or “fashion design.”
- **1899:** *Gauntlets*. Ceremonial gloves designed by Charles Ricketts and embroidered by May; they are a collaborative object in which her contribution is material execution rather than overall design.
- **1899–1900:** *Design for an ecclesiastical glove*. A working design for liturgical embroidery, preserved as a print-room object rather than known through a completed glove. [[10]](https://ashmolean.org/sitefiles/ashmolean-annual-review-2017-18)
- **c. 1900:** *Battye embroidered wall hanging*. A heraldic and naturalistic hanging designed by May for Frances Battye, then worked by Battye and her daughters Audrey and Norah; it is decisively a collaborative obra. [[11]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object-type/textile/)
- **early twentieth century:** *Bed hangings*. A set of bird-filled embroidered hangings designed and worked by May and exhibited in London and Detroit; the sources describe the ensemble but do not provide a secure fixed title or exact date. [[3]](https://www.pascal-theatre.com/biographies/may-morris/)
- **c. 1905:** *Dress*. A garment designed and probably embroidered by May; “probably” should be retained because the execution is not securely documented.
- **c. 1910s:** *Three embroidery patterns for a frame*. A watercolour sheet presenting three patterns later reproduced in the Morris & Co. embroidery catalogue; simultaneously design, sales instrument, and protocol for a customer-made object. [[4]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/)
- **c. 1919–1923:** *The River Thames*. An unfinished tapestry known from May’s loom and the 1939 Kelmscott Manor sale catalogue. Later removed from the loom, it survives principally as an interrupted project, and its incompletion is part of the work’s status. [[4]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/)
- **c. 1932:** *Embroidered work bag*. A wool-on-canvas bag bearing the initial “M,” designed and worked by May; it is a functional personal object as well as decorative needlework. [[12]](https://www.williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object/embroidered-work-bag/)
- **undated:** *Lotus*. A panel or curtain credited to May for both design and embroidery; reflected light from the silk is integral to the design, making it as much a material-light composition as a drawn pattern. [[9]](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2018WMSSpring.pdf)
- **late nineteenth century:** *Bedcover*. A silk embroidery attributed, rather than securely assigned, to May on stylistic and technical grounds; no corresponding design has been located. [[13]](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/744453)
- **late nineteenth century:** *Valance*. The companion to the attributed bedcover; both its design and embroidery are only possibly May’s, and the Metropolitan Museum explicitly treats the attribution as provisional. [[13]](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/744453)
- **c. 1890–1910:** *Squirrel*. A Morris & Co. fire-screen panel known from a unique surviving example; its design is attributed to May by stylistic comparison, not documentary proof, while its embroiderer is unidentified. [[11]](https://williammorrisgallery.co.uk/object-type/textile/)
- **1885–after 1918:** *Working designs for embroidery*. More than 300 surviving sketches, botanical studies, tracings, outline patterns, and finished designs at the Ashmolean alone—for hangings, portières, screens, cushions, frames, table covers, tea cosies, book covers, clothing, bags, sachets, monograms, and ecclesiastical objects. Most lack stable individual titles and some never became finished objects, so this corpus resists honest conversion into invented “works”; it is best understood as an open-ended design archive and body of projects. [[5]](https://www.ashmolean.org/article/many-talents-of-may-morris-art-and-crafts-designer-and-embroidery-legend)

### Jewellery and wearable metalwork

- **c. 1900–1910:** *Girdle*. A silver waist ornament designed and made by May; the title identifies an object type rather than a unique authorial title. [[14]](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O77405/girdle-morris-may/)
- **c. 1900–1910:** *Pendant*. A silver and stone pendant designed and made by May; surviving museum records connect her jewellery practice with the Gaskins’ Birmingham work. [[15]](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O77406/pendant-morris-may/)
- **c. 1900–1910:** *Earrings*. A pair of earrings by May, now in the V&A; “earrings” is a curatorial object description, not a documented historical title. [[16]](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O115638/earring-morris-may/)
- **1906:** *Girdle, pendant, and hatpins*. An exhibited jewellery ensemble; whether its components were conceived as one work or merely displayed together is not firmly established.
- **early twentieth century:** *Silver and chalcedony necklace*. A necklace attributed to May’s jewellery practice; its descriptive name should not be mistaken for an authorial title.
- **early twentieth century:** *Jewellery corpus*. Further necklaces, pendants, brooches, clasps, buckles, rings, hatpins, earrings, chains, and girdles survive in public and private collections. Many have no individual titles or secure dates, and some were assembled from reused stones or components; the corpus therefore resists a definitive item-by-item catalogue on presently published evidence. [[17]](https://museum.wales/articles/2020-03-02/Queering-the-collections-Taking-a-fresh-look-at-art-through-an-LGBTQ-lens-at-National-Museum-Cardiff/)

### Political, craft, and art-historical texts

- **1886:** *Persecuted for Opinion … Vindictive Sentence*. A political newspaper article; it acts as socialist advocacy and comes close to a manifesto rather than detached description. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1888:** *Chain Stitch Embroidery*. A technical and historical essay on embroidery. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1889:** *Of Embroidery*. An Arts and Crafts Exhibition Society catalogue essay articulating principles of design and workmanship. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1890:** *Of Materials*. A craft essay treating material choice as an element of artistic practice. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1890:** *Of Colours and Colouring*. A craft essay on colour in applied art. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1892:** *Embroidery*. A practical chapter in *Plain Handicrafts*; because it teaches method, it functions partly as a protocol. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Embroidery*. A revised or reprinted manifestation of the 1889 essay in *Arts and Crafts Essays*, not an entirely separate intellectual obra. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Materials*. A revised or reprinted manifestation of the 1890 essay. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Colour*. A revised or reprinted manifestation of *Of Colours and Colouring*. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Church Embroidery*. The opening instalment of a thirteen-part technical series in *The Building News and Engineering Journal*. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Church Embroidery—II: Appliqué*. A protocol-like essay on ecclesiastical appliqué. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Church Embroidery—III: Couching and Flat Gold Work*. A technical instalment on gold-thread methods. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Church Embroidery—IV: Couching and Raised Gold Work*. A technical continuation of the series. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Church Embroidery—V: Gold Work*. A technical essay on ecclesiastical gold embroidery. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1893:** *Of Church Embroidery—VI: Figure-Work*. The first of two instalments on embroidered figures. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—VII: Figure-Work*. The second instalment on figure-work. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—VIII: White Linen Work*. A technical essay on whitework. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—IX: Burses, Shields, &c.*. A technical essay on particular ecclesiastical objects. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—X: Design*. A prescriptive essay on designing church embroidery. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—XI: Animals and Symbols*. A technical and iconographic essay. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—XII: Design*. A further instalment on design. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1894:** *Of Church Embroidery—XIII*. The final published instalment; its source supplies no more specific subtitle, which should not be invented. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1895:** *Mediaeval Embroidery*. A historical lecture published in the *Journal of the Society of Arts*. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1899:** *First Visions: Our Lady of Chartres*. An architectural and devotional travel essay. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1899:** *Coptic Textiles*. A textile-historical study. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1900:** *Decorative Needlework*. A short professional essay distinct from the 1893 book of the same title. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1900:** *Decorative Art 1800–1885*. A historical survey chapter in *Social England*. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1900:** *Village Building and the Wayfarer*. A preservationist lecture and published paper; as an intervention in building practice, it is partly manifesto. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1902:** *Pageantry and the Masque*. An essay derived from a lecture on communal and theatrical art. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1902:** *Line Embroidery*. A technical essay on linear stitch and design. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1905:** *Opus Anglicanum—The Syon Cope*. The first of three detailed studies of medieval English embroidery. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1905:** *Opus Anglicanum II—The Ascoli Cope*. The second cope study. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1905:** *Opus Anglicanum III—The Pienza Cope*. The third cope study. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1905:** *Opus Anglicanum at the Burlington Fine Arts Club*. An exhibition study extending the cope series. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1913:** *Embroidery*. A survey written for the British Arts and Crafts section of the Ghent International Exhibition catalogue. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1914:** *La Broderie*. A French-language exhibition-catalogue essay; it may be a translated or adapted manifestation of the 1913 survey rather than a wholly independent text. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1917:** *William de Morgan: Recollections*. A memoir of the artist and designer. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1917:** *William de Morgan’s Art*. A critical study complementary to, but distinct from, the recollections. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1919:** *Weaving and Textile Crafts*. A reconstruction-era essay connecting craft instruction with social rebuilding. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1921:** *Preface to William Morris and the Early Days of the Socialist Movement*. A framing text for John Bruce Glasier’s book and an intervention in the historical memory of British socialism. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1934:** *Reminiscences About My Father*. A biographical memoir published in a William Morris memorial issue of *Philobiblon*. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1934:** *William Morris*. An article in the *Times Literary Supplement*; a different text from her 1919 article of the same title. [[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Morris#Publications)

### Books, plays, and lectures

- **1893:** *Decorative Needlework*. An illustrated instructional book: simultaneously a treatise on design and a protocol for embroidery practice. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1898:** *Lady Griselda’s Dream*. A play published in *Longman’s Magazine*; its inclusion by the archive under both articles and plays reflects its double identity as magazine text and dramatic work. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1903:** *White Lies*. A separately printed play issued by the Chiswick Press. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **c. 1909:** *Jewellery*. A lecture surviving as a transcript in the William Morris Gallery; the performance and the transcript are related manifestations, not quite the same thing. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **c. 1909:** *Opus Anglicanum*. A lecture transcript on medieval English embroidery; its relationship to the published 1905 essays is not fully specified by the catalogue. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **c. 1909:** *Pattern-designing*. A lecture surviving in transcript and functioning in part as design instruction. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)
- **1919:** *William Morris*. An article in the *Times Literary Supplement*, distinct from the later article bearing the same title. [[19]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Morris#Publications)
- **1936:** *William Morris: Artist, Writer, Socialist*. A two-volume biographical, critical, documentary, and political work assembled by May; its mixed form makes it closer to an archival obra than a conventional single-author biography. [[18]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/writings/)

### Collective and social projects

- **1885–1896:** *Morris & Co. embroidery department under May Morris*. A managed body of designs, commissions, kits, and workshop-made embroidery rather than a single autograph object. Because May directed designers and embroiderers and the firm often suppressed individual credit, this collective production resists division into “works by May” and “works merely supervised by May.” [[21]](https://maymorrisarchive.wordpress.com/)
- **1907:** *Women’s Guild of Arts*. An institution co-founded with Mary Elizabeth Turner and other women art workers. It is a collective social project—created to provide the professional association denied by the male-only Art Workers’ Guild—rather than a conventional artwork. [[22]](https://morrissociety.org/wp-content/uploads/25.3Grant.pdf)
- **1909–1910:** *United States lecture tour*. A sustained sequence of lectures and demonstrations advocating Arts and Crafts principles. Individual performances were ephemeral, and only some texts survive; the tour is consequently better classified as a pedagogical and political project than as a set of fully recoverable texts. [[23]](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/tsaconf/748/)
- **after 1896–1938:** *Kelmscott preservation and building projects*. May commissioned and shaped buildings at Kelmscott and worked to preserve the village and Kelmscott Manor. These are patronal, architectural, and conservation projects rather than buildings designed solely by her, so sole authorship should not be claimed.
