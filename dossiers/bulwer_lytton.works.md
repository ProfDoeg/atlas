## Works by Edward Bulwer-Lytton

### Novels, romances, and shorter fiction

- **1827:** *Falkland*. A novel, published anonymously; Bulwer-Lytton later substantially revised it.
- **1828:** *Pelham; or, The Adventures of a Gentleman*. A fashionable-life novel and satirical Bildungsroman.
- **1829:** *The Disowned*. A novel combining fashionable, criminal, and philosophical fiction.
- **1829:** *Devereux*. A historical novel set in the reign of Queen Anne.
- **1830:** *Paul Clifford*. A novel of crime and social criticism, commonly classed among the “Newgate novels.”
- **1832:** *Eugene Aram*. A historical crime novel based on the eighteenth-century murderer and scholar Eugene Aram.
- **1833:** *Godolphin*. A society novel with philosophical and occult elements.
- **1833:** *Asmodeus at Large*. A satirical prose fantasy published under Bulwer-Lytton’s name in an American volume; it is omitted from the *Dictionary of National Biography*’s numbered bibliography, so its precise bibliographical status within his canon requires caution.
- **1834:** *The Pilgrims of the Rhine*. An episodic romance incorporating legends and tales within a framing journey.
- **1834:** *The Last Days of Pompeii*. A historical novel set immediately before the eruption of Vesuvius in 79 CE.
- **1835:** *Rienzi, the Last of the Roman Tribunes*. A historical novel about Cola di Rienzo.
- **1837:** *Ernest Maltravers*. A novel later designated the first part of the two-part sequence *The Eleusinia*.
- **1838:** *Alice; or, The Mysteries*. A novel continuing *Ernest Maltravers* and later designated the second part of *The Eleusinia*.
- **1838:** *Leila; or, The Siege of Granada*. A historical romance set during the final Christian conquest of Granada.
- **1838:** *Calderon the Courtier*. A historical romance frequently issued with *Leila* but bibliographically a distinct text.
- **1841:** *Night and Morning*. A novel of inheritance, concealed identity, crime, and social mobility.
- **1841:** *Zicci*. A short occult romance serialized in the *Monthly Chronicle*; it is an earlier manifestation of the narrative project that became *Zanoni*, not merely another edition of that novel.
- **1842:** *Zanoni*. An occult and philosophical novel extensively developed from *Zicci*.
- **1843:** *The Last of the Barons*. A historical novel about Warwick the Kingmaker and the Wars of the Roses.
- **1846:** *Lucretia; or, The Children of Night*. A crime novel published anonymously and partly inspired by the murderer Thomas Griffiths Wainewright.
- **1848:** *Harold, the Last of the Saxon Kings*. A historical novel concerning Harold Godwinson and the Norman Conquest.
- **1849–1850:** *The Caxtons: A Family Picture*. A family novel first serialized anonymously in *Blackwood’s Magazine* in 1848–1849; reference works differ between its serial, volume, and collected-edition dates.
- **1851–1853:** *My Novel; or, Varieties in English Life*. A panoramic social novel narrated under the persona Pisistratus Caxton, first serialized in *Blackwood’s Magazine* before book publication.
- **1857–1859:** *What Will He Do with It?* A long social novel, again associated with the Pisistratus Caxton persona; bibliographies variously date it from the beginning of serialization, 1858, or its 1859 volume publication.
- **1859:** *The Haunted and the Haunters; or, The House and the Brain*. A supernatural novella or long story first published in *Blackwood’s Edinburgh Magazine*. Some secondary lists incorrectly give 1857, and later abridged forms circulated under *The House and the Brain*.
- **1861–1862:** *A Strange Story*. An occult novel first serialized anonymously in *All the Year Round* and then issued in volumes; the serial and book manifestations account for the two dates.
- **1871:** *The Coming Race*. An anonymously published speculative novel—later frequently retitled *Vril: The Power of the Coming Race*—depicting a technologically advanced subterranean society.
- **1873:** *Kenelm Chillingly: His Adventures and Opinions*. A philosophical novel published in the year of Bulwer-Lytton’s death.
- **1872–1873:** *The Parisians*. A social and political novel serialized in *Blackwood’s Magazine* and published in volume form after Bulwer-Lytton’s death; the posthumous form was completed editorially from the text he left.
- **1876:** *Pausanias the Spartan*. An unfinished historical romance published posthumously under the editorship of Bulwer-Lytton’s son. As an incomplete project given a later editorial manifestation, it resists treatment as a fully finished novel.
- **Unfinished; published 1883:** *Greville; or, A Man of the World*. The beginning of an unfinished novel preserved in *The Life, Letters and Literary Remains of Edward Bulwer, Lord Lytton*; it is a surviving project rather than a completed obra.

### Poetry and poetic collections

- **1820:** *Ismael: An Oriental Tale, with Other Poems*. Bulwer-Lytton’s first published poetry collection.
- **1823:** *Delmour; or, A Tale of a Sylphid, and Other Poems*. An anonymously published collection centred on a narrative poem.
- **1825:** *Sculpture*. A prize poem awarded Cambridge’s Chancellor’s Gold Medal.
- **1825:** *Weeds and Wild Flowers*. A privately printed collection consisting chiefly of poems; some later accounts date it 1826.
- **1827:** *O’Neil; or, The Rebel*. A narrative poem.
- **1831:** *The Siamese Twins*. A satirical poem, published together with a poem on Milton; the former was not reprinted, while the latter reappeared in altered form.
- **1831:** *Collected Poems*. A collection and revision of Bulwer-Lytton’s early verse rather than a single newly composed poem.
- **1842:** *Eva: A True Story of Light and Darkness; The Ill-Omened Marriage; and Other Tales and Poems*. A hybrid collection of verse and prose narratives; its mixed media make “poetry book” an incomplete description.
- **1845–1847:** *The New Timon: A Romance of London*. A satirical verse narrative first published anonymously and in an incomplete form in 1845, then completed and revised in 1847.
- **1848–1849:** *King Arthur*. A book-length epic poem; the date range reflects its successive publication and revision.
- **1860:** *St. Stephen’s: A Poem*. A political and historical poem originally published in *Blackwood’s Magazine*.
- **1864:** *The Boatman*. A poem published under the persona “Pisistratus Caxton” and subsequently reprinted separately.
- **1866:** *The Lost Tales of Miletus*. A collection of narrative poems fashioned as reconstructed or invented classical tales.

### Plays and theatrical projects

- **1836–1837:** *The Duchess de la Vallière*. A historical drama; the *Dictionary of National Biography* dates it 1836, while later work-lists commonly use its 1837 publication or production date.
- **1837–1839:** *The Sea-Captain; or, The Birthright*. A drama performed and published anonymously. Sources disagree between 1837 and 1839, apparently reflecting composition, staging, and publication.
- **1838:** *The Lady of Lyons; or, Love and Pride*. A five-act romantic drama written for theatrical performance.
- **1838–1839:** *Richelieu; or, The Conspiracy*. A five-act historical drama in verse; the *Dictionary of National Biography* gives 1838, while the standard published manifestation is dated 1839.
- **1840:** *Money*. A prose comedy constructed for the commercial stage.
- **1851:** *Not So Bad as We Seem; or, Many Sides to a Character*. A comedy written for Dickens’s amateur company in support of the Guild of Literature and Art; it therefore functioned both as drama and as a philanthropic project.
- **1868:** *The Rightful Heir*. A five-act drama rewritten from *The Sea-Captain* and staged at the Lyceum Theatre. It is a new theatrical manifestation of that earlier project, though some bibliographies subsume it under *The Sea-Captain*.
- **1869:** *Walpole; or, Every Man Has His Price*. A historical comedy written in rhymed verse.
- **Unfinished; staged posthumously 1877:** *The House of Darnley* (also called *Darnley*). An unfinished and probably unrevised drama subsequently prepared for performance; the surviving object is partly Bulwer-Lytton’s project and partly a posthumous theatrical realization.
- **Undated and unpublished:** *Several unnamed dramas*. The contemporary biographical record reports multiple unpublished dramatic manuscripts but does not supply stable titles or dates. They resist item-by-item treatment as named works because the record describes an archival remainder rather than a fixed corpus of completed plays.

### Essays, criticism, history, memoir, and collected prose

- **1833:** *England and the English*. A two-volume survey of contemporary English politics, institutions, society, manners, religion, education, science, literature, and art.
- **1835:** *The Student*. A collection of essays substantially drawn from Bulwer-Lytton’s contributions to the *New Monthly Magazine*.
- **1837:** *Athens: Its Rise and Fall, with Views of the Literature, Philosophy, and Social Life of the Athenian People*. A two-volume cultural and political history. It remained an incomplete historical project: a further volume was left unpublished.
- **1845:** *Confessions of a Water-Patient*. An autobiographical and polemical account of hydropathic treatment, poised between memoir, medical testimony, and promotional argument.
- **1863–1864:** *Caxtoniana: A Series of Essays on Life, Literature, and Manners*. An essay sequence voiced through the Caxton literary persona; its initial periodical and subsequent book manifestations produce the differing dates.
- **1868:** *Miscellaneous Prose Works*. A three-volume authorial corpus or collected manifestation, not a single newly made essay.
- **Unfinished and undated:** *A further volume of the History of Athens and other historical fragments*. These manuscript projects were reported after Bulwer-Lytton’s death but were neither completed nor stabilized as publications.
- **Various dates:** *Unfinished novels, plays, poems, and essays*. Bulwer-Lytton’s son described an “immense number” of such remains. This is not one work and cannot honestly be converted into a definitive titled list; its resistance to enumeration is part of the surviving evidence.

### Manifestos, pamphlets, lectures, and public addresses

- **1834:** *A Letter to a Late Cabinet Minister on the Present Crisis*. A political pamphlet addressed to an unnamed former minister; it acts as an intervention in a contemporary constitutional crisis rather than as detached description.
- **1847:** *A Word to the Public*. A defensive public statement concerning Bulwer-Lytton’s crime fiction and its critics; in the vocabulary of this atlas it functions as a manifesto or act of literary self-defence.
- **1851:** *A Letter to John Bull, Esq.* A political pamphlet framed as an address to the personified British public.
- **1852:** *Outlines of the Early History of the East*. A published lecture: simultaneously a performed address and a textual manifestation.
- **1854:** *Inaugural Address at Edinburgh*. A public address produced for Bulwer-Lytton’s installation as lord rector of the University of Edinburgh.
- **1874:** *Speeches of Edward Lord Lytton, with Some of His Political Writings Hitherto Unpublished*. A posthumous two-volume collection edited and prefaced by his son. The individual speeches are Bulwer-Lytton’s acts of political oratory, but the collection as a made bibliographical object is substantially the son’s editorial manifestation.

### Translations

- **1844:** *The Poems and Ballads of Schiller*. A collection of Bulwer-Lytton’s English translations, accompanied by a biographical sketch of Schiller; it is a translational creation rather than an original poetic corpus.
- **1869:** *The Odes and Epodes of Horace*. Bulwer-Lytton’s verse translation of Horace, making an English poetic object from the Latin works.

### Editorial and periodical projects

- **1831–1832:** *The New Monthly Magazine* editorship. An editorial project rather than a single authored text: Bulwer-Lytton shaped a periodical containing many writers’ work and also contributed material to it.
- **1840–1841:** *The Monthly Chronicle* project. Bulwer-Lytton founded and helped direct this literary and semi-scientific periodical. The magazine is a collaborative creation, not wholly his obra, although his editorial intention and contributions belong in an account of what he made.
- **1840 onward:** *Collected editions of the novels*. Bulwer-Lytton repeatedly revised his fiction for collective editions, including the 1840 collection, Routledge’s cheaper series from 1853, and Blackwood’s forty-three-volume library edition of 1859–1863. These are changing authorized manifestations rather than dozens of independent works.
