## Works by Nicholas of Cusa

### Philosophical and theological obras

- **1440:** *De docta ignorantia*. A three-book speculative treatise on the limits of knowledge, mathematical theology, the universe, and Christ; its letter of dedication functions as part of the completed obra. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1441–1442:** *De coniecturis*. A two-book philosophical treatise presenting human knowledge as conjectural and developing a symbolic system of unity, difference, and number. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **Early 1445:** *Dialogus de Deo abscondito*. A short theological dialogue, conventionally also titled *De Deo abscondito*, between a Christian and a pagan concerning the hidden God. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))
- **January–March 1445:** *De quaerendo Deum*. A five-chapter theological treatise or spiritual protocol for seeking God through signs, especially the divine name and light. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **July 1445:** *De filiatione Dei*. A theological treatise on intellectual adoption or “divine sonship,” addressed to Conrad of Wartberg. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))
- **July 1445:** *De dato patris luminum*. A theological-philosophical treatise on every gift’s descent from the Father of lights and the return of created intellect to its source. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1446:** *Coniectura de ultimis diebus*. An eschatological conjecture calculating—but expressly not claiming certain knowledge of—the world’s final period. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))
- **March 1447:** *Dialogus de genesi*. A philosophical-theological dialogue on Genesis and the origin of all things; the critical edition’s division into chapters is partly editorial. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **October 1449:** *Apologia doctae ignorantiae*. A polemical defense of *De docta ignorantia* against Johannes Wenck: not merely description but an intervention intended to vindicate a contested theological method. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **July–September 1450:** *Idiota de sapientia*. A two-part dialogue in which a layman instructs an orator about wisdom; it is one component of the larger *Idiota* dialogue project. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1450:** *Idiota de mente*. A dialogue among a layman, philosopher, and orator about mind, measure, number, and knowledge; it is the longest component of the *Idiota* project. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **September 1450:** *Idiota de staticis experimentis*. An experimental dialogue proposing knowledge through weighing and measurement—part philosophical dialogue, part research protocol—and the final component of the *Idiota* project. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **September 1453:** *De theologicis complementis* (*Complementum theologicum*). A theological treatise that converts geometrical procedures into speculative figures for divine knowledge; its singular and plural titles both circulate in the manuscript and editorial traditions. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))
- **September 1453:** *De pace fidei*. A visionary dialogue, prompted by news of Constantinople’s fall, staging a heavenly council in search of religious peace: simultaneously theological obra and projected intervention in interreligious conflict. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **November 1453:** *De visione Dei*. A contemplative treatise sent with an all-seeing painted icon to the monks of Tegernsee; text, image, placement, and bodily movement form an experiential protocol approaching a small Gesamtkunstwerk. The painting supplied with it is not securely identifiable among surviving objects. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1458:** *De beryllo*. A philosophical treatise offering the “beryl” as an intellectual lens through which coincidence and relationality may be contemplated. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **June 1459:** *De aequalitate*. A speculative theological text on equality, surviving in a form whose incompleteness and organization have required editorial reconstruction. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))
- **June 1459:** *De principio* (*Tu quis es?*). A dialogue or discourse on the Gospel question “Who are you?” and on the divine principle; although transmitted among sermon-like materials, scholarship classifies it as a discourse rather than an ordinary sermon. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **February 1460:** *Trialogus de possest*. A three-speaker philosophical-theological dialogue whose coined name *possest*—“is-able” or actualized possibility—acts as a divine designation. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **1460–1461:** *Cribratio Alkorani*. A three-book Christian “sifting” of the Qur’an, both theological treatise and missionary-apologetic project; its categories and aims are those of fifteenth-century Christian polemic rather than neutral comparative religion. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1461–1462:** *Directio speculantis seu De li non aliud* (*De non aliud*). A four-speaker dialogue and contemplative protocol organized around “not-other” as a divine name and definition. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **Late 1462:** *De venatione sapientiae*. A systematic “hunt for wisdom” through ten conceptual fields, gathering and revising motifs from Nicholas’s earlier work. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1462–1463:** *Dialogus de ludo globi*. A two-book dialogue built around an actual ball game devised by Nicholas; the game, its eccentric ball, diagrammed court, and interpretive dialogue together resist separation into “text” and “object.” ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))
- **December 1463:** *Compendium*. A concise philosophical-theological synthesis, organized as thirteen chapters, conclusion, and epilogue. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **April 1464:** *De apice theoriae*. Nicholas’s final speculative dialogue, accompanied by the *Memoriale apicis theoriae*, a compressed mnemonic table or protocol rather than an independent book. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))

### Mathematical obras and experimental projects

- **September 1445:** *De geometricis transmutationibus*. Nicholas’s first mathematical treatise, dedicated to Paolo dal Pozzo Toscanelli and concerned with transforming rectilinear and curvilinear figures. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **September 1445:** *De arithmeticis complementis*. A companion mathematical treatise that approaches geometrical transformations through numerical calculation. ([Edition Open Sources](https://eos.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/sources/13/toc.html))
- **July 1450:** *De circuli quadratura*. A treatise proposing an approximate construction for squaring the circle; its mathematical result is unsuccessful, but the approximation project is the obra. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **December 1450:** *Quadratura circuli*. A distinct, later attempt at circle-squaring, not merely another title for the July treatise. ([Edition Open Sources](https://www.edition-open-sources.org/sources/13/index.html))
- **September–November 1453:** *De mathematicis complementis*. A two-part mathematical treatise addressed to Pope Nicholas V; both a shorter and an expanded textual form circulated. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1454:** *Declaratio rectilineationis curvae*. A clarification or corrective supplement to the first method in the second book of *De mathematicis complementis*, and therefore a dependent mathematical intervention rather than a wholly autonomous book. ([Edition Open Sources](https://eos.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/sources/13/toc.html))
- **1454–1457:** *De una recti curvique mensura*. A mathematical treatise seeking a single measure for straight and curved magnitudes. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1457:** *Dialogus de circuli quadratura*. A mathematical dialogue between Nicholas and the Florentine physician Toscanelli, presenting another stage of the circle-squaring project. ([Edition Open Sources](https://eos.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/sources/13/toc.html))
- **1457:** *De sinibus et chordis*. A short mathematical piece on sines and chords, transmitted as sections 9–11 of *Quadratura circuli* in the critical tradition; its resistance to treatment as an independent work is bibliographically significant. ([Cusanus Portal, Basel-edition contents](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/basler.php))
- **August 1457:** *De caesarea circuli quadratura*. A mathematical treatise addressed to Emperor Frederick III, again revising Nicholas’s attempted quadrature of the circle. ([Edition Open Sources](https://www.edition-open-sources.org/sources/13/index.html))
- **October 1458:** *De mathematica perfectione*. A late mathematical treatise prepared in both a longer and a shorter form; these are authorial versions of one project rather than securely separate obras. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **August 1459:** *Aurea propositio in mathematicis* (*De mathematica aurea propositione*). A brief “golden proposition” and Nicholas’s final independent mathematical text. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))

### Conciliar, ecclesiological, and reform interventions

- **c. 1432:** *De usu communionis*. A conciliar treatise on communion under one or both kinds in response to the Bohemian controversy; it was designed to act within negotiation, not simply to describe doctrine. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **c. 1432:** *Utrum auctoritas sacrorum conciliorum sit maior quam papae*. A juridical determination asking whether a general council’s authority exceeds the pope’s; its title also appears in expanded or normalized forms. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **1433:** *De concordantia catholica*. A three-book ecclesiological and political treatise composed for the Council of Basel, proposing concord through consent and representation; it is also a constitutional-reform project. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **c. 1433:** *Opusculum contra errorem Bohemorum*. A polemical memorandum concerning Hussite or Bohemian teaching, closely embedded in conciliar business and not always treated as a freestanding literary work. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* series contents](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA00867679?l=en))
- **1434:** *De auctoritate praesidendi in concilio generali*. A juridical-political treatise on the authority to preside over a general council. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **1436–1437:** *De correctione kalendarii* (*Reparatio kalendarii*). A proposal and operational protocol for reforming the ecclesiastical calendar, probably prepared for Basel’s calendar commission; scholarship places its formulation around 1436, with the conciliar report following in 1437. ([Cusanus Portal, Basel-edition contents](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/basler.php), [calendar-reform study](https://www.persee.fr/doc/sohim_0398-3811_2016_edc_42_1))
- **February–May 1441:** *Dialogus concludens Amedistarum errorem ex gestis et doctrina concilii Basiliensis*. A polemical dialogue meant to refute the adherents of antipope Felix V; it acts as a partisan conciliar manifesto. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1442:** *Oratio coram Dieta Francofordiensi*. A speech delivered or prepared for the Frankfurt diet in defense of the papal cause; as an oration, its primary existence is an intervention in an event rather than a “book.” ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **c. 1442:** *Quod recedere de neutralitate seu tralitate sit necessarium*. A political memorandum arguing that the German princes must abandon neutrality in the papal-conciliar conflict. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **c. 1442:** *Contra suspensionem animorum*. A closely related polemical memorandum against continued political indecision; some modern listings bind it bibliographically to *Quod recedere de neutralitate*. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **16 June 1446:** *De sacramento*. A short sacramental or pastoral text preserved among Nicholas’s minor ecclesiastical writings; it resists the scale and independence implied by “book.” ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **2 July 1446:** *De visitatione*. A visitation or devotional discourse, called *De annunciatione* in the early Basel edition; the unstable title reflects its transmission rather than two securely distinct works. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php), [Basel-edition contents](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/basler.php))
- **1452:** *Epistola ad Bohemos*. An open ecclesiastical letter addressing the Bohemian religious dispute; the critical corpus also preserves associated Bohemian letters, counsel, and an *Intentio* whose authenticity is marked doubtful. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* series contents](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA00867679?l=en))
- **July 1459:** *Reformatio generalis*. A draft program for comprehensive reform of the Roman Church, composed while Nicholas governed Rome as papal legate; unfinished as legislation, but complete as a reform project and manifesto. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))

### Letters, responses, and learned working texts

- **1428:** *Excerpts from works of Ramon Llull*. A working compilation made in Paris and preserved in Codex Cusanus 83; it is neither an authored book nor merely passive copying, but an intellectual tool assembled through selection and transcription. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **1442:** *Epistola ad Rodericum Sancium de Arevalo*. A substantial letter explaining the Church through the conceptual language of learned ignorance; its scale and argument make it letter and treatise at once. ([American Cusanus Society chronology](https://www.americancusanussociety.org/haverford-cusanus-chronology))
- **1444–1446:** *Responsio de intellectu Evangelii Ioannis* (*Quomodo ratio divina sit vita*). A short exegetical response on the opening of John’s Gospel; its paired titles describe the same textual object. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **29 December 1454:** *Epistola ad Ioannem de Segobia*. A letter to Juan de Segovia discussing peaceful engagement with Islam; in the critical edition it accompanies *De pace fidei*, but remains a distinct epistolary intervention. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **11 June 1463:** *Epistola ad Nicolaum Bononiensem (Albergati)*. A late philosophical letter to Niccolò Albergati; it survives as a named epistolary work rather than a formal treatise. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))
- **Throughout his working life:** *Marginalia, excerpts, manuscript corrections, and notes*. These marks survive across Nicholas’s manuscript collection and document acts of reading, compilation, and revision; they form a dispersed body of work that resists enumeration as separately titled obras. ([Cusanus-Stift manuscript collection](https://cusanus.de/handschriften/))

### Sermons as performances and texts

- **1430–1463:** *Sermones I–CCXCIII*. The Heidelberg critical edition establishes 293 numbered sermon-texts across volumes XVI–XIX, while the Cusanus Portal’s biographical chronology says that 294 sermons are transmitted; the numerical discrepancy should not be silently resolved. Each sermon was first an event or preaching project and often survives as notes, drafts, or multiple textual states, so the corpus is not simply a collection of 293 finished “books.” ([Meiner, *Opera omnia* sermons](https://meiner.de/programm/editionen-werkausgaben/nikolaus-von-kues-opera-omnia), [Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php), [Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php))

### Institutional, material, and spatial projects

- **1458 onward:** *St.-Nikolaus-Hospital (Cusanusstift), Kues*. A charitable foundation for thirty-three elderly men, established through a foundation charter and endowed with Nicholas’s books; the surviving institution is an obra made through law, property, architecture, collection, and continuing social practice, not a building authored by Nicholas alone. ([Cusanus Portal chronology](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/lw1.php), [Cusanusstift manuscripts](https://cusanus.de/handschriften/))
- **By 1464:** *Library at the St.-Nikolaus-Hospital*. Nicholas assembled and bequeathed a manuscript library spanning theology, philosophy, law, mathematics, astronomy, medicine, history, and geography. It is a curated body of work and intellectual infrastructure, although most individual codices were copied by other hands and therefore are not “works by Nicholas” in the ordinary authorship sense. ([Cusanusstift manuscript collection](https://cusanus.de/handschriften/))
- **1450s:** *The globe game and apparatus*. The eccentric ball and marked playing field interpreted in *De ludo globi* constituted a designed philosophical game: a material and performative protocol whose original physical equipment is not securely identified as surviving. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* portal](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/werke.php))

### Disputed or lost manifestations

- **Before 1464; surviving only through later derivatives:** *Map of Central Europe* (the “Cusanus map”). Later printed maps, most famously the Eichstätt engraving dated 1491, attribute or relate a lost cartographic prototype to Nicholas; because no securely identified autograph map survives and the derivative tradition is complex, this should be recorded as a disputed cartographic project rather than an extant work confidently authored by him. ([*History of Cartography*, “Cartography in the German Lands, 1450–1650”](https://press.uchicago.edu/sites/hoc/HOC_V3_Pt2/HOC_VOLUME3_Part2_chapter42.pdf))
- **Uncertain date and status:** *Correctio tabularum Alphonsi*. The early Basel edition prints this astronomical table-correction among Cusan material, but its precise authorship and independence are not securely established by the principal modern chronology; it therefore resists confident inclusion as an authored obra. ([Cusanus Portal, Basel-edition contents](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/basler.php))
- **Uncertain date and status:** *Stellae inerrantes ex cardinalis Cusani, Niceni et Alliaciensis observationibus supputatae*. A fixed-star computation printed from observations associated jointly with Cusa, “Nicenus,” and Pierre d’Ailly; collaborative data and later editorial mediation prevent its classification as Nicholas’s sole work. ([Cusanus Portal, Basel-edition contents](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/basler.php))
- **Uncertain date and status:** *Catalogus stellarum fixarum*. A fixed-star catalogue printed in the early Cusan corpus and derived from multiple observers; it is better treated as a collaborative or editorial manifestation than as a securely authored Cusan text. ([Cusanus Portal, Basel-edition contents](https://cusanus-portal.de/content/basler.php))
- **Date uncertain:** *Intentio*. A short text transmitted with the Bohemian writings and explicitly classified as an *opusculum dubium* in the modern *Opera omnia* volume; its attribution to Nicholas remains doubtful. ([Heidelberg *Opera omnia* series contents](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA00867679?l=en))
