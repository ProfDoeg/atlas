## Works by Claude des Armoises

### Letters and petitions known only through records

- **Before 9 August 1436:** Letter to the civic authorities of Orléans. The untitled letter is lost; its existence is documented by a municipal payment to the messenger who delivered letters from “Jehanne la Pucelle.” It belongs to Claude’s campaign to establish herself as the surviving Jeanne d’Arc, although only the delivery record—not the wording or physical manuscript—survives. ([Quicherat, vol. V, pp. 326–27](https://books.google.com/books?id=w7sX1Mk7xVwC); [Lecoy de La Marche, pp. 9–10 and note 15](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc))

- **Before 25 August 1436:** Second letter to the civic authorities of Orléans. Another municipal-account entry records a messenger bringing letters from “Jehanne la Pucelle”; the text is lost, so neither its exact date nor its contents can be recovered. ([Quicherat, vol. V, p. 326](https://books.google.com/books?id=w7sX1Mk7xVwC); [Lecoy de La Marche, note 15](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc))

- **August–9 September 1436:** Letter to Charles VII. Claude entrusted this lost letter to the pursuivant Cœur-de-Lis, who carried it to the king at Loches; it was a political petition or claim-making instrument, but its precise request is unknown. ([Quicherat, vol. V, p. 327](https://books.google.com/books?id=w7sX1Mk7xVwC); [Lecoy de La Marche, pp. 10–11 and note 21](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc))

- **September 1439:** Letter or supplication to Charles VII. A Tours account records closed letters sent by the bailli of Touraine concerning Claude together with letters written by Claude herself to the king; her communication is lost and has no recorded title. ([Quicherat, vol. V, p. 332](https://books.google.com/books?id=w7sX1Mk7xVwC); [Lecoy de La Marche, pp. 13–14 and note 32](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc))

- **1436, or an uncertain later date:** Letter to the king of Castile requesting naval assistance. The *Chronicle of the Constable Álvaro de Luna* reports that a letter from the “Maid of France” was preserved as a relic, but its attribution to Claude is doubtful: the associated account of recovering La Rochelle is chronologically and historically inconsistent, and Quicherat and Lecoy de La Marche considered that it might concern the historical Jeanne d’Arc, another place, or another date. The letter itself is not known to survive. ([Quicherat, vol. V, p. 329](https://books.google.com/books?id=w7sX1Mk7xVwC); [Lecoy de La Marche, pp. 15–16 and notes 37–39](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc))

- **Before February 1457:** Petition to René of Anjou for relief from banishment. René’s remission recites a “humble supplication” from a woman named in the record as Jeanne de Sermaises/Sarmoises, but whether this petitioner was Claude des Armoises is disputed: Lecoy de La Marche identified them, while subsequent scholarship has treated Jeanne de Sermaize as a potentially separate false Maid. This is therefore not a secure addition to Claude’s corpus, and the original petition is lost. ([Lecoy de La Marche, pp. 20–23 and note 50](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc); [Girault, “Les procès de Jeanne-Claude des Armoises,” pp. 197–210](https://books.openedition.org/puc/7827))

### Legal instrument

- **7 November 1436:** Untitled deed selling a quarter-share of the seigneury of Haraucourt. This is a jointly executed legal act of Robert des Armoises and his wife, styled “Jehanne du Lys, la Pucelle de France, dame de Thichiemont,” rather than an authored literary text; Claude’s contribution was juridical assent and sealing. It should not be confused with the reported marriage contract, whose survival and contents are much less certain. ([Quicherat, vol. V, p. 328](https://books.google.com/books?id=w7sX1Mk7xVwC); [Lecoy de La Marche, pp. 11–12 and notes 23–24](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Une_fausse_Jeanne_d%E2%80%99Arc))

### Embodied political project

- **20 May 1436–c. 1440:** Public manifestation as the surviving Jeanne d’Arc. This was not a fixed text or conventional obra but a sustained, mobile identity-performance made through speech, masculine military dress, travel, correspondence, courtly presentation, and the cooperation of Jeanne d’Arc’s brothers and other supporters. Its authorship resists singular attribution—the degree to which Claude devised the project herself or served a wider political intrigue remains disputed—and contemporary and modern sources identify it as an imposture rather than accepting her claimed identity. ([MéLoDi–Université de Lorraine, “1436 – La (fausse) Jeanne d’Arc est à la Grange-aux-Ormes”](https://melodi.univ-lorraine.fr/s/projet-cite-de-metz/item/1079); [Atten, “Jeanne-Claude des Armoises,” pp. 149–79](https://www.mgh-bibliothek.de/dokumente/a/a138397.pdf); [Girault, “Les procès de Jeanne-Claude des Armoises,” pp. 197–210](https://books.openedition.org/puc/7827))
