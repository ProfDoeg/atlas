## Works by Archimedes of Syracuse

### Surviving mathematical and scientific obras

- **Third century BCE; conjecturally earliest surviving work:** *On the Equilibrium of Planes, Book I*. A deductive treatise establishing the law of the lever and determining centers of gravity; its transmitted form may be an excerpt from a larger *Elements of Mechanics*, rather than an originally independent book. ([Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally second in sequence:** *Quadrature of the Parabola*. A geometrical and mechanical demonstration that a parabolic segment has a determinate area; “parabola” is a later element of the transmitted title, not certainly Archimedes’ own wording. ([Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally third in sequence:** *On the Equilibrium of Planes, Book II*. A treatise on centers of gravity, especially those of parabolic segments; it appears to have been written later than Book I and resists treatment as merely the second installment of a single composition. ([Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally fourth in sequence:** *The Method of Mechanical Theorems*. A letter-treatise to Eratosthenes explaining how mechanical balancing and indivisibles could discover geometrical results before rigorous proof; it survives incompletely and uniquely in the Archimedes Palimpsest. ([Archimedes Palimpsest](https://archimedespalimpsest.org/about/scholarship/scholarship_wilson1.php); [Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally fifth in sequence:** *On the Sphere and the Cylinder, Book I*. A geometrical treatise on spherical surfaces and volumes, transmitted as a letter to Dositheus and newly edited as a distinct book by Reviel Netz. ([Netz, Cambridge edition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/works-of-archimedes/on-the-sphere-and-the-cylinder-book-i/20EE24A2B6BA2FD4EE86661440E6A549))
- **Third century BCE; after Book I:** *On the Sphere and the Cylinder, Book II*. A second letter-treatise on dividing spheres and spherical segments; a promised construction was already absent from the textual tradition in antiquity, so the obra is not wholly preserved. ([Netz, Cambridge edition](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/the-works-of-archimedes/A8C5A23BE75917D8766B607783E93DE4); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally sixth in sequence:** *On Spirals*. A geometrical treatise defining and investigating the curve now called the Archimedean spiral, including its tangents and enclosed areas. ([Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf); [Heiberg corpus guide](https://math.nyu.edu/Archimedes/Books/ArchimedesInternet.html))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally seventh in sequence:** *On Conoids and Spheroids*. A treatise to Dositheus on volumes of solids and segments generated by conic sections. ([Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally eighth in sequence:** *On Floating Bodies, Books I–II*. A two-book hydrostatical obra defining equilibrium in fluids and analyzing floating solids; Book II is incomplete, and substantial Greek portions survive only through the Archimedes Palimpsest, alongside a medieval Latin version. ([Archimedes Palimpsest](https://archimedespalimpsest.org/about/scholarship/scholarship_wilson1.php); [Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally ninth in sequence:** *Measurement of a Circle*. A short, textually damaged geometrical treatise on circular area and the ratio of circumference to diameter; its three surviving propositions probably do not preserve the original arrangement, and the present second proposition may be a later addition. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes); [Heiberg corpus guide](https://math.nyu.edu/Archimedes/Books/ArchimedesInternet.html))
- **Third century BCE; conjecturally latest surviving major treatise:** *The Sand-Reckoner* (*Psammites*). A mathematical-cosmological obra addressed to King Gelon that creates a notation capable of naming extraordinarily large numbers and applies it to the number of grains required to fill the cosmos. ([Heath](https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/heath/HeathVolII.pdf); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; date and authorship not certain:** *The Cattle Problem*. A mathematical riddle in elegiac verse, apparently sent to Eratosthenes, coupling poetry with indeterminate arithmetic; positive proof of Archimedean authorship is lacking, although substantial scholarly arguments favor it. ([Netz, Acerbi, and Wilson](https://www.sciamvs.org/files/SCIAMVS_05_067-099_Netz_Acerbi_Wilson.pdf); [Heiberg corpus guide](https://math.nyu.edu/Archimedes/Books/ArchimedesInternet.html))
- **Third century BCE; fragmentary:** *Stomachion*. A mathematical investigation connected to a fourteen-piece dissection puzzle, surviving in Greek fragments in the palimpsest and in a differently selected Arabic fragment; whether its principal purpose was combinatorial enumeration remains a scholarly reconstruction. ([Netz, Acerbi, and Wilson](https://www.sciamvs.org/files/SCIAMVS_05_067-099_Netz_Acerbi_Wilson.pdf); [Archimedes Palimpsest](https://archimedespalimpsest.org/about/scholarship/scholarship_wilson1.php))

### Lost or indirectly attested projects

- **Third century BCE; lost:** *Principles* or *The Naming of Numbers*, addressed to Zeuxippus. This was apparently a numerical protocol or preliminary treatise underlying the notation used in *The Sand-Reckoner*; Archimedes mentions it, but its form and even the exact title remain uncertain. ([Netz, Acerbi, and Wilson](https://www.sciamvs.org/files/SCIAMVS_05_067-099_Netz_Acerbi_Wilson.pdf); [Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost and possibly identical with the preceding item:** *To Zeuxippus*. Archimedes refers to an earlier work bearing or described by this addressee’s name; nothing survives to establish whether it was an independent obra or simply another designation for his work on number names. ([Netz, Acerbi, and Wilson](https://www.sciamvs.org/files/SCIAMVS_05_067-099_Netz_Acerbi_Wilson.pdf))
- **Third century BCE; lost or absorbed into surviving texts:** *Elements of Mechanics*. Later testimony suggests a systematic mechanical work, possibly the larger source from which *On the Equilibrium of Planes, Book I* was excerpted; its independent existence and boundaries remain conjectural. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost:** *On Balances*. An indirectly attested mechanical treatise, perhaps concerning the theory or construction of balances; its relation to the equilibrium works is unknown. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost or not independently constituted:** *On Centers of Gravity*. References imply work on centers of gravity preceding or supplementing the surviving equilibrium books, but it may have been a section of another mechanical obra rather than a separate text. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; uncertain and lost:** *On Uprights*. A title or subject reconstructed from later testimony, possibly dealing with vertical supports or mechanical equilibrium; both its title and status as an independent work are doubtful. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost:** *On Sphere-Making*. A technical protocol on constructing celestial spheres or planetaria, attested by Pappus; its relationship to Archimedes’ physical astronomical instruments cannot now be determined. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost:** *On Semi-Regular Polyhedra*. A mathematical classification, reported by Pappus, of the thirteen solids now called Archimedean solids; the surviving report probably preserves only part of the treatise’s reasoning. ([Netz, Acerbi, and Wilson](https://www.sciamvs.org/files/SCIAMVS_05_067-099_Netz_Acerbi_Wilson.pdf))
- **Third century BCE; lost and title uncertain:** *On Blocks and Cylinders*. A possible geometrical or mechanical treatise known only through indirect evidence; the underlying words and the independence of the work are uncertain. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost:** *Catoptrics* or *On Optics*. An optical treatise cited by later writers, including for a proposition concerning refraction; its contents may have ranged beyond mirrors despite the conventional title. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; lost as a separate communication:** preliminary propositions on the spiral and other geometrical results sent to Conon. Archimedes’ surviving prefaces describe earlier communications, including deliberately false propositions intended to expose unsupported claims; these resist classification as formal books but were nevertheless authored mathematical acts. ([Heath](https://www.aproged.pt/biblioteca/worksofarchimede.pdf))

### Works surviving only under disputed attribution

- **Ancient core possible; transmitted form later:** *Book of Lemmas* or *Book of Assumptions* (*Liber assumptorum*). This Arabic collection of geometrical propositions cannot be Archimedes’ own text in its present form because it refers to him in the third person; it may preserve Archimedean material collected or rewritten by a later author. ([Heath](https://www.aproged.pt/biblioteca/worksofarchimede.pdf); [Cambridge description of Heiberg’s corpus](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811080/62565/frontmatter/9781108062565_frontmatter.pdf))
- **Date unknown; Arabic attribution:** *On Water Clocks*. A technical protocol surviving under Archimedes’ name in Arabic tradition, but not securely authenticated as his work. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Date unknown; Arabic attribution:** *On Touching Circles*. A geometrical work attributed to Archimedes in Arabic sources; no adequate evidence establishes that the transmitted work is his. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Date unknown; Arabic attribution:** *On Parallel Lines*. A geometrical text or project assigned to Archimedes by Arabic bibliographical tradition, with authorship unverified. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Date unknown; Arabic attribution:** *On Triangles*. A transmitted geometrical work bearing Archimedes’ name in Arabic sources; its actual author and original form are uncertain. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Date unknown; Arabic attribution:** *On the Properties of the Right Triangle*. A geometrical treatise preserved under Archimedean attribution in Arabic manuscripts, without secure evidence of authorship. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Date unknown; Arabic attribution:** *On Data*. A lost or indirectly transmitted geometrical work attributed to Archimedes; its title, contents, and authorship are not securely recoverable. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Date unknown; Arabic transmission:** *On the Division of the Circle into Seven Equal Parts*. A construction protocol attributed to Archimedes and transmitted in Arabic; the surviving evidence raises unresolved questions about its reliability and authorship. ([Heiberg corpus survey](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes); [modern textual study](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7390/11/3/747))

### Instruments, machines, and engineered manifestations

- **Before 212 BCE:** a moving celestial sphere or planetarium. Cicero reports an Archimedean instrument that represented the differing motions of the Sun, Moon, and five known planets and displayed eclipses; the physical object is lost, while both its gearing and its connection with *On Sphere-Making* remain reconstructive. ([Cicero, *De re publica* 1.21–22](https://www.lateinlex.de/?call=Puc&permalink=Cic_philos_rep_1_14); [Museo Galileo](https://brunelleschi.imss.fi.it/vitrum/evtr.asp?c=8253))
- **Before 212 BCE:** a solid celestial globe marked with stars and constellations. Cicero distinguishes this globe from the moving planetarium and says both were taken from Syracuse, but the extent of Archimedes’ authorship—construction, marking, adaptation, or possession—is not completely clear. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; attribution ancient but not contemporary:** the water-raising screw or “water snail.” Diodorus attributes its invention in Egypt to Archimedes, but the device’s earlier history and the lateness of the testimony prevent secure assignment of the invention to him. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; reported demonstration:** a compound-pulley ship-hauling apparatus. Plutarch says Archimedes used a system of pulleys to draw a heavily laden ship with little effort; this is a reported machine and performance, not a surviving object or securely datable design document. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **Third century BCE; traditionally associated with the ship *Syracusia*:** a screw mechanism used in launching or servicing a great ship. Athenaeus’ report associates an Archimedean endless screw with the vessel, but it is uncertain whether this was a distinct invention, an application of the water screw, or a later conflation. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
- **By the Roman siege of Syracuse, 214–212 BCE:** the integrated defensive machine system of Syracuse. Polybius credits Archimedes with designing and constructing an ensemble of long- and short-range artillery, pivoted beams dropping stones, concealed projectile devices, and crane-like grappling machines capable of lifting or destabilizing ships; the individual machines are not consistently named, so the obra is better treated as an engineered defensive project than as a catalogue of fixed inventions. ([Polybius, *Histories* 8](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0234%3Abook%3D8))
- **By the Roman siege of Syracuse, 214–212 BCE:** the ship-grappling and lifting machine later called the “Claw” or “Iron Hand.” Polybius describes multiple devices that hooked, raised, tilted, or dropped attacking ships, but the familiar singular “Claw of Archimedes” is a modern consolidation of those descriptions rather than an ancient fixed title. ([Polybius, *Histories* 8](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0234%3Abook%3D8))
- **Allegedly 214–212 BCE; attribution exceedingly doubtful:** burning mirrors used against Roman ships. The earliest detailed evidence is centuries later, while Polybius’ near-contemporary account of the defenses says nothing about mirrors; this is better classified as a legendary manifestation attached to Archimedes than as an established thing he made. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes); [history of the legend](https://cs.drexel.edu/~crorres/Archimedes/Mirrors/legend/legend.html))

### Posthumous project

- **Requested before 212 BCE; realized after his death if Cicero’s account is accepted:** the sphere-and-cylinder funerary emblem. Archimedes reportedly asked that a sphere within a cylinder be placed on his tomb to manifest the relation proved in his geometry; the surviving story concerns an authored commemorative intention, not an object made by his own hands, and Cicero later claimed to have identified its weathered remains. ([Dictionary of Scientific Biography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/science-and-technology/mathematics-biographies/archimedes))
