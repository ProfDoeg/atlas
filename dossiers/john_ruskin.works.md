## Works by John Ruskin

### Poems, tales, diaries, and autobiography

- **1829:** *The Puppet Show*. A juvenile play written for a toy theatre; it survives as an early manuscript rather than as a publication planned by its author. ([Library Edition, I](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1830:** *Iteriad; or, Three Weeks among the Lakes*. A juvenile verse travel journal, unpublished in Ruskin’s lifetime and therefore both diary and poem rather than a settled book. ([Library Edition, II](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1835–1846; collected 1850:** *Poems*. A body of separately composed and sometimes previously printed verse, not one continuously conceived work; Cook and Wedderburn make the corpus the second volume of the standard edition. ([Library Edition, II](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1839:** *Salsette and Elephanta*. Ruskin’s Newdigate Prize poem, a verse treatment of Indian landscape and monuments. ([Library Edition, II](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1841; published 1850:** *The King of the Golden River; or, The Black Brothers: A Legend of Stiria*. A literary fairy tale written for Euphemia Gray and published nine years later. ([Library Edition, I](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1827–1889:** *Diaries*. A continuing manuscript practice rather than a single finished obra; modern editors have divided and selectively published it under several titles, so its textual boundaries remain editorial. ([Library Edition, XXXVI–XXXVII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1885–1889:** *Præterita: Outlines of Scenes and Thoughts Perhaps Worthy of Memory in My Past Life*. An autobiography issued serially in parts and left structurally incomplete when illness stopped Ruskin’s work. ([Library Edition, XXXV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1886–1887; 1900:** *Dilecta: Correspondence, Diary Notes, and Extracts from Books, Illustrating “Præterita”*. A documentary supplement assembled from heterogeneous earlier materials; its last part appeared in 1900, and it resists treatment as an independently composed narrative. ([Library Edition, XXXV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Architecture, painting, and the criticism of seeing

- **1837–1838:** *The Poetry of Architecture; or, The Architecture of the Nations of Europe Considered in Its Association with Natural Scenery and National Character*. A serial architectural essay published as “Kata Phusin”; the authorized separate book did not appear until 1893. ([Library Edition, I](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1843–1860:** *Modern Painters*. A five-volume project joining art criticism, natural history, theology, aesthetics, drawing, and autobiography; its twenty-year expansion makes it closer to an intellectual Gesamtkunstwerk than to a single conventional treatise. ([Library Edition, III–VII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1849:** *The Seven Lamps of Architecture*. An architectural treatise organized around seven moral principles, with plates drawn and etched by Ruskin; text and image together form the obra. ([Library Edition, VIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1851:** *Examples of the Architecture of Venice*. An intended twelve-part measured visual survey of Venetian buildings; only three parts and sixteen plates appeared, so it is explicitly an unfinished atlas-project. ([Library Edition, XI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1851–1853:** *The Stones of Venice*. A three-volume architectural, historical, political, and graphic study—*The Foundations*, *The Sea-Stories*, and *The Fall*—whose argument depends upon Ruskin’s drawings and measured observations as well as prose. ([Library Edition, IX–XI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1851:** *Pre-Raphaelitism*. A polemical pamphlet defending contemporary artists through Ruskin’s theory of truthful observation. ([Library Edition, XII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1851 and 1854:** *Letters to The Times on the Pre-Raphaelite Artists*. Public interventions on behalf of Millais, Hunt, and their associates; these are letters acting upon an artistic controversy and thus function as manifestos, although Ruskin did not publish them under a collective title at first. ([Library Edition, XII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1851:** *Notes on the Construction of Sheepfolds*. An ecclesiological pamphlet about Church unity; its architectural title is metaphorical, and the text acts as a religious intervention rather than a building protocol. ([Library Edition, XII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1854:** *Giotto and His Works in Padua*. An explanatory text for the Arundel Society’s woodcut reproductions of the Arena Chapel frescoes; it is therefore part criticism, part guide, and part component of another institution’s reproductive project. ([Library Edition, XXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1854:** *Lectures on Architecture and Painting*. Four Edinburgh lectures of 1853 remade as an illustrated book. ([Library Edition, XII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1854:** *The Opening of the Crystal Palace, Considered in Some of Its Relations to the Prospects of Art*. A topical pamphlet intervening in public exhibition, education, and design. ([Library Edition, XII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1855–1859; 1875:** *Academy Notes*. Annual pamphlet-reviews of exhibited pictures, revived once in 1875; the title names a periodic critical practice rather than one continuous book. ([Library Edition, XIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1856:** *The Harbours of England*. Illustrative prose written around engravings by Thomas Lupton after Turner; authorship of the total object is collaborative, not Ruskin’s alone. ([Library Edition, XIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1857:** *Notes on the Turner Gallery at Marlborough House, 1856*. A catalogue-guide to a particular installation of Turner’s works, revised as the display and its title changed. ([Library Edition, XIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1857:** *Catalogue of the Sketches and Drawings by J. M. W. Turner, R.A., Exhibited in Marlborough House in the Year 1857–8*. A curatorial catalogue with interpretive notes; the arrangement of Turner’s works is itself part of Ruskin’s creation, though the catalogued objects are not his. ([Library Edition, XIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1857; retitled 1880:** *The Political Economy of Art / “A Joy for Ever” (and Its Price in the Market)*. Two lectures enlarged into a book and later substantially reframed under a new title; the two forms should not be mistaken for unrelated works. ([Library Edition, XVI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1859:** *The Two Paths*. Five lectures linking art, manufacture, decoration, and social organization. ([Library Edition, XVI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1864:** *The Cestus of Aglaia*. A serial sequence on art and social life, subsequently revised and absorbed into *On the Old Road*; its separate identity consequently remains unstable. ([Library Edition, XIX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1869:** *The Queen of the Air*. A study of Athena and Greek cloud-and-storm myths that combines mythology, natural observation, religion, and art criticism. ([Library Edition, XIX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1870:** *Lectures on Art*. Ruskin’s inaugural Oxford lectures, turning the spoken performance of his professorship into a book. ([Library Edition, XX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1872:** *Aratra Pentelici*. Six Oxford lectures on sculpture, delivered in 1870 and subsequently remade as an illustrated treatise. ([Library Edition, XX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1871; published 1898:** *Lectures on Landscape*. Oxford lectures withheld as a book for more than a quarter-century; the publication date does not date their composition or delivery. ([Library Edition, XXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1872:** *The Eagle’s Nest*. Ten Oxford lectures relating natural science to art. ([Library Edition, XXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1876:** *Ariadne Florentina*. Six lectures on wood and metal engraving, delivered at Oxford in 1872 and published with an appendix. ([Library Edition, XXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1874:** *Val d’Arno*. Ten Oxford lectures on Tuscan art before the Florentine victories of 1250. ([Library Edition, XXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1874; published 1906:** *The Æsthetic and Mathematic Schools of Art in Florence*. Oxford lectures published only after Ruskin’s death; their status as a finished book is editorial and posthumous. ([Library Edition, XXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1875–1877:** *Mornings in Florence*. Six compact traveller’s guides to Florentine Christian art, conceived as instruction in how and where to look. ([Library Edition, XXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1877–1884:** *St. Mark’s Rest*. A serial guide, history, and devotional interpretation of Venice and St Mark’s Basilica; later editions incorporated contributions by others, so not every component is Ruskin’s composition. ([Library Edition, XXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1884:** *The Art of England*. Six Oxford lectures delivered during Ruskin’s second Slade professorship in 1883. ([Library Edition, XXXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1884–1885:** *The Pleasures of England*. Oxford lectures on English cultural history, delivered in 1884 and issued in parts. ([Library Edition, XXXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Drawing protocols and educational arrangements

- **1857:** *The Elements of Drawing, in Three Letters to Beginners*. An illustrated teaching protocol whose reader is instructed to make drawings, not merely to receive propositions. ([Library Edition, XV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1859:** *The Elements of Perspective*. A school protocol intended for use alongside Euclid, combining geometrical instruction with drawing practice. ([Library Edition, XV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1870 onward:** *The Ruskin Art Collection at Oxford*. A pedagogical Gesamtkunstwerk of original art, copies, casts, photographs, diagrams, catalogues, labels, and exercises assembled for the Drawing School; because Ruskin made the arrangement but not every constituent object, it resists classification as either his artwork or a mere collection. Its historical catalogue contained roughly 1,470 objects, while Ruskin’s revised teaching catalogue selected 300. ([Library Edition, XXI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/); [Ashmolean teaching collection](https://ruskin.ashmolean.org/collection/8990))
- **1870–1878:** *Catalogues of the Educational, Standard, Reference, and Rudimentary Series*. Mutable catalogues and instructional schemes governing how the Oxford collection was to be viewed and used; they are protocols and curatorial works rather than descriptions alone. ([Library Edition, XXI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1877–1878:** *The Laws of Fésole*. A projected drawing-and-painting course derived from Tuscan practice; only four of the intended twelve books appeared, making it an unfinished pedagogical project. ([Library Edition, XV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Political economy, public speech, and social manifestos

- **1860; book 1862:** *Unto This Last*. Four essays first serialized in the *Cornhill Magazine* and then issued as a book; their polemical redefinition of wealth and labor makes the sequence a social manifesto. ([Library Edition, XVII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1862–1863; book 1872:** *Munera Pulveris*. Six essays on political economy, serialized in *Fraser’s Magazine* before illness interrupted publication and later collected with revisions. ([Library Edition, XVII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1865:** *Sesame and Lilies*. Two public lectures on reading and education, later enlarged by a third; its changing contents mean the title denotes several authorially sanctioned forms. ([Library Edition, XVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1866:** *The Ethics of the Dust*. Ten conversational lectures on crystallization delivered to girls at Winnington Hall; it joins scientific instruction, moral allegory, and dramatized dialogue. ([Library Edition, XVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1866; enlarged 1873:** *The Crown of Wild Olive*. Three lectures on work, traffic, and war, later expanded with “The Future of England”; the changing lecture collection has no single immutable form. ([Library Edition, XVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1867:** *Time and Tide, by Weare and Tyne*. Twenty-five public letters to the workingman Thomas Dixon on labor, law, and social organization. ([Library Edition, XVII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1871–1884:** *Fors Clavigera: Letters to the Workmen and Labourers of Great Britain*. Ninety-six monthly or intermittent public letters that report, exhort, organize, teach, and transact Guild business; this is simultaneously serial text, manifesto, ledger, and institutional action. ([Library Edition, XXVII–XXIX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1871 onward:** *The Guild of St George*. A social project and enduring institution founded as the Guild of St George’s Company, with land, rules, collections, educational purposes, and practical labor; it is an obra made through people and property rather than a text. ([Library Edition, XXX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1874:** *The Ferry Hinksey road-digging project*. An intentionally exemplary but practically unsuccessful attempt to organize Oxford undergraduates in manual road work; it resists being called a completed work because the project’s moral action mattered more to Ruskin than its unfinished road. ([Library Edition, XXVIII and XXXVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1875 onward:** *St George’s Museum and its collection*. A museum-project established at Walkley for working people, combining minerals, manuscripts, artworks, casts, books, and Ruskin’s modes of display; its institutional and curatorial form is the made thing, although most individual exhibits were made by others. ([Library Edition, XXX](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Natural history, weather, and the material earth

- **1873–1881:** *Love’s Meinie*. Three studies of Greek and English birds developed from lectures and issued incompletely and irregularly; natural history, etymology, myth, and moral teaching remain deliberately entangled. ([Library Edition, XXV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1875–1883:** *Deucalion: Collected Studies of the Lapse of Waves, and Life of Stones*. A serial geological and hydrological inquiry issued in parts and never brought to the comprehensive form its subtitle projects. ([Library Edition, XXVI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1875–1886:** *Proserpina*. A serial study of flowers combining botany, nomenclature, mythology, memory, and drawing; it deliberately resists the separation of scientific manual from imaginative work. ([Library Edition, XXV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1884:** *The Storm-Cloud of the Nineteenth Century*. Two lectures on observed atmospheric change, industrial pollution, and moral disorder; it is a record of looking and a prophetic manifesto, not a modern climatological study in the strict disciplinary sense. ([Library Edition, XXXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Travel, sacred history, and translation projects

- **1876–1888:** *Bibliotheca Pastorum*. Ruskin’s projected “Shepherd’s Library” of inexpensive instructive texts, involving selection, translation, adaptation, annotation, and editorial design; because many source texts and translations are other people’s, the series is his project but not wholly his authorship. ([Library Edition, XXXI–XXXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1876:** *The Economist of Xenophon*. The first *Bibliotheca Pastorum* volume, edited and partly translated through collaboration; attribution belongs to Xenophon, Ruskin, and his assisting translators in different senses. ([Library Edition, XXXI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1877:** *Rock Honeycomb*. A *Bibliotheca Pastorum* anthology of sayings and passages selected and arranged by Ruskin; its obra lies in compilation rather than original authorship of every sentence. ([Library Edition, XXXI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1880:** *The Elements of English Prosody*. A compact and idiosyncratic protocol for reading English verse, issued within *Bibliotheca Pastorum*. ([Library Edition, XXXI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1885:** *A Knight’s Faith*. Ruskin’s edited presentation of material from Froissart, made as a *Bibliotheca Pastorum* volume rather than authored from nothing. ([Library Edition, XXXI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1883:** *The Story of Ida*. An edited memorial narrative based on Francesca Alexander’s account of Ida; the textual making is collaborative and Ruskin’s role is editorial. ([Library Edition, XXXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1884–1885:** *Roadside Songs of Tuscany*. Francesca Alexander’s collected songs and drawings, edited and framed by Ruskin; the songs belong to oral tradition and Alexander made the translations and images, so the complete object cannot properly be assigned to Ruskin alone. ([Library Edition, XXXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1887:** *Christ’s Folk in the Apennine*. Another Francesca Alexander work mediated and edited by Ruskin; it belongs to his publishing project without being solely his creation. ([Library Edition, XXXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1888:** *Ulric the Farm Servant*. A *Bibliotheca Pastorum* translation-project from Gotthelf, produced through multiple hands; Ruskin’s role was selection and editorial shaping rather than sole translation. ([Library Edition, XXXII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1880–1885:** *The Bible of Amiens*. A study and traveller’s guide to Amiens Cathedral, conceived as the first part of the larger *Our Fathers Have Told Us* project. ([Library Edition, XXXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **projected 1880s:** *Our Fathers Have Told Us: Sketches of the History of Christendom for Boys and Girls Who Have Been Held at Its Fonts*. A projected multi-part history of Christendom; only *The Bible of Amiens* was completed in the intended form, so the project—not a nonexistent finished series—is the work. ([Library Edition, XXXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1880s:** *Valle Crucis*. A projected second component of *Our Fathers Have Told Us*, surviving fragmentarily; it should be named as an unfinished manuscript project, not silently promoted to a completed book. ([Library Edition, XXXIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Recollections, collections, and repurposed texts

- **1840–1845; published 1894:** *Letters to a College Friend*. Personal correspondence later made into a publication; the book is post hoc and should not be confused with a work planned under that title while the letters were written. ([Library Edition, I](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1880:** *Arrows of the Chace*. An authorized collection of Ruskin’s previously scattered newspaper letters, arranged by topic; it is both a new editorial object and a gathering of older interventions. ([Library Edition, XXXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1885:** *On the Old Road*. A collected and revised body of earlier essays and lectures; it is an authorially rearranged retrospective construction, not the original form of the included texts. ([Library Edition, XXXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1880–1881:** *Fiction, Fair and Foul*. A serial essay on fiction later incorporated into *On the Old Road*; it therefore exists both as a sequence and as part of a larger retrospective obra. ([Library Edition, XXXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **various dates; collected posthumously:** *Ruskiniana*. Short notices, prefaces, inscriptions, circulars, and fugitive writings gathered by Cook and Wedderburn; this is an editorial residue rather than a work Ruskin conceived under one title. ([Library Edition, XXXIV](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1827–1889:** *Letters*. Thousands of private, professional, pedagogical, and public communications survive; they constitute a body of work but not a closed authored book, and published selections necessarily impose later boundaries. ([Library Edition, XXXVI–XXXVII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))

### Drawings, watercolours, engravings, and photographic collaborations

- **c. 1826–late 1880s:** *Ruskin’s drawings and watercolours*. Several thousand sheets survive, although the total depends on whether studies, verso sketches, copies, diagrams, and fragments count separately; they form a lifelong observational practice rather than one titleable work. Cook and Wedderburn’s catalogue is extensive but not a modern exhaustive catalogue raisonné, so completeness and some dates remain open. ([Library Edition, XXXVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/); [Ashmolean overview](https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/publication/An-instinct-to-draw-John-Ruskins-drawings-in-the-Ashmolean-Museum--9e0b09741ab5f521a6683b0d3440a66a))
- **1840s–1870s:** *Architectural surveys and measured drawings*. Studies of Venice, northern Italy, France, Switzerland, and Britain made as records, teaching examples, and foundations for printed plates; many lack fixed authorial titles, and individual sheets move between autonomous artwork, field note, and evidence. ([Library Edition, XXXVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1840s–1880s:** *Botanical, geological, cloud, and animal studies*. Watercolours and drawings made to investigate natural form and often reused in lectures or books; calling each a finished artwork would misrepresent Ruskin’s experimental and pedagogical purpose. ([Library Edition, XXV–XXVI and XXXVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1849:** *Etched plates for The Seven Lamps of Architecture*. Fourteen plates drawn and etched by Ruskin as integral parts of the book; later editions sometimes used re-etched substitutes, so image authorship varies by edition. ([Library Edition, VIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1851:** *Plates for Examples of the Architecture of Venice*. Sixteen architectural plates selected and drawn to measurement by Ruskin; they are the realized portion of the abandoned twelve-part atlas. ([Shepherd bibliography](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/cih1150b2210171.pdf); [Library Edition, XI](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
- **1845–1858, approximately:** *Daguerreotypes associated with Ruskin’s continental journeys*. A corpus of architectural and landscape photographs commissioned, directed, acquired, and sometimes apparently taken by Ruskin in collaboration with servants and professional operators. Plate-by-plate photographic authorship is often undocumented or disputed, so the corpus is best described as his photographic project rather than wholly as photographs “by Ruskin.” ([Library Edition, XXXVIII](https://the-ruskin.co.uk/publications-resources/the-complete-works-of-john-ruskin/))
