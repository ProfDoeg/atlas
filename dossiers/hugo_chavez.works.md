## Works by Hugo Rafael Chávez Frías

### Diaries, poems, stories and drama

- **1972–1975:** *Cadet diaries*. A series of manuscript notebooks rather than a single finished book; Chávez said that he began keeping daily agendas in 1972, while the surviving diary published as *Diario del cadete Hugo Chávez* documents March–April 1974. The posthumous edition is a selection, so the extent and survival of the original series remain uncertain. ([*Diario del cadete Hugo Chávez*](https://es.scribd.com/document/758053717/Diario-Del-Cadete-Con-Portada-1); [Ramonet interview](https://alcaldialibertador-carabobo.gob.ve/bibliotecadigital/admgaceta/files/gacetas/1715366882.pdf))

- **Date uncertain, before 1987:** *Vuelvan caras*. Chávez identified this as verse or poetry, but no authoritative bibliography supplies a secure date, complete text or publication history; it therefore resists treatment as a fully documented, independently published work. ([CIDOB biography](https://www.cidob.org/lider-politico/hugo-chavez-frias); [Chávez’s retrospective account](https://k-personajes.blogspot.com/2007/08/chvez-2021-crnica-de-una-hamaca.html))

- **Date uncertain, before 1987:** *Mauricio*. A short story preserved in later compilations and mentioned by Chávez among his youthful writings; its original manuscript date and first circulation have not been established. ([CIDOB biography](https://www.cidob.org/lider-politico/hugo-chavez-frias); [later printed text](https://alcaldialibertador-carabobo.gob.ve/bibliotecadigital/admgaceta/files/gacetas/1715366882.pdf))

- **1987:** *El genio y el centauro* (also recorded as *El genio y el centauro en Cañafístola*). A historical stage play about the meeting of Bolívar and Páez, incorporating music and verse; it reportedly received third prize in a Teatro Histórico Nacional competition. The two forms of the title coexist in the sources, and no critical edition or original production record was located. ([CIDOB biography](https://www.cidob.org/lider-politico/hugo-chavez-frias); [*Cuentos del arañero*](https://www.marxists.org/espanol/chavez/2012/2012-cuentos_del_aranero_libro.pdf))

- **1974–1992:** *Un brazalete tricolor*. A retrospective assemblage of essays, notes and prison documents, first published in 1992 and enlarged or rearranged in later editions. It is an archive-shaped obra rather than a text composed as one continuous book, so the dates belong to its constituent writings and its completeness is editorially contingent. ([UNAM thesis bibliography](https://tesiunamdocumentos.dgb.unam.mx/ptd2016/noviembre/0752677/0752677.pdf); [Institute publication list](https://institutohugochavez.com/index.php/programas-y-proyectos/))

### Political projects, manifestos and protocols

- **1982–1991:** *El árbol de las tres raíces / Sistema EBR*. The ideological model joining Simón Rodríguez, Simón Bolívar and Ezequiel Zamora, developed within the clandestine MBR-200. It is best understood as a collective political project associated particularly with Chávez, not as a securely bounded sole-authored text.

- **1991:** *El libro azul*. A programmatic manifesto setting out the Bolivarian national project and the “three roots” model; scholarly discussion dates the composition to 1991, although editions frequently carry 1992, 2007 or 2013 publication dates. Those dates describe editions, not new works. ([Nueva Sociedad](https://nuso.org/articulo/leer-el-chavismo/); [Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/HUGO_CH%C3%81VEZ_FR%C3%8DAS.html?hl=en&id=z3e8zwEACAAJ&output=html_text))

- **1992:** *Proyecto de gobierno de transición para el 4 de febrero*. A protocol for government following the attempted military seizure of power. It was produced within the MBR-200 and cannot responsibly be assigned to Chávez alone; its collective authorship is part of what the object is.

- **4 February 1992:** *“Por ahora” surrender address*. A brief televised declaration accepting responsibility and ordering the insurgents to surrender. More than a description, it acted upon an armed operation and its audience; it is therefore both broadcast performance and manifesto, not a “book.”

- **1993:** *Programa del MBR-200*. A political program attributed in contemporary bibliographic testimony to Chávez and the MBR-200. Its collective institutional authorship should not be silently converted into exclusive personal authorship. ([Francisco Visconti Osorio](https://www.aporrea.org/actualidad/a272056.html))

- **1996:** *Agenda alternativa bolivariana: una propuesta patriótica para salir del laberinto*. A manifesto and proposed transition program written after Chávez’s release from prison. Later “Libro Rojo” editions are reproductions or editorial presentations of the same project, not separate works. ([Nueva Sociedad](https://nuso.org/articulo/leer-el-chavismo/); [bibliographic testimony](https://www.aporrea.org/actualidad/a272056.html))

- **1998:** *Una revolución democrática: la propuesta de Hugo Chávez para transformar Venezuela*. The electoral program of the Movimiento Quinta República: a candidate’s project bearing Chávez’s name but made through a campaign organization, and therefore not securely a sole-authored literary text.

- **1999:** *Ideas fundamentales para la Constitución Bolivariana de la V República*. A presidential constitutional proposal submitted to the Constituent Assembly. It is a protocol for refounding the state; the resulting 1999 Constitution is a different, collective legal creation and should not be catalogued as a work authored by Chávez alone.

- **2001–2007:** *Líneas generales del Plan de Desarrollo Económico y Social de la Nación 2001–2007*. A state planning protocol issued under Chávez’s presidency. The documentary tradition associates it with his political project, but its drafting was institutional and collective. ([Wikisource work list](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez))

- **2001–2004:** *Alternativa Bolivariana para las Américas (ALBA)*. A transnational integration project proposed by Chávez in 2001 and formalized with Fidel Castro in 2004. It is an institution-making political project rather than a sole-authored text; subsequent treaties and organizations are collective manifestations of it.

- **2007–2013:** *Proyecto Nacional Simón Bolívar: Primer Plan Socialista de Desarrollo Económico y Social de la Nación 2007–2013*. A national planning protocol and declaration of a socialist transition. It belongs to Chávez’s programmatic corpus but was made through the Venezuelan state, so personal and institutional authorship overlap. ([Wikisource work list](https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez); [Nueva Sociedad](https://nuso.org/articulo/leer-el-chavismo/))

- **2010:** *Libro rojo: documentos fundamentales del Partido Socialista Unido de Venezuela*. The PSUV’s declaration of principles, statutes and program, associated with Chávez’s political thought but formally a collective party document. Scholarly guides treat it as a central text of chavismo without establishing that Chávez wrote every part. ([Nueva Sociedad](https://nuso.org/articulo/leer-el-chavismo/))

- **2012:** *Propuesta del candidato de la patria comandante Hugo Chávez para la gestión bolivariana socialista 2013–2019*. Chávez’s final electoral program, commonly called *Programa de la Patria*. Its posthumous transformation by the National Assembly into the official *Plan de la Patria* is a later institutional work and not simply another Chávez text. ([UNESCO/SITEAL copy cited by CLACSO](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/253198/1/Propiedad-y-capital.pdf); [Nueva Sociedad](https://nuso.org/articulo/leer-el-chavismo/))

- **20 October 2012:** *Golpe de timón*. An address to the first cabinet meeting of Chávez’s new presidential term, subsequently canonized and printed under this editorial title. As delivered, it was an oral intervention and governing protocol—especially a demand for communal transformation—not a book composed under that title. ([MINCI edition cited by CLACSO](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/253198/1/Propiedad-y-capital.pdf))

### Broadcast and digital works

- **1999–2012:** *Aló Presidente*. A 378-installment radio-and-television Gesamtkunstwerk combining conversation, improvisatory monologue, music, interviews, audience calls, government announcements and staged public action. Chávez conceived and hosted it, but journalists, guests, callers, musicians and production crews made every episode collaborative; its approximately 1,656 broadcast hours resist reduction to authored “speeches.” ([Kent research repository](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/48023/); [study of the program](https://escueladegob.com/libros/Alo-Presidente-y-otras-llamadas.pdf))

- **1999–2013:** *Presidential addresses, speeches and interventions*. A vast oral and audiovisual corpus that includes annual messages to the National Assembly, international addresses, military and electoral speeches, cabinet interventions and compulsory national broadcasts. The EFICEM collection of National Assembly speeches covers 1999–2012, but scholarship explicitly notes that no systematic complete compilation of Chávez’s superabundant materials exists; consequently, this body cannot honestly be presented as a closed catalogue of separately titled works. ([EFICEM collection record](https://search.worldcat.org/es/title/hugo-chavez-la-construccion-del-socialismo-del-siglo-xxi-discursos-del-comandante-supremo-ante-la-asamblea-nacional-1999-2012/oclc/857772069); [Nueva Sociedad](https://nuso.org/articulo/leer-el-chavismo/))

- **2009:** *Aló Presidente Teórico*. Six televised seminars conceived as an explicitly pedagogical offshoot of *Aló Presidente*. They combine lecture, political workshop and broadcast performance; later books bearing the title are transcriptions or compilations, not six newly written works. ([Institute publication list](https://institutohugochavez.com/index.php/programas-y-proyectos/))

- **22 January 2009–2012:** *Las líneas de Chávez*. A serial newspaper column rather than a single book; the first collected volume contains installments 1–56 from January 2009 to January 2010. Later compilations vary in coverage, so the serial—not any one bound edition—is the obra. ([contemporary announcement and early contents](https://www.aporrea.org/actualidad/n129263.html); [first-volume description](https://es.scribd.com/document/28466839/Las-lineas-de-Chavez))

- **28 April 2010–18 February 2013:** *@chavezcandanga*. Chávez’s verified Twitter production, subsequently collected as *Los 1.824 tuits de Hugo Chávez*. It is a digital serial made through a presidential communications apparatus; Chávez’s direct participation is well documented, but treating every post as unmediated sole authorship would exceed the evidence. ([archival compilation](https://cesdi.net/pluginfile.php/479/mod_data/content/136/chavezcandanga.pdf))

### Oral narratives and dialogic works

- **2001–2005:** *Hugo Chávez Frías: un hombre, un pueblo*. A series of extended interviews with Marta Harnecker. The resulting book is a dialogic work: Chávez made the testimony and argument in speech, while Harnecker selected, questioned and edited it; neither “sole-authored book” nor mere secondary biography adequately describes it.

- **2003:** *Chávez nuestro*. An oral-history and interview composition made with Rosa Miriam Elizalde and Luis Báez. Chávez is a principal speaking subject, not the sole maker of the published book. ([digital edition](https://rebelion.org/docs/164912.pdf))

- **2005:** *Hugo Chávez: mi primera vida*. A book-length interview with Ignacio Ramonet about Chávez’s life through 1992. The conversations are Chávez’s oral autobiographical creation, but the posthumously published form and title are Ramonet’s editorial construction rather than a manuscript left by Chávez. ([Institute publication list](https://institutohugochavez.com/index.php/programas-y-proyectos/))

- **2012:** *Cuentos del arañero*. An editorially arranged collection of autobiographical stories told by Chávez in broadcasts and public appearances. It is neither a conventional authored storybook nor raw transcription: Chávez made the oral narratives, while editors detached and titled them, making the published object collaborative. ([2012 edition](https://www.marxists.org/espanol/chavez/2012/2012-cuentos_del_aranero_libro.pdf))

### Visual work

- **1992–1994:** *La luna de Yare*. An oil painting said to have been made during Chávez’s imprisonment and later donated to the PSUV. Contemporary reporting attributes it to Chávez, but later criticism explicitly questioned that attribution; without a catalogue raisonné or technical provenance study, it should remain “attributed to Hugo Chávez,” not silently authenticated. ([contemporary sale report](https://www.lavozdegalicia.es/noticia/gente/2008/09/17/cuadro-hugo-chavez-subastado-160000-euros/00031221674154615702615.htm); [scholarly discussion of the attribution](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/10622/Dissertation_Silva-Ferrer.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1); [critical investigation](https://piaui.uol.com.br/revista/27/o-renascentista-de-miraflores/))

- **Dates and titles undetermined:** *Other paintings and drawings*. The Fundación Comandante Eterno describes a pinacoteca containing several canvases and original manuscripts, but no consulted scholarly catalogue raisonné identifies, dates and authenticates them individually. They therefore resist enumeration as securely titled works. ([Foundation archive announcement](https://www.hugochavez.ve/noticias/nueva-noticia-ms132bti))

### Musical manifestations

- **Various dates:** *Public performances of Venezuelan and Latin American songs and poems*. Chávez repeatedly sang, played the cuatro and recited verse in broadcasts and political events. These are performances or manifestations, not compositions: for example, the “Corrido de caballería” associated with Maisanta uses a poem by Andrés Eloy Blanco, so it must not be miscatalogued as a Chávez song merely because Chávez performed it. ([Chávez’s attribution to Andrés Eloy Blanco](https://www.aporrea.org/actualidad/a77546.html); [recorded performance report](https://www.aporrea.org/internacionales/n85547.html))
