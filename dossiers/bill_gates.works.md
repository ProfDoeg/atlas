## Works by William Henry Gates III

### Software and computational systems

- **c. 1970–1972:** Untitled Lakeside School scheduling software. Gates and other members of the Lakeside Programming Group made programs for scheduling classes; surviving accounts do not establish a single fixed title or cleanly separable authorship, so this resists classification as an individual work. [S2]
- **c. 1971–1972:** Untitled payroll program. A large COBOL payroll system produced collaboratively by the Lakeside Programming Group; Gates participated but recalled that Paul Allen and Ric Weiland performed much of the programming. [S2]
- **c. 1972–1974:** *Traf-O-Data traffic-analysis system*. A collaborative hardware-and-software project by Gates, Paul Allen, and Paul Gilbert for converting roadway counter tapes into reports. Gates funded the prototype and wrote some—but not all—of its software; the project therefore belongs to him only as a shared work. [S2]
- **1975:** *Altair BASIC*. A BASIC-language interpreter made by Gates and Paul Allen, with Monte Davidoff contributing its floating-point mathematics; it appeared commercially in 4K and 8K forms and became Microsoft’s first product. “Altair BASIC” names a software family with successive manifestations, not one immutable object. [S3][S4]
- **1975–c. 1978:** *Microsoft BASIC*. The larger project of adapting the Altair interpreter to additional processors and machines, including 6800 and 6502 implementations. Authorship varies by version and includes other Microsoft programmers, so the corporate software family should not be silently attributed to Gates alone. [S2][S4]

### Books

- **1995:** *The Road Ahead*. A book about personal computing and the anticipated information economy, made with Nathan Myhrvold and Peter Rinearson; the substantially revised 1996 edition is a later manifestation rather than a separate title. [S5][S6]
- **1999:** *Business @ the Speed of Thought: Using a Digital Nervous System*. A management-and-technology book written with Collins Hemingway; its argument treats digital information systems as an organizational “nervous system.” [S7]
- **2021:** *How to Avoid a Climate Disaster: The Solutions We Have and the Breakthroughs We Need*. A climate-policy book that is also a protocol-like plan for reaching net-zero greenhouse-gas emissions. [S8]
- **2022:** *How to Prevent the Next Pandemic*. A public-health book and proposed preparedness protocol, presenting institutional and technical measures intended to prevent future outbreaks from becoming pandemics. [S9]
- **2025:** *Source Code: My Beginnings*. The first volume of a projected three-part memoir, covering Gates’s childhood through Microsoft’s beginnings; the announced later volumes remain projects and should not yet be counted as completed books. [S1][S10]

### Manifestos, memoranda, and protocols

- **1976:** *An Open Letter to Hobbyists*. An open letter—and effectively a manifesto for proprietary microcomputer software—arguing that unauthorized copying undermined professional software development. It acts upon a nascent market rather than merely describing it. [S11]
- **1995:** *The Internet Tidal Wave*. An internal Microsoft memorandum assigning the Internet the company’s highest strategic importance; it is both a diagnosis and an executive instruction, and thus functions as a corporate protocol. [S12]
- **1996:** *Content Is King*. An essay predicting that much of the Internet’s economic value would lie in publishable content. Its later fame as a slogan can obscure that it first appeared as a short strategic text, not a book. [S13]
- **2002:** *Trustworthy Computing*. A company-wide memorandum that redirected Microsoft toward security, privacy, reliability, and business integrity. Because it changed engineering priorities, it is an operative manifesto as much as an internal communication. [S14]
- **2009–2021:** *Gates Foundation Annual Letters*. A serial body of public strategy texts: some installments are Gates’s alone and later ones are jointly authored with Melinda French Gates. The archive is an evolving institutional obra, not a single stable work, and authorship must be checked letter by letter. [S15]
- **2021:** *This Is How We Build a Zero Emissions Economy*. A white paper setting out investment and policy measures for lowering the cost of clean technologies; its prescriptive structure makes it a climate-policy protocol. [S16]
- **2026:** *The Turbulent AI Era Is Here. The Choices We Make Now Are Critical*. A public memorandum proposing priorities for reducing AI’s harms and distributing its benefits. It remains an initial intervention rather than a complete governance protocol. [S17]

### Serial digital writing

- **2010–present:** *Gates Notes*. A continuously revised website and body of essays, reviews, annual reflections, videos, and project reports. It resists itemization as one “work”: the site is simultaneously publication, archive, platform, and changing public persona; its individual posts retain their own dates and titles. [S18]

### Audio works

- **2020:** *Bill Gates and Rashida Jones Ask Big Questions*. A limited conversational podcast co-created and co-hosted with Rashida Jones; the episodes are collaborative audio texts rather than writings by Gates alone. [S19]
- **2023–present:** *Unconfuse Me with Bill Gates*. An interview podcast in which Gates frames questions and conversations with specialists. Its continuing, multi-episode form makes it a serial project rather than a finished singular work. [S20]

### Organizations and long-duration projects

- **1972–c. 1975:** *Traf-O-Data*. A small venture created with Paul Allen and Paul Gilbert to process traffic-count data. It is both an entrepreneurial project and the organizational container for the traffic-analysis system, not merely another name for that machine. [S2]
- **1975–present:** *Microsoft*. An organization co-founded with Paul Allen and initially formed around the BASIC project. Microsoft is a shared institutional obra involving many thousands of makers; its later products—including MS-DOS, Windows, and Office—cannot responsibly be listed as Gates’s personal creations merely because he led the company. [S4][S21]
- **1994–2000:** *William H. Gates Foundation*. A philanthropic institution established by Gates and administered initially by his father; it became one predecessor of the merged Gates foundation. As an endowed organization made to act through others, it resists the model of a discrete authored work. [S21]
- **1997–2000:** *Gates Library Foundation / Gates Learning Foundation*. A philanthropic project supporting public access to computers and digital resources, subsequently merged into the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation. Its name and structure changed during its short independent existence. [S21]
- **2000–present:** *Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation*, renamed *Gates Foundation* in 2025. A philanthropic institution made through the merger of earlier Gates projects and co-founded with Melinda French Gates; its grants and programs are collective institutional actions, not works authored personally by Gates. [S15][S21]
- **2006–present:** *TerraPower*. An advanced-nuclear-energy company founded by Gates with a group of collaborators. The company is his project, but its reactor designs and scientific products belong to engineering teams and should not be reassigned to him as individual inventions. [S22]
- **2008–present:** *Gates Ventures*. A private office and project platform through which Gates organizes work on energy, health, education, and technology. It has no single medium or finished form and is better understood as infrastructure for other works. [S21]
- **2010–present:** *Giving Pledge*. A philanthropic commitment campaign co-founded by Gates, Melinda French Gates, and Warren Buffett. Its principal made objects are public pledges by independent signatories, so the resulting corpus is facilitated by the founders rather than authored by them. [S21]
- **2015–present:** *Breakthrough Energy*. A network of investment, philanthropic, policy, and commercialization programs founded by Gates to accelerate clean-energy innovation. Its crossing of finance, research, advocacy, and institution-building makes it closer to a Gesamtkunstwerk-like project than to a single company or text. [S21][S23]
