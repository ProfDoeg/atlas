## Works by François Delsarte

### System and pedagogical project

- **1832–1871:** *Science de l’esthétique appliquée* (also called *Esthétique appliquée* or the “Delsarte system”). A lifelong, unfinished project joining vocal technique, oratory, acting, gesture, anatomy, theology, and aesthetics; its principal divisions—semeiotics, statics, and dynamics—make it closer to a pedagogical Gesamtkunstwerk than to a single text. Delsarte never issued a definitive exposition, and later “Delsarte systems” by pupils and American interpreters are transformations rather than works authored by him.

- **1839–1870:** *Cours d’esthétique appliquée*. A changing series of lectures, demonstrations, and embodied exercises rather than a stable written work; surviving texts are Delsarte’s notes, pupil transcriptions, and later redactions. The documented versions include the 1855 lectures, the *Cours de M. Delsarte aux Sociétés savantes* of 1858, and Alphonse Pagès’s corrected notes from the nine-lesson course of 1859.

### Vocal protocols

- **1833:** *Méthode philosophique du chant*. Delsarte’s first published protocol of vocal instruction, presenting singing as a physiological, intellectual, and moral art rather than merely a technique.

- **1852:** *Mémoire adressé à l’Académie des Sciences*. A scientific-pedagogical memorandum on vocal production and Delsarte’s method. It belongs to the project of obtaining institutional recognition for his vocal discoveries, not to a conventional book-length treatise.

- **1862:** *Six Vocalises. À son élève Mme Gaëtane Valantin, professeur de chant à Lyon*. A self-published collection of six wordless vocal studies; its prefatory argument rejects excessive written expression marks and makes the collection both musical exercise and pedagogical manifesto.

- **1862:** *Exercices de chant de F. Delsarte pour servir d’introduction aux six nouvelles vocalises du même auteur*. A four-page preparatory protocol issued separately from the *Six Vocalises*.

### Musical compositions

- **Before 1855:** *Stances à l’Éternité*. A sacred or philosophical song for voice, with words credited in surviving editions to R. Deslandes; it is the one independent Delsarte composition examined in detail by the modern specialist study. Its original composition date is not securely established, and later editions and recordings should not be mistaken for new works by Delsarte.

- **Dates not securely established:** *Mélodies, romances, and religious pieces*. Contemporary and later sources say that Delsarte composed several such works, but the surviving corpus has not been securely reconstructed as a title-by-title catalogue. This resists treatment as a determinate list of works: some music is lost, some remains unidentified, and some catalogue records conflate Delsarte’s composition with his editing or arrangement of older music.

### Editorial and reconstructive works

- **1855–1864:** *Archives du chant*. A serial anthology of historical vocal music, eventually extending to at least twenty-five numbered volumes or instalments. Delsarte selected, edited, annotated, and pedagogically organized sacred chant, medieval and popular song, airs de cour, and French lyric theatre; many piano reductions were made by Rosine Delsarte. It is therefore an editorial obra and teaching project, not a body of compositions by François Delsarte, and some of its historical attributions were already questioned by contemporary critics.

- **By 1860:** *Catalogue des morceaux contenus dans les 18 premières livraisons des Archives du chant*. Delsarte’s own finding aid to the first eighteen instalments—a bibliographical instrument internal to the larger anthology rather than an autonomous creative work.

- **1855–1864:** *Hymnes, proses et antiennes, disposées conformément au type harmonique consacré par l’antique tradition de l’Église*. The sacred-music division of the *Archives du chant*, prepared with Rosine Delsarte. Its “harmonic type” involves nineteenth-century editorial reconstruction, so the object cannot be treated simply as either Delsarte’s composition or an unmediated reproduction of ancient chant.

### Lectures, theoretical manuscripts, and fragments

- **1830:** *Buccologie*. An early manuscript study of the mouth and its expressive or vocal functions. It survives within the dispersed teaching papers and is better understood as a working research document than as a finished treatise.

- **1839:** *École de Delsarte, École de chant morale et scientifique: notes et compte-rendu de ses cours*. Notes documenting Delsarte’s early moral and scientific conception of vocal education; their archival form makes their boundary as a distinct authored “work” uncertain.

- **1839:** *Livre des Procès-Verbaux de la Famille Trinitaire*. A manuscript record associated with Delsarte’s religious-philosophical circle. It belongs to the social and spiritual machinery of his aesthetic project rather than cleanly to literature, theory, or administration alone.

- **1859:** *Esthétique appliquée: cours de F. Delsarte. Exposition en neuf leçons de l’art de l’orateur, du peintre et du musicien*. Alphonse Pagès’s notebook of Delsarte’s lectures, containing corrections by Delsarte. It records his teaching but is not wholly an autograph composition, so authorship is shared between oral lecturer, note-taker, and revising hand.

- **1865:** *Esthétique appliquée*. A lecture published in *Conférences de l’Association philotechnique, année 1865*. This is one of the few portions of Delsarte’s aesthetic teaching printed during his lifetime, but it is a manifestation of the larger course rather than its definitive text.

- **Circa 1865:** *Positivisme dans l’Art* / *Temple de l’art*. Closely overlapping manuscript notes apparently prepared for a lecture. Scholars treat the two titles or incipits as possibly referring to the same presentation; they should not be silently counted as two securely independent works.

- **1869–1871:** *Mes Épisodes révélateurs, ou Histoire d’une idée appelée à constituer la base de la Science et de l’Art*. An unfinished autobiographical-theoretical manuscript explaining the discoveries behind Delsarte’s science of expression. Its incompletion is constitutive: it is a project and fragment, not a completed book.

- **1870:** *Traité de la raison*. A theoretical text preserved in a non-autograph manuscript and published from the Delsarte family papers by Alain Porte. Its content is considered very probably Delsarte’s, but the absence of an autograph makes the attribution less than absolute.

- **Undated:** *Attitudes des bases*. Notes, diagrams, and aphoristic classifications of bodily attitudes and their meanings. These are operational components of Delsarte’s gestural protocol rather than a securely bounded treatise.

- **Undated:** *Notre Méthode*. Fragmentary notes relating bodily, vocal, and spiritual trinities. The title designates archival teaching material, not a publication completed and authorized by Delsarte.

- **Undated:** *L’Écriture décèle*. Notes on handwriting as an expression of the writer’s spirit or character. Its survival as part of the manuscript corpus makes its status as an independent work uncertain.

- **Undated:** *Préliminaires de l’histoire de mes découvertes*. Autobiographical and theoretical notes on the origin of Delsarte’s discoveries, related in subject to *Mes Épisodes révélateurs*. The surviving headings and fragments may represent stages of a projected larger account rather than separate finished works.

- **Undated:** *Le Peintre* / *Introduction: Grandeur de l’art*. Notes on imitation, physiognomy, nature, and the ideal type in painting. The unstable archival titling prevents a firm claim that this was a completed, independently titled treatise.

- **Undated:** *Philosophy of Expression*. A manuscript catalogued under Delsarte’s name in the Louisiana State University papers. Because the archive contains translations and later American reworkings alongside autograph material, its precise textual status requires caution.

- **1830–1871:** *Notebooks, charts, diagrams, correspondence, and loose notes on voice, gesture, anatomy, expression, and theology*. A dispersed research archive rather than a single obra. Some documents are autograph, others are pupil transcripts, translations, or sheets subsequently worked over by Steele MacKaye; treating every folder title as a Delsarte “work” would create false titles and false authorship.

### Instruments and patented inventions

- **1854–1867:** *Guide-accord*, also promoted as the *phonopticon*. A mechanical aid for tuning pianos, patented and repeatedly modified by Delsarte; it received recognition at the Paris Exposition of 1855 and was the only one of his mechanical inventions placed into substantial practical use. The changing names and versions describe an evolving technical project rather than several clearly separate works.

- **Date not securely established:** *Instrument de précision applicable aux observations astronomiques*. A patented mechanical conception reported by early biographical sources, apparently never put into ordinary use. Its surviving documentation is insufficient to supply a more exact title or date.

- **Undated:** *Sono-Type*. An invention represented in the LSU Delsarte papers. The finding aid identifies it as Delsarte’s, but does not establish whether the surviving material represents a completed instrument, a patent, or only a design; it therefore resists classification as a finished object.

### Performative manifestations

- **From 1839:** *Historical concerts and lecture-demonstrations*. Programmed performances through which Delsarte combined singing, declamation, historical repertory, commentary, and bodily demonstration. These were manifestations of his aesthetic project rather than repeatable works with one fixed text.

- **27 March 1856:** *Audition of selections from the Archives du chant at the Salle Pleyel*. A documented concert-demonstration presenting the anthology’s repertory and pedagogical argument in performance. It is not a separate composition, but a public manifestation essential to how the *Archives* was made and received.

### Sources

- Leonella Grasso Caprioli, [*François Delsarte musicista: aspetti biografici e documentali*](https://hdl.handle.net/11573/1713280), doctoral dissertation, Sapienza Università di Roma, 2023. This specialist study—particularly its chapters on vocal pedagogy, compositions, the *Archives du chant*, and inventions—provides the principal work-based framework.

- Franck Waille, [*Corps, arts et spiritualité chez François Delsarte (1811–1871): des interactions dynamiques*](https://scd-resnum.univ-lyon3.fr/out/theses/2009_out_waille_f.pdf), doctoral dissertation, Université Jean Moulin Lyon 3, 2009.

- Alain Porte, ed., *François Delsarte: une anthologie*, Paris: Institut de pédagogie musicale et chorégraphique, 1992. The first substantial French-language scholarly anthology of Delsarte’s manuscript writings, including *Traité de la raison*.

- Elena Randi, [“From the Gestural and Phonetic Sign of Everyday Life to the Actor’s Sign: Aspects of François Delsarte’s System”](https://elibrary.narr.digital/xibrary/kod20123-40239), *Kodikas/Code* 35, nos. 3–4, 2012, pp. 235–250. - Bradley Hoover, [*The Aesthetic System of François Delsarte and Richard Wagner: Catholicism, Romanticism, and Ancient Music*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/elements/aesthetic-system-of-francois-delsarte-and-richard-wagner/), Cambridge University Press, 2025. - LSU Libraries Special Collections, [*Delsarte (François Alexandre Nicolas) Papers, Mss. 1301: Finding Aid*](https://lib.lsu.edu/sites/default/files/sc/findaid/1301.pdf). Used to identify the surviving manuscript, diagrammatic, and inventive materials and to flag mixed or uncertain authorship.

- Orpheus Institute, [*Hymnes, proses et antiennes, disposées conformément au type harmonique consacré par l’antique tradition de l’Église*](https://www.cageweb.be/catalog/orp01%3A000002729). - H. Berlioz, [review of Delsarte’s 1856 Salle Pleyel presentation and the first instalments of the *Archives du chant*, *Journal des débats*, 9 May 1856](https://sas-space.sas.ac.uk/4944/1/Journal_des_Debats_9_mai_1856_proofed_final.pdf).
