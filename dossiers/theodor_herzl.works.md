## Works by Theodor Herzl

### Narratives, sketches, reportage, and author-made collections

- **1881:** *Hagenau*. An early novella or prose narrative; later bibliographies preserve the title, but its publication history and generic status are less securely documented than Herzl’s mature books. ([bibliographic overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Published_works))
- **1887:** *Neues von der Venus*. A collection of short narratives, travel sketches, and feuilletons—an authored book assembled from journalistic forms rather than a single continuous narrative. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **1888:** *Das Buch der Narrheit*. A collection of feuilletons: the book is an obra, while many of its constituent pieces first appeared as newspaper writing. ([Wikisource bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1895:** *Das Palais Bourbon: Bilder aus dem französischen Parlamentsleben*. A collection of political reportage and observational essays made from Herzl’s work as Paris correspondent. ([Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118550241.html#ndbcontent))
- **1895:** *Die Degradirung des Capitäns Dreyfus*. A journalistic account of the public degradation of Alfred Dreyfus; it is reportage rather than a freestanding political program. ([Bibliotheca Augustana](https://www.hs-augsburg.de/~harsch/germanica/Chronologie/20Jh/Herzl/her_intr.html))
- **1887–1900:** *Philosophische Erzählungen*. An author-arranged collection of seventeen philosophical tales, published in 1900; its contents had differing prior lives as feuilletons and stories. ([digitized first edition and contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1903–1904:** *Feuilletons*. A two-volume authorial collection of newspaper essays, sketches, travel writing, criticism, and short fiction. Bibliographies disagree between 1903 and 1904 because printing, issue, and title-page dates do not align neatly. ([Wikisource bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl); [Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))

### Tales gathered in *Philosophische Erzählungen*

- **1900 or earlier:** *Solon in Lydien*. A philosophical-historical tale, not the later three-act stage work made from the same material. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen); [tale record](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Solon_in_Lydien))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Das lenkbare Luftschiff*. A speculative philosophical tale about technological invention. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Sarah Holzmann*. A short narrative included as a separate work within the collection. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Pygmalion*. A philosophical tale reworking the Pygmalion motif. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))

- **1889:** *Die Reise nach einem Lächeln*. A philosophical society tale; the 1889 date is given by the Austrian biographical record’s excerpt, while the collection appeared in 1900. ([Austria-Forum](https://austria-forum.org/af/Biographien/Herzl%2C_Theodor))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Der Sohn*. A short philosophical narrative. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Mumbo*. A tale included as an independent item in the collection. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))

- **1900 or earlier:** *Die Garderobe*. A short narrative or feuilleton-like tale. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Die schöne Rosalinde*. A short philosophical narrative. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Die Heilung vom Spleen*. A satirical-philosophical tale. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Die Raupe*. A philosophical tale. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Eine gute That*. A short moral-philosophical narrative. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Der Unternehmer Buonaparte*. A satirical philosophical tale. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))
- **1900 or earlier:** *Das Wirthshaus zum Anilin*. A speculative or philosophical tale completing the 1900 collection. ([collection contents](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Philosophische_Erz%C3%A4hlungen))

### Plays and musical theatre

- **1880:** *Kompagniearbeit*. A one-act comedy and one of Herzl’s earliest dramatic experiments; surviving work lists give little evidence of a substantial publication or performance history. ([dramatic-work list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Plays))
- **1882:** *Die Causa Hirschkorn*. A one-act comedy. Some later reference works misdate it as 1890, so 1882 should be treated as the usual rather than uncontested date. ([dramatic-work list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Plays); [conflicting reference entry](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118550241.html#ndbcontent))
- **1884:** *Tabarin*. A one-act play, sometimes classified as comedy and sometimes simply as a Schauspiel. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **1885:** *Muttersöhnchen*. A four-act comedy; it was subsequently reworked or retitled *Austoben* by H. Jungmann, making the later form partly another writer’s work. One reference source prints the impossible date 1869, evidently an error. ([dramatic-work list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Plays); [erroneous date in Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118550241.html#ndbcontent))
- **1885:** *Seine Hoheit*. A three-act comedy concerned with money and social power; later references sometimes date its stage life rather than its composition. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **1887:** *Der Flüchtling*. A one-act comedy. Bibliographies variously give 1887 and circa 1889, apparently distinguishing composition, performance, and printed edition. ([Austria-Forum](https://austria-forum.org/af/Biographien/Herzl%2C_Theodor); [first-edition description](https://bo-kedem.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/files/PDF/catalog-CA-41.pdf))
- **1888:** *Wilddiebe*. A four-act comedy co-written with Hugo Wittmann; it therefore resists treatment as Herzl’s work alone, and references sometimes date its publication or production to 1891. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml); [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118550241.html#ndbcontent))
- **1890:** *Die Dame in Schwarz*. A four-act comedy co-written with Hugo Wittmann and first staged on 6 February 1890. ([Hugo Wittmann work list](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Wittmann_%28Schriftsteller%29))
- **1890:** *Was wird man sagen?*. A four-act comedy, published in Vienna by Gabor Steiner. ([first-edition description](https://bo-kedem.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/files/PDF/catalog-CA-41.pdf))
- **1890:** *Des Teufels Weib*. An operetta libretto, reportedly produced at the Theater an der Wien; it is a musical-theatrical component rather than a self-sufficient prose “book,” and the music was made by another hand. ([bibliographic work list](https://www.tidhar.tourolib.org/tidhar/view/4/1565))
- **1891–1892:** *Prinzen aus Genieland*. A four-act comedy; 1891 is often assigned to composition and 1892 to the printed Vienna edition. ([first-edition description](https://bo-kedem.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/files/PDF/catalog-CA-41.pdf); [Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **November 1894; revised and staged 1897–1898:** *Das neue Ghetto*. A four-act social drama and Herzl’s principal Jewish stage work. Its multiple dates describe distinct manifestations—first draft, publication, and performance—not rival titles. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml); [digitized-work bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1895:** *Die Glosse*. A one-act comedy, published in Dresden, Leipzig, and Vienna. ([first-edition description](https://bo-kedem.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/files/PDF/catalog-CA-41.pdf))
- **1898–1899:** *Unser Käthchen*. A four-act comedy: an edition described as Vienna 1898 precedes the commonly cited 1899 publication date, hence the range. ([first-edition description](https://bo-kedem.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/files/PDF/catalog-CA-41.pdf); [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118550241.html#ndbcontent))
- **1899:** *Gretel*. A four-act comedy. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **1900:** *I love you*. A one-act comedy listed in modern dramatic bibliographies; the English-language title is original to those lists, but its textual and performance history is poorly documented, so its status as a fully realized obra remains uncertain. ([dramatic-work list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Plays))
- **1900; published 1904:** *Solon in Lydien*. A three-act play developed after the prose tale of the same title; some catalogues cite 1905 because that was a posthumous issue or performance date, but a Vienna–Leipzig edition exists with a 1904 title date. ([Wikisource bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl); [University of Chicago collection description](https://storage.lib.uchicago.edu/pres/2007/pres2007-0007.pdf))

### Political texts, manifestos, protocols, and speculative state-design

- **1895:** *Address to the Rothschilds* / *Rede an die Rothschilds*. An extensive memorandum drafted to persuade the Rothschild family to support a Jewish national project. It remained an address or working political text rather than an independently published book during Herzl’s life and survives through his papers and diaries. ([Herzl archive](https://www.herzlonline.org/en/the-theodor-herzl-archive/))
- **1896:** *Der Judenstaat: Versuch einer modernen Lösung der Judenfrage*. A political manifesto and operational prospectus: it does not merely describe a state but proposes institutions, financing, migration, and diplomatic action intended to bring one about. ([Deutsches Textarchiv record via Wikisource](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Der_Judenstaat); [Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **17 January 1896:** *A Solution of the Jewish Question*. An English newspaper presentation of Herzl’s program in the *Jewish Chronicle*. Whether it should be counted as a separate obra or as an anticipatory manifestation of *Der Judenstaat* is bibliographically debatable. ([contemporary-publication reference](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sionisme))
- **1897:** *Der Baseler Congress*. Herzl’s published account of the First Zionist Congress: reportage, institutional self-description, and movement-building document at once. ([digitized-work bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1897:** *Basler Programm*. A manifesto-resolution adopted by the First Zionist Congress. Herzl shaped the congress and its objective, but the adopted wording was a collective and negotiated creation, so sole authorship should not be assigned to him. ([World Zionist Organization history](https://www.wzo.org.il/sub/39th-zionist-congress/history/en))
- **1897–1903:** *Zionist Congress addresses*. Six principal presidential or opening addresses were later collected as *Sechs Kongressreden*. They are performative political texts—speeches that organized and directed a movement—and the 1914 collection is a posthumous editorial object, not a book Herzl himself issued. ([University of Chicago collection description](https://storage.lib.uchicago.edu/pres/2007/pres2007-0007.pdf))
- **1902:** *Altneuland*. A utopian novel but also a political and social design project: its imagined “New Society” gives narrative form to technological, civic, economic, and intercommunal institutions. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml); [digitized-work bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1902–1903:** *El-Arish/Sinai settlement project*. A diplomatic and technical colonization project pursued with the British government and investigated on the ground. It produced memoranda and negotiations but not a completed settlement; the unfinished project itself is the work. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **1903–1904:** *East Africa project* (“Uganda Plan”). A proposed emergency territory under British auspices, brought by Herzl before the Sixth Zionist Congress. It was a contested provisional project rather than his final replacement for the Palestine program, and it never became a settlement. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))

### Periodical and editorial creation

- **1897:** *Die Welt*. A Zionist periodical founded by Herzl as an organ for the movement. It is both an ongoing collective publication and an institutional manifestation; Herzl made its editorial project but did not author everything printed in it. ([Wikisource biographical overview](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl); [Herzl overview](https://projekt-gutenberg.org/authors/theodor-herzl/))
- **1880s–1904:** *Newspaper feuilletons, correspondence, criticism, and reportage*. Herzl made a very large dispersed corpus for papers including the *Neue Freie Presse*. It resists enumeration as a finite set of “works”: later volumes select and rearrange individual newspaper manifestations, and no consulted scholarly source claims a complete item-level bibliography of every contribution. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml); [Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118550241.html#ndbcontent))

### Diaries, letters, and archival writing

- **1895–1904:** *Tagebücher*. A sustained manuscript diary that became both a private record and a workshop for Herzl’s political project. The three-volume 1922–1923 publication and later *Complete Diaries* are posthumous editorial constructions; deleted passages and the physical notebooks make the archive larger than any single printed edition. ([Central Zionist Archives digitization notice](https://herzl.haifa.ac.il/images/Item_31267259_20260112114354.pdf); [edition history](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1880s–1904:** *Briefe*. A large correspondence corpus rather than one authored book. The seven-volume *Briefe und Tagebücher* edited by Alex Bein, Hermann Greive, Moshe Schaerf, and Julius H. Schoeps is the principal scholarly edition, but its organization and titles are editorial, and the individual letters remain occasion-bound objects. ([edition citation](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Schriften); [Herzl Archive](https://www.herzlonline.org/en/the-theodor-herzl-archive/))
- **1890s–1904:** *Diplomatic memoranda, petitions, draft charters, audience notes, organizational circulars, and minutes*. These are protocols and instruments of action dispersed through Herzl’s papers and correspondence. They resist being called separate literary works, yet omitting them would erase much of what he actually made as a political organizer. ([Herzl Archive](https://www.herzlonline.org/en/the-theodor-herzl-archive/); [Central Zionist Archives collection guide](https://www.zionistarchives.org.il/collections/Documents/%D7%9E%D7%93%D7%A8%D7%99%D7%9A%20%D7%90%D7%A8%D7%9B%D7%99%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%99%D7%9D%20%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A9%D7%99%D7%99%D7%9D%202018.pdf))

### Organizations and collective projects

- **August 1897:** *First Zionist Congress*. A convened political event made by Herzl with approximately two hundred delegates from seventeen countries. It is a manifestation and institutional performance rather than a text, and its resolutions were collectively produced. ([World Zionist Organization history](https://www.wzo.org.il/sub/39th-zionist-congress/history/en))
- **1897:** *Zionist Organization* (later *World Zionist Organization*). A continuing political institution founded at the First Congress on Herzl’s initiative. Its collective authorship and later transformation mean it cannot be reduced to a personal “work,” but institution-building was central to Herzl’s obra. ([World Zionist Organization history](https://www.wzo.org.il/sub/39th-zionist-congress/history/en))
- **1898–1899:** *Jewish Colonial Trust*. A financial institution promoted by Herzl and the Zionist movement to supply capital for the national project. It was collectively incorporated and therefore should not be attributed to Herzl alone. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml); [World Zionist Organization history](https://www.wzo.org.il/sub/39th-zionist-congress/history/en))
- **1901:** *Keren Kayemeth LeIsrael / Jewish National Fund*. A land-purchasing institution established by delegates at the Fifth Zionist Congress within the organization Herzl led. It belongs to his institutional project but is a collective creation, not his sole obra. ([World Zionist Organization](https://www.wzo.org.il/page/zionist-congress-38/kkl/en))
- **1903:** *Anglo-Palestine Company*. The Palestine subsidiary of the Jewish Colonial Trust, established during Herzl’s leadership. It is an institutional consequence of his program, though its corporate creation was collective. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))

### Posthumous editorial objects—not new works by Herzl

- **1905:** *Zionistische Schriften*. A posthumous collection edited by Leon Kellner. Its constituent writings are Herzl’s, but the selection, ordering, title, and book-object are the editor’s manifestation. ([Wikisource bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1922–1923:** *Theodor Herzls Tagebücher 1895–1904*. The first three-volume printed diary edition; it witnesses the diary but is not identical with Herzl’s manuscript object. ([Wikisource bibliography](https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl))
- **1934–1935:** *Gesammelte zionistische Werke*. A five-volume posthumous collected-works edition and the principal early basis for defining Herzl’s Zionist textual corpus. The collection is an editorial obra containing Herzl’s works, not itself a work he made. ([Österreichisches Biographisches Lexikon](https://www.biographien.ac.at/oebl/oebl_H/Herzl_Theodor_1860_1904.xml))
- **1983–1996:** *Briefe und Tagebücher*. A seven-volume critical edition edited by Alex Bein, Hermann Greive, Moshe Schaerf, and Julius H. Schoeps. It is indispensable evidence for Herzl’s correspondence and diaries but remains a later scholarly construction. ([edition record](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodor_Herzl#Schriften))
