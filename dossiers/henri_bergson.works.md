## Works by Henri-Louis Bergson

### Books, theses, and author-made collections

- **1884:** *Extraits de Lucrèce*. A pedagogical edition of selected passages from *De rerum natura*, accompanied by Bergson’s study of Lucretius’s text, poetry, and philosophy; it is partly an editorial object and partly an original critical work. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1888:** *Quid Aristoteles de loco senserit*. Bergson’s complementary doctoral thesis, written in Latin, on Aristotle’s conception of place; it was later translated into French by Rose-Marie Mossé-Bastide. Some bibliographies date it 1889 from the doctoral publication rather than 1888 from its submission. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1889:** *Essai sur les données immédiates de la conscience*. Bergson’s principal doctoral thesis and first independent philosophical book, concerned with duration, intensity, and freedom. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1896:** *Matière et mémoire. Essai sur la relation du corps à l’esprit*. A philosophical monograph on perception, memory, action, and the relation between mind and body. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1900:** *Le Rire. Essai sur la signification du comique*. A book made by revising and joining three articles first published in the *Revue de Paris* in 1899; its identity is therefore both serial essay and author-fashioned monograph. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1907:** *L’Évolution créatrice*. A philosophical monograph joining metaphysics, evolutionary biology, and a critique of mechanistic and finalist accounts of life. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1919:** *L’Énergie spirituelle. Essais et conférences*. An author-arranged collection of seven previously delivered or published studies; the collection is an obra, but its components remain separately made lectures and essays rather than chapters originally composed for one book. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1922:** *Durée et simultanéité. À propos de la théorie d’Einstein*. A philosophical intervention in relativity theory, repeatedly revised by Bergson. He later declined to authorize further reprinting, so its status within the authorized œuvre is unusually resistant: the Centenary *Œuvres* omitted it, whereas *Mélanges* restored it. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1932:** *Les Deux Sources de la morale et de la religion*. A philosophical monograph distinguishing closed and open morality, static and dynamic religion, and culminating in the figures of mysticism and creative action. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1934:** *La Pensée et le mouvant. Essais et conférences*. An author-made retrospective collection of texts dating principally from 1903–1923, furnished with a new preface and two-part introduction; it is both a late obra and a reconfiguration of earlier, independently existing texts. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)

### Independently made philosophical essays and lectures

- **1882:** *De la simulation inconsciente dans l’état d’hypnotisme*. An early psychological article on hypnotic behaviour, subsequently preserved in *Mélanges*. [Cambridge bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/interpreting-bergson/bibliography/E7CA513249482CF8AF045B4A6C0463CE)
- **1895:** *Le Bon Sens et les études classiques*. A prize-distribution address: an occasional public speech rather than a philosophical book, although later bibliographies treat it as a distinct work. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1901:** *Le Rêve*. A lecture delivered to the Institut général psychologique on 26 March 1901 and later revised for *L’Énergie spirituelle*. Its first manifestation was oral performance, not a book. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1902:** *L’Effort intellectuel*. A journal study of intellectual work, imagery, and mental schemas, later incorporated into *L’Énergie spirituelle*. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1903:** *Introduction à la métaphysique*. A journal essay defining intuition and analysis; it later became the central earlier text in *La Pensée et le mouvant*. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1904:** *Le Paralogisme psycho-physiologique*. A congress paper subsequently published in the *Revue de métaphysique et de morale* and later revised as *Le Cerveau et la pensée : une illusion philosophique*. The two titles designate successive manifestations of substantially the same argument, not safely separable books. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1904:** *La Vie et l’œuvre de Ravaisson*. A commemorative philosophical study, first made for the Académie des sciences morales et politiques and later included in *La Pensée et le mouvant*. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1908:** *Le Souvenir du présent et la fausse reconnaissance*. A journal study of déjà vu and memory, later incorporated into *L’Énergie spirituelle*. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1911:** *La Conscience et la vie* / *Life and Consciousness*. The Huxley Lecture delivered at Birmingham on 29 May 1911, initially manifested in English and later revised in French for *L’Énergie spirituelle*; title, language, and printed form vary across its manifestations. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1911:** *L’Intuition philosophique*. A lecture delivered to the International Congress of Philosophy at Bologna on 10 April 1911 and later collected in *La Pensée et le mouvant*. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1911:** *La Perception du changement*. Two lectures delivered at Oxford on 26 and 27 May 1911; together they form a paired oral work subsequently printed in *La Pensée et le mouvant*. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1911:** *Sur le pragmatisme de William James. Vérité et réalité*. An essay first made as Bergson’s preface to the French translation of William James’s *Pragmatism* and later detached and collected as a work in its own right. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1912:** *L’Âme et le corps*. A public lecture delivered to “Foi et Vie” on 28 April 1912 and later revised for *L’Énergie spirituelle*; its first object was a spoken address. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1913:** *Fantômes de vivants et recherche psychique*. Bergson’s presidential address to the Society for Psychical Research on 28 May 1913, subsequently collected in *L’Énergie spirituelle*. It is an institutional address investigating testimony and psychical research, not a freestanding treatise. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/energie_spirituelle/energie_spirituelle_tdm.html)
- **1913:** *La Philosophie de Claude Bernard*. A centenary address delivered at the Collège de France on 30 December 1913 and later included in *La Pensée et le mouvant*. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1913:** *Lettre sur le jury de cour d’assises*. A public letter printed in *Le Temps* on 15 October 1913; it is an intervention in civic practice rather than a systematic philosophical work. [Cambridge bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/interpreting-bergson/bibliography/E7CA513249482CF8AF045B4A6C0463CE)
- **1920:** *Le Possible et le réel*. A lecture first delivered at Oxford under the title *Prediction and Novelty* and published in revised form in Swedish in 1930 before entering *La Pensée et le mouvant*. Its date and title resist a single answer because performance, first publication, and final French recension occurred at different times. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1922:** *De la position des problèmes*. The second part of the introduction later printed in *La Pensée et le mouvant*; Bergson dates its composition January 1922, making it an independent late methodological essay even though its first canonical placement is inside the 1934 collection. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)
- **1934:** *Croissance de la vérité. Mouvement rétrograde du vrai*. The first part of the new introduction to *La Pensée et le mouvant*, composed for that collection and therefore both an essay and part of the architecture of the book. [Table of contents](https://classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/bergson_henri/pensee_mouvant/pensee_mouvant_tdm.html)

### Public, political, and ceremonial interventions

- **1885:** *Discours sur la politesse*. A school prize-day address. It is an occasional spoken act preserved as a text, and therefore resists assimilation to Bergson’s philosophical books. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1914:** *La Force qui s’use et celle qui ne s’use pas*. A wartime article published in the *Bulletin des armées de la République* on 4 November 1914; it acts rhetorically within a mobilized national public and approaches a manifesto more than a neutral description. [Biographical chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)
- **1914:** *La Signification de la guerre*. A wartime address to the Académie des sciences morales et politiques, casting the conflict as one between force and right. Its institutional and persuasive function makes it a public act as well as a text. [Bergson bibliography](https://www.donaldlandes.com/post/bergson-bibliography)
- **1915:** *L’Évolution de l’impérialisme allemand*. A presidential address to the Académie des sciences morales et politiques; it is a wartime political interpretation, not a chapter of Bergson’s metaphysics. [Biographical chronology](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Bergson)
- **1915:** *La Philosophie française*. An expository and patriotic essay produced during the First World War, presenting a national philosophical tradition; its circumstances give it an interventionist function. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1917–1918:** *Mes missions*. Bergson’s reports or recollections of diplomatic missions, especially his approaches to the United States during the First World War. Published posthumously in 1947, this is not a book completed and issued by Bergson himself, so its editorial status must be distinguished from his authorized works. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1918:** *Discours de réception à l’Académie française*. The speech delivered on 24 January 1918 upon Bergson’s reception into the Académie; a ceremonial performance and institutional act, subsequently preserved as text. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1927:** *Discours de réception du prix Nobel*. Bergson’s Nobel banquet speech, a brief ceremonial address rather than a philosophical treatise. Its existence belongs to the public and performative corpus, not to the sequence of major books. [Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1927/bergson/facts/)
- **1939:** *Hommage à Charles Péguy*. A late commemorative text; the editors of *Mélanges* use it as one endpoint of the miscellaneous authorized corpus. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)

### Teaching projects preserved through students’ records

- **1887–1888:** *Leçons de psychologie et de métaphysique*. A Clermont-Ferrand teaching course known through students’ notes and published posthumously as *Cours I*. It is evidence of Bergson’s pedagogical project and oral creation, but not an author-prepared book or necessarily a verbatim text. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1887–1888:** *Leçons d’esthétique*. A Clermont-Ferrand lecture course reconstructed in *Cours II*. Its textual object was made jointly by Bergson’s teaching and another person’s act of transcription, so singular authorship of every sentence cannot be assumed. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1892–1893:** *Leçons de morale, psychologie et métaphysique*. A Lycée Henri-IV course preserved in student notes and published within *Cours II*; it is a pedagogical project whose surviving textual manifestation is editorial and documentary. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1892–1893:** *Leçons d’histoire de la philosophie moderne*. A Lycée Henri-IV course subsequently reconstructed in *Cours III*. Like the other school courses, it resists being called a book made by Bergson, because the extant wording depends upon auditors’ notes. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1892–1893:** *Théorie de l’âme*. A course or course-unit preserved and edited in *Cours III*; its boundaries are those of a teaching sequence rather than an author-published work. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1898:** *Cours sur Fichte*. An École normale supérieure course published from documentary records in 1988. The posthumous edition manifests Bergson’s teaching project but should not be mistaken for a book he prepared. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1900–1901:** *Cours sur la philosophie grecque*. A body of oral teaching published posthumously as *Cours IV*; the collected title denotes reconstructed lectures rather than a finished authorial manuscript. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1902–1903:** *Histoire de l’idée de temps*. A Collège de France course published posthumously in 2016. Its modern edition preserves an oral research-and-teaching project, while the mediated transcription prevents treating it unqualifiedly as Bergson’s own finished prose. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1903–1904:** *Histoire des théories de la mémoire*. A Collège de France course published posthumously in 2018; it is a documented teaching event and intellectual project rather than an author-authorized book. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)
- **1904–1905:** *L’Évolution du problème de la liberté*. A Collège de France course published posthumously in 2017. It survives through lecture records and therefore belongs simultaneously to Bergson’s work and to the later editors’ reconstruction of it. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)

### Translations and editorial constructions

- **1883:** *James Sully, Les Illusions des sens et de l’esprit*. Bergson’s French translation of Sully’s English psychological study. The translated wording is something Bergson made, but the underlying argument and work are Sully’s, so it resists classification as an original Bergsonian obra. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1957–1959:** *Écrits et paroles*. Rose-Marie Mossé-Bastide’s three-volume posthumous collection of Bergson’s dispersed writings, speeches, and letters. It is an indispensable editorial manifestation of the corpus, but not a collection designed or authorized in this form by Bergson. [Académie française](https://www.academie-francaise.fr/les-immortels/henri-bergson)
- **1959:** *Œuvres, édition du Centenaire*. André Robinet’s posthumous collected edition, prefaced by Henri Gouhier, containing the principal books published during Bergson’s lifetime but excluding the Latin thesis and *Durée et simultanéité*. It is a scholarly container, not a new work Bergson made. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1972:** *Mélanges*. A posthumous scholarly corpus of texts published or authorized by Bergson but not incorporated into his principal books and collections, extending from early school and mathematical writings to the 1939 Péguy homage. Its editors, not Bergson, made the volume and its boundaries. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **2011:** *Écrits philosophiques*. A modern critical edition of Bergson’s philosophical writings. It is a scholarly remanifestation useful for establishing texts and variants, not an additional Bergsonian obra. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/)

### Correspondence and resistant documentary works

- **Throughout Bergson’s adult life; collected 2002:** *Correspondances*. An extensive body of letters rather than a single composed work: each letter is an occasional communicative act, while the published volume is an editorial construction. Earlier *Mélanges* printed only part of this corpus, and additional letters had appeared separately, so completeness has changed over time and cannot be silently assumed. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.sydney.edu.au/entries/bergson/), [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Firmin Abauzit*. A surviving correspondence subsequently published in a scholarly journal; it is a documentary series, not a single author-fashioned work. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **1914–1940:** *Correspondence with Ralph Tyler Flewelling*. A long exchange published posthumously; its dialogic authorship means that the correspondence as a whole was made by two participants, although Bergson made his individual letters. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Élie Halévy*. A dispersed epistolary group published in Halévy’s collected work; it resists the status of a discrete Bergsonian obra because its surviving unity is editorial. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Vladimir Jankélévitch*. A small surviving group, including three letters published in facsimile; the facsimiles are manifestations of private acts, not books Bergson intended to publish. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Xavier Léon and others*. A posthumously assembled correspondence documenting Bergson’s relations with the *Revue de métaphysique et de morale* milieu; its boundaries belong to its editor. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Alfred Loisy*. A separately published epistolary group concerning, among other matters, religion and Bergson’s reception; it is archival evidence rather than an author-declared work. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Correspondence with Lucien Lévy-Bruhl*. A dialogic archival object published posthumously; neither its later title nor its selection was made by Bergson. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Eugène Minkowski*. Seven known letters published with a study of Minkowski; they are individual interventions subsequently made into a documentary set by editors. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Correspondence with Maurice Pradines*. A small posthumously published epistolary group whose identity as one “work” is editorial rather than authorial. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to Georges Sorel*. A published group of letters whose historical importance does not remove their resistance to being classified as a unified work. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
- **Dates vary:** *Letters to André Suarès*. Private communications later printed within an edition of Suarès; each letter is a made text, but the named group is a posthumous editorial creation. [Frédéric Worms, “Bibliographie indicative”](https://www.psychaanalyse.com/pdf/philosophe_BERGSON_Bibliographie.pdf)
