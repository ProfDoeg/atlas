## Works by David ben Jesse

### Psalms attributed to David in the Masoretic Psalter

The Masoretic Text heads the following seventy-three compositions *le-David*, a phrase that can denote authorship, association, dedication, or membership in a Davidic collection. Their individual dates are unknown; modern scholarship generally treats the superscriptions as later paratexts rather than proof that the historical David composed the poems.

- *Psalm 3*. A poetic prayer attributed to David and narratively associated by its superscription with his flight from Absalom.
- *Psalm 4*. A psalmic prayer attributed to David; no occasion or date is supplied.
- *Psalm 5*. A psalmic prayer attributed to David; no occasion or date is supplied.
- *Psalm 6*. A petitionary psalm attributed to David; its musical directions make it also a performance text.
- *Psalm 7*. A lament attributed to David and connected in its superscription with an otherwise unidentified “Cush the Benjaminite.”
- *Psalm 8*. A hymn attributed to David; its musical direction identifies it as a performed composition.
- *Psalm 9*. A song attributed to David. Its textual relationship with Psalm 10—together they form an incomplete alphabetic acrostic—makes its boundaries as a separate obra uncertain.
- *Psalm 11*. A psalm attributed to David; no occasion or date is supplied.
- *Psalm 12*. A psalm attributed to David and furnished with a musical direction.
- *Psalm 13*. A lament and prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 14*. A reflective psalm attributed to David; Psalm 53 preserves a closely related recension, so the composition exists in more than one textual manifestation.
- *Psalm 15*. A liturgical or entrance psalm attributed to David.
- *Psalm 16*. A *miktam* attributed to David; the precise genre meant by *miktam* is uncertain.
- *Psalm 17*. A prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 18*. A lengthy victory or thanksgiving song attributed to David and linked with deliverance from Saul and other enemies. Second Samuel 22 preserves substantially the same obra in another textual setting and recension.
- *Psalm 19*. A hymn and prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 20*. A royal prayer attributed to David; whether David is imagined as author, dedicatee, or king prayed for is uncertain.
- *Psalm 21*. A royal thanksgiving attributed to David, with the same uncertainty about the force of the attribution.
- *Psalm 22*. A lament attributed to David and supplied with a musical direction.
- *Psalm 23*. A song of trust attributed to David.
- *Psalm 24*. A liturgical hymn attributed to David.
- *Psalm 25*. An alphabetic prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 26*. A prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 27*. A prayer and song of trust attributed to David.
- *Psalm 28*. A petition and thanksgiving attributed to David.
- *Psalm 29*. A hymn attributed to David.
- *Psalm 30*. A song attributed to David and designated for the dedication of “the house”; which house and whether this notice belongs to the poem’s original setting are disputed.
- *Psalm 31*. A lament and thanksgiving attributed to David.
- *Psalm 32*. A *maskil* attributed to David; the exact genre or performance sense of *maskil* is uncertain.
- *Psalm 34*. An alphabetic poem attributed to David and linked by its superscription with his feigned madness before Abimelech, although Samuel calls the Philistine king Achish.
- *Psalm 35*. A petition attributed to David.
- *Psalm 36*. A composition attributed to David, who is additionally styled the deity’s servant.
- *Psalm 37*. An alphabetic wisdom poem attributed to David.
- *Psalm 38*. A memorial petition attributed to David; the precise ritual function indicated by its heading is uncertain.
- *Psalm 39*. A psalm attributed to David and directed to Jeduthun, making it a text conceived for musical realization.
- *Psalm 40*. A thanksgiving and petition attributed to David.
- *Psalm 41*. A psalm attributed to David and the concluding composition of the Psalter’s first book.
- *Psalm 51*. A penitential poem attributed to David and connected by a later narrative superscription with Nathan’s confrontation after David’s relations with Bathsheba.
- *Psalm 52*. A *maskil* attributed to David and associated with Doeg’s report to Saul.
- *Psalm 53*. A *maskil* attributed to David. It is a variant manifestation of substantially the same composition as Psalm 14 rather than an entirely independent invention.
- *Psalm 54*. A *maskil* attributed to David and connected with the Ziphites’ disclosure of his location to Saul.
- *Psalm 55*. A *maskil* attributed to David and supplied with musical directions.
- *Psalm 56*. A *miktam* attributed to David and associated with his seizure by Philistines at Gath.
- *Psalm 57*. A *miktam* attributed to David and linked with his flight from Saul into a cave.
- *Psalm 58*. A *miktam* attributed to David and furnished with a musical direction.
- *Psalm 59*. A *miktam* attributed to David and linked with Saul’s attempt to have his house watched so that he could be killed.
- *Psalm 60*. A *miktam* attributed to David, designated for instruction, and attached to a campaign against Aram and Edom; its heading does not correspond neatly to a single extant prose account.
- *Psalm 61*. A psalm attributed to David and written as a performance text for stringed accompaniment.
- *Psalm 62*. A psalm attributed to David and directed to Jeduthun.
- *Psalm 63*. A psalm attributed to David and situated by its heading in the wilderness of Judah.
- *Psalm 64*. A psalm attributed to David.
- *Psalm 65*. A song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 68*. A song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 69*. A song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 70*. A memorial petition attributed to David; it also survives, with variations, as the closing portion of Psalm 40.
- *Psalm 86*. A prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 101*. A psalm attributed to David, conventionally described as a royal declaration or program.
- *Psalm 103*. A hymn attributed to David.
- *Psalm 108*. A song attributed to David and assembled substantially from material also preserved in Psalms 57 and 60; it therefore resists classification as wholly new composition.
- *Psalm 109*. A psalm attributed to David.
- *Psalm 110*. A psalm attributed to David, although its opening addresses “my lord”; the relation between attributed speaker, king, and addressee remains disputed.
- *Psalm 122*. A pilgrimage song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 124*. A pilgrimage song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 131*. A pilgrimage song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 133*. A pilgrimage song attributed to David.
- *Psalm 138*. A thanksgiving attributed to David.
- *Psalm 139*. A prayer attributed to David.
- *Psalm 140*. A psalm attributed to David.
- *Psalm 141*. A psalm attributed to David.
- *Psalm 142*. A *maskil* and prayer attributed to David and associated with his presence in a cave.
- *Psalm 143*. A psalm attributed to David.
- *Psalm 144*. A psalm attributed to David. It reworks language found elsewhere in the Psalter and is therefore a composite or tradition-dependent obra.
- *Psalm 145*. An alphabetic praise poem attributed to David.

### Poems and liturgies placed directly in David’s mouth

- **Narrative setting: before David’s kingship:** *Lyre performances for Saul*. First Samuel presents David repeatedly playing for Saul, but preserves neither titles nor musical notation. These are performances or improvisations, not recoverable fixed texts, and resist enumeration as individual works.
- **Narrative setting: after the deaths of Saul and Jonathan:** *The Lament of the Bow*. A funeral poem that 2 Samuel 1:17–27 expressly says David uttered and ordered taught to Judah; its traditional Davidic authorship is plausible to some scholars but remains historically unprovable.
- **Narrative setting: after Abner’s death:** *Lament for Abner*. A short funeral poem placed in David’s mouth in 2 Samuel 3:33–34; no independent title or date survives.
- **Narrative setting: late in David’s reign:** *David’s Song of Deliverance*. The victory or thanksgiving song in 2 Samuel 22, also transmitted as Psalm 18. These are two recensions or manifestations of one obra, not securely two separate compositions.
- **Narrative setting: David’s final period:** *The Last Words of David*. The poetic oracle in 2 Samuel 23:1–7 presents David as an inspired speaker and “pleasant singer” or “favorite of the songs” of Israel; whether it preserves David’s own words is disputed.
- **Narrative setting: installation of the ark in Jerusalem:** *Thanksgiving assigned to Asaph and his associates*. First Chronicles 16:7–36 says David first appointed or delivered this liturgy, which combines material now found in Psalms 105, 96, and 106. David may be represented as author, compiler, commissioner, or liturgical initiator; the text does not permit a silent choice among those roles.
- **Narrative setting: David’s final assembly:** *Prayer before the assembly*. First Chronicles 29:10–19 places this public blessing and petition in David’s mouth. It is a liturgical speech preserved only within the Chronicler’s much later narrative, not a securely datable autograph.

### Additional psalmic traditions

- **Second Temple period manuscript:** *Psalm 151A*. A Hebrew Davidic poem in the Great Psalms Scroll from Qumran, later adapted as part of Greek Psalm 151. Its first-person Davidic voice is a literary attribution, not evidence of composition by the historical king.
- **Second Temple period manuscript:** *Psalm 151B*. A fragmentary Hebrew poem about David and Goliath in the Great Psalms Scroll, also incorporated into Greek Psalm 151. Its completeness, original extent, and historical authorship are uncertain.
- **Second Temple period manuscript:** *David’s Compositions*. The prose catalogue in 11Q5/11QPsᵃ credits David with 3,600 psalms, 364 daily songs, 52 Sabbath songs, 30 festival songs, and four songs for the afflicted, totaling 4,050 compositions. This is an idealized catalogue of a largely unattested corpus, not 4,050 identifiable surviving obras.
- **Septuagintal form:** *Psalm 151*. A Greek composition formed from material related to Hebrew Psalms 151A–B and explicitly placed outside the numbered 150 psalms. It is Davidic pseudepigraphy or reception rather than a securely historical work.
- **Later Greek attribution:** *Additional Davidic psalms of the Septuagint*. The Greek Psalter attaches David’s name to more psalms than the Masoretic Text does, producing a corpus of about eighty-four Davidic headings. Because these are variant paratextual attributions rather than separately surviving works by David, they resist inclusion as a second set of historical compositions.

### Instruments, structures, and architectural projects

- **Narrative setting: David’s settlement in Jerusalem:** *David’s houses*. First Chronicles 15:1 says David made houses for himself in the City of David, while 2 Samuel 5:11 credits Tyrian builders with constructing his cedar palace. This is therefore best classified as a commissioned building complex rather than David’s personal fabrication.
- **Narrative setting: after Jerusalem’s capture:** *Expansion of the City of David*. Second Samuel 5:9 credits David with building around the captured stronghold from the Millo inward. The archaeological identification, extent, and personal agency behind this royal building program remain disputed.
- **Narrative setting: transfer of the ark:** *Tent for the Ark*. David prepared a place and pitched a tent for the ark in Jerusalem. It was a temporary cult structure, not the later temple.
- **Narrative setting: plague and purchase of the threshing floor:** *Altar on Araunah’s/Ornan’s threshing floor*. Second Samuel 24 and 1 Chronicles 21 say David built this altar; Chronicles makes the site the future location of the temple.
- **Unfinished royal project:** *Temple for YHWH*. David intends to build it but is prohibited, so the temple as David’s obra exists only as project, preparation, and delegated intention; Solomon is its builder.
- **Narrative setting: David’s final years:** *Plan of the Jerusalem Temple and its furnishings*. First Chronicles 28 says David handed Solomon written plans for the building, courts, chambers, treasuries, and vessels, while also presenting those plans as divinely communicated. It is simultaneously architectural protocol, revelation, and royal project; its attribution comes from the postexilic Chronicler and is not independently verifiable.
- **Narrative setting: David’s final years:** *Temple materials and workforce preparations*. First Chronicles 22 and 29 credit David with accumulating stone, metal, timber, and treasure and arranging labor for a building he would not complete. This is an unfinished project infrastructure rather than a building.
- **Narrative setting: David’s final years:** *Musical instruments for worship*. First Chronicles 23:5 represents David as having made instruments for praise; later passages call them “David’s instruments.” Their number, shapes, and degree of David’s own craftsmanship are not preserved.
- **Narrative setting: David’s final years:** *Priestly and Levitical divisions*. First Chronicles 23–26 attributes to David, working with priests, Levites, commanders, and lots, an administrative system for priests, musicians, gatekeepers, treasurers, and officials. This institutional protocol is a collective creation and is widely understood as part of Chronicles’ later projection of temple organization back onto David.
- **Narrative setting: installation of the ark:** *Jerusalem’s organized musical cult*. First Chronicles 15–16 credits David with appointing singers, instrumentalists, gatekeepers, and continuing ministers before the ark. It is a performative institution—a continuing body of people, offices, music, and ritual—rather than a single text or object.
