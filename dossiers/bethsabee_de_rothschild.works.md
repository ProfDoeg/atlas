## Works by Bethsabée

### Published texts

- **1949:** *La danse artistique aux U.S.A. : tendances modernes*. A 160-page study of American modern dance, published in Paris by Éditions Elzévir with a preface by Pierre Tugal; this is de Rothschild’s only separately published book recorded in the Rothschild Archive’s standard bibliography. [[1]](https://family.rothschildarchive.org/bibliography?person_id=113) [[2]](https://www.rothschildarchive.org/family/family_interests/the_arts)
- **1988:** *Bat-Dor Repertoire Highlights*. An illustrated or documentary survey in *Batmatdor*, no. 3, rather than a freestanding book; the bibliography’s irregular pagination (“2–3, 47–46, 42”) is retained as a catalogue ambiguity. [[1]](https://family.rothschildarchive.org/bibliography?person_id=113)
- **1990:** *A Tribute to Jeannette Ordman*. A short commemorative text, pages 76–81 of Dora Sowden’s compiled volume *Bat-Dor: A Tribute to Jeannette Ordman*; it is an authored contribution, not authorship of the whole book. [[1]](https://family.rothschildarchive.org/bibliography?person_id=113)

### Unpublished and incompletely catalogued writings

- **Undated:** *Essays, poems and memoirs*. The Rothschild Archive records these collectively among her papers without supplying individual titles, dates, or a count. They therefore resist treatment as separately identifiable works: the surviving archival corpus exists, but its completeness and internal boundaries cannot be established from the published finding aid. [[3]](https://www.rothschildarchive.org/materials/ar2002.pdf)
- **1950–1993:** *Correspondence with dancers*. An archival body of letters rather than a fixed work; the finding aid records the date range but neither enumerates the letters nor distinguishes her outgoing texts from letters she received, so individual authorship cannot safely be assigned. [[3]](https://www.rothschildarchive.org/materials/ar2002.pdf)

### Foundations and cultural organizations

- **Early 1950s:** *Bethsabée de Rothschild Foundation for the Arts and Sciences*. A philanthropic and producing institution founded in New York: an institutional obra whose first reported project was a 1953 Broadway festival of American modern dance. Sources variously associate the similarly named Israeli activities with her later move, so the precise continuity between the American foundation and subsequent Israeli bodies is not fully clear. [[4]](https://www.sfchronicle.com/news/article/Batsheva-de-Rothschild-2935048.php) [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
- **1958; registered in Israel 1965:** *Batsheva de Rothschild Fund for the Advancement of Science in Israel*. A private endowment designed as a flexible instrument for basic research, seminars, workshops, visiting fellows, and research travel; de Rothschild headed it until her death and transferred it to the Israel Academy of Sciences and Humanities in 1993. The Academy gives 1958 as its New York foundation date, 1965 as its Israeli registration date, and 1963–2022 as its operational span. [[6]](https://www.academy.ac.il/RichText/GeneralPage.aspx?nodeId=849)
- **After 1956; exact date not established:** *Batsheva Society for the Arts*. A cultural-support organization created after de Rothschild settled in Israel, especially associated with ethnic artists, Maskit, the Chamber Music Association, and the Chamber Ensemble. It is an enabling institution rather than a discrete artwork. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)

### Dance institutions and environments

- **1963–1964:** *Batsheva Dance Company*. De Rothschild conceived the company in 1963 and established it in 1964, inviting Martha Graham to serve as artistic adviser and then acting as chief executive and patron for its first eleven years. The company is a continuing collective obra, not a choreography by de Rothschild; contemporary and retrospective sources sometimes call 1963 its founding year because preparation preceded the 1964 public establishment. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/) [[7]](https://archive.batsheva.co.il/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/1419422021k21Fn.pdf)
- **1967 or 1968:** *Bat-Dor Dance School*. A professional school created with Jeannette Ordman as director and conceived as infrastructure for classical and modern dance. Sources disagree between 1967 and 1968, possibly reflecting planning, opening, or institutional registration; the Batsheva company’s own history gives 1968. [[2]](https://www.rothschildarchive.org/family/family_interests/the_arts) [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
- **1967 or after the school’s establishment in 1968:** *Bat-Dor Dance Company*. A dance company formed by de Rothschild with Ordman, who supplied its artistic direction; it should therefore be treated as a co-created institutional project rather than as de Rothschild’s personal choreography. Accounts disagree on whether the company dates from 1967 or followed the school in 1968. [[4]](https://www.sfchronicle.com/news/article/Batsheva-de-Rothschild-2935048.php) [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
- **After 1968; exact date not established:** *Bat-Dor center in Tel Aviv*. A purpose-built home for the school and company commissioned or financed by de Rothschild. It is part architectural environment and part performing-arts infrastructure, but the consulted sources do not name its architect or justify assigning the building’s architectural authorship to her. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
- **1975:** *Proposed consolidation of Batsheva and Bat-Dor*. An unrealized institutional project rather than a completed work; the attempted merger failed, after which de Rothschild ended her support for Batsheva. Its failure is part of its status and should not be silently converted into a completed organization. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)

### Produced manifestations and cultural programs

- **1953:** *Festival of American Modern Dance*. A two-week Broadway festival sponsored as the first project of her Foundation for the Arts and Sciences. It was a produced manifestation involving many artists, not a dance authored or choreographed by de Rothschild. [[4]](https://www.sfchronicle.com/news/article/Batsheva-de-Rothschild-2935048.php)
- **1956 and subsequently:** *Martha Graham Dance Company tours in Israel*. De Rothschild extended the company’s Asian tour to Israel in 1956 and organized two later Israeli tours. These were curatorial and producing acts whose performed dances remained the works of their choreographers. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
- **After 1956; individual dates and titles not established:** *Israeli dance courses*. A recurring educational program organized by de Rothschild under the artistic direction of Martha Graham and Antony Tudor. The courses are collective manifestations rather than securely bounded authored works. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
- **After 1956; individual titles not established:** *Israeli-music recording and Hebrew-translation programs*. De Rothschild sponsored recordings of Israeli music and translations of literary works into Hebrew, but the consulted institutional history does not enumerate them or credit her as composer, performer, translator, or textual author. They belong to her producing program, not to a falsely enlarged personal bibliography. [[5]](https://batsheva.co.il/en/about/batsheva-de-rothschild/)
