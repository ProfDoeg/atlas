## Works by Motecuhzoma Xocoyotzin

### Royal architecture and collections

- **1502–1520:** *The New Houses of Motecuhzoma* (*Casas Nuevas*). A vast palace and administrative complex commissioned after his accession, containing residential, judicial, treasury, storage, workshop, and ceremonial spaces; only foundations and reused fragments survive beneath and around Mexico’s National Palace. This was a collective royal obra rather than architecture physically made by Motecuhzoma himself, and its precise plan remains reconstructive. [McEwan and López Luján](https://www.mesoweb.com/es/articulos/sub/Moctezuma.pdf)

- **By 1519:** *Totocalli* (“House of Birds”). An aviary, animal collection, garden, and production establishment associated with the palace, described by Cortés and other conquest-era witnesses. It resists classification as a single work: it was simultaneously architecture, living collection, workshop, spectacle, and state institution, and may have developed across several reigns rather than having been created wholly by Motecuhzoma. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/resource/gdcwdl.wdl_19994/?sp=1&st=gallery)

- **1502–1520:** *Coateocalli* or *Coacalco* (“Temple of Diverse Gods”). Diego Durán says Motecuhzoma ordered this sanctuary for the deity-images of different peoples and provinces; it was therefore an architectural and political-religious project rather than merely a building. Its form and location are known principally through colonial testimony, so the attribution cannot be independently confirmed archaeologically. [Gran Diccionario Náhuatl, citing Durán](https://gdn.iib.unam.mx/diccionario/coateocalli)

- **1502–1520:** *The gilding and refurnishing of Huitzilopochtli’s sanctuary*. Colonial accounts attribute to Motecuhzoma a project to cover the god’s “house” with gold and furnish its interior with jade and quetzal feathers. This was an alteration of an inherited temple, not the creation of the Templo Mayor itself, and the report is preserved through later narrative rather than surviving fabric. [Miguel León-Portilla](https://arqueologiamexicana.mx/mexico-antiguo/el-ocaso-de-los-dioses-moctezuma-ii)

### Inscribed monuments and ritual objects

- **1503:** *Coronation Stone of Motecuhzoma II*, also called the *Stone of the Five Suns*. A carved cosmological monument dated by its inscriptions to the beginning of his reign and probably made to commemorate his coronation; it should be treated as a royal commission produced by unnamed sculptors, not as Motecuhzoma’s personal handiwork. [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/75644/coronation-stone-of-motecuhzoma-ii-stone-of-the-five-suns)

- **1502–1520:** *Hackmack Box*. A carved, lidded stone *tepetlacalli* showing Motecuhzoma performing autosacrifice and bearing calendrical and royal imagery. Its museum dates it to his reign and says he may have commissioned it, but ownership, patronage, and original ritual use—offerings, bloodletting implements, or royal ashes—remain uncertain. [MARKK Hamburg](https://markk-hamburg.de/en/objekte/lidded-box-decorated-with-reliefs-tepetlacalli/)

- **1502–1520:** An unnamed stone *tepetlacalli* bearing Motecuhzoma’s name glyph inside its lid. This surviving ritual container has been interpreted as a personal royal object, but the glyph establishes association more securely than it establishes authorship or ownership. [McEwan and López Luján](https://www.mesoweb.com/es/articulos/sub/Moctezuma.pdf)

- **1507 or later:** *Xiuhcoatl sculpture commemorating the New Fire ceremony*. A diorite fire-serpent bearing Motecuhzoma’s name glyph and the calendrical sign 2 Reed, which identifies him as patron of the 1507 ceremony. The inscription dates the event being commemorated, not necessarily the carving itself. [McEwan and López Luján](https://www.mesoweb.com/es/articulos/sub/Moctezuma.pdf)

- **1507 or later:** *Teocalli of Sacred Warfare*. A monolithic temple model—possibly a ceremonial throne—whose reliefs join Motecuhzoma, Huitzilopochtli, sacrifice, warfare, cosmic renewal, and the 2-Reed New Fire date. It is a Gesamtkunstwerk-like royal manifestation combining miniature architecture, sculpture, inscription, throne, and political theology; its exact function and date of carving remain disputed. [Museo Nacional de Antropología](https://mna.inah.gob.mx/detalle_pieza_mes.php?id=95), [Patrick Thomas Hajovsky](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7560/766686)

- **1502–1520:** *Sun Stone*, also called the *Calendar Stone*. A monumental calendrical-cosmological sculpture probably commissioned during Motecuhzoma’s reign; identification of a peripheral glyph—and, more controversially, the central face—with Motecuhzoma supports royal attribution, but both the monument’s exact function and the portrait interpretation remain debated. [David Stuart, *King and Cosmos*](https://www.mesoweb.org/publications/PMP4/PMP4.s.pdf), [Patrick Thomas Hajovsky](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7560/766686)

- **About 1519:** *Chapultepec portrait of Motecuhzoma II*. A monumental relief cut into the living rock, formerly showing the ruler with his name glyph and dates referring to his coronation, the 1507 New Fire ceremony, and perhaps 1519. It was probably his last commission, but it survives only in damaged fragments and early drawings, making its original composition partly conjectural. [McEwan and López Luján](https://www.mesoweb.com/es/articulos/sub/Moctezuma.pdf), [Patrick Thomas Hajovsky](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7560/766686)

### Ritual and institutional projects

- **1507:** *The 2-Reed New Fire ceremony*. A state-wide ritual manifestation sponsored and presided over by Motecuhzoma at the completion of a fifty-two-year calendar cycle. It was an enacted, multisensory work—architecture, fire, procession, sacrifice, speech, costume, and political renewal—rather than a stable object, and survives only through monuments and later accounts. [McEwan and López Luján](https://www.mesoweb.com/es/articulos/sub/Moctezuma.pdf), [Patrick Thomas Hajovsky](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.7560/766686)

- **1502–1520:** *Religious reforms of Motecuhzoma*. Scholars use this name for an interconnected project that reorganized cult, royal ideology, warfare, and the ruler’s identification with solar and divine powers. It resists treatment as one finished “work”: it is inferred from monuments, rituals, and colonial narratives rather than preserved as a manifesto or protocol written by Motecuhzoma. [Eduardo Matos Moctezuma](https://arqueologiamexicana.mx/mexico-antiguo/motecuhzoma-y-sus-reformas-religiosas)

### Attributed oral compositions

- **No secure date:** Songs or *cuicatl* attributed to Motecuhzoma Xocoyotzin. Later anthologies have treated him as a maker of songs concerning Tenochtitlan, Mexica power, and the death of the warrior Tlacahuepan, but no securely delimited authorial corpus survives: the extant songs were transmitted through collective performance and written down only in colonial manuscripts. Naming Motecuhzoma within a song may mark its speaker, patron, subject, or remembered ruler rather than its individual author, so these compositions resist being listed as securely authored works. [Miguel León-Portilla, *Quince poetas del mundo náhuatl*](https://enriquedussel.com/txt/Textos_200_Obras/PyF_pueblos_originarios/Quince_poetas-Miguel_Portilla.pdf), [*Cantares mexicanos: estudio y edición*](https://historicas.unam.mx/publicaciones/publicadigital/libros/cantares/cm02/38_xxxv_sin%20titulo.pdf)
