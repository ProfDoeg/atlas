## Works by Adi Shamir

### Dissertation and book-length obra

- **1976:** *The Fixedpoints of Recursive Definitions*. Shamir’s doctoral dissertation in theoretical computer science; bibliographies variously date the degree or thesis to 1976–1977.
- **1993:** *Differential Cryptanalysis of the Data Encryption Standard*. A research monograph co-authored with Eli Biham, consolidating their differential-cryptanalytic method and its application to DES.

### Cryptosystems, schemes, and protocols

- **1977–1978:** *RSA*. A public-key encryption and digital-signature scheme made with Ronald L. Rivest and Leonard M. Adleman. The construction dates from 1977; its canonical paper, “A Method for Obtaining Digital Signatures and Public-Key Cryptosystems,” appeared in 1978.
- **1979:** *Shamir’s secret-sharing scheme*. A threshold protocol presented in “How to Share a Secret”: a secret is encoded as evaluations of a polynomial so that sufficiently many shares reconstruct it while fewer do not.
- **circa 1980:** *Shamir’s three-pass protocol*. A commutative-encryption protocol for transmitting a message without a previously shared key. Its status as a discrete scholarly text is unusually weak: it circulated primarily as a protocol attributed to Shamir rather than through one universally recognized originating paper.
- **1984:** *Identity-Based Cryptosystems and Signature Schemes*. Both a programmatic proposal and a concrete signature protocol: it introduced identity-based cryptography, while leaving practical identity-based encryption as an open problem.
- **1986:** *Fiat–Shamir identification and signature scheme*. An authentication protocol and signature construction made with Amos Fiat and presented in “How to Prove Yourself: Practical Solutions to Identification and Signature Problems.”
- **1986:** *Fiat–Shamir heuristic*. A protocol-transforming method, introduced in the same work, that replaces an interactive verifier’s random challenge with a hash value to obtain a noninteractive proof or signature. Later security formulations vary by model, so it is better understood as a family of transformations than as one immutable algorithm.
- **1987–1988:** *Feige–Fiat–Shamir identification scheme*. A zero-knowledge-style identification protocol made with Uriel Feige and Amos Fiat; its conference manifestation appeared in 1987 and the journal article in 1988.
- **1988:** *Zero-knowledge proofs of identity*. A practical identification protocol co-authored with Amos Fiat and Uriel Feige; it overlaps substantially with the work usually named the Feige–Fiat–Shamir scheme and therefore resists treatment as a wholly separate invention.
- **1990:** *Witness-indistinguishable and witness-hiding protocols*. Protocol notions and constructions made with Uriel Feige, distinguishing proofs that conceal which witness was used from those that prevent recovery of any witness.
- **1994–1995:** *Visual cryptography*. An image-sharing scheme made with Moni Naor in which stacked transparencies reveal a secret image without cryptographic computation. It first appeared in conference proceedings in 1994 and in the associated LNCS volume dated 1995.
- **2001:** *Ring signatures*. An anonymous-signature construction made with Ronald L. Rivest and Yael Tauman: a signer selects an ad hoc group whose members need neither cooperate nor enroll. The work is a protocol and a new cryptographic primitive, not merely a paper title.
- **2020:** *Privacy-Preserving Automated Exposure Notification*. A protocol proposal co-authored with Ran Canetti, Yael Tauman Kalai, Anna Lysyanskaya, Ronald L. Rivest, Emily Shen, Ari Trachtenberg, Mayank Varia, and Daniel J. Weitzner for exposure notification without centralized disclosure of users’ contact histories.

### Algorithms and complexity-theoretic constructions

- **1975:** *A Generalization of the Dijkstra–Scholten Algorithm for Recursive Predicates*. An early theoretical-computer-science paper concerning fixed-point computation; it belongs to Shamir’s pre-cryptographic body of work rather than to a cryptographic scheme.
- **1977:** *The Convergence of Generalized Dijkstra–Scholten Algorithms*. A fixed-point and program-semantics study continuous with his dissertation research.
- **1979:** *Factoring Numbers in O(log n) Arithmetic Steps*. An arithmetic-complexity result co-authored with Richard P. Brent.
- **1979:** *On the Cryptocomplexity of Knapsack Systems*. A cryptanalytic and complexity-theoretic investigation of knapsack-based public-key systems.
- **1981:** *A Linear Time Algorithm for Deciding 2-Satisfiability*. An algorithm for the 2-SAT decision problem. Its object is an algorithmic procedure and complexity bound, although it survives bibliographically as a journal article.
- **1983:** *On the Generation of Cryptographically Strong Pseudorandom Sequences*. A pseudorandom-generation construction co-authored with Manuel Blum.
- **1990–1992:** *IP = PSPACE*. A complexity-theoretic proof that polynomial-space computation equals interactive-proof computation. Shamir’s result followed the sum-check and arithmetization work of Lund, Fortnow, Karloff, and Nisan; the conference result appeared in 1990 and the definitive journal article in 1992.
- **1998:** *Playing “Twenty Questions” with a Liar*. A combinatorial coding and search result co-authored with Yair Rivest and Bernard Chazelle; it is an algorithmic-mathematical obra rather than a cryptographic protocol.
- **2001:** *Guaranteeing the Diversity of Number Generators*. A construction and analysis co-authored with Boaz Tsaban for combining number generators so that diversity is preserved even when components are defective.

### Cryptanalytic methods and attacks

- **1982–1984:** *Polynomial-time attack on the basic Merkle–Hellman cryptosystem*. A method that broke the foundational knapsack public-key construction. The result circulated in conference form before its 1984 IEEE journal manifestation.
- **1985:** *An Efficient Identification Scheme Based on Permuted Kernels*. A proposed identification construction co-authored with Amos Fiat; subsequent cryptanalysis means its historical status is that of a proposed, not enduringly secure, protocol.
- **late 1980s–1991:** *Differential cryptanalysis*. A general method for attacking block ciphers, developed with Eli Biham and publicly presented around 1990–1991. IBM researchers had independently known related techniques while designing DES, and the NSA also knew the method, so priority must be described as independent public discovery rather than unqualified first invention.
- **1991:** *Differential Cryptanalysis of DES-like Cryptosystems*. The principal journal manifestation of Biham and Shamir’s method, explaining attacks through the propagation of input differences.
- **1994:** *Differential Fault Analysis*. A fault-induced cryptanalytic method made with Eli Biham for extracting keys by comparing correct and deliberately corrupted computations.
- **1996:** *Cryptanalytic Time/Memory/Data Tradeoffs for Stream Ciphers*. A generic tradeoff attack co-authored with Alex Biryukov, later influential in practical stream-cipher cryptanalysis.
- **1999:** *TWINKLE*. A proposed optoelectronic sieving device for accelerating integer factorization. It is a hardware project rather than merely an algorithm, and the published design did not become a completed general-purpose factoring machine.
- **2000:** *Real Time Cryptanalysis of A5/1 on a PC*. An attack made with Alex Biryukov and David Wagner against the GSM stream cipher, showing that precomputation could make practical key recovery possible.
- **2001:** *Fluhrer–Mantin–Shamir attack*. A statistical key-recovery attack made with Scott Fluhrer and Itsik Mantin against weaknesses in RC4’s key-scheduling algorithm. Its application to WEP made the attack operationally consequential, although the paper attacks RC4 key scheduling rather than defining WEP itself.
- **2002:** *A Cryptanalytic Time-Memory Tradeoff*. A refined generic attack made with Alex Biryukov, extending earlier tradeoff techniques by incorporating available data.
- **2003:** *TWIRL*. A proposed special-purpose electronic device made with Eran Tromer to accelerate the sieving stage of integer factorization. Like TWINKLE, it is primarily an engineering design and cost model; it resists being called a completed physical work because the full machine remained hypothetical.
- **2004:** *Power Analysis of the Key Scheduling of the AES Candidates*. A side-channel analysis co-authored with Eli Biham, examining information leakage during key scheduling.
- **2004:** *Acoustic cryptanalysis*. A side-channel method introduced with Eran Tromer, showing that sounds emitted by computers can reveal information about cryptographic computation; later experiments made the extraction substantially more concrete.
- **2005:** *Cache-timing attacks on AES*. A software side-channel technique made with Daniel J. Bernstein? Attribution requires care: Shamir co-authored cache-attack work with Eran Tromer and Dag Arne Osvik, while Bernstein independently published a prominent remote cache-timing attack. The Shamir-associated obra is the Osvik–Shamir–Tromer family of cache attacks, not Bernstein’s paper.
- **2006:** *Cache Attacks and Countermeasures: The Case of AES*. A suite of practical cache-based attacks and defenses made with Dag Arne Osvik and Eran Tromer.
- **2006:** *Length-Based Cryptanalysis: The Case of Thompson’s Group*. A cryptanalytic method and experimental attack made with Dima Ruinskiy and Boaz Tsaban against proposed group-based cryptography.
- **2008:** *Cold-boot attack*. A physical-memory-recovery attack demonstrated with J. Alex Halderman, Seth D. Schoen, Nadia Heninger, William Clarkson, William Paul, Joseph A. Calandrino, Ariel J. Feldman, and Edward W. Felten. Shamir was a co-author of the research, but the obra is emphatically collaborative and should not be represented as his invention alone.
- **2008:** *Bug attacks*. A fault-oriented cryptanalytic model made with Eli Biham and Yaniv Carmeli, exploiting persistent implementation errors rather than transient injected faults.
- **2009:** *Cube attacks*. An algebraic black-box cryptanalytic technique made with Itai Dinur for recovering secret information from low-degree Boolean polynomials.
- **2009:** *Side-channel cube attacks*. An adaptation of cube attacks to leakage and implementation settings, made with Itai Dinur.
- **2010:** *Dynamic cube attacks*. A refinement made with Itai Dinur that dynamically assigns public variables, demonstrated against Grain-128.
- **2011:** *Biclique cryptanalysis of the full AES*. A meet-in-the-middle-style attack made with Andrey Bogdanov and Dmitry Khovratovich. It was the first published attack on full-round AES with complexity below exhaustive key search, but only by a small theoretical margin and not as a practical break.
- **2011:** *Self-differential cryptanalysis*. A differential technique made with Itai Dinur and Orr Dunkelman and applied to reduced-round SHA-3 candidates.
- **2012:** *A practical polynomial-time known-plaintext attack on John Nash’s cryptosystem*. An attack made with Eldad Zinger on a cipher proposed in Nash’s unpublished correspondence.
- **2014:** *RSA key extraction via acoustic cryptanalysis*. An experimental system made with Daniel Genkin and Eran Tromer that recovered RSA keys from computer-generated sound. It is a laboratory attack and measurement apparatus as well as a textual publication.
- **2016:** *The Dangers of Key Reuse: Practical Attacks on IPsec IKE*. A protocol attack co-authored with Dennis Felsch, Martin Grothe, Jan Steffen, Adam Czubak, and Marit Hansen.
- **2018:** *Screaming Channels*. A side-channel attack made with Eyal Ronen and collaborators, recovering cryptographic information from unintended radio emissions produced by mixed-signal devices.
- **2020:** *Lamphone*. An optical side-channel system made with Ben Nassi, Yaron Pirutin, Yuval Elovici, and Boris Zadov that reconstructs room sound from measurements of a hanging light bulb’s vibrations.
- **2024:** *Polynomial-Time Cryptanalytic Extraction of Deep Neural Networks in the Hard-Label Setting*. A model-extraction method made with Nicholas Carlini, Jorge Chávez-Saab, Anna Hambitzer, and Francisco Rodríguez-Henríquez; its use of “cryptanalytic” is methodological, treating learned parameters as hidden secrets.
- **2026:** *New Attacks on Feistel Structures with Improved Memory Complexities*. A family of cryptanalytic attacks co-authored with Itai Dinur, Orr Dunkelman, Nathan Keller, and D. Ross.
- **2026:** *Deep Neural Cryptography*. A cryptographic and machine-learning investigation co-authored with David Gérault, Anna Hambitzer, and Eyal Ronen. At the atlas’s cutoff it is a forthcoming/2026 conference manifestation, not a long-settled body of results.

### Analytical models, surveys, and empirical studies

- **2012:** *Quantitative Analysis of the Full Bitcoin Transaction Graph*. An empirical graph study made with Dorit Ron; it is an analysis of Bitcoin activity, not a cryptocurrency or protocol authored by Shamir.
- **2013:** *How Did Dread Pirate Roberts Acquire and Protect His Bitcoin Wealth?* An attribution-oriented transaction analysis made with Dorit Ron. Its conclusions concern probabilistic linkage and should not be mistaken for judicially established attribution.
- **2019:** *A Simple Explanation for the Existence of Adversarial Examples with Small Hamming Distance*. A theoretical explanatory model made with Itay Safran, Eyal Ronen, and Orr Dunkelman.
- **2019:** *The Dimpled Manifold Model of Adversarial Examples in Machine Learning*. A geometric model made with Eyal Ronen and collaborators for describing the evolution of neural-network decision boundaries; it is a conceptual model, not a cryptographic protocol.
- **2026:** *Error Resilient Space Partitioning*. A discrete-geometric and algorithmic construction co-authored with Orr Dunkelman, Z. Geyzel, C. Keller, Nathan Keller, Eyal Ronen, and R. J. Tessler.

### Commercial and institutional projects

- **1982:** *RSA Data Security*. A company co-founded by Shamir, Ronald L. Rivest, and Leonard M. Adleman to commercialize RSA-related security technology. A company is a collective institutional project rather than a text or single technical work, and Shamir’s precise operational role should not be inferred from co-foundership alone.
