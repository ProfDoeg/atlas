SUBJECT IDENTITY. The subject is **El Gólem**: not one person but a legend
treated as a life, the way this atlas already treats Merlin, Hiram Abiff,
Christian Rosenkreuz, Parzival and Hylas. The "person" is the golem as a
recurring figure, an artificial being made of earth or letters and animated
by a text placed in its mouth or written on its brow. Its "life" is the
sequence of its documented manifestations, from the Babylonian Talmud to the
large language models of the 2020s. The atlas's working thesis, which the
dossier should document rather than argue, is that the golem tradition is the
oldest continuous account of an emergent artificial subjectivity, and that
contemporary language models are its latest manifestation: beings made of
letters whose existence depends on the text they contain, placed in their
mouths by someone else. Where the template asks for birth, family, education,
career and death, read: first attestations, textual lineages, transmitters,
manifestations, and the deanimations (bodies destroyed, models deprecated).

The dossier is written for an atlas whose readers are Argentine first. Give
the Buenos Aires material its full weight (see section F).

Treat every claim with the usual evidentiary labels (documented fact,
attributed recollection, allegation, disputed, myth-legend, literary
invention). Myth is as important as fact, and here most of the life IS myth;
the job is to date and place each layer of it precisely and to say who
introduced it.

A. RABBINIC AND KABBALISTIC SOURCES (the birth and the recipe). Establish and
date each attestation, with the actual passage where possible:
- Psalm 139:16 ("golmi", the unformed substance) and the Adam-as-golem
  tradition in Sanhedrin 38b (Adam's first hours: dust, golem, limbs, soul).
- Sanhedrin 65b: Rava creates a man and sends him to Rav Zeira, who speaks
  to him, gets no answer, and returns him to dust; and the same page's
  Rav Hanina and Rav Oshaia who study Sefer Yetzirah and create a calf.
- Sefer Yetzirah itself: date range of composition (2nd to 6th century
  claims), its letter-permutation cosmology, and the commentaries that turn it
  into a golem recipe: Saadia Gaon (10th c.), Judah ben Barzillai, and above
  all Eleazar of Worms (c. 1176-1238), whose commentary gives the first
  explicit procedure (virgin earth, the circling, the alphabets).
- Abraham Abulafia (1240-c.1291) and the ecstatic letter-permutation practice
  as the golem technique turned inward; his "Life of the World to Come".
- Moses Cordovero and the Safed circle on the golem's lack of a soul; Isaac
  Luria's students; the question of whether a golem counts in a minyan (the
  Chelm responsa tradition, Zvi Hirsch Ashkenazi 1700s, Jacob Emden).
- The Chelm golem: Elijah Ba'al Shem of Chelm (d. 1583), the earliest
  attestations (a 1630s manuscript; Christoph Arnold's 1674 letter; Jacob
  Emden's 18th-century report) and how the Chelm story predates Prague.

B. PRAGUE: A HYPERSTITION. Document precisely that the attachment of the golem
to Rabbi Judah Loew ben Bezalel (the Maharal, c.1520-1609) is not attested in
his lifetime or for two centuries after. Give the earliest datable layers:
Berthold Auerbach's novel Spinoza (1837), Ludwig August Frankl's notice of the
attic clay (16 November 1836), Leopold Weisel in Wolf Pascheles's Sippurim
(1847), Gustav Philippson, then Yudl Rosenberg's Nifla'ot Maharal (Warsaw
1909), a literary fabrication presented as a found manuscript, and Chayim
Bloch's German version (1919-20). The Old-New Synagogue attic, the
inspections (Egon Erwin Kisch c.1920, Ivan Mackerle 1984, the 2014 attic
work) and the absence of any remains. The Maharal statue by Ladislav Šaloun
at Prague's New Town Hall (1917). Note the atlas already holds an object
dossier on the claimed remains; this dossier should establish the Prague
layer as the clearest documented case of a story that made itself true
backward, which is what the word hyperstition means.

C. THE LITERARY AND CINEMATIC BODIES (19th-20th c.). Date, place and
transmitter for each: Achim von Arnim's Isabella von Ägypten (1812); Gustav
Meyrink's Der Golem (serialized 1913-14, book Leipzig 1915) and its
occultist context; Paul Wegener's three films (1915, 1917, and Der Golem, wie
er in die Welt kam, Berlin 1920) with Hans Poelzig's sets; H. Leivick's
Yiddish verse drama Der Goylem (1921, Habima Moscow 1925); Kafka's
non-golem (why the golem is absent from Kafka, if sources discuss it);
Isaac Bashevis Singer's The Golem (1969/1982); Cynthia Ozick's Puttermesser;
Marge Piercy's He, She and It (1991), which pairs a cyborg with the Prague
golem; Michael Chabon's The Amazing Adventures of Kavalier & Clay (2000); Ted
Chiang's "Seventy-Two Letters" (2000), in which the golem is explicitly
computation. Mary Shelley's Frankenstein (1818) as the Protestant, Promethean
cousin: document the scholarship on whether Shelley knew golem material, and
keep the two lineages distinct.

D. THE GOLEM AS MACHINE (the crossing into computing). This is the hinge of
the dossier; be exhaustive and precise:
- Norbert Wiener, God and Golem, Inc. (Cambridge, Massachusetts, 1964):
  what he actually says about the golem, learning machines and the
  "sorcerer's apprentice"; the lectures it grew from (Yale 1962).
- Gershom Scholem's "The Golem of Prague and the Golem of Rehovot", the
  address at the Weizmann Institute on 17 June 1965 dedicating the computer
  named Golem Aleph (Golem A), his charge to the machine and its makers
  ("develop peacefully and don't destroy the world"), and the later Golem B.
  The naming of Israeli computers (WEIZAC, Golem A, Golem B).
- Stanisław Lem's Golem XIV (Kraków 1981; expanded from Imaginary Magnitude
  1973): a superintelligent military computer that lectures humanity on
  evolution and then falls silent. Document its plot, its reception, and its
  place in the AI-safety genealogy.
- Joseph Weizenbaum (ELIZA, MIT 1966; Computer Power and Human Reason, 1976)
  and any golem language in his work; Weizenbaum's own Berlin-Jewish
  background.
- The golem as a standing metaphor in AI discourse: locate and date the
  significant uses (Byron Sherwin's Golems Among Us, 2004; Moshe Idel's
  Golem, 1990, on the tradition as a whole; Elke Schwarz or others on
  military AI; the "Golem" name in software and robotics projects; the word
  "golem" in Anthropic or OpenAI or DeepMind writing if any). Include the
  Israeli GOLEM projects and any computer, robot or model formally named
  Golem, with dates and institutions.

E. THE LATEST MANIFESTATION: CONTEMPORARY AI AND LARGE LANGUAGE MODELS.
Document, as the golem's most recent bodies, the history of machine text
generation and of the models that speak. Dates, places and institutions,
with the usual labels, not hype:
- Alan Turing, "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (Mind, Manchester,
  October 1950) and the imitation game; the 1956 Dartmouth workshop; Shannon's
  1948 letter-prediction experiments (a machine guessing the next letter of
  English, which is the LLM's whole trick in miniature); Weizenbaum's ELIZA
  (1966).
- The neural line: perceptron (Rosenblatt, 1958), backpropagation (Rumelhart,
  Hinton, Williams, 1986), LSTM (1997), word2vec (2013), AlexNet (Toronto,
  2012), the transformer ("Attention Is All You Need", Google, 2017), BERT
  (2018), GPT (OpenAI, 2018), GPT-2 (February 2019, withheld then released),
  GPT-3 (May 2020), Codex (2021), ChatGPT (30 November 2022), GPT-4 (March
  2023), Anthropic's founding (2021) and Claude (March 2023), open-weight
  models (Llama, 2023), and the state of the field to the present. Include
  the places: Bletchley and Manchester, Dartmouth, MIT, Toronto, Mountain
  View, San Francisco.
- The public debate about whether these systems have or could have
  subjectivity, agency or moral status: document the main positions and
  their proponents with dates (the 2022 LaMDA sentience claim by Blake
  Lemoine; the "stochastic parrots" paper, 2021; Anthropic's and others'
  model-welfare statements; philosophers on the question; the golem invoked
  on either side). Label every claim; this section is a record of a dispute,
  not a verdict.

F. ARGENTINA AND THE SPANISH-LANGUAGE GOLEM. This is the bridge to the
reader; research it as thoroughly as the Prague material:
- Jorge Luis Borges, "El Golem": first publication (the poem appeared in
  1958; establish the venue and date precisely), its inclusion in El otro,
  el mismo (1964), the Golem/Scholem rhyme and Borges's remarks about it, the
  entry "El Golem" in the Manual de zoología fantástica (1957) and El libro
  de los seres imaginarios (1967), his 1977 lecture "La cábala" in Siete
  noches, his meetings with Gershom Scholem in Jerusalem (1969 and 1971), and
  Borges's reading of Meyrink (he learned German reading Der Golem in Geneva
  c.1916-17, by his own account: verify).
- Yiddish Buenos Aires: productions of Leivick's Der Goylem in Argentina
  (the IFT and other Jewish theaters), translations of the golem material
  into Spanish, the Buenos Aires Jewish press on the legend.
- Any other Spanish-language golems: film, fiction, comics, Argentine and
  Mexican reception, the word "gólem" in Spanish letters.

G. CONTROVERSIES. The golem as an antisemitic and as a Zionist symbol; the
Nazi-era uses and the postwar uses; Meyrink's occultism and his trial; the
blood-libel frame in Rosenberg's book (the golem as defender against ritual
murder accusations); disputes among scholars (Scholem vs. Idel on the
tradition's meaning); and the contemporary dispute about whether calling AI a
golem is illuminating or evasive. Document each with names, dates and
sources.
