## Works by Guinevere

### Attributable obra

- No securely attributable obra survives or is attested. Guinevere is a legendary character represented differently across medieval Welsh, Latin, French, German, and English texts—not a historically established maker with an authorial or artistic corpus; the standard scholarly bibliographies catalogue works *about or containing* Guinevere, but none made by her.

### Literary actions that resist classification as works

- **Late fourteenth to fifteenth century tradition:** Guinevere’s penitential life at Amesbury. In the anonymous *Stanzaic Morte Arthur* and Malory’s *Le Morte Darthur*, she enters the convent at Amesbury and eventually becomes its abbess; this is a narrative act and vocation, not evidence that she founded the historical house, composed a rule, or made a separately identifiable obra. The episode’s association with Amesbury appears to be a late medieval literary development, not fifth- or sixth-century biography.

### Posthumous manifestation—not Guinevere’s work

- **1191:** The purported remains and grave of Guinevere at Glastonbury. Glastonbury’s monks announced the discovery of remains attributed to Arthur and Guinevere, accompanied in some accounts by an inscribed lead cross; this was a contested institutional manifestation produced or interpreted by the abbey, not something made by Guinevere. Gerald of Wales supplied the earliest surviving account around 1193, while modern archaeological scholarship treats the identification as legend or possible monastic fabrication rather than a “rediscovery” establishing Guinevere’s historical life or death.
