## Works by Franz Anton Mesmer

### Dissertations, treatises, and systematic expositions

- **1766:** *Dissertatio physico-medica de planetarum influxu*. A Latin medical dissertation proposing that celestial bodies act upon living bodies through a pervasive fluid. Frank A. Pattie found that twenty-two of its twenty-three cited propositions derive from Richard Mead, so its intellectual originality—and even the precise form of its title—requires qualification; Mesmer nevertheless added material of his own and later treated the dissertation as the beginning of his system.

- **1779:** *Mémoire sur la découverte du magnétisme animal*. A theoretical and autobiographical treatise presenting twenty-seven propositions about “animal magnetism” and narrating Mesmer’s claimed discovery. It is both exposition and manifesto: it declares a universal therapeutic principle and demands institutional recognition for it.

- **1781:** *Précis historique des faits relatifs au magnétisme animal, jusques en avril 1781*. A polemical history of Mesmer’s discovery, treatments, disputes, and unsuccessful approaches to medical authorities. Although issued as a book, it behaves partly as a dossier assembled to establish priority and vindicate its author.

- **1799:** *Mémoire de F. A. Mesmer, docteur en médecine, sur ses découvertes*. A late restatement of Mesmer’s physical and medical system, published in Revolutionary year VII. The German *Ueber meine Entdeckungen* of 1800 is a translation and therefore a new manifestation, not a separate obra.

- **1803:** *Précis de la découverte du magnétisme animal, avec une lettre sur le flux et le reflux*. A concise retrospective exposition joined to a letter about the tides; Bruno Belhoste’s bibliography records it without a place or fixed publication date, so “1803” should be treated as the conventional bibliographical date rather than silently strengthened into certainty.

- **1812:** *Allgemeine Erläuterungen über den Magnetismus und den Somnambulismus, als vorläufige Einleitung in das Natursystem*. A preliminary systematic introduction to magnetism and somnambulism. Its relation to the papers edited shortly afterward by Karl Christian Wolfart makes it part of Mesmer’s late system-building project rather than an entirely self-contained new doctrine.

- **1814:** *Mesmerismus, oder System der Wechselwirkungen: Theorie und Anwendung des thierischen Magnetismus als die allgemeine Heilkunde zur Erhaltung des Menschen*. A composite summa edited by Karl Christian Wolfart from Mesmer’s manuscripts, with a portrait and six engraved plates. It is not a transparent authorial book: Wolfart translated and published only part of the material entrusted to him, and the volume combines natural philosophy, therapeutic protocol, morality, education, and legislation.

- **1846–1849 (posthumous publication of an earlier manuscript):** *Notions élémentaires sur la morale, l’éducation et la législation, pour servir à l’instruction publique en France*. A political-pedagogical treatise serialized after Mesmer’s death; scholarship identifies it as the French original underlying the second part of the 1814 *Mesmerismus*. Its composition date is not securely fixed, and its posthumous serialization is a manifestation rather than its moment of making.

### Letters and public interventions

- **5 January 1775:** *Schreiben über die Magnet-Cur von Hrn A. Mesmer, Doktor der Arzneygelährtheit, an einen auswärtigen Arzt*. An open letter to the physician Johann Christoph Unzer describing Mesmer’s magnetic cures. It is simultaneously a case report, priority claim, and public defense.

- **January 1775:** *Zweites Schreiben die Magnet-Cur betreffend an das Publikum*. A second public letter in Mesmer’s controversy with the astronomer Maximilian Hell. It acts polemically rather than merely reporting clinical practice.

- **1775:** *Drittes Schreiben über die Magnet-Cur*. A third intervention in the magnetic-cure dispute, addressed to readers in Frankfurt. Its circulation through later collections complicates the distinction between the original text and its subsequent editions.

- **1775:** *Two letters to unidentified recipients concerning magnetic cures*. Two otherwise untitled letters preserved in C. F. von Reuss’s 1778 documentary collection. They resist treatment as separately titled “works”: they survive as pieces of correspondence incorporated into another compiler’s book.

- **18 December 1777; published 1778:** *Lettre de M. le Docteur Mesmer à M. Le Roux*. A reply concerning reports of Mesmer’s cures, printed in the *Gazette d’agriculture, commerce, arts et finances* and subsequently reprinted elsewhere. Its date belongs to the letter; 1778 belongs to its public manifestation.

- **Before 1 November and 1 December 1779:** *Letters to Benjamin Franklin*. Two approaches to Franklin seeking attention for animal magnetism. They are surviving correspondence, not books, and scholarship treats them as documents in Mesmer’s continuing project of obtaining scientific recognition.

- **1783:** *Lettre sur un fait relatif à l’histoire de la découverte du magnétisme animal*. A priority-claim letter first issued separately and reproduced in periodical and polemical contexts.

- **25 November 1783:** *Lettre de M. Mesmer aux auteurs du Journal*. A public letter printed in the supplement to the *Journal de Paris* on 13 December 1783. Its intended action was intervention in a live controversy, making it closer to a manifesto or public act than to a retrospective essay.

- **14 May 1784:** *Letter to Benjamin Franklin*. A letter addressed to the head of the royal commission investigating animal magnetism. It belongs to Mesmer’s campaign for a hearing and survives through documentary publication rather than as an independently titled work.

- **20 August 1784:** *Lettre de M. Mesmer à Messieurs les auteurs du Journal de Paris*. A public response to the investigation of animal magnetism. It was issued together with the Franklin letter as *Lettres de M. Mesmer à Messieurs les Auteurs du Journal de Paris et à M. Franklin*.

- **16 and 29 August 1784:** *Lettres de M. Mesmer à Monsieur Vicq d’Azyr et à MM. les auteurs du Journal de Paris*. Mesmer’s two letters frame Félix Vicq d’Azyr’s reply of 27 August in the collected pamphlet. Because the publication incorporates another author’s letter, the whole pamphlet is not exclusively Mesmer’s obra.

- **31 August 1784:** *Lettre de M. Mesmer à M. le Comte de C***. A polemical letter published with a copy of a petition to the Parlement of Paris. The composite pamphlet joins personal address to juridical action.

- **1785:** *Lettre de l’auteur de la découverte du magnétisme animal à l’auteur des Réflexions préliminaires*. A response to the printed financial and institutional accusations surrounding the Société de l’Harmonie. It functions as a defensive manifesto asserting Mesmer’s authority over both the discovery and its dissemination.

- **1800:** *Lettres de F. A. Mesmer, D.M., sur l’origine de la petite vérole et le moyen de la faire cesser*. Letters proposing a theory of smallpox and a way to eliminate it through practices associated with birth. Psyalpha gives 1799, while Bruno Belhoste’s newer bibliography dates the Paris printing to fructidor year VIII, or 1800; the discrepancy should remain visible.

- **Written 1804; published 1814:** *Auszug aus einem das gelbe Fieber betreffenden Brief*. An extract from a letter on yellow fever, written in 1804 but first printed in the *Neues Askläpieion* in 1814. Its excerpted form means that neither its original completeness nor its status as an autonomous work can be assumed.

### Aphorisms, diagrams, rules, and protocols

- **1784:** *Théorie du monde et des êtres organisés, suivant les principes de M***.* An engraved diagram or synoptic table of the cosmos and organized beings according to Mesmer’s principles. The title’s “following the principles of” formulation does not establish that Mesmer personally drew or engraved it, so it is best treated as an attributed visual manifestation of his system rather than an unquestioned authored text.

- **1785:** *Aphorismes de M. Mesmer dictés à l’assemblée de ses élèves, et dans lesquels on trouve ses principes, sa théorie et les moyens de magnétiser*. A protocol-like body of propositions dictated in teaching and edited for print by Louis Caullet de Veaumorel. Three editions appeared in 1785, the third substantially enlarged; the words are presented as Mesmer’s, but their textual form is inseparable from the pupil-editor’s mediation.

- **1785:** *Règlemens des Sociétés de l’Harmonie universelle*. Institutional rules associated with Mesmer’s Société de l’Harmonie and preserved within the polemical *Supplément aux Observations de M. Bergasse*. They are protocols meant to organize conduct, authority, money, and transmission; the surrounding pamphlet is attributed to Nicolas Bergasse, so Mesmer’s authorship of the complete printed object must not be inferred.

- **1780s, exact date uncertain:** *Catéchisme du magnétisme animal*. A question-and-answer teaching text transmitted among Mesmer’s followers and included by Robert Amadou among documents of Mesmer’s system. Its attribution and textual history are less secure than those of the signed books, and it may record doctrine communicated orally rather than a finished authorial manuscript.

- **1780s, exact date uncertain:** *Plan de l’ordre de l’Harmonie universelle selon le rite septentrional*. A manuscript plan for an initiatory order, accompanied by explanations of symbolic “carpets” or diagrams for its first grades. Amadou published it as a document of Mesmer’s institutional project; its incomplete, ritual, and possibly collaborative character makes “work” an unstable designation.

### Therapeutic apparatus and embodied practice

- **From 1773–1774:** *Animal magnetism*. A therapeutic-natural-philosophical project asserting that a universal fluid linked celestial bodies, earth, and living beings and could be directed to restore health. It is not one text or object: its principal obra is a changing assemblage of doctrine, bodily technique, clinical performance, and institutional claim.

- **From the mid-1770s:** *Magnetic passes and the directed crisis*. A treatment protocol using gaze, touch, hand movements, iron rods, music, and collective attention to transmit or redistribute the supposed fluid and provoke a therapeutic “crisis.” Because Mesmer adapted it in performance and trained others to repeat it, the thing made is a reproducible choreography rather than a fixed textual work.

- **By 1780:** *The baquet*. A communal therapeutic apparatus: a tub or vat whose bottles, water, iron filings, ropes, and projecting rods were arranged to conduct or accumulate animal magnetism. Surviving descriptions and images vary, so “the baquet” names a design and treatment environment rather than one securely surviving object fabricated entirely by Mesmer’s own hands.

- **Late 1770s–1780s:** *The magnetic salon*. A multisensory therapeutic environment combining the baquet, linked patients, mirrors, controlled lighting, silence or spoken direction, and music. It is reasonably called a Gesamtkunstwerk of treatment, but it resists enumeration as a discrete work because each session remade it through patients’ bodies and responses.

- **Late 1770s–1780s:** *Glass-armonica therapy*. Mesmer incorporated performance on Benjamin Franklin’s glass armonica into magnetic treatment to induce and regulate crises. Mesmer did not invent the instrument and no securely identified composition by him supplies a separate musical work; what he made was a therapeutic use and performance practice.

### Institutional and social projects

- **1783–1785:** *Société de l’Harmonie universelle*. A subscription society created to teach, finance, regulate, and propagate Mesmer’s discovery while preserving his control over it. It was an institutional project and social technology, not simply a learned society or a publication; internal financial and constitutional conflicts produced a rupture with leading disciples.

- **1785:** *Société harmonique des amis réunis*. A successor organization founded after the rupture in the original Société de l’Harmonie. Mesmer helped make it as a vehicle for continued instruction and dissemination, although an institution with many members cannot be reduced to his sole-authored obra.

### Cultural commissions and spaces with disputed status

- **1768, traditionally:** *Commission and garden performance of Mozart’s Bastien und Bastienne*. Tradition says that Mesmer commissioned Mozart’s German operetta and hosted its première in his Viennese garden theatre. Both the commissioning and the performance location remain disputed, and the opera itself is Mozart’s work—not Mesmer’s—so this is at most Mesmer’s cultural project or act of patronage.

- **1760s–1770s:** *Musical and theatrical gatherings at the Viennese garden house*. Mesmer fashioned his residence as a social environment joining medicine, music, theatre, and learned sociability. No fixed title or stable boundary belongs to this creation, and it resists being called a single work; it matters chiefly as the precursor to the deliberately staged magnetic salon.
