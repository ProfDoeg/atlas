## Works by Marsilio Ficino

### Philosophical and theological works

- **c. 1454–1456:** *Summa philosophiae*. A scholastic teaching compendium preserved with several short philosophical tracts; its date is uncertain, and Ficino never included it in his own later lists of works. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1454–1456:** *Tractatus physicus* (first collection). A compact set of Aristotelian, Averroist, and Platonic definitions of nature; it survives as part of the early instructional dossier edited by Kristeller. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1454–1456:** *Tractatus de Deo, natura et arte*. A short metaphysical tract opposing God as pure act to matter as pure potency; its precise date is unknown. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1454–1456:** *Tractatus de anima*. An early scholastic account of the soul and its vegetative, sensitive, motive, and intellectual powers; attribution is supported by its manuscript heading, but Ficino did not mention it in his later catalogue. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1454–1456:** *Tractatus physicus* (second collection). A separate discussion of ancient physical theories and of matter, form, and privation as principles; its relation to the other early tract is uncertain. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1454–1456:** *Quaestiones de luce et aliae multae*. A set of questions on light, vision, sensation, and perception; it is a manuscript dossier rather than a finished book with a secure date. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1454–1456:** *Divisio philosophiae*. An early classification of philosophy, preserved in more than one non-identical form; it resists treatment as a single finalized work. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1456:** *Institutiones ad Platonicam disciplinam*. Ficino’s first substantial Platonic project, written at Cristoforo Landino’s urging and deliberately withheld from circulation; the sole copy is lost, so its completeness and contents cannot now be established. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1457:** *De virtutibus moralibus, sive De magnificentia*. An ethical epistle to Antonio Canigiani, dated 1 June, which survives both as a letter and as an independent moral tract. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1457:** *De quattuor sectis philosophorum*. A doxographical epistle comparing the Platonic, Peripatetic, Stoic, and Epicurean schools. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1457:** *De divino furore*. A philosophical epistle on inspired or divine frenzy; Ficino’s original catalogue describes a vernacular work, while the transmitted Latin epistle complicates the identity of the object. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1457:** *Commentariola in Lucretium*. Juvenile notes or commentaries on Lucretius that Ficino later said he burned; six surviving letters preserve an abridged reflection of their contents, so the lost project cannot be reconstructed as a stable text. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1457–1458:** *De voluptate*. A sustained philosophical treatment of pleasure, addressed to Antonio Canigiani and completed on 30 December 1457 according to Florentine dating. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1458:** *Di Dio et anima*, later titled *Compendium de opinionibus philosophorum circa Deum et animam*. A vernacular philosophical letter to Francesco Capponi surveying opinions about God and the soul; its epistolary and treatise identities coexist. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *Oeconomica*. A work named by Ficino in his own retrospective catalogue; Kristeller tentatively identified it with an epistle to Ficino’s brothers dated 6 August 1455, while Raymond Marcel proposed a different connection, so neither its text nor its independent existence is secure. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *De felicitate*. A moral-philosophical work named in Ficino’s own catalogue; it survives among material subsequently absorbed into the epistolary corpus rather than as an unequivocally autonomous book. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *De iustitia*. A work on justice named by Ficino in his catalogue of early writings; its boundary as an independent work is obscured by later epistolary compilation. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *De consolatione parentum in obitu filii*. A vernacular consolation for parents after a child’s death; a related consolatory letter to an aunt repeats much of it, making the relationship between “work” and reused letter-text uncertain. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *De appetitu*. A vernacular work on appetite named in Ficino’s retrospective catalogue; it was not issued as a securely bounded independent book. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *Oratio ad Deum theologica*. A theological address to God named by Ficino among his early writings; its form lies between prayer, oration, and philosophical composition. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1462:** *Dialogus inter Deum et animam theologicus*. A theological dialogue between God and the soul, known from Ficino’s own catalogue and transmitted among his minor writings. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1469–1474:** *Theologia Platonica de immortalitate animorum*. An eighteen-book philosophical theology arguing for the soul’s immortality and reconciling Platonism with Christianity; composed over several years, revised afterward, and first printed in 1482. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1474–1476:** *De Christiana religione*. A Christian apologetic and philosophical theology made in several Latin redactions: Kristeller distinguished a lost first version, a manuscript second version, and the printed version of 1476. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1474–1484:** *Della christiana religione*. Ficino’s own vernacular recasting of *De Christiana religione*, first printed in 1474/75 and substantially revised and enlarged for the 1484 Pisa edition; it is not merely a later translator’s edition. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477–1478:** *Argumentum in Platonicam theologiam*. A concise announcement or epitome of the larger *Platonic Theology*, dedicated to Lorenzo de’ Medici; its function is partly promotional and programmatic. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477:** *De divina providentia*. The first of a group of theological opuscules on ascent toward God; its relation to the same year’s anti-astrological *Disputatio* is close but not securely identical. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477:** *De ascensu a materia elementorum ad caelum sine materia*. A short theological-metaphysical work describing ascent from elemental matter toward an immaterial heaven. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477:** *De raptu Pauli ad tertium caelum*. A theological meditation on Paul’s ascent to the third heaven, made as one of Ficino’s “three ways” of rising to God. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477:** *De impedimento mentis a corpore eiusque immortalitate*. A short argument about the body’s obstruction of mind and the mind’s immortality. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1476–1477:** *Quid sit lumen*. A philosophical-theological opuscule asking what light is; Ficino later reworked it into *De lumine*, so it is both an independent text and an earlier state of another work. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1476–1477:** *De officiis*. A short ethical work written for the musician Cherubino Quarquaglioli, not to be confused with Cicero’s work of the same title. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477:** *Argumentum de summo bono*. A concise philosophical argument concerning the highest good. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1477:** *Disputatio contra iudicium astrologorum*. An unfinished or deliberately withheld critique of judicial astrology defending providence and human freedom; political events in 1478 may have interrupted its revision and circulation. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1484:** *Concordia Mosis et Platonis*. A short concordance of Mosaic and Platonic wisdom, composed while the Plato edition was being completed. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1484:** *Confirmatio Christianorum per Socratica*. An apologetic argument confirming Christian teaching through Socratic material; it survives in the orbit of Ficino’s Platonic arguments rather than as a major separate treatise. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1489:** *De vita libri tres*. A composite medical, philosophical, astrological, and magical protocol for preserving scholars’ health and aligning human life with celestial influences; its third book grew from Ficino’s work on Plotinus and prompted formal accusations of magic. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1489:** *Apologia in librum De vita*. A defensive manifesto appended to or circulated with *De vita*, asserting Ficino’s Christian orthodoxy against charges of illicit magic. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1492–1493:** *De Sole*. A solar theology and cosmology first prepared in 1492 and printed in 1493; it expands earlier “heliosophic” letters into an independent work. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1492–1493:** *De lumine*. A metaphysical treatise on light made by revising and enlarging the earlier *Quid sit lumen* and printed with *De Sole*. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1496:** *Commentarium in numerum fatalem*. A commentary on the “nuptial” or fatal number in Book VIII of Plato’s *Republic*; it is at once mathematical exegesis and a philosophical work in its own right. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))

### Commentaries and interpretive works

- **1460s:** *Expositio in Oracula Chaldaica*. A commentary on the Chaldean Oracles attributed to Zoroaster; its textual history is fragmentary, and it belongs to Ficino’s larger project of reconstructing an ancient theology. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **c. 1464–1469, revised later:** *Commentarium in Philebum*. A commentary arising from public exposition of Plato’s *Philebus*; an early manuscript version survives, while the trace of Ficino’s public lectures reported by his biographer is otherwise lost. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1469, revised later:** *Commentarium in Convivium Platonis de amore*. A seven-speech dialogue-commentary on Plato’s *Symposium*, commonly called *De amore*; it is less a line-by-line commentary than a new philosophical dialogue staged through Ficino’s contemporaries. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **after 1469:** *El libro dell’amore*. Ficino’s own vernacular version or recasting of his *Symposium* commentary; it is a distinct act of making for a non-Latin audience, not simply a modern translation. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **1460s–1490s:** *Argumenta in Platonis dialogos*. Prefaces and synoptic arguments accompanying Ficino’s translations of the Platonic corpus; their serial, revisable nature makes them a distributed interpretive project rather than one homogeneous commentary. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **date uncertain:** *Commentarium in Cratylum*. A surviving commentary on Plato’s *Cratylus*; modern scholarship has had to reconstruct it from manuscript transmission. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **date uncertain:** *Argumentum et commentarius in Phaedrum*. A combined introduction and commentary on Plato’s *Phaedrus*, especially concerned with divine frenzy, love, and the soul. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **date uncertain:** *Argumentum in Platonis Ionem de furore poetico*. A compact interpretive essay on poetic inspiration attached to Ficino’s translation of the *Ion*. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **by 1484:** *Compendium in Timaeum*. An extended summary and commentary on Plato’s *Timaeus*, joining cosmological exposition to Ficino’s own metaphysics. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **by 1484:** *Epistola de rationibus musicae*. A technical-philosophical letter on musical proportions connected with Ficino’s interpretation of the *Timaeus*; it is both correspondence and a small music-theoretical treatise. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1484–1491:** *Commentaria in Plotinum*. A commentary written alongside and after Ficino’s complete Latin Plotinus; after the thirtieth treatise it contracts into briefer analyses, so the “complete commentary” is deliberately uneven rather than uniformly exhaustive. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1490–1492:** *Commentaria in Dionysium Areopagitam*. Extensive interpretive work on Pseudo-Dionysius’s *Mystical Theology* and *Divine Names*, combining translation, chapter-by-chapter exposition, and Ficino’s Christian Platonism. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **1494:** *Commentarium in Parmenidem*. Ficino’s completed commentary on Plato’s *Parmenides*, finished by 16 October 1494 and among the last parts of his projected Platonic exegesis. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1490s:** *Commentarium in Sophistam*. Ficino’s interpretation of Plato’s *Sophist*, often known through the editorial title *Icastes*; it is an ontological commentary, not a translation alone. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **date uncertain:** *Commentarium in Epistolas Pauli*. A substantial Christian exegetical work on the Pauline epistles; its distinct critical edition confirms that it is more than a scattered group of sermons or letters. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))

### Translations and cultural-transfer projects

- **1462–1463:** *Pimander sive de potestate et sapientia Dei*. Ficino’s Latin translation of fourteen Greek Hermetic treatises, now called the *Corpus Hermeticum*; “Pimander” became the title of the collection although it properly names its first treatise. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **early 1460s:** *Orphic Hymns*. Latin translations of hymns then attributed to Orpheus; Ficino withheld them from print, and their status includes both textual translation and material for sung ritual performance. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **early 1460s:** *Argonautica Orphica*. A Latin translation of the poem then attributed to Orpheus, made within the ancient-theology project. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **early 1460s:** *Homeric Hymns*. Latin translations of hymns attributed to Homer; the surviving corpus should be understood as a translation project rather than an original poetic cycle. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **early 1460s:** *Theogonia* of Hesiod. A Latin translation produced during Ficino’s first intensive recovery of Greek theological poetry. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **early 1460s:** *Hymns of Proclus*. Latin translations of Proclean hymns, participating in Ficino’s reconstruction of a continuous ancient theology. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **early 1460s:** *Aurea praecepta* and *Symbola Pythagorica*. Ficino’s Latin versions of the pseudo-Pythagorean Golden Verses and Symbols, circulated with his earliest Platonic translations. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1464:** *De doctrina Platonis* of Alcinous. A Latin translation of the ancient Platonic handbook, presented with Ficino’s first group of ten translated dialogues. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1464:** *Definitiones Platonicae* of Speusippus. A Latin translation of definitions attributed to Speusippus, also incorporated into Ficino’s introductory Platonic dossier. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1464:** *De morte* of Xenocrates. Ficino’s Latin translation of a text attributed to Xenocrates; modern attribution of the ancient work is not secure. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1462–1469; revised through 1484:** *Platonis Opera omnia*. The first complete Latin translation of the Platonic corpus available in print: thirty-five dialogues and the Letters, including works now judged spurious or doubtful; it was first printed in 1484 after prolonged revision. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1468:** *Monarchia* of Dante. Ficino’s Italian modernization or vernacular translation of Dante’s Latin political treatise, made to place Dante within a Florentine Platonic lineage. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1484–1486; printed 1492:** *Plotini Enneades*. The first complete Greek-to-Latin translation of Plotinus’s *Enneads*, conceived together with Ficino’s commentary and completed on 16 January 1486 before further editorial work. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *De mysteriis Aegyptiorum, Chaldaeorum, Assyriorum* of Iamblichus. A Latin translation-paraphrase rather than a strictly literal version, made as part of Ficino’s post-Plotinian anthology of ancient theology. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *De abstinentia* of Porphyry, excerpts. Ficino translated only selected portions of Porphyry’s work, so incompleteness is an essential property of the surviving object. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *De occasionibus* of Porphyry. A Latin translation transmitted in Ficino’s Neoplatonic anthology. Older catalogues sometimes assign the work differently, making the ancient authorship less stable than Ficino’s own translatorial role. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1489:** *Prisciani Lydi Interpretationes in Theophrastum*. Latin translations of Priscian of Lydia on Theophrastus’s *De sensu* and on imagination and intellect, dedicated to Filippo Valori on 25 March. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *De somniis* of Synesius. Ficino’s Latin translation of Synesius on dreams, dedicated to Piero de’ Medici. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *De daemonibus* of Michael Psellus. A Latin translation of Psellus’s demonological dialogue, dedicated with the Synesius translation to Piero de’ Medici. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *Excerpta ex Procli commentariis in Alcibiadem primum*. Selected Latin translations from Proclus’s commentary on Plato’s *First Alcibiades*; they are explicitly excerpts, not a completed version of the whole commentary. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **by 1489:** *De sacrificio et magia* of Proclus. A Latin translation of a short work on sacrifice and magic whose Greek original is lost, making Ficino’s version also a unique witness to the ancient text. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1480s–1490s:** *Elementatio theologica* and *Elementatio physica* of Proclus, fragments. Ficino translated and used portions of Proclus’s *Elements of Theology* and *Elements of Physics*, but no complete translation survives; these are fragmentary projects rather than finished books. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **1492:** *Excerpta ex Proclo in Rempublicam Platonis*. A selected Latin translation from Proclus’s commentary on the *Republic*, sent with its own proem and later incorporated into Ficino’s letters. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1490–1491:** *Mystica theologia* of Pseudo-Dionysius. Ficino’s Latin translation, completed in December 1490 and subsequently joined to his extensive commentary. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1491:** *De divinis nominibus* of Pseudo-Dionysius. Ficino’s Latin translation of the *Divine Names*, undertaken during the spring and summer and accompanied by an expansive interpretive apparatus. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1492:** *Simulacrum Iovis* of Porphyry. A short Latin translation or adaptation sent to Martin “Uranius” Prenninger; it belongs to the porous boundary between antiquarian excerpt, translation, and Ficino’s own theological compilation. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1490s:** *De resurrectione mortuorum* of Athenagoras, fragments or translation work. Ficino encountered and translated material from the treatise after new Greek manuscripts reached Florence; the extent and final form of the project are uncertain. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))

### Medical protocol

- **1479; printed 1481:** *Consiglio contro la pestilenza*. A vernacular medical protocol for avoiding and treating plague, written during an epidemic; its practical instructions make it an intervention in public health rather than simply a philosophical book. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))

### Letters, sermons, orations, and short forms

- **1454–1499:** *Epistolae*. Ficino made a vast correspondence and then remade selected letters into a deliberately ordered twelve-book literary-philosophical corpus containing letters, treatises, dialogues, consolations, apologues, and public addresses; the collected corpus is therefore an authored architecture, not a neutral archive, and additional letters survive outside it. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1499:** *Epistolae extravagantes*. Letters excluded from Ficino’s twelve-book collection and recovered from manuscripts; they resist being called one work because their grouping is modern and editorial, although each letter is a made text. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1476–1477:** *Declamatiunculae ad genus humanum*. Several short exhortatory addresses to humankind; their dispersed transmission and uncertain collective title make them a cluster rather than a securely designed book. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1478:** *Sermoni morali della stultitia et miseria degli huomini*. Vernacular moral sermons completed in June, themselves adaptations of Latin epistles; they cross the categories of sermon, translation, and moral essay. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1479:** *Orphica comparatio Solis ad Deum*. An epistolary solar meditation comparing the sun with God, later enlarged and reused in Ficino’s mature solar works. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1492:** *Laudes seculi nostri tamquam aurei*. A letter-or-panegyric proclaiming Ficino’s own age golden because of its recoveries in learning and the arts; it acts as a cultural manifesto as much as it describes its century. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1499:** *Predicationes*. A surviving corpus of Latin sermons reflecting Ficino’s work as a priest and preacher; the modern critical edition treats them collectively, although individual sermons originated as performances on separate occasions. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **before 1499:** *Apologi de voluptate quattuor*. Four brief moral-philosophical fables about pleasure incorporated into the epistolary collection; their form is apologue rather than systematic treatise. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **before 1499:** *Nugae*. Minor verses and occasional pieces gathered under this title by Kristeller; the collection is an editorial creation, not a book Ficino demonstrably designed, and some component attributions remain correspondingly fragile. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1498:** *Apologia contra Savonarolam*. A polemical manifesto repudiating Girolamo Savonarola after his fall; its sincerity and circumstances have been debated, but Ficino’s authorship is represented in the manuscript and critical tradition. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))

### Reference and manuscript objects

- **before 1462:** *Lessico greco-latino*. Ficino’s working Greek–Latin lexicon, preserved in manuscript; it is a tool made for reading and translating rather than a literary work intended for publication. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
- **1455:** *Riccardiana MS 135: annotated Aristotle*. Ficino’s hand-copy and dense annotation of Leonardo Bruni’s Latin *Nicomachean Ethics* and *Economics*; this is a scholarly artifact and record of study, not an authored text in the usual sense. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1455:** *Ambrosiana MS S.14 sup.: copied philosophical miscellany*. A manuscript copied by Ficino containing Apuleius, Calcidius, Plato in Bruni’s translation, and Cicero; Ficino made the codex but did not author its component texts. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1456:** *Riccardiana MS 709: Hermetic and philosophical miscellany*. A partly autograph compilation of Hermetic, Platonic, Stoic, Ciceronian, and Christian materials with Ficino’s notes; it resists classification as a “work” because its making lies in selection, copying, ordering, and annotation. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1490:** *Liber qui inscribitur Homo*. A title and proem sent to Lorenzo de’ Medici, but it is unclear whether “Man” designated a planned new book or a collection of existing pieces; this is an attested project whose completed work, if any, cannot be identified. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))

### Performances and institutional projects

- **from the 1460s:** *Singing of Orphic hymns to the lyre*. Ficino reportedly performed ancient hymns in his own Latin versions with instrumental accompaniment; this was a recurrent philosophical-religious manifestation, not a securely notated musical composition, and no complete Ficinian musical score survives to make it a conventional work.
- **1460s–1490s:** *The Platonic Academy of Florence*. Ficino cultivated a network of patrons, pupils, correspondents, readers, and “fellow Platonists,” but the familiar image of a continuously organized academy at Careggi is a contested later construction. It is safer to call it a social and pedagogical project than an institution Ficino single-handedly made. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/marsilio-ficino_(Dizionario-Biografico)/))
- **1462–1499:** *Renovatio Platonica*. Ficino’s lifelong project of translating, interpreting, teaching, publishing, and Christianizing Plato and the ancient theological succession from Hermes through the later Platonists. It is not one text, and its unfinished projected commentary on all Plato is part of the work rather than merely a failure of completion. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/ficino/))
