## Works by Pasquale Simonetti

### Documentary fabrications

- **1940s:** Untitled counterfeit ration books. These were forged administrative documents produced to obtain food for black-market resale; no surviving examples, individual dates, quantities, or fixed titles are identified in the scholarly account, so they constitute a dispersed and possibly lost corpus rather than a conventional textual oeuvre. ([Allum, pp. 142–45](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/5085/1/FulltextThesis.pdf))

### Criminal-commercial systems

- **Late 1940s–early 1950s:** Untitled cigarette-smuggling and distribution operation. Simonetti participated in and helped organize illicit distribution, but the system depended on foreign suppliers and local *guappi* and cannot securely be attributed to him as a self-contained obra; the sources describe an activity and network, not a named work or project. ([Allum, pp. 209–13](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/5085/1/FulltextThesis.pdf))

- **Early 1950s–1955:** Untitled fruit-and-vegetable market mediation and price-control regime. A jointly produced criminal-commercial system through which Simonetti mediated between rural producers and urban buyers and became known as a “president of prices”; it resists classification as a work because it was an evolving practice of market control, contested and shared with figures including Alfredo Maisto and Antonio Esposito, rather than a bounded creation. ([Allum, “Becoming a camorrista,” pp. 340–42](https://doi.org/10.1080/13545710110084262); [Brancaccio, “Napoli. Lo stato della città e la camorra dei mercati”](https://www.lavoroculturale.org/camorra-dei-mercati/luciano-brancaccio/2016/))

### Attributed acts that are not works

- **1952:** Shoot-out and attempted killing arising from territorial conflict with Alfredo Maisto’s group. This is a documented criminal act for which Simonetti was convicted, not an obra, project, text, or creation; it is recorded here only to prevent an event in his biography from being silently converted into a “work.” ([Allum, pp. 142–45, 212–13](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/5085/1/FulltextThesis.pdf))

- **Date uncertain:** Alleged slapping of Lucky Luciano. This anecdote is not a work and its attribution to Simonetti is rejected in the scholarly account, which identifies Francesco Pirozzi as the actor; it therefore does not belong to Simonetti’s made corpus. ([Allum, pp. 142–45](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/5085/1/FulltextThesis.pdf))

### Corpus status

- No standard bibliography, catalogue raisonné, collected-works edition, or discography exists for Simonetti, who is treated by scholarship as a *guappo* and criminal-market operator rather than an author or artist. The sources document no books, songs, films, manifestos, protocols, buildings, or titled artworks made by him; Francesco Rosi’s *La sfida* is a later film inspired by his life, not Simonetti’s work. ([Allum, *The Neapolitan Camorra*, pp. 142–45](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/bitstream/2438/5085/1/FulltextThesis.pdf); [Allum, “Becoming a camorrista”](https://doi.org/10.1080/13545710110084262))
