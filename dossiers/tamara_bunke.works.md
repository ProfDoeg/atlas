## Works by Haydée Tamara Bunke Bíder

### Autobiographical and personal writings

- **1956–1966:** *Letters to family, friends, and comrades*. A dispersed correspondence rather than a single titled obra; *Tania, la guerrillera inolvidable* reproduces or excerpts letters to her parents and others, including dated letters from 1956, 1957, 22 August 1960, 31 October 1963, 11 April 1964, and 15 October 1964. No complete scholarly edition or definitive inventory of the surviving letters has been published, so individual completeness cannot be claimed. [Sources 1–3]

- **1963–1964:** *First page of a diary*. A single surviving or reproduced page on which Bunke copied a sentence attributed to Nikolai Ostrovsky; the 1970 documentary biography calls it her diary’s “only page.” It resists classification as a diary proper: the source preserves one page, not evidence of a sustained journal. [Sources 2–3]

- **c. 1963:** *Autobiografía elaborada por el “Caso Tania”*. A secret third-person autobiographical dossier prepared during her selection and training for clandestine work. The archival edition attributes its preparation to Bunke but its bureaucratic voice and case-file form suggest institutional editing; it is therefore both self-narrative and intelligence document. [Source 1, annex 1]

### Operational reports, messages, and constructed texts

- **21 February–1 March 1964:** *Informe de Tania sobre práctica realizada… en Cienfuegos*. A detailed operational report on an eleven-day clandestine exercise, signed “Tania,” combining narrative, self-criticism, technical observations, and recommendations. It is a report rather than a literary work. [Source 1, annex 3]

- **February–March 1964:** *Disguised Cienfuegos diary*. During the exercise Bunke destroyed her written work plan and replaced it with a retrospectively fabricated diary whose apparently personal entries concealed the plan’s instructions. Its present survival is unverified; it is best understood as a camouflage-object or operational fiction rather than an authentic diary. [Source 1, annex 3]

- **February–March 1964:** *Cienfuegos photographic series and photo-documents*. Photographs, maps, copied documents, and undeveloped miniature records made during the exercise as intelligence-training objects. The collected report refers to numbered series and negatives but does not supply a complete catalogue, titles, or confirmed survival history. [Source 1, annexes 2–3]

- **April 1964:** *Mensaje número 3*. A secret report sent from Prague after Bunke’s first operational journey through Western Europe, signed with the code name “Bolívar.” It is simultaneously travel narrative, intelligence assessment, and coded communication. [Source 1, annex 4]

- **1964, before 25 July:** *Mensaje número 6*. A secret message written in Prague before her second journey to West Germany and West Berlin. Bunke herself calls part of it an extract from a diary, but the whole object functions as an operational report and intimate letter addressed through an intelligence channel. [Source 1, annex 5]

- **25 July 1964:** *Leyenda de Laura Gutiérrez Bauer*. A fully elaborated fictive biography—family history, education, finances, itinerary, temperament, and intended ethnological vocation—used to sustain Bunke’s Bolivian identity. The surviving document was transmitted and signed by her handler “Diosdado”; Bunke embodied and probably helped refine it, but sole authorship cannot be assigned to her. It is a collaborative project or operative persona, not simply a text. [Source 1, annex 6]

- **1964–1967:** *Laura Gutiérrez Bauer*. The enacted continuation of the written legend through documents, travel, employment, social relationships, a legal marriage, folklore research, and intelligence work. This life-performance resists being called a discrete “work”: it was a collectively designed clandestine project that Bunke continually made credible through action. [Sources 1 and 4]

- **1964–1966:** *Reports and clandestine communications from Bolivia*. Bunke repeatedly reported through secret writing, coded radio traffic, couriers, and personal contacts; her 1966 oral debrief explicitly refers to information she had “already reported.” The surviving scholarly corpus does not enumerate every transmission, and intelligence mediation makes individual authorship and completeness impossible to establish. [Source 1, annexes 7–9]

- **16 April 1966:** *Informe oral sobre su primer año en Bolivia*. A lengthy oral intelligence report delivered to “Ariel” during a contact in Mexico and preserved in transcription. Bunke supplied the account, but the textual object was produced by an unidentified transcriber and contains editorial insertions and reconstructed questions; it is therefore a documented manifestation of her speech, not an autograph work. [Source 1, annex 9]

### Notebooks and research materials

- **c. 1964–1967:** *Two Bolivian notebooks*. Miscellaneous notebooks attributed to Bunke containing purchases, addresses, Quechua lessons, and notes on socialist literature. The historian Diego Martínez Estévez stresses that they are not campaign diaries; their exact dates, titles, present archival location, and completeness remain unstated. [Source 5]

- **c. 1965–1967:** *Bolivian folklore and music collection*. A large group of magnetic tapes containing songs and folk music, especially music of the Altiplano, was seized from Bunke’s residence. Sources do not establish whether she recorded every tape herself, copied commercial or institutional recordings, or assembled material made by others; the collection is therefore securely hers as a research assemblage but not securely hers as sound-recordist or performer. [Source 1, chapter XIII]

- **c. 1965–1967:** *Folklore and ethnological research project*. Under the Laura Gutiérrez Bauer identity, Bunke cultivated contacts with folklore researchers and collected music while presenting herself as a specialist in folklore, archaeology, and anthropology. Because this research also served as operational cover and no finished study is known, it is an unfinished, double-purpose project rather than a conventional scholarly obra. [Source 1, annexes 6 and 9]

### Broadcasts and performances

- **1961–1963:** *Radio Habana Cuba programs supporting the Nicaraguan revolutionary movement*. Bunke’s secret autobiography says that she collaborated with Nicaraguan activists first on Radio Habana Cuba programs and later on plans that did not materialize. The corpus provides no program titles, dates, scripts, or precise division of authorship, so these can be named only as collaborative broadcasts, not as individually catalogued works. [Source 1, annex 1]

- **1950s–1960s:** *Latin American folk-music performances*. Bunke performed on accordion, guitar, and piano in political, social, and cultural settings in the GDR and Cuba. These were ephemeral manifestations rather than compositions or released recordings: no scholarly discography, stable titles, or evidence that she authored the music has been located. [Sources 1 and 4]

### Revolutionary project

- **1963–1967:** *“Caso Tania” / participation in Operación Fantasma and the Bolivian guerrilla project*. Bunke’s training, clandestine network-building, logistical work, and eventual incorporation into the Ejército de Liberación Nacional constituted the overarching project within which most of the reports, identities, photographs, recordings, and communications above were made. It was collective, unfinished, and directed by others as well as enacted by her; calling it her individual “work” would erase that disputed and distributed authorship. [Sources 1 and 4]

### Sources

- **1.** Ulises Estrada Lescaille, *Tania la guerrillera y la epopeya suramericana del Che* (Ocean Press, 2005), especially annexes 1–9. This collected-document edition draws on the “Caso Tania” files in the Historical Archive of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Cuba and reproduces Bunke’s autobiography, operational report, messages, constructed legend, and oral debrief: [complete PDF](https://oceansur.com/uploads/catalogue/publications/files/tania-guerrillera.pdf).

- **2.** Marta Rojas, Mirta Rodríguez Calderón, and Ulises Estrada, *Tania, la guerrillera inolvidable* (Instituto del Libro, 1970; corrected and enlarged edition, Editorial de Ciencias Sociales, 2001). The 1970 documentary photobook is the foundational assembled corpus of Bunke’s letters, photographs, and operational documents; its publication and revision history are documented in [Estrada’s 2005 edition](https://oceansur.com/uploads/catalogue/publications/files/tania-guerrillera.pdf).

- **3.** María Fernanda Pampín, “Tania, historia de una mujer latinoamericana,” a scholarly analysis of the textual and photographic construction of *Tania, la guerrillera inolvidable*, identifying its letters, reports, photographs, and chapter “Única página de su diario”: [Dialnet PDF](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/5837663.pdf).

- **4.** “Bunke, Tamara,” *Diccionario Biográfico de las Izquierdas Latinoamericanas*, CeDInCI. Scholarly biographical entry documenting her names, aliases, political trajectory, translation, cultural activity, and clandestine mission: [entry](https://diccionario.cedinci.org/bunke-tamara/).

- **5.** Diego Martínez Estévez, discussion of the surviving Bolivian documentation, distinguishing Bunke’s two miscellaneous notebooks from a nonexistent campaign diary: [historical commentary](https://martinezestevez.wordpress.com/).
