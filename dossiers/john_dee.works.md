## Works by John Dee

### Printed mathematical, astronomical, and natural-philosophical works

- **1557:** *Epistola praefixa Ephemeridibus Joannis Felde Angli*. A prefatory letter to John Field’s ephemerides explaining the method Dee had given Field for constructing them; Dee counted it as a separate printed work in his own retrospective catalogue. [Dee, *Compendious Rehearsal*, catalogue no. 7](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1558; revised 1568:** *Propaedeumata Aphoristica, de praestantioribus quibusdam naturae virtutibus*. A sequence of 120 aphorisms offering a mathematical and astrological natural philosophy; the substantially revised second edition is another state of the same obra, not a separate title. Dee reported that contemporaries had attributed its ideas to Mercator, Ursus, or Alcabitius, an attribution he vehemently rejected. [Shumaker’s critical edition](https://search.worldcat.org/title/John-Dee-on-astronomy-Propaedeumata-aphoristica-%281558-and-1568%29/oclc/3753479); [Dee’s defence of authorship](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1564:** *Monas Hieroglyphica*. A manifesto-like exposition of Dee’s composite “hieroglyphic monad”: simultaneously a printed treatise, symbolic image, cosmological argument, and program for transforming knowledge—almost a miniature Gesamtkunstwerk. [Nicholas H. Clulee, *John Dee’s Natural Philosophy*](https://books.google.com/books?id=Jw8yAAAAMAAJ); [Stephen Clucas, “The Hermetic Frontispiece”](https://doi.org/10.1080/00026980.2017.1353247)

- **1570:** *Epistola ad Fredericum Commandinum*, prefixed to *De superficierum divisionibus* attributed to Muḥammad al-Baghdādī. A dedicatory scholarly letter attached to Commandino’s edition, whose production Dee said he helped bring about. [Dee, *Compendious Rehearsal*, catalogue no. 3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1570:** *The Mathematicall Praeface* to Henry Billingsley’s English Euclid. An independent manifesto for the mathematical arts embedded inside another man’s translation; it maps mathematics into practical and speculative disciplines and therefore resists being treated merely as a preface. [University of Michigan digital text](https://quod.lib.umich.edu/e/eebo/A00429.0001.001); [Dee, catalogue no. 5](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1570:** *Annotations and Mathematical Inventions added to the English Euclid*. Notes, demonstrations, and inventions dispersed through and after Book X of Billingsley’s Euclid; these are a distributed scholarly apparatus rather than a self-contained book. [Dee, catalogue no. 6](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1573:** *Parallaticae Commentationis Praxeosque Nucleus quidam*. A short printed nucleus of parallax theory and practice. Its survival and bibliographical identity are problematic, but Dee unequivocally included it among his printed works. [Dee, catalogue no. 8](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

### Unprinted mathematical, astronomical, and mechanical treatises

- **1547:** *The Art of Logicke*. An English logical treatise, now lost; its existence rests principally on Dee’s late autobiographical catalogue. [Dee, catalogue no. 15](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1548:** *The Thirteen Sophistical Fallacies, with Their Discoveries*. A mnemonic or pedagogical protocol written in English verse, now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 16](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1549:** *Mercurius Caelestis*. A twenty-four-book work reportedly written at Leuven and now lost; Dee’s earlier 1592 list dated it 1550, so its date is not wholly stable. [Dee, 1594 catalogue no. 17 and 1592 list](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1550:** *Prolegomena et Dictata Parisiensia in Euclidis Elementorum Geometricorum librum primum et secundum*. Lectures or lecture notes on the first two books of Euclid, associated with Dee’s public teaching in Paris; they are lost and resist a clean distinction between oral performance and text. [Dee, catalogue no. 13](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1550:** *De usu Globi Coelestis*. A treatise on using the celestial globe, addressed to Edward VI and now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 14](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1551:** *De nubium, solis, lunae, ac reliquorum planetarum … distantiis … et magnitudine*. A demonstrative astronomical book on the distances, intervals, and magnitudes of the clouds, celestial bodies, and starry sphere, dedicated to Edward VI; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 18](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1553:** *Aphorismi Astrologici*. A collection of 300 astrological aphorisms in Dee’s revised catalogue, although his 1592 list says 800; the numerical extent is therefore disputed, and the work is lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 19](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1553:** *The True Cause and Account (Not Vulgar) of Floods and Ebbs*. A tidal treatise written at the request of Jane Dudley, duchess of Northumberland; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 20](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1553:** *The Philosophical and Poetical Original Occasions of the Configurations and Names of the Heavenly Asterisms*. An account of constellational forms and names, also commissioned by the duchess of Northumberland; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 21](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1553:** *Astronomical and Logistical Rules and Canons to Calculate the Ephemerides*. A practical computational protocol prepared for Richard Chancellor’s final voyage to Muscovy; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 22](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1555:** *De Acribologia Mathematica*. A large sixteen-book project on mathematical exactness, now lost; its scale makes it one of Dee’s major vanished obras. [Dee, catalogue no. 23](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1556:** *Inventum Mechanicum Paradoxum de nova ratione delineandi Circumferentiam Circularem*. A mechanical invention and its textual explanation, concerning a new way to draw a circular circumference; the device and treatise are lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 24](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1557:** *De Speculis Comburentibus*. A six-book optical work on burning mirrors; only a fragment and associated diagrams survive in Cotton MS Vitellius C VII, so the complete obra is lost. [British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius C VII](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011); [Dee, catalogue no. 25](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1557:** *De Perspectiva illa, qua peritissimi utuntur Pictores*. A treatise on painters’ perspective, now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 26](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1557:** *Speculum Unitatis, sive Apologia pro Fratre Rogerio Bachone Anglo*. An apologetic defence of Roger Bacon as a lawful natural philosopher rather than a demonic magician; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 27](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1557:** *De Annuli Astronomici multiplici usu*. A two-book protocol for the multiple uses of an astronomical ring, now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 28](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1557:** *Paradoxall Compasse*. A navigational instrument or method invented, according to Dee, in this year and later incorporated into his projected *Brytish Complement*. It resists classification as a separate work because no securely identified object survives and the invention is known chiefly through Dee’s textual claims. [Dee, catalogue no. 10](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1558:** *Trochilica Inventa*. A two-book collection of Dee’s mechanical inventions involving wheels, pulleys, or rotary motion; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 29](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1558:** *Peri Anabibasmon Theologikon*. A three-book theological work whose Greek title is transmitted imperfectly in the surviving witnesses and editions; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 30](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1559:** *De tertia et praecipua Perspectivae parte, quae de Radiorum fractione tractat*. A three-book demonstrative treatise on refraction, understood as the third and principal part of perspective; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 31](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1560:** *De Itinere Subterraneo*. A two-book work on subterranean travel or investigation, now lost; the title alone does not establish whether Dee meant mining, natural philosophy, or an imaginative itinerary. [Dee, catalogue no. 32](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1560:** *De Triangulorum Rectilineorum Areis*. Three demonstrative books on the areas of rectilinear triangles, addressed to Pedro Nunes; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 33](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1562:** *Cabalae Hebraicae compendiosa tabella*. A compact table of Hebrew Kabbalah, now lost; as a diagrammatic table it may have been a visual knowledge-object rather than a continuous prose text. [Dee, catalogue no. 34](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1565:** *De Trigono Circinoque Analogico*. A four-book mathematical and mechanical work on an analogical triangle and compass; a manuscript with diagrams survives, though whether it represents all four books is uncertain. [British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius C VII](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011); [Dee, catalogue no. 36](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1573:** *De Stella Admiranda in Cassiopeiae Asterismo*. A three-book study of the 1572 “new star” in Cassiopeia and its supposed movement through the planetary spheres; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 37](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1573:** *Hipparchus Redivivus*. A short astronomical treatise, “Hipparchus Revived,” now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 38](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1591:** *De hominis Corpore, Spiritu, et Anima, sive Microcosmicum totius Philosophiae Naturalis Compendium*. A one-book compendium treating the human body, spirit, and soul as a microcosm of natural philosophy; apparently lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 48](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1590s, exact year uncertain:** *Elegans et utilis libellus de arte mensurandi cum circino et regula*. An illustrated manual on measurement with compass and rule; its manuscript date is damaged or incomplete, and it survives in Cotton MS Vitellius C VII. [British Library manuscript description](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011)

### Statecraft, navigation, empire, and historical projects

- **1556:** *A Supplication to Queen Mary for the Recovery and Preservation of Ancient Writers and Monuments*. A proposal for rescuing manuscripts and establishing a royal library; it is a manifesto and institutional project rather than merely a petition, and the proposed institution was not realized. [Crossley, *Autobiographical Tracts*, pp. 46–49](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1565 or 1570:** *Reipublicae Britannicae Synopsis*. An English schematic synopsis of the British commonwealth, trade, ethics, and political strength. Dee dated it 1565 in his own later catalogue, while William Sherman treats the surviving manuscript as a work of 1570; the dating should therefore remain disputed. [William H. Sherman, *John Dee: The Politics of Reading and Writing in the English Renaissance*](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/); [Dee, catalogue no. 35](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1576–1577:** *The Brytish Monarchy*, otherwise *The Petty Navy Royal* and conventionally *General and Rare Memorials Pertayning to the Perfect Arte of Navigation*. The only printed part of an intended four-volume national-maritime project, combining naval policy, finance, navigation, visual emblems, and imperial argument; the larger project remained incomplete. [Sherman, *John Dee*, contents and chapters](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/); [Dee, catalogue no. 4](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1576–1577:** *A Necessary Advertizement*. A pseudonymous prefatory manifesto printed within *General and Rare Memorials* as the words of an “unknown friend,” although Crossley and later scholars identify the friend as Dee himself. Its deliberately displaced authorship makes it both part of the larger book and an independent rhetorical act. [Crossley’s editorial account and text](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1576, revised or copied 1577:** *The First Great Volume of Famous and Rich Discoveries*. A vast historical-geographical compilation covering Solomon’s Ophir voyages, Prester John, the Great Khan, northern islands, and maritime discoveries; a long but defective manuscript survives without its first five chapters. [British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius C VII](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011); [Dee, catalogue no. 9](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1576:** *The Brytish Complement of the Perfect Art of Navigation*. An unprinted “great volume” of navigational tables, great-circle methods, longitude and latitude procedures, compass-variation techniques, and other inventions. It was conceived as another component of Dee’s maritime Gesamtkunstwerk but is lost or was never completed in the projected form. [Dee, catalogue no. 10](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1576–1578:** *Brytanici Imperii Limites*. A manuscript compilation defining the historical and geographical limits of a claimed British empire; the surviving manuscript is in another hand, and scholarship distinguishes the compilation from the particular tract bearing the same title within it. [Ken MacMillan, “John Dee’s ‘Brytanici Imperii Limites’”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3817919); [British Library Add MS 59681 discussion](https://www.hyperdream.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/dee_on-_empire.-limits.pdf)

- **1577:** *Declaration of Queen Elizabeth’s Title to Greenland, Estotiland, and Friseland*. A royal-title argument presented to Elizabeth and Francis Walsingham; it may survive within the *Limites* compilation rather than as an autonomous authorial manuscript. [MacMillan, “John Dee’s ‘Brytanici Imperii Limites’”](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3817919)

- **1577:** *Discourse of the Queen’s Majesty’s Title to the Sovereignty of the Sea*. An imperial-legal discourse known from Dee’s records; its precise relationship to the *Limites* materials and later *Thalattokratia* is uncertain, and no securely independent complete text survives. [Sherman, *John Dee*, maritime-empire chapters](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/)

- **1578:** *Her Majesty’s Title Royal to Many Foreign Countries, Kingdoms, and Provinces*. Twelve vellum skins prepared at Elizabeth’s command: simultaneously historical argument, cartographic display, legal instrument, and presentation object. The original set is lost, though related matter survives in *Brytanici Imperii Limites*. [Dee, catalogue no. 11](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf); [MacMillan](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3817919)

- **1579:** *De Imperatoris Nomine, Authoritate, et Potentia*. An English political tract on the emperor’s name, authority, and power, dedicated to Elizabeth; it may correspond to a section preserved in the *Limites* compilation, but Dee catalogued it separately. [Dee, catalogue no. 12](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf); [Sherman, *John Dee*](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/)

- **1580:** *Two Rolls of the Queen’s Majesty’s Title*. Two large presentation rolls given to Elizabeth and Lord Burghley, probably distillations of Dee’s imperial research. They are lost, and scholarship cautions against silently treating them as merely alternate titles for an extant treatise. [MacMillan](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3817919)

- **c. 1580:** *A Brief Remembraunce of Sondrye Foreyne Regions, Discovered, Inhabited, and Partlie Conquered by the Subjects of This Brytish Monarchie*. A concise tabular or cartographic memorial of English overseas claims, surviving in Cotton MS Augustus I.i.1; it is related to, but materially distinct from, Dee’s larger imperial works. [MacMillan](https://www.jstor.org/stable/3817919)

- **1580:** *Atlantidis … emendatior descriptio Hydrographica*. A revised hydrographic description or chart of the region commonly called the West Indies, claimed by Dee to improve on all published predecessors; no securely identified copy survives. [Dee, catalogue no. 41](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1580:** *Navigationis ad Cathayum per Septentrionalia Scythiae et Tartariae litora Delineatio Hydrographica*. A hydrographic route-design to Cathay, supplied to Arthur Pet and Charles Jackman with annotations on subpolar islands; it was a working chart or navigational protocol, not simply a book, and is lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 43](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1581:** *De modo Evangelii Jesu Christi publicandi, propagandi, stabiliendique inter infideles Atlanticos*. A four-book project for publishing and establishing Christianity among Atlantic peoples, with separate books addressed to Elizabeth, her councillors, Philip II, and the pope; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 42](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1582:** *An Advice and Discourse about the Reformation of the Vulgar Julian Year*. A two-book calendar-reform proposal written at the command of Elizabeth and the Privy Council. The government did not adopt it, so it survives historically as both text and unrealized reform project. [Dee, catalogue no. 46](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1583:** *Hemisphaerii Borealis Geographica atque Hydrographica Descriptio*. A northern-hemisphere geographical and hydrographic chart given to English navigators bound toward northern Atlantic shores; it is lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 44](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1583:** *The Originals and Chief Points of Our Ancient British Histories, Discoursed upon and Examined*. A critical historical work on Britain’s foundational narratives; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 45](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1597:** *Thalattokratia Brettanike, sive De Brytanico Maris Imperio Collectanea Extemporanea*. A rapidly composed collection on British dominion of the sea, which Dee said he wrote in four days at Manchester. It is a late political memorandum rather than the completion of his earlier maritime system. [Sherman, *John Dee*](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/); [Dee’s appended notice](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

### Religious, philosophical, heraldic, and polemical works

- **1570:** *De unico Mago, et triplici Herode, eosque Antichristiano*. A one-book religious or apocalyptic treatise on the one Magus and threefold Herod as Antichristian figures; now lost. [Dee, catalogue no. 39](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1574:** *Ten Sundry and Very Rare Heraldical Blasonings of One Crest or Cognisance*. A book of ten symbolic interpretations of a crest legally confirmed to Dee’s ancestral arms; it combined heraldry, image, genealogy, and philosophy and is now lost. Dee’s 1592 list described the work in the singular as his “hieroglyphical and philosophical blason,” making its exact internal form uncertain. [Dee, catalogue no. 40](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1592:** *Certain Considerations and Conferrings Together of Three Sentences: Nosce te ipsum; Homo homini Deus; Homo homini lupus*. A comparative moral-philosophical discourse on three proverbial “oracles”; Dee later envisioned an enlarged and unusually presented continuation, so the work shades into an unfinished project. [Dee, catalogue no. 47](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1594–1595:** *De Horizonte Aeternitatis*. A projected three-book reply to Andreas Libavius’s criticism of a phrase in *Monas Hieroglyphica*, divided into mathematical-physical, theological-metaphysical-mathematical, and “hierotechnic” books. Dee announces what he intends to write, so it resists being called a completed work; no finished text is known. [Dee, *A Letter Containing a Most Brief Discourse Apologeticall*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1604:** *To the King’s Most Excellent Majesty*. A printed supplication asking James I for a formal trial to clear Dee of accusations of conjuring; it is a manifesto-like juridical act rather than a descriptive treatise. [Oxford Text Archive record](https://ota.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/repository/xmlui/handle/20.500.12024/A20081)

### Spiritual conferences, revealed books, and ritual systems

- **1581–1583:** *Mysteriorum Libri Quinque*. Five manuscript books recording Dee’s earliest surviving “actions” or conferences with spirits, initially through Barnabas Saul and principally through Edward Kelley. Dee is scribe, questioner, editor, and ritual participant, but much of the speech is attributed within the records to Kelley’s visions or angelic speakers; authorship therefore cannot be reduced to ordinary sole composition. [British Library, Sloane MS 3188](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002115572); [Joseph H. Peterson’s scholarly edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=6wU2AAAAQBAJ)

- **1582–1583:** *Heptarchia Mystica*. A developing ritual and political-theological system of seven angelic kings and princes, preserved in several authorial redactions rather than one stable finished text. It is simultaneously revelation, Dee’s editorial construction, diagrammatic system, and protocol for action. [British Library, Sloane MSS 3188–3189 descriptions](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002115572); [Peterson’s edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=6wU2AAAAQBAJ)

- **1583:** *Liber Loagaeth*, also called *Liber Mysteriorum Sextus et Sanctus*. A “revealed” book of forty-nine large letter-tables produced through Kelley’s scrying and Dee’s transcription and arrangement. It strongly resists the category of authored work: Dee treated it as a manifestation dictated by angels, while the material manuscript is plainly the result of collaborative human labour. [British Library, Sloane MS 3189; manuscript bibliography](https://www.arcane-archive.org/occultism/magic/ceremonial/enochian/bibliography-of-enochian-and-diary-mss-1.php)

- **1583–1584:** *Liber Mysteriorum Sextus et Sanctus Parallelus* and *Liber Mysteriorum Septimus*. Continuing records of the spiritual actions after the five-book sequence; their titles and numbering must not be confused with the differently constituted *Liber Loagaeth*. They survive within Cotton Appendix MS XLVI and related copies. [Manuscript bibliography](https://www.arcane-archive.org/occultism/magic/ceremonial/enochian/bibliography-of-enochian-and-diary-mss-1.php)

- **1584:** *The Forty-Eight Angelic Keys or Calls*. A sequence dictated during the spiritual conferences in an otherwise unattested “angelic” language, with English renderings for most calls. They function as ritual protocols and alleged revelations; Dee recorded and organized them, but the diaries attribute their verbal manifestation to angelic mediation through Kelley. [Deborah E. Harkness, *John Dee’s Conversations with Angels*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/john-dees-conversations-with-angels/B490499C3A92B0E424D42F55A5A357E0); [manuscript bibliography](https://www.arcane-archive.org/occultism/magic/ceremonial/enochian/bibliography-of-enochian-and-diary-mss-1.php)

- **1584:** *Tabula Recensa* or “Great Table.” A forty-nine-by-forty-nine letter-table produced in the Kraków conferences as a corrected or renewed ritual object. It is at once text, diagram, instrument, and claimed revelation, and thus resists treatment as a conventionally authored work. [Harkness, *John Dee’s Conversations with Angels*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/john-dees-conversations-with-angels/B490499C3A92B0E424D42F55A5A357E0)

- **1583–1589, with later entries:** *Spiritual Diaries after the Five Books of Mystery*. An extended sequence of action-books documenting visions, prayers, instructions, journeys, alchemical experiments, and conflicts involving Dee, Kelley, their households, and claimed spirits. Meric Casaubon printed a selective and sometimes defective version in 1659 as *A True & Faithful Relation*; the manuscript corpus is not one book and its collaborative, reportorial form resists ordinary authorship. [Harkness](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/john-dees-conversations-with-angels/B490499C3A92B0E424D42F55A5A357E0); [manuscript bibliography](https://www.arcane-archive.org/occultism/magic/ceremonial/enochian/bibliography-of-enochian-and-diary-mss-1.php)

- **1582–1584:** *Sigillum Dei Aemeth diagrams and ritual furniture designs*. Dee drew and recorded instructions for a great seal, four supporting seals, a square “table of practice,” an AGLA cross, and related arrangements. The designs are Dee’s documentary work, but the diaries present them as revealed protocols; fabrication may have involved other hands, and the original wooden table does not survive. [British Museum research on the associated objects](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2011.12.001)

### Maps, diagrams, instruments, and ritual objects

- **March 1558:** *Inventa Joannis Dee circa … parabolam*. An illustrated manuscript fragment on the parabolic section of a right cone, associated with *De Speculis Comburentibus*. It survives as mathematical diagrams and demonstrations rather than as a complete treatise. [British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius C VII](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011)

- **Date uncertain, sixteenth century:** *Diagrams of image formation in complex and concave mirrors*. A set of annotated optical drawings surviving in Dee’s hand; their relationship to *De Speculis Comburentibus* is probable but not conclusively fixed. [British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius C VII](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011)

- **1582 or later:** *Three surviving wax magical discs*. One large engraved seal and two smaller supports closely match the dimensions and inscriptions in Dee’s ritual records. Their association with Dee is strong but not absolutely proven, and the hand that physically engraved them is unknown; they are therefore manifestations of his ritual design rather than securely autograph objects. [British Museum, large magical disc](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1838-1232-90-a); [Turner, “The Lord of the Smoking Mirror”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2011.12.001)

- **1584 or later:** *Gold disc engraved with the Vision of the Four Castles*. A ritual image associated with a vision recorded at Kraków. Its traditional connection with Dee’s practice is documented, but neither its manufacture by Dee nor its continuous provenance can be established. [British Museum overview](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/dr-dee-s-magic/jgH1be-D72po1Q)

- **Not made by Dee; associated later:** *Obsidian mirror, crystal show-stones, and related scrying objects*. These objects are traditionally called “Dee’s,” but they should not be counted straightforwardly among things he made: the obsidian mirror was manufactured in Mexico, and the provenance of several crystals is uncertain. Their resistance to inclusion is itself significant—they are tools attributed to his practice, not securely his creations. [Cambridge archaeological study of the obsidian mirror](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2021.132); [British Museum object study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2011.12.001)

### Catalogues, diaries, petitions, and self-fashioning texts

- **1577–1601, intermittently:** *Private Diary*. A daybook recording travel, weather, dreams, illnesses, finances, visitors, family events, experiments, and court business. It is not a composed literary work in the usual sense but an accumulating documentary practice, and the surviving record has gaps. [Halliwell’s edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/19553/19553-h/19553-h.htm); [Cambridge reissue description](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/john-dees-diary-catalogue-of-manuscripts-and-selected-letters/229094F682333A909A643699BC372C95)

- **1583:** *Catalogus Librorum Bibliothecae Externae Mortlacensis*. Dee’s manuscript catalogue of his Mortlake library, preserving a designed map of a collection of printed books, manuscripts, instruments, and scholarly resources. It is both finding protocol and intellectual self-portrait, not merely an administrative list. [Trinity College Cambridge, MS O.4.20](https://mss-cat.trin.cam.ac.uk/Manuscript/O.4.20/); [Roberts and Watson, *John Dee’s Library Catalogue*](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/jc95hvbn)

- **1583:** *Catalogue of Dee’s Manuscripts*. A separate inventory of manuscript holdings made before the dispersal of the Mortlake library; like the external catalogue, it is a documentary structure imposed on a larger material obra. [Halliwell’s edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/19553/19553-h/19553-h.htm); [Roberts and Watson](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/jc95hvbn)

- **1592:** *The Compendious Rehearsal of John Dee His Dutiful Declaration and Proof of the Course and Race of His Studious Life*. An autobiographical petition, evidentiary dossier, and self-bibliography presented to royal commissioners. Because it acts to secure redress and patronage, it is as much manifesto and legal performance as life narrative. [British Library, Cotton MS Vitellius C VII](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-001103011); [Crossley’s edition](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1592–1594:** *Appendix to the Compendious Rehearsal*. Dee described an appendix covering “two years and more,” but its status and survival as a distinct finished text are uncertain. It is best treated as an announced continuation rather than silently promoted to a completed work. [Dee, *A Letter Containing a Most Brief Discourse Apologeticall*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **1594–1595; printed 1599 and 1604:** *A Letter Containing a Most Brief Discourse Apologeticall*. A public defence addressed to Archbishop John Whitgift that reprints and expands Dee’s catalogue of works while rebutting charges of conjuring. It is a manifesto of lawful Christian philosophy and a strategic act of self-authorship. [Crossley’s facsimile and editorial account](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/71/Autobiographical_tracts_of_Dr._John_Dee_%28IA_autobiographical00deej%29.pdf)

- **Throughout his working life:** *Letters, memoranda, horoscopes, marginalia, and annotations*. Dee produced a large dispersed documentary body in other people’s books and in correspondence with rulers, courtiers, navigators, scholars, patients, and family members. It resists enumeration as discrete works: Roberts and Watson catalogue the surviving library and annotations, while modern studies stress that reading, correspondence, and manuscript circulation were constitutive parts of Dee’s practice. [Roberts and Watson](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/jc95hvbn); [Sherman, *John Dee*](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/)

### Institutional and collective obra

- **c. 1570s–1583:** *The Mortlake library, museum, laboratory, and scholarly household*. A deliberately assembled working environment of roughly 4,000 books—including about 700 manuscripts—alongside maps, instruments, antiquities, laboratories, and spaces for consultation. It was not authored like a book or built wholly by Dee’s hands, but scholarship treats it as an organized research institution and perhaps his largest material obra; much was dispersed or damaged while he was abroad. [Roberts and Watson](https://wellcomecollection.org/works/jc95hvbn); [Sherman, *John Dee*](https://www.umasspress.com/9780870239403/john-dee/); [study of Dee’s proposed research institute](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsa.2011.12.003)

- **1581–1589:** *The angelic-conversation enterprise*. The encompassing project joining diaries, prayer, scrying performances, invented or manifested language, letter-tables, seals, furniture, travel, and attempted alchemy. No single participant straightforwardly “made” it: Dee organized and recorded it, Kelley supplied most visions and voices, craftsmen produced objects, and the records themselves assign agency to angels; it is therefore best understood as a contested collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk. [Harkness, *John Dee’s Conversations with Angels*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/john-dees-conversations-with-angels/B490499C3A92B0E424D42F55A5A357E0); [British Library, Sloane MS 3188](https://searcharchives.bl.uk/catalog/040-002115572)
