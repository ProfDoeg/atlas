## Works by Leonard Max Adleman

### Dissertation and research monograph

- **1976:** *[Number-Theoretic Aspects of Computational Complexity](https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/134974/files/ERL-m-615.pdf)*. Adleman’s Ph.D. dissertation at the University of California, Berkeley; a book-length mathematical research text supervised by Manuel Blum.

- **1992:** *[Primality Testing and Abelian Varieties over Finite Fields](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A 142-page research monograph coauthored with Ming-Deh Huang. The USC bibliography also calls it *Primality Testing and Two Dimensional Abelian Varieties over Finite Fields* in its selected-publications commentary; the shorter title is the one given in its formal publication list.

### Cryptographic systems, molecular computers, and other patent-defined inventions

- **1977–1978:** *[RSA public-key cryptosystem / Cryptographic Communications System and Method](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4405829)*. A cryptographic protocol and mathematical system made with Ronald Rivest and Adi Shamir, not merely the paper that describes it; the patent was filed in 1977 and granted in 1983, while the canonical article appeared in 1978.

- **1994:** *[The first experimental DNA computation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7973651/)*. An experimental computation in which DNA strands, laboratory procedures, and enzymes collectively solved a seven-vertex directed Hamiltonian-path instance. It is best understood as a Gesamtkunstwerk of algorithm, protocol, molecules, and performed experiment; the corresponding Science article is a textual manifestation of that event.

- **1995–1997:** *[Molecular Computer](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO1997007440A1/en)*. A patented protocol and system for massively parallel computation using reactive substances, especially DNA; Adleman is the sole named inventor. The family claims 1995 priority, was published in 1997, and includes ceased or abandoned national applications, so the patent family is a legal manifestation of an invention rather than evidence of a commercial machine.

- **2014–2022:** *[Methods and Systems for Creating and Using Massless Currency](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/leonard-m-adleman)*. A cryptographic currency project by Adleman alone, represented by applications and U.S. Patent 11,245,653. The several filings are manifestations of one invention, not separate obras.

- **2014–2018:** *[Methods and Systems for Using Deep-Strings in Computation and Communication](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/leonard-m-adleman)*. A communication and computation protocol based on cryptographically measurable “deep-strings,” represented by related applications and U.S. Patents 9,621,552 and 9,979,686; those documents form a patent family around one project.

### Mathematical, computational, and cryptographic research texts

- **1974:** *[Short Permutation Strings](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/dm/Adleman74)*. A journal article and Adleman’s earliest item in the USC and DBLP scholarly bibliographies.

- **1975:** *[Computational Complexity of Decision Procedures for Polynomials](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanM75)*. An extended conference abstract coauthored with Kenneth Manders.

- **1976:** *[NP-Complete Decision Problems for Quadratic Polynomials](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/MandersA76)*. A conference paper with Kenneth Manders.

- **1976:** *[Diophantine Complexity](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanM76)*. A conference paper with Kenneth Manders on computational complexity in Diophantine problems.

- **1977:** *[Reducibility, Randomness, and Intractability](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/AdlemanM77)*. A conference abstract coauthored with Kenneth Manders.

- **1977:** *[On Taking Roots in Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanMM77)*. A conference paper with Kenneth Manders and Gary Miller.

- **1978:** *[NP-Complete Decision Problems for Binary Quadratics](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/jcss/MandersA78)*. A journal article with Kenneth Manders, related to but not simply an edition of their earlier quadratic-polynomial paper.

- **1978:** *[A Method for Obtaining Digital Signatures and Public-Key Cryptosystems](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/RivestSA78)*. The canonical article with Ronald Rivest and Adi Shamir that presents RSA; it is a textual description of the cryptosystem, not the system itself. A 1983 reprint is only a later manifestation and is not counted as a new work.

- **1978:** *[Improved Time and Space Bounds for Boolean Matrix Multiplication](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/acta/AdlemanBPR78)*. A journal article with Kellogg Booth, Franco Preparata, and Walter Ruzzo.

- **1978:** *[Two Theorems on Random Polynomial Time](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/Adleman78)*. A conference paper in probabilistic computational complexity.

- **1978:** *[Intractability Proofs and the Computational Complexity of Binary Quadratics](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A Berkeley Electronics Research Laboratory memorandum with Kenneth Manders; it is a technical report rather than a conventionally published article.

- **1978:** *[The Use of Public Key Cryptography in Communications System Design](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A conference paper with Ronald Rivest on public-key cryptography as a communications architecture.

- **1978:** *[On Data Banks and Privacy Homomorphisms](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A book chapter with Michael Dertouzos and Ronald Rivest proposing computation on protected data; the USC bibliography misspells Dertouzos’s surname.

- **1979:** *[A Subexponential Algorithm for the Discrete Logarithm Problem with Applications to Cryptography](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/Adleman79)*. A conference abstract presenting an algorithmic result rather than a deployed cryptosystem.

- **1979:** *[Reductions that Lie](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanM79)*. A conference paper with Kenneth Manders.

- **1980–1983:** *[On Distinguishing Prime Numbers from Composite Numbers](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2006975)*. A primality-testing work with Carl Pomerance and Robert Rumely, first manifested as a 1980 conference abstract and then as a substantially documented 1983 *Annals of Mathematics* article. The resulting procedure is also known as the Adleman–Pomerance–Rumely test.

- **1981:** *[Space-Bounded Simulation of Multitape Turing Machines](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/mst/AdlemanL81)*. A journal article with Michael Loui.

- **1981:** *[Irreducibility Testing and Factorization of Polynomials](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanO81)*. An extended conference abstract with Andrew Odlyzko.

- **1981:** *[Primality Testing](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/Adleman81)*. A very short CRYPTO proceedings contribution, recorded as one page; it resists easy identification as either a full paper or merely a program abstract.

- **1981:** *[An \(O(n^{1/10.89})\) Primality Testing Algorithm](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A journal article with F. Thomson Leighton.

- **1981:** *[Mental Poker](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A chapter with Adi Shamir and Ronald Rivest describing a cryptographic protocol for conducting a card game without a trusted dealer.

- **1982:** *[Implementing an Electronic Notary Public](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/Adleman82)*. A protocol paper from the 1982 cryptography workshop; the USC list gives both a proceedings citation and a workshop citation, which are treated here as manifestations of one text.

- **1982:** *[On Breaking the Iterated Merkle–Hellman Public-Key Cryptosystem](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/Adleman82a)*. A cryptanalytic conference paper.

- **1982:** *[An Application of Higher Reciprocity to Computational Number Theory](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanM82)*. A conference abstract with Robert McDonnell.

- **1983:** *[On Breaking Generalized Knapsack Public Key Cryptosystems](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/Adleman83)*. A conference abstract in cryptanalysis.

- **1985:** *[The First Case of Fermat’s Last Theorem](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A journal article with Roger Heath-Brown proving that the first case holds for infinitely many prime exponents; USC notes that Wiles’s later proof superseded its role in the larger Fermat problem, not the historical existence of this result.

- **1985–1986:** *[Breaking the Ong–Schnorr–Shamir Signature Scheme for Quadratic Number Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/EstesAKMM85)*. A cryptanalytic paper with Dennis Estes, Kireeti Kompella, Kevin McCurley, and Gary Miller; it was presented at CRYPTO 1985 and published in the 1986 proceedings.

- **1986:** *[Finding Irreducible Polynomials over Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/AdlemanL86)*. A conference paper with Hendrik Lenstra Jr.

- **1986:** *[Open Problems in Number Theoretic Complexity](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A problem-list and survey chapter with Kevin McCurley; its purpose is projective rather than conclusive, making unresolved questions part of the work.

- **1987:** *[Recognizing Primes in Random Polynomial Time](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/AdlemanH87)*. A conference paper with Ming-Deh Huang.

- **1987:** *[Solving Bivariate Quadratic Congruences in Random Polynomial Time](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A journal article with Dennis Estes and Kevin McCurley; the USC bibliography leaves the year unfinished after the volume citation, although the journal issue is from 1987.

- **1988:** *[Using Smoothness to Achieve Parallelism](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/AdlemanK88)*. A conference abstract with Kireeti Kompella.

- **1988–1990:** *[An Abstract Theory of Computer Viruses](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/Adleman88)*. A formal-theory paper from CRYPTO 1988, later manifested as a chapter in *Rogue Programs* in 1990. Adleman’s influential act of naming and formalizing the “computer virus” belongs to this work, but the term itself resists treatment as a separate obra.

- **1990:** *[Fast Checkers for Cryptography](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/KompellaA90)*. A conference paper with Kireeti Kompella on programs that probabilistically check cryptographic computations.

- **1991:** *[Inductive Inference and Unsolvability](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/jsyml/AdlemanB91)*. A mathematical-logic article with Manuel Blum.

- **1991:** *[Factoring Numbers Using Singular Integers](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/Adleman91)*. A conference paper proposing a number-factoring method.

- **1993:** *[A Subexponential Algorithm for Discrete Logarithms over All Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/crypto/AdlemanD93)*. A work with Jonathan DeMarrais that appeared both in *Mathematics of Computation* and the CRYPTO ’93 proceedings; these are treated as journal and conference manifestations of one result.

- **1994:** *[A Subexponential Algorithm for Discrete Logarithms over the Rational Subgroup of the Jacobians of Large-Genus Hyperelliptic Curves over Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ants/AdlemanDH94)*. A conference paper with Jonathan DeMarrais and Ming-Deh Huang.

- **1994:** *[The Function Field Sieve](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ants/Adleman94)*. A conference paper presenting the named algorithmic method.

- **1994:** *[Efficient Checking of Computations in Number Theory](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ants/AdlemanHK94)*. A one-page ANTS contribution with Ming-Deh Huang and Kireeti Kompella, later developed as a 1995 journal article.

- **1994:** *[Open Problems in Number-Theoretic Complexity II](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ants/AdlemanM94)*. A problem collection with Kevin McCurley; like its predecessor, it is a deliberately unfinished project in textual form.

- **1994:** *[Algorithmic Number Theory—The Complexity Contribution](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/Adleman94)*. A substantial conference survey.

- **1995:** *[Efficient Checkers for Number-Theoretic Computations](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/iandc/AdlemanHK95)*. A journal article with Ming-Deh Huang and Kireeti Kompella, developing the shorter 1994 ANTS manifestation.

- **1995:** *[Algorithmic Number Theory and Its Relationship to Computational Complexity](https://dblp.org/rec/books/sp/cstoday95/Adleman95)*. A survey chapter situating algorithmic number theory within complexity theory.

- **1996:** *[Counting Rational Points on Curves and Abelian Varieties over Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ants/AdlemanH96)*. A conference paper with Ming-Deh Huang.

- **1997:** *[Quantum Computability](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/siamcomp/AdlemanDH97)*. A journal article with Jonathan DeMarrais and Ming-Deh Huang on the formal power of quantum computation.

- **1999:** *[Function Field Sieve Method for Discrete Logarithms over Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/iandc/AdlemanH99)*. A journal article with Ming-Deh Huang extending the function-field-sieve project.

- **1999:** *[A Subexponential Algorithm for Discrete Logarithms over Hyperelliptic Curves of Large Genus over GF(q)](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/tcs/AdlemanDH99)*. A journal article with Jonathan DeMarrais and Ming-Deh Huang, related to the 1994 ANTS paper but published as a developed journal work.

- **2001:** *[Counting Points on Curves and Abelian Varieties over Finite Fields](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/jsc/AdlemanH01)*. A journal article with Ming-Deh Huang that develops their earlier conference work.

- **2024:** *[Darwin Turing Dawkins: Building a General Theory of Evolution](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/corr/abs-2402-10393)*. A preprint presenting a proposed general theory of evolution. As a versioned online manuscript and an explicitly theory-building undertaking, it remains both a text and an open project rather than a settled book.

### Molecular computation, self-assembly, and biological research texts

- **1988:** *[On the Maintenance of T Cell Subpopulations](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A USC Computer Science Department technical report. Its institutional form is unusual for immunological work and makes it resist a simple disciplinary category.

- **1991:** *[Selective Depletion of CD4+ T-Cells Elicits the Production of CD8+ T-Cells in Mice](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A conference abstract with David Wofsy; its bibliographic existence is secure, but it is an abstract of reported research rather than a full paper.

- **1991:** *[T-Cell Reconstitution Following Selective Depletion of CD4+ T-Cells](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/papers/)*. A second abstract with David Wofsy, published in *Clinical Research*.

- **1993:** *[T-Cell Homeostasis: Implications in HIV Infection](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8094457/)*. An immunology article with David Wofsy proposing and testing a “blind” T-cell homeostasis hypothesis.

- **1994:** *[Molecular Computation of Solutions to Combinatorial Problems](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7973651/)*. A research article that is simultaneously report, protocol, and record of the first experimental DNA computation; unlike an ordinary theoretical paper, part of its argument was physically enacted in a test tube.

- **1995:** *[Response: On the Potential of Molecular Computing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17756702/)*. A short reply in *Science*. It is a responsive intervention in a published debate, not an independent research report.

- **1995–1996:** *[On Constructing a Molecular Computer](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/dimacs/Adleman95)*. A protocol-like conference paper describing how a molecular computer might be constructed; the workshop occurred in 1995, while the USC bibliography dates the published volume to 1996.

- **1996:** *[Blind T-Cell Homeostasis in CD4-Deficient Mice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8601219/)*. An experimental immunology article with David Wofsy, testing the homeostasis hypothesis in mice.

- **1996–1998:** *[A Sticker-Based Model for DNA Computation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10072080/)*. A jointly made computational architecture with Sam Roweis, Erik Winfree, Richard Burgoyne, Nickolas Chelyapov, Myron Goodman, and Paul Rothemund. A 1996 workshop paper and the 1998 journal article are manifestations of the same sticker-model project; the USC bibliography also varies the title between “model” and “architecture.”

- **1996–1999:** *[On Applying Molecular Computation to the Data Encryption Standard](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10223664/)*. A proposed DNA-computing attack on DES with Paul Rothemund, Sam Roweis, and Erik Winfree, manifested first in workshop proceedings and later as a journal article. It is a design and feasibility analysis, not a report that DES was actually broken with DNA.

- **1998:** *[Computing with DNA](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/computing-with-dna/)*. An expository magazine article explaining molecular computation to a general scientific audience.

- **2000–2002:** *[Solution of a Satisfiability Problem on a Gel-Based DNA Computer / Solution of a 20-Variable 3-SAT Problem on a DNA Computer](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11896237/)*. A performed molecular computation and its textual manifestations, made with Ravinderjit Braich, Nickolas Chelyapov, Cliff Johnson, Paul Rothemund, and, in the 2000 proceedings version, Darryl Hwang. The changed author list and title make the relationship between the 2000 conference paper and 2002 *Science* article more complicated than a simple reprint.

- **2001:** *[Running Time and Program Size for Self-Assembled Squares](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/AdlemanCGH01)*. A theoretical self-assembly paper with Qi Cheng, Ashish Goel, and Ming-Deh Huang.

- **2002:** *[Combinatorial Optimization Problems in Self-Assembly](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/stoc/AdlemanCGHKER02)*. A conference paper with Qi Cheng, Ashish Goel, Ming-Deh Huang, David Kempe, Pablo Moisset de Espanés, and Paul Rothemund.

- **2002:** *[On the Decidability of Self-Assembly of Infinite Ribbons](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/focs/AdlemanKKR02)*. A conference paper with Jarkko Kari, Lila Kari, and Dustin Reishus.

- **2004:** *[DNA Triangles and Self-Assembled Hexagonal Tilings](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15506744/)*. A scientific article and record of fabricated DNA triangles and planar tilings, made with Nickolas Chelyapov, Yuriy Brun, Manoj Gopalkrishnan, Dustin Reishus, and Bilal Shaw. The material structures, not just their description, are part of the obra.

- **2005:** *[Self-Assembly of DNA Double-Double Crossover Complexes into High-Density, Doubly Connected, Planar Structures](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16351073/)*. A scientific article with Dustin Reishus, Bilal Shaw, Yuriy Brun, and Nickolas Chelyapov, accompanied by physically constructed molecular complexes and assemblies.

- **2009:** *[The Undecidability of the Infinite Ribbon Problem: Implications for Computing by Self-Assembly](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/siamcomp/AdlemanKKRS09)*. A journal article with Jarkko Kari, Lila Kari, Dustin Reishus, and Petr Sosík developing the 2002 infinite-ribbon project.

### Edited scholarly object

- **1994:** *[Algorithmic Number Theory: First International Symposium, ANTS-I, Proceedings](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ants/1994)*. A conference-proceedings volume edited with Ming-Deh Huang. Adleman made the editorial and organizational object, not every constituent paper.

### Performed and screen-bound works

- **circa 1992:** *[Mathematical lecture scene for Sneakers](https://molecularscience.usc.edu/sneakers/)*. Adleman wrote the words and designed mathematical slides for a filmed lecture about a fictional factoring breakthrough, serving as the film’s mathematical consultant. The production redrew his slides by hand, so the surviving screen images are manifestations mediated by the art department rather than unaltered Adleman originals.

- **2012:** *[An Algorithmic View of the Universe](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/acm/PapadimitriouAK12)*. A Turing-centenary panel or collective presentation with Christos Papadimitriou, Richard Karp, Donald Knuth, Robert Tarjan, and Leslie Valiant. DBLP records only a one-page proceedings manifestation, so it resists classification as an ordinary coauthored research paper.
