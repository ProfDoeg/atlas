## Works by John Foster Dulles

### Books and extended arguments

- **1939:** *War, Peace and Change*. A book-length obra arguing that durable peace requires mechanisms for peaceful political and economic change; the archival chronology identifies it as Dulles’s first major book, rather than counting its later editions separately. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1950:** *War or Peace*. A book-length statement of Dulles’s early Cold War outlook, developed from lectures and completed through several manuscript stages preserved in his papers. [Princeton University Library](https://libguides.princeton.edu/johndulles/collections), [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

### Articles, addresses, and political interventions

- **1930:** *The Young Plan in Relation to World Economy*. A published address or pamphlet on the German-reparations settlement, issued jointly with material by Hjalmar Schacht; it is therefore a composite publication rather than a wholly Dulles-authored book. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Dulles%2C%20John%20Foster%2C%201888-1959)

- **1932:** *Should Economic Sanctions Be Applied in International Disputes?* A legal-policy article examining sanctions as an instrument of international order. [VLex journal record](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/should-economic-sanctions-be-874252279)

- **1935:** *The Road to Peace*. An essay in the *Atlantic Monthly*, not a book, setting out Dulles’s belief that international arrangements must accommodate political change. [Ronald W. Pruessen, *John Foster Dulles: The Road to Power*](https://dokumen.pub/john-foster-dulles-the-road-to-power-0029254604-9780029254608.html)

- **1940:** *The United States and the World of Nations*. An address delivered to the National Study Conference on the Churches and the International Situation in Philadelphia; its separate pamphlet manifestation should not be mistaken for a monograph. [AGRIS bibliographic record](https://agris.fao.org/search/en/providers/122535/records/65ddc7f56eef00c2ce9ed3de)

- **1943:** *Six Pillars of Peace*. A manifesto-like program for postwar international organization, announced by Dulles as chairman of the Federal Council of Churches’ Commission to Study the Bases of a Just and Durable Peace. It was a collective commission project drafted largely—but not indisputably or exclusively—by Dulles, and appeared as a speech, statement, pamphlet, study guide, and public campaign rather than one stable text. [Contemporary speech text and publication data](https://www.ibiblio.org/pha/policy/1943/1943-03-18a.html), [scholarly account of the drafting](https://upeace.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Faith-in-UN-Human-Rights-Treaties-1.pdf)

- **1944:** *The Dumbarton Oaks Proposals*. A speech or policy paper explaining and criticizing the projected United Nations organization. Surviving references identify several textual manifestations, so it resists treatment as a single fixed publication. [Scholarly study citing the Dulles Papers](https://law.meijo-u.ac.jp/staff/contents/pdf_55-60/550403_hida.pdf)

- **1946:** *Thoughts on Soviet Foreign Policy and What to Do About It*. A foreign-policy essay published in *Life*, functioning as public argument and political intervention rather than official government policy.

- **1948:** *“Not War, Not Peace”*. An address before the Foreign Policy Association of New York, also broadcast nationally on 17 January; the printed booklet is a manifestation of the speech, not a separate obra.

- **1948:** *The Christian Citizen in a Changing World*. An essay or chapter in the ecumenical collection *The Church and the International Disorder*, connecting Christian responsibility with international politics. [Cambridge scholarly bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/after-hiroshima/bibliography/7634C14156BE868603199C4F696245C8)

- **1952:** *Security in the Pacific*. A *Foreign Affairs* article presenting the security logic behind the Japanese settlement and the emerging Pacific alliance system. [Cambridge scholarly bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/after-hiroshima/bibliography/7634C14156BE868603199C4F696245C8)

- **1952:** *A Policy of Boldness*. A *Life* magazine manifesto for replacing containment with a more assertive policy of political “liberation”; the archival chronology dates its publication to May 1952. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1954:** *The Evolution of Foreign Policy*. An address to the Council on Foreign Relations on 12 January, subsequently associated with the doctrine of “massive retaliation.” It is a performative policy statement—almost a manifesto—not a treaty or independently enacted protocol. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1954:** *Policy for Security and Peace*. A *Foreign Affairs* article published in April, elaborating the Eisenhower administration’s deterrence and collective-security policy. [Cambridge scholarly bibliography](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/after-hiroshima/bibliography/7634C14156BE868603199C4F696245C8), [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

### Collective diplomatic obras

- **1945:** *Preamble to the Charter of the United Nations*. Dulles participated in the San Francisco Conference and influenced the preamble, but neither the Charter nor its preamble is properly a work “by” him: it is a negotiated, collectively authored diplomatic instrument. His precise verbal contribution should therefore be treated as contributory and not exclusive authorship. [National Diet Library archival guide](https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/rnavi/occupation/JFD_1)

- **1950–1951:** *Project for a Japanese Peace Settlement*. A diplomatic project comprising drafts, consultations, speeches, memoranda, and negotiations rather than one text. President Truman appointed Dulles to lead the work, but the surviving settlement was made collectively by governments and diplomatic staffs. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1951:** *Treaty of Peace with Japan (Treaty of San Francisco)*. A multilateral treaty signed by forty-eight Allied states on 8 September; Dulles was its principal American negotiator and is often called its architect, but the treaty resists attribution as his individual work because its language and authority resulted from multilateral negotiation. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1951:** *Security Treaty Between the United States and Japan*. A bilateral diplomatic protocol produced alongside the peace settlement. Dulles was a principal designer and negotiator, but the enacted text belongs institutionally to the two governments rather than to a personal oeuvre.

- **1951:** *Security Treaty Between Australia, New Zealand, and the United States (ANZUS)*. A collective treaty within the Pacific security architecture Dulles helped construct; his role was diplomatic and architectural, not sole textual authorship.

- **1954:** *Southeast Asia Collective Defense Treaty (Manila Pact)*. A multilateral security protocol signed by Dulles for the United States on 8 September and constitutive of SEATO. Signature and political design do not make the treaty an individually authored text. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1954:** *Mutual Defense Treaty Between the United States and the Republic of China*. A bilateral protocol signed by Dulles on 2 December; it is a collective state instrument, not a personal work, despite his central negotiating role. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1955:** *Austrian State Treaty*. A multilateral instrument restoring an independent Austria, signed by Dulles for the United States on 15 May. It belongs here as a negotiated obra in which he acted, while resisting any claim of individual authorship. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

### Journals, drafts, and administrative writing

- **1907–1938:** *Diaries and Journals*. Intermittent travel and working journals, especially concerning the Paris Peace Conference, a 1930s St. Lawrence journey, and Dulles’s 1938 visit to China. They form an archival project rather than a deliberately published work, and the archival description does not support inventing individual titles. [Microfilm editorial guide](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/8331000C.pdf), [National Diet Library](https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/rnavi/occupation/JFD_1)

- **1896–1958:** *Articles, Papers, Reports, Etc.* A body of work catalogued chronologically in Nancy Bressler’s three-volume *Guide to the Papers of John Foster Dulles*, with individual descriptions occupying pages 667–703. This archival category contains numerous titled and untitled things; without reproducing that documentary inventory, it cannot honestly be converted into a supposedly complete list of autonomous “works.” [Microfilm editorial guide](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/8331000C.pdf)

- **Dates unspecified:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters and related documents extending principally from 1891 to 1959. These are things Dulles made, but most are transactional communications rather than self-declared works; the archive preserves them as a corpus, with authorship sometimes shared with secretaries or institutional staff. [Princeton University Library](https://libguides.princeton.edu/johndulles/collections), [Microfilm editorial guide](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/8331000C.pdf)

- **1907–1959:** *Speeches, Statements, Press Conferences, Etc.* A large performative corpus occupying microfilm reels 59–66 and individually listed on pages 704–915 of Bressler’s guide. These texts acted in several capacities—argument, diplomacy, policy announcement, testimony, and improvisational response—and therefore resist reduction to “books.” [Microfilm editorial guide](https://assets.cengage.com/gale/psm/8331000C.pdf), [National Diet Library](https://ndlsearch.ndl.go.jp/en/rnavi/occupation/JFD_1)

- **1953–1959:** *“Think Pieces” on national security*. Working memoranda composed by Dulles on nuclear strategy, strategic posture, arms control, and related subjects. They are an unfinished and internally circulated intellectual project, often untitled and sometimes embedded in staff files rather than discrete works. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf), [Subject Series description](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/research/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/subject-series.pdf)

- **1953:** *Contributions to Eisenhower’s “The Chance for Peace”*. Draft language and advice for President Eisenhower’s 16 April address. This material resists classification as a Dulles work because the public speech appeared under Eisenhower’s authority, although the archival record preserves Dulles’s compositional input. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)

- **1953–1959:** *Official memoranda, correspondence, and policy drafts*. A large administrative corpus—outgoing correspondence, memoranda, treaty drafts, messages, statements, and reports—produced by Dulles and his staff. Authorship is frequently institutional or secretarial rather than securely personal, while approximately 88,400 pages at the Eisenhower Library and much larger Princeton and State Department holdings make any short item-level claim of completeness untenable. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf), [Princeton University Library](https://libguides.princeton.edu/johndulles/collections)

- **1953–1959:** *Memoranda of telephone conversations*. Approximately 13,000 pages documenting conversations with Eisenhower, Allen Dulles, officials, legislators, and others. Most were prepared by staff and record Dulles’s spoken interventions, so they are manifestations of his activity but cannot automatically be called texts authored by him. [Eisenhower Presidential Library](https://www.eisenhowerlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/finding-aids/pdf/dulles-john-foster-papers/scope-and-content.pdf)
