## Works by Savitri Devi

### Philosophical and scholarly studies

- **1935:** *Essai-Critique sur Théophile Kaïris*. Her principal doctoral thesis, published under her birth name, Maximine Portaz; it is a philosophical and historical study of the Greek religious reformer Theophilos Kaïris.
- **1935:** *La simplicité mathématique*. Her complementary doctoral thesis in philosophy of mathematics, likewise issued as Maximine Portaz.
- **1935–1937; published 1940:** *L’Étang aux Lotus*. A French book of impressions and ideological reflections on India, rather than a conventional travel narrative.
- **1940:** *Akhnaton’s Eternal Message: A Scientific Religion 3,300 Years Old*. A short religious-historical pamphlet and the first manifestation of her long Akhnaton project.
- **1940–1942?:** *A Perfect Man: Akhnaton, King of Egypt*. A bibliographically resistant object: it was advertised as published, but no copy or publication details are known. It may have been a lost book, an unrealized project, or an early title for *A Son of God*.
- **1942:** *Joy of the Sun: The Beautiful Life of Akhnaton, King of Egypt, Told to Young People*. An illustrated or juvenile biographical narrative presenting Akhnaton as an exemplary religious ruler.
- **1942–1945; published 1946:** *A Son of God: The Life and Philosophy of Akhnaton, King of Egypt*. A large biographical-philosophical study, later issued in altered form as *Son of the Sun*; the later version omitted her introductory correspondence with Aldous Huxley.
- **1947; published 1948:** *Akhnaton: A Play*. A dramatic rather than expository manifestation of her Akhnaton project.
- **1945–1946; published 1959:** *Impeachment of Man*. An anti-anthropocentric philosophical polemic advocating animal welfare and a hierarchical conception of nature.
- **1979:** *The Egyptian Conquest of Nubia*. An unfinished historical vignette that became an article only through editorial intervention: Martin Kerr supplied its title and concluding paragraphs without showing them to Devi. Authorship of the published whole is therefore divided and disputed.

### Hindu-nationalist and National Socialist manifestos

- **1938; published 1939:** *A Warning to the Hindus*. A manifesto-pamphlet intended to mobilize Hindus around religious nationalism and against Christianity and Islam; bibliographies sometimes misdate it to 1936.
- **1940:** *The Non-Hindu Indians and Indian Unity*. A political pamphlet on national belonging in India, formulated as an intervention rather than a detached study.
- **1948–1949:** *National Socialist leaflets and handbills distributed in occupied Germany*. Manifestos in the most literal sense—anonymous or untitled texts intended to produce political action. Their survival and exact textual boundaries are uncertain, but contemporary reports and Devi’s memoir establish campaigns in 1948 and 1949; sources disagree over the total number circulated.
- **1948–1949; published 1952:** *Gold in the Furnace*. A political travel memoir and testimonial assembled partly during her imprisonment; its declared purpose was to preserve and glorify underground National Socialist allegiance in postwar Germany.
- **1950; published 1951:** *Defiance*. A prison memoir that also operates as a manifesto, narrating her arrest, trial, and imprisonment for distributing Nazi propaganda.
- **1948–1956; published 1958:** *The Lightning and the Sun*. Her principal work of esoteric history, classifying historical figures as “above,” “against,” or “in” time and presenting Hitler through a Hindu cyclical cosmology.
- **1957; published 1958:** *Paul de Tarse, ou Christianisme et Juiverie*. A polemical pamphlet about Paul of Tarsus and the formation of Christianity; later translations sometimes substitute “Judaism” for “Jewry,” so those titles should not be mistaken for separate original works.
- **1961–1963; posthumously published 2020:** *Hart wie Kruppstahl*. A German-language National Socialist treatise completed as a typescript but unpublished during her lifetime.
- **early 1960s:** *“After the Deluge—We!”*. Her own English translation of part of the epilogue to *Hart wie Kruppstahl*, prepared for an American Nazi periodical. It is a derivative manifestation of the larger work, not an independent composition in the ordinary sense.
- **1980:** *Hitlerism and Hindudom* (published as *Hitlerism and the Hindu World*). A periodical essay asserting a relationship between Hindu traditions and Hitlerism; its original and editorially supplied titles differ.

### Memoir, pilgrimage, and animal narrative

- **1953–1954; published 1958:** *Pilgrimage*. A travel diary and ritual itinerary through sites associated with Hitler and National Socialism; it treats physical travel as political-religious pilgrimage.
- **1957–1960; published without a printed date, conventionally 1965:** *Long-Whiskers and the Two-Legged Goddess, or the True Story of a “Most Objectionable Nazi” and… Half-a-Dozen Cats*. A hybrid of autobiography, animal biography, fiction, and ethical fable whose unstable genre is part of the object.
- **1968–1971; published 1976:** *Souvenirs et réflexions d’une Aryenne*. A French autobiographical and ideological memoir.
- **1978; published 2005:** *And Time Rolls On: The Savitri Devi Interviews*. An oral autobiographical work created through recorded interviews in New Delhi and posthumously edited for publication. Its dialogic and editorial formation makes sole authorship less straightforward than that of a written book.

### Poetry and fiction

- **1952–1953; posthumously published:** *For-Ever and Ever* (also styled *Forever and Ever*). A cycle of sixteen prose poems about Hitler and National Socialism; it circulated as an unpublished manuscript before its posthumous serial and book publication.
- **c. 1963–1968, perhaps resumed 1979:** *Tyrtée l’Athénien*. An unfinished historical novel about Tyrtaeus. Because Devi stopped before completing it, the project—not a finished novel—is the obra.

### Unfinished final project

- **1979–1980:** *Ironies et paradoxes dans l’histoire et la légende*. A projected book about irony, falsehood, and paradox in historical memory. Only roughly one and a half chapters were written before failing eyesight stopped the work; the manuscript’s present whereabouts are unknown, so the surviving obra is principally a documented intention with fragments.

### Translations

- **1944:** *Confessions of a Nun*. Devi’s English translation, under the name Maximine Portaz, of Denis Diderot’s *La Religieuse*. The underlying novel is Diderot’s; her made object is the translation.
- **1945:** *Candide*. Her English translation, as Maximine Portaz, of Voltaire’s tale; again, the translation rather than the originating narrative is her work.

### Articles and shorter essays

- **July 1939:** *Shinto, the National Religion of Japan*. A periodical essay in *New Asia* comparing Shinto with Hinduism and considering it as a living national religion.
- **c. 1938–1941:** *Article on Shintoism* and *article on the Divine Wind of Ise*. Two contributions reportedly published in *The Eastern Economist* but not recovered. They resist individual description because their titles, dates, and texts remain unknown; the standard bibliography classifies them as lost essays.
- **early 1960s:** *A Fighter for Truth and Justice: A Review of the Books of Olga von Barényi*. A periodical review-essay published in Colin Jordan’s *The National Socialist*; the surviving bibliography does not establish a precise issue date.
- **1979:** *Shinto: The Way of the Gods*. An essay published in English and subsequently translated into Italian, French, and German. Its relationship to the 1939 Shinto essay is close, but the bibliography treats it as a separate later text.
- **1930s–1940s, exact dates unknown:** *Lost Essays*. The descriptive bibliography records additional articles on India, Japan, religion, and politics whose titles or texts have not been recovered. This is not securely a single work: it is a placeholder for an incompletely reconstructable periodical corpus.

### Correspondence, recorded speech, and documentary corpora

- **1940s–1982:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters to political associates, publishers, friends, and readers survive unevenly in public and private collections. Individual letters are texts she made, but the correspondence was not conceived as a single titled work; later selections organized by correspondent are editorial corpora rather than separate authorial obras.
- **dates uncertain, principally late 1970s–early 1980s:** *Audio letters*. At least four recorded letters—to Miriam Hirn and Georg Schrader—survive as sound objects. They are spoken communications rather than books, and their archival titles are supplied from recipient and medium.
- **1967:** *Commander Rockwell as I Knew Him*. An unpublished memoir of George Lincoln Rockwell recorded by the descriptive bibliography; its surviving status and completeness are not clearly established.
- **1980:** *Autobiographical Letter*. A long letter dated 1 October 1980 and later translated and published as autobiography. It resists separation from ordinary correspondence: its later editorial framing, rather than its original private form, makes it resemble a work.
