## Works by Robert Maxwell

### Edited and contributed texts

- **1962:** *Information USSR: An Authoritative Encyclopaedia about the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics*. A 982-page English-language reference work edited by Maxwell, largely adapted from Soviet encyclopaedic material; it is an editorial and publishing obra, not a book wholly written by him. ([The Russian Review/JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/127657))

- **1968:** *Man Alive! A Current Affairs Annual for Schools and Colleges*. A current-affairs annual written by Keith Hindell with Maxwell named only as a contributor; the surviving bibliographic description does not identify his contribution, so it resists classification as a Maxwell-authored work. ([Open British National Bibliography](https://obnb.uk/p9909727-man-alive-a-current-affairs-annual-for-schools-and-colleges))

- **1985:** *Jaruzelski, Prime Minister of Poland: Selected Speeches, Interview with Robert Maxwell, and a Biographical Sketch*. A documentary compilation in Pergamon’s *Leaders of the World* series: Jaruzelski’s speeches are not Maxwell’s, while the interview is a text made jointly through Maxwell’s questions and Jaruzelski’s answers; catalogues disagree over whether Maxwell should be described as editor or merely interviewer. ([CiNii Books series record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA00695493.amp?l=en), [scholarly citation](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9780429314643/a707d997-dc5a-4dfe-931a-f4452e091cee/preview.pdf))

- **1988:** *Robert Maxwell & Pergamon Press: 40 Years’ Service to Science, Technology and Education*. An 887-page corporate commemorative volume sometimes bibliographically attributed to Robert Maxwell and consequently cited as his book. Its actual coordinating editor was Elisabeth Maxwell, however, so at most Robert Maxwell supplied material and served as its subject and institutional sponsor; the volume as a whole should not securely be called his work. ([IOS Press scholarly bibliography](https://ebooks.iospress.nl/pdf/doi/10.3233/978-1-58603-148-0-77), [Caxton collection catalogue](https://www.bunsei.co.jp/wp-content/uploads/2010/09/caxton%20catalogue.pdf), [Geochemical Society bibliography](https://geochemsoc.org/download_file/26e004c6-d30c-46c3-bc7e-43f319aadf8f/417))

- **1990:** *Information Technology as a Way of Reducing the Costs and Time in the Dissemination of Scientific and Technological Information*. The published text of Maxwell’s British Library Dainton Lecture, delivered on 5 March 1990: an authored lecture-manifesto advocating electronic dissemination rather than a conventional monograph. ([University of Ghana scholarly bibliography](https://pdfproc.lib.msu.edu/?file=%2FDMC%2FAfrican+Journals%2Fpdfs%2FInstitue+of+African+Studies+Research+Review%2F1995v11n1-2%2Fasrv011001-2005.pdf), [British Library bibliographic record via OBNB](https://obnb.uk/a00085296-robert-maxwell))

### Publishing projects and institutional obra

- **1948–1991:** *Pergamon Press*. A scientific-publishing institution founded and run by Maxwell with Paul Rosbaud; its thousands of books and journals were made by their individual authors and editors, not by Maxwell, but the publishing system and commercial programme constitute his principal institutional obra. The collaborative founding prevents treating Pergamon as his creation alone. ([Brian Cox, “The Pergamon phenomenon 1951–1991”](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1087/095315102760319233), [A Century of Science Publishing](https://ebooks.iospress.nl/pdf/doi/10.3233/978-1-58603-148-0-77))

- **1981–1990:** *Leaders of the World*. A Pergamon biographical and documentary series encompassing speeches, autobiographical narratives, official biographies, and interviews with political leaders. It was Maxwell’s publishing project rather than a uniformly authored oeuvre: the series record identifies seventeen associated books, while contemporary criticism singles out its volumes on Erich Honecker, Todor Zhivkov, Nicolae Ceaușescu, János Kádár, and Wojciech Jaruzelski as apologetic official portraits; Maxwell’s precise textual contribution varies by volume and cannot safely be generalized. ([CiNii Books series record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA00695493.amp?l=en), [TIME, “Maxwell’s Hall of Shame”](https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0%2C33009%2C972683%2C00.html))

- **1987:** *London Daily News*. A London evening newspaper conceived and launched by Maxwell on 24 February 1987 as an intended round-the-clock publication; it closed that July. It is best classified as a short-lived collective publishing project, since its journalism was made by its staff rather than personally by Maxwell. ([Le Monde contemporary report](https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1987/02/26/guerre-des-quotidiens-du-soir-a-londres-m-maxwell-lance-le-london-daily-news_4020490_1819218.html), [The Spectator archive](https://archive.spectator.co.uk/article/26th-october-1991/16/a-very-private-person))

- **1990:** *The European*. A transnational weekly newspaper launched by Maxwell on 11 May 1990 and presented as Europe’s first “national” newspaper. Like the *London Daily News*, it is his project and institutional creation but not a personally authored text. ([Los Angeles Times contemporary report](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1990-05-11-fi-1542-story.html), [UPI obituary](https://www.upi.com/Archives/1991/11/05/Robert-Maxwell-British-communications-tycoon/8640689317200/))

### Unfinished project

- **1983:** *Thames Valley Royals*. Maxwell’s proposed merger of Oxford United and Reading football clubs under a newly invented name. Opposition prevented its realization, so it is an abandoned institutional project rather than a created club or completed work. ([Robert Maxwell biographical record](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/literature-and-arts/journalism-and-publishing-biographies/robert-maxwell))

### Rejected attribution

- **1995, sometimes miscatalogued as 1991:** *The Protocols of the Learned Elders of Punjab*. A six-page satirical pamphlet that claims to have been abridged and introduced by Robert Maxwell, although its publisher records publication on 15 December 1995—four years after Maxwell’s death—and describes the attribution in an openly facetious context. It should not be accepted as Maxwell’s work without independent manuscript evidence; its appearance under his name in bibliographic aggregations is a false or parodic attribution. ([publisher’s bibliography](https://infotextmanuscripts.org/baron_biblio_6.html), [conflicting OBNB aggregation](https://obnb.uk/a00085296-robert-maxwell))
