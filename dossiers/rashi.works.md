## Works by Rashi

### Scriptural commentaries

- **Before 1105:** *Commentary on the Torah*. An exegetical obra on Genesis through Deuteronomy, composed as concise glosses combining contextual interpretation, philology, and selected midrash; later transmission introduced identifiable comments by pupils and copyists. [Avraham Grossman treats it as a distinct major composition](https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/41686), while the [*Encyclopaedia Judaica* account describes its composite transmission](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html).

- **Before 1105:** *Commentaries on Joshua, Judges, Samuel, and Kings*. Exegetical commentaries on the Former Prophets, surviving within Rashi’s larger biblical-commentary project; as with that project generally, the received texts contain additions associated with his pupils and later copyists. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica* surveys the scope and textual layering](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html).

- **Before 1105:** *Commentaries on Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, and the Twelve Minor Prophets*. Commentaries on the Latter Prophets; Rashi demonstrably revised at least part of his Ezekiel commentary in consultation with his pupil Shemaiah, so the obra is better understood as a revisable teaching text than as a fixed first edition. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica* documents the revision and manuscript evidence](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html).

- **Before 1105:** *Commentary on Psalms*. A complete exegetical commentary, including an introduction concerned with language and interpretation; the surviving text has an independent manuscript tradition, while a different commentary attributed to Rashi in the Second Rabbinic Bible is treated separately by modern editors. [Mayer Gruber’s critical study and edition describes both witnesses](https://brill.com/display/title/8449).

- **Before 1105:** *Commentaries on Proverbs, Song of Songs, Ecclesiastes, Lamentations, Esther, Daniel, Ruth, and possibly other Writings*. These glossaries form part of Rashi’s biblical-exegesis project rather than a single continuously argued “book.” Their textual boundaries are not uniformly secure, and interpolations by pupils and copyists occur throughout the transmitted biblical commentaries. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html).

- **Before 1105; unfinished:** *Commentary on Job*. Rashi’s commentary apparently stops at Job 40:25; the continuation traditionally printed under his name is judged non-Rashian by many scholars. The unfinished state makes this a project as well as an obra. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html).

- **Attribution disputed:** *Commentary on Ezra–Nehemiah*. Traditionally printed as Rashi’s, but commonly classified as Pseudo-Rashi because its style and method differ from his; an alternative scholarly position holds that substantially Rashian material was recast and enlarged by pupils. It therefore resists classification as either wholly Rashi’s obra or wholly independent of him. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html); [Gruber’s survey of the attribution problem](https://brill.com/display/title/8449).

- **Attribution disputed:** *Commentary on Chronicles*. Conventionally attributed to Rashi but generally treated as Pseudo-Rashi; here too, some scholarship allows the possibility of Rashian material reworked by his school. It should not be silently counted as an uncontested work by Rashi. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html); [Gruber’s survey](https://brill.com/display/title/8449).

### Talmudic commentary

- **Before 1105; unfinished:** *Commentary on the Babylonian Talmud*. Rashi’s largest exegetical project consists of closely keyed glosses on most tractates rather than an autonomous treatise. The received corpus is incomplete and textually mixed: his commentary on *Bava Batra* was completed by Rashbam, and that on *Makkot* by Judah ben Nathan; the standard commentaries on *Taʿanit*, *Nedarim*, *Nazir*, *Horayot*, and *Moʿed Katan* are not his, although a different Rashian commentary on *Moʿed Katan* survives. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html); [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

- **Attribution rejected:** *Commentary on Pirkei Avot*. Printed under Rashi’s name from the sixteenth century onward, but modern criticism does not accept it as his; because *Avot* has no Babylonian Gemara, it also lies outside the stated scope of his Talmudic project. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0017_0_16452.html); [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

- **Attribution rejected:** *Commentaries on Genesis Rabbah and Exodus Rabbah*. These midrashic commentaries circulated under Rashi’s name, but the attribution—especially that of the Genesis Rabbah commentary—has long been rejected or seriously doubted. They are manifestations of Rashi’s later authority, not securely his obras. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

### Legal responses and protocols

- **Before 1105:** *Responsa*. A dispersed corpus of roughly 350 surviving answers to legal and communal questions, not a book assembled by Rashi himself; individual responses were preserved in manuscripts and in compilations made by his pupils and successors. The corpus is thus an obra of repeated juridical acts whose later collected form is editorial. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica*, second edition, reports the approximately 350 items collected by Israel Elfenbein](https://ketab3.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/11/encyclopaedia-judaica-v-17-ra-sam.pdf); [Elfenbein’s collected *Teshuvot Rashi* is available as a structured text](https://www.sefaria.org/Teshuvot_Rashi).

- **Before 1105:** *Dinei Niqquṛ ha-Basar* (“Laws for Removing Forbidden Parts from Meat”). A short halakhic protocol traditionally attributed to Rashi, prescribing the preparation of meat; its independent textual status and attribution are less secure than those of the major commentaries. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

- **Before 1105; transmitted fragmentarily:** *Rulings on prohibited and permitted foods*. Halakhic instructions associated with Rashi survive inside later collections and are sometimes called his *Issur ve-Hetter*, but this is not a securely bounded book of the later *issur ve-hetter* genre. The material resists being called one work because it survives as rulings, teaching, and redacted school tradition. [*Encyclopaedia Judaica* on the genre and the exceptional status of Rashi’s material](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/issur-ve-hetter); [Grossman on Rashi’s rulings and halakhic monographs](https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/41686/chapter-abstract/353889286).

### Liturgical poetry

- **Before 1105; attribution traditional:** *Adonai Elohei ha-Tzevaʾot*. A *seliḥah*, or penitential liturgical poem, attributed to Rashi and incorporated into the Ashkenazic penitential rite. Its date is unknown. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

- **Before 1105; attribution traditional:** *Az Terem Nimtaḥu*. A penitential liturgical poem attributed to Rashi; it is a performative prayer rather than a descriptive treatise. Its date is unknown. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

- **Before 1105; attribution traditional:** *Tannot Tzarot Lo Nukhal*. A penitential liturgical poem attributed to Rashi and shaped by persecution, communal suffering, and hope for redemption. Its precise occasion and date are not fixed. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak); [Grossman’s scholarly account of the themes of Rashi’s poems](https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/41686/chapter-abstract/353889286).

- **Before 1105; formerly unpublished and attribution traditional:** *Shir ʿal Aḥdut ha-Bore* (“Poem on the Unity of the Creator”). A theological hymn attributed to Rashi; older bibliography records it without a published text or secure date. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).

- **Before 1105; uncertain corpus:** *Three further liturgical poems attributed to Rashi*. Leopold Zunz’s older enumeration assigned seven poems to Rashi “with more or less certainty,” while standard summaries securely name only four; the remaining three therefore cannot responsibly be supplied with normalized titles here. This is an unstable attributed corpus, not seven equally authenticated obras. [Maurice Liber’s bibliographical discussion](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3165/pg3165.html); [Grossman’s modern treatment of Rashi’s liturgical poetry](https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/41686/chapter-abstract/353889286).

- **Attribution conjectural:** *Yonata de-Vei Malka*. An Aramaic *seliḥah* recently proposed as Rashi’s on grounds of provenance, style, language, and use of midrash; the attribution is explicitly a scholarly conjecture rather than an established addition to the canon. [English abstract of the proposing study](https://jstudies.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/000_-_sifrut_-_32_-_15.7.2021_-_book_-_complete_-_416_pp.pdf).

### Commentary on liturgy and language

- **Before 1105; dispersed:** *Commentaries on liturgical poems*. Explanatory glosses on difficult *piyyutim*, transmitted as a body of liturgical exegesis rather than as one securely titled book. Their medium and function resemble Rashi’s biblical and Talmudic glossing, but the corpus’s limits are not fixed. [Gruber includes these commentaries in his survey of Rashi’s literary output](https://brill.com/display/title/8449); [Grossman treats them alongside Rashi’s poems and responsa](https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/41686/chapter-abstract/353889286).

- **Before 1105:** *Essay on Homonymity in Biblical Exegesis*. A short programmatic linguistic text explaining how one word may bear different meanings in different scriptural settings. It functions as an exegetical protocol and may also be understood as part of the apparatus surrounding Rashi’s biblical commentaries. [Gruber identifies and translates the essay in his critical study](https://brill.com/display/title/8449).

### School compilations bearing Rashi’s name

- **After 1105; not Rashi’s authored book:** *Sefer ha-Pardes*. A compilation of ritual law, rulings, responsa, and customs assembled by disciples—or possibly disciples of disciples—from Rashian and other material. It contains contributions by contemporaries and successors, so its traditional attribution cannot mean direct authorship by Rashi. [Contemporary scholarly review of the critical edition](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rjuiv_0484-8616_1924_num_79_158_6113_t1_0215_0000_3); [Liber’s discussion of its composite character](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3165/pg3165.html).

- **After 1105; not Rashi’s authored book:** *Siddur Rashi*. A school compilation of prayers, customs, rulings, and explanations derived partly from Rashi’s teaching. It bears his name as an authority but was composed by pupils, making it a manifestation of his teaching rather than an obra he made as a book. [Overview of its compilation and attribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddur_Rashi); [survey of early medieval halakhic works](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/early-medieval-halakhic-texts/).

- **After 1105; not Rashi’s authored book:** *Sefer ha-Orah*. A halakhic compilation produced within Rashi’s school and preserving material connected with him, but authored or assembled by students rather than by Rashi as a unitary work. [Survey of early medieval halakhic texts](https://www.myjewishlearning.com/article/early-medieval-halakhic-texts/).

- **After 1105; not Rashi’s authored book:** *Liqquṭei ha-Pardes*. An abridged derivative of *Sefer ha-Pardes*, reportedly compiled around 1220 by Samuel of Bamberg. Its inclusion in the Rashi tradition records reception and recomposition, not Rashi’s manufacture of the object. [*Jewish Encyclopedia*](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/15110-yizhak).
