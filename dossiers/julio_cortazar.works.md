## Works by Julio Florencio Cortázar Descotte

### Poetry and poetic drama

- **1938:** *Presencia*. A collection of forty-three sonnets published under the pseudonym Julio Denis; its classical form makes it unlike the formally experimental poetry Cortázar later retained. [[1]](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/cortazar_julio.htm)
- **1949:** *Los reyes*. A dramatic poem that rewrites the Minotaur myth; although commonly catalogued as theatre, its first manifestation was a literary text rather than a documented stage production. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1971:** *Pameos y meopas*. A poetry collection whose deliberately distorted title announces Cortázar’s resistance to conventional genre and diction. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1984:** *Salvo el crepúsculo*. A deliberately assembled poetic autobiography combining poems, prose, memories, quotations and visual arrangements; it is closer to an intimate poetic atlas than to a uniform verse collection. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)

### Novels and novel-projects

- **1949; first published 1986:** *Divertimento*. An early novel withheld during Cortázar’s lifetime; its posthumous publication is a manifestation of an abandoned or deferred project, not a book he released as finished. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1950; first published 1986:** *El examen*. A novel rejected by publishers and left unpublished by Cortázar; it survives as a completed manuscript-project whose posthumous editorial status must not be confused with authorial publication. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1950; first published 1995:** *Diario de Andrés Fava*. A fictional diary excised from the manuscript of *El examen* and later constituted as a separate posthumous book; its independence is therefore editorially ambiguous. [[3]](https://rilzea.cialc.unam.mx/jspui/bitstream/CIALC-UNAM/CL463/1/PLS_8.pdf)
- **1960:** *Los premios*. A novel organized around a mysterious sea voyage and a group of lottery winners. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1963:** *Rayuela*. A combinatory novel whose table of instructions offers more than one reading sequence; the reader’s traversal is part of the made thing, so it operates partly as narrative and partly as reading protocol. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1968:** *62/Modelo para armar*. An open, discontinuous novel developed from chapter 62 of *Rayuela*; “model to assemble” describes both its title and its reader-operated construction. [[4]](https://www.cervantes.es/imagenes/File/biblioteca/bibliografias/cortazar_julio_bibliografia_2015.pdf)
- **1973:** *Libro de Manuel*. A politically engaged collage-novel incorporating contemporary press clippings into its fictional structure; it is both narrative obra and documentary assemblage. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)

### Story collections and books of brief prose

- **c. 1937–1945; first published 1994:** *La otra orilla*. An early, unpublished cycle of stories arranged in three sections; because Cortázar did not issue it, the posthumous collection preserves a youthful project rather than an authorized lifetime book. [[5]](https://www.scielo.br/j/lh/a/V4NJsg9ggQqNfGjxcqbQk8M/?lang=pt)
- **1951:** *Bestiario*. A collection of eight stories and Cortázar’s first published book of narrative under his own name. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1956; enlarged 1964:** *Final del juego*. A story collection first issued with nine stories and substantially enlarged to eighteen; the two authorial states are not merely reprints and completeness depends on which manifestation is meant. [[6]](https://cadenaser.com/nacional/2025/06/22/cuentos-de-julio-cortazar-una-seleccion-de-cuatro-relatos-maravillosos-cadena-ser/)
- **1959:** *Las armas secretas*. A collection of five stories, including the novella-length “El perseguidor”; later combinations such as *Ceremonias* are editorial regroupings rather than new creations. [[7]](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/cortazar_julio_1.htm)
- **1962:** *Historias de cronopios y de famas*. A composite book of instructions, vignettes, absurd protocols and very short narratives; “story collection” inadequately describes its invented beings and mock-administrative forms. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1966:** *Todos los fuegos el fuego*. A collection of eight stories built repeatedly around doubled times, spaces and identities. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1974:** *Octaedro*. Eight stories presented as the textual faces of a geometric object; the title makes spatial arrangement a metaphor for the collection. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1977:** *Alguien que anda por ahí*. A story collection whose politically explicit title story was initially suppressed in Argentina; the publishing history therefore has distinct censored and uncensored manifestations. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1979:** *Un tal Lucas*. A sequence of short prose pieces centred on the unstable figure Lucas; it lies between fictional biography, self-portrait, anecdote and prose poem. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1980:** *Queremos tanto a Glenda*. A collection of ten stories, not to be confused with later selections bearing the title of one of its pieces. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1982:** *Deshoras*. Cortázar’s final lifetime collection of stories. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)

### Libros-almanaque and multimedia assemblages

- **1966:** *Les discours du Pince-Gueule*. A French text by Cortázar made with Julio Silva’s lithographs; the bilingual artist’s book is a joint visual-verbal obra, later manifested in Spanish as *Los discursos del Pinchajeta*. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1966:** *On déplore la…*. A limited artist’s book joining Cortázar’s text to Guido Llinás’s woodcuts; it resists separation into “illustration” and “writing.” [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1967:** *La vuelta al día en ochenta mundos*. A two-volume libro-almanaque made with Julio Silva from stories, essays, poems, photographs, engravings, typography and visual jokes—a deliberate Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a conventional miscellany. [[9]](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/cortazar_julio.htm)
- **1967:** *Buenos Aires, de la fundación a la angustia* (also known as *Buenos Aires Buenos Aires*). A photo-textual city book made with photographs by Sara Facio and Alicia D’Amico; Cortázar’s prose is one component of a collaborative urban portrait. [[4]](https://www.cervantes.es/imagenes/File/biblioteca/bibliografias/cortazar_julio_bibliografia_2015.pdf)
- **1969:** *Último Round*. A two-part libro-almanaque made with Julio Silva, combining fiction, political writing, poems, photographs, graphics and page architecture; its physical arrangement is inseparable from its meaning. [[9]](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/cortazar_julio.htm)
- **1972:** *Prosa del observatorio*. A photo-essay joining Cortázar’s photographs of the astronomical observatories of Jaipur and Delhi to a poetic meditation on eels, stars and systems; it is neither simply essay nor photographic book. [[10]](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1975:** *Leonardo Nierman*. A collaborative art book with Nierman and Max-Pol Fouchet; Cortázar’s contribution exists in relation to Nierman’s images rather than as an autonomous narrative book. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1975:** *Silvalandia*. A visual-verbal territory made from Julio Silva’s images and Cortázar’s texts; its later incorporation into *El último combate* is a new editorial manifestation. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1976:** *Humanario*. A photographic book by Sara Facio and Alicia D’Amico with Cortázar’s text “Estrictamente no profesional”; authorship is collaborative, and the text was later detached and republished in *Territorios*. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1978:** *Tendres parcours*. A limited photographic artist’s book made with Frédéric Barzilay; it is a sequence of photographs and Cortázar’s accompanying verbal parcours, not an ordinary prose volume. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1978:** *Territorios*. A collage-book of essays and encounters with painters, photographers and other artists; it maps Cortázar’s relations with other media rather than maintaining a single literary genre. [[3]](https://rilzea.cialc.unam.mx/jspui/bitstream/CIALC-UNAM/CL463/1/PLS_8.pdf)
- **1980:** *Un elogio del tres*. A multilingual artist’s book made with Luis Tomasello’s linocuts; text, number, colour and serial image form the work together. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1980:** *Monsieur Lautrec: un gotán para Lautrec*. A visual narrative made with Hermenegildo Sábat’s drawings; earlier French and later Spanish titles are distinct manifestations of the collaboration. [[11]](https://www.scielo.cl/pdf/actalit/n61/0717-6848-actalit-61-15.pdf)
- **1981:** *Paris ou la vocation de l’image* / *París: ritmos de una ciudad*. A photo-textual portrait made with Alécio de Andrade; the French and Spanish titles mark different linguistic manifestations of the same collaborative object. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1982:** *La puñalada: Tango op zijn retour*. An artist’s book made with Pat Andrea’s drawings and a specially written Cortázar narrative; it converts the proposal for a conventional preface into a joint graphic-literary work. [[11]](https://www.scielo.cl/pdf/actalit/n61/0717-6848-actalit-61-15.pdf)
- **1983:** *Negro el 10*. A multilingual artist’s book made with Luis Tomasello’s serigraphs; colour, typography, translation and image make it a Gesamtkunstwerk in miniature. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1984:** *Alto el Perú*. A posthumously issued photographic book combining Manja Offerhaus’s images with Cortázar’s texts; the collaboration was prepared during his lifetime but appeared after his death. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)

### Graphic narrative and political manifesto

- **1975:** *Fantomas contra los vampiros multinacionales: una utopía realizable*. A political comic-collage that appropriates pages and characters from a Mexican *Fantomas* comic, inserts Cortázar as a character and reports on the Second Russell Tribunal; it acts as manifesto as much as narrative, and its layered authorship resists attribution to Cortázar alone. [[11]](https://www.scielo.cl/pdf/actalit/n61/0717-6848-actalit-61-15.pdf)
- **1981:** *La raíz del ombú*. A graphic narrative made with Alberto Cedrón; Cortázar supplied the verbal scenario and Cedrón the images, so neither component is merely supplementary. Sources variously date it 1980 and 1981, apparently reflecting preparation and publication. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)
- **1983:** *Nicaragua tan violentamente dulce*. A politically committed assemblage of reports, testimony and photographs arising from Cortázar’s encounters with revolutionary Nicaragua; it is documentary intervention and manifesto rather than detached description. [[3]](https://rilzea.cialc.unam.mx/jspui/bitstream/CIALC-UNAM/CL463/1/PLS_8.pdf)
- **1984:** *Argentina: años de alambradas culturales*. A collection of political interventions on dictatorship, exile and cultural repression, assembled by Saúl Yurkievich; the individual texts are Cortázar’s, but the book as a unified object is partly posthumous editorial construction. [[9]](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/cortazar_julio.htm)

### Journey-work

- **1982–1983:** *Los autonautas de la cosmopista, o Un viaje atemporal París-Marsella*. A collaborative expedition and book made with Carol Dunlop from their rule-bound journey through every motorway rest area; it is simultaneously journey, protocol, field diary, photographs, drawings and invented travel literature. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)

### Essays, criticism and lectures

- **1941:** *Rimbaud*. An early critical essay published under the name Julio Denis; it is an individual periodical text rather than a book. [[12]](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/research/bibliografia-de-julio-cortazar/ed988604-1ec4-11e2-b1fb-00163ebf5e63.pdf)
- **1946:** *La urna griega en la poesía de John Keats*. A scholarly essay first published in the Universidad Nacional de Cuyo’s *Revista de Estudios Clásicos*. [[13]](https://elpais.com/cultura/2025-08-18/cuando-julio-cortazar-se-atrevio-a-ser-julio-cortazar.html)
- **1947; first published 1994:** *Teoría del túnel: notas para una ubicación del surrealismo y el existencialismo*. A book-length poetics left unpublished by Cortázar and recovered in *Obra crítica*; it is a sustained intellectual project, but not a lifetime-authorized book. [[14]](https://wikipedia.cabillot.eu/content/wikipedia_fr_all_maxi_2024-05/A///Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1951–1952; first published 1996:** *Imagen de John Keats*. A book-length, deliberately unacademic critical-biographical experiment; Cortázar completed the manuscript but withheld it, making the published book a posthumous manifestation of an earlier project. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1962–1963:** *Algunos aspectos del cuento*. A lecture-essay that formulates Cortázar’s poetics of short fiction; it circulated through lecture and periodical forms rather than originating as a separate book. [[15]](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-latin-american-literature/bibliographies/D300B8B6C717BE3DC883CE895FD0C9FF)
- **1980; first published 2013:** *Clases de literatura: Berkeley, 1980*. Eight recorded university lectures and discussions; the speech-events are Cortázar’s, while their transformation into a book is posthumous transcription and editing. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)

### Theatre and radio pieces

- **1948:** *Adiós, Robinson*. A dramatic dialogue conceived for radio and only much later collected in book form; its primary ontology is performed voice, not printed drama. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1956:** *Dos juegos de palabras*. Two short dramatic pieces—“Pieza en tres escenas” and “Tiempo de Barrilete”—later issued posthumously as a small book; their publication history is separate from their composition. [[4]](https://www.cervantes.es/imagenes/File/biblioteca/bibliografias/cortazar_julio_bibliografia_2015.pdf)
- **1958:** *Nada a Pehuajó*. A short play whose absurd dialogue approaches theatrical anti-protocol; it was later gathered with Cortázar’s other dramatic pieces. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1958:** *Un misterio, quizá*. A brief play later included in the posthumous dramatic collection *Adiós, Robinson y otras piezas breves*. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1960:** *La banda*. A dramatic or dialogic piece whose status between story, script and theatre is deliberately unstable. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1967:** *La señorita Cora*. A dramatic adaptation by Cortázar of his own polyphonic story; adaptation creates a distinct performative object, not merely another edition of the story. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)

### Film work

- **1947:** *La sombra del pasado*. A film made in Chivilcoy by Ignacio Tankel from a script on which Cortázar collaborated; surviving accounts differ over the exact extent of his authorship, so it should be treated as a disputed collaborative screenplay rather than solely “a Cortázar film.” [[16]](https://www.cervantes.es/imagenes/File/biblioteca/bibliografias/cortazar_julio_bibliografia_2015.pdf)
- **1980:** *La insaciable*. A film script associated with Cortázar and director Armando Bó; its production and authorship history are insufficiently stable to equate the screenplay automatically with a completed Cortázar obra. [[12]](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/research/bibliografia-de-julio-cortazar/ed988604-1ec4-11e2-b1fb-00163ebf5e63.pdf)

### Song-texts and voice recordings

- **1966:** *Cortázar lee a Cortázar*. A phonographic self-reading: the performed voice, timing and sound are the made object, not simply another printing of the texts read. Some discographies date releases or reissues to 1970, so the recording’s first-issue date is not reported consistently. [[17]](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1967:** *Readings by Julio Cortázar*. A Library of Congress recording of Cortázar reading his own work; it is an archival sound performance. [[17]](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1968:** *Julio Cortázar* (Voz Viva de América Latina). An authorial voice recording issued by the Universidad Nacional Autónoma de México. [[17]](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1970:** *Cortázar por él mismo*. A Paris-recorded spoken-word album of self-readings; the voice is a distinct manifestation of works otherwise known in print. [[17]](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1978:** *Narraciones y poemas*. A Casa de las Américas spoken-word record containing Cortázar’s readings; it is a curated performance-object rather than a new written collection. [[17]](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **1980:** *Trottoirs de Buenos Aires*. An album made with composer Edgardo Cantón and singer Juan Cedrón, containing six Cortázar song-texts; at least “Veredas de Buenos Aires,” “Java” and “La Cruz del Sur” adapt pre-existing poems, so the songs are musical manifestations rather than six wholly new literary works. [[18]](https://poemas.uned.es/portal-especializado/tango/)

### Translations as made works

- **1945:** *Robinson Crusoe*, by Daniel Defoe. An abridged Spanish translation; later publications under expanded titles and dates are manifestations of this translation, not new Cortázar originals. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1946:** *Memorias de una enana*, by Walter de la Mare. A Spanish translation from English. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)

- **1946:** *El inmoralista*, by André Gide. A Spanish translation from French; some bibliographies give 1947, so the first-edition year is disputed. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1946:** *El nacimiento de la Odisea*, by Jean Giono. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)

- **1949:** *La sombra de Meyerbeer*, by Auguste de Villiers de L’Isle-Adam. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1950:** *Filosofía de la risa y del llanto*, by Alfred Stern. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1951:** *La filosofía de Sartre y el psicoanálisis existencialista*, by Alfred Stern. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1951:** *Mujercitas*, by Louisa May Alcott. A Spanish translation from English. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1951:** *Tom Brown en la escuela*, by Thomas Hughes. A Spanish translation from English. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1952:** *La víbora*, by Marcel Aymé. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1952:** *La vida de los otros*, by Ladislas Dormandi. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1953:** *Así sea o la suerte está echada*, by André Gide. A Spanish translation from French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1955:** *Vida y cartas de John Keats*, by Lord Houghton. A Spanish translation from English, related to—but not identical with—Cortázar’s own *Imagen de John Keats*. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1955:** *Memorias de Adriano*, by Marguerite Yourcenar. A Spanish translation from French; its many reissues are editions of the same translated work. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1956:** *Obras en prosa*, by Edgar Allan Poe. A large commissioned translation of Poe’s prose for the University of Puerto Rico; later separations into tales, essays, *Arthur Gordon Pym* and *Eureka* complicate any count of it as one or several translated obras. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1966:** *Música en Buenos Aires*, by Virgil Thomson. Cortázar translated Thomson’s prefatory text for Jorge D’Urbano’s book; the translation is a component, not authorship of the whole volume. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1968:** *Aventuras de Arthur Gordon Pym*, by Edgar Allan Poe. A separately issued manifestation of material belonging to Cortázar’s larger Poe translation. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1972:** *Eureka: ensayo sobre el universo material y espiritual*, by Edgar Allan Poe. A separately issued Spanish translation, also related to the earlier Poe commission. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1983:** *Llenos de niños los árboles*, by Carol Dunlop. Cortázar’s Spanish translation of Dunlop’s writing; sources do not consistently identify whether the immediate source text was English or French. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **Before 1951, dates and titles unidentified:** *Translations for Leoplan*. Cortázar performed translations for the magazine, but it printed them without translators’ names; these are real labour and possible obras of translation that resist itemization or secure attribution. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)
- **1954–1967, titles generally unidentified:** *UNESCO conference translations*. Cortázar worked as a professional conference translator, including at Montevideo in 1954 and Athens in 1967; these acts produced translated speech, but the surviving bibliography does not establish fixed literary titles, so they should not be falsified into book-like entries. [[19]](https://virgulilla.wordpress.com/2014/09/07/cortazar-traductor/)

### Correspondence, conversations and posthumous editorial manifestations

- **1939–1984; published in selections from 1992 onward:** *Cartas*. Thousands of letters made as communications rather than as a single work; *Cartas desconocidas*, *Cartas a los Jonquières*, *Cartas a una pelirroja* and the multi-volume *Cartas* are overlapping editorial manifestations, not separate author-planned obras. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1967–1977; published 1978:** *Conversaciones con Cortázar*. Interviews conducted and shaped by Ernesto González Bermejo; Cortázar’s spoken answers are his manifestations, but the book’s selection and architecture belong to the interviewer-editor. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **1970s–1984; published 1984:** *La fascinación de las palabras*. A book-length conversation with Omar Prego; it is jointly produced oral testimony rather than a sole-authored treatise. [[3]](https://rilzea.cialc.unam.mx/jspui/bitstream/CIALC-UNAM/CL463/1/PLS_8.pdf)
- **1983:** *Cuaderno de bitácora de Rayuela*. Cortázar’s working notebook for *Rayuela*, published in facsimile and transcription with scholarly apparatus; it is evidence of a project and also a graphic manuscript-object, but Cortázar did not design it as an independent public work. [[4]](https://www.cervantes.es/imagenes/File/biblioteca/bibliografias/cortazar_julio_bibliografia_2015.pdf)
- **1994:** *Obra crítica*. A three-volume posthumous scholarly collection edited by Saúl Yurkievich, Jaime Alazraki and Saúl Sosnowski; the constituent essays are Cortázar’s, but the collected obra is an editorial construction. [[2]](https://www.bnf.fr/sites/default/files/2024-01/Bibliographie_Julio_Cortazar.pdf)
- **2009:** *Papeles inesperados*. A posthumous collection edited by Aurora Bernárdez and Carles Álvarez Garriga from manuscripts and uncollected publications; it manifests archival discoveries but should not be mistaken for a book planned by Cortázar. [[20]](https://riull.ull.es/xmlui/bitstream/915/1328/1/ATOMOS%2BDE%2BLA%2BESCRITURA%2B%28LOS%2BMICRORRELATOS%2BDE%2BJULIO%2BCORTAZAR%29.pdf)
- **2012:** *Corrección de pruebas en Alta Provenza*. A separately published travel-text recovered from the archive; its late book form is editorial, though the underlying text is Cortázar’s. [[21]](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **2012:** *Las paradas de De Sanctis*. A posthumously published narrative recovered from Cortázar’s papers; its existence as a separate book is a later editorial manifestation rather than a lifetime work-form. [[21]](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Cort%C3%A1zar)
- **2013:** *El último combate*. A posthumous visual-verbal reconstruction bringing together *Les discours du Pince-Gueule* and material associated with *Silvalandia*; it preserves Cortázar–Silva collaborations but is not itself a lifetime-authorized new obra. [[8]](https://www.memoria.fahce.unlp.edu.ar/tesis/te.2566/te.2566.pdf)

### Projects that resist a stable work-list

- **Before 1946:** *An early book destroyed by Cortázar*. Contemporary scholarship reports that Cortázar had a book in hand and later destroyed it; without a securely surviving title or text, it remains a lost project and must not be converted into an invented bibliographic item. [[13]](https://elpais.com/cultura/2025-08-18/cuando-julio-cortazar-se-atrevio-a-ser-julio-cortazar.html)
- **Throughout his career:** *Uncollected periodical texts, prologues, jacket texts and catalogue contributions*. The standard bio-bibliography records hundreds of such pieces in addition to books, artist’s books, mimeographs and recordings; many were later absorbed into collections, while others remain discrete manifestations. Treating every later reprint or excerpt as a new work would confuse obra with edition. [[22]](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/articulo/cultura/2015/05/5/todo-cortazar-la-bibliografia-completa/)
- **Throughout his career:** *Photographs, drawings and book-design interventions*. Cortázar acted not only as writer but at times as photographer, illustrator and active maker of page structures; these components survive principally inside collaborative books and cannot always be detached, titled or dated as autonomous works. [[23]](https://recursos.march.es/web/bibliotecas/cronologia/pdf/Bibliotecas-Historia-Cortazar.pdf)
