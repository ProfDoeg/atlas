## Works by Mircea Eliade

### Fiction: novels and “indirect novels”

- **1924–1927; first complete publication 1989:** *Romanul adolescentului miop*. Autobiographical novel assembled from adolescent writing and partly serialized during Eliade’s lifetime; its dating varies because composition, serialization, and complete book publication were separate events. Eliade placed the manuscript in the appendix to volume I of his projected *Opera omnia*.[^1][^2]
- **1928; first publication 1986:** *Gaudeamus*. Sequel to *Romanul adolescentului miop*, left in manuscript for decades and therefore both an early novel and a posthumously established text.[^1][^2]
- **1930:** *Isabel și apele diavolului*. Novel and Eliade’s first published book-length fiction.[^1][^2]
- **Written 1932; published 1934:** *Întoarcerea din rai*. Novel conceived as the first part of a trilogy with *Huliganii* and the unrealized *Viața nouă*; the intended trilogy is therefore an unfinished project rather than a completed work.[^1][^2]
- **1933:** *Maitreyi*. Novel, subsequently recreated through translations under titles including *La Nuit bengali* and *Bengal Nights*; those are manifestations of the Romanian work rather than additional novels by Eliade.[^1][^3]
- **1934:** *Lumina ce se stinge*. Novel; Eliade later relegated it to an appendix in his own plan for the complete works, marking an authorial distinction between the made text and his preferred canon.[^1][^2]
- **1934; published 1935:** *Șantier*. Diary-derived “indirect novel”: neither unmediated journal nor conventional fiction, but a deliberately fashioned hybrid.[^1][^4]
- **1935:** *Huliganii*. Novel and second completed component of the projected trilogy begun with *Întoarcerea din rai*; *Viața nouă*, the intended third part, was not completed.[^1][^2]
- **1936; published 1937:** *Șarpele*. Fantastic novella or short novel; bibliographies differ over its genre but not over the text’s identity.[^1][^2]
- **1938:** *Nuntă în cer*. Novel built from two interlocking confessional narratives.[^1][^2]
- **1949–1954; French 1955, Romanian 1971:** *Noaptea de Sânziene*. Exile novel, first issued in French as *Forêt interdite* before the Romanian original reached print; publication date therefore depends on whether the work, translation, or original-language manifestation is meant.[^1][^2]

### Fiction: tales, novellas, and story cycles

- **1921:** *Cum am găsit piatra filosofală*. Juvenile tale and Eliade’s first published story, written when he was fourteen.[^1]
- **1923–1928:** *Schițe și nuvele din tinerețe*. A body of juvenile sketches and stories dispersed through periodicals rather than one fixed-titled work; Eliade grouped them retrospectively as an appendix to his projected complete fiction.[^2]
- **1932:** *Într-o mănăstire din Himalaya*. Travel-inflected narrative whose bibliographical classification shifts between fiction, reportage, and recollection.[^1]
- **1937:** *Aventura*. Short story first printed with *Șarpele*.[^1][^2]
- **1937:** *Întâlnire*. Short story first printed with *Șarpele*.[^1][^2]
- **1940:** *Nopți la Serampore*. Fantastic novella.[^1][^2]
- **1940:** *Secretul doctorului Honigberger*. Fantastic novella structured as a scholarly-occult investigation.[^1][^2]
- **1945:** *Un om mare*. Fantastic tale; Eliade included it in the first novella volume of his projected *Opera omnia*.[^2]
- **1952:** *Douăsprezece mii de capete de vite*. Fantastic short story.[^1][^2]
- **1955:** *Fata căpitanului*. Short story, published later in a Romanian collection.[^1][^2]
- **1955:** *Adio!*. Metatheatrical fantastic story; some bibliographies date its later publication rather than its composition.[^1][^2]
- **1959–1960:** *La țigănci*. Fantastic novella; frequently dated 1960, although composition and first publication are not always distinguished.[^1][^2]
- **1963:** *O fotografie veche de 14 ani*. Fantastic story. Eliade’s own complete-works outline calls it *O fotografie veche de 12 ani*, while the established published title says fourteen; the discrepancy should not be silently harmonized.[^1][^2]
- **1963:** *Ivan*. Short story.[^1][^2]
- **1963:** *Podul*. Short story whose conversations and suspended narrative make it resist conventional plot classification.[^1][^2]
- **1963:** *Ghicitor în pietre*. Short story, also translated as *The Man Who Could Read Stone*.[^1][^2]
- **1964:** *Într-o cazarmă*. Short story first printed in the exile journal *Destin*; Eliade treated it as an appendix rather than part of the principal second novella sequence.[^1][^2]
- **1967:** *Pe strada Mântuleasa*. Novella, also circulated as *The Old Man and the Bureaucrats*.[^1][^2]
- **By 1975:** *Șanțurile*. Story named in Eliade’s *Opera omnia* plan but poorly stabilized in shorter bibliographies; its exact independent publication history remains unclear.[^2]
- **1968:** *În curte la Dionis*. Fantastic story; the same title was later used for a collection, so the story and the containing book should not be conflated.[^1][^2]
- **1971:** *Uniforme de general*. Novella or long story.[^1][^2]
- **1974:** *Incognito la Buchenwald*. Fantastic story with a theatrical structure.[^1][^2]
- **1975:** *Pelerina*. Novella; published in Romanian only after Eliade’s death, although an English translation appeared earlier.[^1][^2]
- **1976:** *Les Trois Grâces*. Novella; the French title belongs to a Romanian-language fiction and is not evidence of a separate French original.[^1]
- **1976:** *Tinerețe fără de tinerețe*. Novella, later adapted for film by another maker.[^1]
- **1979:** *Nouăsprezece trandafiri*. Novella organized around theatrical performance, disappearance, and political coercion.[^1]
- **1979:** *Dayan*. Novella, published posthumously in Romanian.[^1]
- **1982:** *La umbra unui crin*. Late novella, likewise published posthumously.[^1]

### Drama

- **1939; first book publication 1951:** *Iphigenia* / *Ifigenia*. Three-act play; variant spellings and subtitles belong to different publications of the same drama.[^1][^2]
- **1943:** *Oameni și pietre*. Play written in Portugal and long retained in manuscript; its limited circulation makes it a completed dramatic text without a stable lifetime public form.[^1][^2]
- **1945:** *1241*. Surviving dramatic fragments, not a completed play; Eliade explicitly classified them as manuscript fragments.[^2]
- **1946; first publication 2012:** *Aventura spirituală*. Play discovered and translated by Mac Linscott Ricketts after Eliade’s death; it is a completed manuscript but not a lifetime publication.[^1][^2]
- **1969–1970; first Romanian book publication 1996:** *Coloana nesfârșită*. Play about Constantin Brâncuși. Its dates vary between composition, circulation in translation, and posthumous Romanian publication.[^1][^2]

### Essays, philosophy, and Romanian cultural studies

- **1927:** *Itinerar spiritual*. Serial essay-cycle and generational manifesto: it acts by summoning a “young generation,” rather than merely describing one.[^1][^2]
- **1928:** *Apologia virilității*. Polemical essay or manifesto published in *Gândirea*; its rhetoric and political-cultural function resist treatment as neutral scholarship.[^1]
- **1932:** *Solilocvii*. Collection of philosophical and autobiographical essays.[^3][^4]
- **1934:** *Oceanografie*. Collection of newspaper essays and cultural feuilletons, not a systematic treatise.[^1][^2][^4]
- **1934:** *India*. Travel book assembled from observation, memoir, cultural essay, and imaginative reconstruction.[^2][^5]
- **1936–1938:** *Carnete de drum*. Travel-notebook sequence published in periodicals; a dispersed textual body rather than a single securely bounded book.[^2]
- **1939:** *Fragmentarium*. Collection of philosophical, literary, and cultural fragments.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1942:** *Mitul reintegrării*. Comparative essay on reintegration and coincident opposites.[^2][^3]
- **1943:** *Insula lui Euthanasius*. Collection of literary and cultural studies, named for its essay on Eminescu.[^2][^3]
- **1937:** *Introducere la scrierile lui B. P. Hasdeu*. Editorial introduction and interpretive study made for Eliade’s Hasdeu edition; it is both scholarship and part of another author’s edited textual object.[^2][^5]
- **1940s:** *Limbajele secrete*. Scholarly essay later assigned by Eliade to volume IX of his projected complete works.[^2]
- **1940s:** *Ierburile de sub cruce*. Folkloric essay later assigned to the same projected volume; the plan supplies no secure date, so none should be invented.[^2]
- **1940–1975:** *Eseuri și articole*. A large multilingual body of essays and journalism that Eliade proposed collecting as a volume; it resists enumeration as a single work but constitutes an author-defined obra.[^2]
- **1921–1928:** *Articole din adolescență și prima tinerețe*. Hundreds of juvenile pieces, reviews, notes, and popular-science articles dispersed in newspapers and magazines. This is an archival and editorial corpus rather than one work, and its boundaries remain dependent on continuing bibliographical recovery.[^2][^6]

### History of religions, Indology, and comparative scholarship

- **1928; revised university thesis 1932–1933:** *Contribuții la filosofia Renașterii* / *Contributions to the Philosophy of the Renaissance*. Academic thesis and early scholarly project; bibliographies vary in naming and dating its manuscript and printed forms.[^4][^7]
- **1933:** *The Comparative History of Yoga Techniques*. Doctoral research project underlying Eliade’s subsequent Yoga books; the English title in later bibliographies may describe the thesis rather than a distinct English publication.[^1]
- **1935:** *Alchimia asiatică*. Scholarly monograph on Asian alchemy; some lists give 1934, while the first-edition chronology used by Humanitas gives 1935.[^1][^3]
- **1936:** *Yoga: Essai sur les origines de la mystique indienne*. French scholarly monograph developed from the doctoral project; it is not simply an edition of the later, substantially enlarged *Le Yoga*.[^1][^3]
- **1937:** *Cosmologie și alchimie babiloniană*. Comparative monograph on Babylonian cosmology and alchemy.[^1][^3]
- **1943:** *Comentarii la legenda Meșterului Manole*. Folklore and history-of-religions study centered on the Romanian building-sacrifice legend.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1948:** *Techniques du Yoga*. Concise scholarly synthesis, later treated by Eliade as an appendix to his larger Yoga work.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1949:** *Traité d’histoire des religions*. Comparative morphology of religious phenomena, translated as *Patterns in Comparative Religion*; later translations and revised editions are manifestations of this work.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1949:** *Le Mythe de l’éternel retour: archétypes et répétition*. Comparative study of archaic temporality and repetition, later retitled in English *Cosmos and History*; the English title does not denote another work.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1951:** *Le Chamanisme et les techniques archaïques de l’extase*. Large comparative monograph; the revised and enlarged 1968 French edition materially changes the same continuing obra rather than creating an unrelated title.[^1][^3]
- **1952:** *Images et symboles*. Collection of studies in religious symbolism.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1954:** *Le Yoga: Immortalité et liberté*. Major rewriting and enlargement of Eliade’s earlier Yoga scholarship; sufficiently transformed to be treated as a new scholarly work, while still continuous with the 1936 project.[^2][^3]
- **1956:** *Forgerons et alchimistes*. Comparative study of metallurgical myth, ritual, and alchemy.[^1][^3]
- **1957:** *Das Heilige und das Profane*. Synthetic study first published in German and later in French as *Le Sacré et le profane*; its publication history begins with the German manifestation, not the later French edition.[^1][^3]
- **1957:** *Mythes, rêves et mystères*. Collection of studies joining myth, dream, initiation, and modernity.[^2][^3]
- **1956 lectures; 1958 book:** *Birth and Rebirth: The Religious Meanings of Initiation in Human Culture*. Haskell Lectures made into a book, later revised in French as *Naissances mystiques*; lecture event and book are related but not identical objects.[^1][^3]
- **1958:** *Patterns of Initiation*. Title associated with Eliade’s Haskell lecture project and sometimes treated as an alternate designation for *Birth and Rebirth*, not securely a separate authored book.[^1]
- **1959:** *Naissances mystiques*. Revised French manifestation of the initiation study; because the revision is authorial, it is more than a straightforward translation but remains part of the same project.[^2][^3]
- **1962:** *Méphistophélès et l’androgyne*. Collection of comparative essays, translated as *The Two and the One*.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1962:** *Patañjali et le Yoga*. Concise monograph on Patañjali and classical Yoga.[^1][^3]
- **1963:** *Aspects du mythe*. Synthetic study of myth in archaic and modern societies.[^1][^3]
- **1967:** *From Primitives to Zen: A Thematic Sourcebook of the History of Religions*. Anthology conceived and organized by Eliade; it is an editorial architecture made from translated primary sources, not a monograph wholly written by him.[^1][^3]
- **1969:** *The Quest: History and Meaning in Religion*. Collection of methodological essays, later issued in French as *La Nostalgie des origines*.[^1][^3]
- **1970:** *La Nostalgie des origines*. Authorially reorganized French manifestation of *The Quest*, not an independent research project.[^2][^3]
- **1970:** *De Zalmoxis à Gengis-Khan*. Comparative studies of Dacian and eastern-European religions and folklore.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1972:** *Religions australiennes*. Monograph on Australian Indigenous religions, published in English as *Australian Religions* in 1973.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1976:** *Occultism, Witchcraft and Cultural Fashions*. Collection of lectures and essays addressing modern occult revivals and the history of religions.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1976:** *Histoire des croyances et des idées religieuses, I: De l’âge de la pierre aux mystères d’Éleusis*. First volume of Eliade’s large synthetic history of religious ideas.[^1][^3][^5]
- **1978:** *Histoire des croyances et des idées religieuses, II: De Gautama Bouddha au triomphe du christianisme*. Second volume of the synthetic history.[^1][^3][^5]
- **1983:** *Histoire des croyances et des idées religieuses, III: De Mahomet à l’âge des Réformes*. Third completed volume of the synthetic history.[^1][^3][^5]
- **Unfinished at Eliade’s death:** *Histoire des croyances et des idées religieuses, IV*. Projected continuation of the history into modern religions. The posthumous fourth volume assembled by Ioan Petru Culianu from previously published writings is an editorial construction and should not be represented as a completed fourth volume made by Eliade.[^5][^8]
- **1986:** *Briser le toit de la maison*. Late essay collection; a lifetime book, though close enough to Eliade’s death that some catalogues treat it inconsistently as posthumous.[^3]
- **1986:** *Symbolism, the Sacred, and the Arts*. Thematic reader edited by Diane Apostolos-Cappadona from Eliade’s writings; it is a post-selection manifestation of his work, not a wholly new book authored in 1986.[^1]
- **1987:** *The Encyclopedia of Religion*. Sixteen-volume collective reference work conceived and directed by Eliade as editor-in-chief. It is an editorial Gesamtkunstwerk of commissioned scholarship rather than a text authored by him alone, and it appeared after his death.[^1][^5]

### Political and national-cultural interventions

- **1942:** *Salazar și revoluția în Portugalia*. Political portrait of António de Oliveira Salazar and the Portuguese Estado Novo. It functions partly as advocacy, so “manifesto” is more accurate than neutral biography, although its precise ideological interpretation remains disputed.[^1][^3]
- **1943:** *Os Romenos, Latinos do Oriente*. Portuguese-language national-cultural presentation of Romanians; a diplomatic-cultural intervention as well as a book.[^1][^2][^3]
- **1927–1940:** Political, cultural, and newspaper articles in *Cuvântul*, *Vremea*, *Buna Vestire*, and other periodicals. This large corpus contains texts associated with Eliade’s controversial support for the Romanian far right and Iron Guard; authorship, pseudonyms, and the boundaries of the political corpus remain matters of bibliographical and historical dispute.[^2][^4][^6]

### Journals, notebooks, memoir, and autobiography

- **1928–1931:** *Jurnal indian* / Indian notebooks. Working diaries from India, only partly transformed into Eliade’s lifetime books; surviving notebooks include unpublished material and therefore resist identification with any one published journal.[^2][^9]
- **1935:** *Șantier*. Besides being advertised as an “indirect novel,” this is a constructed publication from the Indian journal; it belongs simultaneously to fiction, diary, and autobiographical obra.[^1][^4]
- **1944:** *Jurnal de la Córdoba*. Travel journal named in Eliade’s complete-works plan; its archival and later editorial forms do not establish a single uncontested lifetime edition.[^2]
- **1941–1985:** *Jurnal*. Long-running private journal, published only in selections and editorially divided volumes. The surviving manuscript, translations, lost typescripts, censored selections, and posthumous Romanian editions are manifestations of an incompletely published textual continuum, not one stable book.[^2][^5]
- **1945–1969; published 1973:** *Fragments d’un journal I*. Author-selected French journal fragments; a fashioned public work extracted from the larger private diary.[^5]
- **1970–1978; published 1981:** *Fragments d’un journal II*. Second author-selected public journal volume.[^5]
- **1979–1985; published posthumously:** *Journal IV*. Late journal section edited after Eliade’s death; the numbering follows the English publication sequence rather than an authorial four-part architecture.[^5][^10]
- **Adolescent years; posthumous:** *Jurnalul din adolescență*. Juvenile diary surviving in manuscript and assigned by Eliade to the appendix of his projected journal volumes; it is an archival text rather than a lifetime work.[^2]
- **1907–1937; Romanian publication 1980:** *Memorii I: Mansarda*. First part of Eliade’s autobiography; English publication as *Autobiography, Volume I: Journey East, Journey West* rearranges its public form but does not create a separate autobiography.[^5][^10]
- **1937–1960; posthumous English publication 1988:** *Memorii II* / *Autobiography, Volume II: Exile’s Odyssey*. Continuation composed by Eliade but edited and published after his death; its final English object is therefore partly editorial.[^1][^10]
- **Indian period and 1931–1940:** *Amintiri II–III*. Memoir sections that Eliade described as partly published and intended for volume XV of his *Opera omnia*; their fragmented state makes the projected three-part memoir larger than the stable lifetime book.[^2]

### Editorial, institutional, and periodical creations

- **1938–1942:** *Zalmoxis: Revue des études religieuses*. International history-of-religions journal founded and edited by Eliade. It is an institutional and editorial creation made from many authors’ texts, not a book by Eliade alone.[^5][^7]
- **1961 onward:** *History of Religions*. Academic journal co-founded and edited by Eliade at the University of Chicago; an enduring institutional obra rather than an individually authored text.[^5]
- **1937:** *B. P. Hasdeu, Scrieri literare, morale și politice*. Edition selected and introduced by Eliade; the editorial arrangement and introduction are his work, while Hasdeu’s writings are not.[^2][^5]
- **1967:** *From Primitives to Zen*. Sourcebook selected, translated through collaborators, classified, and framed by Eliade; its made form is curatorial and taxonomic.[^1][^3]
- **1987:** *The Encyclopedia of Religion*. Collective sixteen-volume reference environment planned under Eliade’s direction; death before publication means the finished material object cannot be attributed to him without also crediting its contributors and posthumous editorial completion.[^1][^5]

### Projects, abandoned works, and resistant objects

- **1932–1935:** *Viața nouă*. Intended third novel of the sequence begun by *Întoarcerea din rai* and continued by *Huliganii*. It is a project, not a completed or independently published novel.[^1]
- **Before 1976:** *Glossarium*. Partly unpublished assemblage of articles from 1932–1940, proposed by Eliade for volume VIII of his complete works. It resists being called a finished book because its boundaries remained editorial and prospective.[^2]
- **Undated:** *La Mandragore*. Abandoned scholarly work named by Eliade among the *Scripta minora* for his projected volume XXVI; surviving essays on the mandrake may manifest the project but do not prove completion of the planned book.[^2]
- **Undated:** *Mythologie de la Mort*. Abandoned or unfinished research project listed by Eliade; no completed work should be inferred from the title alone.[^2]
- **Undated:** *Le symbolisme du Centre*. Abandoned book project. Eliade published related essays, but the projected volume itself resists being counted as a completed work.[^2]
- **Undated:** *South-American Religions*. Abandoned scholarly project named in the *Opera omnia* plan; it survives as an intention and research trace, not an established monograph.[^2]
- **1973–1976:** *Opera omnia*. Eliade’s own projected twenty-six-volume arrangement of his fiction, drama, essays, journals, scholarship, fragments, and abandoned works. It is a major authorial project and map of the oeuvre, but it was never realized in the proposed form.[^2]
- **Posthumous and ongoing:** *Opere* / complete critical editions. No genuinely complete collected edition yet exists: estimates place the whole corpus at roughly 80–90 volumes, while current critical-edition projects continue to recover manuscripts and early scholarship. These editions are manifestations assembled by later editors, not new works made by Eliade.[^6][^9]
- **1921–1986:** Approximately 1,200 articles and reviews. The number describes a dispersed corpus documented by specialist bibliographies, not 1,200 securely delimited “works”: revised reprints, translations, unsigned journalism, lecture texts, and pieces later absorbed into books make any simple item count misleading.[^6]
- **1926–1986:** Manuscripts, lecture texts, interviews, notes, dream notebooks, research files, recordings, and damaged papers. The University of Chicago archive preserves these as material traces of intellectual work; many resist the status of finished works, and the 1986 office fire destroyed or damaged part of the record.[^5]
