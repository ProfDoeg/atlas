## Works by Bernard of Clairvaux

### Treatises, exhortations, and polemical interventions

- **before 1124:** *De gradibus humilitatis et superbiae* (*The Steps of Humility and Pride*). Bernard’s first treatise: a monastic analysis of humility and its inverse, organized around the Rule of Benedict’s twelve degrees. Its date is approximate, but the critical editors place it before 1124. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2))

- **c. 1124–1125:** *Apologia ad Guillelmum abbatem* (*Apology to William of Saint-Thierry*). A polemical monastic manifesto defending Cistercian austerity while criticizing Cluniac luxury; Bernard wrote it at William’s request, although its attack and its conciliatory frame pull in different directions. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2))

- **1126–1141:** *De diligendo Deo* (*On Loving God*). A theological and contemplative treatise addressed to Cardinal Haimeric, explaining love of God as an ascending transformation of desire; the recipient’s career supplies the broad dating limits, while 1126 is sometimes proposed more narrowly. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2), [University of Liège description](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/168708/1/Bernard%20de%20Clairvaux_R.13D.4.pdf))

- **before 1128, probably c. 1127:** *De gratia et libero arbitrio* (*On Grace and Free Choice*). A doctrinal treatise, sent to William of Saint-Thierry while William was still an abbot, on how human freedom participates in salvation without originating it. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2), [University of Liège description](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/168708/1/Bernard%20de%20Clairvaux_R.13D.4.pdf))

- **c. 1128–1136:** *Liber ad milites Templi de laude novae militiae* (*In Praise of the New Knighthood*). An exhortation and ideological manifesto addressed to the Templar master Hugh of Payns, contrasting secular chivalry with a militant religious vocation; it is not the Templar Rule, whose direct authorship should not be assigned to Bernard. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2), [BnF critical-text record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35306279v))

- **after 1128; possibly c. 1142:** *De praecepto et dispensatione* (*On Precept and Dispensation*). A public answer to questions from the Benedictine abbot of Coulombs about obedience, vows, abbatial authority, and dispensations from the Rule; proposed datings vary substantially. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2), [University of Liège description](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/168708/1/Bernard%20de%20Clairvaux_R.13D.4.pdf))

- **1139–early 1140:** *Ad clericos de conversione* (*On the Conversion of Clerics*). A sermon delivered to scholars and clerics at Paris and subsequently made into a carefully organized exhortatory book; the older date 1122 remains common in reference works, but modern chronology places the address in 1139 or early 1140. ([Brill companion bibliography](https://brill.com/previewpdf/book/edcoll/9789004211988/Bej.9789004201392.i-406_014.xml), [chronological discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux#Works))

- **before 1128:** *De moribus et officio episcoporum* (*On the Conduct and Office of Bishops*; Letter 42). A letter-treatise to Archbishop Henry of Sens which functions as a mirror and admonitory protocol for episcopal office; it is both an individual letter and a separately circulating treatise. ([Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02498d.htm), [modern publication description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux#Works))

- **c. 1127–1128:** *De baptismo aliisque quaestionibus* (*On Baptism and Other Questions*; Letter 77). A letter-treatise answering Hugh of Saint-Victor on baptism and related theological problems; like *De moribus*, it resists a clean division between correspondence and formal treatise. ([modern publication description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux#Works))

- **1140:** *Contra quaedam capitula errorum Abaelardi* (*Against Certain Articles of Abelard’s Errors*; Letter 190). A dossier-like polemical letter to Pope Innocent II, intended to bring about ecclesiastical action against Peter Abelard’s propositions: in the vocabulary of the atlas, it is closer to a manifesto or prosecutorial brief than to a detached description. It remains simultaneously part of the numbered correspondence. ([Brill companion bibliography](https://brill.com/previewpdf/book/edcoll/9789004211988/Bej.9789004201392.i-406_014.xml))

- **1148–1153:** *De consideratione libri quinque ad Eugenium III* (*Five Books on Consideration*). A five-part mirror for Bernard’s former monk, Pope Eugenius III, combining papal counsel, institutional criticism, spiritual exercise, and speculative theology; composed and dispatched in stages, with Book I substantially ready in 1149 and Book III written in 1152. ([composition chronology](https://www.citeaux.net/brem/intro-3.htm), [work description](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_consideratione))

### Biography and memorial writing

- **c. 1149–1152:** *Vita sancti Malachiae episcopi* (*Life of Saint Malachy*). A commissioned saint’s life based partly on Irish Cistercian reports and partly on Bernard’s personal knowledge of Malachy; it is Bernard’s only full biography and is at once memorial, reform history, and hagiography. ([SBO III review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1966_num_9_33_1368_t1_0078_0000_2), [BnF critical-text record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35306279v))

- **after 2 November 1148:** *Epitaphium sancti Malachiae* (*Epitaph for Saint Malachy*). A short memorial poem made for Malachy after his death at Clairvaux; it is an inscriptional object as well as a literary text. ([BiblIndex](https://www.biblindex.org/en/work-editions/eloge-de-la-nouvelle-chevalerie-vie-de-saint-malachie-epitaphe-hymne-lettres-2), [BnF critical-text record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35306279v))

- **after 2 November 1148:** *Hymnus de sancto Malachia* (*Hymn for Saint Malachy*). A liturgical poem intended for communal singing rather than silent reading; scholarship recognizes it as one of only two hymns securely associated with Bernard. ([BnF critical-text record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb35306279v), [New Catholic Encyclopedia, “Hymnology”](https://cvdvn.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/new-catholic-encyclopedia-vol-7.pdf))

### Sustained sermon-projects

- **1118–1123:** *Homiliae super “Missus est” / In laudibus Virginis Matris* (*Homilies in Praise of the Virgin Mother*). Four connected homilies on the Annunciation, transmitted as an early Marian work and classified variously as sermons or a treatise. ([JERIHNA–Sources Chrétiennes](https://jerihna.sourceschretiennes.org/en/work-editions/a-la-louange-de-la-vierge-mere))

- **c. 1125:** *Sermones super “Qui habitat”*. Seventeen Lenten sermons on Psalm 90/91; together they form a continuous scriptural exposition rather than seventeen independently titled books. ([Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02498d.htm), [SBO corpus overview](https://brill.com/previewpdf/book/edcoll/9789004211988/Bej.9789004201392.i-406_014.xml))

- **1135–1153:** *Sermones super Cantica Canticorum* (*Sermons on the Song of Songs*), Sermons 1–86. Bernard’s unfinished long-form exegetical project, composed over nearly two decades and ending near the opening of the Song’s third chapter; its incompleteness is constitutive, since death stopped the project rather than supplying it with a conclusion. ([chronology](https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/brief-chronology-of-bernard-of-clairvaux), [SBO corpus overview](https://brill.com/previewpdf/book/edcoll/9789004211988/Bej.9789004201392.i-406_014.xml))

- **various dates, before 1153:** *Sermones per annum* (*Sermons for the Liturgical Year*). The critical corpus preserves 125 sermons organized by seasons and saints; these are individual preaching texts but also a posthumously stabilized liturgical cycle, not a single book planned under that title by Bernard. Older enumerations divide them differently and consequently produce different totals. ([critical-edition overview](https://brill.com/previewpdf/book/edcoll/9789004211988/Bej.9789004201392.i-406_014.xml), [corpus count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux#Works))

- **various dates, before 1153:** *Sermones de diversis* (*Sermons on Diverse Subjects*), Sermons 1–125. A numbered corpus of sermons and conference-like addresses not tied consistently to the calendar; the collection is an editorial body rather than a single author-designed book, and older collections included pieces now rejected as spurious. ([ARCCIS bibliography](https://www.arccis.org/downloads/biblio2-0net.pdf), [corpus overview and attribution warning](https://azioneromana.com/article/bernardo-di-chiaravalle))

- **various dates, before 1153; corpus enlarged after 1970:** *Sermones varii* (*Various Sermons*), ten items in the modern supplementary group. These sermons sit outside the principal medieval collections; three were added in *Sancti Bernardi Opera* VI/1, and a sermon for Saint Benedict was authenticated subsequently, making the boundaries of Bernard’s sermon corpus demonstrably revisable. ([Sources Chrétiennes review](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhef_0048-7988_2012_num_98_240_4645_t9_0150_0000_2))

### Letters and administrative texts

- **c. 1119–1153:** *Epistolae*, Letters 1–547. Bernard’s surviving correspondence is a body of diplomatic, pastoral, political, polemical, and personal acts rather than one authored “book”: roughly half entered an official medieval *corpus* or register, while Letters 311–547 survive outside that collection, and some letters were drafted or stylistically mediated by secretaries. The critical edition’s 547-number sequence is therefore a catalogue of transmitted items, not 547 uncontested autograph compositions. ([study of the official letter collection](https://www.persee.fr/doc/ccmed_0007-9731_1971_num_14_55_1892), [SBO table of contents](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL14765588M/Sancti_Bernardi_opera.))

### Sentences, teaching materials, and narrative miniatures

- **undated, before 1153:** *Sententiae*, Series I–III. Three series of short theological and monastic teaching-units preserved in *Sancti Bernardi Opera* VI/2; their form lies between notes, oral instruction, excerpt, and finished text, so individual authorship and authorial finality are less secure than for the treatises. ([ARCCIS bibliography](https://www.arccis.org/downloads/biblio2-0net.pdf))

- **undated, before 1153:** *Parabolae*, nos. 1–8. Eight allegorical narratives or spiritual teaching-fables in *Sancti Bernardi Opera* VI/2; only five appeared in the older *Patrologia Latina* corpus, illustrating that the object called “the work” depends partly on manuscript reconstruction. ([ARCCIS bibliography](https://www.arccis.org/downloads/biblio2-0net.pdf))

### Liturgical Gesamtkunstwerke and collaborative projects

- **probably before 1147:** *Officium sancti Victoris* (*Office of Saint Victor*). A proper liturgical office honoring Victor of Arcis, joining words, biblical selection, melody, calendar, and communal performance; because Bernard composed both text and music, it is his clearest Gesamtkunstwerk and the only complete liturgical office securely known as his. ([Sources Chrétiennes 527](https://www.editionsducerf.fr/librairie/sc-527-office-de-saint-victor/), [review of the critical edition](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhef_0048-7988_2010_num_96_237_4533_t21_0535_0000_2))

- **c. 1140–1147:** *Revision of the Cistercian antiphoner and hymnal*. A collective liturgical-reform project undertaken under Bernard’s authority, producing the so-called Bernardine recension of Cistercian chant; surviving evidence does not license treating every altered text or melody as Bernard’s personal composition. The made thing is therefore the supervised recension and performance system, not an individually authored book. ([Western Michigan University critical edition](https://digitalcollections-wmich.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p15032coll5/id/9/), [Sources Chrétiennes 527](https://www.editionsducerf.fr/librairie/sc-527-office-de-saint-victor/))

- **c. 1140–1147:** *Prologus in antiphonarium* (*Prologue to the Antiphoner*). A programmatic statement attached to the revised chant-book and functioning as a protocol for liturgical music; it belongs to the collaborative reform project but survives as a discrete text. ([Sources Chrétiennes 527](https://www.editionsducerf.fr/librairie/sc-527-office-de-saint-victor/))

- **c. 1140–1147:** *Epistola 398, de revisione cantus Cisterciensis* (*Letter on the Revision of Cistercian Chant*). A letter accompanying or explaining the musical reform, addressed to Guy of Montiéramey; it is simultaneously correspondence, aesthetic manifesto, and protocol, and therefore should not be counted as an additional work without acknowledging its place in the 547-letter corpus. ([Sources Chrétiennes 527](https://www.editionsducerf.fr/librairie/sc-527-office-de-saint-victor/), [manuscript study](https://www.persee.fr/doc/scrip_0036-9772_1956_num_10_2_2705))

### Attributed works that resist inclusion

- **medieval attribution, now rejected:** *Scala claustralium* (*The Ladder of Monks*). Frequently transmitted or catalogued under Bernard’s name, this contemplative protocol is by Guigo II the Carthusian and is not a work Bernard made. ([Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02498d.htm))

- **medieval attribution, now rejected:** *Meditationes* (*Meditations*). A widely circulated pseudo-Bernardine devotional compilation excluded from the authentic corpus; “Pseudo-Bernard” names a transmission history, not a securely identifiable work by Bernard. ([Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02498d.htm))

- **medieval attribution, now rejected:** *De interiori domo* (*On the Interior House*). Another influential devotional work long printed among Bernard’s writings but rejected by modern criticism. ([Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02498d.htm))

- **medieval attribution, now rejected:** *Ad sororem modus bene vivendi*. A conduct-book repeatedly printed under Bernard’s name but absent from the authentic critical corpus; early printed catalogues document its attribution, not Bernard’s authorship. ([BnF early-printing bibliography](https://bp16.bnf.fr/11891532/bernard_de_clairvaux/))

- **medieval or modern attribution, unsupported:** *Jesu dulcis memoria* and the later hymn families derived from it. These devotional lyrics have often been called Bernard’s, but they are not admitted as his in the standard critical edition; Bernard’s securely recognized hymn-making is limited to the Saint Victor and Saint Malachy material. ([New Catholic Encyclopedia, “Hymnology”](https://cvdvn.net/wp-content/uploads/2018/05/new-catholic-encyclopedia-vol-7.pdf))
