## Works by Adnan Muhammad Khashoggi

### Published text

- **1980:** *Europe Will Suffer from Oil Depletion, Not Us*. An article published in the April 1980 issue of *World Trade Magazine*. It is the only conventionally published work by Khashoggi identified in the consulted scholarly bibliography; the surviving citation does not establish whether the printed wording was entirely his or editorially mediated.[^1]

### Political papers and projects

- **Before July 1985:** Untitled Middle East “think pieces.” Policy papers that Khashoggi sent to U.S. national-security adviser Robert McFarlane; their number, dates, titles, and present completeness are not established in the public record.[^2]

- **July 1985:** Untitled paper on the political situation in Iran. A lengthy policy paper sent to McFarlane during the contacts that developed into the Iran–Contra affair. Congressional evidence expressly calls it Khashoggi’s writing, but does not supply a title or reproduce a complete bibliographical description.[^2]

- **1980s:** Middle East peace proposal and proposed *Mideast Peace Fund*. A diplomatic-economic project developed with writer Eugene Kolb, promising a fund to support a negotiated settlement and reportedly pledging $100 million of Khashoggi’s money. It remained a proposal; because the available account says Kolb rewrote the plan after White House consultation, single authorship cannot safely be assigned to Khashoggi.[^3]

- **1993–2000, exact dates unresolved:** Economic proposals for Middle East peace and an *Islamic Development Fund*. These survive as correspondence and funding proposals in Clinton-administration records. The archive describes ideas and projects rather than stable, published works, and warns that its 341-page holding is selective and partly closed for national-security reasons.[^4]

- **1990s, exact date unresolved:** Proposed children’s hospital in Gaza. A philanthropic project represented by Khashoggi’s request for the U.S. First Lady’s assistance; the Clinton Library finding aid does not establish that the hospital was built, and contemporary reporting associates the fundraising substantially with Khashoggi’s wife Shahpari, so this resists attribution as his sole obra.[^4][^5]

### Corporate obra

- **1960s or 1973:** *Triad*. Khashoggi’s multinational holding-company structure, named for Adnan and his brothers Essam and Adil, through which he assembled arms-agency, property, petroleum, banking, vehicle-distribution, mining, and agricultural interests. Sources disagree on whether Triad began in the 1960s or was formally created in 1973; moreover, a corporation is a collective legal and commercial construction, not a personally authored object.[^6][^7]

- **1970s:** *Salt Lake International Center*. A roughly 900-acre industrial and free-port development beside Salt Lake City’s airport, initiated through Khashoggi’s companies; contemporary accounts call it a successful development, although later debt and bankruptcy proceedings overtook the property.[^8][^9]

- **1981–1987:** *Triad Center*. A projected office, retail, residential, hotel, and entertainment complex in downtown Salt Lake City. One phase, including the KSL Broadcast House, appeared, but its proposed twin towers and most later phases did not: as an obra it consists jointly of buildings, plans, models, groundbreakings, corporate promises, and conspicuous non-completion.[^8][^10]

- **1985–1986:** Untitled Houston office-complex project. Triad’s proposed billion-dollar downtown development collapsed after the local property market and its principal financial partner failed. It is properly a project rather than a building: no completed architectural object resulted.[^8]

### Commissioned object

- **1978–1980:** *Nabila*. An approximately 86-metre motor yacht commissioned by Khashoggi, launched by Benetti in 1979 and delivered in 1980; he named it for his daughter. It is attributable to him as patron and commissioner, not designer: Jon Bannenberg designed its exterior and Luigi Sturchio its interior, while Benetti manufactured it.[^11][^12]
