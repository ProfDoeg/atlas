## Works by Herbert Marcuse

### Monographs and sustained philosophical obras

- **1922:** *Der deutsche Künstlerroman*. Doctoral dissertation and Marcuse’s first book-length scholarly obra; later collected with his early essays. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1925:** *Schiller-Bibliographie: unter Benutzung der Trömelschen Schiller-Bibliothek (1865)*. An annotated research bibliography rather than an argumentative book; Marcuse later expressed reservations about this youthful work. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1932:** *Hegels Ontologie und die Theorie der Geschichtlichkeit*. A proposed habilitation on Hegelian ontology and historicity. The Nazi seizure of power prevented the habilitation from proceeding, and it remains disputed whether Heidegger read the manuscript, although it was published that year. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1941:** *Reason and Revolution: An Introduction to the Dialectical Thinking of Hegel and Marx*. Marcuse’s first major English-language monograph; later editions changed the subtitle to *Hegel and the Rise of Social Theory* and added a new preface and “A Note on Dialectic.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1955:** *Eros and Civilization: A Philosophical Inquiry into Freud*. A philosophical synthesis of Freud and Marx and a speculative project for a non-repressive civilization; its 1966 edition contains a new political preface. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1958:** *Soviet Marxism: A Critical Analysis*. A critical study of Soviet ideology and society, developed partly from Marcuse’s government-sponsored research. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1964:** *One-Dimensional Man: Studies in the Ideology of Advanced Industrial Society*. A monographic critique of technological rationality, administered needs, and the containment of opposition in advanced industrial societies. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1969:** *An Essay on Liberation*. A short theoretical and political obra joining sensibility, aesthetics, anti-imperialism, and revolutionary transformation. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1972:** *Counterrevolution and Revolt*. A monograph on preventive counterrevolution and the possibilities of liberation in politics, culture, and humanity’s relation to nature. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1977:** *Die Permanenz der Kunst: Wider eine bestimmte marxistische Ästhetik*. Marcuse’s final monograph, an essay opposing reductive Marxist aesthetics and defending art’s autonomous critical force; its English version appeared in 1978 as *The Aesthetic Dimension*. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Authored collections and composite books

- **1965:** *Kultur und Gesellschaft*. A two-volume German gathering of earlier essays; it is an authorial collection, not a wholly new work. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *Philosophie und Revolution*. A composite book of three essays originally written in 1929–32. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *Das Ende der Utopie: Vorträge und Diskussionen in Berlin 1967*. A book made from Marcuse’s Berlin lectures and discussions, including “Das Problem der Gewalt in der radikalen Opposition”; it preserves an event of speech and debate rather than a conventional monograph. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1968:** *Negations: Essays in Critical Theory*. An English collection of translated and previously published essays, including Marcuse’s writings on liberalism, essence, affirmative culture, critical theory, hedonism, Weber, Norman O. Brown, and industrial aggression. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1968:** *Psychoanalyse und Politik*. A compact German collection joining writings and lectures on instinct, progress, violence, and utopia. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1969:** *Ideen zu einer kritischen Theorie der Gesellschaft*. A German collection of older works, including Marcuse’s essay on negation in dialectics. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1970:** *Five Lectures: Psychoanalysis, Politics, and Utopia*. An English lecture collection, largely translated from *Psychoanalyse und Politik*, combining written lecture texts with the record of Berlin discussions. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1970:** *Sociedad carnívora*. A Spanish collection of four existing Marcuse essays; it is an editorial manifestation of his work rather than a newly composed Spanish-language obra. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1972:** *Studies in Critical Philosophy*. An English translated collection of five earlier studies; the later title *From Luther to Popper* denotes substantially the same composite object, not a separate work. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1975:** *Zeit-Messungen: Drei Vorträge und ein Interview*. A collection of three lectures and an interview, including a revised German version of “Marxism and Feminism.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1978:** *Gespräche mit Herbert Marcuse*. A book of conversations with Marcuse and several interlocutors. Authorship is necessarily distributed: Marcuse made his spoken interventions, while the published book is also an editorial construction. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Essays, studies, manifestos, interventions, and reviews

- **1928:** *Beiträge zu einer Phänomenologie des Historischen Materialismus*. An early programmatic essay attempting to bring phenomenology and historical materialism together. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1929:** *Über konkrete Philosophie*. A philosophical essay proposing the possibility and contemporary necessity of a “concrete philosophy.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1929:** *Karl Marx, sein Leben und sein Werk* [review of Karl Vorländer]. A signed critical review, not a book by Marcuse. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1929:** *Zur Wahrheitsproblematik der soziologischen Methode: Karl Mannheim, Die Ideologie und Utopie*. A substantial review-essay on Mannheim and sociological truth. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1930:** *Zum Problem der Dialektik I*. The first part of a two-part inquiry into dialectics. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1930:** *Transzendentaler Marxismus?* A critical philosophical essay framed as a question about transcendental Marxism. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1930:** *Geschichte und Systeme der Philosophie* [review of Hermann Noack]. A review rather than an independent treatise. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1931:** *Das Problem der geschichtlichen Wirklichkeit: Wilhelm Dilthey*. A study of Dilthey and historical reality. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1931:** *Zur Kritik der Soziologie*. A critical essay on sociology. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1931:** *Zum Problem der Dialektik II*. The second part of Marcuse’s inquiry into dialectics. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1931:** *Zur Auseinandersetzung mit Hans Freyers Soziologie als Wirklichkeitswissenschaft*. A critical engagement with Hans Freyer’s conception of sociology. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1932:** *Die Errungenschaften des deutschen Idealismus* [review of Heinz Heimsoeth]. A review published in the *Deutsche Literaturzeitung*. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1932:** *Neue Quellen zur Grundlegung des Historischen Materialismus*. A major essay responding to the newly available early Marx manuscripts and rethinking the foundations of historical materialism. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1933:** *Über die philosophischen Grundlagen des wirtschaftswissenschaftlichen Arbeitsbegriffs*. A philosophical study of the economic concept of labor. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1933:** *Philosophie des Scheiterns: Karl Jaspers’ Werk*. A newspaper essay on Jaspers and the philosophy of failure. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1933:** *Goethe und der deutsche Idealismus* [review of Johannes Hoffmeister]. A scholarly review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1934:** *Der Kampf gegen den Liberalismus in der totalitären Staatsauffassung*. An analysis of the totalitarian attack on liberalism, later repeatedly collected and translated. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1934:** *Das Verhältnis des jungen Hegel zu Kant* [review of Herbert Wacker]. A brief scholarly review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1936:** *Zum Begriff des Wesens*. A philosophical essay on the concept of essence, later translated as “The Concept of Essence.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1936:** *Studie über Autorität und Familie*. Marcuse’s large contribution to the Institute for Social Research’s collective study, comprising an intellectual-historical section and a section on authority and family in German sociology. Its status is both an individual obra and one component of a collaborative research project. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1937:** *Philosophie und kritische Theorie*. A programmatic statement of critical theory and historically situated reason. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1937:** *Über den affirmativen Charakter der Kultur*. A critical study of “affirmative culture.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1938:** *Zur Kritik des Hedonismus*. An essay reconstructing and criticizing hedonism within critical social theory. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1940:** *An Introduction to Hegel’s Philosophy*. An English-language essay anticipating parts of *Reason and Revolution*. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1941:** *Some Social Implications of Modern Technology*. A critical-theoretical study of technological rationality. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1941:** *Marxism: Is It Science?* [review of Max Eastman]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1942:** *A Rejoinder to Karl Löwith’s Review of Reason and Revolution*. A short polemical reply rather than an autonomous treatise. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1942:** *From Luther to Hitler* [review of William Montgomery McGovern]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1942:** *Von Hegel bis Nietzsche* [review of Karl Löwith]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1943:** *The Destiny of Western Man* [review of W. T. Stace]. A substantial review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1948:** *Existentialism: Remarks on Jean-Paul Sartre’s L’Être et le néant*. An extended critical essay on Sartre, later included in Marcuse collections. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1949:** *Essays on Freedom and Power* [review of Lord Acton]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1950:** *Die Wiederkunft des Dionysos* [review of J. H. W. Rosteutscher]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1950:** *Francis Bacon: Philosopher of Industrial Science* [review of Benjamin Farrington]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1950:** *Goethe und seine Zeit* [review of Georg Lukács]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1951:** *Anti-Democratic Popular Movements*. A short political analysis of postwar Germany. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1951:** *Erasmus and Our Struggle for Peace* [review of José Chapiro]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1951:** *War and Human Progress* [review of John U. Nef]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1952:** *The Anatomy of Communism* [review of Andrew M. Scott]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1953:** *Pioneers of Russian Social Thought* [review of Richard Hare]. A book review. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1954:** *Recent Literature on Communism*. A multi-book review essay; the bibliography dates the underlying work to 1953 in places, but the journal issue is July 1954. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1955:** *Dialectic and Logic Since the War*. An essay on Soviet and Russian thought. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1955:** *Trieblehre und Freiheit*. An essay on instinct theory and freedom. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1955:** *The Social Implications of Freudian “Revisionism”*. A polemical essay criticizing neo-Freudian revisionism. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1955:** *Eros and Culture*. A short essay closely related to the project of *Eros and Civilization*. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1956:** *A Reply to Erich Fromm*. A brief polemical continuation of the debate over Freudian revisionism. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1956:** *La théorie des instincts et la socialisation*. A French-language essay on instinct theory and socialization. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1957:** *The Indictment of Western Philosophy in Freud’s Theory*. A short philosophical intervention. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1957:** *Theory and Therapy in Freud*. A review-essay published in *The Nation*. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1957:** *Critical Remarks on Neo-Freudian Revisionism*. A further intervention in Marcuse’s dispute with revisionist psychoanalysis. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1959:** *Soviet Theory and Practice*. A brief political intervention. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1959:** *The Ideology of Death*. An essay contributed to *The Meaning of Death* and later collected under slightly varying titles. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1960:** *De l’ontologie à la technologie: les tendances de la société industrielle*. A French essay tracing a movement from ontology to technological society. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1960:** *A Note on Dialectic*. A newly written preface-section for the revised *Reason and Revolution*; separable as an essay but materially part of that edition. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1961:** *Language and Technological Society*. An essay on language under technological rationality. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1962:** *Idéologie et société industrielle avancée*. A French essay on ideology in advanced industrial society. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1963:** *Dynamismes de la société industrielle*. A French-language analysis of industrial society. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1963:** *Notes on Philosophy*. A short statement published in the Brandeis University yearbook. [Publications index](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/publications/index-full.html)

- **1963:** *Zur Stellung des Denkens heute*. A contribution to a volume honoring Theodor W. Adorno. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1964:** *World Without a Logos*. A short essay on science, abstraction, and social domination. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1965:** *Repressive Tolerance*. A manifesto-like philosophical intervention: it argues not merely about tolerance but for a discriminatory, “liberating” practice of tolerance. It first appeared within the collaborative book *A Critique of Pure Tolerance*. [Publications index](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/publications/index-full.html)

- **1965:** *Remarks on a Redefinition of Culture*. An essay proposing a politically consequential redefinition of culture. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1965:** *Perspektiven des Sozialismus in der entwickelten Industriegesellschaft*. An essay also published in English as “Socialism in the Developed Countries.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1965:** *A Tribute to Paul A. Baran*. A memorial text. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1965:** *Der Einfluss der deutschen Emigration auf das amerikanische Geistesleben: Philosophie und Soziologie*. An essay on German émigré intellectual influence in the United States. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1977:** *Mord darf keine Waffe der Politik sein*. A newspaper intervention declaring that murder must not become a political weapon. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1978–1979:** *Protosozialismus und Spätkapitalismus: Versuch einer revolutionstheoretischen Synthese von Bahros Ansatz*. An extended response to Rudolf Bahro. Sources variously date the journal issue 1978 or 1979, so the discrepancy should be retained. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1979:** *Die Angst des Prometheus*. A newspaper text published shortly after Marcuse’s death. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1979:** *Die Ideologie des Todes*. A German manifestation of Marcuse’s earlier essay on the ideology of death. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1979:** *The Reification of the Proletariat*. A short theoretical essay published during Marcuse’s final year. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Lectures, interviews, discussions, and recorded manifestations

- **1956:** *Aggression in Industrial Society*. A presentation to the Chicago Psychiatric Society; it remained primarily an oral event until revised publication in 1967–68. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1962:** *Emanzipation der Frau in der repressiven Gesellschaft*. A published conversation with Peter Furth, not a single-author essay. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1963:** *The Obsolescence of the Freudian Concept of Man*. A lecture to the American Political Science Association, later published in *Five Lectures*. A separate pamphlet titled *The Obsolescence of Psychoanalysis* is tentatively dated 1969 by the bibliography and may be another manifestation of this lecture. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1964:** *Ethics and Revolution*. A University of Kansas lecture subsequently rewritten and published; the spoken event and printed essay are manifestations of one developing text. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1966:** *The Rationality of Philosophy*. A San Diego lecture first published posthumously in 2017. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *Liberation from the Affluent Society*. A lecture at the Dialectics of Liberation congress, preserved both as text and sound recording. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *The End of Utopia*. A Berlin lecture and discussion whose dialogic publication resists being reduced to a self-contained essay. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *The Problem of Violence and the Radical Opposition*. A Berlin lecture and discussion later published in German and English collections. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *Protest and Futility*. A Berkeley lecture first published posthumously in 2017. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1969:** *Herbert Marcuse Speech in Sproul Plaza*. A recorded Berkeley speech concerning Angela Davis, the university, and students’ social role; a transcript also survives. As a recorded event, it is not merely a text. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1970–1971:** *Aggression: Das dritte Gespräch*. An interview with Friedrich Hacker, recorded in December 1970 and published in 1971. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1970s, date uncertain:** *Apocalypse or Apocrypha? Herbert Marcuse, His Prescription for the Next World*. A commercially issued 48-minute sound cassette. The catalogue record supplies only “[197-],” so no exact year should be invented. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1974:** *Paris Lectures at Vincennes University*. Seven lecture manuscripts on capitalism and radical opposition, discovered in the Frankfurt archive in 2014 and edited for publication in 2015. The 2015 book is an editorial creation; Marcuse’s works are the 1974 lectures. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1976:** *Art and the Transvaluation of Values*. A Toronto lecture first published posthumously in 2017. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1976:** *The University and Radical Social Change*. A Kent State lecture first published posthumously in 2017; the 2017 table of contents misspells “Radical” as “Raidical.” [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1977:** *The Radical Transformation of Norms, Needs and Values*. A Saint Louis lecture first published posthumously in 2017. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1977–1979:** *Theory and Politics: A Discussion*. A discussion with Jürgen Habermas, Tilman Spengler, and Heinz Lubasz, held in 1977 and published in 1978–79; it is a jointly made conversational object. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1978:** *On The Aesthetic Dimension: A Conversation with Herbert Marcuse*. An interview conducted in 1978 but published in 1981; the published form is jointly made by Marcuse and interviewer Larry Hartwick. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1979:** *Ecology and the Critique of Modern Society*. Marcuse’s final lecture, delivered shortly before his death and published in 1992. Its status is an oral intervention reconstructed as a posthumous text. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Government research and unfinished collective projects

- **1942:** *State and Individual under National Socialism*. A manuscript intended for an Institute for Social Research collection that was never published as planned; the essay itself survived and appeared posthumously. It is both a finished study and a fragment of an unrealized collective project. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1942–1943:** *A History of the Doctrine of Social Change*. A study made with Franz Neumann. Its collaborative authorship and archival publication prevent its treatment as Marcuse’s sole obra. [Collected Papers](https://www.routledge.com/Herbert-Marcuse-Collected-Papers/book-series/HMCP)

- **1942–1943:** *A Theory of Social Change*. A collaborative study with Franz Neumann, preserved among Marcuse’s papers rather than completed as the projected book. [Collected Papers](https://www.routledge.com/Herbert-Marcuse-Collected-Papers/book-series/HMCP)

- **1943:** *Note sulla corretta propaganda di guerra verso i lavoratori tedeschi* / memorandum on propaganda toward German workers. A wartime government memorandum whose commonly cited Italian title is a later translation, not necessarily Marcuse’s original title. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1943:** *Sulla neutralità psicologica* / memorandum on psychological neutrality. Another wartime memorandum known through later editorial publication and translation; its exact original title is not securely represented by the translated citation. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1943–1945:** Wartime reports on Nazi Germany. Marcuse helped produce classified analyses for United States agencies, later selected in *Secret Reports on Nazi Germany*. The corpus resists entry-by-entry attribution because many reports emerged from an institutional and sometimes collaborative research process involving Franz Neumann, Otto Kirchheimer, and others. [Princeton University Press](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691134130/secret-reports-on-nazi-germany)

- **1940s:** *The New German Mentality*. An extensive wartime research project with supplemental memoranda, preserved in Marcuse’s papers; it is an institutional diagnostic project rather than an ordinary published essay. [Collected Papers, volume 1](https://www.routledge.com/Technology-War-and-Fascism-Collected-Papers-of-Herbert-Marcuse-Volume-1/Kellner-Marcuse/p/book/9780415137836)

- **1940s:** *Description of Three Major Projects*. An archival planning document describing projected research. The projects’ status as intentions rather than completed obras is essential to their classification. [Collected Papers, volume 1](https://www.routledge.com/Technology-War-and-Fascism-Collected-Papers-of-Herbert-Marcuse-Volume-1/Kellner-Marcuse/p/book/9780415137836)

### Editorial and bibliographical making

- **1924–1925:** *Das Dreieck: Monatsschrift für Philosophie, Dichtung und Kritik*. A periodical edited by Marcuse with Walter Gutkelch from April 1924 through March 1925. The magazine is a collaborative editorial obra, not a Marcuse-authored book. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1957:** *The Democratic and the Authoritarian State: Essays in Political and Legal Theory*, by Franz Neumann. Marcuse selected and edited Neumann’s posthumous essays and supplied a preface; the underlying essays are Neumann’s, while the arrangement and prefatory apparatus are Marcuse’s editorial work. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1967:** *Faschismus und Kapitalismus: Theorien über die sozialen Ursprünge und die Funktion des Faschismus*. A multi-author anthology containing Marcuse’s work; the whole book should not be attributed to him, despite bibliographies sometimes listing it among his publications. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1972:** *The Defense of Gracchus Babeuf Before the High Court of Vendôme*. John Anthony Scott edited and translated Babeuf; Marcuse supplied an essay and Thomas Cornell supplied illustrations. It is a collaborative book-object, not a Marcuse monograph. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1973:** *Existentialistische Marx-Interpretation*. A book pairing work by Marcuse and Alfred Schmidt. Its composite authorship should not be collapsed into sole authorship by Marcuse. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1979:** *Studentenbewegung: Und was danach?* A multi-author pamphlet or study booklet containing Marcuse’s work; the complete object is collaborative. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Correspondence as a dispersed obra

- **1930:** *Brief an Ernst Manheim*. A private letter later published in a 2000 exhibition catalogue; it became a public text only through posthumous editorial selection. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1946:** Letter to Max Horkheimer of October 1946. A surviving piece of correspondence; the bibliography notes uncertainty over whether the published German text is original or translated. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1947–1948:** Marcuse–Heidegger correspondence. An exchange confronting Heidegger’s political responsibility after Nazism; it is a jointly made sequence of letters rather than a single Marcuse text. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1954–1979:** Marcuse–Raya Dunayevskaya correspondence. A long philosophical and political dialogue preserved in archives and later published in selections and an edited volume. The correspondence is a serial, jointly made body rather than a finished book conceived by Marcuse. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1962–1965:** *Streit über John F. Kennedy*. A private exchange between Marcuse and Paul Tillich, posthumously edited and published in 2007. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1969:** Marcuse–Adorno correspondence on the German student movement. Letters exchanged during 1969 and published together in 1999; the resulting sequence is dialogic and editorially constituted. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **1971–1973:** Letters to the Chicago Surrealists. A correspondence subsequently selected and published by Franklin Rosemont; it remains a dispersed exchange rather than a work Marcuse prepared as a book. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Unfinished and posthumously manifested texts

- **Late 1960s–early 1970s:** *Beyond One-Dimensional Man*. An unpublished manuscript later printed in the *Collected Papers*. Its title identifies a continuing project beyond the earlier book, but the surviving object was not brought to publication by Marcuse. [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Towards-a-Critical-Theory-of-Society-Collected-Papers-of-Herbert-Marcuse-Volume-2/Kellner-Marcuse/p/book/9781138006928)

- **Late 1960s–early 1970s:** *Cultural Revolution*. An unpublished manuscript posthumously edited in the *Collected Papers*; its degree of completion is not specified by the publisher. [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Towards-a-Critical-Theory-of-Society-Collected-Papers-of-Herbert-Marcuse-Volume-2/Kellner-Marcuse/p/book/9781138006928)

- **Late 1960s–early 1970s:** *The Historical Fate of Bourgeois Democracy*. An unpublished manuscript later made public by editors. It survives as a posthumous manifestation, not a publication authorized in final form by Marcuse. [Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/Towards-a-Critical-Theory-of-Society-Collected-Papers-of-Herbert-Marcuse-Volume-2/Kellner-Marcuse/p/book/9781138006928)

- **1978:** *Lyric Poetry after Auschwitz*. An unfinished, untitled-in-final-form archival draft consisting of English and German pages, some fragmentary. Editors supplied or stabilized the English and German titles; its origin, intended destination, and reason for switching languages remain unknown, so it resists being called a completed work. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **Date not established; published 2006:** *Proust*. A posthumously published archival text. The accessible bibliographic record fixes the publication year but not the date of composition, so no composition date should be invented. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

- **Date not established; published 2014:** *Baran’s Critique of Modern Society and of the Social Sciences*. A posthumously published text whose composition date is not supplied in the standard online bibliography. [Bibliography](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)

### Rejected attribution

- **1970:** *Riot and Representation: The Significance of the Chicano Riot*. This text was published under Marcuse’s name, but Marcuse publicly denied writing it. It therefore belongs in the record as a rejected attribution, not as one of his obras. [Bibliography and disavowal](https://www.marcuse.org/herbert/pubs.htm)
