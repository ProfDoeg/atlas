## Works by Joseph Süß Oppenheimer

### Memoranda, correspondence, and documentary obra

- **1724–c. 1725:** *Reports, petitions, and memorandum concerning Oppenheimer’s Mannheim commercial losses*. This untitled dossier culminated in a formally bound memorandum with supporting papers submitted to the Elector Palatine; it is an administrative-commercial obra rather than a published book. The surviving reports include a letter dated 8 August 1724, but the memorandum itself is not securely dated or separately titled. ([Mannheimer Geschichtsblätter](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/files/22202/BLB_MA_Geschichtsblaetter_1929_30.pdf))

- **By 13 October 1732:** *Kontobuch aus dem Mannheimer Büro*. A working account book, not a literary text: Oppenheimer personally marked it closed on that date, while the extent to which clerks produced its other entries is not specified. ([Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, A 48/14 Bü. 116](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495))

- **1733, 1735–1736:** *Entwürfe von Antwortschreiben an Geheimrat von Zang*. Eight surviving draft replies formed part of an administrative correspondence rather than a titled work; the archival inventory attributes the drafts to Oppenheimer but does not enumerate their individual dates or titles. ([Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, A 48/14 Bü. 121 I](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495))

- **1734:** *Manual über die Aktiv- und Passiv-Wechselbriefe*. A commercial register recording bills receivable and payable. It resists classification as a wholly autograph work: it belonged to Oppenheimer’s Frankfurt business archive, but the catalogue description does not establish whether he or his clerks wrote every entry. ([Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, A 48/14 Bü. 118, Bl. 126](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495))

- **Before March 1737:** *Haushaltungsrechnungen und Notizen*. An untitled working archive of 490 household accounts and notes in four fascicles, preserved among the confiscated “Süßische Scripturen.” This is a body of records, not a single authored text, and individual scribal responsibility remains unseparated. ([Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, A 48/14 Bü. 121 I](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495))

- **Before March 1737:** *Wechselbriefe*. Numerous surviving signed bills of exchange are financial instruments—texts that perform transactions—rather than descriptive writings. Oppenheimer’s signatures establish his authorization, not necessarily his penmanship or sole composition. ([Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, A 48/14 Bü. 118 II, Bl. 611](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495))

- **1734–1737:** *Gutachten und Einzelprojektvorschläge zur württembergischen Wirtschafts- und Finanzpolitik*. This is an untitled, dispersed corpus of expert opinions and project proposals rather than one work. Joachim Brüser confirms that Oppenheimer repeatedly submitted such papers, but warns that many neighboring proposals were written by others and that the overall reform program belonged principally to Duke Karl Alexander; a complete item-by-item attribution has therefore not been established. ([Brüser, *Herzog Karl Alexander von Württemberg und die Landschaft*, pp. 297–299](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/files/24515/BLB_Brueser_Herzog_Karl_Alexander.pdf))

- **1737:** *Answers in the inquisitorial examinations*. Oppenheimer’s answers survive inside commission-produced interrogation protocols, including the first examination of 28 March and later examinations beginning 28 May, 4 June, and August–September. These are manifestations of compelled speech recorded and shaped by officials, not texts freely composed by Oppenheimer; his signature authenticates at least the first protocol without making him its sole maker. ([Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg, A 48/14 Bü. 2](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495); [University Library Tübingen, Mh 468](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/Mh468))

- **1737–1738:** *Prison letters and two Kassiber*. A manuscript corpus occupying fols. 1r–61v of the Tübingen trial miscellany, including two clandestine notes. The repository groups letters by and concerning Oppenheimer together, so the catalogue alone does not support attributing every leaf to him; this is an incompletely disentangled correspondence corpus, not a fixed work. ([University Library Tübingen, Mh 468](https://opendigi.ub.uni-tuebingen.de/opendigi/Mh468))

### Financial and administrative projects

- **1733:** *Hessen-Darmstadt mint undertaking*. A commercial project in which Oppenheimer took over the landgrave’s mint lease and organized outside financing through Frankfurt bankers. Coins produced under the arrangement are manifestations of the enterprise, not individually authored artworks, and the surviving account emphasizes Oppenheimer’s coordination rather than solitary creation. ([Jüdische Geschichte, “Joseph Suess”](https://www.juedischegeschichte.de/html/joseph_suess.html))

- **1734–1737:** *Württemberg mint administration*. Oppenheimer undertook management of the ducal mint under the agreement of 5 June 1734, guaranteeing increased revenue and arranging precious-metal supplies. It is best classified as an operating project whose products were coinage and revenue, although older accounts that make him the architect of the duke’s entire financial policy are rejected by recent scholarship. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11740-oppenheimer-joseph-suss); [Brüser, pp. 297–299](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/files/24515/BLB_Brueser_Herzog_Karl_Alexander.pdf))

- **1734–1737:** *Court-supply and credit network*. Oppenheimer built and operated a chain that procured army provisions, credit, precious metals, jewels, and luxury goods for Duke Karl Alexander. This was a continuing commercial obra rather than a single object; individual transactions were collaborative and frequently financed through third parties. ([Stadtlexikon Stuttgart, “Joseph Süß Oppenheimer”](https://www.stadtlexikon-stuttgart.de/article/aedd5996-c81e-4a67-bd21-3a51cb9904ef/1/Joseph_Suess_Oppenheimer.html); [Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg](https://www.landesarchiv-bw.de/de/themen/praesentationen---themenzugaenge/72495))

- **1735:** *Playing-card printing monopoly*. A revenue project granting Moses Drach and associates the printing monopoly for a fixed annual payment. It is traditionally associated with Oppenheimer’s financial administration, but the privilege was a ducal act and the available source does not prove that Oppenheimer alone devised it. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11740-oppenheimer-joseph-suss))

- **1736–1737:** *Gratialamt*. An administrative mechanism that converted grants of titles and offices into payments to the ducal privy purse. Oppenheimer supervised the newly created office, but it resists attribution as his individual “work”: scholarship places the wider program under Karl Alexander and distinguishes Oppenheimer’s advice and implementation from sovereign authorship. ([Stadtlexikon Stuttgart](https://www.stadtlexikon-stuttgart.de/article/aedd5996-c81e-4a67-bd21-3a51cb9904ef/1/Joseph_Suess_Oppenheimer.html); [Brüser, pp. 297–299](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/files/24515/BLB_Brueser_Herzog_Karl_Alexander.pdf))

- **1736–1737:** *Fiskalatamt*. An administrative and fiscal mechanism allowing accused persons to avert prosecution by paying money into the ducal treasury. It was placed under Oppenheimer, but calling it his creation would silently resolve a disputed attribution: the duke established the institution, while Oppenheimer administered and may have advised on it. ([Stadtlexikon Stuttgart](https://www.stadtlexikon-stuttgart.de/article/aedd5996-c81e-4a67-bd21-3a51cb9904ef/1/Joseph_Suess_Oppenheimer.html); [Brüser, pp. 297–299](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/files/24515/BLB_Brueser_Herzog_Karl_Alexander.pdf))

- **1736–1737:** *Taxes, monopolies, and related revenue schemes*. The surviving record associates Oppenheimer’s administration with stamped-paper and indirect taxes, salary deductions, a lottery, and monopolies involving coffeehouses, chimney-sweeping, tobacco, salt, wine, spices, hides, and leather. These measures form a disputed collective project rather than a catalogue of securely authored works: newer archival scholarship finds that many proposals came from other advisers and that Karl Alexander’s comparable pre-Oppenheimer policies establish the duke as author of the overall program. ([Jewish Encyclopedia](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/11740-oppenheimer-joseph-suss); [Brüser, pp. 297–299](https://regionalia.blb-karlsruhe.de/files/24515/BLB_Brueser_Herzog_Karl_Alexander.pdf))
