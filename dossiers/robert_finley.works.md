## Works by Robert Finley

### Published sermons and theological discourses

- **1807:** *A Sermon on the Baptism of John, Shewing It to Be a Peculiar Dispensation, and No Example for Christians*. A printed doctrinal sermon. Sprague’s bibliographical notice lists it among Finley’s publications, while Brown’s near-contemporary memoir calls Finley’s published writings a complete list. ([Sprague](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Annals_of_the_American_Pulpit_Volume_4.pdf); [Brown](https://archive.org/details/memoirsofrevrobe00brow))

- **1807:** *A Sermon on the Victory of Christ over Death: Preached at the Funeral of the Rev. William Boyd, of Lamington, on May the Seventeenth, A.D. Eighteen Hundred and Seven*. A separately printed funeral sermon: both memorial discourse and theological argument. ([Brown](https://archive.org/details/memoirsofrevrobe00brow); [Log College Press](https://www.logcollegepress.com/robert-finley-17721817/))

- **1808:** *A Discourse on the Nature and Design, the Benefits and Proper Subjects of Baptism*. A separately printed theological discourse defending Presbyterian baptismal doctrine; contemporary bibliographies call it either a sermon or a discourse. ([Sprague](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Annals_of_the_American_Pulpit_Volume_4.pdf); [Princeton University Library’s Nelson Collection catalogue](https://static-prod.lib.princeton.edu/sc/aids/1920_Nelson_Collection_New_Jerseyana.pdf))

- **1813:** *The Disciples of Christ, the Light of the World*. A sermon on Matthew 5:14, contributed at the editors’ request to the serial collection *The New-Jersey Preacher*; it is therefore a component text rather than a freestanding book. ([Brown](https://archive.org/details/memoirsofrevrobe00brow); [Confessional Presbyterian](https://confessional.org/authors/robert-finley))

- **1813:** *The Benefits Resulting from Being Found in Christ*. A sermon on Philippians 3:9, likewise published as a component of *The New-Jersey Preacher*. ([Brown](https://archive.org/details/memoirsofrevrobe00brow); [Confessional Presbyterian](https://confessional.org/authors/robert-finley))

### Programmatic pamphlet

- **1816:** *Thoughts on the Colonization of Free Blacks*. A programmatic pamphlet advocating African colonization. It approaches a manifesto because it was meant to recruit support and bring an organization into being, not merely to describe an idea; its racial assumptions and proposed removal of free Black Americans made that project contested from its inception. ([Sprague](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Annals_of_the_American_Pulpit_Volume_4.pdf); [Princeton & Slavery Project](https://slavery.princeton.edu/stories/princeton-and-the-colonization-movement); [Samantha Seeley, “Beyond the American Colonization Society”](https://doi.org/10.1111/hic3.12302))

### Manuscripts

- **1793:** *Untitled Latin manuscript*. A surviving manuscript identified only by language and date in the Presbyterian Historical Society finding aid; its supplied archival description is not an authorial title. ([Presbyterian Historical Society, Robert Finley Papers, RG 368](https://pcusa.org/historical-society/collections/research-tools/guides-archival-collections/rg-368))

- **1796, 1799, and 1803:** *Four untitled manuscript sermons*. Four surviving sermons housed together in Finley’s papers. The finding aid supplies three years for four objects but does not publicly assign a year or title to each manuscript, so a more exact itemization would invent information. ([Presbyterian Historical Society, Robert Finley Papers, RG 368](https://pcusa.org/historical-society/collections/research-tools/guides-archival-collections/rg-368))

### Educational protocols and institutions

- **1795–1798:** Basking Ridge Classical School. An educational institution and long-running pedagogical project established and conducted by Finley, not a text or solely a building; sources differ on whether its beginning should be dated 1795, 1797, or more cautiously within 1795–1798. ([National Register summary for the Brick Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_Academy); [Historical Society marker record](https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=18305); [Brown](https://archive.org/details/memoirsofrevrobe00brow))

- **1809:** Brick Academy. A Federal-style brick schoolhouse made to accommodate Finley’s expanding classical school. Finley organized or commissioned its construction rather than personally designing or laying the building, so it resists attribution as his solitary architectural obra. ([Visit Somerset County](https://visitsomersetnj.org/fun-somerset-nj/brick-academy/); [Historical Society of the Somerset Hills](https://somersethillshistoricalsociety.org/news_items/thssh-submits-grant-to-purchase-rickey-property/?print=print))

- **1815–1816:** Plan for systematic biblical instruction of young people. A pedagogical protocol originated jointly by Finley and George S. Woodhull, circulated through Presbyterian churches in 1815 and recommended by the General Assembly in 1816. No stable authorial title is established in the cited account, and sole authorship should not be assigned to Finley. ([Brown](https://archive.org/details/memoirsofrevrobe00brow))

### Institutional project

- **1815–1817:** American Society for Colonizing the Free People of Colour of the United States, later the American Colonization Society. A collaborative institutional project that Finley helped turn from correspondence and advocacy into an organization at Washington in December 1816. Older accounts call him its “author” or “father,” but scholarship distributes its making among Finley, Samuel J. Mills, Charles Fenton Mercer, Elias B. Caldwell, Francis Scott Key, and others; the institution therefore resists treatment as Finley’s solitary work. ([Henry Noble Sherwood, *The American Colonization Society, 1817–1840*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/e7/The_American_Colonization_Society%2C_1817-1840_%28IA_colonizationsoc00foxerich%29.pdf); [Princeton & Slavery Project](https://slavery.princeton.edu/stories/princeton-and-the-colonization-movement); [Seeley](https://doi.org/10.1111/hic3.12302))

### Delivered but not established as surviving works

- **1817:** Baccalaureate discourse at Franklin College. An address delivered immediately before Finley assumed the college presidency and reported as having been favorably received. No fixed title, printed edition, or surviving manuscript is identified in the standard bibliographical notice, so it is an attested performance rather than a recoverable text. ([Sprague](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/98/Annals_of_the_American_Pulpit_Volume_4.pdf))
