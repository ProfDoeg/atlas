## Works by Aishah bint Abi Bakr

### Commissioned manuscript

- **After c. 650; exact date unknown:** *Personal Qurʾān codex*. A manuscript commissioned from her client Abū Yūnus, with at least one reading dictated by ʿĀʾisha herself. It is lost and survives only through reports; scholars caution that it was probably a personal copy of the ʿUthmānic text, not an independently compiled recension ([Anthony and Bronson, pp. 116–17](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/Library/Antony%2C%20S%20and%20Bronson%2C%20C%20-%20Did%20Hafsa%20edit%20the%20Qur%27an.pdf); [Abbott, pp. 204–05](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).

### Transmitted intellectual corpus

- **632–678:** *Hadith corpus attributed to ʿĀʾisha*. A large, untitled body of oral reports about Muḥammad, ritual, domestic life, law, medicine, history, and her own experiences, subsequently dispersed among independently compiled hadith collections and later gathered into musnads. It resists designation as a single authored work: the wording, divisions, and chains belong to a multigenerational transmission process, and the authenticity of individual reports remains a matter of hadith and historical criticism ([Geissinger](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1749-8171.2010.00260.x); [critical edition of her traditions in the *Musnad* of Isḥāq b. Rāhawayh](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/d3c2aa5a-28e3-48ae-8db0-d9fac4336034)).

- **632–678:** *Exegetical traditions attributed to ʿĀʾisha*. An untitled oral corpus of Qurʾānic readings, explanations, occasions of revelation, and arguments about interpretation. These are manifestations of teaching rather than a commentary she wrote; their textual forms occur only in later hadith and tafsīr compilations, sometimes with materially different chains or wording ([Geissinger, “The Exegetical Traditions of ʿĀʾisha”](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315287803_The_Exegetical_Traditions_of_c_A_c_isha_Notes_on_their_Impact_and_Significance); [Geissinger, *Gender and Muslim Constructions of Exegetical Authority*](https://books.google.com/books?id=7lPFCQAAQBAJ)).

- **632–678:** *Legal opinions and corrections attributed to ʿĀʾisha*. An untitled body of oral responsa, rulings, and criticisms of other Companions’ reports, later collected most systematically by al-Zarkashī in *al-Ijāba li-īrād mā istadrakathu ʿĀʾisha ʿalā al-ṣaḥāba*. It is a juridical and critical practice rather than a book made by ʿĀʾisha, and modern analysis finds that not every item in the medieval collection is necessarily a genuine correction by her ([al-Zarkashī, *al-Ijāba*, contents and edition](https://khzanah.com/books/12703); [Rehman, critical study](https://etheses.whiterose.ac.uk/26060/1/Sofia%20Rehman%20PhD.pdf)).

### Public oratory and political manifestos

- **656:** *Untitled address at the Ḥijr in Mecca*. A public speech attributed to ʿĀʾisha after the killing of the caliph ʿUthmān, defending him and calling for punishment of his killers. It functioned as a manifesto by recruiting adherents to political action; its surviving wording is a later historiographical transmission, not a contemporary transcript ([Abbott, pp. 130–32](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf); [al-Ṭabarī, *History*, vol. XVI](https://sunypress.edu/Books/T/The-History-of-al-abari-Vol.-162)).

- **656:** *Untitled address to the people of Basra*. An oration attributed to ʿĀʾisha during the occupation of Basra, presenting the expedition as a demand for justice for ʿUthmān and attempting to legitimate the coalition’s authority. Its versions belong to partisan accounts of the first civil war and cannot be treated as a verbatim authorial text ([al-Ṭabarī, *History*, vol. XVI](https://sunypress.edu/Books/T/The-History-of-al-abari-Vol.-162); [Abbott, pp. 151–57](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).

- **656:** *Battlefield exhortations and prayer at the Battle of the Camel*. Short oral interventions attributed to ʿĀʾisha—commands, appeals for endurance, and an imprecation against ʿUthmān’s killers—which acted directly upon the battle and were repeated by her supporters. They resist classification as stable “works”: their reported wording varies and is inseparable from later narratives of the conflict ([Abbott, pp. 158–60](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf); [al-Ṭabarī, *History*, vol. XVI](https://sunypress.edu/Books/T/The-History-of-al-abari-Vol.-162)).

- **661; attribution disputed:** *Untitled funeral encomium for ʿAlī*. A public memorial speech allegedly delivered at Muḥammad’s tomb, enumerating ʿAlī’s virtues. Abbott judges its phrasing marked by later Shiʿi enthusiasm while allowing that it may preserve the memory of a genuine public demonstration; the transmitted text therefore cannot securely be treated as ʿĀʾisha’s composition ([Abbott, pp. 182–83](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).

### Letters and epistolary protocols

- **656:** *Letter to Zayd ibn Ṣūḥān*. A political summons asking the Kufan notable to assist her undertaking or, failing that, to deter others from supporting ʿAlī. It survives only inside later historical narratives, which preserve both the letter and Zayd’s refusal ([al-Ṭabarī, *History*, vol. XVI](https://sunypress.edu/Books/T/The-History-of-al-abari-Vol.-162); [Abbott, p. 153](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).

- **656:** *General letter to the Kufans*. A circular political appeal urging the Kufans to remain at home and act only against ʿUthmān’s killers. It accompanied the personal letter to Zayd ibn Ṣūḥān and survives as reported correspondence rather than as a manuscript ([Abbott, pp. 153–54](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf); [al-Ṭabarī, *History*, vol. XVI](https://sunypress.edu/Books/T/The-History-of-al-abari-Vol.-162)).

- **656:** *Letter to Ḥafṣa bint ʿUmar*. A dispatch from Basra announcing ʿAlī’s position at Dhū Qār and expressing confidence in his defeat. It is transmitted by accounts of the Battle of the Camel, but its exact wording and authenticity remain dependent on those later, politically charged sources ([al-Mufīd, *The Battle of the Camel*, pp. 219–20](https://alkarbala.org/team/uploads/al-Mufid%20-%20The%20Battle%20of%20Camel%20-%20al%20Jamal%20wa_l%20Nusrah%20li-Sayyid%20al-Itrah%20fi%20Harb%20al-Basrah-Ansariyan%20Publications.pdf)).

- **656:** *Letters to Medina, Syria, and al-Yamāma*. A group of political circulars sent after the seizure of Basra to solicit support for ʿĀʾisha’s coalition. The record identifies their destinations and purpose but does not preserve a secure, separately recoverable text for each; they are therefore a documented epistolary project rather than extant works ([Abbott, pp. 156–57](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).

- **c. 664 or later:** *Letter on behalf of Murra to Ziyād ibn Abī Sufyān*. An intercessory letter whose politically consequential address recognized Ziyād’s Umayyad filiation; the body of the request is not securely preserved. The physical letter is lost, and even its date must remain approximate ([Abbott, pp. 189–90](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).

- **661–678; exact date unknown:** *Warning to Muʿāwiya*. A brief admonitory letter stating that one who incurs divine displeasure to satisfy people will ultimately be reproached by them. Multiple transmission settings associate this counsel with ʿĀʾisha, but the occasion and precise textual history are uncertain ([Abbott, p. 190](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf); [al-Tirmidhī transmission and authentication history](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/hadithanswers/119820/)).

### Final protocol

- **678:** *Funeral directions*. An oral protocol instructing that she not be buried in her chamber beside Muḥammad but with the other wives in al-Baqīʿ. Variant reports assign different explanations to the instruction, so the directive is firmer than the motives later attached to it ([Abbott, pp. 215–16](https://isac.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/aishah.pdf)).
