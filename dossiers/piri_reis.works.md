## Works by Muhiddin Piri

### Cartographic obra

- **1513:** The world map conventionally called the *Piri Reis Map*. This is a portolan-style manuscript world map compiled at Gallipoli from numerous earlier charts; only its Atlantic-facing western third survives, so the surviving object is a fragment of a substantially larger obra rather than the whole work. ([Soucek, pp. 66–67](https://makale.isam.org.tr/bitstreams/32493809-869e-4b34-a380-1bb2762f87c4/download); [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/memory-world/piri-reis-world-map-1513))

- **1528–1529 (AH 935):** The untitled second world-map project. Only a decorated northwestern section survives, showing parts of the North Atlantic, Caribbean, and Central and North American coasts; scholars disagree whether it is the sole remnant of a lost multisheet world map or an incipient, abandoned map that was never completed, so it resists classification as a finished obra. ([Soucek, pp. 70–71](https://makale.isam.org.tr/bitstreams/32493809-869e-4b34-a380-1bb2762f87c4/download); [*History of Cartography*, plate 21](https://press.uchicago.edu/books/HOC/HOC_V2_B1/HOC_VOLUME2_Book1_gallery1.pdf))

### Composite navigational protocols and atlases

- **1521:** *Kitâb-ı Bahriyye*—first recension, also named in its own preface *Cezâir-i Bahr-i Sefîd Eşkâli*, *Eşkâl-i Cezâir*, and *Sevâhil-i Bahr-i Sefîd*. This is simultaneously a Mediterranean sailing protocol, coastal geography, portolan atlas, and record of accumulated experience: approximately 130 prose chapters paired with charts, although surviving copies contain between 120 and 134 maps and no manuscript has been identified as Piri’s autograph. ([Sarıcaoğlu](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kitab-i-bahriyye); [Lowry, pp. 8–10](https://osmanliarastirmalari.isam.org.tr/dergi/article/download/492/495/1475); [Soucek, “Islamic Charting in the Mediterranean”](https://press.uchicago.edu/books/hoc/HOC_V2_B1/HOC_VOLUME2_Book1_chapter14.pdf))

- **1526:** *Kitâb-ı Bahriyye*—revised and expanded second recension. Made for presentation to Süleyman I, it combines sailing directions, coastal charts, hydrography, autobiographical material, navigational instruction, and a long verse framework; it is therefore one composite obra rather than 200-plus independently titled maps, and it does not simply supersede the first recension because some earlier material was shortened or omitted. Its authorship is collaborative and materially unstable: Piri supplied the principal prose and cartographic substance, the verse sections are attributed to Seyyid Murâdî, no surviving copy is demonstrably in Piri’s hand, and copyists added or omitted charts, producing conflicting totals of roughly 209–210 chapters and 210–233 maps. ([Sarıcaoğlu](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/kitab-i-bahriyye); [Lowry, pp. 8–10](https://osmanliarastirmalari.isam.org.tr/dergi/article/download/492/495/1475); [Soucek, pp. 67–71](https://makale.isam.org.tr/bitstreams/32493809-869e-4b34-a380-1bb2762f87c4/download))
