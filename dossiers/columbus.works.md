## Works by Christopher Columbus

### Composite books and documentary projects

- **c. 1480s–1506:** *Apostillas*. Hundreds of marginal annotations in at least five surviving books from Columbus’s library; they are reading traces rather than an autonomous book and sometimes cannot be securely separated from notes by his brother Bartolomé.
- **1498–1502:** *Libro de los privilegios*. A deliberately assembled documentary dossier of charters, grants, papal material, and legal claims, prepared in several manuscript copies to preserve and enforce the privileges promised to Columbus and his heirs; most constituent instruments were issued by others, so Columbus’s obra is chiefly the compilation and its juridical project.
- **1501–1502:** *Libro de las profecías*. An unfinished compilation of biblical and patristic passages, letters, chronology, and argument edited by Columbus with substantial assistance from the Carthusian Gaspar Gorricio. It is at once a prophetic manifesto, a crusading project, and a miscellany rather than a wholly authored book.
- **1493–1503; surviving copy made later:** *Libro copiador*. A register preserving copies of nine Columbus communications to the monarchs, including otherwise unknown accounts of the first and second voyages; the surviving manuscript is not autograph, and its authenticity and textual relationship to the lost originals have been debated.

### Voyage journals, relations, and geographical reports

- **1492–1493:** *Diario del primer viaje*. A shipboard log and daily relation of the first Atlantic voyage; Columbus’s original is lost, and the text survives principally through Bartolomé de las Casas’s abridgment and excerpts, making it a mediated textual object rather than an intact autograph work.
- **1492–1493:** *Fragmento de un escrito en el cuaderno de a bordo*. A small textual remainder associated with the first-voyage log; its fragmentary transmission prevents reconstruction of the document from which it came.
- **15 February 1493:** *Carta a Luis de Santángel*. An announcement and interpretation of the first voyage addressed to the royal finance official; manuscript and early printed forms differ, and its exact relationship to the nearly parallel letter to Gabriel Sánchez remains a textual question.
- **1493:** *Carta a Gabriel Sánchez*. The Latin-print tradition presents this as a letter to the royal treasurer announcing the discoveries; no Spanish original survives, and whether it represents a distinct letter or a recast version of the Santángel text is disputed.
- **1493:** *Carta a Rodrigo de Escobedo*. A purported first-voyage letter included by Varela and Gil for documentary completeness but treated by them as doubtful.
- **4 March 1493:** *Carta a los reyes anunciando el descubrimiento*. A first-voyage relation written near Lisbon and known from the *Libro copiador* rather than an autograph original.
- **1493–1494:** *Relación del segundo viaje*. A narrative report to the monarchs preserved through the *Libro copiador*; it belongs to the official accounting of the voyage rather than to a separately titled literary work.
- **1494:** *Carta a los reyes*. A report from the second voyage, editorially distinguished from other untitled royal correspondence.
- **1494:** *Fragmentos de una carta a los reyes*. Surviving portions of a lost second-voyage communication; the fragments resist treatment as a complete work.
- **1494–1495:** *Relación del viaje a Cuba y Jamaica*. A report of exploration made during the second voyage and transmitted indirectly.
- **1495:** *Carta a los reyes*. A further second-voyage report, known under an editorial descriptive title.
- **1498:** *Relación del tercer viaje*. Columbus’s extended account of the voyage to Trinidad and the South American mainland, including geographical and cosmographical speculation; it survives in variant copied forms rather than as a single settled autograph text.
- **1498–1500:** *Fragmentos de cartas a los reyes I–VI*. Six separately catalogued remnants of royal correspondence concerning the third voyage and Hispaniola; each is incomplete, and their boundaries depend partly on editorial reconstruction.
- **1500:** *Carta a doña Juana de la Torre*. A defense of Columbus’s government written after his arrest and return in chains; formally a personal letter, it functions as a political apologia.
- **1500:** *Hoja suelta escrita al parecer cuando le trajeron preso*. An autograph or near-autograph loose sheet associated conjecturally with Columbus’s imprisonment; both occasion and status as an independent work are uncertain.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a los reyes*. A post-imprisonment defense and petition, distinguished editorially from Columbus’s other untitled royal letters.
- **1501–1502:** *Memorial preparatorio del cuarto viaje*. A planning document that turns the proposed expedition into a textual project before its execution.
- **1502:** *Carta a los reyes*. A communication concerning preparations for, or the opening of, the fourth voyage.
- **7 July 1503:** *Relación del cuarto viaje*, also called *Carta de Jamaica*. A narrative, petition, and self-defense written during the expedition and addressed to the monarchs; its mixture of report, lament, prophecy, and political action makes it more than a voyage narrative.

### Instructions, ordinances, and administrative protocols

- **1493:** *Ordenanza de Colón*. A purported ordinance regulating colonial affairs; Varela and Gil classify its attribution as doubtful.
- **30 January 1494:** *Instrucción a Antonio de Torres*. A protocol directing the returning fleet’s reports, requests, trade, and provisioning.
- **9 April 1494:** *Instrucción a mosén Pedro Margarit*. Military and governmental instructions for operations on Hispaniola.
- **1496:** *Nombramiento de teniente de gobernador a Bartolomé Colón*. An administrative instrument appointing Columbus’s brother as deputy governor.
- **1495–1496:** *Memorial de La Mejorada*. A memorandum traditionally connected with Columbus but judged very doubtful in the standard corpus.
- **1496:** *Poder a Jerónimo de Agüero*. A legal delegation of authority rather than a descriptive text.
- **1496–1497:** *Respuesta del almirante a los capítulos de sus privilegios*. A point-by-point legal answer defending Columbus’s contractual rights.
- **1497:** *Contrato de Cristóbal Colón y Juan Rodríguez de Fonseca con Antón Mariño*. A jointly made contractual instrument; Columbus is a party, not its sole textual author.
- **1498–1499:** *Salvoconducto a Francisco Roldán*. A performative document granting protection during negotiations with the Hispaniolan rebels.
- **1499:** *Provisión a Pedro de Salcedo*. An administrative appointment or command whose force lay in what it authorized.
- **1500:** *Traslado de una carta de los reyes a Bobadilla con la respuesta del almirante*. A composite documentary object joining a royal letter written by others to Columbus’s response.
- **1501–1502:** *Información de los privilegios y mercedes*. A legal-information dossier marshaling Columbus’s grants and claims.
- **1502:** *Memorial a los reyes sobre la población de las Indias*. A colonial-policy proposal concerning settlement and government.
- **1502:** *Memorial a Diego Colón antes de emprender el cuarto viaje*. A protocol of familial and legal instructions intended to safeguard the Columbus claims during the expedition.
- **1503–1504:** *Carta a Nicolás de Ovando I*. An official communication to the governor of Hispaniola.
- **1503–1504:** *Carta a Nicolás de Ovando II*. A second separately catalogued communication to Ovando.
- **1504:** *Libramiento a favor de Diego Rodríguez*. A payment order: a text whose primary function was to move money.
- **1504:** *Libramiento a favor de Rodrigo Vizcaíno y Francisco Niño*. A joint payment order.
- **1504:** *Libramiento a favor de Diego de Salcedo*. A payment order made within Columbus’s continuing financial administration.
- **1505:** *Poder a favor de Francisco de Bardi*. A delegation authorizing Bardi to act for Columbus.

### Memorials, claims, and petitions

- **1495–1496:** *Memorial de agravios*. A written catalogue of injuries and unmet claims; its function is petitionary and juridical.
- **1496–1497:** *Fragmento de un memorial a los reyes*. Part of a lost petition, incapable of being treated as a complete work.
- **1496–1497:** *Memorial a los reyes*. A formal presentation of requests and grievances.
- **1498–1500:** *Memorial de agravios*. A later, separately catalogued statement of wrongs arising from Columbus’s government and privileges.
- **1501–1502:** *Memorial del almirante sobre agravios que recibió*. A further legal-political inventory of alleged injuries.
- **1504:** *Memorial a Diego Colón*. Instructions and claims conveyed to his son for prosecution at court.
- **1505–1506:** *Fragmento de un memorial al rey Fernando*. The remainder of an otherwise lost petition to the king.

### Letters and epistolary interventions

- **1497:** *Carta a Juan Rodríguez de Fonseca, obispo de Badajoz*. Administrative correspondence with the official overseeing Indies affairs.
- **1498:** *Fragmentos de una carta a Bartolomé Colón*. Portions of a lost family and governmental letter to Columbus’s brother.
- **1498:** *Carta a Diego Colón*. A letter to his elder son, the first of several separately preserved communications.
- **12 May 1498:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio I*. A letter concerning preparations, ships, money, and Columbus’s affairs.
- **28 May 1498:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio II*. A separate letter continuing those practical and devotional concerns.
- **1498–1499:** *Carta a Francisco Roldán*. A communication made during the rebellion on Hispaniola and inseparable from its political negotiations.
- **1499:** *Carta a Miguel Ballester*. A letter to Columbus’s ally on Hispaniola.
- **1499:** *Fragmento de una carta a los reyes I*. A separately transmitted remnant of royal correspondence.
- **1499:** *Fragmento de una carta a los reyes II*. A second remnant, not demonstrably a complete letter.
- **1499–1500:** *Fragmento de una carta a los reyes*. Another incomplete royal communication.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio III*. One of six letters grouped consecutively in the Varela–Gil corpus.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio IV*. A separately catalogued letter in the same sequence.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio V*. A separately catalogued letter in the same sequence.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio VI*. A separately catalogued letter in the same sequence.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio VII*. A separately catalogued letter in the same sequence.
- **1500–1501:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio VIII*. A separately catalogued letter in the same sequence.
- **1501–1502:** *Carta a la reina Isabel*. A direct petition to the queen concerning Columbus’s rights and circumstances.
- **1502:** *Carta a los reyes*. A further separately catalogued royal communication.
- **February 1502:** *Carta al papa Alejandro VI*. A diplomatic-religious letter presenting Columbus’s discoveries and missionary and crusading ambitions.
- **1502:** *Carta a Nicolò Oderigo I*. A letter to the Genoese ambassador, associated with delivery and preservation of Columbus’s documentary claims.
- **1502:** *Carta a la Banca de San Giorgio*. An offer or proposal involving Genoa’s governing financial institution; it is a civic-financial project expressed as a letter.
- **1502:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio IX*. A separately preserved communication associated with the books and privileges.
- **1502:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio X*. Another separately preserved communication in that documentary project.
- **1503–1504:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio XI*. A letter written during or shortly after the fourth voyage.
- **1504:** *Carta a Diego Colón I*. The first of four consecutively catalogued letters instructing Columbus’s son at court.
- **1504:** *Carta a Diego Colón II*. A separate letter in the same campaign for the family’s claims.
- **1504:** *Carta a Diego Colón III*. A separate letter combining family direction with political business.
- **1504:** *Carta a Diego Colón IV*. A further separately preserved instruction to his son.
- **1504–1505:** *Carta a Diego Colón V*. A later letter in the same sustained epistolary project.
- **1504–1505:** *Carta a Diego Colón VI*. Another separately catalogued letter.
- **1505:** *Carta a Nicolò Oderigo II*. A later communication to the Genoese diplomat.
- **1505:** *Carta a Juan Luis de Mayo*. A business letter to a Genoese associate.
- **1505:** *Carta a Diego Colón VII*. A separately preserved late letter to his elder son.
- **1505:** *Carta a fray Gaspar Gorricio XII*. A late communication to his Carthusian collaborator.
- **1505:** *Carta a Diego Colón VIII*. One of three consecutively catalogued late letters.
- **1505:** *Carta a Diego Colón IX*. A separate letter in that sequence.
- **1505:** *Carta a Diego Colón X*. A separate letter in that sequence.
- **1505:** *Carta al rey Fernando*. A direct petition concerning Columbus’s privileges and recompense.
- **1505–1506:** *Fragmento de una carta al rey Fernando*. An incomplete late appeal.
- **1505–1506:** *Fragmento de una carta a fray Diego de Deza*. The surviving part of a letter to Columbus’s ecclesiastical supporter.
- **1506:** *Carta a los reyes Felipe y Juana*. A late appeal to the newly reigning monarchs.

### Legal and financial instruments

- **1497:** *Recibí de 350,094 maravedís*. A signed acknowledgment of funds; it is an accounting act rather than a literary composition.
- **1497–1498:** *Institución de mayorazgo*. The instrument establishing an entailed Columbus estate and succession project. Its received text and date have generated authenticity and interpolation disputes, so it should not be treated as an uncomplicated autograph will.
- **1497–1498:** *Presentación de un albalá a Duardo Escaja y Bernardo Grimaldo*. A record of formally presenting a royal warrant to Genoese financiers; Columbus participated in the act, while the surviving record is scribal and juridical.
- **1502:** *Conocimiento de cien castellanos de oro a Alonso de Morales*. A receipt or acknowledgment of debt, made to establish a financial obligation.
- **19 May 1506:** *Testamento y codicilo*. The final testamentary instruments governing debts, heirs, religious obligations, and the family estate; the codicil is securely tied to Columbus’s last day, while the corpus also preserves and discusses earlier testamentary and entail traditions whose authenticity is less straightforward.

### Graphic and cartographic objects

- **c. 1492:** *Portolan chart and mappamundi attributed to Columbus*. A large manuscript chart in the Bibliothèque nationale de France was attributed to Columbus by Charles de La Roncière in 1924, but the attribution remains highly controversial; it must be listed as a disputed object, not a secure Columbus work.
- **1490s:** *Sketch maps accompanying geographical reports*. Contemporary testimony and later copies indicate that Columbus sent maps or chart sketches with some reports, but most originals are lost and particular surviving “Columbus maps” cannot usually be authenticated.
- **1498:** *Sketch of the Gulf of Paria and adjacent mainland*. A cartographic communication associated with the third voyage is known through documentary transmission or derivatives rather than a securely preserved autograph original.
- **By the 1490s:** *Hieratic signature device*. Columbus designed and repeatedly used a distinctive stacked monogram above “Xpo FERENS”; it is a graphic identity and performative authentication mark, not a conventional titled work.

### Settlements and colonial systems

- **1492:** *La Navidad*. A fortified settlement assembled from the wreckage of the *Santa María* under Columbus’s direction. It was a collective construction, was destroyed before his return, and resists attribution as his individual obra.
- **1493–1494:** *La Isabela*. The first sustained Spanish colonial town founded under Columbus on Hispaniola; architects, sailors, laborers, and settlers physically made it, while Columbus supplied the governing project.
- **1494–1496:** *Santo Tomás and other Hispaniolan forts*. A network of colonial strongpoints ordered during Columbus’s government. These are collective military constructions and instruments of territorial coercion rather than personal artworks.
- **1496:** *Nueva Isabela, later Santo Domingo*. The settlement was founded by Bartolomé Colón under Christopher’s viceregal regime; calling it simply Christopher’s work would erase its proximate founder and its many builders.
- **1493–1500:** *Columbian government of Hispaniola*. An administrative and extractive system involving tribute, compulsory labor, punishment, enslavement, and Atlantic shipment of captives. It is a consequential institutional creation, but a collective and coercive one that resists honorific treatment as a “work.”
- **1492–1504:** *Western-voyage and Indies enterprise*. Columbus’s overarching project joined navigation, commercial monopoly, Christian mission, crusading expectation, dynastic service, colonization, and family advancement. Its voyages and resulting empire were collective creations involving Indigenous peoples, mariners, financiers, royal officials, and enslaved captives, not a solitary Gesamtkunstwerk by Columbus.

### Mortal body and disputed relics

- **1506:** *Mortal remains of Christopher Columbus*. A bodily manifestation rather than something intentionally “made” by Columbus; the remains became a relic-like political object after his death.
- **1506–1509:** *Valladolid deposit and transfer to the Monastery of La Cartuja, Seville*. This posthumous funerary object was made through burial, exhumation, transport, and commemoration by others, not by Columbus.
- **1530s–1540s:** *Remains transferred to Santo Domingo*. Documentary evidence supports removal of remains identified as Columbus’s to Hispaniola, but the date is variously given and later transfers created an enduring identity dispute.
- **1795–1796:** *Havana relic deposit*. Spanish authorities removed bones believed to be Columbus’s from Santo Domingo and reinterred them in Havana after Santo Domingo passed to France; this is a state-produced relic assemblage.
- **1877:** *Santo Domingo lead casket attributed to Columbus*. Bones found in a labelled lead box generated the Dominican claim that Columbus’s authentic remains had never left the cathedral. They resist classification as a Columbus work and remain untested against the Seville material.
- **1898–1899:** *Havana–Seville relic transfer and cathedral deposit*. The remains claimed by Spain were removed from Havana in 1898 and installed in Seville Cathedral in 1899; modern genetic work supports a familial identification for the Seville fragments, but it does not by itself exclude the possibility that Santo Domingo retains other portions.
