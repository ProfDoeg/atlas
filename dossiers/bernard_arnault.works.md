## Works by Bernard Jean Étienne Arnault

### Book-length text

- **2000:** *La Passion créative: entretiens avec Yves Messarovitch*. An autobiographical interview-book made jointly through Arnault’s testimony and Messarovitch’s questioning; bibliographic records differ over whether to treat Messarovitch as author, interviewer, or co-author, so sole authorship should not be assumed.[^1][^2]

### Prefaces and forewords

- **2014:** *Preface to Fondation Louis Vuitton: Frank Gehry*. A short commissioned paratext introducing the architectural monograph; the building and its designs are Gehry’s work, not Arnault’s.[^3]
- **2017:** *Foreword to Being Modern: MoMA in Paris*. A catalogue foreword, also carried in the exhibition’s shorter bilingual album; these are two publishing manifestations of substantially the same contribution, not two securely distinct works.[^4]
- **2019:** *Foreword to The Courtauld Collection: A Vision for Impressionism*. Arnault’s institutional foreword to the exhibition catalogue, distinct from the scholarly essays and catalogue entries written by its curators and historians.[^5]
- **2019:** *Contribution to The Collection of the Fondation Louis Vuitton: Selected Works*. The publisher names Arnault among the contributors but does not identify the contribution’s title or extent; it therefore resists more exact classification without examination of the volume itself.[^6]
- **2022:** *Forewords to Monet—Mitchell and the Joan Mitchell Retrospective*. Two presidential forewords printed in the press documentation for the paired Fondation Louis Vuitton exhibitions; whether the corresponding catalogue preserves them as two independent texts or as related manifestations requires examination of the book.[^7]
- **2023:** *Foreword to Basquiat × Warhol: Painting Four Hands*. A catalogue foreword rather than authorship of the catalogue, exhibition, or artists’ collaborative paintings.[^8]
- **2023:** *Foreword to Rothko*. A presidential foreword to the Fondation Louis Vuitton retrospective catalogue; the publisher’s contents distinguish it from Suzanne Pagé’s preface and the scholarly essays.[^9]

### Corporate and institutional projects

- **1987:** *Maison Christian Lacroix*. A fashion house launched by Arnault’s Financière Agache with Christian Lacroix and image consultant Jean-Jacques Picart. It is a collective corporate project—not Arnault’s authorship of Lacroix’s fashions—and contemporary accounts divide “creation” among the designer, financial patron, and adviser.[^10][^11]
- **1989–present:** *LVMH as an integrated luxury group*. Arnault did not create the legal entity: LVMH resulted from the 1987 merger of Moët Hennessy and Louis Vuitton. What can be attributed to him is the continuing corporate project begun with his acquisition of control in 1988–89—organizing an expanding constellation of maisons as a single luxury group—so it resists treatment as a discrete, solely authored obra.[^12][^13]
- **2001–2014:** *Fondation Louis Vuitton*. An institutional and architectural project initiated by Arnault after meeting Frank Gehry in 2001, formally announced by Arnault and Yves Carcelle in 2006, and opened in 2014. Arnault was initiator and commissioner; Gehry was the architect, while the legal foundation was created corporately by LVMH and its maisons.[^14][^15]
- **2001–2014:** *Fondation Louis Vuitton building commission*. The material manifestation of the preceding project: a cultural building commissioned by Arnault but designed by Gehry. Calling it simply “a work by Arnault” would erase the decisive architectural authorship, although the commission and institutional brief belong to Arnault’s project.[^15][^16]
- **By 2014–present:** *Collection of the Fondation Louis Vuitton*. An ongoing, multi-medium collection organized around four broad lines—Contemplative, Pop, Expressionist, and Music/Sound—and shaped according to aims attributed to its founder, Arnault. It is an acquisitional and curatorial project rather than an artwork he personally made; the Fondation expressly rejects completeness, and its evolving online presentation is not a fixed catalogue raisonné.[^17]

### Private collection

- **Date not fixed–present:** *Bernard Arnault’s private art collection*. An ongoing act of collecting rather than a closed work with an authoritative title or public catalogue. Because reporting and even reference works sometimes blur Arnault’s personal holdings, LVMH’s holdings, and the Fondation’s collection, neither its contents nor its completeness can be stated securely; that resistance to delimitation is intrinsic to the object.[^17][^18]
