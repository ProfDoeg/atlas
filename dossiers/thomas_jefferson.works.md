## Works by Thomas Jefferson

### Books, treatises, compilations, and retrospective writings

- **c. 1758–1772:** *Literary Commonplace Book*. A manuscript compilation of copied poetry, philosophy, and other reading rather than a wholly authored book; its mixed authorship makes it an intellectual workshop more than a conventional obra. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/thomas-jefferson-papers/about-this-collection/))
- **1762–1767:** *Legal Commonplace Book*. A manuscript repertory of cases, precedents, terms, and definitions assembled during Jefferson’s legal studies; because much of its language was copied, it resists classification as an authored work. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/thomas-jefferson-papers/about-this-collection/))
- **1774:** *A Summary View of the Rights of British America*. Political manifesto drafted as instructions for Virginia’s delegates to the First Continental Congress, rejected as too radical by the convention and published anonymously without Jefferson’s prior authorization. ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/jefferson-thomas-1743-1826/); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1781–1785, revised later:** *Notes on the State of Virginia*. A book-length response to twenty-three queries that became a hybrid of natural history, political argument, ethnography, statistics, and memoir; its antislavery passages coexist with foundational assertions of racial hierarchy. ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/notes-on-the-state-of-virginia-1785/); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1786:** *Thoughts on English Prosody*. A linguistic essay on the meters of English verse, not a book in the ordinary bibliographical sense. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1796:** *An Essay Towards Facilitating Instruction in the Anglo-Saxon and Modern Dialects of the English Language*. An unfinished pedagogical and philological project, eventually published posthumously; the surviving text is better understood as teaching apparatus than completed treatise. ([Founders Online edition via National Archives](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-29-02-0054))
- **1800–1801:** *A Manual of Parliamentary Practice for the Use of the Senate of the United States*. A protocol: Jefferson systematized parliamentary authorities and Senate practice into an instructional manual rather than writing a descriptive political book. ([U.S. House of Representatives](https://constitution.congress.gov/resources/jeffersons-manual/); [Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3117))
- **1804:** *The Philosophy of Jesus of Nazareth*. A private compilation made by cutting passages from printed Gospels and arranging them into a continuous account; it is simultaneously textual argument and materially fabricated book-object. ([Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1199660))
- **c. 1819–1820:** *The Life and Morals of Jesus of Nazareth*. A multilingual cut-and-paste Gospel harmony commonly called the “Jefferson Bible”; its physical manufacture is inseparable from its theological project, making it a small Gesamtkunstwerk of editing, typography, and binding. ([Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1199660))
- **1821:** *Autobiography*. An unfinished retrospective narrative ending in 1790; Jefferson began it for his family, so its incompleteness is part of the work rather than a missing conclusion supplied by later editors. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1791–1806; organized later:** *The Anas*. Memoranda of political conversations and events, later rearranged by Jefferson as a documentary record of the Federalist era; neither diary nor seamless memoir, it is a constructed archive. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1818:** *The Explanations*. A retrospective framing text for the *Anas*, written to establish Jefferson’s version of the political conflicts of the 1790s. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **c. 1800:** *Services to My Country*. A memorandum inventorying Jefferson’s public achievements; it is self-accounting rather than autobiography in narrative form. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1826:** *Epitaph*. The inscription Jefferson composed for his own grave, naming only three accomplishments; it is both text and protocol for a future monument. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/jeffersons-gravestone/))

### Manifestos, constitutions, laws, reports, and state papers

- **1775:** *Resolutions of Congress on Lord North’s Conciliatory Proposal*. A congressional response drafted by Jefferson: a collective state paper whose final institutional authorship cannot be reduced to the person who composed its language. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1775:** *Declaration of the Causes and Necessity of Taking Up Arms*. Jefferson prepared the principal preliminary draft; John Dickinson substantially recast it, so the adopted declaration is a collaborative congressional work rather than Jefferson’s alone. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mtj1.001_0188_0198/))
- **1776:** *Declaration of Independence*. A manifesto drafted by Jefferson and altered by the Committee of Five and Continental Congress; attribution to Jefferson is secure, but sole authorship of the enacted text is not. ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration); [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/declara/declara4.html))
- **1776:** *Draft Constitution for Virginia*. An unrealized constitutional project sent to Virginia while its convention was already completing another constitution; several differently developed drafts survive. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1776–1779:** *Revisal of the Laws of Virginia*. A body of 126 bills produced by a committee of revisors, principally Jefferson, George Wythe, and Edmund Pendleton; individual drafting shares vary, so it is a collaborative legislative obra rather than 126 securely solo works. ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/revisal-of-the-laws-1776-1786/))
- **1777–1779; enacted 1786:** *A Bill for Establishing Religious Freedom*. A legislative manifesto declaring civil independence from religious opinion; Madison guided its enactment after Jefferson had left Virginia. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/jefferson/jeffleg.html); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1778–1779:** *A Bill for Proportioning Crimes and Punishments in Cases Heretofore Capital*. A proposed penal-code protocol intended to restrict capital punishment; the legislature rejected it. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1778:** *A Bill for the More General Diffusion of Knowledge*. An unrealized educational system specifying publicly supported elementary schools and advanced selection; simultaneously manifesto and administrative protocol. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1779:** *A Bill Declaring Who Shall Be Deemed Citizens of This Commonwealth*. A proposed statutory definition of Virginia citizenship, including provisions now inseparable from Jefferson’s politics of race and expatriation. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1784:** *Report of a Plan of Government for the Western Territory*. A congressional plan for organizing prospective states, including an antislavery clause defeated in the Continental Congress. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mtj1.003_0539_0544/); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1784:** *Reply to the Representations of Affairs in America by British Newspapers*. A public rebuttal designed for circulation in the press rather than a freestanding book. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1786:** *Answers and Observations for Démeunier’s Article on the United States*. A sequence of answers, corrections, and letters made to shape an encyclopedia article; it is collaborative knowledge-production, not Jefferson’s encyclopedia entry in a simple authorial sense. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1788:** *Observations on the Whale-Fishery*. A diplomatic-economic report on the American whaling industry and French trade policy. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1789:** *Draft Charter of Rights for France*. A proposed constitutional manifesto enclosed to Rabaut de Saint-Étienne during the French Revolution; it remained advice, not enacted law. ([Founders Online](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-15-02-0192))
- **1790:** *Plan for Establishing Uniformity in the Coinage, Weights, and Measures of the United States*. A scientific-administrative protocol proposing decimalized systems; Congress did not adopt the complete plan. ([National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://www.nist.gov/pml/owm/si-units-length); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1791:** *Opinion on the Constitutionality of a National Bank*. A cabinet opinion arguing that Congress lacked power to charter the proposed bank; an advisory state paper, not a judicial opinion. ([National Archives](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-19-02-0051); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1793:** *Opinion on the French Treaties*. A cabinet opinion on whether revolutionary France’s change of government released the United States from treaty obligations. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1793:** *Report on the Privileges and Restrictions on the Commerce of the United States in Foreign Countries*. An official survey and policy argument made as secretary of state. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1798:** *Draft of the Kentucky Resolutions*. An anonymously transmitted states’-rights manifesto against the Alien and Sedition Acts; Kentucky altered Jefferson’s language, and his authorship was not publicly acknowledged during his lifetime. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/resource/mtj1.021_0385_0392/); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1801–1809:** *Presidential addresses and messages*. A body of enacted political speech comprising two inaugural addresses, eight annual messages, special messages, replies, and proclamations; secretaries and clerks participated in governmental production, but the presidential texts are conventionally assigned to Jefferson. ([The American Presidency Project](https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/people/president/thomas-jefferson); [Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1802:** *Letter to the Danbury Baptist Association*. A public reply that functioned as a concise manifesto of religious liberty and introduced the influential “wall of separation” formulation; formally it remains a letter, not legislation. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/loc/lcib/9806/danpre.html))
- **1803:** *Instructions to Meriwether Lewis*. A protocol for the Lewis and Clark expedition specifying geography, natural history, diplomacy, ethnography, and recordkeeping. ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/education/lessons/lewis-clark))
- **1818:** *Report of the Commissioners for the University of Virginia*. A collaboratively signed institutional plan, substantially shaped and drafted by Jefferson, defining the university’s site, curriculum, governance, and architecture. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/); [University of Virginia](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/03-13-02-0316))
- **1821:** *Memorial on the Book Duty*. A petition opposing duties on imported books as an obstruction to learning. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1822–1825:** *Minutes of the Board of Visitors of the University of Virginia*. Institutional records in which Jefferson repeatedly acted as draftsman; they resist treatment as exclusively authored works because the board adopted them collectively. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1825:** *Draft Declaration and Protest of the Commonwealth of Virginia*. An unfinished constitutional manifesto against federal internal-improvement powers, prepared at the request of the Virginia legislature but not adopted in Jefferson’s exact form. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))

### Journals, record systems, correspondence, and documentary bodies

- **1767–1826:** *Memorandum Books*. A continuing personal system of accounts, travel records, weather, reading, measurements, and daily transactions; the unit of creation is the evolving record practice rather than any one finished text. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/research-education/thomas-jefferson-encyclopedia/memorandum-books/); [Library of Congress](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3117))
- **1774–1824:** *Garden Book*. A long-running horticultural notebook recording sowing, harvesting, weather, experiments, and failures; it is a working protocol and data archive rather than a retrospective gardening book. ([Massachusetts Historical Society](https://www.masshist.org/thomasjeffersonpapers/garden/))
- **1774–1822:** *Farm Books*. Plantation-management records covering crops, livestock, labor, manufacturing, and enslaved people; these are administrative artefacts of slavery as well as agricultural texts. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3117))
- **1760–1826:** *Correspondence*. More than twenty thousand letters and retained copies form a distributed lifetime corpus rather than a single obra; individual letters are separately dated documents, while the authoritative scholarly edition remains unfinished. ([The Papers of Thomas Jefferson](https://jeffersonpapers.princeton.edu/); [Founders Online overview](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2010/winter/founders.html))
- **1786:** *A Tour to Some of the Gardens of England*. A travel journal and design notebook documenting English gardens, costs, measurements, and aesthetic judgments. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1787:** *Notes of a Tour into the Southern Parts of France, &c.* A travel record combining agriculture, architecture, technology, landscape, and political economy rather than conventional literary travel writing. ([Founders Online](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Jefferson/01-11-02-0229))
- **1788:** *Memorandums Made on a Tour from Paris through the Southern Parts of France and Northern Italy, to Amsterdam, Strasburg, and Back to Paris*. A composite travel-and-observation notebook; its commonly shortened title conceals its nature as a working record. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1788:** *Travelling Notes for Mr. Rutledge and Mr. Shippen*. A practical itinerary and advice protocol made for other travelers. ([Library of America](https://www.loa.org/books/67-writings/))
- **1783 and later:** *Library catalogues*. Manuscript systems made to classify Jefferson’s collections, most famously by “Memory,” “Reason,” and “Imagination”; they catalogue books Jefferson owned rather than books he made, yet the classificatory architecture itself is his obra. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/jefferson/jefflib.html); [Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3117))

### Architectural works and projects

- **c. 1768–1809:** *Monticello*. A house, plantation center, laboratory, garden, and evolving architectural project repeatedly redesigned by Jefferson and physically built by free and enslaved craftspeople; calling it his work must not erase their making. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/house-gardens/the-house/architectural-image/); [Kimball catalogue index](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/wilson/catalogs/kimball.html))
- **1771:** *Proposed addition to the College of William & Mary*. An architectural commission from Governor Dunmore; the project’s documentary status is firmer than its realization, and it should not be treated as a securely surviving Jefferson building. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/jeffersons-plan-academical-village))
- **1785–1788:** *Virginia State Capitol*. A public-building design modeled on the Maison Carrée and developed with the French architect Charles-Louis Clérisseau; the executed building departed from Jefferson’s model and drawings, so authorship is collaborative and mediated. ([Virginia State Capitol](https://www.virginiacapitol.gov/about/capitol-history/); [Library of Congress exhibition](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/jefferson/jeffdec.html))
- **1790s–1820s:** *Poplar Forest*. Jefferson’s octagonal retreat house and landscape project, constructed and altered over decades by enslaved and free laborers; its long incompletion was integral to Jefferson’s experimental design practice. ([Thomas Jefferson’s Poplar Forest](https://www.poplarforest.org/learn/thomas-jeffersons-design/))
- **1792:** *Anonymous design for the President’s House*. A Palladian competition drawing traditionally attributed to Jefferson, but the attribution is not conclusive; it is an unrealized and possibly anonymous-by-intention project. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/washington-dc))
- **1796–c. 1800:** *Farmington*. A remodeling and enlargement designed for George Divers; later alterations complicate the boundary between Jefferson’s drawings and the house now standing. ([Historic American Buildings Survey, Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/va0023/))
- **c. 1796:** *Edgemont*. A plantation-house design long attributed to Jefferson on circumstantial and stylistic evidence; no definitive proof establishes his authorship. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/edgemont))
- **c. 1808–1810:** *Garden Pavilion at Monticello*. A small cubic garden building derived from Palladian proportions; the present pavilion is a reconstruction based on Jefferson’s memorandum and archaeological evidence because no design drawing survives. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/garden-pavilion))
- **1810–1822:** *Barboursville*. A plantation house designed for James Barbour; the intended dome was never built, and the surviving object is a fire-damaged ruin, making the project, drawings, former building, and ruin distinct manifestations. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/barboursville))
- **1814–1826:** *University of Virginia “Academical Village”*. A Gesamtkunstwerk joining institutional plan, landscape, curriculum, Rotunda, Lawn, ranges, hotels, and ten differently ordered pavilions; Jefferson directed the ensemble, while builders, enslaved laborers, artisans, patrons, and consulting architects participated in making it. ([University of Virginia](https://www.virginia.edu/academicalvillage/); [Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/construction-university-1817-1826))
- **1817–1826:** *The Rotunda*. The library and visual focus of the University of Virginia, adapted from the Pantheon; it remained unfinished when Jefferson died and was later altered, burned, rebuilt, and restored. ([University of Virginia](https://rotunda.virginia.edu/history); [Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/construction-university-1817-1826))
- **1817–1826:** *Ten Lawn Pavilions and colonnades*. A suite of faculty residences and teaching rooms, each demonstrating a different classical order; they are individual buildings but also components of the larger Academical Village. ([University of Virginia](https://www.virginia.edu/academicalvillage/); [Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/construction-university-1817-1826))
- **c. 1770s–1819:** *Architectural drawings*. Hundreds of plans, elevations, details, calculations, copies, studies, and unrealized schemes constitute a graphic body of work catalogued by Fiske Kimball and Frederick Doveton Nichols; many sheets are projects rather than autonomous artworks, and some attributions remain uncertain. ([Kimball catalogue index](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/wilson/catalogs/kimball.html); [Nichols drawing list](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/wilson/Nichols/nichols.body.html))

### Machines, furniture, and technical designs

- **c. 1787:** *Macaroni-machine drawing and instructions*. A technical sketch copied or adapted after Jefferson observed pasta-making equipment in Europe; it is documentation and proposed replication, not evidence that he invented macaroni or necessarily built the machine. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2019_1112.pdf))
- **1788–1794:** *Moldboard of least resistance*. A mathematically generated plow moldboard intended to reduce soil resistance; Jefferson designed and repeatedly refined it, while craftsmen fabricated the working plows. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/jefferson-day/attending-to-my-farm/agricultural-innovations))
- **1792–1793:** *Great Clock*. A dual-faced, seven-day clock and building mechanism designed jointly by Jefferson and Robert Leslie and made by Peter Spurck; attribution therefore belongs to a design-and-fabrication collaboration. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/great-clock))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Revolving Windsor chair*. A modified chair designed to rotate, one element of Jefferson’s working environment; its production depended on unidentified or incompletely documented craftspeople. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2019_1112.pdf))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Revolving bookstand*. A multi-sided reading stand permitting several open books to be consulted in succession; it is furniture as intellectual protocol. ([Monticello Collections](https://www.monticello.org/research-education/thomas-jefferson-encyclopedia/revolving-bookstand/))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Folding ladder*. A compact ladder devised for Monticello’s high shelves and Great Clock; fabrication occurred in the Monticello joinery and should not be collapsed into Jefferson’s design authorship alone. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/great-clock))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Folding campstool*. A portable furniture design associated with Jefferson’s interest in transformable objects; surviving descriptions do not justify treating every extant example as personally made by him. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2019_1112.pdf))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Revolving clothes rack*. A mechanical storage fitting designed for Monticello; it belongs to the house’s integrated machinery rather than to an independent “invention” canon. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2019_1112.pdf))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Double-acting doors at Monticello*. Mechanically linked doors arranged to open together; an architectural mechanism and part of Monticello’s Gesamtkunstwerk, not a freestanding authored object. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2019_1112.pdf))
- **late eighteenth–early nineteenth century:** *Bottle dumbwaiters and dining-room service mechanisms*. Lifts and concealed service arrangements incorporated into Monticello; Jefferson adapted known technologies, while enslaved and free craftspeople made and operated them. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/dumbwaiters))
- **c. 1800s:** *Wheel cipher*. A proposed encryption apparatus employing alphabetic disks; evidence supports Jefferson’s design, but surviving “Jefferson disks” are later reconstructions rather than an authenticated original machine. ([National Cryptologic Museum](https://www.nsa.gov/History/National-Cryptologic-Museum/Exhibits-Artifacts/Artifact-View/Article/2718923/jefferson-cipher-wheel/))
- **1804 onward:** *Polygraph modifications*. Suggestions for improving John Isaac Hawkins’s copying machine, communicated to its American maker Charles Willson Peale; Jefferson was an intensive collaborator and user, not the polygraph’s inventor. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/stories/jeffersons-correspondence))
- **various dates:** *Domestic, agricultural, and architectural mechanisms*. Swivel seating, sash systems, “air closets,” alcove beds, ventilation arrangements, service lifts, and other devices form a dispersed design practice rather than a securely bounded catalogue of inventions; many were adaptations, and surviving objects were made by craftspeople whose labor must remain visible. ([Monticello on air closets](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/privies); [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/lcm/pdf/LCM_2019_1112.pdf))

### Collections and institutions as made wholes

- **c. 1770–1815:** *Jefferson’s library and its classification*. A deliberately assembled and organized collection of 6,487 books sold to Congress in 1815; Jefferson did not author the books, but the selection and Baconian arrangement made the library an intellectual architecture and the nucleus of the rebuilt Library of Congress. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/jefferson/jefflib.html))
- **1780s–1809:** *Native American vocabularies*. A planned comparative linguistic archive gathered through officials and correspondents; much of the collection was lost or destroyed, so the project survives incompletely and cannot be reduced to a finished text. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/research-education/thomas-jefferson-encyclopedia/indian-vocabularies/))
- **1801–1809:** *Presidential information system*. Letterpress copies, polygraph copies, indexes, filing conventions, and official papers formed an administrative apparatus rather than one text; its products are distributed across the documentary record. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/stories/jeffersons-correspondence); [Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/3117))
- **1814–1826:** *University of Virginia*. Beyond its buildings, Jefferson made an institutional project comprising legislation, governance, curriculum, faculty recruitment, library selection, regulations, and ceremonial identity; because boards, legislators, donors, workers, and enslaved people materially constituted it, “Jefferson’s creation” is useful only with that resistance stated. ([Monticello](https://www.monticello.org/encyclopedia/establishment-university-virginia/); [University of Virginia](https://www.virginia.edu/academicalvillage/))
