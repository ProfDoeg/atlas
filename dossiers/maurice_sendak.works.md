## Works by Maurice Bernard Sendak

### Authored-and-illustrated books: word-and-image obras

- **1956:** *Kenny’s Window*. Sendak’s first book as both writer and illustrator: a picture-book fantasy.
- **1957:** *Very Far Away*. An authored-and-illustrated picture book.
- **1960:** *The Sign on Rosie’s Door*. A picture book developed from Sendak’s Brooklyn “Nutshell Kids.”
- **1962:** *The Nutshell Library*. A boxed Gesamtkunstwerk of four miniature books—*Alligators All Around*, *Chicken Soup with Rice*, *One Was Johnny*, and *Pierre*—each combining Sendak’s verse, typography, and pictures.
- **1963:** *Where the Wild Things Are*. A picture book in which verbal narrative, page design, and expanding-and-contracting image field form a single work.
- **1967:** *Higglety Pigglety Pop! or There Must Be More to Life*. An authored-and-illustrated picture-book novella.
- **1970:** *In the Night Kitchen*. A picture book conceived through comics-like panels, hand lettering, and continuous pictorial narrative.
- **1970/1975; republished 2024–25:** *Ten Little Rabbits: A Counting Book with Mino the Magician*. A tiny counting book originally made as a limited museum-benefit pamphlet; its later trade publication is a posthumous manifestation, not a newly made Sendak work.
- **1975:** *Really Rosie, Starring the Nutshell Kids*. A picture-book manifestation of Sendak’s animated musical, with his book and lyrics and Carole King’s music.
- **1976:** *Some Swell Pup, or Are You Sure You Want a Dog?*. A picture-book protocol for dog ownership, co-written with dog trainer Matthew Margolis and illustrated by Sendak.
- **1977:** *Seven Little Monsters*. A picture book derived from the animated counting film Sendak had made for *Sesame Street*; sources variously date an earlier European issue to 1975 and the Harper edition to 1977.
- **1981:** *Outside Over There*. An authored-and-illustrated picture book and the third of the loosely designated dream/fantasy trilogy with *Wild Things* and *Night Kitchen*.
- **1988:** *Caldecott & Co.: Notes on Books and Pictures*. A collected body of Sendak’s essays, lectures, appreciations, and occasional writings rather than a single continuous treatise.
- **1993:** *We Are All in the Dumps with Jack and Guy: Two Nursery Rhymes with Pictures*. A picture-book recomposition of two traditional rhymes; authorship lies in Sendak’s joining, visual interpretation, and book-making rather than in the inherited words.
- **1995:** *Maurice Sendak’s Christmas Mystery*. A boxed book-and-jigsaw object; it resists classification as a book alone.
- **2011:** *Bumble-Ardy*. A picture book remade from Sendak’s 1970 *Sesame Street* animation rather than a wholly independent first conception.
- **2013:** *My Brother’s Book*. Sendak’s final completed picture book, an elegy for his brother Jack; publication was posthumous.
- **2018:** *Presto and Zesto in Limboland*. A posthumously published collaboration: Sendak made the pictures in 1990 and later devised the story with Arthur Yorinks, so 2018 dates the manifestation, not the artwork.
- **2025:** *Hansel and Gretel*. A posthumous picture-book manifestation pairing Stephen King’s newly written retelling with Sendak’s 1997 scenic and costume designs; Sendak and King never collaborated during Sendak’s lifetime, so it should not be mistaken for a jointly conceived book.

- **1953:** *Shadrach*, by Meindert DeJong. An illustrated children’s novel.
- **1954:** *Mrs. Piggle-Wiggle’s Farm*, by Betty MacDonald. An illustrated children’s novel.

- **1954:** *The Magic Pictures*, by Marcel Aymé. An illustrated collection of stories.

- **1955:** *Seven Little Stories on Big Subjects*, by Gladys Baker Bond. A group of didactic stories illustrated by Sendak.

- **1956:** *I Want to Paint My Bathroom Blue*, by Ruth Krauss. A collaborative picture book.

- **1957:** *You Can’t Get There from Here*, by Ogden Nash. A collection for which Sendak supplied illustrations.
- **1957:** *Circus Girl*, by Jack Sendak. A picture book illustrated by Maurice Sendak.
- **1957:** *Little Bear*, by Else Holmelund Minarik. The first of five early-reader books whose characters Sendak visually created.
- **1958:** *Along Came a Dog*, by Meindert DeJong. An illustrated novel.
- **1958:** *No Fighting, No Biting!*, by Else Holmelund Minarik. An illustrated early reader.
- **1958:** *Somebody Else’s Nut Tree and Other Tales from Children*, by Ruth Krauss. A collection illustrated by Sendak; later reissues do not constitute new works.
- **1958:** *What Do You Say, Dear?*, by Sesyle Joslin. An illustrated mock-etiquette picture book.
- **1959:** *Seven Tales*, by Hans Christian Andersen, translated by Eva Le Gallienne. A fairy-tale collection illustrated by Sendak.
- **1959:** *Father Bear Comes Home*, by Else Holmelund Minarik. An illustrated early reader.
- **1959:** *The Moon Jumpers*, by Janice May Udry. A picture book and 1960 Caldecott Honor book.
- **1960:** *Dwarf Long-Nose*, by Wilhelm Hauff, translated by Doris Orgel. An illustrated fairy tale.
- **1960:** *Little Bear’s Friend*, by Else Holmelund Minarik. An illustrated early reader.
- **1960:** *Open House for Butterflies*, by Ruth Krauss. A collaborative picture book.
- **1961:** *Little Bear’s Visit*, by Else Holmelund Minarik. An illustrated early reader and Caldecott Honor book.
- **1961:** *What Do You Do, Dear?*, by Sesyle Joslin. A second illustrated mock-etiquette book.
- **1961:** *The Tale of Gockel, Hinkel and Gackeliah*, by Clemens Brentano, translated by Doris Orgel. An illustrated literary fairy tale.
- **1961:** *What the Good-Man Does Is Always Right*, by Hans Christian Andersen. Sendak’s illustrated contribution appeared in *Best in Children’s Books*, volume 41; it is a component, not an independently issued Sendak book.
- **1962:** *Schoolmaster Whackwell’s Wonderful Sons*, by Clemens Brentano, retold by Doris Orgel. An illustrated fairy tale.

- **1962:** *The Big Green Book*, by Robert Graves. An illustrated fantasy.
- **1962:** *The Singing Hill*, by Meindert DeJong. An illustrated novel.
- **1963:** *She Loves Me … She Loves Me Not …*, by Robert Keeshan. An illustrated children’s book.

- **1963:** *Nikolenka’s Childhood*, by Leo Tolstoy. An illustrated edition of Tolstoy’s autobiographical fiction; Sendak made the pictures, not the underlying text.
- **1963:** *Sarah’s Room*, by Doris Orgel. An illustrated picture book; later republication is not a separate obra.
- **1963:** *The Griffin and the Minor Canon*, by Frank R. Stockton. An illustrated literary fairy tale.
- **1964:** *Pleasant Fieldmouse*, by Jan Wahl. An illustrated picture book.

- **1964:** *The Bee-Man of Orn*, by Frank R. Stockton. An illustrated fairy tale.

- **1965:** *The Animal Family*, by Randall Jarrell. An illustrated novella.
- **1965:** *Lullabies and Night Songs*, edited by William Engvick, with music by Alec Wilder. An illustrated songbook whose words, music, and pictures remain distinct contributions within one composite object.
- **1966:** *Zlateh the Goat and Other Stories*, by Isaac Bashevis Singer. A story collection illustrated by Sendak and awarded a Newbery Honor.
- **1967:** *The Golden Key*, by George MacDonald. An illustrated fantasy.
- **1968:** *A Kiss for Little Bear*, by Else Holmelund Minarik. The fifth Sendak-illustrated Little Bear early reader.
- **1969:** *The Light Princess*, by George MacDonald. An illustrated fantasy.
- **1972:** *Down the Rabbit Hole: Adventures and Misadventures in the Realm of Children’s Literature*, by Selma G. Lanes. Sendak made the cover art and design rather than the critical text.
- **1973:** *King Grisly-Beard*, by the Brothers Grimm, translated by Edgar Taylor. An illustrated fairy tale.
- **1973:** *The Juniper Tree and Other Tales from Grimm*, selected by Lore Segal and Maurice Sendak. A two-volume Gesamtkunstwerk of selection, translation, typography, and extensive illustration; Sendak was selector and artist, not author of the inherited tales.
- **1974:** *Hans Christian Andersen: The Complete Fairy Tales and Stories*, translated by Erik Christian Haugaard. Sendak made the cover art rather than illustrating the complete contents.
- **1976:** *Fly by Night*, by Randall Jarrell. A picture book illustrated by Sendak after Jarrell’s death.
- **1977:** *The Disney Poster Book*. Sendak served as editor/selector of a visual anthology made from Walt Disney Studios posters; the reproduced images are not his creations.
- **1984:** *Nutcracker*, by E. T. A. Hoffmann, translated by Ralph Manheim. A picture-book manifestation developed from Sendak’s designs for the Pacific Northwest Ballet production.
- **1984:** *The Love for Three Oranges*, by Frank Corsaro. An illustrated book drawn from Corsaro and Sendak’s operatic production; it records and reworks scenic imagery rather than merely illustrating a freestanding new story.
- **1985:** *In Grandpa’s House*, by Philip Sendak. Maurice illustrated his father’s translated family tale.
- **1985:** *The Cunning Little Vixen*, by Rudolf Těsnohlídek, translated by Tatiana Firkusny, Maritza Morgan, and Robert T. Jones. A book manifestation of material related to Sendak’s opera designs.
- **1988:** *Dear Mili*, by Wilhelm Grimm, translated by Ralph Manheim. An illustrated picture book made from a previously unpublished Grimm manuscript.
- **1988:** *Sing a Song of Popcorn: Every Child’s Book of Poems*, selected by Beatrice Schenk de Regniers and others. Sendak supplied a section of illustrations alongside several other artists; the anthology as a whole is not solely his work.
- **1988:** *Tail Feathers from Mother Goose: The Opie Rhyme Book*, edited by Iona Opie. Sendak contributed the jacket/title-page image, also issued as a benefit print.
- **1990:** *Seven Tales*, by Hans Christian Andersen. A new collected manifestation of Sendak’s Andersen illustrations rather than seven newly illustrated works.
- **1990:** *The Big Book for Peace*. Sendak contributed cover art to a multi-author, multi-illustrator anthology; attribution of the whole book to him would be misleading.
- **1992:** *I Saw Esau: The Schoolchild’s Pocket Book*, edited by Iona and Peter Opie. A densely illustrated collection of playground verse.
- **1995:** *Pierre; or, The Ambiguities: The Kraken Edition*, by Herman Melville. An illustrated adult novel; Sendak’s pictorial cycle is an interpretive work within Melville’s text.
- **1995:** *The Miami Giant*, by Arthur Yorinks. A picture-book collaboration illustrated by Sendak.
- **1996:** *Frank and Joey Eat Lunch*, by Arthur Yorinks. A photographed miniature-theatre book: Yorinks directed, Sendak designed sets and costumes, and Ky Chung photographed them.
- **1996:** *Frank and Joey Go to Work*, by Arthur Yorinks. A second photographed miniature-theatre book with Sendak’s sets and costumes.
- **1998:** *Penthesilea*, by Heinrich von Kleist, translated by Joel Agee. An illustrated dramatic poem.
- **1999:** *Swine Lake*, by James Marshall. A posthumous Marshall story illustrated and visually staged by Sendak.
- **2003:** *Brundibár*, adapted by Tony Kushner. A picture-book collaboration related to Kushner and Sendak’s stage project but distinct from the staged opera.
- **2005:** *Bears!*, by Ruth Krauss. A newly illustrated manifestation of Krauss’s 1948 text.
- **2006:** *Mommy?*, scenario by Arthur Yorinks and paper engineering by Matthew Reinhart. Sendak’s only pop-up book: a collaborative paper-engineering object that resists reduction to either text or illustration.

### Moving-image obras and adaptations made with others

- **1969:** *Where the Wild Things Are*. An animated short directed by Gene Deitch from Sendak’s book, with Sendak participating in the adaptation; a revised sound version followed in 1973, so sources differ over which year names the work.
- **1970:** *Bumble Ardy*. An animated *Sesame Street* segment scripted and storyboarded by Sendak and produced with Jim Henson’s team; it precedes the 2011 picture book.
- **1971:** *Seven Little Monsters*. An animated *Sesame Street* counting segment scripted and storyboarded by Sendak; the later book and 2000–03 series are separate manifestations.
- **1975:** *Really Rosie*. A half-hour animated television musical directed and designed by Sendak, with his book and lyrics and Carole King’s music; it is a Gesamtkunstwerk shared among writing, song, drawing, voice, and animation.
- **1987:** *In the Night Kitchen*. An animated short directed by Gene Deitch from Sendak’s picture book; Sendak’s status is principally source author and visual originator, not sole maker of the film.
- **1995–2003:** *Maurice Sendak’s Little Bear*. An animated television series developed from Minarik and Sendak’s books; Sendak acted as creator/story editor or producer, but the episodes are collective television productions rather than individually authored Sendak works.
- **2000–2003:** *Seven Little Monsters*. An animated television series based on Sendak’s book, with Sendak credited as creator and producer; it should not be collapsed into the earlier film or book.
- **2001:** *The Little Bear Movie*. A feature-length animated manifestation of the television property, with Sendak as producer and source creator.
- **2009:** *Where the Wild Things Are*. Spike Jonze’s feature-film adaptation, for which Sendak served as producer; the film is a collective adaptation, not a film authored by Sendak alone.

### Musical theatre, opera, and ballet Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1978–1980:** *Really Rosie*. Sendak expanded the television musical for the stage, supplying book, lyrics, visual design, and dramatic conception; Carole King composed the music, and the 1980 Off-Broadway staging was directed and choreographed by Patricia Birch.
- **1979–1983:** *Where the Wild Things Are*. An opera by Oliver Knussen with a libretto by Sendak, who also designed the production; the project began before its complete staged manifestation and belongs jointly to composer and librettist-designer.
- **1979–1985:** *Higglety Pigglety Pop! or There Must Be More to Life*. A companion opera by Knussen, with Sendak’s libretto and designs; its long gestation makes “project” as accurate as “opera” for the earlier years.
- **1980:** *The Magic Flute*. Sendak’s first realized opera production as set-and-costume designer, created with director Frank Corsaro for Houston Grand Opera.
- **1981:** *The Cunning Little Vixen*. Sendak designed sets and costumes for New York City Opera’s production of Janáček’s opera and later made additional imagery for its television manifestation.
- **1982:** *The Love for Three Oranges*. Sendak designed Prokofiev’s opera with Frank Corsaro; the stage work generated a related illustrated book.
- **1982/1984:** *The Goose of Cairo*. Sendak designed Mozart’s unfinished opera for the Lyric Opera of Kansas City; sources attach 1982 to the designs and 1984 to the production, so both dates must remain visible.
- **1983:** *Nutcracker*. A Pacific Northwest Ballet production choreographed by Kent Stowell with sets and costumes by Sendak; the designs, ballet, subsequent film, and 1984 book are related but non-identical manifestations.
- **1986:** *Nutcracker: The Motion Picture*. A film of the Stowell–Sendak ballet directed by Carroll Ballard; Sendak’s sets, costumes, and production concept survive through another maker’s film.
- **1987:** *Der Schauspieldirektor / The Impresario*. A Mozart opera project documented by Sendak fantasy sketches; the surviving designs make it an identifiable project even where a fully realized production is uncertain.
- **1990:** *Idomeneo*. Sendak designed sets and costumes for Los Angeles Music Center Opera’s production of Mozart’s opera.
- **1997:** *Hansel and Gretel*. Sendak designed sets and costumes for Houston Grand Opera’s production of Humperdinck’s opera; those designs later became the images in the 2025 King–Sendak book.
- **2003–2006:** *Brundibár* and *Comedy on the Bridge*. A paired stage project developed by Tony Kushner and Sendak: Kushner made the English adaptations, while Sendak devised the production design with scenic designer Kris Stone. The two operas remain separate compositions but were conceived and toured as a theatrical diptych.

### Prints, portfolios, posters, and commercial graphic works

- **1946–1948:** *FAO Schwarz window displays*. Sendak built and painted models and display environments for the toy store; these were temporary commercial installations, mostly untitled and now resistant to reconstruction as a checklist of autonomous works.
- **1970s–2010s:** *New York Is Book Country posters*. A recurring body of festival posters rather than one fixed work; individual sheets have their own dates, but the institutional record describes the continuing commission as a series.
- **1975:** *Fantasy Sketches*. A limited portfolio or artist’s-book object of fantasy drawings; bibliographies sometimes list it among books even though its identity is closer to a print portfolio.
- **1976:** *What the Night Tells Me*. Cover artwork commissioned for James Levine and the Chicago Symphony Orchestra’s RCA recording of Mahler’s Third Symphony; the image is Sendak’s work, not the musical performance.
- **1979:** *Reading Is Fun! International Year of the Child*. A literacy poster: a public graphic manifesto promoting children’s reading rather than a narrative book.
- **1985:** *The Mother Goose Collection*. Sendak contributed one print to a six-artist limited-edition portfolio; the complete portfolio is collective, not solely his obra.
- **1986:** *Posters by Maurice Sendak*. An artist-selected collection of twenty-four favored posters and pictures with a Sendak introduction; it is both a new book-object and a retrospective manifestation of earlier ephemeral graphics.
- **Undated and variously dated:** *Book-fair, library, theatre, opera, museum, charity, and publishing posters*. Sendak produced a large body of commissioned sheets, many gathered in *Posters by Maurice Sendak* and later exhibitions. The body resists honest item-by-item claims of completeness because no published catalogue raisonné assigns stable titles and dates to every poster.

### Drawings, designs, and unrealized projects

- **1940s–2012:** *Independent drawings, watercolors, paintings, cartoons, studies, dummies, and sketchbooks*. These comprise thousands of individual objects, including preliminary states for books and stage works; treating every sheet as a separately titled obra would invent titles and boundaries not supplied by the catalogues.
- **1979–1997:** *Opera and ballet design archive*. More than nine hundred surviving sketches, storyboards, costume studies, finished watercolors, dioramas, and related objects were bequeathed to the Morgan Library & Museum. The archive is simultaneously a body of preparatory works and evidence for collaborative, sometimes ephemeral productions—not one autonomous “book.”
- **1985:** *Return to Oz preliminary artwork*. Sendak made development drawings for Walter Murch’s film, but they were preparatory contributions rather than a realized Sendak film.
- **Unrealized or incompletely documented:** *Stage and book dummies, abandoned stories, and variant projects*. The Sendak archives preserve intentions that never became public books or productions; without a fixed title, date, or scholarly project-level inventory, they resist responsible enumeration and should not be silently promoted into completed works.
