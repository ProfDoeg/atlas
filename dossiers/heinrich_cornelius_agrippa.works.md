## Works by Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa von Nettesheim

### Occult-philosophical projects

- **1510; radically revised 1510–1533:** *De occulta philosophia libri tres*. A three-book synthesis of natural, celestial, and ceremonial magic: first circulated as a youthful manuscript, then more than doubled and reconceived as a project for restoring a learned, Christian “magic”; Book I appeared in 1531 and the complete form in 1533. The surviving 1510 dedication manuscript makes the work’s prolonged, unfinished-then-reopened condition unusually visible. ([Perrone Compagni, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Van der Poel, pp. 17–18](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Before 1516; lost:** *Annotationes super Pimandrum Trismegisti*. An announced body of annotations growing out of Agrippa’s Pavia teaching on the *Pimander*. It apparently never reached print and does not survive; it may have been less a finished obra than the working apparatus for a course and for other writings. ([Van der Poel, pp. 23–24](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Theological and humanist treatises, declamations, and polemics

- **1509; revised before 1529:** *De nobilitate et praecellentia foeminei sexus declamatio*. A humanist declamation arguing for the nobility and superiority of women, initially conceived in connection with Agrippa’s lectures at Dôle and his pursuit of Margaret of Austria’s patronage. Its designation as a “declamation” was added when it was published in the 1529 collected treatises, and scholars dispute whether its rhetoric is paradoxical play, serious theology, or both. ([Van der Poel, pp. 18, 185–224](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/))

- **1510:** *Expostulatio super expositione sua in librum De verbo mirifico cum Ioanne Catilineti*. A polemical letter or remonstrance answering the Franciscan Jean Catilinet’s attack on Agrippa’s Dôle lectures; it acts as a public defense of his methods of scriptural and kabbalistic interpretation. ([Van der Poel, pp. xii, 95–96](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1515–1516; fragmentary:** *Dialogus de homine*. A philosophical-theological dialogue between Agrippa and his pupil Christoph Schilling, digesting material from his Pavia course and examining humanity through Pico’s *Heptaplus*. It resists treatment as a completed work: only a fragment survives, and Vittoria Perrone Compagni argues that it may always have been a provisional draft later cannibalized for other writings. ([Van der Poel, pp. 23–24](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [SEP bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/#Bib))

- **1516; expanded before 1529:** *De triplici ratione cognoscendi Deum*. A six-chapter theological book or *sermo* on three ways of knowing God—through nature, Scripture, and Christ—dedicated to Guglielmo Paleologo, Marquis of Monferrato. Agrippa enlarged it before its first printing in the 1529 collected treatises. ([Van der Poel, pp. 23–24](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/))

- **Probably 1516–1518; revised 1526:** *Dehortatio gentilis theologiae*. The written manifestation of an informal address to friends, warning that enthusiasm for Hermetic or “pagan” theology must not displace Scripture. Its original date is uncertain; Van der Poel places a first version tentatively in Agrippa’s Italian period and records its revision and dedication in 1526. ([Van der Poel, pp. 24–25](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1518:** *De originali peccato disputabilis opinionis declamatio*. A theological declamation advancing the disputable thesis that Adam, rather than Eve, bore primary responsibility for original sin. Its deliberately disputational form has encouraged disagreement over how literally its position should be read. ([Van der Poel, pp. 225–245](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [SEP bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/#Bib))

- **Circa 1518–1520:** *Sermo de vita monastica*. An informal theological address on monastic life, associated with Agrippa’s contacts at the Celestine monastery in Metz; it entered print only in the enlarged 1532 collection of his shorter treatises. ([Van der Poel, pp. 24–25, 42–43](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1518–1520:** *Sermo de inventione reliquiarum beati Antonii eremitae*. An informal address concerning the discovery of relics of Saint Antony the Hermit, likewise preserved through the 1532 collected treatises. It is a sermon-like occasional text, not a systematic book. ([Van der Poel, pp. 24–25, 42–43](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1526:** *De sacramento matrimonii declamatio*. A theological declamation on Christian marriage, dedicated to Margaret of Angoulême; it interprets biblical marriage texts and attacks impediments to remarriage. ([Van der Poel, pp. 39–40, 246–262](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1526:** *Du sacrement de mariage*. Agrippa’s own French translation of *De sacramento matrimonii*, printed with the Latin. It is a distinct vernacular manifestation made by the author, not merely a later translator’s edition. ([Van der Poel, pp. 39–40](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1526; revised before publication in 1530:** *De incertitudine et vanitate scientiarum et artium atque excellentia verbi Dei declamatio invectiva*. An encyclopedic invective or manifesto-like declamation that subjects human arts, sciences, institutions, and learned authority to skeptical criticism before affirming the excellence of the divine Word. The extent to which its 1526 version was complete is uncertain, and Agrippa expanded or reworked portions before printing. ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Van der Poel, pp. 40–41](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1531–1533:** *Apologia adversus calumnias propter declamationem De vanitate scientiarum et excellentia verbi Dei*. A point-by-point forensic defense against the Louvain theologians’ censures of *De incertitudine*, completed substantially before its 1533 publication and slightly revised while awaiting official action. It is a text designed to intervene in an active legal-theological controversy. ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Van der Poel, pp. 116–152](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1531–1533:** *Querela super calumnia ob eandem Declamationem*. A companion complaint accusing Agrippa’s adversaries of secretly defaming him before Charles V. Although printed with the *Apologia*, it is a separate polemical act directed at courtly and political authority. ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Van der Poel, pp. 116–152](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1533:** *Epistola apologetica ad clarissimum urbis Agrippinae Romanorum Coloniae senatum contra insaniam Conradi Coelin de Ulma*. An open defensive letter to Cologne’s senate opposing the Dominican inquisitor Conrad Köllin’s attempt to suppress *De occulta philosophia*. It functions as an appeal and public polemic rather than as a descriptive treatise. ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Van der Poel, p. 129](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1534:** *De beatissimae Annae monogamia ac unico puerperio propositiones abbreviatae et articulatae*. A set of abbreviated propositions supporting Jacques Lefèvre d’Étaples’s position that Saint Anne married once and bore one child. It is an articulated intervention in a controversy, closer to theses for dispute than to a continuous book. ([SEP bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/#Bib); [Van der Poel, pp. xi–xii](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1534:** *Defensio propositionum praenarratarum contra quendam Dominicastrum*. A polemical defense of the Saint Anne propositions against an unnamed Dominican opponent, printed as part of the same controversial dossier. ([Van der Poel, pp. xi–xii, 125–126](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1534:** *Quaedam epistolae super eadem materia atque super lite contra eiusdem ordinis haereticorum magistro habita*. A small documentary assemblage of letters concerning Saint Anne and Agrippa’s struggle with Dominican authorities. As a mixed dossier, it resists being called a wholly authored work: Agrippa made or arranged the collection, but not every letter in it is necessarily his. ([SEP bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/#Bib); [Van der Poel, pp. xi–xii](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1534; attribution disputed:** *Dialogus de vanitate scientiarum et ruina Christianae religionis*. A dialogue issued under the name of an otherwise unknown Cistercian, “Godoschalcus Moncordius,” and possibly written by Agrippa as a disguised self-defense. Paola Zambelli argued for Agrippan authorship, but the attribution remains a hypothesis and the text should not be silently absorbed into his secure oeuvre. ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Zambelli bibliographical record](https://philpapers.org/rec/ZAMCAS))

- **1534; lost:** A book collecting scandalous or heretical passages from Cologne Dominican writers. Agrippa’s correspondence and later scholarship attest the project, but neither a fixed title nor a surviving text is known; its status may have been that of an unfinished polemical dossier. ([Van der Poel, p. 129](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Lectures, ceremonial speeches, and public oratory

- **1509; lost:** An inaugural address at Dôle in praise of Margaret of Austria. Contemporary correspondence attests the performance, but no text or secure original title survives; what Agrippa made here may have been primarily an event of speech rather than a durable text. ([Van der Poel, pp. 18, 104–105](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Before 1515:** *Oratio in praelectionem Convivii Platonis, amoris laudem continens*. An inaugural university lecture introducing a course on Plato’s *Symposium* and praising Neoplatonic love. Its exact date and place are not securely established; its position in the collected orations suggests that it predates the firmly dated Pavia speech of 1515. ([Van der Poel, pp. 22–23](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1515:** *Oratio habita Papiae in praelectione Hermetis Trismegisti, de potestate et sapientia Dei*. An inaugural lecture at Pavia introducing Agrippa’s course on the *Pimander* and presenting Hermes Trismegistus through a Christian theological frame. It is the only one of the ten collected orations supplied with an explicit date. ([Van der Poel, pp. 23, 63–72](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Date uncertain, probably before 1518:** *Oratio pro quodam doctorando*. A ceremonial academic speech composed for a law student receiving his doctorate. The candidate is unidentified, and the piece is occasional proxy speech rather than Agrippa speaking simply in his own person. ([Van der Poel, pp. 25–26](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1517–1518; lost:** An oration in praise of Charles III, Duke of Savoy. A letter of January 1518 asks Agrippa for a copy of this circulated speech, but the work itself has disappeared and its exact title is unknown. ([Van der Poel, pp. 26–27](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1518:** A speech accepting office as Metz’s *advocatus, syndicus et orator* (*Oratio IV* in the 1535 collection). An institutional self-presentation made for oral delivery on entering civic service; no more distinctive title is securely supplied by the scholarly account. ([Van der Poel, pp. 27–28](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1518–1520:** A speech to the senate of Luxembourg (*Oratio V*). A diplomatic civic address delivered on behalf of Metz, and therefore a commissioned act of representation as well as Agrippa’s composition. ([Van der Poel, pp. 27–28](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1518–1520:** A welcoming speech for an unidentified prince-bishop (*Oratio VI*). Its occasion survives more clearly than the recipient’s identity, so the work resists a fully specific title. ([Van der Poel, pp. 27–28](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1518–1520:** A welcoming speech for an unidentified eminent lord (*Oratio VII*). Like the preceding address, it is an occasional diplomatic performance preserved in the later collection but imperfectly recoverable as an independent work. ([Van der Poel, pp. 27–28](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Probably 1528:** A Paris speech written for a Carmelite relative (*Oratio VIII*). The scholarly chronology is explicitly tentative; it appears to have been proxy oratory composed for another speaker. ([Van der Poel, pp. 41–42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **1530:** *Oratio in funere divae Margaretae*. A funeral oration for Margaret of Austria, delivered or composed in Agrippa’s capacity as imperial historiographer and published separately in 1531. It is both memorial rhetoric and an official court production. ([Van der Poel, pp. 41–42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1530:** A welcoming speech to Charles V, composed for John of Denmark (*Oratio X*). This is another proxy text: Agrippa made the speech, but the emperor’s nephew was its intended speaking persona. ([Van der Poel, pp. 41–42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Historiography and official documentary projects

- **1530:** *De duplici coronatione Caroli V Caesaris apud Bononiam historiola*. A short eyewitness-style official history of Charles V’s coronations as king of Lombardy and Holy Roman Emperor at Bologna. It was a product of Agrippa’s office as historiographer, not merely a private chronicle. ([Van der Poel, pp. 41–42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [1535 collection record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb30005285b))

- **Circa 1529–1530; lost:** A history of the Italian war between Charles, Duke of Bourbon, and the French monarchy. Agrippa later listed it among his historiographical labors, but no fixed title or text survives. ([Van der Poel, pp. 41–42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1529–1530; lost:** A dossier of instructions sent to German and Italian princes concerning an expedition against the Turks. This was probably an archival or chancery compilation rather than a conventionally authored book, but Agrippa claimed it among the things he produced as imperial historiographer. ([Van der Poel, pp. 41–42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Before 1530; lost:** A detailed survey of the origins of contemporary royal houses. Its former existence is inferred from Agrippa’s reference to a fuller work behind chapter 80 of *De incertitudine*; neither title nor state of completion is known. ([Van der Poel, p. 111](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Logical and combinatorial works

- **Date uncertain; completed by 1530, printed 1533:** *In artem brevem Raymundi Lullii commentaria*. A substantial commentary and teaching apparatus for Ramon Llull’s combinatorial *Ars brevis*. It is not simply exposition: Agrippa reorganizes Llull’s procedure as a usable method of inquiry and discourse. ([SEP bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/#Bib); [Van der Poel, p. 43](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **By 1530:** *Tabula abbreviata commentariorum in artem brevem Raymundi Lullii*. A condensed table or schematic instrument accompanying the Lullian commentary. It is a protocol-like working device rather than a discursive book, and some editions of Agrippa’s collected works omit it. ([Van der Poel, pp. xi–xii, 43](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Medical protocols and natural investigation

- **1518; printed 1529:** *Regimen adversus pestilentiam* (also transmitted as *Antidotum contra pestem*). A short medical protocol prescribing treatment or protection against epidemic disease. The variation of title reflects variant transmission rather than a securely separate second work. ([Van der Poel, pp. 9–10, 42](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [Marburg study](https://archiv.ub.uni-marburg.de/eb/2018/0038/))

- **Date unknown; lost:** *Pyromachia*. A report or technical project on firearms and engines of war prepared for Francis I. Only references to it survive, so its scope, completeness, and even its status as a finished text cannot now be determined. ([Van der Poel, pp. 9–10](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Date unknown; lost and untitled:** A work on mines, mining, and minerals. It is attested as one of Agrippa’s practical investigations, but scholarship supplies neither a secure title nor enough evidence to decide whether it was a finished treatise, a report, or working notes. ([Van der Poel, pp. 9–10](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Probably before 1518; lost or hidden:** A commentary on Romans 1–6. Agrippa began it after studying Paul with John Colet and left the unfinished manuscript in Pavia; a student reportedly preserved it, but no copy is presently known. It is explicitly an unfinished project rather than a completed obra. ([Van der Poel, pp. 20–21](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1510; lost:** Quodlibetal disputations on contemporary religious practices. These were performances at Cologne’s theological faculty, not necessarily a single written work; nothing survives, although some substance may have entered chapter 56 of *De incertitudine*. ([Van der Poel, pp. 20–21](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Epigrams and occasional poetry

- **Circa 1530:** An epigram praising Charles V. A nine-distich coronation poem preserved in the 1535 *Orationes X* collection; the scholarly description does not supply a separate fixed title. ([Van der Poel, pp. 123–124](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1530:** An epigram praising Bologna. An eighteen-hexameter civic encomium apparently connected with Charles V’s Bologna coronation. ([Van der Poel, pp. 123–124](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1530:** An epigram on a portrait of Charles V. A ten-hexameter ekphrastic poem responding to the emperor’s image. ([Van der Poel, pp. 123–124](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Circa 1530:** An epigram on an equestrian sculpture of Charles V. A two-distich response to a representation of the emperor on horseback. ([Van der Poel, pp. 123–124](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Date uncertain:** An epigram on the death of Mercurinus, a former imperial councillor. A three-distich funerary occasional poem; the subject’s precise identity and the poem’s date remain uncertain in the consulted scholarly account. ([Van der Poel, pp. 123–124](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **Date uncertain:** Two epigrams on the emblem of Lord Rosebaldius. Each consists of two distichs; they are separate emblematic poems but lack securely reported individual titles. ([Van der Poel, pp. 123–124](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

- **After 1516:** Three distichs headed *In libellum de triplici ratione cognoscendi Deum*. A brief paratextual poem attached to the 1529 printing of *De triplici ratione* and *Dehortatio* but omitted from the later *Opera*. Its position makes it part poem, part framing device for another work. ([Van der Poel, pp. 24–25](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Correspondence and composite textual bodies

- **Circa 1507–1533; collected posthumously:** *Epistolarum ad familiares libri VII*. A seven-book correspondence containing Agrippa’s letters alongside letters addressed to him. It is indispensable evidence for his intellectual and practical projects, but as a reciprocally produced archive it resists being called a work authored wholly by Agrippa; its seven-book arrangement is a later editorial construction. ([Van der Poel, pp. xii–xiii](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html); [Opera description in Gurashi](https://brill.com/display/book/9783846766514/front-4.pdf))

- **1524–1535:** *Opuscula* / collected shorter treatises. Agrippa repeatedly pursued the project of gathering his smaller works, realized first in the 1529 Antwerp collection, enlarged in Cologne in 1532, and complemented by the 1535 *Orationes X*. This is best treated as a publication project or changing constellation of works, not as one stable, self-identical text. ([Van der Poel, pp. 42–43](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))

### Disputed and rejected attributions

- **Probably sixteenth century; first printed 1572; attribution disputed:** *De arte chemica*. An alchemical dialogue or instructional treatise printed anonymously, subsequently attributed to Marsilio Ficino, and more recently argued by Sylvain Matton to be Agrippa’s. Matton presents cumulative internal evidence rather than decisive external proof, so it belongs at the edge of Agrippa’s oeuvre, not among the secure works. ([Matton, *De arte chemica* introduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/750970873/Is-Agrippa-the-author-of-the-pseudo-Fic); [SEP bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/#Bib))

- **First printed 1559; spurious:** *De occulta philosophia, liber quartus* / *De caeremoniis magicis*. A practical manual of ritual magic published after Agrippa’s death as the “fourth book” and long transmitted under his name. Its authorship is rejected in modern scholarship; its resistance to being called Agrippa’s work is itself historically important, because it profoundly reshaped the reception of what “Agrippan magic” was thought to be. ([SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/); [Source Library attribution](https://sourcelibrary.org/collections/index-librorum-prohibitorum/catalog))

- **First printed with pseudo-Agrippan magical materials; spurious:** *Geomantia* / *Of Geomancy*. A practical geomantic protocol often presented as Agrippa’s and sometimes attached to the pseudo-*Fourth Book*. Agrippa discussed geomancy in the authentic *De occulta philosophia*, but the independent manual is not accepted as his work. ([Divinity Atlas](https://www.divinityatlas.com/articles/carcer-agrippa-cattan-and-fludd-the-books-it-came-through); [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/agrippa-nettesheim/))

- **Posthumous collected editions; not Agrippa’s creations:** *Ars notoria*, *Heptameron*, and other magical appendices printed in editions of Agrippa’s *Opera*. Their physical association with his writings produced a misleading composite Gesamtkunstwerk of “Agrippan” magic, but the standard scholarly bibliography explicitly identifies such appendices as works by other authors. ([Van der Poel, pp. xi–xii](https://dokumen.pub/cornelius-agrippa-the-humanist-theologian-and-his-declamations-9004107568-9789004107564.html))
