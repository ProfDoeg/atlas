## Works by Harry Houdini

### Books, manuals, and exposés

- **1900–1922:** *The Adventurous Life of a Versatile Artist: Houdini*. An evolving autobiographical publicity book—revised, retitled, and translated repeatedly rather than a text with one stable edition or date. Houdini’s own later version claimed circulation from 1900 and listed several projected or variant titles, so its bibliographic boundaries remain porous. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm))

- **1906:** *The Right Way to Do Wrong: An Exposé of Successful Criminals*. A popular criminological exposé describing criminal methods as cautionary instruction; its protocol-like explanations sit uneasily beside its professed purpose of prevention. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1906 onward:** *Life, History and Handcuff Secrets of Houdini*. A pitchbook combining autobiography, advertising, press testimony, and escape instruction; numerous revised forms survive, making it a mutable promotional obra rather than a single fixed edition. ([Houdini Pitchbook Project](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/2011/06/pitchbook-project.html), [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm))

- **1908:** *The Unmasking of Robert-Houdin*. A polemical history intended to displace Robert-Houdin from his customary position in magic history; both historical argument and manifesto of Houdini’s own lineage. The surviving research notes do not always reveal which material reached the book. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1910:** *Handcuff Secrets*. An illustrated escape manual and protocol for understanding or defeating restraints. Some bibliographies date an earlier form to 1907, while the standard book-form citation is London, 1910. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1920–1921:** *Miracle Mongers and Their Methods*. A historical exposé of fire-eaters, poison-defiers, sword-swallowers, and related performers. The work appeared in serialized or preliminary form before the commonly cited 1921 Dutton edition, accounting for conflicting 1920/1921 dates. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html), [Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1921:** *Magical Rope Ties and Escapes*. A practical protocol for rope escapes and restraint effects, published through Will Goldston; sources also assign it 1920 when referring to its preparation or earlier issue. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1922:** *Houdini’s Paper Magic*. A manual of paper-folding, tearing, restoration, and stage effects; Houdini’s earlier publicity called the project *Paper Prestidigitation*, but that should not automatically be counted as a separate completed book. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html), [The Adventurous Life](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm))

- **1923:** *Elliott’s Last Legacy*. Houdini edited this posthumous collection of James William Elliott’s magic, rather than originating all the tricks or text; it is therefore an editorial obra with divided authorship. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1924:** *A Magician Among the Spirits*. A book-length anti-spiritualist investigation combining history, autobiography, documentation, and exposé. Its collaborative research and editorial assistance complicate solitary authorship, but contemporary and archival bibliographies treat it as Houdini’s book. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275), [Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html))

- **1925:** *Houdini Exposes the Tricks Used by the Boston Medium “Margery”*. A polemical pamphlet documenting and attempting to act against Mina Crandon’s mediumship claims; closer to an evidentiary manifesto than a neutral description. Some lists give 1924, reflecting the investigation and preliminary circulation, while the standard Adams Press citation is 1925. ([Houdini bibliography](https://www.wildabouthoudini.com/p/bibliography.html))

### Periodical projects and shorter texts

- **1906–1908:** *Conjurers’ Monthly Magazine*. A 24-issue periodical project founded, published, and edited by Houdini, who also supplied articles and material from his collection. It is a collective textual space rather than a wholly Houdini-authored book. ([Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/item/08004682/), [Conjuring Arts index](https://askalexander.org/browse/13/Conjurer%27s%2BMonthly%2BMagazine))

- **1907:** *Dan Cupid—Magician*. A short story published in the *Buffalo Evening News* on 1 November; Houdini later advertised “Dan Cupid the Magician” as a series, but the evidence does not justify treating every proposed installment as completed. ([Harry Ransom Center chronology](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275), [The Adventurous Life](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm))

- **1924:** *The Spirit-Fakirs of Hermannstadt*. A *Weird Tales* story issued under Houdini’s name but ghostwritten; it belongs to his commissioned public persona, while resistance to calling it his authored work is essential bibliographic information. ([Lovecraft collaborations collection](https://pulp-lit.com/assets-bundles/34-HPLcollab/348.2.Lovecraft-collab.1803b.pdf))

- **1924:** *The Hoax of the Spirit-Lover*. Another *Weird Tales* story presented as Houdini’s but ghostwritten. It is best classified as an attributed manifestation of the Houdini persona, not securely as prose composed by him. ([Lovecraft collaborations collection](https://pulp-lit.com/assets-bundles/34-HPLcollab/348.2.Lovecraft-collab.1803b.pdf))

- **1924:** *Imprisoned with the Pharaohs* (also *Under the Pyramids* and *Entombed with the Pharaohs*). A fantastic first-person story commissioned for Houdini and published under his name, but substantially written by H. P. Lovecraft from Houdini’s purported Egyptian experience; sources variously call it ghostwriting or collaboration. ([Sotheby’s manuscript description](https://www.sothebys.com/en/auctions/ecatalogue/2006/fine-books-and-manuscripts-including-a-private-collection-of-historical-hawaiiana-n08251/lot.68.html), [Lovecraft bibliography](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/54/Joseph_Payne_Brennan_-_H._P._Lovecraft-A_Bibliography.pdf))

- **1924:** *Ask Houdini*. A short-lived *Weird Tales* advice or correspondence column constructed around Houdini’s authority. Because editorial and ghostwriting mediation is substantial, it resists classification as a body of texts written solely by Houdini. ([Lovecraft collaborations collection](https://pulp-lit.com/assets-bundles/34-HPLcollab/348.2.Lovecraft-collab.1803b.pdf))

- **1925:** *How I Unmask the Spirit Fakers*. A *Popular Science* article explaining Houdini’s investigative methods; an exposé whose purpose was intervention in the public contest over spiritualism. ([Published-works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Houdini#Publications))

- **1925:** *How I Do My “Spirit Tricks”*. A *Popular Science* article demonstrating simulated mediumistic phenomena as manufactured stage effects—a protocol used to undermine supernatural claims. ([Published-works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Houdini#Publications))

- **1926:** *Conjuring*. Houdini’s signed contribution to the thirteenth edition of the *Encyclopædia Britannica*, a compact reference article rather than a freestanding book. ([Published-works list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Houdini#Publications))

- **1926, unfinished:** *The Cancer of Superstition*. A commissioned book project initiated by Houdini with H. P. Lovecraft and C. M. Eddy Jr.; an incomplete 31-leaf typescript survives with missing pages. Scholars dispute how much was written by Lovecraft or Eddy, so the defensible Houdini work is the directing project, not sole authorship of the surviving prose. ([Potter & Potter catalogue](https://potterauctions.com/pdf/Catalog_037_web.pdf), [H. P. Lovecraft Archive](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/science/))

- **circa 1894–1926:** *Magic, magician, spiritualism, and biographical research notes*. Thousands of manuscript notes, interviews, fragments, and unidentified drafts survive; many cannot be connected securely to a finished publication. This is an archival body of work, but its resistance to item-by-item authorship and titling means it cannot honestly be converted into invented “works.” ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

### Dramatic protocols for illusions

- **1911:** *Challenged; or, Houdini Upside Down*. A sixteen-page “magical dramatic playlet” created to register the Water Torture Cell performance as drama. It is simultaneously script, legal instrument, stage protocol, apparatus-concealing strategy, and performance—a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a literary play. ([U.S. Copyright Office/Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2021/03/houdini-and-the-magic-of-copyright/))

- **1914:** *Walking Through a Brick Wall*. A two-scene playlet enclosing the wall illusion within a romantic plot and recording its stage method. Its identity as drama partly arose from the need to protect an illusion that ordinary copyright did not comfortably accommodate. ([U.S. Copyright Office/Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2021/03/houdini-and-the-magic-of-copyright/))

- **1914:** *Buried Alive*. A copyrighted playlet in which a bound tourist is boxed, buried, and revealed free. Script, illusion, apparatus, publicity, and enacted bodily risk are inseparable, making it another Gesamtkunstwerk-like performance protocol. ([U.S. Copyright Office/Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2021/03/houdini-and-the-magic-of-copyright/))

### Moving-image works and film projects

- **1918–1919:** *The Master Mystery*. A fifteen-part motion-picture serial starring Houdini; the surviving papers include scripts for fourteen episodes and title cards. Houdini contracted to perform rather than originating the entire serial, so this is part of his performed oeuvre but not securely a film he authored or produced. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1919:** *The Grim Game*. A feature film starring Houdini. Houdini later claimed story collaboration with Arthur B. Reeve and John W. Grey, but the archival finding aid states that his production role was limited to acting; the authorship claim should therefore remain disputed. ([The Adventurous Life](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm), [Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1920:** *Terror Island*. A feature film starring Houdini. As with *The Grim Game*, Houdini’s publicity claimed collaborative story authorship, whereas the Ransom Center describes him as acting only; it is unquestionably a performance but not unquestionably his authored film. ([The Adventurous Life](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm), [Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1921–1922:** *The Man from Beyond*. A feature written, produced, and acted in by Houdini through the Houdini Picture Corporation. Film, star vehicle, production venture, and self-mythologizing spectacle converge here; sources variously date it by completion, copyright, or release. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275), [The Adventurous Life](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm))

- **1923:** *Haldane of the Secret Service*. A feature written, produced, and acted in by Houdini through his corporation. Like *The Man from Beyond*, it is a genuinely authored cinematic obra rather than merely a film appearance. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **circa 1923–1924:** *Il Mistero di Osiris / The Mystery of the Jewel (Talisman)*, also developed as *Ashes of Passion* and *Reincarnation*. An unrealized or incompletely realized American adaptation project undertaken by Houdini’s Mystery Pictures Corporation from an Italian film and an Agnes Fletcher Baine play. Its shifting titles, translations, scripts, and unresolved production status make the project—not a single definitive film—the work. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1921–1925:** *Film Developing Corporation*. A technical and commercial project established by Houdini and investors to develop motion-picture film. It produced corporate records and processes rather than a securely catalogued creative title, and therefore resists being called an artwork even though it was something Houdini made and attempted to operate. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

### Sound recording

- **1914:** *Houdini on His Water Torture Cell* / Edison machine-rehearsal cylinders. Houdini recorded spoken rehearsals on wax cylinders in Flatbush on 29 October; these are the only known recordings of his voice. The surviving audio is a set of working recordings rather than a commercially titled release, so modern titles are descriptive manifestations, not necessarily Houdini’s titles. ([Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/collection/houdini-on-his-water-torture-cell-1914), [Wikimedia Commons record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AHarry_Houdini%27s_voice.ogg))

### Invention and apparatus

- **1917–1921:** *Diver’s Suit* (U.S. Patent 1,370,316). A quick-release deep-sea diving suit designed so that a submerged wearer could escape the apparatus without assistance; filed in 1917 and patented in 1921. It is an engineered object and protocol of bodily escape, not a text except in its patent manifestation. ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1370316A/en))

- **circa 1890s–1926:** *Houdini’s escape and illusion apparatus*. Handcuffs, restraints, trunks, cells, cabinets, and other devices formed a large practical oeuvre, but much was adapted, commissioned, purchased, kept secret, or destroyed rather than individually signed and catalogued. The Ransom Center warns that even surviving apparatus in its archive has uncertain provenance; assigning every object to Houdini as maker would therefore manufacture attribution. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275), [U.S. Copyright Office/Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2021/03/houdini-and-the-magic-of-copyright/))

### Editorial and institutional creations

- **1920s:** *Red Magic*. A children’s Sunday supplement of tricks, jokes, riddles, and optical illusions published by the *New York World* with Houdini named as editor. The archive cautions that he probably had little direct role in selecting its contents, so it is an attributed editorial manifestation rather than confidently his made text. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275))

- **1921–1923:** *Houdini Picture Corporation*. Houdini’s film-production organization, responsible for *The Man from Beyond* and *Haldane of the Secret Service*. A company is not ordinarily called a work, but here it was an enabling project through which Houdini joined authorship, production, performance, and publicity. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://research.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingAid.cfm?eadid=01275), [The Adventurous Life](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/45370/45370-h/45370-h.htm))
