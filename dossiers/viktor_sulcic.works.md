## Works by Viktor Sulčič

### Built architecture and architectural obra

- **1923–1924:** *Adaptation of Ivan Meštrović’s house and studio, Mletačka Street, Zagreb*. Architectural drawings made as a collaborator in Harold Bilinić’s—elsewhere called Viktor Kovačić/Lubinski’s—office; the surviving accounts disagree over the supervising architect, so Sulčič’s contribution should be treated as collaborative rather than sole authorship. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue); [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **c. 1928:** *Entrance to the Municipal Cemetery of Luján*. A monumental classical gateway, attributed to the Delpini–Sulčič–Bes practice; the principal scholarly accounts name it but do not agree on or document a precise completion date. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/); [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue)

- **1929–1935:** *Mercado de Abasto Proveedor, Buenos Aires*. Market building and unfinished Gesamtkunstwerk of architectural space, engineering, daylight and circulation, developed with José Luis Delpini and Raúl Bes from Sulčič’s initial sketch; two sections were built, while the intended third remained a project. Sources variously date its design and construction to 1929–34, 1931–35 or 1932–36, and the later shopping-centre conversion substantially altered its interior. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue); [Moderna Buenos Aires](https://w.modernabuenosaires.org/obras/20s-a-70s/ex-mercado-de-abasto-proveedor); [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1929:** *Apartment building at Venezuela and Piedras, Buenos Aires*. An eclectic residential building attributed to the Delpini–Sulčič–Bes practice; published inventories identify it by its street corner rather than by a fixed title. [Buenos Aires official bulletin](https://documentosboletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/publico/20090922.pdf); [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **c. 1929–1930s:** *Apartment building on Avenida Jujuy between Hipólito Yrigoyen and Alsina, Buenos Aires*. A residential block with commercial premises, attributed to the partnership; its precise date and Sulčič’s individual share are not established in the consulted catalogue. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1932:** *Asociación Cultural and Biblioteca Popular Florentino Ameghino, Luján*. Renaissance-inflected library and cultural building designed and directed by Delpini, Sulčič and Bes; it is a collective obra rather than securely separable individual authorship. [Universidad Nacional de Luján study](https://ri.unlu.edu.ar/xmlui/bitstream/handle/rediunlu/1395/Historia-y-periodizaci%C3%B3n-de-la-salud-mental-y-la-psicolog%C3%ADa-pre-profesional-en-Luj%C3%A1n.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1); [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1932–1938:** *Reconstruction of the cold-storage buildings adjoining the Mercado de Abasto*. An industrial-architecture commission designed by the partnership in 1932 and reportedly built in 1938; the standard biography records it separately from the market proper. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1932–1940:** *Estadio Alberto J. Armando (“La Bombonera”), Club Atlético Boca Juniors*. A football stadium—neither a text nor merely a sculptural object—designed with Delpini and Bes after their 1932 competition victory, begun in 1938 and inaugurated in 1940. Its steep reinforced-concrete structure was Delpini’s engineering solution and Sulčič’s architectural project; the built stadium was only a partial realization of a larger intention and was subsequently altered. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue); [Buenos Aires Legislature](https://parlamentaria.legislatura.gob.ar/pages/download.aspx?IdDoc=216778)

- **1930s:** *Mercado Vélez Sársfield, Buenos Aires*. A neighbourhood market at Rivadavia and Chivilcoy attributed to Delpini–Sulčič–Bes; the consulted scholarly inventories name it but supply neither a secure design date nor a division of authorship. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1930s–1940s:** *Hogar de Ancianos of the Hospital Italiano, San Justo*. A complex of pavilions, service buildings and a sisters’ residence produced by Delpini–Sulčič–Bes; because the parts are incompletely inventoried, it resists reduction to a single building or subdivision into a reliable list of individual works. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D); [Universidad Nacional de La Matanza study](https://institutodehistoria.unlam.edu.ar/upload/adjuntos/articulo/89_Manifestacionesculturales.pdf)

- **1939–early 1940s:** *Church of the Sacred Heart of Jesus, Hospital Italiano, San Justo*. A reinforced-concrete church that transforms neo-Gothic ribs, vaults, light and liturgical furnishings into one architectural manifestation; Sulčič is credited especially with its architectural conception, although a later specialist study argues for Delpini’s authorship within the shared office. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D); [specialist attribution study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/326260794_LA_CAPILLA_DEL_HOSPITAL_ITALIANO_EN_LA_MATANZA_OBRA_RELEVANTE_DE_ARQUITECTURA_DEL_INGENIERO_DELPINI_THE_CHAPEL_IN_THE_HOSPITAL_ITALIANO_IN_LA_MATANZA_RELEVANT_ARCHITECTURAL_WORK_OF_ENGINEER_DELPINI)

- **1939–1940:** *Hogar Yugoslavo, Dock Sud*. Community centre commissioned through Yugoslav ambassador Izidor Cankar and realized by Sulčič, himself a member of its organizing committee; an official provincial history dates the institution’s establishment to 1940. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D); [Buenos Aires Chamber of Deputies](https://intranet.hcdiputados-ba.gov.ar/asuntos/asuntos13619.pdf)

- **Date unknown:** *Monumental tombs in Argentina*. The standard biographical inventory attributes several funerary monuments to Sulčič but supplies no titles, locations, dates or count; they therefore remain an incompletely identified body of obra rather than safely enumerable individual works. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **Date unknown:** *Residential, industrial and hospital buildings and interiors*. The standard inventory records numerous houses, apartment blocks, additions to the Hospital Italiano and domestic interiors, including seven-, six- and four-storey buildings, but does not identify each object. Treating this anonymous corpus as “everything” would be misleading: its existence is documented, its contents are not catalogued completely. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/); [Svobodna Slovenija](https://svobodnaslovenija.com.ar/arh-viktor-sulcic-1895-1973/)

### Unbuilt and incompletely realized projects

- **1926:** *Municipal Palace of Bragado*. Competition project made with engineer Luis V. Migone and awarded second prize; the sources do not report that it was built. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D)

- **1926:** *Improbus*. One of two anonymous competition schemes for the Banco Hipotecario Nacional headquarters, initially developed by Sulčič and Raúl Bes and formally signed by José Luis Delpini because their qualifications were not recognized in Argentina; it was not the winning variant and remained a project. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D); [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1926–1928:** *Icatú: Banco Hipotecario Nacional headquarters*. The first-prize competition project by Delpini, Sulčič and Bes, fully developed but cancelled after the 1928 change of government. Its unbuilt status is essential: the project—the intention embodied in drawings—is the obra. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue); [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/)

- **1939:** *Secondary school for the Slovenian Franciscan Sisters, Formosa*. Architectural plans whose realization is not established by the consulted sources; it should therefore be listed as a project, not silently converted into a building. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D)

- **1944–after:** *Demountable, earthquake-resistant and fire-resistant houses for San Juan*. A repeatable emergency-housing system prompted by the San Juan earthquake, published by the Asociación Sarmientina and presented or donated to public-works minister Joaquín Sauri. It is simultaneously project and protocol—a design intended to instruct reconstruction—and the sources do not establish that examples were built. [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8Di%C4%8D); [Giovannardi](https://jannaludlow.co.uk/Assets_pdf_files/Viktor_Sulcic_architetto_pittore_e_poeta.pdf)

### Drawings, studies and graphic obra

- **1919–1922:** *Historical-architecture study drawings*. A surviving corpus of 54 small pen-and-pencil studies, extending from Egyptian architecture onward; it is pedagogical work rather than an autonomous “book,” and the catalogue does not give individual titles. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue)

- **1920–1922:** *Italian Renaissance and travel sketchbooks*. A surviving group of 46 mostly on-site pencil drawings of buildings, sculpture and architectural details in Florence, Tuscany, Rome, Trento and Venice; some sheets are dated 1920–21 or 1922, but the catalogue does not publish a complete title-by-title checklist. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue)

- **Buenos Aires period, dates unknown:** *Figure, caricature and newspaper-illustration drawings*. Twenty-seven surviving pencil or ink figure studies, some made as illustrations for stories in Slovene-Argentine newspapers. Their boundary as “works” is unstable: the archive counts sheets, while some were components of other texts rather than independent obra. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue)

- **1965:** *Design and illustrations for Luces y sombras*. Sulčič designed and illustrated his own poetry collection, making the volume a modest Gesamtkunstwerk of poems, drawing and typography rather than text alone. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/); [Svobodna Slovenija](https://svobodnaslovenija.com.ar/arh-viktor-sulcic-1895-1973/)

### Watercolours and paintings

- **Late 1920s–1973:** *South American landscape watercolours*. A substantial body of landscapes—especially mountains and scenes encountered across Argentina and southern Chile—shown from 1929 onward and now divided chiefly between the Pilon Gallery and the Nova Gorica museum. No consulted catalogue supplies a complete title-level inventory, so the corpus cannot honestly be expanded into “one bullet per painting.” [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/); [Giovannardi](https://jannaludlow.co.uk/Assets_pdf_files/Viktor_Sulcic_architetto_pittore_e_poeta.pdf)

- **1952:** *Patio*. A titled and dated courtyard watercolour reproduced in the biographical study; it is one of the few paintings individually identifiable in the accessible scholarly material. [Giovannardi](https://jannaludlow.co.uk/Assets_pdf_files/Viktor_Sulcic_architetto_pittore_e_poeta.pdf)

### Periodical protocol and cultural writing

- **1933–1934:** *Priseljenčeva hiša / La casa del inmigrante*. A series of articles in the Slovene-language weekly *Novi list*, combining practical advice, plans and drawings for immigrant family houses. It functions less as descriptive literature than as a protocol for making and inhabiting a home. [Zveza slovenskih kulturnih društev](https://www.zskd.eu/2024/05/31/viktor-sulcic-argentinski-arquitecto-esloveno-slovenski-klub-in-nsk); [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8D%C4%8D)

- **Dates unknown:** *Stories and illustrations in Slovene-Argentine newspapers*. The exhibition catalogue documents drawings prepared for such stories but does not provide a dependable bibliography of their titles, dates or publication venues. This is evidence of a body of work, but it resists itemization as discrete, securely named texts. [Viktor Sulčič: arhitekt–arquitecto](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389039050_Viktor_Sulcic_arhitekt-arquitecto_1895-1973_exhibition_catalogue)

### Books

- **1965:** *Luces y sombras*. A self-published, self-illustrated poetry collection, predominantly in Spanish but including three poems in Slovene. The standard biographies and exhibition catalogue give 1965, although a later cultural notice gives 1968; 1965 is retained here with that discrepancy recorded. [Slovenska biografija](https://www.slovenska-biografija.si/oseba/sbi629661/); [Obrazi slovenskih pokrajin](https://www.obrazislovenskihpokrajin.si/oseba/viktor-sulcic/); [Zveza slovenskih kulturnih društev](https://www.zskd.eu/2024/05/31/viktor-sulcic-argentinski-arquitecto-esloveno-slovenski-klub-in-nsk)

- **1968:** *La olla*. A self-published collection of short prose narratives drawing on homeland and immigrant experience; it is a story collection, not a novel. [Obrazi slovenskih pokrajin](https://www.obrazislovenskihpokrajin.si/oseba/viktor-sulcic/); [Svobodna Slovenija](https://svobodnaslovenija.com.ar/arh-viktor-sulcic-1895-1973/)

- **1970:** *Juan Benigar, el sabio que murió sentado*. A biographical memoir of the Slovene-born engineer and anthropologist Juan/Ivan Benigar, grounded in Sulčič’s 1943 visit to him at Aluminé. Some sources shorten the title to *El sabio que murió sentado*; the fuller form is used by the scholarly biography. [Obrazi slovenskih pokrajin](https://www.obrazislovenskihpokrajin.si/oseba/viktor-sulcic/); [Mislej and Cigoj](https://studylib.es/doc/224317/curriculum-del-arquitecto-viktor-su%C4%8D%C4%8D)
