## Works by Felipe Guamán Poma de Ayala

### Gesamtkunstwerk: illustrated chronicle, political memorial, and reform project

- **c. 1600–1615/1616:** *El primer nueva corónica y buen gobierno*. Guaman Poma’s only known independent obra is an autograph manuscript—chronicle, visual history, Christian moral argument, royal petition, and manifesto for colonial reform—combining Spanish and Quechua with roughly 400 full-page pen drawings across about 1,200 pages. Its composition developed over many years; the conventional 1615/1616 date reflects completion and dispatch rather than a securely dated beginning, while late-twentieth-century claims that Jesuits or Blas Valera authored it rested partly on the disputed Miccinelli documents, now widely treated as modern falsifications. ([Royal Danish Library](https://www.kb.dk/en/find-materials/collections/manuscript-collection/chronicle-guaman-poma); [González Díaz](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?lng=es&nrm=iso&pid=S0717-88322025000100209&script=sci_arttext&tlng=es))

### Collaborative manuscript illustration

- **c. 1596/1597–early seventeenth century:** *Drawings and annotations for Martín de Murúa’s Historia del origen y genealogía real de los reyes Incas del Pirú* (the Galvin Murúa). This is a collaborative graphic corpus rather than Guaman Poma’s book: scholarship attributes most of the manuscript’s 112 illustrations—“almost one hundred,” rather than an uncontested exact total—to him, often supported by captions or annotations in his hand; other pictures belong to unidentified artists, and later rearrangement and pasted-in leaves make the corpus resist division into a definitive list of separate works. ([Boserup](https://doi.org/10.18800/9786123172046.002); [Getty Conservation Institute](https://www.getty.edu/conservation/our_projects/science/coll_res/murua_manuscripts.pdf))

- **c. 1596/1597–1613:** *Three drawings now in Martín de Murúa’s Historia general del Piru* (the Getty Murúa). These are surviving or transferred components of the Murúa collaboration, not authorship of Murúa’s textual obra: three drawings in the later manuscript are attributed to Guaman Poma, at least some having been removed from the earlier Galvin manuscript and pasted into this one. Their present location therefore should not be mistaken for a separate commission securely datable to 1613. ([Boserup](https://doi.org/10.18800/9786123172046.002); [Getty Conservation Institute](https://www.getty.edu/conservation/our_projects/science/coll_res/murua_manuscripts.pdf))

### Epistolary text

- **14 February 1615:** *Carta de Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala al rey Felipe III*. A signed letter sent from Santiago de Chipao announcing that his chronicle or general history had been completed and seeking its presentation to the king; it is an autonomous archival text but also functions as a protocol of transmission for the larger manuscript project. The original survives in Archivo General de Indias, Audiencia de Lima 145. ([UNESCO nomination dossier](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mow001/el_primer_nueva_coronica_y_buen_gobierno_nomination_form.pdf); [digital facsimile record](https://www.fromthepage.com/alejo-rojas/nueva-cronica/carta-1615))

### Legal and cartographic interventions that resist classification as works

- **1590s, especially 1597–1600:** *Petitions and three land drawings preserved as copies in the Expediente Prado Tello*. Guaman Poma appears as a litigant in this composite dossier concerning Chupas lands; scholarship identifies a petition and three drawings as copies of materials originally produced by him, but the extant expediente also contains documents made by notaries, opponents, and later copyists. It is therefore evidence of his legal-textual and graphic making, not securely a single authored obra, and the lost originals cannot be catalogued as intact surviving works. ([Adorno’s digital research corpus, described by Hurtado Ames](https://shial.colmex.mx/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Carlos-H.-Hurtado-Ames.pdf); [González Díaz](https://www.scielo.cl/scielo.php?lng=es&nrm=iso&pid=S0717-88322025000100209&script=sci_arttext&tlng=es))

- **20 November 1595:** *Ratification in the Quinua land-sale record*. Guaman Poma signed or ratified this administrative document while serving as interpreter and witness; because the record was drawn up by another official, it is better understood as an authenticated intervention than as a text authored by Guaman Poma. ([Pereyra, “Un documento sobre Guaman Poma de Ayala”](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/historica/article/download/8504/8849/33592); [“Texto y contexto en la Nueva corónica”](https://revistaletras.unmsm.edu.pe/index.php/le/en/article/download/813/719/2273?inline=1))
