## Works by Vera Skoronel

### Choreographic works

- **1924:** *Das böse Quadrat* (also *Das Quadrat*). An abstract solo choreography first documented at Oberhausen; both titles refer to the same work, not two separate dances.
- **1924:** *Nenia*. A solo dance presented in the Oberhausen/Hamborn/Gladbeck theatre programme; the surviving programme evidence does not establish whether Skoronel also composed its music.
- **1924:** *Kriegerrhythmus*. A group choreography premiered at the Stadttheater Oberhausen; it subsequently appeared alongside *Nenia*.
- **1925:** *Schwebung*. A solo or movement study known principally through a photographed pose; it resists identification as a fully recoverable choreography because the surviving image records a moment rather than the dance’s complete temporal form.
- **1925 or earlier:** *Variationen über ein maschinelles Thema*. A choreography identified in a contemporary programme as part of a *Tanztrilogie* and performed there by Berthe Trümpy. The programme does not securely identify the other parts of the trilogy.
- **1926:** *Tanzspiel*. An ambitious group choreography with a detailed written scenario and specially composed accompaniment; the work joined bodies, costume, music, and spatial design and therefore approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a separable “text.”
- **1927:** *Erweckung des Menschen* (also reported as *Erweckung der Masse* or *Erweckung der Massen*). A large-scale movement-choir choreography associated with the Essen Dance Congress and subsequently with a Berlin Volksbühne matinée; surviving movement-notation leaves make it both a performed event and an incomplete textual trace. The fluctuating singular/plural titles and the variant *des Menschen* should not be silently normalized.
- **1928:** *Tänze des wilden Gottes*. A cycle of solo dances; at least one component was titled *Frohlackend*. The evidence establishes a cycle but does not provide a secure complete list of its constituent dances.
- **1928:** *Heroische Suite*. A choreographic suite performed at Skoronel’s Volksbühne matinée.
- **1928:** *Skizze*. A dance work or study presented at the same matinée; its generic title makes it difficult to distinguish a deliberately provisional “sketch” from a finished repertory piece.
- **1928:** *Mächtig*. A dance presented at the Volksbühne matinée.
- **1928:** *Sprungtanz*. A dance presented at the Volksbühne matinée.
- **1928:** *Tanzlied*. A dance presented at the Volksbühne matinée. It should not be confused with Gret Palucca’s work bearing the same title.
- **1928:** *Tanz der Demut*. A dance presented at the Volksbühne matinée.
- **1928:** *Fandango*. A dance presented at the Volksbühne matinée; the title names a dance type as well as Skoronel’s particular choreography.
- **1928:** *Phantasie über althebräische Weisen*. A solo-and-group choreography in which Skoronel’s solo material was taken up and transformed by her chamber group.
- **1929 or earlier:** *Schwertlied*. A solo choreography distinguished in contemporary criticism by forceful, sharply articulated arm movement.
- **1929 or earlier:** *Die Formel*. A solo choreography described by contemporary criticism as formally closed and hieratic.
- **1929 or earlier:** *Zarte Melodie*. A solo dance documented in period repertory listings.
- **1929 or earlier:** *Fernes Lied*. A solo dance documented in period repertory listings.
- **1929:** *Gehämmerte Rhythmen*. A choreography performed at a dance evening in Berlin’s Bachsaal; its exact scoring and cast remain unclear in the accessible documentation.
- **1931:** *Auftakt*. A dance made for Skoronel and the Trümpy School master class.
- **1931:** *Rigaudon*. A choreography named for the historical dance form and performed by Skoronel’s school ensemble.
- **1931:** *Burleske*. A choreography performed by Skoronel and the Trümpy School master class.
- **1932:** *Drei Studien*. A solo programme comprising three studies. The accessible programme summary does not securely supply individual titles, so the studies should not be converted into three invented works.
- **Undated:** *Magischer Raumtanz*. A spatial group choreography preserved in Skoronel’s repertory and performed by the Trümpy group; the available evidence does not provide a secure creation date.
- **Undated; possibly unfinished or posthumously realized:** *Traum*. A choreography performed by the Trümpy group “after a manuscript” by Skoronel. It resists treatment as an unequivocally completed Skoronel work because the surviving manifestation may have been assembled or staged by others from her project.
- **Undated:** *Getragen* (from *Themen der Vierheit*). A dance identified as one component of a larger four-part conception; neither the completeness nor the full contents of *Themen der Vierheit* are securely established in the accessible record.
- **Undated:** *Rittertanz*. A group dance in the Skoronel–Trümpy repertory.
- **Undated:** *Marsch im formalen Stil*. A group choreography embodying Skoronel’s deliberately formal, abstract approach.
- **Undated:** *Spanische Suite*. A choreographic suite in the Skoronel–Trümpy repertory.
- **Undated:** *Die Luftnummer*. A dance in the Skoronel–Trümpy repertory; the title may be comic or ironic and should not be taken as evidence that the work was literally an aerial act.

### Collaborative stage works

- **1927:** *Der gespaltene Mensch*. A spoken-and-movement-choir work by Bruno Schönlank, choreographed or movement-directed collaboratively by Skoronel and Berthe Trümpy with speech teacher Karl Vogt for the Berlin Volksbühne. Its divided authorship and fusion of speech, massed bodies, rhythm, and theatrical action make it a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than a wholly authored Skoronel choreography.

### Choreographic method and pedagogical project

- **1926–1932:** *Abstrakter Tanz*. Skoronel’s evolving movement technique and compositional project, based on independent and counter-rhythmic action of different body parts, angular or “machine-like” movement, facial articulation, and polyphonic group construction. It is an obra in the sense of a made body of practice, but it resists being called a single work: it existed through teaching, performing bodies, choreography, and theoretical writing.
- **1926–1932:** *Skoronel–Trümpy school practice*. The professional training system developed through Skoronel’s collaboration with Berthe Trümpy’s Berlin school. Authorship cannot be cleanly divided: Skoronel contributed technique, choreography, ensemble work, and compositional principles, while Trümpy directed the institution and shared its pedagogy.
- **Late 1920s–1932:** *Tanzgruppe Vera Skoronel / Skoronel–Trümpy chamber group*. A performing collective and continuing choreographic project rather than one bounded artwork. Its repertory, personnel, and names changed, so the group should not be mistaken for a single stable “work.”

### Published writings

- **1929:** *Meine tänzerische Arbeit und Stellungnahme zur Tanzentwicklung*. A programmatic essay published in Liesel Freund’s *Monographien der Ausbildungsschulen für Tanz und tänzerische Körperbildung*, vol. 1, pp. 73–74. It functions partly as manifesto: Skoronel defines her formal, polyphonic conception of dance rather than merely reporting on her career.
- **1929:** *Laban*. A short critical or commemorative essay published in issue 14 of *Singchor und Tanz* for Rudolf von Laban’s fiftieth birthday.
- **1930:** *Mary Wigmans Führertum*. A critical essay published in *Deutsche Tanz-Gemeinschaft*, vol. 2, no. 2, pp. 4–6.
- **1930:** *Mary Wigmans Kompositionsstil*. A critical analysis published in *Schrifttanz*, no. 3.

### Manuscripts and uncertainly attributed texts

- **Undated:** *Über Eurythmie und Tanz*. An unpublished prose manuscript whose title was supplied by an archivist. Kalliope attributes it conjecturally to Skoronel and Berthe Trümpy, so neither sole authorship nor a finished-work status is secure.
- **Undated:** *Mary Wigman*. An unpublished prose typescript or copy conjecturally attributed to Skoronel and Trümpy. Its relationship to Skoronel’s two published Wigman essays is not securely established, and it may represent a draft, variant, or separate text.
- **Undated:** *Aufsatz über Rudolf von Laban zu dessen 50. Geburtstag*. A manuscript and handwritten draft conjecturally attributed to Skoronel and Trümpy. It may be preparatory material for the published *Laban*, rather than an independent work.
- **Undated:** *Gespräch Vera Skoronels mit Landry über sein Nietzsche-Buch*. An eight-leaf prose record identified by a manuscript title and conjecturally attributed to Skoronel and Trümpy. As a record of a conversation, it resists classification as a deliberately authored literary work.
- **Undated:** *Choreographische Skizze „Erweckung der Massen“*. A working notation or protocol for the movement-choir work, not a separate choreography: it instructs and preserves action while remaining materially incomplete.
- **Undated; archive record anomalously dated 4–5 May 1932:** *Skizzen über Abstraktion/Tanz*. A typescript group of theoretical sketches. The catalogue date falls after Skoronel’s death on 24 March 1932 and therefore requires verification; it may describe cataloguing, copying, or posthumous arrangement rather than composition.
