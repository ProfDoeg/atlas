## Works by Charles Babbage

### Calculating engines, machine designs, and programs

- **c. 1819–1822:** *Small difference-engine models (“Difference Engine 0”)*. Experimental calculating mechanisms made before the government-supported engine; their exact sequence and boundaries are not securely fixed, so this is better treated as a group of prototypes than as one finished machine. [Science Museum/Computer History Museum]
- **1821–1842:** *Difference Engine No. 1*. A project for an automatic calculator and printer using finite differences. The complete engine was never made: Joseph Clement constructed a working demonstration portion in 1832, while thousands of other parts remained unassembled; authorship is therefore divided between Babbage’s design and Clement’s manufacture. [Computer History Museum, “The Engines”]
- **1832:** *Demonstration portion of Difference Engine No. 1*. A physical calculating assembly of about 2,000 parts—roughly one-seventh of the intended calculating section—made by Clement to Babbage’s plans. It is a fragment, not the projected engine, but is itself a completed and working object. [Computer History Museum, “A Brief History”]
- **1834–1871:** *Analytical Engine*. A continuously revised project for a punched-card-controlled, general-purpose mechanical computer, incorporating a “store,” “mill,” conditional operations, iteration, and several forms of output. It resists treatment as a single fixed machine: at least three substantially different designs survive, together with drawings, notations, programs, and experimental mechanisms, but no complete engine. [Computer History Museum, “The Engines”]
- **1837–1840, with a later example:** *Programs for the Analytical Engine*. A manuscript body of roughly two dozen operation schedules and program-like plans prepared by Babbage, plus a later example; they are protocols for a machine that did not yet exist, not published software in the modern sense. The famous published Bernoulli-number table belongs to Ada Lovelace’s 1843 notes, although the respective contributions of Lovelace and Babbage remain disputed. [Bromley; Misa]
- **1837:** *On the Mathematical Powers of the Calculating Engine*. An unpublished manuscript exposition of what the Analytical Engine could do. It is an intellectual component of the engine project rather than a realized machine. [History of Science Museum manuscript summary]
- **1838:** *Plan 3 of the Analytical Engine*. A relatively complete design phase reconstructed by Allan Bromley from Babbage’s drawings and notations; “Plan 3” names a documentary state of the evolving project, not a built engine. [Bromley]
- **1840:** *General Plan No. 25 of Mr. Babbage’s Great Calculating or Analytical Engine*. A large lithographed plan, 24 by 36 inches, made in Paris: simultaneously a technical drawing, a public manifestation of an unbuilt engine, and one of the few contemporary visual syntheses of the design. [Babbage list, no. 54; Van Sinderen]
- **1841:** *Sketch of the Principles of the Analytical Engine*. An unpublished manuscript account of the engine project; its surviving revisions make it a working design document rather than a settled publication. [History of Science Museum manuscript summary]
- **1844–1850:** *Skeleton of Principles and Grammar of Mechanical Notation*. An unfinished manuscript protocol for describing machines symbolically. Its “grammar” makes mechanical action into a writable system, so it belongs equally to engineering, notation, and text. [History of Science Museum manuscript summary]
- **1846–1849:** *Difference Engine No. 2*. A completed design for a simpler and more powerful calculating-and-printing engine, requiring roughly one-third as many parts as No. 1. Babbage did not attempt to build it; the working machine completed by the Science Museum in 1991, and its printer in 2002, are posthumous realizations rather than objects made by Babbage. [Computer History Museum, “A Brief History”]
- **by 1851:** *Laws of Mechanical Notation*. A privately printed protocol, accompanied by a lithographic plate, codifying how the structure and motions of machinery could be represented. [Babbage list, no. 61; Van Sinderen]
- **by 1871:** *Experimental portion of the Analytical Engine*. A small physical assembly left under construction at Babbage’s death, alongside other test mechanisms. It is evidence of manufacture but not a miniature or completed version of the projected engine. [Computer History Museum, “The Engines”]
- **1869:** *Of the Analytical Engine*. An unpublished late manuscript account, distinct from the 1841 text of the same general subject and from the projected *History of the Analytical Engine*. [History of Science Museum manuscript summary]
- **1869:** *The Analytical Engine*. An unpublished late manuscript listed separately in the surviving papers. Its precise relationship to *Of the Analytical Engine* requires archival comparison and should not be silently collapsed into it. [History of Science Museum manuscript summary]

### Mathematical and scientific texts

- **1813:** *Preface to Memoirs of the Analytical Society*. A jointly authored programmatic preface by Babbage and John Herschel, advocating continental analysis; the collaboration prevents attribution to Babbage alone. [Babbage list, no. 1; Van Sinderen]
- **1813:** *On Continued Products*. Babbage’s memoir in the Analytical Society’s volume: a mathematical paper, not a separate book despite its appearance within a collective publication. [Babbage list, no. 2; Van Sinderen]
- **1815:** *An Essay towards the Calculus of Functions*. A research paper establishing Babbage’s calculus-of-functions program. [Babbage list, no. 3; Van Sinderen]
- **1816:** *An Essay towards the Calculus of Functions, Part II*. A second, substantially extended research paper rather than a new edition of the first. [Babbage list, no. 4; Van Sinderen]
- **1816:** *Demonstrations of Some of Dr. Matthew Stewart’s General Theorems, to Which Is Added an Account of Some New Properties of the Circle*. A geometrical research article combining demonstrations with additional propositions. [Babbage list, no. 5; Van Sinderen]
- **1816:** *Observations on the Present State of the Mathematical Sciences in Great Britain*. An anonymous polemical article signed “B.” Tessa Mobbs and Robert Unwin attribute it to Babbage from circumstantial and textual evidence, but the attribution remains a scholarly proposal, not an acknowledged authorship. [Mobbs and Unwin]
- **1817:** *Observations on the Analogy Which Subsists between the Calculus of Functions and Other Branches of Analysis*. A research paper extending the conceptual claims of Babbage’s functional calculus. [Babbage list, no. 6; Van Sinderen]
- **1817:** *Solution of Some Problems by Means of the Calculus of Functions*. A mathematical problem-solving article. [Babbage list, no. 7; Van Sinderen]
- **1817:** *Note Respecting Elimination*. A short mathematical note. [Babbage list, no. 8; Van Sinderen]
- **1817:** *An Account of Euler’s Method of Solving a Problem Relating to the Knight’s Move at Chess*. An expository mathematical article about Euler’s knight’s-tour method. [Babbage list, no. 9; Van Sinderen]
- **1819:** *On Some New Methods of Investigating the Sums of Several Classes of Infinite Series*. A research paper on series. [Babbage list, no. 10; Van Sinderen]
- **1819:** *Demonstration of a Theorem Relating to Prime Numbers*. A short number-theoretical paper. [Babbage list, no. 11; Van Sinderen]
- **1820:** *An Examination of Some Questions Connected with Games of Chance*. A probability paper treating games mathematically. [Babbage list, no. 12; Van Sinderen]
- **1820:** *Observations on the Notation Employed in the Calculus of Functions*. A paper about mathematical representation as well as mathematical operations. [Babbage list, no. 13; Van Sinderen]
- **1820:** *On the Application of Analysis to the Discovery of Local Theorems and Porisms*. A geometrical research paper. [Babbage list, no. 14; Van Sinderen]
- **1820:** *Examples of the Solution of Functional Equations*. A separately issued extraction from *Examples of the Differential and Integral Calculus*, and thus a derivative manifestation rather than an entirely independent composition. [Babbage list, no. 17; Van Sinderen]
- **1824:** *On the Determination of the General Term of a New Class of Infinite Series*. A mathematical research paper. [Babbage list, no. 22; Van Sinderen]
- **1824:** *Observations on the Measurement of Heights by the Barometer*. A scientific-instrument and measurement paper. [Babbage list, no. 23; Van Sinderen]
- **1825:** *On a New Zenith Micrometer*. A description of an astronomical measuring instrument. The printed text establishes the design; surviving evidence does not justify calling every later instrument a physical object made by Babbage. [Babbage list, no. 24; Van Sinderen]
- **1825:** *Account of the Repetition of M. Arago’s Experiments on the Magnetism Manifested by Various Substances during Rotation*. An experimental report jointly made with John Herschel. [Babbage list, no. 25; Van Sinderen]
- **1826:** *On Electric and Magnetic Rotations*. A physical-science research paper. [Babbage list, no. 27; Van Sinderen]
- **1826:** *On the Influence of Signs in Mathematical Reasoning*. A paper on mathematical signs and inference. [Babbage list, no. 29; Van Sinderen]
- **date not fixed in Babbage’s list:** *Notation*. An encyclopedia article; Babbage supplied no date in his own bibliography, so none should be inferred from the neighboring entries. [Babbage list, no. 31; Van Sinderen]
- **date not fixed in Babbage’s list:** *Porisms*. An encyclopedia article on geometry, likewise undated in Babbage’s list. [Babbage list, no. 32; Van Sinderen]
- **1827:** *Notice Respecting Some Errors Common to Many Tables of Logarithms*. A short paper on recurrent tabular errors. [Babbage list, no. 35; Van Sinderen]
- **1829:** *Letter to T. P. Courtenay on the Proportion of Births of the Two Sexes amongst Legitimate and Illegitimate Children*. A statistical letter, later translated into French. [Babbage list, no. 37; Van Sinderen]
- **1829:** *Note on the Description of Mammalia*. A short classificatory note in natural history. [Babbage list, no. 39; Van Sinderen]
- **date not stated in Babbage’s list:** *On the Proportion of Letters Occurring in Various Languages*. A letter to Adolphe Quetelet using letter-frequency analysis; Babbage’s own bibliography gives the journal location but no year. [Babbage list, no. 42; Van Sinderen]
- **1832:** *Barometrical Observations Made at the Fall of the Staubbach*. A jointly authored observational report by Babbage and John Herschel. [Babbage list, no. 46; Van Sinderen]
- **1834:** *Abstract of a Paper Entitled Observations on the Temple of Serapis at Pozzuoli*. The first printed manifestation of Babbage’s theory of terrestrial isothermal surfaces; it is an abstract of a larger project, not the full paper. [Babbage list, no. 47; Van Sinderen]
- **1837:** *On Some Impressions in Sandstone*. A geological note published in two periodicals. Those appearances are manifestations of one paper, not two works. [Babbage list, no. 52; Van Sinderen]
- **1846:** *Observations on the Temple of Serapis at Pozzuoli*. The full geological paper whose abstract appeared in 1834. [Babbage list, no. 48; Van Sinderen]
- **1847:** *Observations on the Temple of Serapis at Pozzuoli, with Supplemental Conjectures on the Physical State of the Surface of the Moon*. An expanded reprint whose lunar supplement makes it more than a purely mechanical reissue. [Babbage list, no. 49; Van Sinderen]
- **1847:** *On the Planet Neptune*. A newspaper intervention concerning the discovery and calculation of Neptune. [Babbage list, no. 58; Van Sinderen]
- **1851–1852:** *Note Respecting the Pink Projections from the Sun’s Disc Observed during the Total Solar Eclipse in 1851*. An astronomical observation published in the proceedings of the Astronomical Society. [Babbage list, no. 60; Van Sinderen]
- **1853:** *On the Statistics of Lighthouses*. A statistical congress paper about lighthouse systems. [Babbage list, no. 64; Van Sinderen]
- **1856:** *On the Action of Ocean-Currents in the Formation of the Strata of the Earth*. A geological research paper. [Babbage list, no. 72; Van Sinderen]
- **1856:** *Statistics of the Clearing-House*. A statistical paper, separately reprinted from the Statistical Society’s transactions. [Babbage list, no. 74; Van Sinderen]
- **1857:** *Table of the Relative Frequency of Occurrence of the Causes of Breaking Plate-Glass Windows*. A statistical table: a data object and visual argument rather than a conventional essay. [Babbage list, no. 77; Van Sinderen]
- **1859:** *On Remains of Human Art, Mixed with the Bones of Extinct Races of Animals*. An archaeological and geological paper. [Babbage list, no. 78; Van Sinderen]

### Mathematical tables, translations, and experimental printing

- **1816:** *An Elementary Treatise on the Differential and Integral Calculus*. An English translation of S. F. Lacroix, produced jointly by Babbage, George Peacock, and John Herschel; it is a collaborative transformation of another author’s work, not an original treatise by Babbage alone. [Babbage list, no. 15; Van Sinderen]
- **1820:** *Examples of the Differential and Integral Calculus*. A two-volume collaborative collection made with Peacock and Herschel to accompany the Lacroix translation. [Babbage list, no. 16; Van Sinderen]
- **1827:** *Table of the Logarithms of the Natural Numbers from 1 to 108,000*. A stereotyped numerical table and manufactured reference instrument. Babbage’s own list dates it 1826, but bibliographical scholarship accepts 1827; subsequent editions and reprints are manifestations, not new works. [Babbage list, no. 33; Campbell-Kelly; Van Sinderen]
- **1831:** *Specimen of Logarithmic Tables Printed with Different Coloured Inks and on Variously-Coloured Papers*. A unique twenty-one-volume printing experiment testing visual fatigue across 151 papers, ten ink colors, and metallic printing. It is closer to an experimental archive or Gesamtkunstwerk of typography, color, data, and physiology than to an ordinary book. [Babbage list, no. 43; Van Sinderen]
- **1834:** *German and Hungarian coloured-paper issues of the logarithm tables*. Three translated manifestations prepared with Charles Nagy and published at Pest and Vienna. Babbage counted them separately, but they are editions and material experiments derived from the 1827 table, not wholly new compositions. [Babbage list, no. 34; Van Sinderen]

### Machinery, manufacturing, transport, and optical inventions

- **1822:** *Note Respecting the Application of Machinery to the Calculation of Mathematical Tables*. The first brief printed announcement of the difference-engine project. [Babbage list, no. 18; Van Sinderen]
- **1822:** *A Letter to Sir Humphry Davy on the Application of Machinery to the Purpose of Calculating and Printing Mathematical Tables*. A proposal and advocacy document addressed to the Royal Society’s president; it helped act upon the world by seeking institutional support, giving it a manifesto-like function. [Babbage list, no. 19; Van Sinderen]
- **1822:** *On the Theoretical Principles of the Machinery for Calculating Tables*. A technical account of the mathematical principles behind automatic tabulation. [Babbage list, no. 20; Van Sinderen]
- **1822–1824:** *Observations on the Application of Machinery to the Computation of Mathematical Tables*. A fuller paper dated December 1822 and published in the Astronomical Society’s memoirs in 1824. [Babbage list, no. 21; Van Sinderen]
- **1826:** *Diving Bell*. An encyclopedia article on a technological object; it describes and analyzes rather than itself constituting the apparatus. [Babbage list, no. 26; Van Sinderen]
- **1826:** *On a Method of Expressing by Signs the Action of Machinery*. The first published version of Babbage’s mechanical notation: a representational system and protocol for machine action. [Babbage list, no. 28; Van Sinderen]
- **1829:** *Essay on the General Principles Which Regulate the Application of Machinery*. An encyclopedia treatise on machinery, later incorporated into Babbage’s larger political economy of manufacture. [Babbage list, no. 36; Van Sinderen]
- **1832:** *On the Economy of Machinery and Manufactures*. A book-length analysis of manufacturing processes, division of labor, costing, and industrial organization. It is both descriptive treatise and reform project, and appeared in numerous revised editions and translations. [Babbage list, no. 44; Van Sinderen]
- **1838:** *Short Account of a Method by Which Engraving on Wood May Be Rendered More Useful for the Illustration and Description of Machinery*. A technical proposal for making wood engraving carry systematic mechanical information. [Babbage list, no. 52*; Van Sinderen]
- **1846:** *On the Principles of Tools for Turning and Planing Metals*. A technical essay printed as an appendix to Charles Holtzapffel’s *Turning and Mechanical Manipulation*. [Babbage list, no. 57; Van Sinderen]
- **by 1854:** *Ophthalmoscope*. An optical instrument devised independently by Babbage before its description in T. Wharton Jones’s 1854 report. Babbage did not publish a standalone account and apparently did not develop the invention into clinical use; priority claims must therefore distinguish conception and demonstration from Helmholtz’s practical instrument. [Babbage list, no. 65; Van Sinderen]
- **1855:** *On Submarine Navigation*. An illustrated-newspaper article presenting a technological proposal. [Babbage list, no. 68; Van Sinderen]
- **1855:** *On a Method of Laying Guns in a Battery without Exposing the Men to the Shot of the Enemy*. A wartime technical proposal published in *The Times*: a protocol for remote or protected gun-laying rather than a documented completed weapon. [Babbage list, no. 70; Van Sinderen]
- **1855:** *Sur la machine suédoise de M. Scheutz pour calculer les tables mathématiques*. A French-language report on the Scheutz difference engine, a machine derived from Babbage’s published principles but designed and built by Georg and Edvard Scheutz. The text is Babbage’s; the machine is not. [Babbage list, no. 71; Van Sinderen]
- **1856:** *Observations on the Mechanical Notation of Scheutz’s Difference Engine*. Babbage’s observations on notation, prepared and drawn up by his son Henry Prevost Babbage; the divided labor makes sole authorship inappropriate. [Babbage list, no. 73; Van Sinderen]
- **date not securely fixed as a single work:** *Locomotive pilot or “cow-catcher”*. Babbage devised a frame intended to clear obstacles from railway tracks, but surviving accounts do not establish a single canonical titled artifact, date, or production history. It resists classification as a work: it is an attributed practical invention represented through models or descriptions rather than a securely documented manufactured series. [Hyman; Swade]
- **1830s:** *Railway dynamometer and recording apparatus*. An experimental system for recording the forces and motion of railway vehicles. It should be treated as an evolving instrument project, not a single securely titled machine. [Hyman; Swade]
- **date not fixed:** *Occulting-light apparatus for lighthouses*. A signaling system in which light and darkness followed distinctive patterns; its identity lies in the protocol and mechanism together rather than in one surviving object. [Babbage list, nos. 62 and 69; Van Sinderen]

### Cryptography and decipherment

- **1816:** *A New Cypher Proposed*. A brief article signed “C. B.” Mobbs and Unwin propose Babbage as its author; the identification is persuasive but not certain and was not acknowledged in Babbage’s own bibliography. [Mobbs and Unwin]
- **1835:** *Decipherment of Abraham Sharp’s Letter to J. Crosthwait of 2 February 1721/22*. A cryptanalytic performance published in Francis Baily’s *Account of the Revd. John Flamsteed*. The made thing is the decipherment rather than Sharp’s letter itself. [Babbage list, no. 50; Van Sinderen]
- **1854:** *On Secret or Cipher Writing: Mr. T.’s Cipher Deciphered by C.* A published cryptanalytic demonstration under abbreviated names. [Babbage list, no. 66; Van Sinderen]
- **1854:** *On Mr. T.’s Second Inscrutable Cipher Deciphered by C.* A second published decipherment responding to another challenge. [Babbage list, no. 67; Van Sinderen]
- **1840s–1850s:** *Work on the Vigenère cipher and the “method of the key-word”*. An unpublished body of cryptanalytic work, including a method later independently associated with Friedrich Kasiski. Because Babbage withheld much of it, it is a research project and set of procedures rather than a fixed published text. [Franksen; Kidwell]

### Political economy, institutions, and public interventions

- **1826:** *A Comparative View of the Various Institutions for the Assurance of Lives*. A book combining actuarial comparison, tabular evidence, and consumer guidance. [Babbage list, no. 30; Van Sinderen]
- **1827:** *Evidence on Savings Banks before a Committee of the House of Commons*. Parliamentary testimony: a performed and officially printed intervention, not a book composed in the ordinary sense. Babbage included it without a number in his 1864 sequence. [Babbage list, between nos. 35 and 36; Van Sinderen]
- **1829:** *Account of the Great Congress of Philosophers at Berlin*. A report signed only as communicated by “a Correspondent [C. B.]”; Babbage acknowledged the authorship in his later list. [Babbage list, no. 38; Van Sinderen]
- **1830:** *Reflections on the Decline of Science in England, and on Some of Its Causes*. A polemical reform book attacking scientific patronage and institutions. It behaves as a manifesto by attempting to reorganize English science, not merely describe its condition. [Babbage list, no. 40; Van Sinderen]
- **1830:** *Sketch of the Philosophical Characters of Dr. Wollaston and Sir Humphry Davy*. A separately issued extraction from *Reflections on the Decline of Science*; it is a derivative manifestation with an independent circulation history. [Babbage list, no. 41; Van Sinderen]
- **1832:** *Letter to Sir David Brewster on the Advantage of a Collection of the Constants of Nature and Art*. A proposal for a coordinated reference collection, reprinted by the British Association and later by an international statistical congress. [Babbage list, no. 45; Van Sinderen]
- **1833:** *Observations on Peerage for Life*. A political pamphlet first issued in 1833 and reprinted in 1856. [Babbage list, no. 75; Van Sinderen]
- **1839:** *Letter to the Members of the British Association*. A privately circulated institutional intervention; its epistolary title does not make it private correspondence. [Babbage list, no. 53; Van Sinderen]
- **1843:** *Statement of the Circumstances Respecting Mr. Babbage’s Calculating Engines*. A pamphlet assembled from Babbage’s papers by Sir Harris Nicolas. The underlying case is Babbage’s, but the textual shaping and stated authorship belong partly to Nicolas. [Babbage list, no. 55; Van Sinderen]
- **1843:** *Addition to the Memoir of M. Menabrea on the Analytical Engine*. An anonymous “authorized statement” concerning the history of Babbage’s engines. Van Sinderen attributed it to Babbage on contemporary authority, but later comparison shows variant printings and the attribution is not absolutely secure. [Van Sinderen; Trinity College Dublin study]
- **1843:** *Note on the Boracic Acid Works in Tuscany*. A short industrial and travel note embedded in the first edition of Murray’s *Handbook for Travellers in Central Italy*. [Babbage list, no. 56; Van Sinderen]
- **1848:** *Thoughts on the Principles of Taxation, with Reference to a Property Tax and Its Exceptions*. A political-economic pamphlet, revised in later editions and translated into Italian. [Babbage list, no. 59; Van Sinderen]
- **1851:** *The Exposition of 1851; or, Views of the Industry, the Science, and the Government of England*. A polemical book occasioned by the Great Exhibition, combining exhibition criticism with proposals for industrial and scientific government. [Babbage list, no. 63; Van Sinderen]
- **1851:** *Note Respecting Lighthouses (Occulting Lights)*. A technical and administrative proposal sent to Trinity House and widely reprinted; it is both an engineering protocol and an attempt to establish a signaling standard. [Babbage list, no. 62; Van Sinderen]
- **1855:** *Letter to the Editor of The Times on Occulting Lights for Lighthouses and Night Signals: Flashing Lights at Sebastopol*. A public letter applying Babbage’s signaling system to navigation and war. [Babbage list, no. 69; Van Sinderen]
- **1856:** *Observations Addressed to the President and Fellows of the Royal Society on the Award of Their Medals for 1856*. A printed protest about scientific judgment and institutional procedure. [Babbage list, no. 76; Van Sinderen]

### Philosophical, autobiographical, and occasional writing

- **1837:** *The Ninth Bridgewater Treatise: A Fragment*. A book of natural theology and philosophy of calculation, explicitly presented as a “fragment” and as an unsolicited addition to the official Bridgewater Treatises. Its discussions of repeated laws and calculating machines make it a philosophical extension of the engine project. [Babbage list, no. 51; Van Sinderen]
- **1864:** *A Chapter on Street Nuisances*. A separately circulated extract from the forthcoming *Passages from the Life of a Philosopher*, issued in multiple editions. It is a public campaign document as much as an autobiographical chapter, and should not be counted as a wholly independent composition. [Van Sinderen; History of Science Museum manuscript summary]
- **1864:** *Passages from the Life of a Philosopher*. An autobiographical book combining memoir, technical history, polemic, travel, institutional criticism, and accounts of unfinished projects. It refuses the unity of a conventional autobiography and is the principal Gesamtkunstwerk of Babbage’s textual career. [Babbage list, no. 79; Van Sinderen]
- **announced 1864; unfinished:** *History of the Analytical Engine*. A projected quarto described as “in the press,” intended to combine chapters from *Passages* with Ada Lovelace’s translation of Menabrea and her notes. Only 294 pages were reportedly typeset during Babbage’s lifetime; it therefore survives as an incomplete publishing project, not a finished book. [Babbage list, no. 80; Van Sinderen]
- **date not fixed:** *The Science of Number Reduced to Mechanism*. An unfinished manuscript known through a transcription in the Buxton papers. Its incompleteness is intrinsic evidence: it is a projected theoretical account, not a lost finished publication. [History of Science Museum manuscript summary]
- **date not fixed:** *Scene from a New After-Piece*. A dramatic scene embedded in *Passages from the Life of a Philosopher*. It is an occasional theatrical text within another obra, not evidence of a completed or staged play. [*Passages*, ch. XXII]
