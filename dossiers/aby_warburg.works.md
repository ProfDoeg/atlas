## Works by Aby Moritz Warburg

### Published studies and scholarly monographs

- **1893:** *[Sandro Botticellis „Geburt der Venus“ und „Frühling“: Eine Untersuchung über die Vorstellungen von der Antike in der italienischen Frührenaissance](https://www.suhrkamp.de/buch/aby-warburg-werke-in-einem-band-t-9783518298428).* Warburg’s doctoral dissertation: a monographic study of moving bodies, drapery, literary sources, and the Renaissance reception of antiquity.
- **1895:** *Die Theaterkostüme für die Intermedien von 1589.* An archival study of costume designs for the Florentine intermedi; its object is a multimedia court spectacle rather than an autonomous artwork.
- **1898:** *Sandro Botticelli.* A short biographical-critical essay written for an artists’ monograph series.
- **1902:** *[Bildniskunst und florentinisches Bürgertum](https://digi.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/diglit/warburg1980/0158/scroll).* An illustrated historical study of portraiture, patronage, votive practice, and Florentine civic life, incorporating the essay on Domenico Ghirlandaio and Francesco Sassetti.
- **1902:** *Flandrische Kunst und florentinische Frührenaissance.* A group of studies on artistic and commercial exchange between Flanders and Florence; later collected under this editorial grouping.
- **1905:** *Dürer und die italienische Antike.* An essay, originally accompanying a Hamburg lecture and small exhibition, that formulates the migration of antique “pathos formulas.”
- **1907:** *Francesco Sassettis letztwillige Verfügung.* An archival essay about Sassetti’s will, chapel, family memory, and the conflict between Christian and antique forms.
- **1907:** *Arbeitende Bauern auf burgundischen Teppichen.* A short iconographic study of working peasants represented in Burgundian tapestries.
- **1912; revised 1922:** *Italienische Kunst und internationale Astrologie im Palazzo Schifanoja zu Ferrara.* A congress lecture subsequently revised for publication; an iconological reconstruction of the astrological program of the Ferrara frescoes.
- **1913:** *Die Fixsternhimmelsbilder der Sphaera Barbarica auf der Wanderung von Ost nach West.* A study of the migration and transformation of constellation images; its argument moves across images, manuscripts, languages, and regions.
- **1913:** *Die Planetenbilder auf der Wanderung von Süd nach Nord und ihre Rückkehr nach Italien.* A companion investigation of travelling planetary imagery.
- **1913:** *Luftschiff und Tauchboot in der mittelalterlichen Vorstellungswelt.* A short study connecting modern technologies with medieval and antique imaginative forms.
- **1914:** *Der Eintritt des antikisierenden Idealstils in die Malerei der Frührenaissance.* A lecture-text on the Renaissance adoption of an idealizing antique style.
- **1920:** *Heidnisch-antike Weissagung in Wort und Bild zu Luthers Zeiten.* Warburg’s largest lifetime publication, joining Reformation history, astrology, political prophecy, pamphlets, calendars, words, and images.
- **1926:** *Italienische Antike im Zeitalter Rembrandts.* A lecture on the afterlife of antique expressive formulas in Rembrandt’s milieu, conceived together with a photographic image sequence rather than as a self-sufficient written essay.
- **1929:** *Manet’s Déjeuner sur l’herbe.* A late study tracing antique compositional memory into Manet; left in manuscript and working-note forms and therefore resistant to classification as a finished publication.

### Lectures, addresses, and working texts

- **1896–1901:** *Symbolismus als Umfangsbestimmung.* Notes toward a theory of symbolism rather than a completed treatise; the title and textual unity are editorially stabilized from manuscript material.
- **1897:** *Eine Reise durch das Gebiet der Pueblo-Indianer in Neu-Mexiko und Arizona.* A photographic travel lecture based on Warburg’s 1895–96 journey; it is distinct from the later Kreuzlingen lecture with which it is sometimes conflated.
- **1900:** *Ninfa Fiorentina.* An experimental, partly epistolary dialogue about the Renaissance nymph; deliberately poised between scholarly analysis, fiction, and intellectual self-dramatization.
- **1901:** *Florentinische Wirklichkeit und antikisierender Idealismus.* A lecture or working study of the tension between observed Florentine life and antique idealization; it remained unpublished during Warburg’s lifetime.
- **1901:** *Elegie auf Arnold Böcklin.* An occasional commemorative text whose form approaches literary elegy rather than conventional art history.
- **1903:** *Die Richtungen der Kunstgeschichte.* A programmatic lecture mapping competing approaches to art history.
- **1923:** *[Bilder aus dem Gebiet der Pueblo-Indianer in Nord-Amerika](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/library-collections/warburg-institute-archive/aby-warburgs-pueblo-materials).* The Kreuzlingen lecture commonly—but posthumously—published as *Das Schlangenritual*; Warburg did not authorize that familiar title or prepare the lecture as a finished book. English-language readers generally meet it in Michael P. Steinberg's translation, *Images from the Region of the Pueblo Indians of North America* (Cornell University Press, 1995).
- **1923:** *Reise-Erinnerungen aus dem Gebiet der Pueblo-Indianer in Nordamerika.* Recollections and working materials related to the Kreuzlingen presentation, not an independently completed monograph.
- **1924:** *Zum Vortrage von Karl Reinhardt über „Ovids Metamorphosen“ in der Bibliothek Warburg.* An introductory or responsive address created for a library event.
- **1925:** *Die Einwirkung der Sphaera barbarica auf die kosmischen Orientierungsversuche des Abendlandes.* A memorial lecture for Franz Boll accompanied by Warburg’s first mature photographic *Bilderreihe*.
- **1927:** *Vom Arsenal zum Laboratorium.* A programmatic address recasting Warburg’s collection as an active laboratory of cultural science.
- **1927:** *Die Bedeutung des „seltenen Buches“ für eine universell und geisteswissenschaftlich orientierte Kunstgeschichte.* A lecture and accompanying three-panel display made for visiting librarians; surviving knowledge of the spoken presentation partly depends on Gertrud Bing’s report.
- **1927:** *Schlußsitzung der Burckhardt-Uebung.* A concluding seminar address associated with Warburg’s Hamburg teaching on Jacob Burckhardt.
- **1928:** *Ernst Cassirer.* A ceremonial or commemorative address rather than a systematic study.
- **1929:** *Mnemosyne: Einleitung.* The projected introduction to the image atlas; a working text left unfinished and transmitted in drafts.
- **1927–1929:** *Mnemosyne I: Aufzeichnungen.* Notes, formulations, and drafts produced during work on the atlas; their later editorial grouping should not be mistaken for a book completed by Warburg.
- **1887–1929:** *Kleine Schriften und Vorträge.* More than forty student papers, lectures, speeches, dramatic sketches, occasional writings, drafts, and discussion interventions survive for the projected critical-edition volume III.1. This is an editorial corpus, not a single obra, and the edition project remains the proper authority for separating its individual, frequently unpublished and unstable texts.

### Photographic image sequences and exhibitions

- **1925:** *Die Einwirkung der Sphaera barbarica auf die kosmischen Orientierungsversuche des Abendlandes.* A mounted photographic image sequence made to think alongside the lecture of the same title; it is neither merely illustration nor an autonomous conventional exhibition.
- **1926:** *Orientalisierende Astrologie.* A photographic *Bilderreihe* reconstructable from surviving sources.
- **1926:** *Italienische Antike im Zeitalter Rembrandts.* A lecture-accompanying arrangement of reproductions; its repeated revisions make it a changing visual argument rather than a fixed text.
- **1927:** *Urworte leidenschaftlicher Gebärdensprache.* A comparative image sequence organized around inherited formulas of passionate gesture.
- **1927:** *Die Bedeutung des „seltenen Buches“ für eine universell und geisteswissenschaftlich orientierte Kunstgeschichte.* A three-panel exhibition integrated with Warburg’s lecture on rare books.
- **1927:** *Die Funktion der nachlebenden Antike bei der Ausprägung energetischer Symbolik.* A visual essay composed by mounting and comparing photographic reproductions.
- **1927:** *Die Funktion der sozialen Mneme als Bewahrerin der antikisierenden Dynamo-Engramme der Gebärdensprache.* An image sequence that makes a theoretical claim through juxtaposition; the long title belongs to Warburg’s working vocabulary rather than to a separately published book.
- **1927:** *Die Funktion des Briefmarkenbildes im Geistesverkehr der Welt.* A visual-cultural study of postage-stamp imagery and international circulation, made as both an image sequence and an associated text.
- **1927:** *Menschengleichnis am Himmel.* A constellation-image sequence treating projection of the human figure onto the heavens.
- **29 January–6 February 1927:** *[Ovid-Ausstellung](https://www.centropalazzote.it/warburg-eng/).* A temporary installation of illustrated editions and comparative images in the oval reading room of the Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg; it is part exhibition, part visual research instrument, and part precursor to *Mnemosyne*.
- **1927:** *Medicaeische Festlichkeiten am Hofe der Valois auf Brüsseler Teppichen in der Galleria der Uffizi.* A photographic sequence comparing tapestries, court festivals, and Medici-Valois imagery.
- **1928:** *Bilder aus dem Festwesen der Renaissance.* A visual essay on Renaissance festival culture.
- **1929:** *Römische Antike in der Werkstatt des Domenico Ghirlandaio.* A late photographic sequence about the operative presence of antiquity in Ghirlandaio’s workshop.
- **1929:** *Manet und die italienische Antike.* A panel-based visual argument related to the *Déjeuner sur l’herbe* manuscript.
- **1930–1941:** *Bildersammlung zur Geschichte von Sternglaube und Sternkunde.* A Hamburg Planetarium display developed from Warburgian materials but completed after his death by Fritz Saxl and collaborators; it should not be attributed to Warburg alone, although the critical edition includes it to document the project’s continuation.
- **2012 editorial reconstruction:** *[Bilderreihen und Ausstellungen](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/de/document/isbn/9783050042688/html).* The critical edition reconstructs thirteen Warburg image presentations from photographs, sketches, and archival evidence. The volume is not itself a work made in 2012 by Warburg, and the reconstructions cannot erase the provisional, repeatedly altered status of the historical arrangements.

### Unfinished atlas — project, visual essay, and possible Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1924–1929:** *[Bilderatlas Mnemosyne](https://www.warburg-haus.de/publikationen/aby-warburg-studienausgabe/).* An unfinished atlas of roughly 2,000 reproductions arranged on about sixty cloth-covered panels, intended to synthesize Warburg’s research on the afterlife of antiquity. Multiple photographed states survive; no state is an authorially completed final version, and its combination of spatial montage, photographs, captions, performance, lecture, and library environment makes it approach a *Gesamtkunstwerk* while resisting identification as a conventional book.
- **1927–1929:** *Mnemosyne panel states and working configurations.* Successive arrangements of the atlas panels are manifestations of an evolving project, not separate finished works; later editions necessarily select or reconstruct particular photographed states.

### Photographs and ethnographic materials

- **1895–1896:** *[Photographs and field materials from New Mexico and Arizona](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/library-collections/warburg-institute-archive/aby-warburgs-pueblo-materials).* Photographs, notes, collected objects, and observations made during Warburg’s journey among Pueblo and Hopi communities. They form an ethically and authorially complicated research corpus—not a finished “work”—and later lecture and book presentations reorganized only portions of it.
- **1897 and 1923:** *Pueblo lecture slide and photograph sequences.* Visual presentations derived from the journey but assembled for different lectures; their changing selection and order make them distinct manifestations rather than one stable photographic œuvre.

### Library, institute, and architectural-intellectual environment

- **c. 1886–1929:** *[Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/library-collections/library/about-library/library-aby-warburg).* A personally assembled, continually rearranged research library organized by the “law of the good neighbour.” It is an institutional obra and intellectual instrument rather than a written work; Fritz Saxl and other collaborators were indispensable to its transformation into a functioning research institute.
- **1914–1918:** *War collection.* A deliberately accumulated corpus of books, pamphlets, periodicals, images, and press cuttings concerning the First World War. It resists attribution as a sole-authored work: Warburg initiated and directed the collection, while its material was produced and organized by many hands.
- **1921–1929:** *Kulturwissenschaftliche Bibliothek Warburg as research institute.* The library’s lecture program, publications, photographic resources, and scholarly network constitute a collective institutional project. Warburg founded and shaped it, but Saxl’s administrative and scholarly work means that sole authorship would be misleading.
- **1925–1926:** *Warburg Library building, Heilwigstraße 116, Hamburg.* A purpose-built research environment designed architecturally by Gerhard Langmaack with substantial conceptual involvement from Warburg, especially in the elliptical reading room. It is a collaborative building and spatial research apparatus, not a solely authored architectural work.
- **1926–1929:** *The elliptical reading room as presentation apparatus.* The room’s curved shelving and limited panel capacity conditioned Warburg’s lectures, exhibitions, and atlas arrangements; it is best understood as one component of the library-project’s multimedia ecology rather than as an independent artwork.

### Collaborative notebooks, correspondence, and archival corpora

- **1926–1929:** *Tagebuch der Kulturwissenschaftlichen Bibliothek Warburg.* An institutional working diary with entries by Warburg, Gertrud Bing, and Fritz Saxl. Its collective authorship is explicit, so it is not properly a sole-authored Warburg text.
- **1873–1929:** *[Personal, family, and institutional correspondence](https://warburg.sas.ac.uk/library-collections/warburg-institute-archive/warburg-institute-archive-online-catalogues).* Thousands of letters survive as a life-long communicative corpus. Individual letters are made texts, but “the correspondence” is an archival aggregate constructed jointly by sender, recipient, preservation, and later cataloguing—not a unitary work.
- **1910–1919; 1920–1929:** *Warburg–Saxl correspondence.* A collaborative intellectual exchange later edited in chronological volumes; the editorial books are posthumous manifestations, not books Warburg composed.
- **Undated and various dates:** *Fragmente zur Ausdruckskunde.* Notes, diagrams, drafts, and conceptual fragments on expression, gesture, polarity, and the *Pathosformel*. The title denotes a posthumously constituted critical-edition corpus rather than a finished treatise.
- **Various dates:** *Zettelkästen and working-paper groups.* Card indexes, excerpts, diagrams, and classified research notes preserved in Warburg’s archive. They are intellectual tools and material traces of work; their resistance to being called individual “works” is essential to their status.
