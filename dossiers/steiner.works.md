## Works by Rudolf Steiner

### Authored books and substantial written works

- **1884–1897:** *Einleitungen zu Goethes naturwissenschaftlichen Schriften* (GA 1). A body of editorial introductions written for Kürschner’s edition of Goethe’s scientific writings; simultaneously scholarship, editorial apparatus, and the groundwork for Steiner’s epistemology.
- **1886:** *Grundlinien einer Erkenntnistheorie der Goetheschen Weltanschauung, mit besonderer Rücksicht auf Schiller* (GA 2). A philosophical monograph on Goethean epistemology.
- **1891–1892:** *Die Grundfrage der Erkenntnistheorie mit besonderer Rücksicht auf Fichtes Wissenschaftslehre* / *Wahrheit und Wissenschaft: Vorspiel einer Philosophie der Freiheit* (GA 3). Steiner’s doctoral dissertation, subsequently revised and enlarged into a book; the two forms are related but not identical objects.
- **1894; substantially revised 1918:** *Die Philosophie der Freiheit: Grundzüge einer modernen Weltanschauung* (GA 4). A philosophical monograph whose extensively revised second edition makes it a text with two historically distinct states.
- **1895:** *Friedrich Nietzsche, ein Kämpfer gegen seine Zeit* (GA 5). A philosophical and biographical study, later supplemented in the collected edition by shorter writings on Nietzsche and psychopathology.
- **1897:** *Goethes Weltanschauung* (GA 6). A synthetic study of Goethe’s conception of nature and knowledge.
- **1901:** *Die Mystik im Aufgange des neuzeitlichen Geisteslebens und ihr Verhältnis zur modernen Weltanschauung* (GA 7). A historical-philosophical account of European mysticism, revised in later editions.
- **1902:** *Das Christentum als mystische Tatsache und die Mysterien des Altertums* (GA 8). A book interpreting Christianity through ancient mystery traditions; it developed from lectures but was authored for print.
- **1904; revised through 1922:** *Theosophie: Einführung in übersinnliche Welterkenntnis und Menschenbestimmung* (GA 9). A systematic introductory book repeatedly rewritten by Steiner, so no single edition exhausts the work’s textual identity.
- **1904–1905; enlarged thereafter:** *Wie erlangt man Erkenntnisse der höheren Welten?* (GA 10). A protocol-like sequence of spiritual exercises first issued serially and later made into a book.
- **1904–1908:** *Aus der Akasha-Chronik* (GA 11). A serial essay sequence presenting an esoteric prehistory; its book form is a later gathering of periodical texts.
- **1905–1908:** *Die Stufen der höheren Erkenntnis* (GA 12). An unfinished continuation of *Wie erlangt man Erkenntnisse der höheren Welten?*; project and surviving fragment rather than a completed book.
- **1910:** *Die Geheimwissenschaft im Umriss* (GA 13). A systematic exposition of anthroposophical cosmology and spiritual development.
- **1910:** *Die Pforte der Einweihung (Initiation): Ein Rosenkreuzermysterium* (GA 14/I). The first mystery drama: a theatrical text intended for embodied performance rather than merely private reading.
- **1911:** *Die Prüfung der Seele: Szenisches Lebensbild als Nachspiel zur Pforte der Einweihung* (GA 14/II). The second mystery drama.
- **1911:** *Die geistige Führung des Menschen und der Menschheit* (GA 15). A short esoteric treatise.
- **1912:** *Ein Weg zur Selbsterkenntnis des Menschen in acht Meditationen* (GA 16). A sequence of meditations functioning as both exposition and protocol.
- **1912:** *Der Hüter der Schwelle: Seelenvorgänge in szenischen Bildern* (GA 14/III). The third mystery drama.
- **1913:** *Die Schwelle der geistigen Welt: Aphoristische Ausführungen* (GA 17). A sequence of aphoristic contemplations rather than a continuous treatise.
- **1913:** *Der Seelen Erwachen: Seelische und geistige Vorgänge in szenischen Bildern* (GA 14/IV). The fourth and last completed mystery drama; drafts and paralipomena show that the dramatic project exceeded the four finished plays.
- **1914:** *Die Rätsel der Philosophie in ihrer Geschichte als Umriss dargestellt* (GA 18). A two-part history of philosophical thought, remade from Steiner’s earlier *Welt- und Lebensanschauungen im neunzehnten Jahrhundert* rather than simply reprinted from it.
- **1915:** *Gedanken während der Zeit des Krieges* (GA 19). Wartime essays and public interventions; the collected volume is a composite body, not one continuously composed book.
- **1916:** *Vom Menschenrätsel* (GA 20). A set of linked studies of German and Austrian intellectual figures.
- **1917:** *Von Seelenrätseln* (GA 21). A philosophical, psychological, and physiological essay collection, including Steiner’s formulation of the threefold human organism.
- **1918:** *Goethes Geistesart in ihrer Offenbarung durch seinen Faust und durch das Märchen von der Schlange und der Lilie* (GA 22). Two Goethe studies made into a single book.
- **1919:** *Die Kernpunkte der sozialen Frage in den Lebensnotwendigkeiten der Gegenwart und Zukunft* (GA 23). A political-economic manifesto for the “threefold social organism,” intended to intervene in postwar public life rather than merely describe it.
- **1919–1921:** *In Ausführung der Dreigliederung des sozialen Organismus* (principal contemporary nucleus of GA 24). A body of articles, appeals, memoranda, and political interventions; the present GA 24 also contains related writings from 1915–1921 and is therefore an editorial corpus larger than Steiner’s own book.
- **1922:** *Kosmologie, Religion und Philosophie*—later titled *Drei Schritte der Anthroposophie: Philosophie, Kosmologie, Religion* (GA 25). Ten written summaries made by Steiner from his French course; they are authorial précis of lectures, not transcripts.
- **1924–1925:** *Anthroposophische Leitsätze: Der Erkenntnisweg der Anthroposophie – Das Michael-Mysterium* (GA 26). Guiding propositions and accompanying letters to members; simultaneously doctrinal summary, institutional communication, and manifesto.
- **1925:** *Grundlegendes für eine Erweiterung der Heilkunst nach geisteswissenschaftlichen Erkenntnissen* (GA 27), with Ita Wegman. A jointly authored medical treatise; assigning every sentence separately to Steiner or Wegman is neither possible nor warranted.
- **1924–1925:** *Mein Lebensgang* (GA 28). An autobiography issued serially and left unfinished at Steiner’s death; the surviving obra is therefore also an interrupted project.

### Short writings, journalism, correspondence, verses, and unfinished texts

- **1884–1925:** *Gesammelte Aufsätze* (GA 29–36). Eight catalogued bodies of dramatic criticism, philosophy, science, aesthetics, cultural and literary criticism, biography, *Lucifer-Gnosis* articles, and contributions to *Das Goetheanum*. These are editorial corpora containing numerous individually titled things, not eight books conceived by Steiner as such.
- **1881–1925:** *Briefe* (GA 38–39, with movement-related correspondence also in GA 262–264). A large, incompletely published body of letters; each letter is a made text, but the numbered volumes and their selection are posthumous editorial objects.
- **Various dates:** *Wahrspruchworte* (GA 40). A corpus of verses, inscriptions, dedications, meditative sayings, and related verbal forms; many resist classification as literature because they were made for particular persons, rituals, occasions, or exercises.
- **Various dates:** *Mysteriendramen: Entwürfe, Fragmente und Paralipomena* (GA 44). Drafts and abandoned dramatic material belonging to the mystery-drama project; their incompleteness is part of their status.
- **1910:** *Anthroposophie: Ein Fragment* (GA 45). An unfinished manuscript intended to articulate an anthroposophical account of the human senses; it is a project surviving as a fragment, not a completed treatise.
- **Various dates:** Literary drafts, stories, poems, dramatic fragments, notes, inscriptions, and other Nachlass texts (principally GA 38–49). The Gesamtausgabe preserves these as heterogeneous archival remains; some are finished miniature works, others preparatory traces that resist being called independent works.

### Spoken works and texts made by transmission

- **1888–1924:** Public lectures (GA 51–85). Hundreds of oral events subsequently organized into collected volumes; the events were Steiner’s spoken creations, but almost all printed “texts” derive from auditors’ shorthand rather than manuscripts written or authorized by him.
- **1900–1924:** Lectures to the Theosophical and Anthroposophical societies (GA 88–270). A vast corpus of extemporaneous speech—part teaching, part institutional practice, part recurring research project—preserved through notes and stenograms of radically unequal completeness.
- **1907–1924:** Specialist lectures and courses on art, speech, eurythmy, education, medicine, science, agriculture, social questions, theology, and practical work (GA 271–354). These include course-like protocols that founded or redirected practices, but their published wording remains an editorial manifestation produced from listeners’ records.
- **1904–1924:** Esoteric lessons, meditations, cultic texts, and ritual instructions (especially GA 264–270). These are protocols and performative texts made for restricted groups; secrecy, oral transmission, later recollection, and posthumous editing complicate both completeness and attribution.
- **1922–1924:** Lectures to workers at the Goetheanum (GA 347–354). Dialogic spoken works shaped by workers’ questions; treating them as conventionally authored books suppresses both their oral occasion and their editorial mediation.

### Institutional and social projects

- **1902–1912:** The German Section of the Theosophical Society under Steiner’s leadership. An institutional and pedagogical project made through organization, lectures, publications, rituals, and collaboration; it is not solely his obra even though he decisively formed it.
- **1904–1914:** The Esoteric School and its later independent classes. A teaching and ritual project consisting of oral instruction, written exercises, symbols, and communal practice; much survives only through participant records.
- **1912 onward:** Anthroposophy as a named movement and field of practice. This is a body of work and a continuing collective manifestation, not a single text or an exclusively Steiner-made object.
- **1913:** The Anthroposophical Society. Steiner helped bring the organization into being but initially declined its presidency; institutional authorship is necessarily collective.
- **1917–1922:** The movement for the threefold social organism. A political project made through manifestos, petitions, associations, lectures, newspapers, and attempted institutions; its failures and incompletion belong to the work.
- **1919:** The first Waldorf school in Stuttgart and its educational design. Steiner devised the curriculum, trained and supervised teachers, and directed the founding, but the school itself was collaboratively financed, administered, taught, and inhabited.
- **1920–1924:** Anthroposophical medicine. A collaborative medical project developed especially with Ita Wegman and practicing physicians through courses, clinical work, medicines, and the jointly authored GA 27; it should not be attributed to Steiner alone.
- **1921:** The Christian Community. Steiner supplied lectures, counsel, and ritual texts to its founders but did not found or govern the church himself; calling the denomination his creation would erase the agency of Friedrich Rittelmeyer and the founding priests.
- **1922–1924:** Curative education. A practical-pedagogical impulse articulated in consultations and the 1924 course later published as GA 317; its institutional realization was made by practitioners around him.
- **1923–1924:** The refounded General Anthroposophical Society and the Free School of Spiritual Science. A constitutional, pedagogical, and performative project inaugurated at the Christmas Conference; it remained unfinished at Steiner’s death.
- **1924:** The Agriculture Course and the agricultural experimental project (GA 327). Eight lectures, discussions, preparations, and experimental instructions that became a protocol for biodynamic agriculture; the subsequent movement is a collective afterlife, not wholly Steiner’s work.

### Theatre, speech, and eurythmy

- **1907–1913:** Munich congress and festival productions. Programmes, seals, stage environments, dramatic presentations, and collaborative performances made these events early Gesamtkunstwerke rather than isolated stage texts.
- **1910–1913:** Productions of the four mystery dramas. Steiner wrote and directed the dramas and shaped scenery, costume, colour, gesture, and speech, while performers and craftspeople realized them; authorship of the resulting stage events is collaborative.
- **1912 onward:** Eurythmy. A movement art developed through indications first explored with Lory Smits and subsequently elaborated with Marie Steiner and many performers; it is better understood as a continuing artistic project and embodied vocabulary than as one object invented at a single moment.
- **1912–1924:** Eurythmy forms for poems, music, speech sounds, and dramatic works. Choreographic diagrams and movement indications whose completed manifestation requires performers; many were drawn for particular pieces and survive in the artistic Nachlass.
- **1912–1924:** Speech formation (*Sprachgestaltung*). An artistic-pedagogical practice developed principally with Marie Steiner through exercises, rehearsals, directing, and courses; it resists exclusive individual attribution.
- **1921:** *Eurythmie als sichtbare Sprache* (GA 279). A lecture-and-demonstration course functioning as an oral and embodied protocol; the printed text alone is not the whole work.
- **1924:** *Eurythmie als sichtbarer Gesang* (GA 278). A course establishing principles for tone eurythmy; again, its diagrams, demonstrations, and performed movement are integral.
- **1921–1922:** *Heileurythmie* (GA 315). A therapeutic movement protocol developed for medical practitioners and therapists; its realization belongs to a collaborative clinical practice.

### Architecture and designed environments

- **1907:** Theosophical Congress Hall, Munich. Steiner’s scheme of seals, columns, colour, and spatial symbolism was an event architecture and the first major manifestation of his architectural-artistic vocabulary; execution was collaborative.
- **1908–1909:** Malsch model building. A small domed meeting structure developed from Steiner’s ideas and realized by Ernst August Karl Stockmeyer and others; it is an experimental architectural manifestation rather than an unambiguously sole-authored Steiner building.
- **1911:** Stuttgart branch house interior. A designed anthroposophical meeting environment incorporating Steiner’s colour and column motifs; the distinction between his indications and collaborators’ execution must be retained.
- **1913–1920:** *First Goetheanum* (originally the Johannesbau). A double-domed timber building integrating architecture, sculpture, painting, stained glass, furniture, drama, and movement—a paradigmatic Gesamtkunstwerk. Steiner directed and designed it with a large international workshop; it was destroyed by fire in 1922–1923, and sole authorship would misdescribe its making.
- **1913–1922:** Interior carvings, capitals, architraves, murals, colour schemes, and furnishings of the First Goetheanum. Interdependent components of the building rather than readily separable works; many were executed by collaborating artists from Steiner’s models, sketches, or verbal indications.
- **1913–1922:** Stained-glass window programme for the First Goetheanum. Steiner supplied motifs, sketches, and direction, while Assja Turgenieff and other collaborators translated them into engraved glass; the surviving designs and the realized windows are different objects.
- **1914:** Heizhaus, Dornach. A boiler house whose concrete form and chimney make the combustion process into architectural imagery.
- **1914:** Glass houses (*Glashäuser*), Dornach. Twin workshop buildings made for cutting and engraving the Goetheanum windows; utility buildings shaped as part of the wider campus composition.
- **1914–1915:** Haus Duldeck, Dornach. A residence designed for the Grossheintz family and an architectural transformation of the Goetheanum’s double-dome motif.
- **1918:** Haus Hansi, Dornach. A residential design associated with the developing Goetheanum colony; realization involved architectural collaborators.
- **1920–1921:** Eurythmy houses, Dornach. Three residences developed with the participation of Edith Maryon; their collaboration makes exclusive attribution inappropriate.
- **1921:** Transformer house, Dornach. A small technical building whose interlocking cubic forms give architectural expression to its electrical function.
- **1921–1922:** Haus de Jaager, Dornach. A combined dwelling and exhibition studio designed for Isabella de Jaager; its carved interior was executed by sculptors associated with the Goetheanum, making it another collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk.
- **1923:** North addition to Haus Brodbeck, later part of the Rudolf-Steiner-Halde. A built extension designed by Steiner; it should not be confused with the pre-existing house.
- **1924:** Eurythmy rehearsal-room addition at the Rudolf-Steiner-Halde. A rehearsal space anticipating formal elements of the Second Goetheanum.
- **1924:** Verlagshaus, Dornach. A book-storage building for the Philosophisch-Anthroposophischer Verlag.
- **1924:** Haus Schuurman, Dornach. A comparatively restrained residence designed for musicians Max and Ina Schuurman.
- **1924:** Second Goetheanum project. Steiner’s concrete design survives in plans and a sculptural model; construction occurred after his death, with substantial technical interpretation by other architects. The project is his, while the completed 1928 building cannot simply be treated as an untouched Steiner autograph.

### Sculpture, models, and carving

- **1913–1922:** Architectural models and plastic studies for the First Goetheanum. Working objects used to communicate forms to a collective carving workshop; their protocol-like function complicates their status as autonomous sculptures.
- **1917–1922:** *Der Menschheitsrepräsentant zwischen Luzifer und Ahriman* (“Representative of Humanity”), with Edith Maryon. A monumental wooden sculptural group conceived by Steiner and carved in close collaboration with Maryon; the persistent attribution to Steiner alone is disputed by the practical history of its making.
- **1924:** Model for the Second Goetheanum. A sculptural architectural project model made when Steiner’s illness prevented completion of full construction documents; it is both an object and the chief vehicle of an unfinished building intention.
- **Various dates:** Furniture, lecterns, chairs, cabinets, door fittings, and interior objects. Functional designs catalogued in the artistic work; individual pieces range from Steiner’s direct drawings to workshop realizations based on oral indications, so attribution is not uniform.
- **Various dates:** Jewellery and small precious objects (*Kleinodienkunst*; GA K 51). Pendants, brooches, rings, crosses, and related designs given to Bertha Meyer-Jacobs and other goldsmiths; Steiner generally supplied designs or indications while the goldsmiths made the physical objects.

### Painting, drawing, and graphic design

- **1914–1918:** Designs for the paintings of the First Goetheanum’s small cupola (GA K 14). Coloured and pencil studies for a mural programme, including later detail studies; the executed ceiling involved several painters and is not identical with the surviving designs.
- **1913–1922:** Painted programme of the First Goetheanum. Murals and colour fields conceived within the building’s Gesamtkunstwerk and executed by Steiner with collaborating painters; much was destroyed with the building.
- **Various dates:** *Das malerische Werk* (GA K 13–16/52–56). The catalogue body includes paintings, Goetheanum mural designs, an eurythmy-programme design, teaching sketches, “nature mood” exercises, plant-colour studies, and motif sketches. It is a catalogue-defined corpus, not a single work made under that title.
- **1907–1925:** *Das graphische Werk* (GA K 45). A catalogue raisonné of seals, zodiac signs, membership cards, book and journal designs, initials, organizational graphics, and undated designs; individual responsibility ranges from finished autograph drawings to workshop executions.
- **1907:** Seven apocalyptic seals and column motifs. Symbolic designs made for the Munich congress and subsequently transformed for Malsch, Stuttgart, and the First Goetheanum; painted realizations by Clara Rettich, Karl Stahl, and others must be distinguished from Steiner’s designs.
- **1912–1913:** Zodiac designs and the 1912/13 calendar. Graphic and calendrical constructions combining new emblematic forms with an alternative naming system; they are design objects as much as texts.
- **1910–1913:** Seals and graphic material for the four mystery dramas. Emblematic adjuncts to the theatrical cycle, not independent literary works.
- **1919–1925:** Book, periodical, membership-card, and organizational graphics. Applied graphic works for the social-threefolding movement, publishing houses, enterprises, the Anthroposophical Society, and the Free School; the catalogue contains dated and undated pieces.
- **1919–1924:** Blackboard drawings accompanying lectures (published as GA K 58 in numerous portfolio volumes). Ephemeral coloured-chalk diagrams produced while speaking and preserved because black paper was placed over the boards; they are traces of events, visual arguments, and performances rather than conventional easel drawings.

### Calendars, diagrams, teaching images, and other hybrid objects

- **1912–1913:** *Kalender 1912/13*. A printed calendar combining chronology, zodiacal graphic design, new names, and spiritual-historical indications; a hybrid informational and symbolic object.
- **1912–1913:** *Seelenkalender*. Fifty-two weekly verses first issued within the calendar; simultaneously a poetic cycle, meditative protocol, and temporal instrument.
- **Various dates:** Planetary, colour, sound, speech, educational, medical, and scientific diagrams. These occur in manuscripts, course notes, blackboard drawings, and artistic portfolios; many function only within a lecture or exercise and resist elevation into individually titled autonomous works.
- **1922–1924:** Designs for eurythmy figures (GA K 26). Thirty-eight principal figure designs encoding sound, movement, colour, and bodily emphasis; the drawings are protocols for movement, while the later coloured figures made by others are collaborative realizations.
- **Various dates:** Teaching drawings and painting exercises. Images made to train seeing or guide another artist rather than to circulate as finished artworks; their resistance to conventional “work” status is precisely their instructional function.
