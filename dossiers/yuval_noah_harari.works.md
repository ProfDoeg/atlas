## Works by Yuval Noah Harari

### Scholarly thesis and monographs

- **2002:** *History and I: War and the Relations between History and Personal Identity in Renaissance Military Memoirs, c. 1450–1600*. Harari’s Oxford DPhil thesis: an unpublished scholarly dissertation and the project from which his first monograph developed.
- **2004:** *Renaissance Military Memoirs: War, History and Identity, 1450–1600*. A scholarly monograph on soldiers’ autobiographical writing, substantially derived from the doctoral project.
- **2007:** *Special Operations in the Age of Chivalry, 1100–1550*. A scholarly military-history monograph about small-scale covert and irregular operations; “special operations” is Harari’s analytical category, not a medieval institutional designation.
- **2008:** *The Ultimate Experience: Battlefield Revelations and the Making of Modern War Culture, 1450–2000*. A scholarly monograph tracing the emergence of battle as a privileged source of personal revelation.

### Scholarly articles and chapters

- **1999:** *Inter-frontal Cooperation in the Fourteenth Century and Edward III’s 1346 Campaign*. A research article in *War in History* on coordination between geographically separate forces during the Crécy campaign.
- **2000:** *Strategy and Supply in Fourteenth-Century Western European Invasion Campaigns*. A research article in *The Journal of Military History* treating logistics as a determinant of medieval strategy.
- **2005:** *Martial Illusions: War and Disillusionment in Twentieth-Century and Renaissance Military Memoirs*. A comparative research article—classified somewhat misleadingly as a “review article” in the Hebrew University repository—about changing expectations of war.
- **2007:** *Knowledge, Power and the Medieval Soldier, 1096–1550*. A scholarly chapter in *In Laudem Hierosolymitani: Studies in Crusades and Medieval Culture in Honour of Benjamin Z. Kedar*.
- **2007:** *The Concept of “Decisive Battles” in World History*. A historiographical article testing whether the traditional category of the decisive battle remains useful in world history.
- **2008:** *Combat Flow: Military, Political, and Ethical Dimensions of Subjective Well-Being in War*. An interdisciplinary article in *Review of General Psychology* connecting soldiers’ reported absorption in combat with psychological “flow” and examining the ethical dangers of cultivating it.
- **2010:** *Armchairs, Coffee and Authority: Eye-witnesses and Flesh-witnesses Speak about War, 1100–2000*. A research article in *The Journal of Military History* about the changing authority accorded to direct bodily experience of war.
- **2023–2024:** *Managing Extreme AI Risks amid Rapid Progress*. A short consensus paper first circulated as a 2023 preprint and subsequently published in *Science* in 2024. Harari is one of twenty-four co-authors, so this is a collective intervention—part analysis, part governance protocol—rather than a solely authored work.

### Macrohistory, futurism, and public philosophy

- **2011–2014:** *A Brief History of Humankind* / *Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind*. A macrohistorical synthesis originating in Harari’s Hebrew University survey lectures, first published in Hebrew in 2011 and substantially revised and translated into English in 2014. The translated editions are manifestations of the same obra, not separate works, although the English text was not a mechanically identical reproduction of the Hebrew book.
- **2015–2016:** *The History of Tomorrow* / *Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow*. A speculative history of humanity’s technological projects, first published under a Hebrew title in 2015 and internationally as *Homo Deus* in 2016–17; the differing publication dates belong to language manifestations rather than distinct obras.
- **2018:** *21 Lessons for the 21st Century*. A collection of interconnected public-philosophy essays about contemporary political, technological, and existential problems.
- **2024:** *Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI*. A macrohistorical book treating stories, documents, bureaucracies, mass media, computers, and AI as successive forms of information network.

### Graphic-history Gesamtkunstwerk

- **2020:** *Sapiens: A Graphic History, Volume 1—The Birth of Humankind*. A full-colour graphic adaptation created and co-written by Harari with writer David Vandermeulen and illustrator Daniel Casanave, with colours by Claire Champion. It is not simply an illustrated edition of *Sapiens*: fictional presenters, comics, game shows, art parody, and popular-culture forms make it a collaborative, multimedia-like re-composition.
- **2021:** *Sapiens: A Graphic History, Volume 2—The Pillars of Civilization*. The second collaborative graphic-history volume, reworking the Agricultural Revolution through comics, mock journalism, tragedy, and travel advertising.
- **2024:** *Sapiens: A Graphic History, Volume 3—The Masters of History*. The third collaborative volume, staging money, religion, empire, contingency, and historical causation through a fictional “superpowers” competition.
- **2026:** *Sapiens: A Graphic History, Volume 4—The Age of Revolutions*. The announced fourth and concluding graphic volume, scheduled for 3 November 2026 and therefore, as of 23 October 2026, a completed or near-completed publishing project rather than a publicly manifested book. Its announced subject is the scientific and industrial revolutions through the emergence of AI.

### Illustrated history for children

- **2022:** *Unstoppable Us, Volume 1: How Humans Took Over the World*. A middle-grade illustrated history written by Harari and illustrated by Ricard Zaplana Ruiz; it adapts elements of the *Sapiens* project for children but is a newly composed work rather than an abridged edition.
- **2024:** *Unstoppable Us, Volume 2: Why the World Isn’t Fair*. The second illustrated middle-grade volume, about agriculture, hierarchy, inequality, and the human stories that sustain social orders.
- **2026:** *Unstoppable Us, Volume 3: How Enemies Become Friends*. The third illustrated volume, about trade, migration, intermarriage, cities, religions, and other mechanisms through which formerly separate peoples form shared societies.
- **Announced as a four-volume project; fourth volume undated and untitled:** *Unstoppable Us, Volume 4*. This resists treatment as a finished work: Harari’s official account establishes the intention to make four volumes, but supplies neither a fixed title nor a publication date. It is therefore a project, not yet a manifestation.

### Institutional and social project

- **2019–:** *Sapienship*. A social-impact company co-founded by Harari and Itzik Yahav for research, education, and storytelling. It is not straightforwardly an authored text or solitary artwork: it is an ongoing collaborative project and production framework through which several later creations have been developed.

### Forthcoming project

- **Announced for 21 September 2027:** *The Laws of History*. A forthcoming book proposing six laws governing historical processes. Because publication lies in the future, the defensible classification is an announced project bearing a fixed title and date, not yet a manifested book.
