## Works by Engelbert Humperdinck

### Operas, Singspiele, and other staged creations

- **1890:** *Hänsel und Gretel*. A Liederspiel, the first completed form of the work; it is distinct from the later Singspiel and through-composed opera (Ir 1.1.1; EHWV 93.1).
- **1890–1891:** *Hänsel und Gretel*. A Singspiel expansion of the Liederspiel and an intermediate manifestation rather than merely an edition of the opera (Ir 1.1.2; EHWV 93.2).
- **1891–1893:** *Hänsel und Gretel*. A through-composed Märchenoper in three scenes, with a libretto by Adelheid Wette; Humperdinck subsequently supplied alternative performance material, including the “Dessau ending” (Ir 1.1.3; EHWV 93.3).
- **1895–1897:** *Königskinder*. A three-act melodrama on Elsa Bernstein’s play, notable for its specially notated accompanied speech; it is the first, substantially different manifestation of the work (Ir 1.2.1; EHWV 106.1).
- **1908–1910:** *Königskinder*. A through-composed Märchenoper made by extensively recasting the earlier melodrama, not simply by revising its orchestration (Ir 1.2.2; EHWV 106.2).
- **1902:** *Dornröschen*. A Märchenoper with text by Elisabeth Ebeling (Ir 1.3; EHWV 121).
- **1902–1905:** *Die Heirat wider Willen*. A comic opera in three acts, with a libretto adapted by Hedwig Humperdinck from Alexandre Dumas (Ir 1.4; EHWV 130).
- **1913:** *Die Marketenderin*. A Singspiel with a libretto by Robert Misch (Ir 1.5; EHWV 155).
- **1915–1919:** *Gaudeamus*. An opera completed late in Humperdinck’s life, to a libretto by Robert Misch (Ir 1.6; EHWV 162).
- **1888:** *Schneewittchen*. A Liederspiel on a text by Adelheid Wette, conceived for domestic or children’s theatre rather than as a full-scale opera (Ir 1.7; EHWV 9).
- **1895:** *Die sieben Geißlein*. A one-act Singspiel on a text by Adelheid Wette (Ir 1.8; EHWV 100).
- **1906:** *Bübchens Weihnachtstraum*. A Christmas Singspiel or melodramatic stage piece on a text by Gustav Falke (Ir 1.9; EHWV 136).
- *Rizzio’s Tod*. An opera fragment; its incomplete state makes it a surviving project rather than a finished stage work (Ir 1.10).
- *Uriel Akosta*. An opera fragment and unrealized dramatic project (Ir 1.11).
- **1883:** *Fedalma*. An opera fragment, also encountered under the variant spelling *Fedelma*; it was not completed (Ir 1.12; EHWV 80).
- **1868:** *Harziperes*. A juvenile opera or music drama now lost; only the record of the project survives (Ir 1.13; EHWV 3).
- **1868:** *Perla*. A juvenile Singspiel now lost (Ir 1.14; EHWV 4).
- **1868–1872:** *Claudine von Villabella*. A juvenile opera after Goethe, now lost; the title also appears as *Claudine von Villa Bella* (Ir 1.15; EHWV 5).
- *Dr. Katzenberger im Bad*. An unrealized operatic project. It resists classification as a completed work because the catalogue records an intention rather than a finished score (Ir 1.16).
- *Der Bärenhäuter*. An unrealized stage project (Ir 1.17).
- *Das Wunder zu Köln*. An unrealized stage project, distinct from the later music for Vollmöller’s *Das Wunder* (Ir 1.18).
- *Das steinerne Herz*. An unrealized stage project (Ir 1.19).
- *Fra Angelico*. An unrealized stage project (Ir 1.20).

### Incidental music and theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk contributions

- *Jeanne d’Arc*. Incidental music now lost; Irmen’s contents spell the title *Jeanne d’Are*, apparently a typographical error (Ir 2.1).
- **1879–1881, revised later:** *Die Frösche*. Incidental music for Aristophanes’ comedy; sources disagree on whether the broader working span should extend to 1886 (Ir 2.2; EHWV 61).
- **1883:** *Der Richter von Zalamea*. Incidental music for Calderón’s play (Ir 2.3; EHWV 82).
- **1905:** *Der Kaufmann von Venedig*. Incidental music for Shakespeare’s *The Merchant of Venice*, created for Max Reinhardt’s theatre (Ir 2.4; EHWV 133).
- **1906:** *Ein Wintermärchen*. Incidental music for Shakespeare’s *The Winter’s Tale*; it is also catalogued as *Das Wintermärchen* (Ir 2.5; EHWV 135).
- **1906:** *Der Sturm*. Incidental music for Shakespeare’s *The Tempest* (Ir 2.6; EHWV 138).
- **1907:** *Was ihr wollt*. Incidental music for Shakespeare’s *Twelfth Night* (Ir 2.7; EHWV 140).
- **1908:** *Lysistrata*. Incidental music for Aristophanes’ comedy (Ir 2.8; EHWV 141).
- **1910:** *Der blaue Vogel*. Incidental music for Maurice Maeterlinck’s play (Ir 2.9; EHWV 150).
- **1911–1912:** *Das Wunder*. Music for Karl Vollmöller and Max Reinhardt’s large-scale wordless mystery spectacle. Because music, mass movement, architecture, lighting, and performance were conceived together, Humperdinck’s contribution belongs to a theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an autonomous “book” (Ir 2.10; EHWV 151).
- **1914:** *Weihnachten im Schützengraben*. Incidental music for Ludwig Thoma’s wartime one-act play, initially titled *Christnacht 1914* (Ir 2.11; EHWV 157).

### Arrangements, editions, and collaborative transformations

- *Le Cheval de bronze (Das eherne Pferd)*. Humperdinck’s German-stage adaptation of Daniel-François-Esprit Auber’s opera; authorship is transformative and collaborative, not wholly original composition (Ir 8.1).
- *Huldigungsmarsch in E-flat major, WWV 97*. An arrangement of Wagner’s march (Ir 8.2.1).
- *Symphony in C major, WWV 29*. An arrangement of Wagner’s symphony (Ir 8.2.2).
- *Tannhäuser*. A group of arrangements from Wagner’s opera rather than a new opera by Humperdinck (Ir 8.2.3).
- *Tristan und Isolde*. A group of arrangements from Wagner’s music drama (Ir 8.2.4).
- *Die Walküre*. A group of arrangements from Wagner’s music drama (Ir 8.2.5).
- *Götterdämmerung*. A group of arrangements from Wagner’s music drama (Ir 8.2.6).
- *Parsifal*. A group of arrangements from Wagner’s stage-consecrating festival drama, including a four-hand piano reduction; these are manifestations made from Wagner’s work (Ir 8.2.7).
- *Selections from The Well-Tempered Clavier*. Arrangements of music by Johann Sebastian Bach (Ir 8.3).
- *Adagio cantabile*. An arrangement of music by Joseph Haydn (Ir 8.4).
- *Cadenza for Mozart’s Piano Concerto in C minor, K. 491*. A new performative insertion made for Mozart’s concerto rather than an autonomous concerto (Ir 8.5).
- *Cadenza for Beethoven’s Piano Concerto No. 4 in G major*. A new performative insertion for Beethoven’s concerto (Ir 8.6).
- *Beethoven’s Piano Sonata, Op. 2 No. 3*. An arrangement or editorial transformation of Beethoven’s sonata (Ir 8.7).
- *Sérénade berceuse*. An arrangement of music by Charles Gounod (Ir 8.8).
- *Scherzo from String Quartet, Op. 12*. An arrangement of music by Giovanni Sgambati (Ir 8.9).

### Working books, studies, and compositional protocols

- *Sketchbooks I–VII*. Seven working books containing compositional sketches. They resist being called seven finished works: they document process, alternatives, and unrealized intentions (Ir 9.1).
- *Contrapunkt-Übungen*. Counterpoint exercises; pedagogical studies rather than public-facing compositions (Ir 9.2).
- *Partimenti ossia Basso numerato*. Exercises or studies in figured bass, functioning as compositional protocols (Ir 9.3).
- *Copies from scores by Auber, Cherubini, Wagner, and others*. Study copies made in Humperdinck’s hand. They are material artefacts he made, but not works he authored in the ordinary compositional sense (Ir 9.4).
- **1885:** *Ensayo de un Metodo del Armonia*. An unpublished Spanish-language project for a method of harmony; as an instructional text it is a protocol as much as a treatise (Ir 9.5).
- *Instrumentationslehre*. An instrumentation treatise or teaching manuscript; it is instructional rather than a musical composition (Ir 9.6).

### Prose, criticism, and literary projects

- **1887–1888:** *Konzertkritiken von Engelbert Humperdinck*. A body of concert criticism written principally for the *Bonner Zeitung*. This is a corpus rather than one fixed text, and its boundaries depend on attribution of individual newspaper notices (Ir 10.1).
- **1895:** *Joseph Haydn: Symphonie in Es-dur*. An analytical concert guide or musicological essay (Ir 10.2).
- **1879–1881:** *Transzendental-ästhetisches System der Plastik*. An unfinished aesthetic-philosophical manuscript project. It resists classification as a completed treatise because it survives as sketches toward a system (Ir 10.3).
- *Über Tonmalerei*. A prose essay on musical tone-painting (Ir 10.4).
- **1907:** *Parsifal-Skizzen*. Personal recollections and reflections on Wagner and the first *Parsifal* production, published under a title that identifies them as sketches rather than a systematic history (Ir 10.5).
- *Die Zeitlose: Ein modernes Traummärchen*. A literary dream-tale drawing on childhood memories of Siegburg; it survives as a prose creation rather than a musical work and was published posthumously (Ir 10.6).

- Landschaftsverband Rheinland, *Portal Rheinische Geschichte*, “Engelbert Humperdinck.” Consulted for the dating and three-movement conception of the Piano Quintet, *Maurische Rhapsodie*, and late C-major String Quartet: [biographical essay](https://www.rheinische-geschichte.lvr.de/Persoenlichkeiten/engelbert-humperdinck/DE-2086/lido/57c9265a97df43.67274711).
