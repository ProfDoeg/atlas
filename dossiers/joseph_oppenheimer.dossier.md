# Joseph Süß Oppenheimer: Research Dossier

## Scope and evidentiary method

Joseph Süß Oppenheimer’s surviving record is unusually large but unusually compromised. Approximately 7.5 linear metres of investigative, trial, property, and bankruptcy records survive in the Hauptstaatsarchiv Stuttgart, principally in record group A 48/14. Much of this material was created or selected by officials seeking evidence for a prosecution whose capital outcome appears to have been politically predetermined. The archive also preserves confiscated business correspondence, account books, bills of exchange, inventories, and private papers that predate the prosecution and therefore provide a partly independent documentary base.

Labels used below:

- **Documented fact:** directly established by contemporary records or strong archival evidence.
- **Reported fact:** stated by credible later scholarship but not independently verified here from the underlying document.
- **Allegation:** asserted during the investigation, trial, or later controversy without an adequate finding of fact.
- **Court finding:** formally accepted by the tribunal; this does not imply that the proceeding was lawful or evidentially sound.
- **Propaganda:** a claim or representation designed primarily for political or confessional mobilization.
- **Rumor or myth:** a recurrent story lacking adequate documentary support.
- **Unresolved:** available evidence does not permit a secure conclusion.

Dates before 1732, particularly the birth year and itinerant apprenticeship, remain less securely documented than his Württemberg career.

## Basic Identifying Information

**Full name:** Joseph Süß Oppenheimer.

**Expanded Jewish patronymic:** Joseph ben Issachar Süßkind Oppenheimer; modern commemorative usage sometimes gives “Joseph Ben Issachar Süßkind Oppenheimer.”

**Variants:** Joseph Süss Oppenheimer; Josef Süß Oppenheimer; Joseph Süß-Oppenheimer; Joseph ben Issachar; Joseph Oppenheimer.

**Historical epithet:** “Jud Süß” or “Jew Süss.” This became the best-known designation but was commonly derogatory and eventually inseparable from hostile literature and Nazi propaganda. It should not be mistaken for a pseudonym chosen by Oppenheimer.

**Titles and functions:**

- Merchant and private banker
- Dealer in jewels, precious metals, commodities, and bills of exchange
- Tax and revenue contractor
- Mint contractor or mint entrepreneur
- Military supplier and war factor
- Court factor (*Hof- und Kriegsfaktor*)
- Private-purse administrator or agent (*Schatullenverwalter*)
- Württemberg resident or minister-resident at Frankfurt
- Ducal financial adviser
- Privy financial councillor (*Geheimer Finanzrat*), from 1736

**Religion:** Jewish. The degree and character of his observance during his commercial career are debated; his refusal to convert before execution and his preparation for death with Jewish visitors are well attested.

**Birth:** Most recent institutional chronologies give **February or March 1698**, or more cautiously **1698/99**, at Heidelberg in the Electoral Palatinate. Older literature gives 1692 or approximately 1690. No surviving birth registration settles the matter conclusively.

**Death:** 4 February 1738, at the public execution ground near Stuttgart, commonly identified with the Galgenberg/Mönchshalde or later Pragsattel area.

**Cause of death:** Judicial strangulation or hanging within a specially constructed iron cage.

**Burial:** His body was displayed in the cage for approximately six years. In 1744, when Duke Carl Eugen assumed personal rule, the remains were reportedly taken down and buried beneath or near the gallows. The exact grave is not identifiable.

**Marital status:** No documented marriage. Contemporary records identify Luciana Fischer as his companion or lover. No securely documented children are known.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents

**Father — Issachar or Isachar Süßkind Oppenheimer:** **Reported/documented in family and fiscal records.** He is described as a merchant, tax collector for the Jewish population of the Palatinate, rabbi, and at times director or participant in a travelling Jewish singing company. The descriptions may refer to several phases or overlapping functions rather than one fixed occupation.

**Mother — Michal, Michele, or Michaela Chasan:** **Reported fact.** She came from a Frankfurt Jewish family and was reportedly the daughter of Salomon, called “Salmele,” a rabbi or cantor. She later lived in Frankfurt’s Judengasse while Joseph maintained business premises elsewhere in the city.

The family was connected to the extensive Oppenheimer commercial network. Joseph is regularly described as a relative—although the exact degree is uncertain—of the celebrated imperial court factor **Samuel Oppenheimer** of Vienna.

### Disputed paternity

Older biographical literature claimed that Joseph’s biological father was Baron Georg Eberhard von Heydersdorf/Heydersdorff, a former Heidelberg commandant accused of prematurely surrendering the city or castle to French forces.

- **Classification:** Rumor or unresolved genealogical claim.
- **Evidence for it:** Repetition in nineteenth-century biography and later popular accounts.
- **Evidence against or limiting it:** No conclusive paternity document has been produced. Modern biographical reference works identify Issachar Süßkind as the father while placing a question mark beside some genealogical particulars.
- **Historical effect:** The story supported later portrayals of Oppenheimer as socially or religiously divided and was readily absorbed into fictional narratives about concealed ancestry.

### Siblings

A brother and sister are said in older sources to have converted to Christianity and adopted the surname **Tauffenberger**; the brother reportedly became a private secretary at the Darmstadt court.

- **Classification:** Reported fact, requiring confirmation from baptismal or court-personnel records.
- Their identities, dates, and subsequent relationships with Joseph remain obscure.

### Marriage, partners, and children

Oppenheimer remained legally unmarried. The trial archive includes a dedicated Ludwigsburg interrogation record concerning **Luciana Fischer**, identified by the Landesarchiv as his *Lebensgefährtin*, or life companion.

Claims that he fathered one or more children, or had a daughter whose death transformed him spiritually, belong chiefly to fiction—notably Lion Feuchtwanger’s novel—and are not established biography.

## Childhood and Early Life

Oppenheimer grew up in or near Heidelberg’s Jewish commercial community. His father acquired property in the Ingramgasse, now Ingrimstraße, in 1699; local historical reconstruction associates Joseph’s childhood with the house later numbered 8 and possibly with neighboring family properties.

Very little reliable evidence survives concerning his childhood curriculum. He probably received the basic religious and practical education expected in a commercially connected Jewish household: Hebrew literacy, Jewish observance, arithmetic, correspondence, and commercial calculation. Claims that he rejected serious study at an early age originate largely in older moralizing biographies.

No university attendance or formal professional qualification is known.

## Education and Formation

### Commercial apprenticeship and travel

The *Neue Deutsche Biographie* reports that from approximately **1713** Oppenheimer’s commercial apprenticeship or journeyman years took him to:

- Prague
- Amsterdam
- Vienna
- Frankfurt am Main
- Possibly other commercial centres

This itinerary is plausible because the Oppenheimer family network and the principal exchange, jewel, precious-metal, and court-finance markets connected those cities. The exact order and duration of the journeys are uncertain.

Older sources state that he worked in major Jewish merchant houses and learned:

- Bills of exchange and credit brokerage
- Currency and precious-metal arbitrage
- Court procurement
- Military supply
- Jewellery dealing
- Lending to noble clients
- The political uses of private credit

No individually documented teacher or mentor is known. The broader Oppenheimer family network, Frankfurt’s Jewish commercial community, and court-finance practitioners in Vienna, Mannheim, Darmstadt, and Bonn supplied his formative professional environment.

---

## Early Career, c. 1717–1732

### Frankfurt and Mannheim

By about **1717**, and more securely during the 1720s, Oppenheimer was operating as a private banker and merchant in Frankfurt and Mannheim. He maintained residences or offices in both cities and continued those businesses until his arrest.

His activities included:

- Lending and bill brokerage
- Jewellery and gemstone transactions
- Gold and silver trading
- Court procurement
- Currency and mint contracts
- Commodity contracting
- Dealings with aristocratic households

His Frankfurt clients reportedly included members of the German high nobility, among them the **Thurn und Taxis** family. This connection was important because Karl Alexander’s wife, Marie Auguste, belonged to that dynasty.

### Palatine stamped-paper lease

In **1723**, Oppenheimer became lessee or contractor for Electoral Palatinate stamped paper. Legal instruments and certain registrations had to be written on taxed, officially stamped paper.

- **Documented fact:** The concession itself is recorded in modern institutional chronologies.
- **Unresolved financial question:** Surviving summaries do not establish how profitable the undertaking was.

### Court business outside Württemberg

Before joining Karl Alexander, Oppenheimer undertook business for courts or governments at:

- Mannheim and the Electoral Palatinate
- Hesse-Darmstadt
- Bonn and Electoral Cologne
- Frankfurt

In 1733 he was still described as a court and war factor and mint entrepreneur for Landgrave Ernst Ludwig of Hesse-Darmstadt.

## Chronological Life History

## 1732: Meeting Karl Alexander

Oppenheimer reportedly met **Karl Alexander of Württemberg-Winnental** at Wildbad in autumn 1732. Stuttgart’s municipal historical reference work identifies the Württemberg court factor **Isaak Simon Landauer** as intermediary.

On **14 November 1732**, Karl Alexander issued Oppenheimer a protection patent appointing him court and war factor. The surviving patent assigned him principally to money transactions and military provisioning. Additional patents made him an agent or private-purse administrator for the princely couple.

He was not initially a salaried civil servant. Like many court factors, he combined service to the prince with commissions, concessions, brokerage, and private business.

## 1733: Accession and expanded authority

Karl Alexander became Duke of Württemberg in December 1733 after the death of Eberhard Ludwig. He was Catholic, while Württemberg’s population and constitutional establishment were overwhelmingly Lutheran.

Oppenheimer became Württemberg’s resident at Frankfurt. His ducal privilege permitted him to live outside the Judengasse, reportedly at the inn **Zum Goldenen Schwan** (“Golden Swan”), provoking opposition from Frankfurt’s council.

He displaced or superseded **Heinrich Ehrenfried Luther**, the previous Württemberg resident. Luther later participated in organizing confiscated material against him and regained the office after Oppenheimer’s arrest.

The War of the Polish Succession increased the need for troops, supplies, and credit. Oppenheimer received responsibility for provisioning ducal and Swabian forces and arranging loans, purchases, transport, and currency.

## 1734–1736: Württemberg financial and administrative ascendancy

From 1734 his work increasingly centred on Stuttgart and Ludwigsburg. Karl Alexander’s government needed to:

- Reduce inherited debt
- Finance an expensive court
- Support military commitments
- Raise revenue without dependence on the Protestant estates
- Expand the prince’s direct administrative authority

Oppenheimer’s surviving activities concerned minting, taxes, tolls, jewels, precious metals, military provisions, loans, commercial concessions, and proposals for state-directed manufactures.

### Policies and projects attributed to his administration

The degree to which each measure originated with Oppenheimer rather than the duke or other councillors varies. Contemporary and later sources associate him with:

- Restructuring mint and currency operations
- Leasing taxes and customs revenues
- Salt, leather, tobacco, and wine monopolies
- Expansion of ducal manufacturing privileges
- Silk and porcelain projects
- A state or court-supported bank, sometimes described as the first bank in southern Germany
- Commercial and industrial concessions
- Road tolls and related revenue devices
- Military provisioning
- Sale of honours or offices through the **Gratialamt**
- Attempts to bypass the estates’ control of taxation

The Gratialamt, created in 1736, collected payments connected with titles, positions, offices, and services. Its fiscal purpose was to increase ducal revenue and diminish the entrenched elite’s influence over appointments. It was widely regarded as office-selling and became one of Oppenheimer’s most hated projects.

### Wealth and private organization

Oppenheimer maintained business and residential premises in Frankfurt, Mannheim, Heidelberg, Stuttgart, and Ludwigsburg. His offices used Christian employees and agents as well as Jewish correspondents. His documentary estate contained household accounts, bills of exchange, commercial ledgers, correspondence, gold-and-silver records, and files on jewellery, customs, currency, and provisioning.

A surviving Mannheim account book was closed in Oppenheimer’s own hand on **13 October 1732**. A manual of active and passive bills of exchange survives for 1734. Numerous signed bills were recovered from his Frankfurt premises.

Accounts of lavish entertaining, costly furnishings, fashionable dress, art collecting, mistresses, and access to noble society recur in contemporary and later descriptions. The confiscation records confirm substantial possessions and a socially ambitious household, but publicized estimates of his fortune must be treated cautiously: after arrest, his assets were processed as a confiscated estate and bankruptcy mass, with disputed debts and receivables extending into the 1760s and 1770s.

No dependable single net-worth figure has been established here.

### The duchess

The archive contains financial papers involving Duchess Marie Auguste, including an instruction dated **23 September 1733** that Oppenheimer pay 100 gulden to a Darmstadt physician who assisted at her confinement. Investigators assembled a special file on what they characterized as suspicious relations between Oppenheimer and the duchess.

- **Documented fact:** He carried out financial and personal commissions for her.
- **Allegation:** That this relationship was conspiratorial, corrupt, or sexual.
- **Finding:** No persuasive evidence of a sexual relationship is established in the consulted sources.

### Privy financial councillor

In **June 1736**, Karl Alexander appointed Oppenheimer *Geheimer Finanzrat*. His influence extended beyond finance into appointments, administration, and occasionally judicial matters.

His enemies said he intended to become a de facto first minister. Modern scholarship treats that as a rumor. He was influential but was apparently not included in Karl Alexander’s innermost planning for a possible constitutional or religious coup against the estates.

### Attempts to resign

Beginning in August 1735, Oppenheimer repeatedly sought permission to leave ducal service. Karl Alexander refused and renewed promises of protection. The petitions suggest that Oppenheimer recognized the political danger created by his office, methods, and isolation.

## March 1737: Death of Karl Alexander and arrest

On **8 March 1737**, according to an older account, Oppenheimer balanced his accounts and obtained the duke’s acknowledgment that they were correct. This reported episode requires verification in the account records.

On the evening of **12 March 1737**, Karl Alexander died unexpectedly at Ludwigsburg Palace. There is no sound evidence that Oppenheimer caused or contributed to the death.

Within hours, officials placed Oppenheimer under arrest. Some accounts say he attempted to flee; others emphasize that Colonel Röder induced him to travel to Stuttgart under the pretext of informing the duchess. Both may describe successive moments. Stuttgart’s Jews were also temporarily detained or subjected to restrictions.

Oppenheimer was transferred to **Hohenneuffen fortress** and later, on or about **30 May 1737**, to **Hohenasperg**.

The duke’s minor son Carl Eugen could not yet rule. The sudden political reversal allowed officials and representatives of the estates to dismantle Karl Alexander’s government and pursue its most exposed, religiously isolated agent.

## Investigation and Trial, 1737–1738

### Investigative machinery

The criminal proceeding formally opened on **22 May 1737**. A special *Süßische Inquisitionskommission* directed the inquiry.

The surviving record includes:

- Oppenheimer’s interrogations
- Sworn testimony
- Confiscated correspondence and accounts
- Inventories from Stuttgart, Ludwigsburg, Frankfurt, Heidelberg, and Mannheim
- 607 *Landberichte* submitted from across Württemberg
- Defense papers
- Property-administration and bankruptcy records
- Judgment and execution reports

His first formal examination contained **170 questions**.

Authorities publicly ordered officials and subjects throughout Württemberg to report every accusation involving him. Between late March and June 1737, this produced 607 denunciatory reports alleging “country-destroying manipulations.” These were not findings of guilt. Their solicited character and the political climate sharply limit their evidentiary value.

Investigators sorted confiscated documents under potential charges such as business dealings, projects, customs privileges, coinage, jewellery, and provisioning. The Landesarchiv’s presentation concludes that the process sought material capable of “substantiating” a death sentence already envisaged.

Hebrew-language papers were usually translated for investigators and then destroyed, although some originals survived because they remained in Frankfurt. This destruction deprived later historians of an important independent part of the record.

### Charges and accusations

Oppenheimer was accused at various stages of:

- High treason
- Lèse-majesté or insulting sovereign authority
- Fraud
- Embezzlement
- Bribery and corrupt enrichment
- Sale of offices
- Oppression or “sucking dry” the country
- Debasement or manipulation of coinage
- Illegal monopolies and taxation
- Religious offenses against Protestant Württemberg
- Sexual intercourse with Christian women
- Adultery, seduction, or sexual misconduct
- Manipulating the duke
- Conspiratorial relations with the duchess
- Attempting to alter Württemberg’s constitution or confessional order

### Evidentiary assessment

**Financial self-enrichment:** Oppenheimer combined public service with private commercial profit. Conflicts of interest were inherent in court-factor arrangements, and evidence exists that he profited from concessions and commissions. The scale and criminality of particular gains were never impartially adjudicated.

**Bribery and office-selling:** Payments through the Gratialamt and patronage surrounding appointments were real. Whether individual transactions constituted punishable corruption under the applicable law was disputed by the defense.

**Coinage:** He participated in mint business and benefited from precious-metal and currency arrangements. The broad claim that he personally ruined the currency was politically amplified and cannot be treated as established by the judgment.

**Constitutional subversion:** Karl Alexander sought to reduce the estates’ power. Oppenheimer helped devise revenue outside their control. The stronger claim that he directed a coup or planned the forced Catholicization of Württemberg is unproven; modern scholarship notes that he was apparently excluded from the duke’s final secret plans.

**Sexual offenses:** Intimate relations with Christian women were criminalized under contemporary law and became a major source of sensational allegations. Luciana Fischer’s relationship with him is documented. Other claims were inconsistent or dropped, reportedly to avoid exposing socially prominent women. The later rape-and-suicide story central to the 1940 film is fiction, not a documented charge proved at trial.

**Religious offenses:** These allegations reflected the confessional politics of a Lutheran territory governed by a Catholic duke and the defendant’s Jewish status. They were not supported as distinct capital crimes.

### Defense

Oppenheimer was denied the lawyer of his own choosing. In June 1737 the authorities appointed the relatively inexperienced Tübingen advocate **Michael Andreas Mögling**.

Mögling prepared a substantial defense brief dated **11 January 1738**. The brief challenged the factual and legal sufficiency of the prosecution. It was later missing from the official trial file and was reacquired for the archive only in modern times.

Oppenheimer secretly passed notes to Mögling, asking whether his case endangered his life and reportedly promising 500 ducats. These notes show fear and an effort to secure energetic representation; they do not by themselves prove bribery of the lawyer.

### Judgment

The consulted sources give several procedural dates:

- A preliminary or commission-level condemnation is sometimes dated 17 December 1737 or 9 January 1738.
- The Landesarchiv dates the definitive death judgment to **25 January 1738**.
- It was announced to Oppenheimer on **31 January 1738**.

The published sentence ordered execution for his many committed and acknowledged “country-destroying misdeeds” but did not identify a specific proven capital offense or supply a reasoned legal analysis.

- **Court finding:** Guilty of numerous unspecified injurious crimes and condemned to death.
- **Modern historical assessment:** The proceeding involved fundamental irregularities, a politically selected tribunal, solicited denunciations, denial of chosen counsel, manipulation of evidence, and a sentence without a defined capital offense. It is consequently widely described as a show trial and judicial murder.
- **Important qualification:** That assessment does not establish that every challenged financial transaction was innocent. It means the proceeding did not reliably distinguish administrative policy, conflicts of interest, private profit, civil debt, and actual crime.

No principal Christian associate received an equivalent death sentence. Sentences against co-defendants were reduced or remitted.

## Imprisonment, Religious Pressure, and Final Days

Oppenheimer’s imprisonment lasted nearly eleven months. He became severely emaciated. One account attributes part of his deterioration to refusal of food that was not kosher. A surviving jailer’s report depicts him walking restlessly and appearing wasted, contradicting a contemporary propaganda image that presented him differently.

From December 1737 onward, Lutheran clergy and a converted Jewish lecturer attempted to persuade him to accept baptism. Pastor **Georg Konrad Rieger** is associated with one conversion effort. Oppenheimer refused.

On **30 January 1738**, he was brought from Hohenasperg to Stuttgart. The sentence was read the following day.

Jewish visitors, including representatives connected with Stuttgart’s small Jewish community, attended him shortly before death. Later accounts particularly identify **Mardochai Schloss**, **Salomon Schächter**, and **Marx Nathan**. Their reports must be read as religiously framed testimony but provide important evidence concerning his final declarations.

A reported statement—“I will die as a Jew; I suffer violence and injustice”—captures the consistent substance of several accounts, although the exact wording cannot be guaranteed.

---

## Execution and Death

On **4 February 1738**, Oppenheimer was transported before a crowd numbering in the thousands to a specially prepared execution structure outside Stuttgart.

He was placed in a red-painted iron cage and strangled or hanged. Contemporary and later Jewish accounts report that he recited the **Shema Yisrael**. The precise final words vary, but his refusal of conversion and Jewish profession at death are strongly supported.

His corpse remained suspended in the locked cage as a warning and spectacle for approximately six years. In 1744 Carl Eugen ordered or permitted the remains to be removed and buried near the gallows.

The prolonged display converted the execution into both punitive theatre and a durable visual symbol. Prints showed Oppenheimer with cages, gallows, devils, torture instruments, bags of money, and alleged victims.

### Financial structure

Oppenheimer often received no fixed salary. Profit came through commissions, spreads, leases, supply contracts, concessions, interest, and privileged access. This arrangement aligned his wealth with ducal policies while making it difficult to distinguish state money, princely private expenditure, working capital, receivables, and personal gain.

After arrest, an inventory deputation seized and administered his estate. The resulting proceedings were treated partly as a bankruptcy and continued for decades. Assets were taken from several residences, while creditors and debtors made competing claims. This post-arrest process makes simple claims that he possessed—or stole—a specified fortune unreliable without a full reconstruction of the estate accounts.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### Political position

Oppenheimer held no representative mandate and produced no known political treatise. His politics are reconstructed from the policies he implemented.

He served a program of:

- Stronger centralized ducal authority
- Reduced dependence on the Württemberg estates
- Mercantilist regulation
- State-directed monopoly and manufacturing
- Fiscal extraction through indirect revenues, fees, and concessions
- Professionalized procurement and credit

It is unclear how far he supported these policies ideologically rather than as a court entrepreneur executing his patron’s orders.

### Religion

During his rise, contemporaries criticized his cosmopolitan habits and relationships with Christians. Some later writers have called him a freethinker or atheist. That description remains interpretive rather than documented self-identification.

His final conduct shows a clear refusal to abandon Judaism under coercive conditions. It does not by itself establish how observant he had been throughout his earlier life.

### Social position

As a Jew in an early-modern Christian polity, he depended on personal protection rather than equal civil status. His access to the ruler gave him exceptional influence while leaving him without a stable institutional constituency. The court-factor system made him useful precisely because he could mobilize credit and act outside traditional administration; the same condition made him expendable when the patron died.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Karl Alexander, Duke of Württemberg

**Nature:** Patron, employer, debtor, political principal, and protector.

Their relationship was the central fact of Oppenheimer’s career. Karl Alexander directed policy and issued the relevant privileges. Later prosecution and propaganda commonly recast the duke as weak and deceived. Archival scholarship instead describes him as forceful, martial, impatient, and personally responsible for his commands.

### Duchess Marie Auguste of Thurn und Taxis

**Nature:** Client and member of the employing princely household.

Oppenheimer paid bills, procured goods, and handled confidential financial tasks for her. Investigators treated this proximity as suspicious. No sexual liaison is proved.

### Isaak Simon Landauer

**Nature:** Court-factor intermediary and later religious visitor or Jewish community contact.

Landauer reportedly introduced Oppenheimer to Karl Alexander at Wildbad. The precise relationship thereafter was complicated by competition among court factors.

### Luciana Fischer

**Nature:** Documented companion.

A dedicated interrogation record survives. Assertions beyond companionship—including criminal sexual conduct—must be evaluated individually and were not securely resolved.

### Heinrich Ehrenfried Luther

**Nature:** Displaced officeholder and institutional adversary.

Oppenheimer replaced him as Württemberg’s Frankfurt resident. After the arrest, Luther resumed that position and received confiscated papers for prosecutorial review.

### Michael Andreas Mögling

**Nature:** Court-appointed defense lawyer.

Mögling authored the defense brief and received secret notes from his client. He was appointed late and operated under severe procedural limitations.

### Karl Rudolf of Württemberg-Neuenstadt

**Nature:** Regent or administrator involved in confirming the capital sentence.

He exercised authority during Carl Eugen’s minority.

### Mardochai Schloss, Salomon Schächter, and Marx Nathan

**Nature:** Jewish communal figures connected with Oppenheimer’s final religious preparation and the publication of an account of his last hours.

Their narratives helped preserve a counter-memory of steadfast Jewish death against Christian conversion literature and hostile broadsides.

### Württemberg estates and “respectability”

**Nature:** Institutional opponents.

The estates defended traditional privileges, especially tax approval and Protestant constitutional arrangements. Oppenheimer’s revenue measures threatened their authority and economic interests. They were not a unitary modern parliament; they represented established corporate and elite interests.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, and Financial Questions

### Did he exploit Württemberg?

- **Documented:** He introduced or operated unpopular taxes, monopolies, fees, and concessions; mixed private business with public service; accumulated wealth and patronage.
- **Allegation:** He alone “ruined” or “sucked dry” the country.
- **Counter-evidence:** The duke ordered the policies; the government faced inherited debt, court expenditure, and war costs; investigators deliberately personalized collective government decisions.
- **Status:** Particular transactions may have involved profiteering, but the totalizing accusation was never fairly adjudicated.

### Was he corrupt?

- **Documented:** Office payments, commissions, concessions, and private profit existed.
- **Problem:** Early-modern court finance did not observe modern public/private separation. Some activities later called corruption were institutionalized.
- **Status:** Conflicts of interest are clear; the criminal extent is unresolved.

### Did he debase the currency?

- **Documented:** He participated in minting and precious-metal transactions.
- **Allegation:** Deliberate destructive debasement for personal enrichment.
- **Status:** The consulted sources do not establish a quantified fraudulent debasement attributable solely to him.

### Did he conspire to overthrow the Protestant constitution?

- **Documented:** He helped Karl Alexander raise revenue independently of the estates.
- **Allegation:** He planned a coup and Catholic transformation of Württemberg.
- **Counter-evidence:** Modern biographical scholarship states that Karl Alexander did not include him in the final secret planning.
- **Status:** Unsupported in its strongest form.

### Sexual allegations

- **Documented:** Luciana Fischer was his companion; Oppenheimer had intimate associations that violated social and possibly legal boundaries.
- **Allegations:** Numerous seductions, adultery, coercion, and relations with Christian women.
- **Procedural outcome:** Some sexual accusations were dropped or not specified in the final judgment.
- **Status:** Consensual relationships are plausible and partly documented; rape and the suicide of a virtuous Christian victim are later fictional propaganda.

### Relationship with the duchess

- **Documented:** Financial closeness and personal commissions.
- **Allegation:** Conspiracy or affair.
- **Status:** Unproved.

### Responsibility for Karl Alexander’s death

No credible evidence implicates Oppenheimer. The duke’s sudden death created the opportunity for his arrest. Suggestions of poisoning belong to later sensationalism unless new medical or documentary evidence emerges.

### Flight

Some sources say Oppenheimer tried to escape after the duke’s death; others show that officials maneuvered him into custody. A brief effort at flight is plausible and would have been rational in the circumstances. It does not demonstrate guilt on the substantive charges.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

## The omnipotent Jewish minister

**Story:** Oppenheimer secretly ruled Württemberg, removed honest councillors, staffed government with creatures, and controlled a weak duke.

**Appearance and circulation:** Trial propaganda, eighteenth- and nineteenth-century hostile histories, broadsides, and the 1940 film.

**Evidence:** He had exceptional influence and affected appointments. The duke, however, was not a passive puppet, and Oppenheimer was excluded from some of his patron’s most confidential plans.

**Effect:** It made him an archetype of the supposedly hidden Jewish adviser controlling Christian government.

## The plan to flood Württemberg with Jews

**Story:** Oppenheimer intended to abolish restrictions and bring masses of Jews into the duchy.

**Evidence:** No adequate evidence in the consulted archival summaries. Karl Alexander and Oppenheimer occasionally employed or admitted Jewish financiers, but this does not establish the alleged demographic conspiracy.

**Classification:** Propaganda.

## The rapist whose victim drowned herself

**Story:** Oppenheimer rapes the Christian Dorothea Sturm; she commits suicide, making his execution morally inevitable.

**Origin:** Veit Harlan’s 1940 film, using invented characters and motifs from earlier fictional treatments while reversing sympathetic elements found in Feuchtwanger.

**Evidence:** None as biography. No Dorothea Sturm corresponding to the film victim is established in the historical case.

**Effect:** This became one of the most influential antisemitic sexual narratives in European cinema.

## A secret daughter whose death redeemed him

**Story:** In Feuchtwanger’s novel, Oppenheimer has a daughter, Naemi; her death causes him to renounce power.

**Evidence:** Fictional construction, not documented family history.

**Effect:** It turned the historical court factor into a philosophical tragic protagonist divided between power and spiritual withdrawal.

## Illegitimate Christian noble ancestry

**Story:** Baron von Heydersdorff, rather than Issachar Oppenheimer, fathered Joseph.

**Evidence:** Repeated but unproved.

**Effect:** It enabled stories of divided blood, concealed identity, and ambiguous belonging.

## The coward at the gallows versus the Jewish martyr

Two opposed traditions developed immediately:

- Hostile broadsides portrayed him as terrified, damned, grotesque, and finally humiliated.
- Jewish and later sympathetic accounts portrayed him as composed, faithful, and martyred while reciting the Shema.

The jailer’s report suggests severe physical and emotional distress during the final hours. His refusal of baptism is nevertheless well supported. Terror and religious steadfastness are not mutually exclusive.

## The purely innocent modernizer

A twentieth-century counter-myth sometimes presents Oppenheimer as a disinterested capitalist reformer guilty of nothing but competence and Jewishness.

The prosecution was fundamentally unjust, but that does not resolve every question about commissions, monopolies, patronage, or private enrichment. The documentary position is more complex: he was both a politically exposed victim of judicial murder and an active participant in an extractive court-finance system.

## Works by Joseph Süß Oppenheimer

Oppenheimer was not an author in the conventional literary sense. No book, treatise, manifesto, or memoir is known to have been published by him during his lifetime.

### Genuine documentary writings

1. **Commercial correspondence**, principally 1720s–1737.  
   Letters to financiers, suppliers, court officials, noble clients, and agents. Many survive within Hauptstaatsarchiv Stuttgart A 48/14. Authorship varies between autograph letters, dictated correspondence, office copies, and letters addressed to him.

2. **Bills of exchange and financial instruments**, including numerous signed examples from his Frankfurt business.  
   Genuine business documents, not published works.

3. **Mannheim account book**, closed in his hand on 13 October 1732.  
   Archival manuscript.

4. **Manual über die Aktiv- und Passiv-Wechselbriefe**, 1734.  
   Office record of bills receivable and payable; the degree of personal authorship versus clerk preparation is uncertain.

5. **Household accounts, project memoranda, commercial proposals, and responses to officials**, chiefly 1733–1737.  
   Mixed autograph, dictated, and clerical material.

6. **Interrogation answers**, 1737.  
   Recorded by commission clerks and signed by Oppenheimer. They are attributable testimony rather than freely composed autobiography.

7. **Secret notes to Michael Andreas Mögling**, 1737–1738.  
   Short autograph or attributable prison notes concerning his defense.

8. **Defensive instructions and articles**, 1737–1738.  
   Prepared with or through counsel. Authorship is collaborative or dictated.

### Works sometimes catalogued under his name but not authored by him

1. **Briefe und Akten zum Prozess und zur Verteidigung des Joseph Süß Oppenheimer**, 1737–1738.  
   Modern digital title for a composite manuscript volume at Tübingen University Library, shelfmark Mh 468. It contains correspondence, defense material, special interrogatories, a defense-roll extract, and an asset list. It is an archival compilation, not a book published by Oppenheimer.

2. **Rechtliche Defensionsschrift des Juden Joseph Süss Oppenheimer**, 1738.  
   Legal defense associated with Michael Andreas Mögling. It represents Oppenheimer’s cause but was drafted by counsel.

3. **Trial interrogations and sentence publications**, 1737–1738.  
   Government or legal productions in which Oppenheimer is the subject or examined party, not the sole author.

No patents for inventions are known. His “projects” were fiscal, manufacturing, mint, monopoly, and banking proposals rather than patented technologies.

## Books and Major Works About the Person

### Primary and near-contemporary material

- **Briefe und Akten zum Prozess und zur Verteidigung des Joseph Süß Oppenheimer** (1737–1738), Tübingen University Library, Mh 468. Digital manuscript collection.
- **Rechtliche Defensionsschrift des Juden Joseph Süss Oppenheimer** (1738), defense prepared by Michael Andreas Mögling; modern scholarly edition by Robert Kretzschmar.
- **Salomon Schächter**, account of Oppenheimer’s last hours, reportedly financed or published with assistance from Marx Nathan in 1738. Important Jewish counter-narrative; titles and editions vary.
- Numerous anonymous or pseudonymous execution broadsides and pamphlets, including hostile poems describing Oppenheimer as an arch-deceiver and destroyer of the land (1738). These are primary sources for propaganda, not reliable neutral biographies.
- Trial, property, and bankruptcy records in Hauptstaatsarchiv Stuttgart A 48/14.

### Historical and biographical scholarship

- **Selma Stern**, *Jud Süss: Ein Beitrag zur deutschen und zur jüdischen Geschichte* (1929; reprinted 1973). Foundational archival biography.
- **Selma Stern**, *The Court Jew: A Contribution to the History of the Period of Absolutism in Central Europe* (1950). Places Oppenheimer within the institution of court Jewry.
- **Heinrich Schnee**, *Die Hoffinanz und der moderne Staat*, vol. III (1955) and vol. IV (1963). Court finance and early-modern state formation.
- **Peter Baumgart**, “Joseph Süß Oppenheimer: Das Dilemma des Hofjuden im absoluten Fürstenstaat,” in *Geschichte und Kultur des Judentums* (1988), pp. 91–110.

- **Hellmut G. Haasis**, *Joseph Süß Oppenheimer, genannt Jud Süß: Finanzier, Freidenker, Justizopfer* (1998). Extensive source-based popular biography; some interpretive claims remain contested.
- **Yair Mintzker**, *The Many Deaths of Jew Süss: The Notorious Trial and Execution of an Eighteenth-Century Court Jew* (Princeton University Press, 2017). Major modern study emphasizing four conflicting contemporary narrators and the construction of historical testimony.
- **Peter H. Wilson**, studies of Oppenheimer, Karl Alexander, and Württemberg constitutional politics, including analysis used by the Landesarchiv exhibition.
- **Robert Kretzschmar**, editor, *Die Verteidigungsschrift des Michael Andreas Moegling für Joseph Süß Oppenheimer*. Critical edition of the rediscovered defense.
- **Susan Tegel**, *Jew Süss: Life, Legend, Fiction, Film* (Continuum, 2011). Study of the historical figure’s transformation through literature and cinema.
- **Friedrich Knilli**, studies of the 1940 film and the media history of “Jud Süß.”
- **Alexandre Badenoch and scholars of film/propaganda**, studies of the divergent 1934 and 1940 films and their reception.

### Literary works

1. **Wilhelm Hauff**, “Jud Süß” (1827).  
   Novella first serialized in Cotta’s *Morgenblatt für gebildete Stände*. It presents Oppenheimer through a mixture of historical romance, Christian-Jewish social boundaries, and inherited stereotypes.

2. **Lion Feuchtwanger**, *Jud Süß* (1925; earlier dramatic treatment 1916).  
   Internationally successful historical novel. It transforms Oppenheimer into a tragic figure whose pursuit and renunciation of power are driven by the fictional death of his daughter Naemi. Published in English as *Jew Süss* and in the United States as *Power*.

3. **Paul Kornfeld**, *Jud Süß* (Berlin stage adaptation, 1930).  
   Dramatic adaptation of Feuchtwanger.

4. **Fritz Runge**, *Jud Süß* (1912).  
   Hostile theatrical treatment reflecting contemporary antisemitic stereotypes.

5. **Klaus Pohl**, *Jud Süß* (modern stage work).  
   Reconsiders the historical and propagandistic figure.

6. **Joshua Sobol**, *Das Vermögen des Herrn Süß* / related stage versions (twenty-first century).  
   Theatrical reinterpretation emphasizing finance, power, and historical memory.

These works should not be mined for biographical facts unless independently corroborated.

## Films, Documentaries, and Broadcast Material

### *Jew Süss* (1934)

- Director: **Lothar Mendes**
- Producer: Michael Balcon/Gaumont-British
- Screenplay: Dorothy Farnum and A. R. Rawlinson
- Oppenheimer: **Conrad Veidt**
- Basis: Feuchtwanger’s novel
- Premiere: 4 October 1934, London and New York
- Character: Sympathetic tragic treatment, consciously opposed to antisemitism
- Historical reliability: Dramatized and dependent on Feuchtwanger’s fictional daughter and spiritual plot

Albert Einstein reportedly attended the New York premiere. This is a connection to the reception of an Oppenheimer-derived work, not to the historical Oppenheimer.

### *Jud Süß* (1940)

- Director: **Veit Harlan**
- Oppenheimer: Ferdinand Marian
- Principal cast: Heinrich George, Kristina Söderbaum, Werner Krauss
- Commissioning and supervision: Reich Propaganda Minister Joseph Goebbels
- Premiere: Venice, 5 September 1940
- Character: State-sponsored antisemitic propaganda
- Historical distortions: Invented rape and suicide; racialized disguises; representation of Oppenheimer as conspiratorial corrupter; collective expulsion framed as public salvation
- Reception: A major commercial and compulsory-screening success in Nazi-controlled Europe; shown to military, police, and SS audiences
- Postwar history: Harlan faced criminal proceedings but was acquitted. The film remains restricted in Germany to controlled educational exhibition.

The exact dependence on Hauff, Feuchtwanger, the 1934 film, encyclopedias, and original screenwriting has been disputed. Harlan denied using Feuchtwanger, while scholarship identifies shared narrative and visual material but also fundamental ideological reversal.

### Later screen and documentary works

- **Jud Süß – ein Film als Verbrechen?** (ARD television documentary/dramatized documentary, 2001). Examines production and responsibility for the 1940 film.
- **Harlan – Im Schatten von Jud Süß** (documentary, 2008/2009), directed by Felix Moeller. Studies Veit Harlan and family memory.
- **Jud Süß – Film ohne Gewissen** (2010), directed by Oskar Roehler. Dramatization of the making of Harlan’s film, centred on Ferdinand Marian.
- German radio programs and historical broadcasts by Deutschlandfunk have revisited the trial and the contrast between the historical person and his filmic afterlives.
- The Landesarchiv Baden-Württemberg’s virtual exhibition, *Beschlagnahmte Briefschaften*, provides digitized images and explanations of core archival objects.

### Source destruction and survival bias

The commission normally translated and destroyed Hebrew papers. Prosecutors organized files according to contemplated charges. The surviving archive is consequently rich but not neutral: it preserves what authorities seized and selected more fully than what Oppenheimer or his associates would have chosen to preserve.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### 1738–early nineteenth century

The execution immediately generated a European media event. Broadsides, poems, engravings, sermons, and execution accounts portrayed Oppenheimer as:

- An arch-Jewish deceiver
- A corrupt favorite
- A sexual predator
- A destroyer of constitutional and religious order
- A damned man defeated by Christian justice

Jewish accounts emphasized his refusal of baptism and death with the Shema. Thus the hostile villain and steadfast martyr emerged almost simultaneously.

### Nineteenth century

Hauff’s novella converted the political trial into historical romance. Historians frequently repeated trial allegations without adequate source criticism. The name “Jud Süß” became more recognizable than Joseph Süß Oppenheimer’s own name.

### Weimar era

Feuchtwanger reimagined him as a psychologically complex tragic protagonist. Selma Stern simultaneously returned to the archival record, establishing a scholarly tradition that treated the case within German-Jewish history and the structure of absolutist court finance.

### Nazi era

The 1940 film detached the story from documentary restraint and made Oppenheimer an embodiment of racial propaganda. Its invented rape narrative and mass expulsion finale connected the eighteenth-century case to contemporary persecution. The historical name thereafter became globally associated with one of Nazi cinema’s most notorious productions.

### Postwar interpretation

Research increasingly classified the trial as judicial murder while continuing to investigate Oppenheimer’s actual financial methods. Film scholarship examined the responsibilities of Goebbels, Harlan, actors, distributors, compulsory exhibitors, and postwar courts.

### Modern commemoration

Heidelberg and Stuttgart institutions have developed exhibitions and commemorative projects. The Baden-Württemberg Landtag and the Israelite Religious Community of Württemberg established the **Joseph-Ben-Issachar-Süßkind-Oppenheimer Award**, presented biennially from 2015 for work opposing hostility toward minorities and prejudice in scholarship and journalism.

In November 2024, Stuttgart’s Stiftung Geißstraße inaugurated a memorial site connected with Oppenheimer.

His historical position remains tripartite:

1. A skilled and self-interested early-modern financier embedded in court capitalism.
2. A politically isolated agent sacrificed after his patron’s death in a procedurally corrupt capital prosecution.
3. A posthumous symbolic figure repeatedly reconstructed for incompatible antisemitic, Jewish, liberal, literary, and memorial purposes.

## Atlas Connections

### Samuel Oppenheimer

**Nature:** Family and professional-network connection. Samuel Oppenheimer (1630–1703), imperial court factor in Vienna, belonged to the extended Oppenheimer court-finance family to which Joseph’s parents were related. Later accounts say Joseph’s Vienna formation benefited from access to associated Oppenheimer houses.

**Dates and places:** Samuel died before Joseph’s commercial career; therefore no personal meeting is established. The connection concerns ancestry, kinship network, and professional precedent, especially Heidelberg, Frankfurt, and Vienna.

**Evidentiary label:** **Reported fact**, with the exact degree of kinship unresolved.

### Albert Einstein

**Nature:** Reception-history connection only. Einstein reportedly attended the New York premiere of the sympathetic 1934 film *Jew Süss*, based on Feuchtwanger’s fictionalized life of Oppenheimer.

**Date and place:** 4 October 1934, New York City.

**Evidentiary label:** **Reported fact.** There was obviously no personal connection to the eighteenth-century Oppenheimer.

### Friedrich Schiller

**Nature:** No documented direct connection. Schiller was born in Württemberg in 1759, twenty-one years after Oppenheimer’s death. Although later criticism compares the dramatic structure of *Jud Süß* adaptations with Württemberg political tragedy and Schillerian drama, no explicit personal or acknowledged influence involving Oppenheimer is established.

**Evidentiary label:** **No connection found; thematic comparison only.**

### J. Robert Oppenheimer

**Nature:** No documented genealogical or historical connection established by the consulted sources. Shared surname alone is not evidence.

**Evidentiary label:** **No connection found.**

### Salomon Oppenheim Jr.

**Nature:** No documented connection established. He belonged to a later Cologne banking dynasty. No reliable source consulted here links him genealogically or institutionally to Joseph Süß Oppenheimer.

**Evidentiary label:** **No connection found.**
