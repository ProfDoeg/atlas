## Works by Eadweard Muybridge

### Photographic bodies, surveys, and albums

- **1867–1872:** *Yosemite photographs*. A continuing photographic obra—mammoth-plate views, stereographs, and album images—rather than a single fixed publication; twenty early photographs appeared in John S. Hittell’s *Yosemite: Its Wonders and Its Beauties* (1868), while later Yosemite campaigns produced differently titled and assembled sets. [The surviving corpus is incomplete and its boundaries vary by collection.](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf6t1nb6w7)

- **1868:** *Alaska survey photographs*. A commissioned photographic body depicting the recently acquired American territory, including landscapes, settlements, and Indigenous people; surviving prints and stereographs are dispersed, so it resists treatment as a closed album. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/muybridge-timeline/)

- **1868:** *San Francisco earthquake photographs*. A documentary series of earthquake damage, now embedded in broader Bay Area and stereograph collections rather than preserved as one authorially fixed work. [Bancroft Library finding aid](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf6t1nb6w7)

- **c. 1868–1875:** *Pacific Coast and Far West stereographs*. An open commercial body—about 1,700 examples survive in the Lone Mountain collection alone—covering California, British Columbia, railroads, lighthouses, missions, vineyards, Woodward’s Gardens, and other subjects. It resists the singular “work”: negatives were numbered, reissued, remounted, and gathered into sample albums, and photographs numbered 1–278 are absent from Muybridge’s published 1873 catalogue. [Bancroft Library finding aid](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf6t1nb6w7)

- **1869:** *Moonlight effects from Woodward’s Gardens, San Francisco*. A nocturnal photographic experiment known through later lantern-slide material; whether the surviving slide should be called the work, a lecture copy, or a manifestation of a lost negative is uncertain. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1872–1873:** *Central Pacific and Union Pacific Railroad photographs*. A commissioned/documentary body of railroad scenery and infrastructure, circulated chiefly as stereographs and later absorbed into broader Far West catalogues. It has no single stable album form. [Bancroft Library finding aid](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf6t1nb6w7)

- **1873:** *Modoc War photographs*. A documentary series of soldiers, terrain, and Modoc subjects in northern California; Muybridge made it amid military operations, but surviving institutional groupings do not constitute a single authorially fixed publication. [Bancroft Library finding aid](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf6t1nb6w7)

- **1873:** *Catalogue of Photographic Views Illustrating the Yosemite, Mammoth Trees, Geyser Springs, and Other Remarkable and Interesting Scenery of the Far West*. A sales catalogue and finding protocol for Muybridge’s numbered photographs, published by Bradley & Rulofson; it describes and organizes the photographic obra rather than reproducing it as a conventional picture book. [Contemporary catalogue transcription](https://cprr.org/Museum/Muybridge_Catalog.html)

- **1875–1876:** *The Pacific Coast of Central America and Mexico; the Isthmus of Panama; Guatemala; and the Cultivation and Shipment of Coffee*. A commissioned photographic survey, issued in 1876 as an album of views made principally for the Pacific Mail Steamship Company. Because individual stereographs and lecture plates circulated separately, the title names both an album and a larger, not perfectly closed photographic body. [Kingston Museum timeline](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/muybridge-timeline/), [Kingston collection account](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1877:** *Panorama of San Francisco from California Street Hill*. An eleven-part, 360-degree photographic album made from the Mark Hopkins mansion; multiple copies were assembled and sold, making each mounted album a manifestation of the larger panorama. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1878:** *Panorama of San Francisco from California Street Hill*. A thirteen-part mammoth-plate panorama, about 17.5 feet long when unfolded, mounted as an accordion album. Nine copies are presently known; one segment was apparently rephotographed, so even this seemingly continuous view records different moments. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

### Motion-photography projects

- **1872:** *First photographs of Leland Stanford’s horse in motion*. An experimental project intended to resolve whether a galloping horse becomes wholly airborne; the result was reportedly inconclusive and is not securely preserved as a complete, fixed work. [University of Pennsylvania Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/upt50m993.pdf)

- **1877:** *Occident in motion*. An instantaneous equine-photography experiment made for Leland Stanford. Sources differ in loosely calling associated results *The Horse in Motion*; it is safer to distinguish the photographic experiment from later card sets and from Stillman’s book. [University of Pennsylvania Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/upt50m993.pdf)

- **1878:** *The Horse in Motion*. A set of six cabinet cards presenting successive phases of horses’ movement. It is an analytic photographic sequence and commercial object, not yet a motion picture in the later film-strip sense. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1878–1879:** *Palo Alto motion studies*. A project comprising sequential photographs of horses, other animals, and people, made through banks of triggered cameras at Stanford’s stock farm. The surviving negatives, prints, cards, albums, and projected tracings are distinct manifestations; no single one exhausts the project. [Kingston Museum timeline](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/muybridge-timeline/), [Kingston Museum collection account](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1881:** *The Attitudes of Animals in Motion: A Series of Photographs Illustrating the Consecutive Positions Assumed by Animals in Performing Various Movements*. A limited presentation album synthesizing the Palo Alto experiments: 173 pages with apparatus views, an index, and 203 photographic plates. Copies differ physically, and Kingston’s two examples appear to have been Muybridge’s working copies. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1881–1882:** *The Horse in Motion: As Shown by Instantaneous Photography*. A collaborative and disputed object: Muybridge supplied the photographs on which the study depended, but J. D. B. Stillman wrote the anatomical text and Leland Stanford presented the enterprise under his own authority, provoking a damaging controversy over Muybridge’s credit. It should not be silently catalogued as a Muybridge-authored book, but neither can his made images be excluded from it. [Biodiversity Heritage Library](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/creator/5654), [University of Pennsylvania Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/upt50m993.pdf)

- **1884–1885; published 1887:** *Animal Locomotion: An Electro-Photographic Investigation of Consecutive Phases of Animal Movements*. Muybridge’s largest photographic project and a photographic atlas: 781 collotype plates, issued in eleven portfolios, showing women, men, children, disabled subjects, domestic and wild animals, and birds. It is simultaneously scientific investigation, image archive, publication, and body of performances staged for cameras—a Gesamtkunstwerk-like object that resists reduction to “a book.” [University of Pennsylvania catalogue](https://archives.upenn.edu/digitized-resources/docs-pubs/muybridge/catalogue/), [Penn collection guide](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/upt50m993.pdf)

- **1887:** *Animal Locomotion: Prospectus and Catalogue of Plates*. A catalogue and descriptive protocol accompanying the 781-plate project, recording subjects, models, actions, camera positions, clothing, and time intervals; it is a separate textual instrument, not merely another edition of the plates. [University of Pennsylvania Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/digitized-resources/docs-pubs/muybridge/catalogue/)

- **1887:** *Animal Locomotion, Author’s Edition*. A small, specially bound portfolio selecting twenty-one plates—twelve human and nine animal—from the larger project. The number made is unknown; three institutional copies are presently identified, and Kingston’s copy lacks plate 408. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1899:** *Animals in Motion: An Electro-Photographic Investigation of Consecutive Phases of Animal Progressive Movements*. A retrospective book in which Muybridge selected, reorganized, and explained animal sequences from his earlier investigations. It is a new authored synthesis, not simply an edition of the complete 1887 atlas. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/the-muybridge-collection/)

- **1901:** *The Human Figure in Motion: An Electro-Photographic Investigation of Consecutive Phases of Muscular Actions*. A retrospective selection and reorganization of human-motion plates, with descriptive apparatus; like *Animals in Motion*, it converts part of an earlier photographic archive into a new reference work. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/the-muybridge-collection/)

### Machines, patents, and moving-image manifestations

- **1860:** *An Improved Method of, and Apparatus for, Plate Printing*. A patented invention registered in Britain under Muybridge’s earlier surname, Muggeridge. The patent documents a project or technical protocol; without an identified surviving machine, it should not automatically be treated as evidence that a working apparatus was completed. [Patent bibliography summarized in Muybridge’s biographical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eadweard_Muybridge)

- **1861:** *Improvements in Machinery or Apparatus for Washing Clothes and Other Textile Articles*. A British patented design, likewise better classified as an inventive project and protocol than as a securely surviving manufactured object. [Patent bibliography summarized in Muybridge’s biographical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eadweard_Muybridge)

- **1878–1879:** *Improvement in the Method and Apparatus for Photographing Objects in Motion*, U.S. patents 212,864 and 212,865. Two related technical protocols covering sequential cameras, shutters, slides, and electrical triggering by the moving subject; the patent texts distinguish Muybridge’s claims even though the working photographic installation was repeatedly rebuilt. [Patent 212,864](https://patents.google.com/patent/US212864A/en), [Patent 212,865](https://patents.google.com/patent/US212865)

- **1879–1880:** *Zoogyroscope / Zoopraxiscope*. A projection machine devised in 1879 and publicly demonstrated in 1880, using rotating glass discs to synthesize apparent movement. The only known original apparatus survives at Kingston; the change of name and its evolving components make it a continuing technical project rather than one immutable object. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **c. 1880–1893:** *Zoopraxiscope discs*. A moving-image corpus made for projection rather than a series of photographs: Muybridge traced, distorted, and sometimes colored figures around glass discs so that the projected images would appear correctly proportioned. Sixty-nine discs survive at Kingston—thirty-five sixteen-inch and thirty-four twelve-inch examples—but the museum also reports seventy-three surviving worldwide, so the corpus is not complete in one place. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1883:** *Apparatus for Photographing Changing or Moving Bodies*, U.S. patent 279,878. A further camera-system protocol employing a motor, electrical circuit, and sequential shutters, expressly adaptable to rapid phenomena such as explosions as well as locomotion. [Patent record](https://patents.google.com/patent/US279878A/en)

### Performances, lectures, and exhibition environments

- **1880–1897:** *Zoopraxigraphical projection lectures*. A changing performance obra presented hundreds of times in the United States, Britain, and continental Europe: spoken exposition, lantern slides, glass discs, machinery, and projected movement operated together. Individual lectures had programs and occasions but not a single fixed text, so the series is better understood as repeatable manifestations than as one book or film. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/), [Kingston timeline](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/muybridge-timeline/)

- **c. 1881–1904:** *Lecture lantern-slide and glass-plate corpus*. More than 2,200 slides and plates survive at Kingston, including over 1,300 motion-study images, over 400 landscapes, historical comparison images, duplicates, and unpublished photographs. This is a working visual archive assembled for performance; some slides reproduce earlier stereographs, and authorship of every copied reference image should not be assigned to Muybridge merely because he used it. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)

- **1882:** *The Attitudes of Animals in Motion, Illustrated with the Zoopraxiscope*. A printed lecture text or report associated with Muybridge’s Royal Institution presentation; its identity varies in cataloguing between pamphlet, lecture, and institutional publication, so it is best treated as the textual manifestation of a performed work. [Biodiversity Heritage Library](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/creator/5654)

- **1891:** *The Science of Animal Locomotion (Zoopraxography): An Electro-Photographic Investigation of Consecutive Phases of Animal Movements*. A lecture-derived explanatory publication advertising and interpreting the larger motion project; it combines scientific claim, self-documentation, and promotional catalogue. [Biodiversity Heritage Library](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/creator/5654)

- **1893:** *Zoopraxographical Hall*. A purpose-built presentation environment at the World’s Columbian Exposition in Chicago, joining architecture, projection apparatus, colored discs, lecture-performance, and photographic research. Muybridge operated the attraction, but the surviving evidence does not justify attributing every element of the building’s design or construction to him; it is therefore a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk and exhibition project, not simply his “work” without qualification. [University of Pennsylvania Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/upt50m993.pdf)

- **1893:** *Descriptive Zoopraxography; or, The Science of Animal Locomotion Made Popular*. A lecture-book and commemorative exposition publication containing reduced outline tracings from *Animal Locomotion*. It explains and promotes Muybridge’s project while functioning as a portable textual substitute for the Chicago projection performance. [University of Pennsylvania Archives](https://archives.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/upt50m993.pdf)

- **1893:** *World’s Columbian Exposition paper discs*. A set of fifty paper projection or animation discs produced in connection with the Chicago presentation. Their status lies between souvenir, diagram, image carrier, and component of an apparatus. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/the-muybridge-collection/)

### Self-archive

- **after 1868–1904, with posthumous additions to 1930:** *Muybridge’s scrapbook*. A 257-page autobiographical and promotional assemblage of press clippings, reviews, announcements, brochures, and inserted material. Muybridge made and maintained the core object, but nine entries were added after his death, probably by Kingston Museum curators; it is therefore both an obra and a later institutional continuation whose authorship is layered. [Kingston Museum](https://muybridgeinkingston.co.uk/highlights/)
