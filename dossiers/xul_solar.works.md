## Works by Xul Solar

### Visual obra: the catalogued body of paintings, drawings, and studies

- **1913–1962:** *Obra completa*. Approximately 1,900 documented paintings, drawings, illustrations, objects, studies, sketches, unfinished works, and rejected works constitute the visual obra established by Patricia M. Artundo’s two-volume catalogue raisonné; it is a body of work, not a single object, and includes untitled and uncertainly dated pieces that should not be supplied with invented names or dates. The catalogue is explicitly presented as complete only insofar as works were documented by 2016. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/paginas/panactividades/), [Fundación Pan Klub archive](https://archivo.xulsolar.org.ar/archivo))

### Illustrated and transformed books

- **1926:** *El tamaño de mi esperanza*. Vignettes made for Jorge Luis Borges’s book; the authored text is Borges’s, while Xul’s contribution is an illustration cycle rather than a book of his own. ([Imaginaria](https://imaginaria.com.ar/18/0/xul.htm))

- **1928:** *El idioma de los argentinos*. Six printed vignettes made for Borges’s book, together with surviving preparatory drawings; Xul also transformed at least one printed copy with an oil-painted cover and watercolors throughout, making that copy a singular painted-book obra distinct from the edition. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Xul.pdf), [Fundación Pan Klub chronology](https://xulsolar.org.ar/img/educacion/Pan%20Klub-Museo%20Xul%20Solar-03-Xul%20y%20su%20tiempo%20-%20Biografia.pdf))

- **1945:** *Un modelo para la muerte*. Vignettes for the detective novella issued under the collective pseudonym B. Suárez Lynch, used by Jorge Luis Borges and Adolfo Bioy Casares; Xul made the illustrations, not the literary work. Some secondary chronologies give 1944, reflecting the book’s complicated bibliographical history. ([Fundación Pan Klub chronology](https://xulsolar.org.ar/img/educacion/Pan%20Klub-Museo%20Xul%20Solar-03-Xul%20y%20su%20tiempo%20-%20Biografia.pdf))

### Texts, translations, visions, and protocols

- **1923:** *Pettoruti y el desconcertante futurismo*. Newspaper article published under the pseudonym “Ramón J.”; its attribution to Xul is accepted in Patricia Artundo’s collected-text edition but should remain visible because the original signature is pseudonymous. ([MALBA collection research](https://coleccion.malba.org.ar/Detail/objects/778))

- **1923–1924:** *Pettoruti*. One of several articles through which Xul publicly defended Emilio Pettoruti’s new art; the collected version is a critical manifesto as much as a description. ([MALBA collection research](https://coleccion.malba.org.ar/Detail/objects/778))

- **1924–c. 1950s:** *Los San Signos, or Libro del Cielo*. Sixty-four written visions corresponding to the sixty-four hexagrams of the *I Ching*, repeatedly transcribed, translated, reordered, and revised in neocriollo. It resists being called a finished book: publication was projected, individual visions appeared separately, and the complete surviving textual process was edited only posthumously. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Xul.pdf), [Revista argentina de historiografía lingüística](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7425456.pdf))

- **1927:** *Despedida a Marechal*. Short neocriollo text published in *Martín Fierro*; its function is simultaneously farewell, linguistic experiment, and public performance. ([Jorge Schwartz, Fundación Pan Klub](https://www.xulsolar.org.ar/img/testimonial/text%20Jorge%20Schwartz.pdf))

- **1927:** *Algunos piensos cortos de Cristian Morgenstern*. Translation or creative recasting of Christian Morgenstern’s aphorisms from *Stufen* into neocriollo, accompanied by a translator’s note. Because the source thoughts are Morgenstern’s, this is a translational manifestation of Xul’s language project rather than wholly original prose. ([Jorge Schwartz, Fundación Pan Klub](https://www.xulsolar.org.ar/img/testimonial/text%20Jorge%20Schwartz.pdf))

- **1931:** *Poema*. A published neocriollo vision subsequently incorporated into the evolving *San Signos* corpus; scholarship disagrees over its internal numbering, identifying it either as the first of the projected publication sequence or as vision 36 in the surviving notebooks. ([Revista argentina de historiografía lingüística](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7425456.pdf), [BHH](https://www.sagw.ch/fileadmin/redaktion-sseh/Actividades_2019-2020/BHH-29.pdf))

- **1931:** *Apuntes de neocriollo*. Published linguistic text containing both an experimental composition and a gloss of forms and rules. It acts partly as a protocol for reading neocriollo, although the language itself remained deliberately mutable rather than becoming a fixed grammar. ([Revista argentina de historiografía lingüística](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7425456.pdf))

- **1933:** *Poema*. A revised or republished manifestation of the 1931 vision in the magazine *Signo*, not securely a separate composition. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Xul.pdf))

- **1934:** *Hablan los libros: “Genghis Khan, Emperador de todos los hombres”*. Review of Harold Lamb’s book, published in *Crítica* under “Fulano de Tal”; inclusion in Artundo’s collected writings supports attribution to Xul, but the pseudonymous first publication makes authorship less transparent than for signed texts. ([Universidad Nacional de Mar del Plata thesis](https://humadoc.mdp.edu.ar/files/original/2833c791e9f88675e9e21280c617344621a3be84.pdf))

- **1936:** *Visión sobrel trilíneo* (also cited as *Visión sobre el trilineo*). Neocriollo vision published in *Destiempo* with an explanatory gloss; it is at once an autonomous magazine text and part of the larger *San Signos* rewriting process. The title’s spelling varies across editions and scholarship. ([Revista argentina de historiografía lingüística](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7425456.pdf), [Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Xul.pdf))

- **Undated:** *Cuentos de Amazonas, de los Mosetenes y Guarayús*. Article or retelling attributed to Xul and signed “Alejandro Schulz”; the surviving scholarly reference does not establish a secure date, so none should be imposed. ([Universidad Nacional de Mar del Plata thesis](https://humadoc.mdp.edu.ar/files/original/2833c791e9f88675e9e21280c617344621a3be84.pdf))

- **Undated, unpublished:** *El mundo despiertio*. Children’s story in neocriollo preserved in manuscript. Its status is resistant: it is a literary project and surviving text, but not a book completed through publication. ([Jorge Schwartz, Fundación Pan Klub](https://www.xulsolar.org.ar/img/testimonial/text%20Jorge%20Schwartz.pdf))

- **1953:** *Explica*. Neocriollo explanatory text associated with the *San Signos* materials; its ostensibly didactic title does not make it a stable grammar or straightforward protocol. ([Jorge Schwartz, Fundación Pan Klub](https://www.xulsolar.org.ar/img/testimonial/text%20Jorge%20Schwartz.pdf))

- **1957:** *Propuestas para más vida futura: algo semitécnico sobre mejoras anatómicas y entes nuevos*. Speculative article proposing transformed human bodies and appendages. It is best treated as a project-manifesto: its imagined beings were proposals, not documented objects fabricated by Xul. ([MALBA collection research](https://coleccion.malba.org.ar/Detail/objects/784))

- **c. 1959:** *Vuelvilla*. Written architectural and social proposal for an airborne city, related to the 1936 painting *Vuel Villa*. It is an unrealized project rather than a building, and sources date the text only approximately. ([Xul Solar chronology](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xul_Solar))

- **1962:** *Conferencia sobre la lengua*. Late statement of Xul’s linguistic project, identified by scholarship as his final sustained effort to explain it; whether it survives as a lecture manuscript, a delivered lecture, or both must not be collapsed without examination of the archive object. ([Jorge Schwartz, Fundación Pan Klub](https://www.xulsolar.org.ar/img/testimonial/text%20Jorge%20Schwartz.pdf))

### Languages and writing systems

- **From the 1920s:** *Neocriollo* (also *criol*). An evolving constructed language combining principally Spanish and Portuguese with material from several other languages, intended for Latin America. It is simultaneously a project, a protocol under perpetual revision, and the medium of paintings and texts—not a single finished work. ([MALBA](https://malba.org.ar/evento/xul-solar-visiones-y-revelaciones/), [Revista argentina de historiografía lingüística](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/7425456.pdf))

- **From the late 1930s:** *Grafías plastiútiles*, or *pensiformas*. A family of pictorial-writing systems derived partly from stenographic signs, devised so that pictures could also be read as verbal statements. The category names both a protocol and a body of individual artworks, so it cannot be reduced to either “alphabet” or “painting.” ([Fundación Pan Klub/Museo Xul Solar](https://www.xulsolar.org.ar/), [Xul Solar chronology](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xul_Solar))

- **By 1951, developed into the 1960s:** *Panlengua*. A monosyllabic, combinatory, numerical and astrological universal-language project linked to the panajedrez board. It never reached a single authorized final form; dictionaries, notes, tables, and explanations are stages of the project rather than editions of a completed language. ([MALBA](https://malba.org.ar/evento/xul-solar-visiones-y-revelaciones/), [Argentina.gob.ar](https://cultura.gob.ar/cinco-inventos-de-xul-solar_3647/))

- **By 1951:** *Grafía musical*. A proposed notation and teaching technique intended to simplify musical study and connected to Xul’s redesigned keyboards. Surviving scores, diagrams, and statements document a protocol, but scholarship does not establish one final, complete version. ([Revista del Instituto de Investigación Musicológica “Carlos Vega”](https://erevistas.uca.edu.ar/index.php/RIIM/article/download/6220/7094/20942), [Fundación Pan Klub archive](https://archivo.xulsolar.org.ar/collections/browse))

### Games, divinatory systems, and syncretic objects

- **c. 1945–1963:** *Panajedrez*, or *Panjuego*. Continually elaborated board game using a twelve-by-thirteen or, in some descriptions, thirteen-by-thirteen field, astrological correspondences, pieces, language, music, and combinatory play. The conflicting board descriptions reflect successive versions and diffuse rules; the project is a Gesamtkunstwerk-like protocol rather than one settled commercial game. ([MALBA collection research](https://coleccion.malba.org.ar/en/ciuda-y-abismos/), [Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/media/uploads/paginas/materialXul_docente.pdf))

- **Dates not securely fixed:** *Tarot*. Several redesigned and painted variants of the Tarot deck. “Xul’s Tarot” therefore denotes a family of divinatory images rather than a single securely dated pack. ([MoMA](https://production-gcp.moma.org/artists/33278-alejandro-xul-solar), [Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/media/uploads/paginas/materialXul_docente.pdf))

- **Dates not securely fixed:** *I Ching hexagrams*. Xul recreated the sixty-four hexagrams as visual and divinatory material linked to *Los San Signos* and other objects. These resist isolation as sixty-four independent artworks because they also operate as components of a larger protocol. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/media/uploads/paginas/materialXul_docente.pdf))

- **c. 1950:** *Pan Juego y Marioneta I Ching*. Variable sculptural assemblage in painted wood and metal combining panajedrez, Kabbalistic structures, the *I Ching*, and puppetry. Its variable configuration makes it less a fixed sculpture than a material node in Xul’s larger Gesamtkunstwerk. ([MoMA](https://production-gcp.moma.org/artists/33278-alejandro-xul-solar), [MoMA exhibition publication](https://www.moma.org/d/pdfs/W1siZiIsIjIwMjIvMDQvMjYvMzVudm9pc3R2a19Bcm1hbmRvX1JldmVyb25uLnBkZiJdXQ/Armando_Revero%25CC%2581n.pdf?sha=61e53b9d05364b57))

- **Date not securely fixed:** *Pan Altar Mundi*. Three-dimensional syncretic altar intended to encompass several spiritual traditions. It is simultaneously sculpture, cosmological model, and ritual proposition. ([MoMA](https://production-gcp.moma.org/artists/33278-alejandro-xul-solar))

### Modified musical instruments

- **1932 or later:** *Modified dulcitone*. Existing instrument rebuilt with Xul’s simplified, colored keyboard system. The purchase is dated to 1932, but the precise stages and completion date of the alteration are not securely established. ([Revista del Instituto de Investigación Musicológica “Carlos Vega”](https://erevistas.uca.edu.ar/index.php/RIIM/article/download/6220/7094/20942))

- **1929–1946:** *Modified harmonium*. Existing harmonium altered as part of Xul’s research into color, touch, tuning, notation, and easier musical learning. It is an object modification rather than an instrument invented wholly from first principles. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://media.bellasartes.gob.ar/h/Xul.pdf), [MALBA](https://malba.org.ar/evento/xul-solar-visiones-y-revelaciones/))

- **1946:** *Modified piano*. His wife Micaela “Lita” Cadenas’s piano rebuilt with a reduced, colored, tactile keyboard and a six-note system. Oral testimony claims that Xul had experimented with a piano keyboard before leaving Argentina in 1912, but scholarship states that no corroborating documents have been found; 1946 is the documented instrument, not proof of the earlier claim. ([Revista del Instituto de Investigación Musicológica “Carlos Vega”](https://erevistas.uca.edu.ar/index.php/RIIM/article/download/6220/7094/20942), [Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/media/uploads/paginas/materialXul_docente.pdf))

### Puppets and projected theatre

- **1951–1953:** *Zodiac puppets*. Articulated puppets representing the zodiac signs, constructed from wire, wood, papier-mâché, and other materials for a projected adult theatre of religious or mystical subjects. They are individual objects within an incompletely realized theatrical project; sources do not establish that the intended plays were ever performed. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/media/uploads/paginas/materialXul_docente.pdf), [Argentina.gob.ar](https://cultura.gob.ar/cinco-inventos-de-xul-solar_3647/))

- **1951–1953:** *Death puppet*. Large articulated figure of Death belonging to the same projected adult puppet theatre. It survives as a made object even though the theatre “that does not yet function,” as Xul described it in 1951, resists classification as a completed dramatic work. ([Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes](https://www.bellasartes.gob.ar/media/uploads/paginas/materialXul_docente.pdf), [SciELO Brasil](https://www.scielo.br/j/alea/a/KmNhPrZQ3FxFZzGZK7nmLyq/))

### Architecture, communities, and unrealized environments

- **1939 onward:** *Pan-Klub*. Gathering place and continuing intellectual-spiritual association established in Xul’s house. It is better called a social manifestation or collective project than a conventional artwork, and its continuation by others means that not everything subsequently done under its name is authored by Xul. ([Xul Solar chronology](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xul_Solar))

- **From 1954:** *Projects for houses, plazas, and cities*. Body of architectural drawings and proposals developed especially during Xul’s Tigre years. Most are projects rather than buildings; treating the corpus as built architecture would erase precisely the distinction between intention and realization. ([MALBA](https://malba.org.ar/evento/xul-solar-visiones-y-revelaciones/))

### Astrological, musical, and working archives

- **Dates various:** *Astrological charts*. Numerous charts prepared for friends, artists, musicians, students, and other sitters, including charts associated with Chopin, Heitor Villa-Lobos, and Juan Carlos Paz. Some may be commissioned documents or teaching aids rather than autonomous artworks; the Foundation preserves them as an archival series, and the available online description does not justify inventing a finite item list. ([Fundación Pan Klub archive](https://archivo.xulsolar.org.ar/collections/browse), [Biblioteca Nacional de Argentina](https://www.bn.gob.ar/micrositios/admin_assets/issues/files/c142b6640fd9e8696a591b8c52f4543a.pdf))

- **Dates various:** *Scores and musical manuscripts*. Surviving compositions, exercises, notation experiments, and manuscripts form an archival series. Their resistance to an ordinary “works list” is material: some are compositions, some studies, and some protocols for the invented musical notation, while the archive’s public description does not assign all of them individual authoritative titles. ([Fundación Pan Klub archive](https://archivo.xulsolar.org.ar/collections/browse), [Cintia Cristiá, *Xul Solar, un músico visual*](https://gourmetmusicalediciones.com/catalogo/xul-solar-un-musico-visual/))

- **Dates various:** *Notebooks and working books*. Carnets containing sketches, calculations, linguistic tables, visions, and revisions document Xul’s processes. They are made objects and sometimes carriers of works, but not every notebook page should be promoted into an autonomous titled obra. ([Fundación Pan Klub archive](https://archivo.xulsolar.org.ar/collections/browse))
