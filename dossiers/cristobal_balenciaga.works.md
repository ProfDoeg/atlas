## Works by Cristóbal Balenciaga Eizaguirre

### Couture oeuvre and collections

- **1907:** *Copy of a Drecoll dress for the Marquise de Casa Torres*. Balenciaga’s earliest documented garment was a copy rather than an autonomous design; the surviving chronology spells the source “De Croll,” generally understood as the house of Drecoll. The garment’s present survival is not established. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 16–17](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf)

- **1912–1971:** *Balenciaga clothing*. This is the corpus-level obra: individually fitted dresses, suits, coats, capes, bridal clothes and other garments designed by Balenciaga and produced collectively by the workshops of his Spanish and Paris houses. No complete catalogue raisonné of every garment exists; the Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa catalogue dates its holdings from 1912 to 1971, while its historical survey treats 1917–1968 as the principal professional span. [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa collection catalogue](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/descubre/coleccion/colecciones/), [*Fashion and Heritage* catalogue](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf)

- **1937–1968:** *Paris haute-couture collections*. A twice-yearly sequence of numbered models rather than one stable object: designs were shown as prototypes and subsequently remade for individual clients, sometimes by licensed houses. The Palais Galliera preserves approximately 300 silhouettes spanning the Paris production, but neither that holding nor any other published museum catalogue is the complete output. [Palais Galliera](https://www.palaisgalliera.paris.fr/collections/les-collections-du-musee/la-haute-couture-de-1947-nos-jours/robe-bustier-et-cape-du), [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/fr/decouvrir/expositions-en-ligne/expositions-en-ligne/cristobal-balenciaga-mode-et-patrimoine-.html)

- **1940–1968:** *Hats and headwear*. An internally produced body of millinery intended to complete Balenciaga silhouettes. Unlike the house’s scarves, jewellery, gloves, stockings and most perfumes, the hats were actually made in Balenciaga’s workshops; consequently, the broader branded-accessory corpus resists wholesale attribution to him. [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa collection catalogue](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/descubre/coleccion/colecciones/), [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/introducing-cristobal-balenciaga)

### Named garments, lines and silhouette inventions

- **1939:** *“Velázquez” or “Infanta” dress*. A silk evening gown translating seventeenth-century Spanish court dress into couture; surviving examples are catalogued as house productions designed by Balenciaga. “Velázquez” and “Infanta dress” are museum and historical names for the model, not the title of a freestanding artwork. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/84758), [National Gallery of Victoria](https://www.ngv.vic.gov.au/explore/collection/work/51284/)

- **By 1940:** *Square coat*. A coat whose sleeve and shoulder yoke were cut as one piece. It is a repeatable constructional model rather than a single unique coat; the Metropolitan Museum records it among the designs for which wartime clients particularly sought Balenciaga. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **1947:** *Tonneau line*. A “barrel” silhouette that displaced the natural waist and surrounded the body with curved volume. The name identifies a line manifested across multiple garments, not one securely bounded object. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf)

- **Early 1950s:** *Balloon-hem dresses*. Dresses whose volume was held away from the body at the hem, sometimes by concealed structural supports. This was an evolving constructional family rather than a single titled work. [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/introducing-cristobal-balenciaga), [V&A X-ray study](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/x-raying-balenciaga)

- **1951–1952:** *Semi-fitted line*. Jackets and dresses fitted at the front but released into volume at the back, initiating the progressive separation of garment from waist and spine. Sources differ between the first “semi-fitted piece” in 1951 and the “watershed” unwaisted suit of 1952, so the innovation cannot safely be reduced to one date or object. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf), [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/85421)

- **1953:** *Balloon jacket*. A spherical jacket enclosing the upper body and framing the wearer’s head. It is a named silhouette represented by more than one realization, not a uniquely titled jacket. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **February 1955:** *Tunic*. A straight two-piece dress with an over-layer falling independently of the waist; subsequent collections developed it toward the chemise and sack. Its status is therefore both garment type and continuing project. [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/833_en-press_kit.pdf), [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/85421)

- **1955–1956:** *Chemise line*. Tubular dresses structured internally but floating over the body. Dating is unstable in the literature: the Met catalogues a chemise-cut dress to 1955–56 but its overview places the named chemise in 1958, illustrating that the form emerged gradually rather than at one uncontested première. [Metropolitan Museum of Art object record](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/81464), [Metropolitan Museum of Art overview](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **1957:** *Cocoon coat*. A coat organized as a softly enclosing volume rather than a fitted outline. It belongs to Balenciaga’s vocabulary of silhouettes and survives in multiple versions, so “the cocoon coat” should not be mistaken for one unique object. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **1957:** *Balloon skirt*. A skirt formed as one rounded pouf or as two superimposed poufs. Like the balloon hem, it is a construction and recurring model family rather than a single work. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **1957:** *Sack dress*. A deliberately waistless dress descending from the semi-fitted, tunic and chemise experiments. The sources agree on its 1957 public emergence, but “sack dress” names a line reproduced in many fabrics and variants—not one definitive garment. [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa](https://balenciagamuseoa.koalition.com/en/), [V&A](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/introducing-cristobal-balenciaga)

- **1957–1958:** *Baby-doll dress*. A short, high-waisted or freely suspended dress that shifts volume away from the adult hourglass figure. Museum chronologies disagree between creation in 1957 and launch in 1958; the range preserves that dispute. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972), [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf)

- **Date not fixed:** *Stand-away collar*. A recurring collar construction held clear of the collarbone and neck. It resists treatment as an individual “work”: it is a design protocol manifested across many garments. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **Date not fixed:** *Seven-eighths or bracelet sleeve*. A shortened sleeve devised to expose the wrist and jewellery. This too is a repeatable design protocol, not a uniquely titled garment. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

- **From 1957:** *Silk gazar garments*. Dresses, suits and evening clothes exploiting the stiff, sculptural silk developed with Gustav Zumsteg’s Swiss textile house Abraham. The fabric itself is a collaborative material creation rather than Balenciaga’s sole invention; his obra lies in the forms he made from it. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/pt/essays/cristobal-balenciaga-1895-1972)

### Theatrical, ceremonial and institutional commissions

- **1933:** *Uniforms for Naviera Ybarra*. Corporate clothing for the Spanish shipping company. The catalogue records the commission but does not enumerate every garment or establish which examples survive, so it is listed as one uniform project rather than as invented individual titles. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf)

- **1941:** *Costumes for Alice Cocéa in Échec à Don Juan*. Stage costumes inspired by Spanish Golden Age painting. These are theatrical manifestations made for a performer and production, not ordinary couture models; the consulted catalogue does not supply a complete costume-by-costume inventory. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf)

- **1945:** *Contribution to the Théâtre de la Mode*. Balenciaga participated with other couturiers in the Chambre Syndicale’s exhibition of miniature couture on dolls, staged by artists including Christian Bérard. Authorship is distributed among designer, workshop, doll makers and scenographers, and the consulted chronology does not isolate or title every Balenciaga miniature. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf)

- **1960:** *Wedding ensemble for Fabiola de Mora y Aragón*. A ceremonial bridal gown and associated ensemble for the future queen of Belgium. It was a singular commission rather than a seasonal line, although its execution necessarily belonged to the collective labour of the couture workshop. [IAACC exhibition catalogue, pp. 18–19](https://iaacc.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/catalogo-balenciaga.pdf), [Kimbell Art Museum chronology](https://kimbellart.org/sites/default/files/2019-01/balenciaga_teacher_packet_final_10.18.18.pdf)

- **1968:** *Air France flight-attendant uniforms*. A system of garments for different climates and duties and Balenciaga’s exceptional experiment with standardized sizes. Sources variously describe the design as 1968 and its operational introduction as 1969; it is better understood as an industrial uniform project than as a single couture work. [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/359_en-download_press_dossier.pdf), [museum congress proceedings](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/714_en-i_congress_proceedings.pdf)

- **1972:** *Wedding dress for María del Carmen Martínez-Bordiú y Franco*. Balenciaga’s final documented couture design, undertaken after retirement for the March 1972 wedding and executed by collaborators outside the closed Paris house. Sources agree on his design authorship, but the distinction between designing and physically making the dress should be retained. [Kimbell Art Museum chronology](https://kimbellart.org/sites/default/files/2019-01/balenciaga_teacher_packet_final_10.18.18.pdf), [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa](https://blog.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/un-legado-para-el-futuro/)

### Olfactory house creations

- **1947:** *Le Dix*. The first fragrance issued under Balenciaga’s direction, accompanied by a bottle and packaging project for which archival drawings by him survive. The scent formula was composed by perfumer Francis Fabron, so it is a collaborative house creation rather than a perfume made by Balenciaga alone. [Osmothèque catalogue](https://www.osmotheque.fr/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Collection-Osmotheque-Juin-2015.pdf), [Kering](https://www.kering.com/en/group/kering-highlights/the-balenciaga-fragrance-collection-honoring-and-reinventing-the-house-s-olfactive-heritage/)

- **1948–1949:** *La Fuite des Heures*. A house fragrance composed by Germaine Cellier. The Osmothèque dates it to 1948, while later house histories give 1949; that discrepancy is preserved rather than silently resolved. [Osmothèque catalogue](https://www.osmotheque.fr/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Collection-Osmotheque-Juin-2015.pdf), [Balenciaga](https://www.balenciaga.com/en-us/fragrances-2)

- **1954:** *Eau de Balenciaga*. A fragrance launched under Balenciaga’s direction; the consulted house history identifies it as one of three perfumes introduced between 1949 and 1962 but does not name its perfumer. Its attribution therefore belongs securely to the house project, not necessarily to Balenciaga as olfactory composer. [Balenciaga](https://www.balenciaga.com/en-us/fragrances-2)

- **1955:** *Quadrille*. A fruity chypre composed by Francis Fabron and issued by the house. As with *Le Dix*, Balenciaga directed the branded Gesamtkunstwerk of scent, bottle, dress and salon identity, but did not alone author the liquid formula. [Osmothèque catalogue](https://www.osmotheque.fr/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/Collection-Osmotheque-Juin-2015.pdf)

- **By 1972:** *Ho Hang*. A fragrance included by the Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa among the scents launched in the wake of his fashion creations. The consulted museum source does not establish Balenciaga’s personal contribution or an exact launch date, so it resists inclusion as an individually authored work and is recorded only as a house manifestation from his lifetime. [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa perfume exhibition](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/fr/decouvrir/expositions-actuelles/le-parfum-au-temps-de-balenciaga.html)

### Fashion houses as creative projects

- **1917–1924:** *C. Balenciaga / Balenciaga y Cía*. Balenciaga’s first San Sebastián couture enterprise, initially established as C. Balenciaga and reorganized in 1918 with the Lizaso sisters. This is an institutional project through which garments were made, not itself a garment or text. [*Fashion and Heritage* catalogue, pp. 42–43](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf)

- **1924:** *Cristóbal Balenciaga, Avenida de la Libertad*. The successor San Sebastián house created after the earlier partnership ended. Its obra comprises an organization, salon practice and changing body of clothes rather than one bounded object. [*Fashion and Heritage* catalogue, pp. 42–43](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf)

- **1927:** *Martina Robes et Manteaux*. A second label named for Balenciaga’s mother and replaced later that year. Its short existence makes it simultaneously a commercial project and a distinct authorship mark on garments. [*Fashion and Heritage* catalogue, p. 43](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf)

- **1927–1968:** *Eisa Costura / Eisa B.E. / Eisa*. The Spanish-house project—its name derived from Eizaguirre—operated through changing labels and locations in San Sebastián, Madrid and Barcelona. Garments bearing these labels are Balenciaga-house works, but their physical production was collective and their labels do not always identify his individual hand. [*Fashion and Heritage* catalogue, p. 43](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf), [Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa collection catalogue](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/descubre/coleccion/colecciones/)

- **1937–1968:** *Balenciaga, 10 Avenue George V, Paris*. The Paris couture house founded with Wladzio d’Attainville and Nicolás Bizcarrondo: at once business, workshop, salon, seasonal performance and collective Gesamtkunstwerk. Calling the entire house Balenciaga’s “work” is useful only if it does not erase the cutters, seamstresses, embroiderers, milliners, textile makers and partners who materially realized it. [*Fashion and Heritage* catalogue, pp. 43–44](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf)

- Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa. [*Cristóbal Balenciaga: Fashion and Heritage*](https://www.cristobalbalenciagamuseoa.com/default/documentos/525_en-.pdf). Museum exhibition catalogue with chronology, catalogue entries and object list.
