## Works by Friedrich Wilhelm Joseph Schelling

### Early theological and philosophical manuscripts

- **1790:** *Oration canicularis*. A Latin school oration rather than a philosophical book; it survives among Schelling’s theological juvenilia. [AA II/1]
- **1791:** *Animadversiones in Jesajam*. A Latin exegetical exercise on Isaiah, preserved as a student manuscript. [AA II/2]
- **1792:** *De malorum origine*. A Latin disputation on the origin of evil; it anticipates a problem that would remain central to Schelling’s philosophy. [AA II/3]
- **1792:** *Vorstellungsarten der alten Welt über verschiedene Gegenstände, gesammelt aus Homer, Plato u. a.* A compilation of ancient conceptions drawn from classical authors; it is a research notebook or commonplace text rather than a finished publication. [AA II/4]
- **1793:** *Kommentar zum Galaterbrief*. An unfinished student commentary on Paul’s Epistle to the Galatians, surviving in manuscript. [AA II/5]
- **1793:** *Stellensammlung zu Paulus*. A collection of passages concerning Paul; it resists treatment as an autonomous work because it is working material rather than a completed argument. [AA II/5]
- **1793–1794:** *Geschichte des Gnosticismus*. An unfinished historical-theological project on Gnosticism, preserved in the estate. [AA II/5]
- **1793–1794:** *Entwurf der Vorrede* [to the projected work on Gnosticism]. A draft preface belonging to an unrealized publication project rather than an independent treatise. [AA II/5]
- **1793–1794:** *Proben eines Commentars über die früheste Geschichte Jesu*. Specimens for a projected commentary on the earliest history of Jesus; the project remained fragmentary. [AA II/5]
- **1794:** *Über den Geist der Platonischen Philosophie*. A manuscript essay on the animating principle of Platonic philosophy. [AA II/5]
- **1794:** *Kommentar zum „Timaeus“*. An unfinished commentary on Plato’s *Timaeus*, surviving as manuscript research rather than as a book Schelling published. [AA II/5]

### Dissertations, books, treatises, and dialogues published during his lifetime

- **1792:** *Antiquissimi de prima malorum humanorum origine philosophematis Genes. III explicandi tentamen criticum et philosophicum*. Schelling’s Latin master’s dissertation, a critical and philosophical interpretation of Genesis 3. [AA I/1]
- **1793:** *Ueber Mythen, historische Sagen und Philosopheme der ältesten Welt*. A published essay on myth, historical legend, and early-world philosophy. [AA I/1]
- **1794:** *Ueber die Möglichkeit einer Form der Philosophie überhaupt*. A philosophical treatise seeking the unconditional form of systematic philosophy. [AA I/1]
- **1795:** *Vom Ich als Princip der Philosophie oder über das Unbedingte im menschlichen Wissen*. A book-length philosophical treatise; it is not simply an exposition of Fichte, despite its place in the post-Kantian debate. [AA I/2]
- **1795:** *De Marcione Paullinarum Epistolarum Emendatore*. Schelling’s Latin theological dissertation on Marcion as an editor of the Pauline epistles. [AA I/2]
- **1795–1796:** *Philosophische Briefe über Dogmatismus und Kriticismus*. Ten philosophical “letters,” first issued serially and then as a separate publication; the epistolary form is part of the argument rather than incidental packaging. [AA I/3]
- **1796–1797:** *Neue Deduction des Naturrechts*. A serial treatise attempting a deduction of natural law. [AA I/3]
- **1797:** *Ideen zu einer Philosophie der Natur als Einleitung in das Studium dieser Wissenschaft*. Schelling’s first major book of nature-philosophy, proposing that nature be understood as productive activity rather than inert mechanism. [AA I/5]
- **1798:** *Von der Weltseele, eine Hypothese der höhern Physik zur Erklärung des allgemeinen Organismus*. A speculative-physical book that offers the “world soul” as a hypothesis for nature’s general organism. [AA I/6]
- **1799:** *Erster Entwurf eines Systems der Naturphilosophie*. A systematic draft published as a book; its title deliberately marks it as an “initial sketch,” so its project-status belongs to the work itself. [AA I/7]
- **1799:** *Einleitung zu seinem Entwurf eines Systems der Naturphilosophie*. A separately published introduction that reformulates the methodological grounds of the *Erster Entwurf*. [AA I/8]
- **1800:** *System des transscendentalen Idealismus*. A systematic philosophical book tracing the production of consciousness and culminating in art. [AA I/9]
- **1802:** *Bruno oder über das göttliche und natürliche Princip der Dinge*. A philosophical dialogue modeled on Platonic dialogue; its dramatic plurality prevents it from being reduced to a conventional treatise. [AA I/11]
- **1803:** *Vorlesungen über die Methode des academischen Studium*. A published sequence of lectures conceived as an encyclopedic orientation for university study and as a presentation of the identity-system. [AA I/14]
- **1803:** *Ideen zu einer Philosophie der Natur als Einleitung in das Studium dieser Wissenschaft*, second, substantially revised edition. Schelling made this a new textual state with corrected and additional material, not merely a reprint of the 1797 book. [AA I/13]
- **1804:** *Philosophie und Religion*. A treatise on the relation of the absolute, finitude, fall, and return; its precise relation to the identity-system has remained disputed. [AA I/14]
- **1806:** *Darlegung des wahren Verhältnisses der Naturphilosophie zu der verbesserten Fichte’schen Lehre*. A polemical book defending nature-philosophy against Fichte’s later doctrine. [AA I/16.1]
- **1809:** *Philosophische Untersuchungen über das Wesen der menschlichen Freyheit und die damit zusammenhängenden Gegenstände*. A published treatise on freedom, evil, personality, and divine life; it was Schelling’s last major philosophical book published in his lifetime. [AA I/17]
- **1812:** *Denkmal der Schrift von den göttlichen Dingen des Herrn Friedrich Heinrich Jacobi und der ihm in derselben gemachten Beschuldigung eines absichtlich täuschenden, Lüge redenden Atheismus*. A polemical memorial and public defense against Jacobi’s accusation of atheism. [AA I/18]
- **1815:** *Ueber die Gottheiten von Samothrace*. An antiquarian-mythological treatise interpreting the Samothracian deities within Schelling’s developing philosophy of mythology. [AA I/19]
- **1817:** *Bericht über die Aeginetischen Bildwerke im Besitz Seiner Königlichen Hoheit des Kronprinzen von Bayern*. An art-historical and archaeological report on the Aegina sculptures, not a philosophical book in the narrow sense. [AA I/19]
- **1850:** *Abhandlung über die Quelle der ewigen Wahrheiten*. A late academy treatise on the ground or source of eternal truths. It was published during Schelling’s lifetime, although closely connected with arguments elaborated in his unpublished late philosophy. [SW I/11]

### Serial essays and systematic presentations

- **1796–1798:** *Allgemeine Übersicht der neuesten philosophischen Literatur*. An anonymously published series of essays in the *Philosophisches Journal*; in 1809 Schelling reworked it as *Abhandlungen zur Erläuterung des Idealismus der Wissenschaftslehre*, so its identity across versions is editorially complex. [AA I/4]
- **1800:** *Allgemeine Deduction des dynamischen Processes oder der Kategorien der Physik*. A journal treatise constructing the dynamic process and categories of physics. [AA I/8]
- **1801:** *Darstellung meines Systems der Philosophie*. A serial systematic presentation in which Schelling publicly formulated his philosophy as a system of identity. [AA I/10]
- **1802:** *Ueber das absolute Identitäts-System und sein Verhältniß zu dem neuesten (Reinholdischen) Dualismus*. A polemical-systematic journal essay directed against Reinhold’s new dualism. [AA I/11]
- **1802:** *Fernere Darstellungen aus dem System der Philosophie*. A sequence of further presentations rather than a freestanding completed system. [AA I/12]
- **1802:** *Ueber das Verhältniß der Naturphilosophie zur Philosophie überhaupt*. A programmatic essay locating nature-philosophy within philosophy as a whole. [AA I/12]
- **1802:** *Ueber die Construction in der Philosophie*. A methodological essay on philosophical construction. [AA I/12]
- **1802–1803:** *Ueber Dante in philosophischer Beziehung*. A philosophical-literary essay on Dante, treating the *Divine Comedy* as an exemplary synthesis of knowledge, history, and poetry. [AA I/12]
- **1805:** *Aphorismen zur Einleitung in die Naturphilosophie*. A numbered aphoristic presentation; its fragmentary form is deliberate rather than evidence of accidental incompleteness. [AA I/15]
- **1805:** *Vorläufige Bezeichnung des Standpunktes der Medicin nach Grundsätzen der Naturphilosophie*. A programmatic essay situating medicine within nature-philosophy. [AA I/15]
- **1806:** *Aphorismen über die Naturphilosophie*. An aphoristic continuation and reformulation of Schelling’s nature-philosophy. [AA I/15]
- **1806:** *Ueber das Verhältniß des Realen und Idealen in der Natur*. A revised systematic essay published as an addition to *Von der Weltseele*. [AA I/16.1]
- **1807:** *Ueber das Verhältniß der bildenden Künste zu der Natur*. An academy address on visual art’s relation to nature; as a performed ceremonial speech, it is both event and text. [AA I/16.2]
- **1809:** *Abhandlungen zur Erläuterung des Idealismus der Wissenschaftslehre*. A revised republication of the 1796–1798 *Allgemeine Übersicht*; because Schelling reshaped earlier serial essays, it is a new version but not an wholly independent composition. [AA I/17]
- **1813:** *Ueber die wissenschaftliche Behandlung des Naturrechts*. A contribution to the *Allgemeine Zeitschrift von Deutschen für Deutsche* concerning the scientific treatment of natural law. [AA I/19]
- **1813:** *Ueber die Geschichte der neueren Philosophie*. A journal contribution on the historical shape of modern philosophy, distinct from the later lecture manuscript conventionally given a similar title. [AA I/19]

### Criticism, reviews, declarations, and interventions

- **1796:** *Antikritik*. A reply to criticism of the *Philosophische Briefe*; the historical-critical edition prints it with the criticism that occasioned it. [AA I/3]
- **1797:** *An Heydenreich*. A short public intervention addressed to Karl Heinrich Heydenreich. [AA I/4]
- **1797:** *Antwort auf Tittmann*. A polemical response in a contemporary philosophical controversy. [AA I/4]
- **1798:** *Carus-Rezension*. A review of work by Friedrich August Carus; the conventional title is editorial shorthand, not Schelling’s independent book title. [AA I/4]
- **1798:** *Ueber Offenbarung und Volksunterricht*. A review-essay prompted by Niethammer’s dissertation on revelation, extending the argument into religious education. [AA I/4]
- **1798:** *Schlosser-Rezension*. A sharply polemical review of Johann Georg Schlosser’s anti-Kantian writing; its customary title is editorial shorthand. [AA I/4]
- **1799:** *Einige Bemerkungen aus Gelegenheit einer Rezension Brownscher Schriften in der A.L.Z.* A brief intervention in disputes over Brownian medicine and nature-philosophy. [AA I/8]
- **1799:** *Zu einem Schreiben J. G. Fichte’s*. A short public response to Fichte. [AA I/8]
- **1799:** *Bitte an die Herren Herausgeber der A.L.Z.* A public request addressed to the editors of the *Allgemeine Literatur-Zeitung*; it is an act within a controversy more than a descriptive essay. [AA I/8]
- **1800:** *Anmerkungen zu „Recension der neuern naturphilosophischen Schriften des Herausgebers von Dr. Steffens“*. Editorial annotations attached to Henrik Steffens’s review. [AA I/8]
- **1800:** *Anhang zu dem voranstehenden Aufsatz, betreffend zwei naturphilosophische Recensionen und die Jenaische Allgemeine Literaturzeitung*. A polemical appendix continuing Schelling’s dispute with the Jena review journal. [AA I/8]
- **1800:** *Vorbericht und Anmerkung zu „Ueber den Oxydations- und Desoxydations-Process der Erde“*. Editorial framing and annotation supplied to an essay by Henrik Steffens; authorship of the hosted essay is Steffens’s, while the paratext is Schelling’s. [AA I/8]
- **1802:** *Rückert und Weiß, oder die Philosophie zu der es keines Denkens und Wissens bedarf*. A satirical-polemical journal essay. [AA I/11]
- **1802:** *An den Herrn Herausgeber, betreffend ein Schreiben über Ion in N. 41*. A brief public letter concerning a piece on August Wilhelm Schlegel’s *Ion*. [AA I/11]
- **1802:** *Benehmen des Obscurantismus gegen die Naturphilosophie*. A polemical miscellany on opposition to nature-philosophy. [AA I/12]
- **1802:** *Die vier edlen Metalle*. A short nature-philosophical piece on the four noble metals. [AA I/12]
- **1803:** *Immanuel Kant*. An obituary or memorial notice; it is an occasional public text rather than a systematic Kant study. [AA I/14]
- **1805:** *An das Publikum*. A public declaration associated with Schelling’s Würzburg controversies. [AA I/15]
- **1805–1806:** *Antwort auf mehrere Anfragen*. A published answer to multiple inquiries; it acts as clarification within a public dispute. [AA I/15]
- **1806:** *Professor Schelling, an die Redaktion der oberdeutschen Literatur-Zeitung*. A public letter to a journal’s editors. [AA I/15]
- **1806:** *Erklärung, den Herrn Kajetan Weiller in München betreffend*. A public declaration concerning Kajetan Weiller. [AA I/15]
- **1806:** *Aus Briefen vom December 1806*. Published extracts from letters; their status lies between correspondence and deliberately released public text. [AA I/15]
- **1807:** *Warnung vor einem Pasquille mit der Aufschrift: Die Fortschritte des Lichts in Bayern*. A polemical notice attributed to Schelling, but the historical-critical edition places it among attributed rather than securely authenticated texts. [AA I/15]
- **1808–1809:** *Niethammer-Rezensionen*. Reviews associated with Friedrich Immanuel Niethammer; the collective label is editorial, and individual attribution and textual boundaries require the critical apparatus. [AA I/18]

### Editorial and collaborative publishing projects

- **1800–1801:** *Zeitschrift für spekulative Physik*. A journal founded and edited by Schelling, combining his texts with contributions by others. It is therefore an editorial project and intellectual platform, not wholly a work authored by him. [AA I/8–10]
- **1802:** *Neue Zeitschrift für speculative Physik*. Schelling’s successor periodical to the *Zeitschrift für spekulative Physik*; again, it is a made editorial framework containing both his and others’ work. [AA I/12]
- **1802–1803:** *Kritisches Journal der Philosophie*. A journal jointly edited and substantially written with G. W. F. Hegel. Several contributions were unsigned or jointly framed, so exclusive authorship of individual pieces is sometimes disputed. [AA I/11–12]
- **1802:** *Einleitung. Ueber das Wesen der philosophischen Kritik überhaupt, und ihr Verhältniß zum gegenwärtigen Zustand der Philosophie insbesondere*. The opening manifesto of the *Kritisches Journal*; it defines what philosophical criticism is to do. Its attribution has been discussed because the journal was a Schelling–Hegel collaboration, though the Academy edition includes it among Schelling’s publications. [AA I/11]
- **1802:** *Notizenblatt*. A recurring critical and bibliographical department of the *Kritisches Journal*, not a single conventional essay; authorship of its component notices is not uniformly secure. [AA I/11]
- **1805–1808:** *Jahrbücher der Medicin als Wissenschaft*. A medical-philosophical journal co-edited with the physician Adalbert Friedrich Marcus. The journal as a whole is a collaborative project, while Schelling’s own contributions are separately identifiable. [AA I/15]
- **1805:** *Ankündigung der „Jahrbücher der Medicin als Wissenschaft“*. A prospectus and manifesto for the journal, stating an intended science of medicine grounded in nature-philosophy. [AA I/15]
- **1805:** *Vorrede* to the *Jahrbücher der Medicin als Wissenschaft*. An editorial preface that establishes the periodical’s program. [AA I/15]
- **1805–1806:** *Kritische Fragmente*. Short critical pieces in the *Jahrbücher*; the collective title names a serial form rather than one continuous treatise. [AA I/15]
- **1805–1806:** *Auszug mit Anmerkungen von „Cogitata nonnulla de Idea Vitae, hujusque formis praecipuis“*. An annotated extract from Karl Eberhard Schelling’s work: the underlying text is his brother’s, while Friedrich Schelling made the selection and annotations. [AA I/15]

### Speeches, translations, poetry, and occasional texts

- **1803–1804:** *Übersetzung aus Petrarca*. A German translation from Petrarch; it is a work of translation rather than an original philosophical composition. [AA I/14]
- **1801:** Published poems collected in the Academy edition’s *Schriften 1801*. These are individual literary compositions, but the edition’s summary does not support silently manufacturing titles or dates for poems whose documentary identity must be established from the volume itself. [AA I/10]
- **1805–1807:** *Fragmente*. Brief published or transmitted pieces gathered under an editorial collective title; they resist being treated as a single authored work. [AA I/15]
- **1805–1807:** An untitled or editorially bracketed sonnet transmitted among texts attributed to Schelling. Its authorship is not secure, and the absence of a fixed authorial title should be retained rather than repaired. [AA I/15]
- **1807:** *Rede zum Namensfest des Königs*. A ceremonial address connected with the Bavarian court; as delivered speech, it was first an event and only secondarily a text. [AA I/16.2]
- **1810:** *Stuttgarter Privatvorlesungen*. A sequence of private oral lectures recorded by listeners. What survives is a manifestation through auditors’ notes, not a book Schelling prepared for publication. [SW I/7]
- **1827:** *Rede bei der Eröffnung der Vorlesungen in München*. An inaugural academic address marking Schelling’s assumption of his Munich professorship. [SW I/9]
- **1835:** *Rede bei der Enthüllung des Denkmals für König Maximilian I. Joseph*. A ceremonial public address, simultaneously a performed civic event and a printed text. [SW I/9]
- **1841:** *Antrittsrede in Berlin*. Schelling’s inaugural Berlin lecture or address; it circulated through reports and listeners’ transcripts as well as authorized textual forms. [SW II/3]

### Unpublished systems, lecture manuscripts, and unfinished projects

- **1800–1801:** *System der gesammten Philosophie und der Naturphilosophie insbesondere*. A lecture-system surviving in manuscript and posthumous edition; it was not published by Schelling as a completed book. [SW I/6]
- **1802–1803:** *Philosophie der Kunst*. A lecture course reconstructed from Schelling’s manuscripts and lecture use. Its familiar book-form is posthumous, so it is better classified as a teaching project and manuscript complex. [SW I/5]
- **1803–1804:** *System der Philosophie überhaupt und der Naturphilosophie insbesondere*. A Würzburg lecture manuscript presenting an identity-philosophical system; it remained unpublished in Schelling’s lifetime. [SW I/6]
- **1804:** *Propädeutik der Philosophie*. An introductory lecture project, transmitted through manuscripts or student records rather than an authorially published book. [SW I/6]
- **1804–1805:** *System der gesammten Philosophie*. An unfinished or lecture-bound systematic presentation; its limits and relation to neighboring Würzburg manuscripts are editorial questions. [SW I/6]
- **1809–1810:** *Clara oder über den Zusammenhang der Natur mit der Geisterwelt*. An unfinished philosophical dialogue on death and the spirit world. Schelling did not publish it, and its fragmentary, dialogical state is part of what it is. [SW I/9]
- **1811:** *Die Weltalter*, first manuscript/draft. The first surviving large-scale version of Schelling’s projected “Ages of the World,” conceived as the opening of a much larger system. [WA]
- **1813:** *Die Weltalter*, second manuscript/draft. A substantially rewritten version, not merely a corrected copy of the 1811 text. [WA]
- **1814–1815:** *Die Weltalter*, printing version and proofs. Schelling brought this version toward publication and then withheld it; the canceled publication is an unfinished project, not a lost completed book. [WA]
- **1815 and later:** Later fragments and revisions associated with *Die Weltalter*. They testify to a continuing project whose intended final form was never achieved, and editors disagree about how they should be ordered into “versions.” [WA; AA II]
- **1817:** *Initia philosophiae universae*. Notes or a transcript from the Erlangen lectures on the beginnings of universal philosophy. Because the wording is mediated by lecture transmission, it is not straightforwardly an authorial text. [SW I/9]
- **1820–1821:** *Erlanger Vorträge* / *Ueber die Natur der Philosophie als Wissenschaft*. An oral lecture project on philosophy’s nature as science; the received text is posthumous and textually dependent on manuscript or transcript evidence. [SW I/9]
- **1821:** *Ueber die Gottheiten von Samothrace* revisions and supplements. Working materials showing that the published 1815 treatise remained part of a developing mythological project rather than a closed antiquarian study. [AA II]
- **1827:** *Zur Geschichte der neueren Philosophie*. A Munich lecture cycle on modern philosophy, published only posthumously. The standard title describes a lecture manuscript rather than a lifetime book. [SW I/10]
- **1827–1828:** *System der Weltalter*. A Munich lecture course that reconfigured the unrealized *Weltalter* project in oral form; the surviving text is mediated by notes and posthumous editing. [SW I/10]
- **1827–1830:** *Einleitung in die Philosophie*. A Munich lecture project transmitted in manuscripts and student notes; it exists in more than one textual state rather than as one authorially fixed book. [AA II; Schellingiana 1]
- **1828:** *Darstellung des philosophischen Empirismus*. A late lecture or treatise manuscript describing Schelling’s “philosophical empiricism”; it was published posthumously. [SW I/10]
- **1828–1829:** *Philosophie der Mythologie*. A repeatedly delivered and revised lecture project interpreting mythology as a historical process of consciousness. The familiar printed work was assembled posthumously and should not be mistaken for a book Schelling issued in this form. [SW II/1–2]
- **1830:** *System der positiven Philosophie*. A lecture project distinguishing positive philosophy from purely rational or negative philosophy; no single lifetime publication fixes its definitive form. [AA II]
- **1831–1832:** *Grundlegung der positiven Philosophie*. Lecture manuscripts and transcripts that establish the program of positive philosophy; their boundaries and titles vary across witnesses. [AA II]
- **1832–1833:** *Einleitung in die Philosophie der Offenbarung oder Begründung der positiven Philosophie*. A Munich lecture cycle serving as a foundation for the philosophy of revelation. [SW II/3]
- **1833–1834:** *Philosophie der Offenbarung*. A major lecture project treating Christianity and revelation within positive philosophy. Schelling repeatedly delivered and revised it, so there is no uncontested single final authorial text. [SW II/3–4]
- **1837:** *Philosophische Einleitung in die Philosophie der Mythologie oder Darstellung der reinrationalen Philosophie*. A lecture-manuscript presentation of “purely rational” or negative philosophy, later printed posthumously as the introduction to the mythology lectures. [SW II/1]
- **1841–1842:** *Philosophie der Offenbarung*, Berlin course. The lectures became a major public event and circulated through unauthorized student transcripts before the posthumous authorized edition; “the work” consequently exists as performance, transcript tradition, and edited text. [SW II/3–4]
- **1842:** *Philosophie der Mythologie*, Berlin course. A new oral manifestation of the long-running mythology project, known through lecture documentation rather than a lifetime authorial edition. [AA II]
- **1842–1843:** *Darstellung der reinrationalen Philosophie*. A Berlin lecture form of Schelling’s negative philosophy; its relation to the posthumously printed “philosophical introduction” is an editorial rather than merely bibliographical question. [SW II/1]
- **1843–1844:** *Philosophie der Offenbarung*, later Berlin course. A revised delivery of the revelation project, not simply a repetition of the 1841–1842 lectures. [AA II]
- **1844–1845:** *Philosophie der Mythologie*, later Berlin course. A further lecture-state of the mythology project, surviving through manuscripts and Nachschriften. [AA II]
- **1845–1846:** *Philosophie der Offenbarung*, final documented Berlin course. It belongs to the evolving lecture-project; no final lifetime book resolved the differences among its courses and witnesses. [AA II]
- **Undated, chiefly late period:** Plans, outlines, fragments, and revisions for the negative and positive philosophies. These resist enumeration as independent “works” whenever they are only working papers without authorial titles or stable boundaries; the historical-critical edition appropriately treats them as estate materials rather than silently converting each sheet into a book. [AA II]

### Correspondence as a made body of work

- **c. 1788–1854:** *Briefe*. Schelling produced a large body of correspondence—family, philosophical, academic, editorial, and political—but individual letters are communicative acts rather than works in the same sense as books or treatises. The historical-critical edition therefore gives them their own series and distinguishes letters by Schelling from letters addressed to him. [AA III]
