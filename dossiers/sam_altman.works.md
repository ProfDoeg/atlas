## Works by Samuel Harris Altman

### Companies, laboratories, and institutional obras

- **2005–2012:** *Loopt*. A location-based mobile social network co-founded by Altman and Nick Sivo; it was both a software product and a startup organization, sold to Green Dot in 2012. It is a collective obra, not software attributable to Altman alone. [S1]

- **2012–:** *Hydrazine Capital*. A venture-investment firm co-founded with Jack Altman using proceeds from the Loopt sale; its “made things” are portfolios and allocations of capital rather than conventional texts or products. [S2]

- **2015–:** *YC Research*. A nonprofit research organization initiated by Altman while he led Y Combinator. Its projects—including early work on universal basic income—were collective investigations; Altman created the institutional frame but did not personally author all their research. [S3]

- **2015–:** *OpenAI*. A nonprofit artificial-intelligence research project conceived and launched collectively by Altman, Greg Brockman, Ilya Sutskever, Elon Musk, and others; contemporary sources disagree about precisely who merits the word “founder,” so sole authorship would be misleading. It subsequently became a changing complex of nonprofit and commercial entities—a corporate and technical Gesamtkunstwerk rather than one product. [S4][S5][S6]

- **2019–:** *Tools for Humanity*. A technology company co-founded by Altman, Alex Blania, and Max Novendstern to build human-verification and financial infrastructure. It is the company behind the project initially called Worldcoin and later World. [S7]

- **2019–:** *Worldcoin / World*. An unfinished global identity-and-financial-network project comprising World ID, the World Chain protocol, the WLD token, the World App, and the Orb biometric device. Altman is a co-founder and conceptual sponsor, but these manifestations are collective engineering works and should not be catalogued as objects made by him alone. [S7][S8]

- **date not fixed in the consulted sources:** *Apollo Projects*. An early-stage investment fund established by Sam, Jack, and Max Altman. Like Hydrazine, it resists ordinary work-catalogue language: its operative medium is financing other people’s companies. [S9]

- **2021–2024:** *AltC Acquisition Corp.* A special-purpose acquisition company co-founded by Altman and Michael Klein and led by Altman as CEO; it was a temporary corporate vehicle whose intended obra was a merger, completed with nuclear-energy company Oklo in 2024. [S10]

- **2026–:** *Merge Labs*. A research laboratory established by scientists and technology entrepreneurs including Altman to pursue non-invasive links between biological and artificial intelligence. It remains an open-ended project rather than a completed product, and OpenAI’s announcement carefully describes Altman as participating “in a personal capacity.” [S11]

### Civic, medical, and social projects

- **2016:** *VotePlz*. A voter-registration project that Altman said he helped start with friends; its manifestations included a website, an iOS application, and a registration-referral sweepstakes. “Helped start” is the available attribution and should not be inflated into sole authorship. [S12]

- **2020:** *Funding for COVID-19 Projects*. An emergency grant project announced through Altman’s blog to finance ideas addressing the pandemic. It was a call that acted by directing money and applicants, making it closer to a protocol than a descriptive essay. [S13]

- **2020:** *Project Covalence*. A collaboration announced by Altman, Lachy Groom, Mark Fishman, and TrialSpark to provide operational, statistical, regulatory, telemedicine, and specimen-collection support for COVID-19 clinical trials. It was infrastructure for producing other studies, not itself a single clinical experiment. [S14]

### Courses, guides, and protocols

- **2014:** *How to Start a Startup*. A Stanford course assembled and led by Altman as a sequence of lectures by multiple startup founders and investors, subsequently published online as videos, transcripts, readings, and slides. It is best classified as a pedagogical Gesamtkunstwerk; Altman created and taught part of it but did not author every lecture. [S15][S16]

- **2015:** *Startup Playbook*. An online startup manual written by Altman and illustrated by Gregory Koberger. It is explicitly instructional—a protocol for forming and operating a young company—rather than a monograph reporting research. [S17]

### Essays, manifestos, and serial writing

- **2013–:** *Sam Altman: Post Archive*. Altman’s continuously revised body of personal blog writing, encompassing short essays, announcements, endorsements, memorials, investment notices, and project updates. Treating the archive as one serial obra avoids pretending that these heterogeneous posts constitute a conventional book, while its official index supplies the authoritative title-and-date catalogue for the individual manifestations. [S18]

- **2019:** *How to Be Successful*. A numbered essay presenting thirteen principles for unusual career success. Its imperatives make it partly a protocol and partly a personal manifesto. [S19]

- **2021:** *Moore’s Law for Everything*. A political-economic manifesto proposing that technological growth, taxation of capital, and distributions to citizens be combined into an “American Equity Fund”; the fund remained a proposal, so the project should not be mistaken for an implemented institution. [S20]

- **2023:** *Planning for AGI and Beyond*. An OpenAI policy manifesto signed by Altman that proposes principles for deploying increasingly capable AI. It announces intentions and governance commitments rather than documenting a completed technical system. [S21]

- **2023:** *Governance of Superintelligence*. A manifesto and preliminary governance protocol co-authored with Greg Brockman and Ilya Sutskever. Its proposed international coordination and oversight institutions remained proposals at publication. [S22]

- **2023:** *Testimony before the U.S. Senate Committee on the Judiciary*. Written testimony submitted under Altman’s name concerning large neural networks and AI regulation. It is a public institutional utterance—a text intended to affect law and policy—not merely a description of OpenAI. [S23]

- **2024:** *The Intelligence Age*. A manifesto forecasting an era of broadly available artificial intelligence, prosperity, and accelerated scientific progress. It is programmatic and speculative rather than a technical report. [S24]

- **2025:** *Three Observations*. A compact manifesto about the scaling economics of AI, declining intelligence costs, and the expected socioeconomic effects of artificial general intelligence. It offers propositions and forecasts, not experimental findings. [S18]

- **2025:** *The Gentle Singularity*. A speculative essay presenting Altman’s preferred account of a gradual transition toward superintelligence. Its subject is a projected process, so the singularity described by the title is not itself a completed work. [S18]

- **2025:** *Abundant Intelligence*. A manifesto arguing for abundant, inexpensive AI infrastructure and wide access to intelligence. It functions as both prediction and public justification for an ongoing industrial project. [S18]

### Collective scientific and technical texts

- **2023:** *GPT-4 Technical Report*. A collectively authored OpenAI report whose alphabetical author list includes Altman among scores of contributors. Its contribution statement assigns him only an “additional contributions” role; cataloguing it as an Altman work is defensible only as highly distributed co-authorship, never as sole or principal authorship. [S25]

### Works whose personal attribution should be resisted

- **2015–:** *OpenAI’s models and products*. GPT systems, ChatGPT, DALL·E, Whisper, Sora, Codex, and related interfaces appeared under an institution Altman co-founded and later led, but the credited research, engineering, design, and deployment teams made them collectively. Leadership is not sufficient evidence that Altman personally made each model or product, so they belong to OpenAI’s catalogue rather than an honest personal catalogue raisonné. [S6][S25]
