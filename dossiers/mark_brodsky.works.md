## Works by Mark Brodsky

### Unpublished novels and projects

- **1976:** *Flesh Is Flesh*. An unpublished pseudo-thriller that Michael Mark Brodsky says was accepted by Urizen Books before the publisher went bankrupt; the manuscript survives. ([Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/))
- **1979:** *Theme and Variations*. An unpublished, approximately 1,200-page mystery that turns into a meditation on life and language; Brodsky subsequently cannibalized parts of it for later works, so its status as a wholly independent obra is unstable. Urizen intended to publish it in three volumes before going bankrupt, and the manuscript survives. ([Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/))

### Novels

- **1977 or 1978:** *Detour*. Brodsky’s first published novel and the recipient of a PEN/Hemingway citation. Contemporary accounts identify the first edition as 1977, while the standard bibliographic list gives 1978; later expanded and electronic editions are manifestations of the same obra, not separate works. ([Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/); [bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
- **1983:** *Circuits*. A novel; subsequent reissues are later manifestations rather than new works. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Books We Live By](https://bookcasetv.com/))
- **1987:** *Xman*. An experimental novel organized around an attenuated, nearly anonymous protagonist; it resists conventional plot-centered classification but remains explicitly presented as a novel. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
- **1989:** *Dyad*. A novel that Brodsky describes as using the plot machinery of Patricia Highsmith’s *The Talented Mr. Ripley* as a deliberately unstable pseudo-thriller framework. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/))
- **1994:** *&#42;&#42;&#42;*. A novel whose title is literally three asterisks; the typographic placeholder is therefore part of the obra, not a missing title supplied by a cataloguer. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
- **1999:** *We Can Report Them*. A novel whose narrative incorporates commercial filmmaking, illness, philosophy, and the protagonist’s obsessive thought; its discursive structure strains, without altogether abandoning, the category “novel.” ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Contemporary Authors profile](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/brodsky-michael-1948))
- **2013:** *Lurianics*. A novel about the attempt to bring a “true work” into existence; the work makes the project or vocation of creation itself its subject. ([Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/); [publication record](https://www.fictiondb.com/book/lurianics~michael-brodsky~1108263.htm))
- **2023:** *Invidicum*. A very long novel concerning an experimental treatment for “Envy Disease,” published with illuminations by Michael Hafftka. Before publication Brodsky variously described it as about 900 and about 1,500 pages, so those preliminary measurements should not be treated as a settled extent of the finished manifestation. ([Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/); [Tough Poets Press](https://www.toughpoets.com/authors.htm))

### Collections and shorter fiction

- **1981:** *Wedding Feast, & Two Novellas*. A collection comprising the title story and two novellas, rather than a single novel. ([Dictionary of Literary Biography-derived bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [first-edition record](https://www.longhousepoetry.com/catalog_b.html))
- **1982:** *Project and Other Short Pieces*. A collection of short prose whose title names “Project” as one constituent work; bibliographies often abbreviate the whole collection to *Project*. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
- **1988:** *X in Paris*. A collection of short fiction, not a novel despite its unified title. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [publisher listing](https://bookcasetv.com/))
- **1991:** *Three Goat Songs*. A collection of three extended fictions or novellas; its “songs” are prose forms, not musical compositions. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [first-edition record](https://www.longhousepoetry.com/catalog_b.html))
- **1996:** *Southernmost and Other Stories*. A short-story collection; shortened bibliographies frequently record it simply as *Southernmost*. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [first-edition record](https://www.longhousepoetry.com/catalog_b.html))
- **2007:** *Limit Point*. An illustrated fiction made in collaboration with artist Michael Hafftka and publisher-artist Che Elias. It is simultaneously a prose work and an art-book manifestation, so assigning the entire physical object to Brodsky alone would obscure its collaborative authorship. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Brodsky interview](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/))

### Plays and theatrical manifestations

- **1980:** *Terrible Sunlight*. A four-act play produced at New York’s South Street Theater; the script remained unpublished, making the production rather than a book its public manifestation. ([Contemporary Authors profile](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/brodsky-michael-1948); [bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
- **1982:** *Packet Piece*. An unpublished play surviving in manuscript. The standard bibliography dates it but supplies no documented production, so it is best treated as a completed dramatic project rather than an established theatrical manifestation. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Brodsky interview on his unpublished plays](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/))
- **1982:** *No Packet Piece*. An unpublished play surviving in manuscript; its title marks a relation to *Packet Piece*, but the bibliography records it as a distinct work. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Brodsky interview on his unpublished plays](https://thecollidescope.com/2023/03/01/stigmata-of-the-intrinsic-lesion-an-interview-with-michael-brodsky/))
- **1990:** *Dose Center*. An unpublished play produced at Theater for the New City in New York. ([Contemporary Authors profile](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/brodsky-michael-1948))
- **1990:** *Night of the Chair*. An unpublished play produced at New York’s Theater Club Funambules. ([Contemporary Authors profile](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/brodsky-michael-1948))
- **1994:** *Six Scenes: A Barracks Brawl*. An unpublished play staged by Target Margin Theater at Ontological–Hysteric Theater/St. Mark’s; the reference bibliography shortens its title to *Six Scenes*. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Contemporary Authors profile](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/brodsky-michael-1948))
- **1996–2000:** *The Anti-Muse*. An unpublished play given a staged reading by Target Margin Theater in 1996 and subsequently performed in 2000; the reading and performance are distinct manifestations of the same dramatic obra. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky); [Contemporary Authors profile](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/brodsky-michael-1948))

### Translation as obra

- **1995:** *Eleuthéria*. Brodsky’s English translation of Samuel Beckett’s previously suppressed 1947 French play. The play itself is Beckett’s obra, while Brodsky made this particular linguistic manifestation; it should therefore not be attributed to him as an original drama. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))

### Essays and criticism

- **1977:** *Svevo: The Artist as Analyzand*. A critical essay published in *Review of Existential Psychology and Psychiatry*, volume 15, numbers 2–3, pages 112–133. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
- **2004:** *Toward the Plane of the Sacred: Hafftka’s Great Chain of Being*. A catalogue essay made for *Michael Hafftka: A Retrospective—Large Oils 1985–2003*; it belongs to an exhibition-catalogue apparatus rather than constituting a freestanding book. ([bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brodsky))
