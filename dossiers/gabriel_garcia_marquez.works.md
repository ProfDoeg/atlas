## Works by Gabriel José García Márquez

### Novels and novellas

- **1955:** *La hojarasca*. Novel set in Macondo; it incorporates the separately published story *Monólogo de Isabel viendo llover en Macondo*. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/bibliografia.htm))
- **1961:** *El coronel no tiene quien le escriba*. Novella, first published in book form after earlier magazine publication. García Márquez later described it, *La mala hora*, and *Los funerales de la Mamá Grande* as effectively one interconnected book. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/garcia_marquez_gabriel_cronologia.htm), [Gabriel García Márquez bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Garc%C3%ADa_M%C3%A1rquez_bibliography))
- **1962:** *La mala hora*. Novel of political violence and anonymous denunciatory pamphlets; an unauthorized, textually altered 1962 Madrid edition was followed by the author-approved Mexican edition of 1966. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/bibliografia.htm))
- **1967:** *Cien años de soledad*. Novel developed from the much earlier, abandoned project *La casa*; the relation between the two is genealogical, not simply a change of title. ([El País](https://elpais.com/babelia/2025-01-15/cien-anos-de-soledad-de-principio-a-fin.html), [Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingaid.cfm?eadid=01083))
- **1975:** *El otoño del patriarca*. Novel composed as six long, recursively structured sections about an archetypal Caribbean dictator. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/bibliografia.htm))
- **1981:** *Crónica de una muerte anunciada*. Short novel fashioned as a retrospective journalistic reconstruction; its hybrid of fiction, testimony, and chronicle resists a clean novel/reportage division. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/bibliografia.htm))
- **1985:** *El amor en los tiempos del cólera*. Novel. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/pdf/01084.pdf))
- **1989:** *El general en su laberinto*. Historical novel about Simón Bolívar’s final journey; it is fiction built through extensive documentary research rather than a biography. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/pdf/01084.pdf))
- **1994:** *Del amor y otros demonios*. Novel whose narrative point of departure García Márquez presented as the rediscovery of a grave he had reported on as a young journalist. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **2004:** *Memoria de mis putas tristes*. Novella and García Márquez’s last work of fiction published during his lifetime. ([Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/comunidad/perfil/gabriel-garcia-marquez))
- **1990s–2004; published 2024:** *En agosto nos vemos*. Unfinished final fiction project assembled posthumously from several drafts. García Márquez, already experiencing cognitive decline, had ultimately said it should be destroyed; his sons published an edited text against that instruction, so its status as a finished novel—and even as an authorized work—remains disputed. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/pdf/01084.pdf), [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/03/13/gabriel-garcia-marquez-until-august-texas))

- **1948:** *Amargura para tres sonámbulos*. Short story.

- **1953:** *La noche de los alcaravanes*. Short story.
- **1955:** *Monólogo de Isabel viendo llover en Macondo*. Short story closely related to *La hojarasca* and subsequently extracted and issued on its own; its simultaneous status as component and independent text resists a simple work/edition distinction.
- **1961:** *El mar del tiempo perdido*. Short story and an important threshold between the early Macondo fictions and *Cien años de soledad*. ([El País](https://elpais.com/babelia/2025-01-15/cien-anos-de-soledad-de-principio-a-fin.html))

- **1962:** *Un día después del sábado*. Short story collected in *Los funerales de la Mamá Grande*.
- **1962:** *Rosas artificiales*. Short story collected in *Los funerales de la Mamá Grande*.

- **1968:** *Blacamán el Bueno, vendedor de milagros*. Short story.
- **1970:** *Muerte constante más allá del amor*. Short story.
- **1972:** *La increíble y triste historia de la cándida Eréndira y de su abuela desalmada*. Long story or novella; also the title of the seven-story collection in which it appeared. Its uncertain generic scale is part of the object rather than a defect in classification. ([Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm))
- **1972/1974:** *Ojos de perro azul*. Authorial collection of early stories written or published between 1947 and 1955. Different national editions appeared with different dates and contents, so the collection has no single universally stable first-edition form. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [Ojos de perro azul](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojos_de_perro_azul))

- **1972:** *La increíble y triste historia de la cándida Eréndira y de su abuela desalmada: siete cuentos*. Collection of seven stories composed at different dates. ([Gabriel García Márquez bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Garc%C3%ADa_M%C3%A1rquez_bibliography))
- **1974–1992:** *Doce cuentos peregrinos*. Eighteen-year story-cycle repeatedly drafted, discarded, recovered, converted in part into screen projects, and finally published as a twelve-story book in 1992. - **1992:** *La luz es como el agua*. Short story in *Doce cuentos peregrinos*.
- **1992:** *El rastro de tu sangre en la nieve*. Short story in *Doce cuentos peregrinos*.

### Reportage, chronicle, testimony, and memoir

- **1955; book 1970:** *Relato de un náufrago*. Fourteen-part newspaper reportage reconstructed from interviews with sailor Luis Alejandro Velasco, later made into a book. It is authored reportage but also mediated testimony, so exclusive possession of its story and voice cannot be assigned without qualification. ([Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador-old))
- **1957; book 1978/1980:** *De viaje por los países socialistas: 90 días en la Cortina de Hierro*. Travel-reportage sequence arising from García Márquez’s journey through Eastern Europe; book dates and titles vary by edition. ([CVC chronology](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ACTCULT/garcia_marquez/cronologia/1927_67.htm), [Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm))
- **1975:** *Chile, el golpe y los gringos*. Political reportage/essay concerning the overthrow of Salvador Allende.
- **1977:** *Operación Carlota*. Long political reportage on Cuba’s military intervention in Angola; it first appeared journalistically and was subsequently issued as a small book.
- **1982:** *Viva Sandino*. Collection or dossier of García Márquez’s writing on the Sandinista revolution rather than a newly composed continuous book. ([Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm))
- **1986:** *La aventura de Miguel Littín clandestino en Chile*. Book-length testimonial reportage made from extensive interviews with filmmaker Miguel Littín; its first-person narration deliberately complicates the boundary between Littín’s testimony and García Márquez’s authored reconstruction. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/pdf/01084.pdf))
- **1996:** *Noticia de un secuestro*. Documentary narrative reconstructed from interviews and records concerning interconnected kidnappings by the Medellín cartel; neither conventional novel nor unshaped testimony. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/pdf/01084.pdf))
- **2002:** *Vivir para contarla*. Memoir covering García Márquez’s life through the 1950s; announced as the first part of a larger autobiographical project that was never completed. ([Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/comunidad/perfil/gabriel-garcia-marquez), [Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm))

### Journalism as an obra

- **1948–2014:** *Periodical journalism*. A dispersed body of hundreds of signed and pseudonymous columns, news reports, film reviews, chronicles, interviews, political essays, and occasional texts produced for outlets including *El Universal*, *El Heraldo*, *El Espectador*, *Momento*, Prensa Latina, *Alternativa*, and international newspapers. This corpus resists treatment as a finite succession of “books”: the Biblioteca Nacional describes it as articles, prologues, letters, and contributions later redistributed among many overlapping compilations. ([Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/), [Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador-old))
- **1981:** *Textos costeños: obra periodística 1, 1948–1952*. Chronological gathering of Caribbean journalism; a retrospective editorial construction of an earlier dispersed obra, not a wholly new text.
- **1982:** *Entre cachacos: obra periodística 2, 1954–1955*. Gathering of Bogotá reportage, columns, and film criticism.
- **1983:** *De Europa y América: obra periodística 3, 1955–1960*. Gathering of European correspondence and subsequent journalism.
- **1999:** *Por la libre: obra periodística 4, 1974–1995*. Retrospective gathering of political and general journalism; its publication date is not the date of the texts it contains. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://www.cervantes.es/bibliotecas_documentacion_espanol/creadores/garcia_marquez_gabriel_cronologia.htm))
- **1991:** *Notas de prensa, 1961–1984: obra periodística 5*. Gathering of shorter journalistic texts; some editions label the volume simply *Notas de prensa*. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/bibliografia.htm), [Editora Record](https://www.record.com.br/products/cronicas-vol-5-obra-jornalistica))
- **1973:** *Cuando era feliz e indocumentado*. Early anthology of journalism assembled from previously published texts; it is principally an editorial manifestation of the journalistic corpus.
- **1976:** *Crónicas y reportajes*. Selection of previously published journalism, not an independent continuous composition.
- **1978:** *Periodismo militante*. Political-journalism selection.
- **1990:** *Primeros reportajes*. Retrospective selection from the early journalistic corpus.
- **1990:** *Escritos sobre arte y literatura, 1948–1984*. Thematic compilation of articles and criticism.
- **2000:** *El amante inconcluso y otros textos de prensa*. Late editorial selection of previously published press writing. ([Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm))
- **2012/2013:** *Gabo periodista*. Posthumously relevant anthology and critical dossier assembled by the Fundación; García Márquez made the journalism reproduced in it, but not the composite volume as a sole-authored obra. ([Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/sites/default/files/informegabo.pdf))

### Essays, conversations, speeches, and manifesto-like texts

- **1968:** *La novela en América Latina: diálogo*. Published conversation with Mario Vargas Llosa. It is a jointly produced oral-critical event subsequently stabilized as a book, rather than a conventional co-written treatise. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/bibliografia.htm))
- **1982:** *El olor de la guayaba: conversaciones con Plinio Apuleyo Mendoza*. Book-length interview or dialogue; Mendoza’s questions, arrangement, and interlocutory role make sole authorship an inadequate description.
- **1982:** *La soledad de América Latina*. Nobel lecture delivered on 8 December 1982. It functions partly as cultural and political manifesto: it asks Europe to alter its understanding of Latin American history rather than merely describing that history. ([Nobel Prize](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/literature/1982/marquez/lecture/?print=1))
- **1982:** *Brindis por la poesía*. Nobel banquet speech; a ceremonial oral text distinct from the Nobel lecture.
- **1986:** *El cataclismo de Damocles*. Anti-nuclear address delivered at Ixtapa; its exhortative purpose gives it the force of a manifesto.
- **1994:** *Por un país al alcance de los niños*. Introductory proclamation for Colombia’s Mission of Science, Education and Development. It is simultaneously essay, civic diagnosis, and programmatic manifesto.
- **1997:** *Botella al mar para el dios de las palabras*. Speech advocating reflection on Spanish usage and spelling; it is a provocative intervention, not a formal linguistic protocol.
- **2010:** *Yo no vengo a decir un discurso*. Author-approved collection of speeches delivered between 1944 and 2007; the individual speeches are performances made at different moments, while the book is their late arrangement.

### Theatre

- **1987–1988; published 1994:** *Diatriba de amor contra un hombre sentado*. Monologue for the stage and García Márquez’s only completed play; it was first staged before its book publication. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))

### Film and television works

- **1954:** *La langosta azul*. Experimental short film made collaboratively with Álvaro Cepeda Samudio, Enrique Grau, and Luis Vicens; credits vary among accounts, making any claim of sole direction or authorship misleading. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1964:** *El gallo de oro*. Screen adaptation of Juan Rulfo’s story, developed with Carlos Fuentes and director Roberto Gavaldón; a collaborative screenplay rather than an original García Márquez narrative. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1965:** *Tiempo de morir*. Original screenplay, developed in collaboration with Carlos Fuentes and filmed by Arturo Ripstein; later remade by Jorge Alí Triana. ([Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm), [Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1965:** *Lola de mi vida*. Original screen work credited by the Fundación Gabo among García Márquez’s scripts; documentation and circulation are substantially thinner than for his principal films. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1965:** *En este pueblo no hay ladrones*. Screen adaptation of his story for Alberto Isaac’s film.
- **1966:** *H.O.*. García Márquez’s segment of the anthology film *Juego peligroso*; filmographies sometimes cite the whole film rather than distinguishing his contributed episode. ([CVC](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ACTCULT/garcia_marquez/obra/cine/cinematografo.htm))
- **1968:** *Patsy, mi amor*. Screen story or argument filmed by Manuel Michel; García Márquez supplied the narrative basis, while the exact screenplay credit is presented inconsistently across filmographies. ([CVC](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ACTCULT/garcia_marquez/obra/cine/cinematografo.htm))
- **1960s, unproduced:** *Es tan fácil que hasta los hombres pueden*. Screenplay offered to Luis Buñuel but never filmed; as an unrealized project, the intention and surviving script—not a film—constitute the work. ([El País](https://elpais.com/eps/2023-12-17/garcia-marquez-y-otros-clasicos-del-boom-literario-latinoamericano-se-convierten-en-series.html))
- **1974:** *Presagio*. Original screenplay made with director Luis Alcoriza. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1978/1979:** *El año de la peste*. Screen adaptation of Daniel Defoe’s plague narrative, filmed by Felipe Cazals; sources differ between 1978 as production/script date and 1979 as release date. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [CVC](https://cvc.cervantes.es/ACTCULT/garcia_marquez/obra/cine/cinematografo.htm))
- **1979:** *María de mi corazón*. Original screenplay filmed by Jaime Humberto Hermosillo. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1979:** *La viuda de Montiel*. Screenplay transforming García Márquez’s earlier story, filmed by Miguel Littín. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1982/1984:** *El secuestro*. Cinematic narrative or screenplay about the Sandinista seizure of a Managua political gathering; edition dates vary, and the published script should not be confused with the later nonfiction book *Noticia de un secuestro*. ([Instituto Cervantes](https://cvc.cervantes.es/actcult/garcia_marquez/bibliografia/secuestro.htm), [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books?id=mIddAAAAMAAJ))
- **1980–1983:** *Eréndira*. Screenplay derived from García Márquez’s long story and filmed by Ruy Guerra; it also incorporates material from *Muerte constante más allá del amor*. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [Eréndira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Er%C3%A9ndira_%28pel%C3%ADcula%29))
- **1988–1989:** *Amores difíciles*. Six-film television cycle founded on García Márquez screenplays. The cycle is a Gesamtkunstwerk-like collaborative object—six directors, several countries, literary and cinematic forms—rather than a single film authored by one maker. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [EICTV](https://media.eictv.org/enfoco_digital/ENFOCO47.pdf))
- **1988:** *Fábula de la bella palomera*. Screenplay/telefilm in the *Amores difíciles* cycle.
- **1988:** *Milagro en Roma*. Screenplay/telefilm in the *Amores difíciles* cycle.
- **1988:** *Cartas del parque*. Screenplay/telefilm in the *Amores difíciles* cycle.
- **1988:** *Un domingo feliz*. Screenplay/telefilm in the *Amores difíciles* cycle.
- **1988:** *El verano feliz de la señora Forbes*. Screenplay/telefilm in the *Amores difíciles* cycle, related to the story later collected in *Doce cuentos peregrinos*.
- **1988:** *Yo soy el que tú buscas*. Screenplay/telefilm in the *Amores difíciles* cycle.
- **1991:** *María*. Television adaptation of Jorge Isaacs’s novel, made with Lisandro Duque Naranjo and Manuel Arias; it is collaborative adaptation, not an original García Márquez story. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1992:** *Me alquilo para soñar*. Six-part television series directed by Ruy Guerra and developed from García Márquez’s story; it is distinct from both that story and the later workshop transcript.
- **1996:** *Edipo alcalde*. Screenplay relocating Sophocles’ *Oedipus Rex* to contemporary Colombia; filmed by Jorge Alí Triana. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [Edipo alcalde](https://www.redalyc.org/pdf/4096/409634371011.pdf))
- **Date uncertain; unproduced:** *Für Elise*. Unproduced film project with a story by García Márquez and screenplay work by García Márquez, Eliseo Alberto Diego, and Tomás Gutiérrez Alea; the archive preserves scripts, but no completed film fixes a definitive version. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingaid.cfm?eadid=01083))

### Workshop works and collective story-making

- **1987 onward:** *Cómo se cuenta un cuento*. Recurring screenwriting workshop at the Escuela Internacional de Cine y Televisión. The workshop itself—not merely its printed transcript—is a collaborative creative protocol in which participants proposed, dismantled, and rebuilt stories under García Márquez’s direction. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [Workshop study](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~borao/2Profesores/Garcia%20Marquez.pdf))
- **1995:** *Cómo se cuenta un cuento*. Edited transcript of seven workshop sessions. It records collective creation and cannot accurately be treated as a conventionally authored manual. ([Revista de Libros](https://www.revistadelibros.com/garcia-marquez-como-se-cuenta-un-cuento/))
- **1995:** *Me alquilo para soñar: taller de guion*. Edited workshop transcript, distinct from the 1992 story and television production with the same title.
- **1998:** *La bendita manía de contar*. Edited record of further screenplay workshops; the printed book manifests a collective oral process shaped by later transcription and editing. ([Workshop study](https://homepage.ntu.edu.tw/~borao/2Profesores/Garcia%20Marquez.pdf))

### Unfinished and transformed projects

- **c. 1948–1954:** *La casa*. Unfinished novel project centered on the family house and the imagined settlement that became Macondo. It resists being called a lost early version of *Cien años de soledad*: material migrated into *La hojarasca*, *El coronel no tiene quien le escriba*, *La mala hora*, *Los funerales de la Mamá Grande*, and eventually the later novel. ([El País](https://elpais.com/babelia/2025-01-15/cien-anos-de-soledad-de-principio-a-fin.html))
- **1980s–2002:** *Memoirs project*. Planned multi-volume autobiography of which only *Vivir para contarla* was completed. The unrealized later volumes remain a project—an intention with preparatory work—not missing books to which titles or dates can responsibly be assigned. ([Gaboteca](https://www.bibliotecanacional.gov.co/es-co/colecciones/biblioteca-digital/gaboteca/obras-de-ggm))
- **Various dates:** *Unproduced and workshop screenplays*. The Harry Ransom Center preserves unidentified, variant, collaborative, and unrealized scripts, including numerous works generated in García Márquez’s workshops. Because attribution and textual boundaries are sometimes indeterminate, the archive itself does not justify inventing stable titles or treating every file as a completed sole-authored work. ([Harry Ransom Center](https://norman.hrc.utexas.edu/fasearch/findingaid.cfm?eadid=01083))

### Editorial, institutional, and pedagogical projects

- **1974–1980:** *Alternativa*. Colombian opposition magazine co-founded by García Márquez with Enrique Santos Calderón, Orlando Fals Borda, Antonio Caballero, and others. It is a collective political-journalistic project and manifestation, not a text authored by García Márquez alone. ([Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador-old))
- **1985:** *Fundación del Nuevo Cine Latinoamericano*. Cultural institution co-founded and thereafter presided over by García Márquez to integrate and strengthen Latin American cinema; an institutional obra made collectively. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador), [FNCL–FNPI history](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/noticias/articulo/fnpi-y-fncl-las-fundaciones-de-gabo-celebran-convenio-de-cooperacion))
- **1986:** *Escuela Internacional de Cine y Televisión de San Antonio de los Baños*. Educational institution created through the Fundación del Nuevo Cine Latinoamericano and collaborators, with García Márquez as a principal founder and teacher. It is not his sole creation, but its workshop model became one of his durable pedagogical works. ([Fundación Gabo](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador))
- **1994–1995:** *Fundación para un Nuevo Periodismo Iberoamericano*. Institution conceived and co-founded by García Márquez around small, practice-based workshops for journalists; now Fundación Gabo. It is both a continuing project and a protocol for transmitting journalistic craft. ([Fundación Gabo](https://fundaciongabo.org/es/institucion/fundador-old), [Foundation history](https://www.fundaciongabo.org/es/la-fundacion-de-gabriel-garcia-marquez-en-cartagena-cumple-20-anos))
