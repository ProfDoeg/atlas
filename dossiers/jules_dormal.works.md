## Works by Jules Dormal Godet

### Landscape and urban projects

- **1868–1869:** *Meat-extract works at Puerto Ruiz*. An unrealized industrial project near Gualeguay that brought Dormal to Argentina; it survives principally as an intention rather than a documented building, and the extent of his architectural authorship is unclear. [Biographical source](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)
- **1874–1875:** *Parque Tres de Febrero*. A public landscape and urban-infrastructure obra begun by Ernesto Oldendorff, Fernando Mauduit and Jordan Wysocki and completed by Dormal; calling it Dormal’s design alone obscures that collective authorship. [Buenos Aires landscape study](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/areas/cultura/paisaje/02_capitulo2.pdf)
- **c. 1875:** *Portones de Palermo*. Monumental entrance gates for Parque Tres de Febrero, subsequently demolished: an architectural threshold rather than a self-contained building. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)
- **c. 1875:** *Original layout and planting of Avenida Sarmiento*. An urban-landscape project associated with the park, initially planted with palms; it is a spatial trace rather than a discrete building. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)
- *Original layout of Parque Chas*. This is frequently assigned to Jules Dormal, including in Buenos Aires heritage legislation, but the city’s own historical chronology identifies the author of the 1924 layout as **Julio Dormal hijo**. It should therefore resist inclusion in Jules Dormal’s œuvre unless new primary evidence resolves the attribution. [Legislative attribution](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/06/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-caba_14.html); [corrective city chronology](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/sites/default/files/media/document/2020/10/29/3a258c48f1f79cd0c9ddbd89d9c57473f1f73906.pdf)

### Public and institutional architecture

- **Late 1870s:** *Grandstands of the Hipódromo Argentino de Palermo*. Spectator infrastructure commissioned by the Jockey Club; the consulted accounts do not establish a firmer design or construction date. [Buenos Aires City](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/qr/florida-460)
- **1882–1895:** *Casa de Gobierno de la Provincia de Buenos Aires*. Dormal designed the façades and interiors, while the pre-existing plan and functional distribution belonged to the provincial Department of Engineers; it is consequently a collaborative obra, not an autonomous Dormal building. [National heritage record](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/414123); [official historical decree](https://www.revistarap.com/app/assets/data/dgratuitos/1adm0154009755000/documentos-1adm0154009755000-dgratuitos-1.html)
- **1895:** *Casa del Gobernador, La Plata*. An official residence linked to the provincial government complex, radically remodeled by Alejandro Bustillo in 1939; Dormal’s building survives only through that transformed manifestation. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2011/09/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-prov_05.html)
- **1904–1908:** *Completion and interiors of the Teatro Colón*. Dormal inherited the unfinished theater from Francesco Tamburini and Vittorio Meano, directed its completion and imposed the French Second-Empire character of the entrance hall, Foyer de los Bustos, Salón Dorado and auditorium ornament. This is a continuation and substantial transformation of other architects’ project, not sole authorship. [National heritage record](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/441069); [Buenos Aires heritage inventory](https://documentosboletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/publico/20110318ax.pdf)
- **1904–1906 and after:** *Completion of the Palacio del Congreso Nacional*. Dormal assumed responsibility after Víctor Meano’s death and brought the building to its 1906 inauguration, although work on the larger complex continued until 1946; the project itself remained Meano’s. [National heritage record](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/node/435487); [Buenos Aires architectural guide](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/sites/default/files/media/document/2019/05/27/7bd8df73d09b3d6a4700a89ef48c257b18fda71b.pdf)
- **1910:** *Estación Mar del Plata Sud*. A railway terminal for the Ferrocarril del Sud, later used as Mar del Plata’s bus terminal; this is an infrastructural building as well as an urban gateway. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal); [Buenos Aires heritage programme](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/cultura/casco-historico/premio-gubbio-2021-intervenciones-sobre-la-ciudad-y-los-centros-historicos)
- **1912:** *Banco de la Nación Argentina, Villa Urquiza branch*. An academicist bank building at Avenida Triunvirato 4802, combining French classicism with Art Nouveau detail; its attribution and date are recorded in the city’s protection proceedings. [Official gazette](https://documentosboletinoficial.buenosaires.gob.ar/publico/20100506.pdf); [catalogue transcription](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/06/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-caba_14.html)
- *Pavilions for the Buenos Aires Zoological Garden*. A dispersed group of zoo structures rather than a single readily bounded work; the consulted standard biography names Dormal’s participation but does not identify or date the individual pavilions, so their completeness cannot be claimed. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)

### Theaters and places of sociability

- **1889:** *Second Teatro Ópera*. A comprehensive remodeling of Émile Landois’s 1872 theater, including a new façade brought forward to the street line; the resulting manifestation was demolished in 1935. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2011/04/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-caba_22.html); [theater history](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_%C3%93pera)
- **c. 1910s:** *Edificio Peña and Confitería Richmond*. A rental and commercial building at Florida 468 whose ground floor housed the Richmond café; it is a mixed-use architectural obra, not the café business or its later interiors alone. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/07/)
- **c. 1910s:** *Hotel León*. A rental building and hotel at Avenida Callao 758–762; the architectural catalogue records the attribution but supplies no reliable construction date. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/07/)

### Houses, palaces and rental buildings

- **1900–1904/1908:** *Palacio Ortiz Basualdo–Peña*. A vast domestic complex at Arenales, Maipú and Basavilbaso containing two connected residences; sources disagree over whether completion should be dated 1904 or 1908 and whether the municipal façade prize belongs to 1904 or 1905. It was demolished in 1969. [Buenos Aires architectural guide](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/sites/default/files/media/document/2015/02/05/17c0fd41d6c46f78f40c5a558bcc34bdea586d29.pdf); [architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/06/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-caba.html)
- **1902:** *Residencia Julio Peña*. An academicist family residence at Florida 460, now occupied by the Sociedad Rural Argentina; a later additional storey altered Dormal’s façade. [Buenos Aires City](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/qr/florida-460); [architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2011/04/)
- *Palacio Peña on Maipú*. A second, now-demolished Peña residence near the Ortiz Basualdo complex; it must not be conflated with either the surviving Residencia Julio Peña at Florida 460 or the Peña dwelling incorporated into the Ortiz Basualdo complex. [Buenos Aires architectural guide](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/sites/default/files/media/document/2015/02/05/17c0fd41d6c46f78f40c5a558bcc34bdea586d29.pdf)
- *Residence for Carmen Lezica de Peña*. A demolished aristocratic house known from retrospective work lists; the consulted corpus does not establish its address or date firmly enough to supply either. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)
- *Palace for Manuel Anselmo Ocampo*. A demolished private residence known from retrospective attribution; its date and precise identification remain unresolved in the consulted corpus. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)
- *Dormal family house, San Martín 1137*. A four-storey eclectic petit hôtel designed as the architect’s own residence, combining dwelling, professional self-representation and decorative architectural manifesto; no secure construction date appears in the consulted catalogue. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/07/)
- **1912:** *Collective-housing building, Avenida Belgrano 456*. A rental apartment building distinguished by the large cornucopia on its façade. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal); [Buenos Aires legislative work list](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/06/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-caba_14.html)
- **c. 1910s:** *Rental and commercial building, Avenida Santa Fe 801–819 / Esmeralda 1120*. A mixed-use corner building bearing Dormal’s visible signature; the catalogue does not provide a secure construction date. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2011/04/)
- **c. 1910s:** *Rental and commercial building, Tucumán 781–799 / Esmeralda 605*. A mixed-use corner building; it is individually catalogued, although no reliable date accompanies the attribution. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2011/04/ingeniero-arquitecto-jules-dormal-caba_6541.html)
- *House at Brown 1180, Mar del Plata*. A single-family residence for Dr. Roberto J. Dormal, described by its catalogue as possibly Dormal’s final work; “possibly” is part of its status, not a settled chronology. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/06/)
- **1919–1924:** *Intervention in Palacio Pereda*. Louis Martin produced the original project; Dormal subsequently modified it and directed or continued the work until his death. Since the palace was not completed until 1936, the finished building cannot be treated as his unaided creation. [Architectural catalogue entry](https://arquitectos-franceses-argentina.blogspot.com/2010/05/arquitecto-louis-martin-proyecto.html); [Buenos Aires City](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/noticias/experiencia-ba-recorrida-con-vecinos-por-la-zona-de-embajadas)
- **1919–1924:** *Neo-Moorish salon in Palacio Pereda*. An interior environment within the larger collaborative palace, begun after Martin’s departure and associated with Dormal’s intervention; its later completion belongs to the unfinished-building history rather than securely to Dormal alone. [Rodrigo Gutiérrez Viñuales, “Arquitectura morisca en Sudamérica”](https://www.ugr.es/~rgutierr/PDF1/174.pdf)

### Monuments and funerary architecture

- **1880:** *Mausoleum of General José de San Martín*. A funerary architectural setting in Buenos Aires Metropolitan Cathedral, made to house the general’s remains; it is simultaneously monument, tomb and interior intervention. [Buenos Aires City](https://buenosaires.gob.ar/gcaba_historico/qr/florida-460)
- *Peña–Lezica family mausoleum*. A funerary monument in Recoleta Cemetery attributed to Dormal; the available source does not establish a secure date and its authorship is less firmly documented than that of the San Martín mausoleum. [Recoleta Cemetery documentation](https://recoletacemetery.com/?p=100)

### Waterfront and recreational structures

- **Late nineteenth century:** *Old wooden rambla, Mar del Plata*. A seaside promenade and commercial-recreational structure, now lost; the consulted biographies attribute it to Dormal but do not agree on a precise date. [Biographical work list](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Dormal)
- **c. 1914:** *Neo-Moorish billiard room in the Ortiz Basualdo palace*. A themed interior rather than a separate building; because the palace itself is variously dated and was demolished, this room survives chiefly through documentation. [Rodrigo Gutiérrez Viñuales, “Arquitectura morisca en Sudamérica”](https://www.ugr.es/~rgutierr/PDF1/174.pdf)

### Exhibition architecture and unrealized projects

- *Project for the Palacio de Aguas Corrientes*. An architectural project associated with Dormal in the scholarly dictionary tradition; the surviving waterworks palace was executed through a different institutional and professional history, so this proposal must not be silently equated with the built building. [Scholarly summary citing Liernur and Aliata](https://ffyl.uncuyo.edu.ar/edifyl/catalogo/upload/1764867932_8d3144e1a56c6db33e90.pdf)
- **1915:** *Architectural organization of the Argentine presentation at the Panama–Pacific International Exposition*. Dormal was charged with architectural aspects of the exhibition alongside Cupertino del Campo’s pictorial work. This is best understood as exhibition-making—a temporary, collective Gesamtkunstwerk of building, display and national representation—rather than secure sole authorship of the pavilion architecture. [CONICET study](https://ri.conicet.gov.ar/bitstream/handle/11336/199620/CONICET_Digital_Nro.24d99397-d5bd-476a-99e8-fcebdfdef1aa_C.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=5)
