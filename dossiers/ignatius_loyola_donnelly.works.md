## Works by Ignatius Loyola Donnelly

### Poems and early literary works

- **1849:** *Historical essay on the ruins of Fort Cripsholm*. An antiquarian essay prompted by Donnelly’s investigation of what he believed to be an early Swedish fortification in Pennsylvania; the archival guide does not establish whether it was published or give a firmer title. ([White, pp. 25–26](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1850:** *The Mourner’s Vision: A Poem*. A self-published book-length poem and Donnelly’s first separately issued literary work. ([White, p. 25](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **circa 1850–1851:** *Poems for Abraham Sink and John T. Creble and Creble’s sister*. A small group of manuscript poems—memorial, occasional, and personal—preserved in Donnelly’s papers; they resist treatment as a single obra because the archival description does not supply all their titles or dates. ([White, p. 25](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **Undated, probably early:** *The Lady Emilie: A Tale of the Norman Conquest*. A manuscript short story preserved among otherwise undated literary materials; no publication is recorded. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **Undated:** *The Human Face*. A draft article, not a demonstrably completed or published work. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **Undated:** *Occasional poems*. A dispersed body of later poems composed for festive occasions and apparently not submitted for publication. This is an archival remainder rather than a fixed, author-arranged work: the finding aid does not enumerate or consistently title its components. ([White, p. 25](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))

### Promotional and political texts

- **1856:** *Nininger City: A Pamphlet*. A promotional pamphlet intended to attract settlers and investment to the projected Minnesota town; it is simultaneously advertising, prospectus, and town-making instrument. ([U.S. House of Representatives bibliography](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/12349))
- **1866:** *Freedman’s Bureau: Speech of Hon. Ignatius Donnelly of Minnesota, Delivered in the House of Representatives, February 1, 1866*. A congressional speech issued as a pamphlet: an oratorical and legislative intervention rather than a conventional book. ([U.S. House of Representatives bibliography](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/12349))
- **1873:** *Facts for the Granges*. A reform pamphlet addressed to the agrarian Grange movement; the Minnesota Historical Society’s archival guide identifies it among Donnelly’s literature of social protest. ([White, p. 28](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1873:** *An imaginary appearance of Sioux Indians in Washington*. An apparently unpublished article preserved in the papers. Its supplied description may not be an authorial title, and the archive calls the piece “curious” without establishing that Donnelly completed it for publication. ([White, p. 23](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1879:** *Contested Election of Donnelly vs. Washburn*. A printed election-contest argument or documentary brief arising from Donnelly’s disputed congressional election, rather than an autonomous literary composition. ([U.S. House of Representatives bibliography](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/12349))
- **1892:** *Preamble to the Omaha Platform*. A manifesto in the strict sense: Donnelly drafted the accusatory preamble through which the People’s Party declared both a political crisis and a program of collective action; the convention adopted it as part of a collaborative party platform, so the whole platform is not solely his work. ([American Studies Journal](https://journals.ku.edu/amsj/article/download/2377/2336))
- **1895:** *The American People’s Money*. An informal economic-political tract explaining monetary reform and People’s Party principles. A revised electoral manifestation followed in 1896, so the two titles should not automatically be counted as wholly unrelated obras. ([White, p. 29](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf); [U.S. House of Representatives bibliography](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/12349))
- **1896:** *The Bryan Campaign for the American People’s Money*. A campaign-oriented revision or re-presentation of Donnelly’s monetary tract for William Jennings Bryan’s presidential campaign, published as number 141 of the *Pastime Series*. ([Google Books bibliographic record](https://books.google.com/books?id=AAxLAAAAYAAJ))
- **1855–1900:** *Speeches and lectures*. A large performance corpus on politics, agriculture, Reconstruction, immigration, education, science, literature, spiritualism, humor, and patriotic occasions. Manuscripts, notes, clippings, and reports survive, but Donnelly repeated and altered speeches and sometimes placed two speeches on opposite sides of one sheet; consequently this corpus resists reliable counting as a list of discrete works. ([White, p. 24](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf); [Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))

### Speculative histories and cipher studies

- **1859:** *The Sonnets of Shakespeare*. A published essay on Shakespeare’s sonnets, predating Donnelly’s full conversion to the Baconian authorship theory. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1873:** *The Authorship of Shakespeare*. A lecture advancing the Baconian authorship argument, apparently first delivered at Red Wing, Minnesota, in February 1873; surviving notes make it partly a performance and partly a manuscript text. ([White, pp. 26–27](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **Early 1870s:** *The Man Shakespeare*. An essay-lecture surviving in notes. Because Donnelly reused lectures, it is not certain that the archival text represents a single stable version. ([White, p. 26](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf); [Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1882:** *Atlantis: The Antediluvian World*. A speculative synthesis of mythology, language, archaeology, religion, and natural history arguing that Atlantis was a real source civilization destroyed by catastrophe. Written in 1881 and published early in 1882, it belongs to nineteenth-century popular science rather than modern archaeological scholarship. ([White, p. 26](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1883:** *Ragnarok: The Age of Fire and Gravel*. A work of speculative natural history proposing that cometary debris, rather than glaciation, explained major terrestrial deposits and catastrophe; the archival guide says it was written in 1882, while the standard first-edition bibliography dates publication to 1883. ([White, p. 26](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf); [U.S. House of Representatives bibliography](https://history.house.gov/People/Detail/12349))
- **1887:** *The Shakespeare Myth*. A short Baconian polemic associated with Donnelly’s developing cipher project. It is bibliographically less secure than his major books: later lists name it, while the principal archival guide emphasizes the lectures, articles, and *Great Cryptogram* rather than describing a distinct surviving manuscript. ([Minnesota Historical Society biographical bibliography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/donnelly-ignatius-1831-1901))
- **1888:** *The Great Cryptogram: Francis Bacon’s Cipher in the So-Called Shakespeare Plays*. A two-volume cipher treatise asserting that numerical structures in the Shakespeare folio disclose Francis Bacon’s authorship. It is both a book and the published manifestation of years of calculations, drafts, lectures, and demonstrations. ([White, pp. 27–28](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1898:** *Imaginary Conversations with Francis Bacon and Other Authors*. A manuscript sequence of invented conversations connected with Donnelly’s authorship researches; the finding aid records it but does not establish publication or completeness. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1898?:** *Shakespeare’s Tomb-Stone*. A manuscript or article draft provisionally dated March 1898 by the archive; both date and textual status remain uncertain. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1899:** *The Cipher in the Plays, and on the Tombstone*. A self-published continuation of Donnelly’s Baconian cipher project, connecting the alleged cipher with Shakespeare’s monument and Rosicrucianism. The surviving manuscript is incomplete, although a printed book appeared. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html); [White, p. 28](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1899:** *The Cipher in Ben Jonson’s Works*. An unpublished manuscript originally designed as chapters 21–33 of *The Cipher in the Plays, and on the Tombstone*. Its separation is editorial and material rather than necessarily Donnelly’s final intention, making it an unfinished project more securely than an independent book. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1880s–1899:** *Cipher studies*. A bulky chronological body of arithmetic computations, trials, corrections, and applications of number formulas to passages in the Shakespeare folio. These working papers are manifestations of a research practice, not a titled or finished work, and many pages cannot meaningfully be classified separately. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))

### Novels and social fictions

- **1890:** *Caesar’s Column: A Story of the Twentieth Century*. A dystopian epistolary novel of oligarchy, technological violence, and revolutionary catastrophe, first published under the pseudonym “Edmund Boisgilbert, M.D.”; Donnelly’s authorship was soon publicly acknowledged. ([White, pp. 28–29](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1891:** *Doctor Huguet: A Novel*. A social-protest and body-exchange novel, also issued as by Edmund Boisgilbert, which attempts to make white readers confront anti-Black racism in the post–Civil War South. Its method relies on racial language and assumptions belonging to its period. ([White, pp. 28–29](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1892:** *The Golden Bottle; or, The Story of Ephraim Benezet of Kansas*. A Populist economic fantasy in novel form, using miraculous abundance to dramatize currency reform and distributive politics. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html); [White, p. 29](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **Undated:** *The Devil’s Needle*. An unfinished novel preserved in manuscript. It is a project rather than a completed obra; neither a secure date nor evidence of publication is supplied by the archive. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))

### Memoir, diary, genealogy, and memorial

- **1857–1900:** *Diaries and memorandum books*. A long-running manuscript practice rather than one stable text: the volumes combine daily entries with accounts, farm records, jokes, research notes, election returns, timetables, maps, appointments, and pasted matter. Missing pages and mixed contents make the corpus resist treatment as a completed diary-work. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1871:** *Recollections of Donnelly’s first journey to Minnesota*. A retrospective travel narrative compiled from notes of the journey and preserved in a diary volume; the archival guide does not give it a fixed authorial title or record separate publication. ([White, p. 29](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1878–1900:** *My Journal*. Donnelly’s manuscript selection and rearrangement of material from his diaries and memorandum books, with a preface dated July 29, 1891 and four discontinuous parts. It is an autobiographical compilation, incomplete as a continuous record and distinct from the underlying diaries. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1892:** *Donnelliana: An Appendix to “Caesar’s Column”: Excerpts from the Wit, Wisdom, Poetry and Eloquence of Ignatius Donnelly, Selected and Collated with a Biography*. A campaign anthology and biography published under Everett W. Fish’s name. Authorship is disputed: the surviving incomplete manuscript is substantially in the hands of Donnelly and John A. Giltinan, leading the archival editors to identify them as probable compilers and writers; the biographical portion also appeared separately. ([White, pp. 29–30](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf); [Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1895:** *In Memoriam, Mrs. Katharine Donnelly*. A privately charged memorial book for Donnelly’s first wife, accompanied in the archive by preparatory notes on her and her ancestors. It lies between family record, biography, and mourning object. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1896:** *A Genealogy and History of the Donnelly Family, with a Survey of the History of Ireland*. An unpublished family-and-national history compiled from research accumulated over more than thirty years. The archive preserves a relatively complete loaned copy plus fragments and possible withdrawn or intended pages, so its final boundaries remain uncertain. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **Undated:** *Ireland and the Irish People*. A collective archival designation for notes, clippings, and manuscripts associated with Donnelly’s Irish and genealogical research, not demonstrably a finished book bearing that exact title. It resists classification as a single work because the materials overlap the family genealogy and include pages of uncertain intended placement. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **Undated:** *The Ancient Races of America*. An incomplete manuscript that may have been part of a speech. Even its medium is unresolved: it survives as text but may represent an oratorical episode rather than an intended publication. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))

### Newspapers and editorial projects

- **1856–1858:** *The Emigrant Aid Journal of Minnesota*. A settlement-promotion newspaper created by Donnelly and Philip Rohr for the Nininger enterprise, with A. W. MacDonald identified as editor; Donnelly’s precise authorship of individual unsigned matter cannot be assumed. It began in Philadelphia on December 1, 1856, and its last known issue is dated May 5, 1858. ([National Library of Australia record](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/2475048); [White, p. 28](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
- **1874–1878:** *The Anti-Monopolist*. A weekly reform newspaper founded and published by Donnelly to intervene in Grange and Anti-Monopoly politics. It is a serial collective obra rather than a single-authored text; the first issue appeared July 16, 1874, and the last on December 12, 1878. ([Minnesota Historical Society Newspaper Hub](https://www.mnhs.org/newspapers/hub/the-anti-monopolist))
- **1893–1901:** *The Representative*. A Farmers’ Alliance and People’s Party weekly founded and published by Donnelly on April 19, 1893. He initially limited his personal contribution to one or two columns a week and did not accept authorship of the rest, then became editor-in-chief in February 1895; issues published after his death plainly belong to the continuing newspaper, not to Donnelly’s personal oeuvre. ([Minnesota Historical Society Newspaper Hub](https://www.mnhs.org/newspapers/hub/the-representative))

### Civic and spatial project

- **1856–late 1850s:** *Nininger City*. A speculative town project undertaken with John Nininger and other partners: Donnelly promoted it, helped plat and build it, published its newspaper, and lived there, but neither sole authorship nor completion can be claimed. The Panic of 1857 defeated the intended settlement, while the residual place survived as a diminished community and later ghost town; it is therefore a project and collective obra, not merely the subject of Donnelly’s pamphlet. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html); [Minnesota Historical Society biographical bibliography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/donnelly-ignatius-1831-1901))

### Dispersed and unindividuated writings

- **1847–1901:** *Articles, editorials, essays, letters, legal papers, legislative texts, and research notes*. Donnelly made a much larger textual field than the separately titled works above, but the principal scholarly archive describes rather than itemizes it: manuscripts are dispersed through chronological correspondence, 130 mixed volumes, pamphlet scrapbooks, and newspaper files, and some apparent writings may be by other hands. Treating every archival sheet as an independent “work” would invent boundaries the surviving record does not support. ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/00782.html))
- **1859–1899:** *The journals as edited and transcribed after Donnelly*. Theodore L. Nydahl’s edited transcription of the 1859–1884 diaries and his typescript of the 1885–1899 diaries are important manifestations of Donnelly’s journal corpus, but they are posthumous editorial objects rather than things made solely by Donnelly. ([White, p. 30](https://www2.mnhs.org/library/findaids/m0138.pdf))
