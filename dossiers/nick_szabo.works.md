## Works by Nick Szabo

### Projects and protocol designs

- **1994:** *[Smart Contracts](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/smart-contracts/).* A short programmatic text defining the smart contract as a computerized transaction protocol. It is simultaneously a manifesto for a field and a protocol-design agenda, not an implemented system.
- **1994:** *[Smart Liens](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/smart-liens/).* A speculative protocol for embedding secured-credit and repossession rules in digitally controlled property; it remained a design proposal.
- **1995:** *[Smart Contracts Glossary](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/smart-contracts-glossary/).* A controlled vocabulary for smart-contract design. It is reference infrastructure rather than a continuous essay.
- **1996:** *[Credit with Privity](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/credit-with-privity/).* A protocol sketch for securing credit while limiting disclosure and third-party power.
- **1996:** *[Smart Contracts: Building Blocks for Digital Markets](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/smart-contracts-building-blocks-for-digital-markets/).* An essay and design manifesto expanding the 1994 proposal into a theory of digitally specified promises, contractual performance, and embedded enforcement. Szabo identifies it as a partial rewrite of an article published in *Extropy* no. 16.
- **1997:** *[Delegation and Agreement Based Certification Policy](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/delegation-and-agreement-based-certification-policy/).* A protocol-analysis essay about delegation, agreement, and certification in SPKI, PolicyMaker, and PGP.
- **1997:** *[Distributing Authorities and Verifying Their Claims](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/distributing-authorities-and-verifying-their-claims/).* A security-design essay on replacing concentrated certification authority with distributed verification.
- **1997:** *[Formalizing and Securing Relationships on Public Networks](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/formalizing-securing-relationships/).* A peer-reviewed *First Monday* article presenting smart contracts as means of formalizing online relationships; it is the principal formally published statement of the project.
- **1997:** *[The Idea of Smart Contracts](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/the-idea-of-smart-contracts/).* A compact conceptual manifesto arguing that contractual clauses can be embedded in hardware and software.
- **1997–1999:** *[The God Protocols](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/the-god-protocols/).* An essay explaining secure multiparty computation through the fiction of an perfectly trustworthy intermediary. Szabo’s archived copy carries a 1997–1999 copyright range, while the collected-text index dates it 1999.
- **1997–1999:** *[Contracts with Bearer](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/contracts-with-bearer/).* A tutorial and protocol essay generalizing blind-signature digital cash into transferable digital bearer certificates. The archived text carries 1997 and 1999 dates; later indexes variously label it 1997 or 1999.
- **1998–2005:** *[Secure Property Titles with Owner Authority](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/secure-property-titles-with-owner-authority/).* A proposal for distributed, cryptographically secured title registries. Its archived revision history bears the dates 1998, 1999, 2002, and 2005, so a single publication year would conceal its accretive form.
- **1998–2001:** *[Coalition Design for Secure Protocols](https://archive.li/Tdrgp).* A protocol-design note about thresholds and coalitions of mutually suspicious participants; the surviving text bears both 1998 and 2001.
- **1998/2005–2008:** *[Bit Gold](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold/).* A project for decentralized digital scarcity using proof of work, timestamping, and distributed title records. Szabo said he worked on the idea in 1998, but the securely traceable public essay is dated 29 December 2005; it was never implemented as the proposed system, and retrospective claims that it was “published in 1998” are disputed.
- **2000:** *[Designing Trusted Services with Group Controls](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/designing-trusted-services-with-group-controls/).* A protocol-design essay on dividing sensitive powers among groups rather than entrusting them to one operator.
- **2001:** *[Proplets: Devices for Controlling Property](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/proplets-devices-for-controlling-property/).* A speculative hardware–software architecture for property that senses, negotiates, records evidence, and enforces digitally encoded deeds. It resists classification as a finished invention: Szabo describes an architecture, not a demonstrated device.
- **2002:** *[A Formal Language for Analyzing Contracts](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/contract-language/).* A proposed computer-readable formalism for drafting contracts and calculating the consequences of contractual events; it is part notation, part protocol, and part unfinished research program.
- **2004:** *[Scarce Objects](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/scarce-objects/).* A software-architecture proposal for finite, excludable computational objects and transferable use rights. The prose uses the present tense, but expressly discusses hypothetical software rather than documenting a completed implementation.
- **2005:** *[P2P Filename Integrity](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/p2p-filename-integrity/).* A protocol proposal for preserving meaningful, transferable names across peer-to-peer trust boundaries.
- **2008:** *[Bit Gold Markets](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold-markets/).* A supplementary design essay about valuation and exchange in a possible bit-gold economy; it elaborates the unimplemented project rather than reporting a functioning market.

### Security, computation, and cryptography texts

- **1996:** *[Introduction to Algorithmic Information Theory](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/introduction-to-algorithmic-information-theory/).* A concise tutorial on Kolmogorov complexity and its applications.
- **1996:** *[Negative Reputations](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/negative-reputations/).* A short security and institutional-design essay on the limitations and possible uses of adverse reputation systems.
- **1997:** *[Accounting Controls](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/accounting.html).* A protocol-oriented essay treating accounting controls as historically evolved security mechanisms. The text says it was derived from *Formalizing and Securing Relationships on Public Networks*, so it resists treatment as wholly independent.
- **1998:** *[Confidential Auditing](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/confidential-auditing/).* A technical essay on using cryptographic computation to verify claims without exposing the underlying confidential records.
- **1998:** *[Quorum Systems](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/quorum-systems/).* A concise tutorial on quorum structures for maintaining consistency and tolerating failures among distributed participants.
- **1998:** *[There Is No Universal Security Architecture](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/universal.html).* A security-design thesis arguing that architectures must follow the relationships they protect rather than one universal access-control model.
- **1999:** *[Intrapolynomial Cryptography](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/intrapolynomial-cryptography/).* A research note warning that adversaries with specialized hardware can defeat computational-cost schemes calibrated to ordinary machines.
- **1999:** *[The Abuse of Statistics in Cryptography](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/statistics.html).* A critical technical essay on treating statistical tests as adequate evidence of cryptographic security.
- **2001:** *[Absolute Compression and True Randomness](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/perfect_compression.html).* A concise theoretical note connecting absolute compression, Kolmogorov complexity, and claims of true randomness.
- **2001–2005:** *[Trusted Third Parties Are Security Holes](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/trusted-third-parties-are-security-holes/).* A security manifesto arguing that dependence on a third party is itself a vulnerability that must be costed and controlled. The surviving text bears revision dates 2001, 2004, and 2005, although the collected-text index files it under 2001.
- **2003:** *[Advances in Distributed Security](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/advances-in-distributed-security/).* A substantial survey/tutorial on probabilistic security, timestamping, Byzantine fault tolerance, replication, and distributed applications.
- **2004:** *[Patterns of Integrity: Separation of Duties](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/patterns-of-integrity-separation-of-duties/).* A security-pattern essay presenting separation of duties as a reusable design form.
- **2004:** *[Rights, Remedies, and Security Models](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/rights-remedies-and-security-models/).* A comparative essay mapping legal concepts of rights and remedies onto computer-security models.
- **2004:** *Smart Contracts* (IEEE International Workshop on Electronic Contracting keynote slides). A slide deck for Szabo’s keynote in San Diego on 6 July 2004; his archived works page records and links the presentation, but the surviving catalogue does not supply a more distinctive title, so none should be invented.
- **2004:** *[Unforgeable Costliness](https://archive.li/kYpjA).* A rough draft theorizing costly-to-produce phenomena as hard-to-fake proxies for value. Szabo explicitly marked it a rough draft, so it resists being treated as a settled publication.
- **2005:** *[Interpreting Power: The Principle of Least Authority](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/interpreting-power-the-principle-of-least-authority/).* An essay joining political delegation, legal authority, and computer-security design around minimizing granted power.

### Institutions, law, money, and interpretive essays

- **1993:** *[Multinational Small Business](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/multinational-small-business/).* A foresight essay arguing that communications technology could let small firms operate across jurisdictions like multinational enterprises.
- **1996:** *[Hermeneutics: An Introduction to the Interpretation of Tradition](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/hermeneutics/).* A concise tutorial on interpreting inherited texts and institutions rather than redesigning them from first principles.
- **1997:** *[Measuring Value](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/measuring-value/).* An institutional-economics essay on measurement as a central transaction cost. Szabo’s archived copy is dated 1997, while the later Satoshi Nakamoto Institute author index labels it 2002; the earlier internal copyright date is retained here and the conflict noted.
- **1997:** *[Recovering Privity](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/recovering-privity/).* A work of conceptual reconstruction that expands the legal doctrine of privity into a theory of bounded knowledge, control, and responsibility.
- **1998:** *[Objective Versus Intersubjective Truth](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/objective-versus-intersubjective-truth/).* A philosophical essay about scientific criticism, tradition, and claims concerning interpersonal life.
- **1998:** *[Winnowed Wisdom](https://www.fon.hum.uva.nl/rob/Courses/InformationInSpeech/CDROM/Literature/LOTwinterschool2006/szabo.best.vwh.net/quotes.html).* An attributed compilation of sayings by other authors. Szabo made the selection and arrangement, not the constituent texts; its resistance to being called an authored work is therefore essential.
- **2002:** *[A Measure of Sacrifice](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/a-measure-of-sacrifice/).* A historical essay about mechanical clocks, sandglasses, and the institutional need for dependable measures of time; the archived version was revised in 2005.
- **2002:** *[The Playdough Protocols](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/the-playdough-protocols/).* An illustrated historical essay treating ancient clay tokens, envelopes, seals, and accounts as commercial-security protocols.
- **2002:** *[Shelling Out: The Origins of Money](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/shelling-out/).* A long illustrated essay proposing that collectible proto-money enabled forms of cooperation, transfer, and wealth preservation before coinage; the archived version carries 2002 and 2005 dates.
- **date not securely established:** *Brehon Law.* An essay on decentralized, pre-literate Irish law listed on Szabo’s own archived works page. The consulted catalogue supplies neither a dependable date nor a surviving full-text record, so a year cannot responsibly be assigned.
- **date not securely established:** *Origins of the Non-Delegation Doctrine.* A legal-historical essay listed and summarized on Szabo’s archived works page, concerning early United States limits on delegating legislative power. The catalogue does not provide a dependable publication date.
- **2006:** *[Wet Code and Dry](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/wet-code-and-dry/).* An essay comparing evolved biological or customary constraints with deliberately written legal and software code.
- **2017:** *[Money, Blockchains, and Social Scalability](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/money-blockchains-and-social-scalability/).* A synthetic essay arguing that institutions and cryptographic systems reduce the cognitive and vulnerability costs of coordinating large societies.

### Micropayment studies

- **1996:** *[The Mental Accounting Barrier to Micropayments](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/the-mental-accounting-barrier-to-micropayments/).* A short economic argument that users’ attention and decision costs impose a practical lower bound on tiny payments.
- **1999:** *[Micropayments and Mental Transaction Costs](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/micropayments-and-mental-transaction-costs/).* A conference paper expanding the mental-accounting argument; it was presented at the Second Berlin Internet Economics Workshop in May 1999.

### Later web essays and serial work

- **2005–present, intermittently:** *[Unenumerated](https://unenumerated.blogspot.com/).* Szabo’s weblog is a continuing serial obra containing short essays, notes, diagrams, historical images, revisions, and occasional republications rather than a single fixed text. No complete scholarly item-level bibliography of its hundreds of posts was found, so treating every post as a securely catalogued independent work would create false completeness.
- **2005:** *[Bit Gold](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold/).* The weblog manifestation through which the bit-gold project became publicly traceable; it is listed above as a protocol project as well as being a web essay.
- **2006:** *[Wet Code and Dry](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/wet-code-and-dry/).* A dated *Unenumerated* post later preserved in the collected-text archive; it is listed above by subject.
- **2008:** *[Bit Gold Markets](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold-markets/).* A dated continuation of the bit-gold project on *Unenumerated*, listed above with that project.
- **2011:** *[Bitcoin, What Took Ye So Long?](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin-what-took-ye-so-long/).* A retrospective web essay relating Bitcoin to earlier digital-money and distributed-security work; it is commentary on Bitcoin, not evidence that Szabo made Bitcoin.
- **2014:** *[The Dawn of Trustworthy Computing](https://unenumerated.blogspot.com/2014/12/the-dawn-of-trustworthy-computing.html).* A web essay on blockchains and minimized trust that later scholarship cites as an extension of Szabo’s smart-contract program.
- **2017:** *[Money, Blockchains, and Social Scalability](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/money-blockchains-and-social-scalability/).* A major *Unenumerated* essay later preserved in the collected-text archive; it is listed above by subject.
