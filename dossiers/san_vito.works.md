## Works by San Vito

### No securely attributable obra

- **No surviving work or project:** The standard hagiographic repertory records no text, object, building, or other made thing authored or created by Vito. Given the near-total absence of reliable biographical evidence, this is necessarily a statement about the surviving record, not proof that he made nothing during his lifetime.

### Manifestation that resists classification as a work

- **Probably early 4th century:** Martyrial witness. Vito’s identity as a martyr is the historical nucleus preserved by the early cult, but his death is an event and subsequent religious manifestation—not securely an intentional *obra*, project, or manifesto made by him; even its circumstances and precise date remain uncertain.

### Works about or attributed to the cult, not made by San Vito

- **6th–7th century and later:** *Passiones of Vitus, Modestus, and Crescentia* (BHL 8711–8723). These anonymous hagiographic narratives and their recensions are texts about Vito, not texts by him; their elaborate biography is late and substantially legendary, so none belongs in his own corpus.
- **From the 5th century to the present:** Cult of San Vito. This continuing complex of liturgy, relic traditions, images, churches, healing practices, and narratives is a collective historical manifestation generated by devotees and institutions. It cannot be treated as something Vito personally made.
