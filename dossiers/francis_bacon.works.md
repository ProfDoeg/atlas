## Works by Francis Bacon

Bacon's corpus resists a single shape. Some pieces are finished, self-contained books; others are fragments of a project he never completed; one text he kept rewriting his entire life. The categories below follow the kind of thing each text actually is, not a uniform "title, date" ledger.

### The Great Instauration: a project, not a book

In 1620 Bacon published a short Latin manifesto, *Instauratio Magna* (The Great Instauration), laying out a six-part plan to reorganize all human knowledge on inductive, experimental foundations. Only fragments of the plan were ever written. Part II — *Novum Organum* (1620) — is the only part Bacon brought to a finished state: it attacks Aristotelian syllogistic logic, proposes induction from observed particulars instead, and introduces his doctrine of the four "idols" that distort human understanding. Later installments toward Parts III and IV followed in his last years but never reached completion: *Historia naturalis et experimentalis* / *Historia ventorum*, a natural history of winds (November 1622), and *Historia vitae et mortis*, on life and death (January 1623). Bacon died in 1626 with the Instauration still, by his own design, an unfinished architecture rather than a closed book. (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Francis Bacon," https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/francis-bacon/)

### The Essays: a work that kept changing

The *Essays* are not one text with one date but a single project Bacon revised across his adult life, each edition substantially larger than the last. The first edition (1597) contained ten short essays, issued together with *Religious Meditations* and *Of the Colours of Good and Evil*. A second, much-enlarged edition followed in 1612 (38 essays). The final edition, printed in 1625, the year before his death, expanded the collection again to 58 essays, several rewritten rather than simply added. Contemporary printers reissued the *Essays* more than twenty times across the seventeenth century, making this the most continuously republished and revised of all his works. (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; Wikipedia, "Francis Bacon," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Bacon)

### Philosophy and natural science

- **The Advancement of Learning** (1605): a two-book English survey defending the value of secular learning against both religious suspicion and scholastic complacency, and cataloguing the gaps in existing knowledge. Bacon later judged the English text insufficient for an international, learned readership.
- **De Augmentis Scientiarum** (*De dignitate et augmentis scientiarum libri IX*, October 1623): not a translation but an expanded, reorganized Latin reworking of the *Advancement*, grown to nine books. It effectively serves as a revised, enlarged edition of the earlier English work, aimed at Continental scholars. (SEP)
- **Novum Organum** (1620): see above — Part II of the Instauration, Bacon's methodological core text.
- **Sylva Sylvarum** (1626, posthumous): a large miscellany of natural-historical observations and experiments, published after Bacon's death under the supervision of his chaplain and literary executor, William Rawley.
- **De Sapientia Veterum** (Of the Wisdom of the Ancients, 1609): a collection of classical myths each given an allegorical, philosophical reading — Bacon's attempt to recover buried natural and moral knowledge he believed was encoded in ancient fable.

### Unfinished and posthumous

- **New Atlantis**: a utopian narrative describing Bensalem and its scientific institution, Salomon's House, usually read as a literary expression of the Instauration's ideals. Bacon left it unfinished; it was published in 1626/1627, after his death, alongside *Sylva Sylvarum*.
- **Historia Vitae et Mortis** and **Historia Ventorum** (1622–23): fragments of the planned natural-historical portion of the Instauration, finished enough to print but never assembled into the larger structure Bacon had sketched.
- Earlier in his career Bacon drafted other programmatic pieces toward the same project that he abandoned or left circulating only in manuscript, including *Valerius Terminus* and *Temporis Partus Masculus* — early, rougher statements of the ideas that the *Novum Organum* later superseded.

### Legal, political, and historical writings

- **The History of the Reign of King Henry VII** (1622): a historical narrative notable for its analytical, almost case-study treatment of Tudor statecraft; reprinted repeatedly through the seventeenth century.
- **A Declaration of the Practices and Treasons... of Robert, late Earl of Essex** (1601): the official crown account of the Essex conspiracy and trial, written at the government's instruction — Bacon had been one of the prosecutors.
- Bacon's legal career produced technical treatises and proposals on English law and its reform (he held the offices of Solicitor General, Attorney General, and Lord Chancellor), though most circulated as manuscripts or were printed later; Wikipedia's bibliography flags these only generally rather than by exhaustive title, and this entry does the same rather than inventing precision the sources don't give.
- **Apophthegms New and Old** (1625): a late collection of historical and anecdotal sayings, closer to a commonplace-book than a treatise, but published in his lifetime.
