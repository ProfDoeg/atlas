## Works by Guido van Rossum

### Programming languages, environments, and software systems

- **1982–1986:** *ABC programming language and environment*. A collaborative obra intended for non-expert programmers; van Rossum’s assigned role was its design and implementation, so it resists attribution to him alone.
- **1986–1991:** *Amoeba distributed operating system*. Van Rossum contributed to this collective systems project rather than creating the whole operating system; the surviving bibliography identifies associated papers and his AIL interface generator.
- **1988:** *STDWIN*. A portable window-system interface designed and implemented by van Rossum; both its source code and his technical report survive.
- **1990:** *AIL*. A class-oriented stub generator made for Amoeba; it is both a software tool and the subject of a conference paper.
- **1989–present:** *Python*. A programming language, reference implementation, standard library, documentation system, and continuing communal project begun by van Rossum in December 1989. Calling it solely his work would obscure thousands of later contributors, but authoritative accounts consistently identify him as its creator and original principal author.
- **1991:** *Python–AIL interface*. A software bridge connecting the AIL stub generator to Python, made with Jelke de Boer and documented in their EurOpen paper.
- **1991–1995:** *CMIF/CMIFed multimedia authoring software*. A collaborative presentation environment for portable hypermedia documents; it belongs to CWI’s larger multimedia project and is not exclusively attributable to van Rossum.
- **1995–1999:** *Grail*. An extensible web browser written in Python by van Rossum as a substantial demonstration of the language; subsequent releases became collaborative.
- **1999–2000:** *Computer Programming for Everybody*. A DARPA-funded educational project intended to make programming broadly accessible. Its obra is partly an intention—proposal, curriculum experiments, and software work—rather than a single finished object.
- **2003–2005:** *pgen2*. A Python implementation of Python’s parser generator, later incorporated into the standard library under `lib2to3`; it is infrastructure rather than an independent end-user application.
- **2006:** *Mondrian*. An internal Google web-based code-review system created by van Rossum; because it was not publicly released, its surviving public identity is chiefly documentary.
- **2008:** *Rietveld*. An open-source, App Engine–hosted code-review system derived from the Mondrian idea and written by van Rossum; it later became a collaborative repository.
- **c. 2008–2012:** *Appstats*. A request-profiling library for Google App Engine to which van Rossum contributed. It resists treatment as a solely authored work because it belonged to the larger App Engine project.
- **c. 2010–2012:** *NDB*. A Python client library for Google App Engine’s Datastore created by van Rossum; later Cloud NDB and other descendants are continuations, not separate original works by him.
- **2013–2019:** *mypy and Dropbox’s typed-Python migration*. Van Rossum worked on the existing mypy project and helped direct a migration exceeding five million lines; this is consequential collaborative engineering, not a work he originated.

### Books and technical manuals

- **1995:** *Python Library Reference*. A CWI technical report defining Python’s library; later release-specific manuals are evolving manifestations of this documentation, not wholly new books.
- **1995:** *Python Reference Manual*. The formal language-reference report for early Python; later versions, including those edited with Fred L. Drake Jr., are revised manifestations.
- **1995:** *Python Tutorial*. An instructional CWI report, issued as version 1.2 on 10 April 1995 and subsequently maintained through many Python releases.
- **1995:** *Extending and Embedding the Python Interpreter*. A technical manual describing Python’s extension and embedding interfaces; it likewise became living release documentation.
- **1996:** *Internet Programming with Python*. A jointly authored book by Aaron Watters, van Rossum, and James C. Ahlstrom; it is distinct from the Python documentation manuals.
- **1996 and 2001:** *Forewords to Programming Python*. Two related but distinct prefatory essays for the first and second editions of Mark Lutz’s book; they are contributions, not authorship of the book itself.

### Research papers and chapters

- **1988:** *STDWIN—A Standard Window System Interface*. A CWI research report specifying and describing the STDWIN software.
- **1990:** *AIL—A Class-Oriented Stub Generator for Amoeba*. A conference paper describing AIL.
- **1990:** *Amoeba: A Distributed Operating System for the 1990s*. A collaboratively authored journal article by Sape Mullender, van Rossum, Robbert van Renesse, and Hans van Staveren.
- **1990:** *Experiences with the Amoeba Distributed Operating System*. A seven-author collaborative article in *Communications of the ACM*; van Rossum is a coauthor, not sole maker.
- **1991:** *Linking a Stub Generator (AIL) to a Prototyping Language (Python)*. A conference paper by van Rossum and Jelke de Boer connecting two software works.
- **1991:** *A Structure for Transportable, Dynamic Multimedia Documents*. A collaborative USENIX paper by Dick Bulterman, van Rossum, and Robert van Liere.
- **1991:** *Multimedia Synchronization and UNIX—or—If Multimedia Support Is the Problem, Is UNIX the Solution?*. A collaborative EurOpen paper by Bulterman, van Rossum, and Dik Winter.
- **1992:** *The ABC Structure Editor: Structure-Based Editing for the ABC Programming Environment*. A CWI report by Lambert Meertens, Steven Pemberton, and van Rossum; its subject and authorship are collective.
- **1993:** *Links in Hypermedia: The Requirement for Context*. A paper by Lynda Hardman, Bulterman, and van Rossum defining contextual behavior for hypermedia links.
- **1993:** *Structured Multimedia Authoring*. A paper by Hardman, van Rossum, and Bulterman; the conference version revises CWI report CS-R9304 and should not be counted as a separate intellectual work.
- **1993:** *CMIFed: A Presentation Environment for Portable Hypermedia Documents*. A paper by van Rossum, Jack Jansen, K. Sjoerd Mullender, and Bulterman describing the CMIFed environment; it revises CWI report CS-R9305.
- **1993:** *The Amsterdam Hypermedia Model: Extending Hypertext to Support Real Multimedia*. A paper by Hardman, Bulterman, and van Rossum, revised from CWI report CS-R9306. Some bibliographies date a later journal appearance or republication differently; the author’s bibliography identifies the *Hypermedia* 5(1) version as July 1993.
- **1995:** *An Interactive Multimedia Management Game*. A collaborative journal article by Hardman, van Rossum, and Aart van Bolhuis.
- **1997:** *Scripting the Web with Python*. An article in the *World Wide Web Journal* describing Python as a web-scripting language.
- **1997:** *A Tour of the Python Language*. A short conference contribution in the TOOLS 23 proceedings; bibliographic databases index it separately from the tutorial and reference manuals.
- **2006:** *Python Programming Language*. A USENIX conference presentation/paper; records variously treat it as an article, talk, or conference item, so its material form is not bibliographically uniform.
- **2025:** *Best Practices for Data-Only Classes in Python*. A multi-author programming-languages research article including van Rossum; its highly collaborative authorship prevents treating it as his alone.

### Essays, position papers, and proposals

- **1996:** *Foreword for Programming Python, First Edition*. An essay combining early Python history with van Rossum’s software-design and project-management views.
- **Date not stated:** *Implementing Graphs*. A short instructional essay demonstrating graph representation in Python.
- **Date not stated:** *An Optimization Anecdote*. A programming essay about improving Python performance.
- **Date not stated:** *Metaclass Programming in Python 1.5*. A technical essay on early Python metaclasses.
- **1997:** *Built-in Package Support in Python 1.5*. Documentation of the package mechanism introduced with Python 1.5.
- **1997; later updated by Barry Warsaw:** *Standard Exception Classes in Python 1.5*. Technical documentation subsequently revised for Python 1.5.2; the later state is collaborative.
- **1998:** *Glue It All Together with Python*. A position paper presented at the OMG–DARPA–MCC workshop on compositional software architectures.
- **Date not stated:** *What Is Python? Executive Summary*. A brief advocacy text describing Python’s principal qualities.
- **Date not stated:** *Comparing Python to Other Languages*. A comparative advocacy essay that van Rossum acknowledged remained unrevised despite extensive reader feedback.
- **1998:** *Proposed Improvements to Module Cleanup*. A language-implementation proposal later adopted in Python 1.5.1; it is a protocol-like text because it prescribes changed interpreter behavior.
- **1998:** *Open Source Summit Trip Report*. A report first circulated on `comp.lang.python` and then published in the *Linux Gazette*.
- **1998:** *Debugging Reference Count Problems*. An edited technical essay derived from a `comp.lang.python` posting of 27 May 1998.
- **1999:** *Computer Programming for Everybody*. The original DARPA funding proposal; as a project text, it attempts to bring an educational program into existence rather than merely describe one.
- **1999–2000:** *Computer Programming for Everybody, revised and extended version*. A substantial revision of the accepted proposal, best treated as a second manifestation of the same project rather than an unrelated work.
- **2001:** *Foreword for Programming Python, Second Edition*. A continuation of the first foreword’s historical account.
- **2001:** *Unifying Types and Classes in Python 2.2*. An explanatory essay accompanying an initially experimental redesign of Python’s object model.
- **2002:** *Parade of the PEPs*. A written reconstruction of van Rossum’s Python10 keynote commentary on open enhancement proposals; because it was recreated after the talk, it is not a transcription of the performance.

### Protocols, standards, and language manifestos

- **2000–present:** *Python Enhancement Proposals coauthored by Guido van Rossum*. This is a distributed body of protocol and manifesto texts rather than one conventional book: it includes active style rules such as PEP 7 and PEP 8; conventions such as PEP 257; language proposals including PEP 228, PEP 234, PEP 260, PEP 308, PEP 342, PEP 343, and PEP 572; release protocols including PEP 251 and PEP 283; and project-governance declarations including PEP 3000, PEP 3003, and PEP 731. Authorship, status, and even text change over time, many PEPs are collaborative, and accepted, rejected, withdrawn, superseded, and active PEPs remain different kinds of acts; consequently this corpus resists being treated as a fixed collected work.
- **2001–present:** *PEP 7—Style Guide for C Code*. A living process protocol coauthored with Barry Warsaw; its active status means the current page is not identical to its 2001 manifestation.
- **2001–present:** *PEP 8—Style Guide for Python Code*. A living protocol by van Rossum, Warsaw, and Alyssa Coghlan, adapted partly from van Rossum’s earlier Python Style Guide; later editorial changes make sole attribution misleading.
- **2001–present:** *PEP 257—Docstring Conventions*. An active informational protocol by David Goodger and van Rossum standardizing the form of Python documentation strings.
- **2006:** *PEP 3000—Python 3000*. A process manifesto establishing the aims and development framework for Python 3 rather than merely documenting an already finished language.
- **2009:** *PEP 3003—Python Language Moratorium*. A coauthored process manifesto that temporarily suspended specified kinds of language change; it acts upon the project and therefore is more than descriptive prose.

### Patent inventions

- **Priority 2004; published 2006:** *System for Distributed Task Execution*. A patented system credited to Robert Kahn, David Ely, van Rossum, Theodore Strollo, and Barry Warsaw; a patent records a claimed collaborative invention, not proof that each inventor wrote every part of an implementation.
- **Priority 2010; granted 2015:** *Structured Content Item Synchronization*. A Dropbox-assigned patented invention listing van Rossum among its inventors.
- **Priority 2014; published 2015:** *Random Identifier Generation for Offline Database*. A Dropbox-assigned patented invention listing van Rossum among its inventors.
- **Priority 2023; published 2025:** *Integrating Non-Native Dependencies in Spreadsheet Applications*. A Microsoft-assigned patented invention by Joseph McDaid, Su-Piao Wu, Alexander Novokhodko, and van Rossum concerning dependency analysis when languages such as Python are embedded in spreadsheets.

### Incomplete, dispersed, or resistant bodies of work

- **1977–present:** *Source-code contributions, patches, reviews, documentation edits, talks, interviews, mailing-list posts, and blog writing*. These are real manifestations of van Rossum’s work but do not form a closed catalogue: his own standard publications list expressly calls itself incomplete, the Python repositories contain collaborative and continuously edited code, and his essay index excludes or separately points to presentations, interviews, and blogs. Treating every commit, message, release-specific manual, translation, or reprint as a separate obra would confuse documentary manifestations with independently conceived works.
