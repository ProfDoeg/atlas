## Works by Ruth St. Denis

### Solo dances and short choreographies

- **1906:** *Radha: The Mystic Dance of the Five Senses*. A solo dance-drama—sometimes called a “Hindu ballet”—organized around five sensory episodes and set to music from Delibes’s *Lakmé*. It is not ethnographic Indian dance but St. Denis’s theatrical construction of an imagined sacred India; the documented public premiere was January 28, 1906. [Schlundt’s chronology via Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873); [Shawn, *Ruth St. Denis: Pioneer & Prophet*, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1906:** *The Incense*. A devotional solo and embodiment of rising incense, premiered with *Radha* and *The Cobras* at the Hudson Theatre on March 22. It is simultaneously choreography, ritual image, and theatrical manifestation. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676); [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1906:** *The Cobras*. A theatrical street-scene solo in which St. Denis’s arms figure as snakes; early productions also employed musicians and bazaar characters. Sources fluctuate between the singular *The Cobra* and St. Denis and Shawn’s plural title *The Cobras*. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676); [Shawn, vols. I–II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1908:** *The Nautch*. A palace dance, premiered in Vienna and subsequently revised through several costumes and formats. It later circulated as *Nautch Dance*, *East Indian Nautch*, *White Nautch*, and *Street Nautch*; the archive does not always make these versions into securely independent works. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf); [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1908:** *The Yogi*. A nearly motionless solo representing meditation and spiritual attainment, first developed in Vienna. It resists an ordinary idea of dance because stillness and projected presence, rather than a sequence of steps, are its primary material. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf); [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1908:** *A Shirabyoshi*. A Japanese-themed solo episode created from a Lafcadio Hearn story and premiered at St. Denis’s London midnight gala on November 26. It combines dance, dramatic character, shrine offering, and memorial rite. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1909:** *The Dance of Jephthah’s Daughter*. A biblical solo made for Ludwig Mond’s garden entertainment in London on May 30; no evidence in the consulted catalogues establishes a continuing repertory life. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1909:** *In a Greek Grove*. A Greek-themed occasional dance made for the same Mond garden performance. Its survival as an autonomous work, rather than an event-specific manifestation, is uncertain. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1909:** *An American Cake Walk*. An occasional social-dance interpretation made for the Mond garden performance; its title marks a distinct dance, but documentation of its choreography is slight. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1910:** *The Lotus Pond*. The sixth dance in St. Denis’s East Indian series, premiered in Boston in January 1910 with music by Walter Meyrowitz. A garden reverie becomes animated by the dancer’s encounter with a bee and an approaching lover. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1912:** *The Vision of Odin*. A Norse-themed occasional dance created for a MacDowell Club Saxon fête at the Waldorf in December. Its status is closer to commissioned pageantry than to a securely preserved repertory work. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1914:** *The Legend of the Peacock* (also *The Peacock*). A solo premiered at Ravinia in June, subsequently enlarged into a ten-person dance-drama and later returned to solo form. The shifting scale means that the title identifies a family of manifestations, not one invariant choreography. [Schlundt’s chronology via Mapping Touring](https://mappingtouring.osu.edu/public/performances/873); [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1914:** *Arabic Suite of Dances*. Two solos and a duet created with Ted Shawn at Ravinia; it was expanded that autumn into *Ourieda*. Whether the suite and the later dance-drama are separate works or successive states is unresolved. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1914:** *From a Grecian Vase*. A pair of Greek-style dances—Archaic and Lyric—first made for the Berkeley pageant context and later performed in vaudeville. The collective title can denote either the pair or an individual excerpt. [Shawn, vols. I–II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1915:** *Greek Veil Plastique*. A solo made for Gluck’s *Orpheus* at Berkeley’s Greek Theatre. It belongs partly to St. Denis’s oeuvre and partly to a larger operatic production, resisting complete separation from that host work. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1916:** *Tillers of the Soil*. An Egyptian ensemble episode in the Berkeley *Dance Pageant*, first performed July 29. It is both a named choreography and a component of a larger Gesamtkunstwerk. [Shawn, vol. II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1917:** *Kuan-Yin*. A Chinese-themed vaudeville solo presenting the goddess of mercy as an animated porcelain figure, performed to music by Erik Satie. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1918:** *The Vision of Yashodhara*. A solo contribution to the Krotona production of *The Light of Asia*; St. Denis also staged a Temptation Scene with Denishawn dancers. Because another producer controlled the encompassing drama, these are contributions rather than an entirely St. Denis-authored production. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1918–1919:** *Royal Siamese Ballet*. Begun as a solo in spring 1918 and developed into a complete ballet the next year, based theatrically on the *Ramayana*. The two states should not be silently collapsed, although the source uses one title for both. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1919:** *The Street of the Dancers*. A vaudeville set of Algerian-themed dances distinct from *Ourieda*, although both center an Ouled-Nail dancer. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1919:** *Nautch Dance*. A revised or newly crystallized version of St. Denis’s earlier *Nautch*, later especially successful on Denishawn’s Asian tour. Scholarship treats its relation to the 1908 palace dance inconsistently, so it is listed as a revision-manifestation rather than an unquestionably new work. [Allen, “Rewriting the Script for South Indian Dance”](https://www.artsrn.ualberta.ca/fwa_mediawiki/images/1/10/1146609.pdf)

- **circa 1919–1920:** *The Scherzo Waltz*. An athletic comic solo developed through improvisation with composer McNair Ilgenfritz. The consulted sources do not provide a secure premiere date. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **circa 1919–1920:** *The St. Denis Mazurka*. A duet for St. Denis and Shawn to Ganne’s *La Czarine*, made for the company’s modern-dance divertissements; no secure premiere date appears in the consulted sources. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **circa 1919–1920:** *Impromptu*. A comic, variably improvised encore in which an awkward aspirant “classic” dancer burlesques the company’s repertory. Its deliberate refusal to remain identical from performance to performance makes it a performance protocol more than a fixed choreography. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **circa 1919–1920:** *Spirit of the Sea*. An elemental solo to MacDowell’s *Sea Pieces*, employing long green silk extensions as both costume and moving environment. [Shawn, vols. I–II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1920:** *Soaring*. A “music visualization” in which bodily movement manifests the structure and dynamics of music rather than narrating a plot. NYPL preserves choreographic notes, but Denishawn-era files can contain mixed or joint authorship; St. Denis is conventionally credited. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676); [University of Washington repertory](https://dance.washington.edu/people/ruth-st-denis)

- **1921:** *Xochitl*. A solo dance-drama associated with Denishawn’s Americana or “exotic” repertory. Surviving repertory lists credit St. Denis, but the consulted archival overview does not provide enough evidence to reconstruct its first-production circumstances. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1922:** *Brahms Waltz*. A music-visualization solo premiered April 17 at the Academy of Music in Lynchburg. NYPL preserves choreographic material but spells the archival title “Brahm’s Waltz.” [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1922:** *Liebestraum*. A music visualization to Liszt, premiered on the same Lynchburg program as *Brahms Waltz*. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1923:** *Dance of the Black and Gold Sari*. A solo centered on unfolding, donning, and animating a sari; it became one of St. Denis’s most successful dances in India. Some sources date it 1922, but specialist scholarship gives a 1923 premiere, so the dating remains disputed. [Allen, “Rewriting the Script for South Indian Dance”](https://www.artsrn.ualberta.ca/fwa_mediawiki/images/1/10/1146609.pdf)

- **circa 1925:** *White Nautch*. A pale-costumed late manifestation of St. Denis’s Nautch material. The University of Washington repertory gives only an approximate date, and its independence from earlier Nautch versions remains uncertain. [University of Washington repertory](https://dance.washington.edu/people/ruth-st-denis)

- **1926:** *White Jade*. A Chinese-themed solo from the post-Asian-tour repertory. It is securely named and dated in dance reference works, although the consulted sources do not expose a full production record. [Larousse, “Ruth Saint Denis”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/374); [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1928:** *The Lamp*. A solo or short dance from St. Denis’s late Denishawn repertory; the available reference catalogue supplies the date but little evidence about its form. [Larousse, “Ruth Saint Denis”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/374)

- **1928:** *Dance of the Red and Gold Saree*. A sari solo named in the NYPL repertory summary. It may be related to, but should not be silently identified with, *Dance of the Black and Gold Sari*. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1928:** *Dance of the Black and Gold Sari*. Larousse dates this title to 1928, conflicting with specialist studies that date the premiere to 1923; this is a dating dispute, not evidence of a securely distinct second work. [Larousse, “Ruth Saint Denis”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/374); [Allen, “Rewriting the Script for South Indian Dance”](https://www.artsrn.ualberta.ca/fwa_mediawiki/images/1/10/1146609.pdf)

- **1930:** *Angkor-Vat*. A Cambodian-themed solo from St. Denis’s late repertory. The work’s title is sometimes normalized as *Angkor Wat*. [Larousse, “Ruth Saint Denis”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/374)

- **1931:** *The Prophetess*. A religious dance work and one of St. Denis’s post-Denishawn choreographic projects; NYPL preserves descriptive or production material, while Larousse supplies the 1931 date. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676); [Larousse, “Ruth Saint Denis”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/374)

- **1964:** *Siddhas of the Upper Air*. A duet choreographed for St. Denis and Ted Shawn’s fiftieth-anniversary performance at Jacob’s Pillow, using a poem by St. Denis and music by Jess Meeker. Authorship is collaborative across choreography, text, music, and performance. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

### Dance-dramas, ballets, pageants, and other Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1904–1910:** *Egypta*. Conceived in 1904 as an ambitious Egyptian production, initially abandoned because of cost, and eventually produced as a five-part cycle in 1910. The long interval matters: *Egypta* is first a project and only later a staged work. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf); [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1910:** *Invocation to the Nile*. The opening dance of *Egypta*, built as a temple procession and embodiment of the river’s inundation. It also circulated as an excerpt, so it is both component and autonomous manifestation. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1910:** *The Palace Dance*. The second part of *Egypta*, combining banquet pageantry, hieroglyphic ensemble movement, musicians, and a featured dancer. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1910:** *The Veil of Isis*. The third dance in *Egypta*. It is a constituent scene whose separate repertory history is not established by the consulted sources. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1910:** *The Dance of Day*. The fourth dance in *Egypta*, documented as a titled component rather than necessarily as an independent work. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1910:** *The Dance of Night*. The fifth dance in *Egypta*, likewise poised between autonomous choreography and scene within the cycle. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **by 1913:** *O-Mika: A Japanese Legend of the Buddha*. A three-scene dance-play after Lafcadio Hearn, combining choreography, spoken text, character acting, music, scenic design, and Japanese-language speech. It is properly a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a dance. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **by 1913:** *Bakawali: A Hindu Love Tale*. A dance-play using speech, dance, music, large scenic effects, and a narrative of a heavenly dancer and mortal lover. The consulted history does not supply a secure premiere date. [Shawn, vols. I–II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **by 1913:** *Dance of the Blue Flame*. A dance extracted from *Bakawali* and repeatedly performed separately. Its status is therefore double: scene within a dance-play and independent repertory excerpt. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1913:** *The Garden of Kama*. An East Indian dance-drama derived from themes in Laurence Hope’s poetry and first produced for a ten-day San Francisco run. It unites dance, drama, song, costume, décor, and ensemble action. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1914:** *Ourieda: A Romance of the Desert*. A two-scene dance-drama developed from the earlier *Arabic Suite of Dances*. Because St. Denis and Shawn created and performed the earlier suite together, sole authorship should not be presumed. [Shawn, vols. I–II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **circa 1914–1919:** *Theodora, Empress of Byzantium*. A court dance-drama with scenic design by Maxwell Armfield. The consulted history describes St. Denis as its creator but does not furnish a secure premiere date. [Shawn, vols. I–II](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis02shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1914:** *Chitra*. A sequence of dances translating Rabindranath Tagore’s play, made for an open-air performance at Mount Kisco. Because it is a danced transformation of another author’s drama, it is an adaptation rather than a wholly original narrative. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1916:** *A Dance Pageant of Egypt, Greece, and India*. A six-part, three-culture pageant for St. Denis, Shawn, and roughly one hundred dancers, premiered at Berkeley on July 29. Choreography, mass movement, costume, music, setting, religious symbolism, and spectacle make it a clear Gesamtkunstwerk; authorship is shared with Shawn and collaborators. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1916:** *Babylonian dances for D. W. Griffith’s Intolerance*. Screen choreography made for another artist’s film rather than a freestanding St. Denis work. Attribution is to St. Denis, but the resulting cinematic object is Griffith’s collaborative production. [Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/st-denis-ruth-1878-1968-1)

- **undated, post-1931:** *Arcadia*. A post-Denishawn choreographic project represented in the NYPL papers by descriptions, choreographic notes, or production arrangements. The catalogue does not establish whether it reached performance, so it must remain a project rather than be promoted silently to a completed ballet. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated, post-1931:** *Ballet of Soul*. A planned or staged ballet documented among St. Denis’s post-Denishawn projects; completion and premiere are not established in the public finding aid. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated, post-1931:** *Freedom*. A post-Denishawn choreographic project ranging in possible status from notes to production plan; the catalogue does not say that it was completed. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated, post-1931:** *Light of Asia*. A choreographic project in the NYPL papers. It must be distinguished from St. Denis’s 1918 dances within the Krotona production of the same name; the catalogue does not establish whether this later project was completed. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated, post-1931:** *Wisdom*. A post-Denishawn ballet or pageant project preserved in descriptive and production materials. Its public-performance status is unresolved. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

### Music visualizations and Denishawn works with unresolved authorship

- **undated:** *Bach’s Inventions*. Choreographic notes survive among approximately 195 Denishawn dance files. The finding aid warns that these files include works by St. Denis, Shawn, or both, so the title’s existence is secure but St. Denis’s sole authorship is not. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Egyptian Suite*. A documented Denishawn choreography, possibly a rearrangement of *Egypta* material. Its exact relationship to the 1910 cycle and its authorship are not resolved by the public catalogue. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Frohsinn*. A Denishawn dance represented by choreographic notes; attribution remains St. Denis, Shawn, or joint unless the folder itself is consulted. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Kinetic Molpai*. A Denishawn choreography whose title survives in the archival index. Public description does not settle its date, realization, or individual authorship. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Negro Spirituals*. A Denishawn choreographic work or suite documented by notes. The historically used title is retained as the work’s name; precise authorship is unresolved in the public finding aid. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Sonata Pathétique*. A music visualization named among St. Denis’s notable works and documented in the Denishawn archive. The surviving Denishawn tunic associated with it was worn by Gertrude Shurr, underscoring that the work existed through performers and material objects, not only through a score. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Allegresse*. A choreography represented by both notes and music in the Denishawn files. The public catalogue does not securely assign sole authorship. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Garland Plastique*. A Denishawn choreography preserved in notes and musical material; date and individual authorship remain unestablished. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *The Goldfish*. A documented Denishawn dance with surviving choreographic and musical material. Attribution cannot be resolved without consulting the folder-level record. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Japanese*. A catalogue title for choreographic and musical material, not necessarily the work’s final public title. Its generic name and unresolved authorship make it resist treatment as a stable finished work. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Jewel Dance*. A Denishawn choreography represented by notes and music; date, premiere, and sole authorship are unresolved. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Moszkowski Waltz*. A music-visualization choreography documented in the Denishawn archive. The title identifies the musical source more securely than it establishes choreographic authorship. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *The Sacred River*. A Denishawn work represented by choreographic notes and music; its relationship to other Egyptian or Indian river imagery is not explained by the finding aid. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Scarf Dance*. A choreographic file and musical accompaniment. The generic title may describe a classroom combination, repertory dance, or both, so its resistance to a conventional “work” identity should be retained. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Serenata Morisca*. A Denishawn choreography represented by notes and score. Other repertory sources sometimes associate this dance with Shawn, making St. Denis’s authorship disputed. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Viva Faraon*. A Denishawn choreography documented by notes and music; date and individual authorship remain unresolved. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

### Books, poems, essays, lectures, and performative texts

- **1916:** *The Dance as an Art Form*. An essay published in *Theatre Arts Magazine*, articulating dance as an autonomous art rather than merely entertainment or accompaniment. [University of Pittsburgh dance bibliography](https://sites.pitt.edu/~gillis/dance/references.html)

- **1920:** *An Essay on the Future of the Dance: Ruth St. Denis’s Prophecy*. A manifesto-like text printed as the conclusion to Ted Shawn’s *Ruth St. Denis: Pioneer & Prophet*. It acts prospectively—announcing a spiritual future for dance—rather than merely describing existing practice. [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **1932:** *Lotus Light*. A book of St. Denis’s poems. It is a literary obra distinct from her dances, though its devotional language belongs to the same spiritual project. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1934–1954:** *History Letters*. A serial autobiographical and professional communication sent in copies to friends and associates. These resist classification as a single work: they are correspondence, self-archive, chronicle, and recurring public address. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1939:** *An Unfinished Life*. St. Denis’s autobiography. Its title is programmatic as well as descriptive: the book presents a life still being made, while earlier journals and autobiographical materials supplied portions of its text. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **circa 1910s–1960s:** *Poems*. Two archival folders contain individually titled poems arranged alphabetically; the public finding aid does not expose a complete title list. This is therefore a documented body of made texts but not responsibly divisible here into invented or unverified entries. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676); [UCLA finding aid](https://www.oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark%3A%2F13030%2Fkt5j49p08s)

- **circa 1910s–1960s:** *Addresses, essays, and lectures*. Approximately sixty works on religion, dance, Asian dance, women, world affairs, and relations among the arts survive in the NYPL papers. They are real made texts, some published and some performative speech manuscripts, but the public catalogue does not disclose all titles and dates; a claim to itemized completeness would therefore be false. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1927–1958:** *Journal*. A continuing manuscript obra encompassing personal, spiritual, and professional writing, portions of which entered *An Unfinished Life*. It is not one finished “book” but an accretive life-writing practice. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *An Unfinished Life* outline and preliminary autobiographical writings. These are projects and working texts rather than separate published works; the archive indicates that many pre-1939 materials were absorbed into the autobiography. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1964:** *Siddhas of the Upper Air*. A poem written as part of the anniversary duet with Shawn. It is simultaneously a literary text, performance score, and constituent of a collaborative dance work; the text itself is not reproduced here. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

### Pedagogical protocols, institutions, and durable projects

- **1915–1931:** *Denishawn School of Dancing and Related Arts*. A school, pedagogical system, performing company, and cultural project co-created with Ted Shawn. It is an obra in the sense of a durable institution and body of practice, not a work solely authored by St. Denis. [Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/st-denis-ruth-1878-1968-1); [Shawn, vol. I](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c9/Ruth_St._Denis%2C_pioneer_%26_prophet_-_being_a_history_of_her_cycle_of_oriental_dances_%28IA_ruthstdenis01shawrich%29.pdf)

- **circa 1915–1933:** *Denishawn technique*. A body of protocols covering exercises, barre and center work, combinations, movement studies, and culturally labeled dance techniques. It resists identification as one choreography: it is an instructional system distributed through classes, notes, teachers, and bodies. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **circa 1915–1933:** *Music visualization*. A choreographic and pedagogical method that maps musical structure into movement. St. Denis developed it within Denishawn, but it is better classified as a protocol and body of work than as a single titled composition. [Larousse, “Ruth Saint Denis”](https://www.larousse.fr/archives/danse/page/374)

- **1935 onward:** *Society of Spiritual Arts*. A religious-arts organization founded by St. Denis to join spiritual practice with dance and related arts. It is an institutional project whose activities—not a single text—constitute the work. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1938 onward:** *Adelphi College dance program*. An academic dance program founded and shaped by St. Denis. Its status is pedagogical and institutional rather than choreographic. [University of Washington biography](https://dance.washington.edu/people/ruth-st-denis)

- **1940–1942:** *School of Natya*. A school devoted to Asian dance forms, co-founded with La Meri. It is a collaborative institutional obra rather than a discrete authored text. [Enciclopedia delle donne](https://www.enciclopediadelledonne.it/biografie/ruth-dennis)

- **1940s–1960s:** *Theatre of the Scriptures*. A religious-theatrical project documented in St. Denis-related archival material. Its surviving form includes proposals and explanatory texts, and its degree of stage realization varies; it should not automatically be called a completed production. [John Dougherty Collection finding aid](https://nyplorg-data-archives.s3.amazonaws.com/uploads/collection/pdf_finding_aid/dandough.pdf)

- **late 1940s–1960s:** *Ruth St. Denis Foundation*. An organization created to assemble, catalogue, and record St. Denis’s career and to further a church dedicated to religious dance and related arts. It is simultaneously archive-making, institution, and unrealized or only partly realized spiritual project. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Creative Arts Colony*. An organization founded by St. Denis to provide an environment for spiritual and aesthetic experiment. The archive notes that some of her organizations flourished while others remained plans, but does not publicly specify this project’s complete realization history. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Church of the Divine Dance*. A projected church in which dance and related arts would serve religious worship. Its intention is itself the work: surviving proposals document a project that should not be misrepresented as a completed building or permanent denomination. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1964–1968:** *Film illustrating her life through dance*. A film project undertaken with William Skipper during St. Denis’s final four years. The NYPL biography records the work in progress but does not identify a completed, released film or fixed title; it therefore remains an unfinished audiovisual project. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

### Archival bodies that resist reduction to individual works

- **circa 1915–1932:** *Approximately 195 Denishawn choreographic files*. These are not 195 securely attributable Ruth St. Denis works: the standard archival catalogue explicitly says that folders may concern choreography by St. Denis, Ted Shawn, or both. Treating every folder title as her individual obra would manufacture authorship rather than document it. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **post-1931:** *Approximately twenty-five post-Denishawn choreographic projects*. These range from short dances to pageants and full-evening ballets and include completed, proposed, and possibly unrealized objects. The public finding aid names only a subset, so the remainder must stay an acknowledged but unitemized body rather than acquire invented titles or dates. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **undated:** *Approximately forty-five music-score files and unidentified fragments*. Most accompanied works by St. Denis, Shawn, or Denishawn, but a score’s presence does not prove St. Denis composed either music or choreography. This is an archival constellation around her making, not forty-five additional works by her. [NYPL Ruth St. Denis papers](https://archives.nypl.org/dan/19676)

- **1900s–1968:** *Journals, essays, poems, lectures, choreographic notes, recordings, and performance ephemera in the UCLA Ruth St. Denis papers*. The collection confirms a much larger field of making than the published repertory, but archive series are not themselves equivalent to finished works. Untitled and fragmentary material must remain undesignated. [UCLA finding aid](https://www.oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark%3A%2F13030%2Fkt5j49p08s)
