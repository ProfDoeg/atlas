## Works by Charles John Huffam Dickens

### Novels and serial fictions

- **1836–1837:** *The Posthumous Papers of the Pickwick Club*. Comic episodic novel first issued in monthly parts; Dickens began as commentator on Robert Seymour’s sporting plates but transformed the commission into his own serial fiction. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1837–1839:** *The Adventures of Oliver Twist; or, The Parish Boy’s Progress*. Novel serialized in *Bentley’s Miscellany* while Dickens edited that journal; its serial and book manifestations differ because Dickens repeatedly revised the text. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1838–1839:** *The Life and Adventures of Nicholas Nickleby*. Monthly-part novel incorporating theatrical satire, melodrama and inset tales. [Dickens Project chronology](https://dickens.ucsc.edu/resources/dickens-chronology/)
- **1840–1841:** *The Old Curiosity Shop*. Weekly serial novel embedded in the larger periodical project *Master Humphrey’s Clock* before becoming a separate book. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1841:** *Barnaby Rudge: A Tale of the Riots of ’Eighty*. Historical novel serialized inside *Master Humphrey’s Clock*; Dickens had conceived versions of the project years earlier. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1843–1844:** *The Life and Adventures of Martin Chuzzlewit*. Monthly-part novel, including an American sequence shaped by Dickens’s recent travels. [Dickens Project chronology](https://dickens.ucsc.edu/resources/dickens-chronology/)
- **1846–1848:** *Dombey and Son*. Monthly-part novel published under the fuller original title *Dealings with the Firm of Dombey and Son: Wholesale, Retail and for Exportation*. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1849–1850:** *The Personal History, Adventures, Experience and Observation of David Copperfield the Younger*. Monthly-part Bildungsroman drawing selectively on Dickens’s life without being identical to autobiography. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1852–1853:** *Bleak House*. Monthly-part novel combining omniscient present-tense narration with Esther Summerson’s retrospective first-person account. [Dickens Project chronology](https://dickens.ucsc.edu/resources/dickens-chronology/)
- **1854:** *Hard Times: For These Times*. Industrial novel written for weekly serialization in *Household Words*, making it simultaneously fiction and an intervention in Dickens’s editorial project. [Dent edition description](https://aba.org.uk/assets/catalogues/265.pdf)
- **1855–1857:** *Little Dorrit*. Monthly-part novel centered on imprisonment, debt and institutional circumlocution. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1859:** *A Tale of Two Cities*. Historical novel first serialized weekly in *All the Year Round*; it also helped launch that journal. [Dickens Journals Online](https://www.buckingham.ac.uk/djo/)
- **1860–1861:** *Great Expectations*. Weekly serial novel in *All the Year Round*; Dickens replaced his original ending after Bulwer-Lytton objected, so the abandoned and published endings are rival manifestations of the project. [Dickens Journals Online](https://www.buckingham.ac.uk/djo/)
- **1864–1865:** *Our Mutual Friend*. Dickens’s last completed novel, issued in monthly parts. [Dickens Project chronology](https://dickens.ucsc.edu/resources/dickens-chronology/)
- **1870, unfinished:** *The Mystery of Edwin Drood*. Monthly-part novel left incomplete by Dickens’s death after six of the intended twelve numbers; later “continuations” are not Dickens’s work, and the intended solution remains disputed. [Kitton bibliography](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_minor_writings_of_Charles_Dickens;_a_bibliography_and_sketch_(IA_minorwritingsofc00kittrich).pdf)

### Christmas books: compact prose Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1843:** *A Christmas Carol in Prose: Being a Ghost Story of Christmas*. Christmas novella conceived as a union of prose, typography and commissioned illustration; Dickens closely supervised the physical book. [Comprehensive short-fiction bibliography](https://victorianweb.org/authors/dickens/pva/5.html)
- **1844:** *The Chimes: A Goblin Story of Some Bells that Rang an Old Year Out and a New Year In*. Illustrated Christmas novella and polemical social fable. [Comprehensive short-fiction bibliography](https://victorianweb.org/authors/dickens/pva/5.html)
- **1845:** *The Cricket on the Hearth: A Fairy Tale of Home*. Illustrated domestic Christmas novella. [Comprehensive short-fiction bibliography](https://victorianweb.org/authors/dickens/pva/5.html)
- **1846:** *The Battle of Life: A Love Story*. Illustrated Christmas novella without a supernatural agent, despite its place in the Christmas-book sequence. [Comprehensive short-fiction bibliography](https://victorianweb.org/authors/dickens/pva/5.html)
- **1848:** *The Haunted Man and the Ghost’s Bargain: A Fancy for Christmas-Time*. Illustrated supernatural novella and the final separate Christmas book. [Comprehensive short-fiction bibliography](https://victorianweb.org/authors/dickens/pva/5.html)

### Short fiction and composite story-projects

- **1833–1836:** *Sketches by Boz*. A developing corpus of urban sketches, tales and character studies—many first published separately and under different titles—collected in two series in 1836 and enlarged in 1839; it resists classification as either one work or merely a container. ### Nonfiction books, travel writing and historical narrative

- **1836:** *Sunday Under Three Heads*. Reform pamphlet published pseudonymously as by “Timothy Sparks,” opposing proposed restrictions on Sunday recreation; it functions as polemic rather than neutral description. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1842:** *American Notes for General Circulation*. Travel book and social critique produced from Dickens’s first American journey. [Kitton bibliography](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_minor_writings_of_Charles_Dickens;_a_bibliography_and_sketch_(IA_minorwritingsofc00kittrich).pdf)
- **1846:** *Pictures from Italy*. Travel writing assembled from letters first published in the *Daily News* under the collective title *Travelling Letters Written on the Road*. [Kitton bibliography](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_minor_writings_of_Charles_Dickens;_a_bibliography_and_sketch_(IA_minorwritingsofc00kittrich).pdf)
- **1851–1853:** *A Child’s History of England*. Didactic national history serialized in *Household Words* and subsequently issued in book form; a protocol-like teaching text, though strongly shaped by Dickens’s political judgments. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1860–1869:** *The Uncommercial Traveller*. A persona, periodical series and expanding book: thirty-seven essays appeared across several authorized manifestations, so no single contents list wholly defines the work during Dickens’s lifetime. [Dent journalism edition](https://aba.org.uk/assets/catalogues/265.pdf)

### Journalism and interventionist prose

- **1834–1836:** *Early newspaper journalism*. Reports, theatrical notices, political commentary and metropolitan sketches for the *Morning Chronicle*, *Evening Chronicle*, *Bell’s Life in London* and related papers; the corpus is bibliographically reconstructed and some unsigned attributions remain uncertain. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)
- **1837–1839:** *Contributions to Bentley’s Miscellany*. Fiction, editorial addresses, reviews and miscellanies produced while Dickens edited the magazine; anonymity and editorial rewriting make some boundaries of authorship uncertain. [Dent journalism edition](https://aba.org.uk/assets/catalogues/265.pdf)
- **1846:** *The Daily News*. Dickens conceived and briefly edited this daily newspaper and wrote its opening leader and other pieces; the newspaper is an editorial project and collective production, not solely his obra. [Dickens Project chronology](https://dickens.ucsc.edu/resources/dickens-chronology/)
- **1850–1859:** *Household Words*. Weekly journal founded, “conducted,” heavily revised and given a collective voice by Dickens; roughly 180 contributions are assigned to him in the Dent scholarly edition, but unsigned collaborative journalism resists clean item-by-item authorship. [Dickens Journals Online](https://www.buckingham.ac.uk/djo/)
- **1859–1870:** *All the Year Round*. Weekly journal founded, owned and conducted by Dickens, combining fiction, essays, investigation and anonymous collaborative writing; it is best treated as an editorial project rather than a single authored text. [Dickens Journals Online](https://www.buckingham.ac.uk/djo/)
- **1850–1870:** *Collected journalism*. The standard modern scholarly corpus comprises four Dent volumes edited by Michael Slater and, for the final volume, John Drew; it contains hundreds of leaders, reports, reviews, sketches and collaborations whose attribution is sometimes editorial rather than signed. [Dent edition bibliographical citation](https://pure.plymouth.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/38414144/2013chapman296152phd.pdf)
- **1845–1849, unfinished/private:** *Autobiographical Fragment*. Intermittently drafted account of Dickens’s childhood employment and family hardship, withheld in his lifetime and partially reproduced by John Forster; it is an abandoned autobiographical project rather than a completed book. [Oxford chronology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34631/chapter/295044354)

### Drama, operetta and theatrical alterations

- **1836:** *The Strange Gentleman*. Two-act comic burletta first performed in 1836 and printed in 1837; surviving prompt and licensing manuscripts are non-identical manifestations. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1836:** *The Village Coquettes*. Comic operetta with libretto and lyrics by Dickens and music by John Hullah; its script, score, songs and performance make it a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than simply a “book.” [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1837:** *Is She His Wife?; or, Something Singular!*. One-act comic burletta; its original undated pamphlet was long thought lost, complicating the textual record. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1838, unperformed in Dickens’s lifetime:** *The Lamplighter*. Farce surviving in a fair-copy manuscript and later recast as prose; the handwriting of that fair copy is not certainly Dickens’s. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1838:** *O’Thello*. Brief comic theatrical text preserved as an appendix in the complete scholarly edition; it resists the scale and autonomy normally implied by “play.” [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1842:** *Prologue to The Patrician’s Daughter*. Occasional verse written to act upon a specific stage performance, not an autonomous drama. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1844:** *Prologue to The Elder Brother*. Occasional theatrical prologue. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1848:** *Closing scene to Animal Magnetism*. Dickens’s alteration or supplementation of an existing farce; it resists treatment as an independent play. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1851:** *Mr. Nightingale’s Diary*. Farce first performed in Dickens’s amateur theatricals; Mark Lemon supplied an early draft and Dickens extensively reshaped it, so sole authorship is disputed or, at minimum, misleading. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1855:** *Prologue to The Lighthouse*. Occasional theatrical prologue for Wilkie Collins’s play. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1856–1866:** *The Frozen Deep*. Collaborative drama initiated by Dickens and Wilkie Collins: Collins drafted it, Dickens annotated and radically adapted it for performance, and Collins later prepared the printed text; it is a mutable theatrical project with inseparable authorship. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1857:** *Prologue to The Housekeeper*. Occasional theatrical prologue. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)
- **1867:** *No Thoroughfare*. Stage version co-created with Wilkie Collins from their Christmas story; drafts passed between the collaborators and the resulting textual history is explicitly described by its editors as chaotic. [Complete scholarly edition of the plays](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3366/jj.20829417)

### Verse, songs and rhymes

- **1836:** *Songs from The Village Coquettes*. Operetta lyrics made for musical setting by John Hullah; the songs belong both to the dramatic whole and to Dickens’s verse corpus. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1836–1837:** *Verse in The Pickwick Papers*. The songs and ballads *The Ivy Green*, *A Christmas Carol*, *Gabriel Grub’s Song* and *The Romance of Dick Turpin* are embedded performances within the novel rather than an independent poetry collection. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1838:** *Duet from The Lamplighter*. Song text belonging to the dramatic project. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1839:** *The Loving Ballad of Lord Bateman*. Comic ballad published with George Cruikshank; Dickens’s exact share was partly masked by pseudonymous presentation and collaborative framing. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1841:** *The Fine Old English Gentleman*. Political squib published in the *Examiner*, parodying an existing song form. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1841:** *The Quack Doctor’s Proclamation*. Political verse squib published in the *Examiner*. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1841:** *Subjects for Painters*. Political verse squib published in the *Examiner*. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **1844:** *The Hymn of the Wiltshire Labourers*. Protest poem responding to rural poverty; a rhetorical intervention as much as a lyric. [Kitton bibliography](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_minor_writings_of_Charles_Dickens;_a_bibliography_and_sketch_(IA_minorwritingsofc00kittrich).pdf)
- **1855:** *The Song of the Wreck*. Song written for Wilkie Collins’s *The Lighthouse*, inseparable in origin from theatrical performance and music. [Dickens bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens_bibliography)
- **Various dates:** *Verses, inscriptions, comic rhymes and occasional poetry*. A scattered corpus gathered posthumously in *The Complete Poems of Charles Dickens* and related editions; the posthumous collection is an editorial manifestation, not a book Dickens himself designed. [Kitton bibliography](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_minor_writings_of_Charles_Dickens;_a_bibliography_and_sketch_(IA_minorwritingsofc00kittrich).pdf)

### Public-reading texts and performances

- **1853–1870:** *The public readings*. Dickens transformed selected fiction into scripted, repeatedly revised performance texts and delivered them on British, Irish and American tours; the obra is therefore both manuscript adaptation and ephemeral performance, not merely a reading aloud of unchanged books. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1853–1870:** *A Christmas Carol: public-reading version*. Condensed and repeatedly annotated performance script made from the novella; Dickens’s prompt-copy records an evolving theatrical manifestation. [Dickens Project bibliography](https://dickens.ucsc.edu/education/a-little-book-about-a-christmas-carol/further-reading/)
- **1858–1870:** *The Pickwick Trial*. Public-reading adaptation from *The Pickwick Papers*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *The Story of Little Dombey*. Public-reading adaptation from *Dombey and Son*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *The Poor Traveller*. Public-reading adaptation of *The Story of Richard Doubledick*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *Mr. Chops, the Dwarf*. Public-reading adaptation from *Going into Society*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *The Boots at the Holly-Tree Inn*. Public-reading adaptation from Dickens’s portion of the collaborative Christmas number. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *Mrs. Gamp*. Public-reading adaptation from *Martin Chuzzlewit*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *David Copperfield*. Public-reading adaptation centered on selected episodes from the novel. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1858–1870:** *Nicholas Nickleby at the Yorkshire School*. Public-reading adaptation from the novel. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1866–1870:** *Doctor Marigold*. Public-reading version of Dickens’s Christmas-number fiction. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1868–1869:** *The Story of Little Nell*. Public-reading adaptation from *The Old Curiosity Shop*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1868–1869:** *The Boy at Mugby*. Public-reading adaptation from *Mugby Junction*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1868–1869:** *Barbox Brothers*. Public-reading adaptation from *Mugby Junction*. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)
- **1868–1869:** *Sikes and Nancy*. Violent public-reading adaptation from *Oliver Twist*; it became a distinct performance object through drastic cutting, rewriting and Dickens’s acting. [Scholarly reading editions](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letture_teatrali_di_Charles_Dickens)

### Speeches, letters and other acts of writing that resist “work” status

- **1837–1870:** *Public speeches and addresses*. Hundreds of after-dinner speeches, charitable appeals, political statements and commemorative addresses survive in reports and collected editions; each was an event and rhetorical act, often reconstructed from journalism rather than an authorially fixed text. [Speeches: Literary and Social](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/824)
- **1820–1870:** *The correspondence*. More than fourteen thousand known letters to roughly 2,500 correspondents form a vast documentary corpus rather than a single intended work; the twelve-volume Pilgrim Edition is the standard scholarly edition, but later discoveries mean that even it is not absolutely complete. [Pilgrim Trust](https://www.thepilgrimtrust.org.uk/cool_timeline/the-letters-of-charles-dickens/)
- **Throughout his career:** *Manuscript plans, number plans, memoranda and working notes*. Preparatory objects for novels, journalism, readings and performances; they document projects and compositional decisions but often resist being called independent works.
- **Throughout his career:** *Editorial revisions of other writers’ contributions*. Dickens cut, rewrote and combined anonymous material in his journals; these interventions made the journals’ collective voice, but the surviving evidence does not always permit a stable title-by-title division between author, collaborator and editor. [Dickens Journals Online](https://www.buckingham.ac.uk/djo/)
