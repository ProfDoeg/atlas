## Works by Ahmad al-Qalqashandi

### Shāfiʿī legal commentaries

- **Undated; probably early:** *al-Ghuyūth al-hawāmiʿ fī sharḥ Jāmiʿ al-mukhtaṣarāt wa-mukhtaṣarāt al-jawāmiʿ*. A commentary on Kamāl al-Dīn al-Nasāʾī’s compendium of Shāfiʿī law; the standard reference records uncertainty over its exact title, including the variant *al-Ghuyūth al-harāmī*. Its surviving extent and completeness are unclear. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf)

- **Undated; probably early:** *Commentary on al-Ḥāwī al-ṣaghīr fī al-furūʿ*. A Shāfiʿī legal commentary on Najm al-Dīn al-Qazwīnī’s treatise. The standard bibliography confirms the composition through later bibliographers but supplies no secure independent title, date, or statement of survival, so it resists treatment as a fully recoverable work. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf)

### Literary debates, commentary, and panegyric

- **Undated:** *Ḥilyat al-faḍl wa-zīnat al-karam fī al-mufākhara bayna al-sayf wa-l-qalam*. An *adab* debate in which sword and pen contend for precedence: a personified disputation rather than a descriptive treatise. It survives in manuscript, but its date is not securely established. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [al-Ziriklī notice reproduced by al-Raqq al-Manshūr](https://app.alreq.com/ar/Authors/Author/4778a9de-07a1-4689-f80f-08d77b5d93f1)

- **Undated; now lost:** *Kunh al-murād fī sharḥ Bānat Suʿād*. A literary commentary on Kaʿb ibn Zuhayr’s celebrated ode *Bānat Suʿād*. Al-Qalqashandi himself says that he composed it, but no copy is known to survive; it is therefore a securely attested lost work rather than an extant text. [Belyaev, “Arabic Manuscripts from Yemen in the Collections of Tashkent”](https://www.cdmy.org/cmy/cmy29.pdf)

- **Before or in 791/1389:** *al-Kawākib al-durriyya fī al-manāqib al-Badriyya*. A rhymed *maqāma* praising the secretarial profession and Badr al-Dīn ibn Faḍl Allāh, the chancery chief under whom al-Qalqashandi entered service. It survives embedded in *Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā* rather than as an independently transmitted book, so its status is that of a named composition incorporated into a larger obra. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [Bosworth, “A maqāma on secretaryship” bibliographical record](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf)

### Genealogical reference works

- **Undated; before *Qalāʾid al-jumān*:** *Nihāyat al-arab fī maʿrifat ansāb al-ʿArab*. A systematic genealogical and historical reference work on Arab peoples and tribes. Older bibliography produced confusion between Ahmad, his father, and his son, but comparison with al-Qalqashandi’s own cross-references supports attribution to Ahmad, author of *Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā*. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [Jurjī Zaydān, *Tārīkh ādāb al-lugha al-ʿarabiyya*](https://zaidanfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Jurji-Zaidan-Ta%CC%84ri%CC%84kh-A%CC%84da%CC%84b-al-Lugha-al-%E2%80%98Arabiyya_SMALL.pdf)

- **During the reign of al-Ẓāhir Barqūq, 1382–1399:** *Qalāʾid al-jumān fī al-taʿrīf bi-qabāʾil ʿArab al-zamān*. A practical register of the Arab tribes current in the author’s own time, expanding material treated more generally in *Nihāyat al-arab* and arranging it by a different plan. It must not be confused with the similarly titled chancery manual made by al-Qalqashandi’s son Muḥammad; older bibliographies sometimes conflate the two. [Iḥsān al-Naṣṣ, study of *Qalāʾid al-jumān*](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [Zaydān, *Tārīkh ādāb al-lugha al-ʿarabiyya*](https://zaidanfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Jurji-Zaidan-Ta%CC%84ri%CC%84kh-A%CC%84da%CC%84b-al-Lugha-al-%E2%80%98Arabiyya_SMALL.pdf)

### Chancery protocols and encyclopedic statecraft

- **Before 805/1402–03; subsequently reworked or transmitted in more than one state:** *Ḍawʾ al-ṣubḥ al-musfir wa-janā al-dawḥ al-muthmir*. A compact chancery manual associated with Jamāl al-Dīn al-Bārizī and closely related to *Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā*. Scholarship disagrees over whether the surviving two-volume form is the preliminary manual from which *Ṣubḥ* grew or a later abridgment completed near 821/1418; only its first volume was printed in 1906, while manuscript evidence preserves more of the project. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [Mamluk Bibliography Online](https://mamluk.lib.uchicago.edu/mamluk-primary.php?caller=3&limit=50&op=AND&searchauthor0=&searchauthor1=&searchauthor2=&searchlanguage=&searchsubject=Administrative+Manuals&searchtitle0=&searchtitle1=&searchtitle2=&start=1); [Bauden, *Mamluk Cairo, a Crossroads for Embassies*](https://dokumen.pub/mamluk-cairo-a-crossroads-for-embassies-studies-on-diplomacy-and-diplomatics-9789004384637-9004384634.html)

- **805–814 AH / 1402–1412; with possible additions thereafter:** *Ṣubḥ al-aʿshā fī ṣināʿat al-inshāʾ*. A monumental chancery encyclopedia and working protocol for state secretaries, joining geography, history, genealogy, titulature, calligraphy, cryptography, postal practice, and documentary models. Although its main composition was completed in 814 AH, blank spaces and later material suggest that al-Qalqashandi treated it as an extensible project rather than an absolutely closed book. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [*Encyclopedia of Arabic Literature*](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [Mamluk Bibliography Online](https://mamluk.lib.uchicago.edu/mamluk-primary.php?caller=4&limit=50&op=AND&searchauthor0=al-Qalqashand%C4%AB%2C+Shih%C4%81b+al-D%C4%ABn+A%E1%B8%A5mad+ibn+%CA%BFAl%C4%AB+%281355+or+6-1418%29&searchauthor1=&searchauthor2=&searchlanguage=&searchsubject=&searchtitle0=&searchtitle1=&searchtitle2=&start=1)

- **After 815 AH / 1413 and before 821 AH / 1418:** *Maʾāthir al-ināfa fī maʿālim al-khilāfa*. An encyclopedia of the caliphate combining constitutional protocol, qualifications and succession, insignia and ceremony, and historical accounts of the caliphs. Its presentation manuscript was prepared for the contemporary Abbasid caliph in Cairo; the dating follows the tenure of the named chancery patron rather than an explicit completion statement. [Bosworth, “al-Ḳalḳashandī”](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/11/DKM110249.pdf); [Bauden, manuscript and presentation-copy discussion](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/166184/1/Maqriziana%20XIII.pdf)
