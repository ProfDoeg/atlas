## Works by Oskar Alfred Victor Schlemmer

### Paintings, watercolours, pastels, drawings, prints, and sculpture

- **1908–1943:** *Paintings, watercolours, pastels, and sculpture*. This is a large corpus rather than one readily enumerable “work”: Karin von Maur’s 1979 catalogue raisonné is the standard published inventory, superseding much of Hans Hildebrandt’s 1952 catalogue. It is not now complete or wholly reliable—the Schlemmer estate reports omitted, subsequently discovered, wrongly attributed, and internally reclassified objects—so no honest public list can claim final completeness. [[Catalogue history](https://www.schlemmer.org/catalogue-raisonne)]

- **1908–1943:** *Drawings and graphic work*. Tut Schlemmer’s 1965 *Oskar Schlemmer: Zeichnungen und Graphik* is the standard œuvre catalogue for this extensive corpus; treating every sheet as a separately titled work would force titles upon numerous studies and untitled drawings. [[Catalogue history](https://www.schlemmer.org/catalogue-raisonne)]

- **1912:** *Vor dem Kloster*. An oil painting in which landscape and architecture are already being reduced to planar structure. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1912:** *Männlicher Kopf I (Selbstbildnis)*. An oil self-portrait, distinct from the following year’s similarly titled second head. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1913:** *Männlicher Kopf II (Selbstbildnis)*. An oil self-portrait catalogued separately from *Männlicher Kopf I*. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1913:** *Mädchenkopf mit Silber*. A painted head on paper mounted on board. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1913:** *Rote Dächer—Jagdschloss Grunewald*. A painting belonging to Schlemmer’s early series of architectonically organized landscapes. [[Mercedes-Benz Art Collection](https://art.daimler.com/media/Werktexte.pdf)]

- **1916:** *Homo*. A painting that establishes the simplified profile figure which Schlemmer repeatedly transformed in later media. [[German Historical Museum biography](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/biografie/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1919/1923:** *Ornamentale Plastik auf geteiltem Rahmen*. A relief sculpture revised over several years; its division between image, relief, and architectural frame makes it resist classification as either autonomous sculpture or painting. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart overview](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1919–1923:** *Abstrakter Kopf*. A series of sculptural heads rather than one securely singular object; individual versions, casts, and dates must be distinguished through the catalogue raisonné.

- **1920:** *Drei Köpfe, ineinander*. A painting or graphic composition in which three heads occupy one interlocking construction. [[MoMA artist inventory](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **c. 1920; published 1923:** *Spiel mit Köpfen*. A portfolio of lithographic permutations of three profiles, including *Drei senkrechte Profile*, *Drei Profile zwischen der Senkrechten und der Diagonalen*, and *Drei Profile, das mittlere senkrecht*. It is simultaneously one serial obra and a set of individually catalogued prints. [[MoMA artist inventory](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1921:** *Konzentrische Gruppe: Figurenplan K1*. A lithographic “figure plan” issued in the Bauhaus portfolio *Neue europäische Graphik*; it turns a group of bodies into a diagrammatic spatial order. [[MoMA artist inventory](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1921–1923:** *Abstrakte Figur*. A sculptural construction of the human figure; versions and related studies exist, so the title should not be assumed to identify only one physical object. [[Schlemmer estate overview](https://www.schlemmer.org/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1921; published 1922:** *Figur von der Seite: Figur H2, Sitzende*. A print from the first *Neue europäische Graphik* portfolio. Its publication date and the date assigned to its execution differ in museum records. [[MoMA artist inventory](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1922; published 1923:** *Abstrakte Figur, nach links: Figur S*. A Bauhaus portfolio print presenting the body as a geometric type rather than an individualized sitter. [[MoMA artist inventory](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1922–1923:** *Tänzerin (Die Geste)*. A large oil-and-tempera painting in which the performing body becomes a spatial sign. [[German Historical Museum biography](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/biografie/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1923:** *Paracelsus (Der Gesetzgeber)*. An oil-and-lacquer painting that joins the historical name “Paracelsus” to Schlemmer’s generalized figure type. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1924:** *Vorübergehender*. A painting of a passing figure, listed among Schlemmer’s major works of that year. [[OLATS chronology](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/biographie.php)]

- **1924:** *Sinnender*. A painting of a meditative figure, classified in the catalogue of paintings rather than as a portrait of a named sitter. [[OLATS chronology](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/biographie.php)]

- **1924:** *Frauentreppe*. A painting that develops the staircase as both architecture and choreography. [[OLATS chronology](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/biographie.php)]

- **1925:** *Römisches (Fünf Figuren im Raum)*. An oil painting also encountered under the variant titles *Vier Figuren im Raum (Römisches)* and *Fünf Figuren (Römisches)*; the title and even the enumerated number of figures are not stable across sources. [[Schlemmer estate overview](https://www.schlemmer.org/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1925:** *Ruheraum*. An oil painting whose assembled figures and architectural enclosure form a pictorial “rest room,” not an actual interior-design commission. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1925:** *Konzentrische Gruppe*. A painting that translates the earlier diagrammatic figure-plan idea into a spatially ordered group. [[OLATS chronology](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/biographie.php)]

- **1925:** *Akt, Frau und Eintretender*. A figure-group painting; translations and secondary references sometimes distort the final noun, so the German title should be retained. [[OLATS chronology](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/biographie.php)]

- **1925:** *Halbfigur nach links*. An oil-and-tempera painting catalogued by von Maur as no. 129; its museum dating is no more precise than “twentieth century.” [[Städel Museum](https://sammlung.staedelmuseum.de/de/werk/halbfigur-nach-links)]

- **1928:** *Tischgesellschaft*. A painting of figures ordered around a table, often treated as one of Schlemmer’s principal investigations of collective bodies in architectural space.

- **1931:** *Szene am Geländer*. A large oil painting, also rendered in English as *Scene at the Railing*. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart collection](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1932:** *Bauhaustreppe*. An oil painting made in response to the closing political threat to the Bauhaus; it is a painted condensation of Bauhaus life, not a design for the staircase itself. [[Museum of Modern Art](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1932:** *Treppenszene*. A separate oil painting on fabric mounted to plywood, not an alternate title for *Bauhaustreppe*. [[Hamburger Kunsthalle record via Artothek](https://www.artothek.de/de/bilder-fotos/Schlemmer%20%20Oskar.html)]

- **1936:** *Dunkle Gruppe*. A painting, and the name commonly extended to a group of Schlemmer’s darker, politically shadowed figure compositions; the individual canvas and the broader “dark group” should not be conflated. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart overview](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1936:** *Figuren im Raum*. A painting continuing his figure-and-architecture project under increasingly constrained working conditions.

- **1940–1942:** *Wuppertaler Stadtansichten*. A body of watercolours and paintings made during Schlemmer’s Wuppertal years, not a single fixed cycle with an authoritative sequence. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart overview](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1942:** *Wuppertaler Fensterbilder*. Schlemmer’s final sustained series: variations on figures and interiors seen through or bounded by window frames. Individual sheets have catalogue titles such as *Fensterbild VI*, but the series itself is also a coherent obra. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart overview](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

### Murals, walls, interiors, and architecture-bound works

- **1914:** *Wall paintings for the main hall of the Deutsche Werkbund exhibition, Cologne*. An architecture-bound collective commission executed under Adolf Hölzel with Willi Baumeister and Hermann Stenner; assigning the whole decoration solely to Schlemmer would erase its collaborative authorship. [[Buchheim Museum chronology](https://sammlung.buchheimmuseum.de/kuenstler/schlemmer-oskar)]

- **1923:** *Wall design for the Bauhaus workshop building, Weimar*. A Gesamtkunstwerk-like ensemble of murals, reliefs, and painted architectural elements, including the spiral-stair wall composition; the Nazi government destroyed or covered the originals, and what visitors now see includes later reconstruction. [[German Historical Museum biography](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/biografie/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1924:** *Wall paintings for Adolf Meyer’s house*. An architecture-bound domestic commission; surviving information treats it as a mural ensemble rather than a portable painting. [[OLATS chronology](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/biographie.php)]

- **1928–1930:** *Folkwang cycle*. A mural project for the fountain room of the Museum Folkwang in Essen, generally known through the related titles *Figurinenfries* and *Treppenhausgestaltung*. Its destruction under National Socialism means that designs, photographs, fragments, and reconstructions are manifestations of a lost architecture-bound obra, not the original itself.

- **c. 1930:** *Wandfries im Haus Mendelsohn, Berlin*. An unrealized mural project: the surviving study is an object, while the intended wall frieze remained a project and should not be described as an executed commission. [[Mercedes-Benz Art Collection](https://art.daimler.com/media/FROM-THE-ARCHIVES_Oskar-Schlemmer.pdf)]

- **1940:** *Familie*. A private mural made for Dieter Keller’s house while Schlemmer was officially marginalized; it was executed in wax colours and bronzes on prepared canvas attached to a plaster wall and was later detached. [[German Federal Cultural Foundation](https://www.kulturstiftung.de/die-famlilie-kehrt-zurueck/)]

- **1940–1942:** *Modulation und Patina*. Experimental lacquer panels made at the Wuppertal paint factory with Willi Baumeister and others. They resist the autonomous-work category: produced without signatures under the cover of materials research and sometimes distributed as promotional samples, they are simultaneously experiments, industrial objects, and covert artistic manifestations. [[Kunstmuseum Stuttgart overview](https://sammlung.kunstmuseum-stuttgart.de/person/oskar-schlemmer)]

### Gesamtkunstwerk, dance, pantomime, and stage experiment

- **1912–1932:** *Das Triadische Ballett*. A continually revised Gesamtkunstwerk of choreography, sculptural costume, colour, music, light, and moving bodies: partial performances occurred before the complete 1922 Stuttgart première, followed by revised manifestations. Schlemmer was not its uncontested sole originator—the dancers Albert Burger and Elsa Hötzel and his brother Carl Schlemmer were collaborators in its long development—so “by Oskar Schlemmer” is shorthand, not a complete authorship statement. [[Schlemmer estate](https://www.schlemmer.org/triadic-ballet)] [[1925 Bauhaus book](https://www.bauhaus-bookshelf.org/bauhausbuecher-4-oskar-schlemmer-die-buehne-im-bauhaus-pdf-1925.html)]

- **1922–1923:** *Das figurale Kabinett*. An experimental stage work, also called a *mechanical cabaret*, built from moving figures, colour, light, and sound rather than conventional dramatic characters. It is both performance and apparatus, and surviving visual designs should not be mistaken for the vanished event. [[MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1924:** *Meta, oder die Pantomime der Orte*. A pantomime organized around places and stage-space rather than literary narrative; its status lies between performance score, experiment, and partly recoverable project. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1925–1929:** *Treppenwitz*. A staircase pantomime or “stair joke” incorporating a musical clown; it remained mutable across Bauhaus manifestations rather than becoming one fixed dramatic text. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1926:** *Raumtanz I und II*. Two “space dances” in which colour-coded bodies trace the geometry and tempo of the stage. Their work-status belongs as much to protocol and demonstration as to repeatable choreography. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1926–1929:** *Musikalischer Clown I und II*. Related clown pieces developed in more than one version; the overlapping dates signal revision rather than two immutable scripts. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1926:** *Gestentanz I und II*. Gesture dances reducing action to a vocabulary of bodily signs; they function as choreographic protocols and performances rather than literary works. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1926:** *Formentanz*. A dance in which bodies manipulate primary geometric forms, making costume, prop, movement, and spatial experiment inseparable. [[Bauhaus Dances](https://bauhausdances.org/)]

- **1926:** *Frauentanz (Drei barocke Tänze)*. A women’s dance also known as *Three Baroque Dances*; the alternative title marks a performance grouping rather than a securely independent written score. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1926:** *Kulissentanz*. A “flats dance” in which movable scenery becomes a performer, blurring dance, stage machinery, and visual composition. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1927:** *Raumlineatur mit Figur*. A performance-demonstration that makes an otherwise invisible spatial grid tangible around a human figure. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1927:** *Schattenpantomime*. A shadow pantomime using figures and translucent partitions; its medium is projected manifestation rather than durable object. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1927:** *Lichtspiel*. An experimental play of light and projected shadow whose “work” is the organized event, not merely surviving diagrams or photographs. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1927:** *Baukastenspiel*. A construction-set or block play in which performers and modular elements continually rebuild the stage picture; it also circulated in a related form as *Bauprobleme*, making its boundaries unstable. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1927:** *Stäbetanz*. A pole dance in which rods attached to the performer extend bodily proportions into architectural space. It is a choreographic protocol realized through a particular costume-apparatus. [[Bauhaus Dances](https://bauhausdances.org/)]

- **1927–1928:** *Reifentanz*. A hoop dance whose moving circular apparatus creates changing geometric figures against the dark stage. More than one part or version is documented. [[Dance Notation Bureau](https://www.dancenotation.org/catalog/bauhaus-dances/)]

- **1927:** *Äquilibristik*. A balancing or equilibristic stage study; documentation is too limited to pretend that a complete, fixed choreography survives. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1927:** *Chorische Pantomime (Maskenchor)*. A choral pantomime or “mask chorus” in which collective visual action supersedes individualized character. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1929:** *Glastanz*. A dance developed from the optical and material properties of glass, light, costume, and movement. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1929:** *Metalltanz*. A dance-manifestation made from reflective metal, light, sound, and a moving masked body. Its surviving costume and photographs are remains of the work, not equivalents of the performed event. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1929:** *Malmaschine*. A theatrical “painting machine” that makes production itself into spectacle; it resists separation into prop, mechanical apparatus, action, and visual composition. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

- **1941:** *Reigen in Lack*. A late lacquer “round dance” or *Lackballett* made under industrial conditions at Wuppertal. It belongs simultaneously to performance, lacquer experiment, and collective factory work, and its authorship should not be detached from that context. [[OLATS stage-work list](https://archive.olats.org/pionniers/pp/schlemmer/scene.php)]

### Designs for other authors’ theatre and music

- **1921:** *Mörder, Hoffnung der Frauen*. Stage and costume design for Paul Hindemith’s opera after Oskar Kokoschka; Schlemmer made the visual manifestation, not the libretto or music. [[Museo Reina Sofía](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/sites/default/files/salas/informacion/206_oskar_schlemmer_ing.pdf)]

- **1921:** *Das Nusch-Nuschi*. Stage and costume design for Hindemith’s opera after Franz Blei; authorship is therefore distributed among composer, dramatist, performers, and Schlemmer’s visual conception. [[Museo Reina Sofía](https://www.museoreinasofia.es/sites/default/files/salas/informacion/206_oskar_schlemmer_ing.pdf)]

- **1924:** *Die Nachtigall*. Designs and figurines for Igor Stravinsky’s opera *Le Rossignol*. These are stage designs and costume figures, not authorship of Stravinsky’s musical work; some plans remained projects rather than fully realized productions. [[Schlemmer estate overview](https://www.schlemmer.org/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1920s:** *Renard*. Stage and costume designs for Stravinsky’s burlesque; again, Schlemmer’s obra is the proposed visual and bodily staging, not the pre-existing musical composition. [[Schlemmer estate overview](https://www.schlemmer.org/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1920s:** *Les Noces*. Stage and costume designs for Stravinsky’s dance cantata. The designs survive more securely than evidence of a complete Schlemmer production, so this is best called a project. [[Schlemmer estate overview](https://www.schlemmer.org/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1930:** *Die glückliche Hand*. Stage design for Arnold Schoenberg’s drama with music at the Kroll Opera, Schlemmer’s last stage design known to have reached performance. Schoenberg remained author of music and libretto; Schlemmer made its visual-spatial manifestation. [[Stage-design record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oskar_Schlemmer_Die_gl%C3%BCckliche_Hand_1930.jpg)]

### Graphic design, typography, and exhibition objects

- **1913:** *Plakat, Neuer Kunstsalon, Stuttgart*. A poster for the exhibition space Schlemmer founded with his brother Wilhelm; it is a public graphic object rather than a painting subsequently reproduced as a poster. [[MoMA](https://www.moma.org/artists/5219-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1922:** *Bauhaus-Signet*. The profile-head emblem designed for the Bauhaus, a reproducible institutional mark rather than a unique autonomous artwork. [[German Historical Museum biography](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/biografie/oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1923:** *Postkarte für die Bauhaus-Ausstellung*. A printed postcard designed for the Weimar Bauhaus exhibition; its multiplication is integral to the obra. [[MoMA Bauhaus checklist](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_checklist_1783_300062926.pdf)]

- **1923:** *Design contributions to Staatliches Bauhaus in Weimar 1919–1923*. Schlemmer contributed text and original lithographic material to the Bauhaus’s collective programmatic book; the volume as a whole is not solely his work. [[Bauhaus exhibition history](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhausausstellung_von_1923)]

- **1926:** *Plakat für die Große Brücken-Revue*. A theatre poster whose proper category is graphic publicity, not the revue itself.

- **1926:** *Das Weiße Fest*. A collectively produced Bauhaus festival supervised within Schlemmer’s stage sphere. It resists individual authorship: decoration, costume, music, architecture, and social performance were made by a community rather than by Schlemmer alone. [[Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/overview/bauhaus-1)]

- **1928:** *Bart-Nasen-Herzensfest*. A themed Bauhaus festival developed collectively by students and masters; it is a social Gesamtkunstwerk and manifestation, not a securely bounded single-author work. [[Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/overview/bauhaus-1)]

- **1929:** *Das Metallische Fest*. A collectively constructed Bauhaus festival transforming the Dessau building through reflective material, costume, music, and dance. Schlemmer’s association with it does not justify sole attribution. [[Routledge Encyclopedia of Modernism](https://www.rem.routledge.com/articles/overview/bauhaus-1)]

### Texts, manifestos, protocols, and teaching projects

- **1922:** *Ballett?* A short programmatic text concerning modern ballet, later preserved in the documentary literature surrounding *Das Triadische Ballett*. It acts partly as a manifesto rather than simply describing an already settled work.

- **1923:** *Contributions to Staatliches Bauhaus in Weimar 1919–1923*. Programmatic writing for the collective Bauhaus publication; it should not be expanded into a separate Schlemmer-authored “book.” [[Bauhaus exhibition history](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhausausstellung_von_1923)]

- **1924; published 1925:** *Mensch und Kunstfigur*. Schlemmer’s central manifesto-theory of the human body’s transformation by costume, abstraction, architecture, and stage space. It was published as his contribution to the collective volume *Die Bühne im Bauhaus*. [[Digital 1925 edition](https://www.bauhaus-bookshelf.org/bauhausbuecher-4-oskar-schlemmer-die-buehne-im-bauhaus-pdf-1925.html)]

- **1925:** *Die Bühne im Bauhaus*. Bauhausbuch 4, jointly made by Schlemmer, László Moholy-Nagy, and Farkas Molnár under the series editorship of Walter Gropius and Moholy-Nagy. Calling it simply “Schlemmer’s book” conceals its composite authorship, typography, and documentation of several makers’ stage projects. [[Gebr. Mann Verlag](https://www.reimer-mann-verlag.de/controller.php?cmd=detail&titelnummer=302816&verlag=3)]

- **1925:** *Der theatralische Kostümtanz*. A programmatic essay on theatrical costume-dance published in the *Europa-Almanach*; it articulates a method for making stage bodies rather than narrating a drama. [[Primary-text bibliography](https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3156/files/doitubungaku_18_43_64.pdf)]

- **1926:** *Tänzerische Mathematik*. A theoretical essay treating dance as a calculable ordering of body, time, and space. Its title signals neither a choreography nor a mathematical textbook but a compact manifesto of method. [[Primary-text bibliography](https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3156/files/doitubungaku_18_43_64.pdf)]

- **1926:** *Die Bühne im Bauhaus*. An article distinct from the similarly titled 1925 Bauhaus book; edition records must not collapse them into one text. [[Bauhaus stage bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhausb%C3%BChne)]

- **1927:** *Bühne*. An article published in the Bauhaus journal, again distinct from Bauhausbuch 4. [[Bauhaus stage bibliography](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauhausb%C3%BChne)]

- **1928:** *Abstraktion in Tanz und Kostüm*. A theoretical text on abstraction through costume and dance, known through later documentary publication. [[Primary-text bibliography](https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3156/files/doitubungaku_18_43_64.pdf)]

- **1928–1929:** *Der Mensch*. A Bauhaus course and teaching project joining formal drawing, mechanics, anatomy, biology, philosophy, and metaphysics. Its surviving notes are protocols and documentary residues of instruction, not a finished book authored for publication. [[Bauhaus Kooperation](https://bauhauskooperation.de/en/knowledge/the-bauhaus/training/curriculum/classes-by-oskar-schlemmer)]

- **1931:** *Bühnen-Elemente*. A lecture delivered at the Kunstgewerbemuseum Zürich; its later printed form is the publication of a performed text, not evidence that Schlemmer issued it as an independent book. [[Primary-text bibliography](https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3156/files/doitubungaku_18_43_64.pdf)]

- **1932:** *Perspektiven*. A lecture later printed in von Maur’s monograph. The surviving publication is posthumous mediation of the lecture-text, not an original 1932 edition. [[Primary-text bibliography](https://glim-re.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/3156/files/doitubungaku_18_43_64.pdf)]

- **1912–1943; posthumously edited:** *Letters, diaries, and writings*. These documents are unquestionably texts Schlemmer made, but they resist treatment as a single work: later volumes such as *Briefe und Tagebücher* and *Idealist der Form* are editorial constructions assembled after his death, not books he designed or completed. [[Bibliographic record](https://sitterwerk-prod-public.s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/books/bib_GM00376262_20180618_134830.pdf)]
