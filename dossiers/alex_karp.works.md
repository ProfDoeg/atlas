## Works by Alexander Caedmon Karp

### Dissertation

- **2002; repository record dated 2003:** *Aggression in der Lebenswelt: Die Erweiterung des Parsonsschen Konzepts der Aggression durch die Beschreibung des Zusammenhangs von Jargon, Aggression und Kultur*. German-language doctoral dissertation in social theory, submitted at Goethe University Frankfurt under Karola Brede; the difference between the reported degree year and the repository’s 22 May 2003 date reflects distinct completion and cataloguing/publication events, not two works.

### Philanthropy research

- **2001:** *A Comparative Study of Mega Gifts: Jewish & Non-Jewish Donors*. A co-authored research report by Gary A. Tobin, Alex Karp, Ayo Griffin, and Aryeh Weinberg. Its existence is documented through a citation in a later Institute for Jewish & Community Research paper, but no complete copy or definitive publication record was located; its title and status should therefore be treated as bibliographically provisional.

- **2003:** *Mega-Gifts in American Philanthropy: General and Jewish Giving Patterns Between 1995–2000*. A co-authored quantitative research report by Karp, Jeffrey R. Solomon, and Gary A. Tobin, rather than a sole-authored book; contemporary reporting identifies this Alex Karp as the chief executive of the Jewish Philanthropy Partnership.

- **2004:** *An Exceptional Nation: American Philanthropy Is Different Because America Is Different*. A co-authored policy essay by Karp, Gary A. Tobin, and Aryeh K. Weinberg, issued by the Philanthropy Roundtable.

- **2005:** *American Mega-Giving: A Comparison to Global Disaster Relief*. A co-authored research paper by Gary A. Tobin, Alex C. Karp, and Aryeh K. Weinberg. It belongs to the authors’ continuing mega-gifts research project rather than constituting a conventional monograph.

- **Announced for 2005, publication unverified:** *Mega-Gifts in American Philanthropy, Volume II: 2001*. A projected report by Gary A. Tobin, Alexander C. Karp, and Aryeh Weinberg, cited as “to be published” in *American Mega-Giving*; because no completed publication was found, this is a **project**, not securely an obra.

- **Announced for 2006, publication unverified:** *Mega-Gifts in American Philanthropy, Volume III: 2002–2003*. Another projected continuation named in *American Mega-Giving*. Its completion could not be verified, so it likewise resists classification as a finished work.

### Public arguments and manifestos

- **2019:** *I’m a Tech CEO, and I Don’t Think Tech CEOs Should Be Making Policy*. A signed Washington Post opinion essay arguing that consequential public decisions should remain with elected institutions; its imperative political purpose makes it partly a manifesto rather than merely commentary.

- **2023:** *Our Oppenheimer Moment: The Creation of A.I. Weapons*. A signed New York Times guest essay advocating Western development and control of military artificial intelligence; it functions as both an essay and a call to action.

- **2024:** *New Weapons Will Eclipse Atomic Bombs. Their Builders Ask Themselves This Question*. A Washington Post opinion essay co-authored with Nicholas W. Zamiska about autonomous weapons, deterrence, and the obligations of American technologists; it is an immediate precursor to the argument of *The Technological Republic*.

- **2025:** *Why Silicon Valley Lost Its Patriotism*. A co-authored Atlantic essay by Karp and Zamiska. It condenses their historical and political argument into manifesto form and appeared immediately before their book.

### Book-length manifesto

- **2025:** *The Technological Republic: Hard Power, Soft Belief, and the Future of the West*. A co-authored nonfiction book by Karp and Nicholas W. Zamiska, published by Crown Currency on 18 February 2025. Although marketed as a treatise, it is also explicitly programmatic—a manifesto urging renewed collaboration among the technology sector, government, and the military.

### Corporate epistles

- **2020–present:** *Letters to Shareholders*. An ongoing body of signed quarterly and annual communications issued in Karp’s capacity as Palantir’s chief executive. These are legally situated corporate communications but frequently operate as political and technological manifestos; because Palantir continually adds letters and translations to its site, the body of work is open-ended and should not be mistaken for a fixed collected edition.

### Institutional and technological projects

- **2003–present:** *Palantir Technologies*. A company and continuing technological project co-founded by Karp with Peter Thiel, Stephen Cohen, Joe Lonsdale, and Nathan Gettings. It is not adequately described as Karp’s individual “work”: its software platforms and institutional identity are collective, continuously revised creations, while Karp’s documented role is co-founder and chief executive rather than sole designer.
