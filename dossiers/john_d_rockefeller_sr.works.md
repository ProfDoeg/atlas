## Works by John Davison Rockefeller Sr.

### Account books and archival texts

- **1855 onward:** *Ledger A*. A personal financial ledger begun when Rockefeller was sixteen, recording income, expenses, loans, and charitable gifts. It is an administrative text rather than a literary work, but its sustained system of accounting became a practical protocol for both business and philanthropy. ([PBS, Rockefeller timeline](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/rockefellers-timeline/))

- **1877–1918:** *Outgoing letterbooks*. A large corpus of copied business, philanthropic, and personal correspondence preserved at the Rockefeller Archive Center. It resists treatment as one obra: the archival index establishes thousands of separately occasioned texts, not a deliberately composed collected work. ([Rockefeller Archive Center collection record](https://dimes.rockarch.org/collections/S9tUZrHS9UnixnGkvc5iod?limit=40); [RAC survey citing the letterbook index](https://www.avenuesassy.com/documents/afroam.pdf))

- **Late nineteenth century–1937:** *Correspondence with John D. Rockefeller Jr.* An epistolary body of work jointly made by father and son, selectively edited after their deaths as *“Dear Father”/“Dear Son.”* The published volume is Joseph W. Ernst’s selection and arrangement, not a book Rockefeller Sr. himself conceived; the underlying letters remain individual archival texts. ([Rockefeller University repository record](https://digitalcommons.rockefeller.edu/the-rockefellers/26/); [Fordham volume description](https://books.google.com/books/about/Dear_Father_dear_Son.html?id=Ta_y9iUHg8UC))

### Published texts

- **1908–1909:** *Some Random Reminiscences of Men and Events*. A seven-part autobiographical and apologetic article series published monthly in *The World’s Work* from October 1908 through April 1909. It is the first manifestation of the memoir later issued as a book, not seven wholly independent works. ([American Heritage](https://www.americanheritage.com/rockefeller-remembers); [Wellcome Collection](https://content.www.wellcomecollection.org/works/fbbpzq52))

- **1909:** *Random Reminiscences of Men and Events*. A memoir of business, philanthropy, and associates, developed from the *World’s Work* series. Although conventionally called an autobiography and published under Rockefeller’s name, it is selective public self-presentation rather than a comprehensive life narrative. ([1909 text and publication data](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/17090/17090-h/17090-h.htm); [1984 Rockefeller Archive Center edition record](https://books.google.com/books?id=u0BRAQAAMAAJ))

- **1917:** *Giving Away Money*. A short magazine article in *Farm and Fireside* presenting Rockefeller’s ideas about organized philanthropy. It is a prescriptive argument—part manifesto for systematic giving—rather than a memoir or freestanding book. ([bibliography in Ron Chernow’s *Titan*](https://erenow.org/common/lifeofjohnrockefeller/40.php))

### Commercial organizations and systems

- **1857–1858:** *Clark & Rockefeller*. A commission-merchant partnership created with Maurice B. Clark to trade produce and other goods. As a jointly owned operating organization, it is a commercial obra rather than an authored text. ([Encyclopedia of Cleveland History](https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/clark-maurice-b))

- **1858–1863:** *Clark, Gardner & Rockefeller*. The enlarged successor to Clark & Rockefeller after George W. Gardner entered the firm. Its boundaries are legal and organizational, and consequently it is better understood as a new manifestation of the mercantile project than as an entirely independent creative work.

- **1863–1865:** *Andrews, Clark & Company*. Rockefeller, the Clark brothers, and Samuel Andrews’s first oil-refining partnership. Authorship is necessarily collective: Rockefeller supplied capital and organization, while Andrews supplied crucial refining knowledge. ([Encyclopedia of Cleveland History](https://case.edu/ech/articles/c/clark-maurice-b))

- **1865–1867:** *Rockefeller & Andrews*. The refining partnership formed after Rockefeller and Andrews bought out the Clark interests. It was both an enterprise and an intermediate organizational manifestation in the development of Standard Oil. ([Encyclopedia of Cleveland History](https://case.edu/ech/articles/a/andrews-samuel))

- **1867–1870:** *Rockefeller, Andrews & Flagler*. A partnership incorporating Henry M. Flagler and, through the associated business, William Rockefeller. It was collectively made and should not be attributed to Rockefeller Sr. alone. ([Library of Congress business-history guide](https://guides.loc.gov/this-month-in-business-history/january/standard-oil-established))

- **1870:** *Standard Oil Company of Ohio*. A corporation co-founded by Rockefeller, William Rockefeller, Samuel Andrews, Henry Flagler, and Stephen V. Harkness. Rockefeller was its principal organizer, but the company—and the coercive competitive system it developed—was a collective institutional construction, not a solitary work. ([Library of Congress](https://guides.loc.gov/this-month-in-business-history/january/standard-oil-established))

- **1882:** *Standard Oil Trust*. A legal and administrative structure that placed numerous nominally separate companies under nine trustees. This organizational protocol is one of Rockefeller’s most consequential creations, although it was devised and executed with lawyers, partners, shareholders, and fellow trustees rather than authored by him alone.

### Philanthropic and educational institutions

- **1889–1910:** *The new University of Chicago*. Rockefeller’s founding commitment and subsequent gifts made the university’s creation financially possible; the university officially calls him its “Founder.” It nevertheless resists sole attribution: the American Baptist Education Society, Frederick T. Gates, William Rainey Harper, faculty, trustees, and donors collectively designed and built the institution. ([University of Chicago Library](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/collex/exhibits/building-long-future/john-d-rockefeller/); [University of Chicago founding-correspondence guide](https://www.lib.uchicago.edu/ead/rlg/ICU.SPCL.ROCKEFELLER.pdf))

- **1901:** *Rockefeller Institute for Medical Research*. A research institution created by Rockefeller with Frederick T. Gates and John D. Rockefeller Jr., now Rockefeller University. It is an institutional project made to continue producing knowledge beyond its founder’s lifetime, not merely a donation or a building. ([Rockefeller University history](https://www.rockefeller.edu/about/history/); [Rockefeller Archive Center](https://rockarch.org/resources/about-the-rockefellers/john-d-rockefeller-jr/))

- **1902–1903:** *General Education Board*. An educational philanthropy organized in 1902 and incorporated by Congress in 1903, initially concentrating on education and agricultural improvement in the American South. The differing dates refer to establishment and legal incorporation, not competing datings of the same event; Gates and Rockefeller Jr. were substantial co-creators. ([Rockefeller Archive Center agricultural timeline](https://resource.rockarch.org/story/timeline-american-philanthropy-agriculture-and-the-challenges-of-feeding-the-world/); [Rockefeller Foundation institutional history](https://resource.rockarch.org/story/rockefeller-foundation-history-origins-to-2013/))

- **1909:** *Rockefeller Sanitary Commission for the Eradication of Hookworm Disease*. A time-limited public-health organization established with Rockefeller’s million-dollar gift. More than a charity, it was an intervention protocol combining surveys, treatment, education, and cooperation with Southern health authorities. ([Rockefeller Archive Center public-health timeline](https://resource.rockarch.org/story/timeline-american-foundations-and-the-history-of-public-health/))

- **1913:** *Rockefeller Foundation*. A general-purpose philanthropic corporation created after an unsuccessful campaign for a federal charter and incorporated in New York on May 14, 1913. Its legal text, capital, and governing design came from Rockefeller and his advisers collectively; Rockefeller approved the charter and supplied its first funds but deliberately refrained from attending board meetings. ([Rockefeller Archive Center institutional history](https://resource.rockarch.org/story/rockefeller-foundation-history-origins-to-2013/); [1913–1914 annual report](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/Annual-Report-1913-1914-1.pdf))

- **1918:** *Laura Spelman Rockefeller Memorial*. A philanthropic institution founded in memory of Rockefeller’s wife to continue her interests in social welfare. Its later program in the social sciences was largely fashioned by professional officers, especially Beardsley Ruml, so the institution is Rockefeller’s foundation but not every work subsequently performed in its name. ([Rockefeller Archive Center institutional history](https://resource.rockarch.org/story/rockefeller-foundation-history-origins-to-2013/); [Rockefeller Archive Center account of Rockefeller Jr.’s participation](https://rockarch.org/resources/about-the-rockefellers/john-d-rockefeller-jr/))

### Designed landscape

- **From 1893:** *Pocantico Hills estate landscape and carriage-road system*. A long-duration landscape project begun with Rockefeller’s acquisition of land near Tarrytown and eventually comprising roads, paths, transplanted trees, engineered water, and scenic compositions. Rockefeller personally planned substantial portions, but the surviving landscape is a layered Gesamtkunstwerk made with John D. Rockefeller Jr., William Welles Bosworth, engineers, gardeners, and laborers; it cannot responsibly be assigned to Rockefeller Sr. alone or given a single completion date. ([National Historic Landmark documentation](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/76001290_text); [New York State historic-landscape documentation](https://parks.ny.gov/documents/parks/RockefellerNationalRegisterofHistoricPlaces.pdf); [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/carriage-roads-partnership.htm))
