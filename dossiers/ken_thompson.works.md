## Works by Kenneth Lane Thompson

### Operating systems and system projects

- **1969–present:** *Unix*. An operating system begun by Thompson on a PDP-7 and subsequently developed with Dennis Ritchie and many others; authorship belongs to a continuing collective system rather than to a single fixed object. Bell Labs dates Thompson’s original implementation to 1969 and records later ports and rewrites. ([Bell Labs](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/unix-history/thompsonbio.html); [Ritchie and Thompson, 1974](https://doi.org/10.1145/361011.361061))
- **mid-1980s–present:** *Plan 9 from Bell Labs*. A distributed operating system and collection of software originally designed and written by Thompson, Rob Pike, Dave Presotto, and Phil Winterbottom, then continually developed by a larger community; it is therefore both an obra and an ongoing collective project. ([Plan 9 project history](https://plan9.io/plan9/about.html); [Pike et al., 1995](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/csys/PikePDFTT95))
- **1980s–1990s:** *Research Unix, Eighth through Tenth Editions*. Successive research systems rather than wholly separate authored works; Thompson and the Bell Labs group revised Unix using substantial BSD-derived material, so individual attribution at edition level is inseparable from collective maintenance. ([Bell Labs Unix history](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/unix-history/thompsonbio.html); [Plan 9 overview](https://plan9.io/plan9/about.html))

### Programming languages and implementations

- **1960s:** *Bon*. An experimental programming language made by Thompson while working on Multics; surviving accounts do not establish one canonical release date or a stable, definitive version. ([Computer History Museum oral history](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/text/Oral_History/Thompson_Ken/thompson.oral_history_transcript.2005.102657921.pdf))
- **1969–1970:** *B*. A systems-programming language and implementation created by Thompson, derived from BCPL and serving as the immediate precursor of Dennis Ritchie’s C. Bell Labs dates it to 1970, while histories place its development across the Unix work of 1969–70. ([Bell Labs](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/unix-history/thompsonbio.html))
- **1976:** *Berkeley Pascal*. Thompson wrote the initial implementation during his Berkeley sabbatical; William Joy, Charles Haley, and Susan Graham subsequently modified and expanded it extensively, making the familiar system a collective continuation rather than Thompson’s unchanged program. ([Computer History Museum oral history](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/text/Oral_History/Thompson_Ken/thompson.oral_history_transcript.2005.102657921.pdf))
- **1970s–1990s:** *Thompson’s C compilers*. A lineage of compact compilers made for Unix and later architectures, rather than one securely titled work; Doug McIlroy’s historical inventory explicitly credits Thompson with C compilers but does not reduce them to a single edition. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **2007–present:** *Go*. An open-source programming language and ecosystem initiated by Thompson, Robert Griesemer, and Rob Pike in September 2007; later compilers, libraries, and releases are the work of a much larger community. Thompson also made its first working compiler, initially translating Go into C. ([Go FAQ](https://go.dev/doc/faq); [“The First Go Program”](https://go.dev/blog/first-go-program); [Go project history](https://go.dev/blog/open-source))

### Editors, commands, and software tools

- **1960s:** *QED for CTSS and Multics*. Thompson’s versions of the QED text editor, in which he developed and implemented regular-expression searching; QED pre-existed him, so these are implementations and extensions, not an invention of the editor itself. ([“Regular Expression Search Algorithm”](https://doi.org/10.1145/363347.363387))
- **1968:** *Thompson’s regular-expression search algorithm*. An algorithmic construction and implementation for compiling regular expressions into executable searching machinery; it is an algorithmic obra rather than a freestanding application. ([“Regular Expression Search Algorithm”](https://doi.org/10.1145/363347.363387))
- **1969–1971:** *ed*. The original Unix line editor, descended from Thompson’s QED work; like many early Unix commands, it evolved through unattributed and collaborative revisions, so no single version exhausts the work. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **early 1970s:** *grep*. A Unix pattern-search command extracted from the editor operation conventionally glossed as “global regular expression print”; it began as a small tool and subsequently became a family of independently maintained implementations. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **early 1970s:** *tmg*. A compiler-writing language and tool implemented by Thompson and used in early Unix development; it resists identification as merely a language or merely a program because its specification and implementation were bound together. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **1970s:** *dbm*. A database-management library created by Thompson for Unix, manifest chiefly through an interface and implementations rather than as a separately published text. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **1970s:** *diff*. A file-comparison utility credited in McIlroy’s historical inventory to Thompson; later versions and modern programs bearing the name are collective descendants, not identical with his implementation. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **1970s:** *join*. A relational text-processing utility credited to Thompson; its obra is an executable operation and interface, not a conventional authored text. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **1970s:** *speak*. A program for reading text aloud, credited to Thompson in McIlroy’s inventory; the historical source gives no secure single date or canonical release. ([McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **1980s:** *rc*. The command-language shell used in Research Unix and Plan 9, designed and implemented principally by Thompson; its later Plan 9 manifestations belong to the larger system and have continued to change. ([Plan 9 project history](https://plan9.io/plan9/about.html); [McIlroy, “A Research Unix Reader”](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf))
- **1980s:** *mk*. A build tool incorporated into Plan 9 and associated with Thompson’s Research Unix work; it is a protocol-like executable description system, though subsequent versions are collectively maintained. ([Plan 9 project history](https://plan9.io/plan9/about.html))
- **1992:** *UTF-8*. A variable-width character-encoding protocol designed by Thompson and Rob Pike in September 1992. It is not simply a text but a rule system for making texts representable, later standardized and implemented by many others. ([Plan 9 project history](https://plan9.io/plan9/about.html); [Pike and Thompson, “Hello World”](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/usenix/PikeT93))

### Games, chess machines, and generated databases

- **1969:** *Space Travel*. A solar-system simulation game written first for Multics, moved to GECOS, and then rewritten for the PDP-7; that last port prompted the tool-building from which Unix emerged. Multiple implementations existed, so the title denotes a software project rather than one fixed artifact. ([Bell Labs, “Ken, Unix and Games”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/ken-games.html))
- **1971:** *chess*. A chess-playing program included in the first edition of Unix; it preceded Belle and should not be conflated with that later hardware-assisted system. ([Bell Labs, “Ken, Unix and Games”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/ken-games.html))
- **1970s–1980:** *Belle*. A series of special-purpose chess machines made with Joseph Condon—Condon principally responsible for hardware and Thompson for software—culminating in the 1980 world computer-chess champion. Because “Belle” names successive hardware/software configurations, it is closer to a Gesamtkunstwerk or evolving project than to one immutable machine. ([Bell Labs biography](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/unix-history/thompsonbio.html); [Bell Labs, “Ken, Unix and Games”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/ken-games.html))
- **1970s:** *Semi-automated wire-wrap machine*. Hardware built by Thompson and Condon to construct Belle and other Bell Labs experimental machines; it is enabling apparatus whose status as an independent “work” is uncertain, but that resistance is historically informative. ([Bell Labs, “Ken, Unix and Games”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/ken-games.html))
- **1980s–1990s:** *Chess endgame tablebases*. Programs and generated databases exhaustively analyzing four-, five-, and six-piece endings. These are simultaneously algorithms, computed manifestations, and data objects; Thompson later distributed results on CD-ROM, but the databases continued to grow and have no single all-encompassing edition. ([Computer History Museum oral history](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/text/Oral_History/Thompson_Ken/thompson.oral_history_transcript.2005.102657921.pdf); [DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/t/KenThompson.html))
- **1980s:** *Chess-move accelerator*. A small special-purpose hardware device made by Thompson to generate legal chess moves more rapidly; it was an experimental component on the path toward Belle, and the oral history does not supply a fixed title or exact date. ([Computer History Museum oral history](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/text/Oral_History/Thompson_Ken/thompson.oral_history_transcript.2005.102657921.pdf))
- **1990:** *Reading Chess*. With Henry S. Baird, an optical character-recognition system for reading printed chess diagrams; the publication describes a software research object rather than a game-playing program. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/pami/BairdT90))

### Manifesto, protocol, and conceptual interventions

- **1983–1984:** *Reflections on Trusting Trust*. Thompson’s Turing Award lecture, published in 1984, and also a manifesto-like executable demonstration: its compiler-backdoor construction acts upon the reader’s idea of what source code can prove. The publication date is 1984, although the award lecture belongs to 1983. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/Thompson84))
- **1992:** *The Use of Name Spaces in Plan 9*. With Rob Pike, David Presotto, Howard Trickey, and Phil Winterbottom, a conference paper describing Plan 9’s per-process namespace design; a revised journal manifestation followed in 1993 and is not a separate underlying project. ([DBLP conference record](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sigopsE/PikePTTW92); [DBLP journal record](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/sigops/PikePTTW93))
- **1993:** *Hello World*. With Rob Pike, a USENIX paper introducing Plan 9’s approach to internationalized text and the UTF-8 protocol. It is both technical exposition and a protocol-design document. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/usenix/PikeT93))
- **1995:** *Plan 9 from Bell Labs*. With Rob Pike, David Presotto, Sean Dorward, Bob Flandrena, Howard Trickey, and Phil Winterbottom, the principal systems paper describing Plan 9; it is a textual manifestation of the larger operating-system project, not the project itself. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/csys/PikePDFTT95))
- **2022:** *The Go Programming Language and Environment*. With Russ Cox, Robert Griesemer, Rob Pike, and Ian Lance Taylor, a retrospective technical account of Go as both a language and engineering environment. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/CoxGPTT22))

### Authored and co-authored publications

- **1968:** *Regular Expression Search Algorithm*. A research article presenting Thompson’s method for implementing regular-expression searches. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/Thompson68))
- **1973:** *The UNIX Time-Sharing System (Abstract)*. With Dennis Ritchie, the abstract of their Symposium on Operating Systems Principles presentation; it is a preliminary conference manifestation, not a separate operating system. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sosp/RitchieT73))
- **1974:** *The UNIX Time-Sharing System*. With Dennis Ritchie, the canonical journal article describing Unix; it names and explains the system but is not identical with it. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/RitchieT74))
- **1978:** *North American Computer Chess Championships 1970–1979*. A tournament report; the date range belongs to the events in its title, while DBLP records publication in 1978. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson78))
- **1979:** *Password Security: A Case History*. With Robert Morris, a research article on Unix password protection and attacks. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/MorrisT79))
- **1981:** *Second World Microcomputer Chess Championship*. With Ben Mittman, a championship report. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/MittmanT81))
- **1981:** *Reprint of ICCA Chapter Support Policy*. A one-page policy text whose title explicitly identifies it as a reprint; it therefore resists treatment as a newly made work in 1981, although DBLP lists Thompson as author. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson81))
- **1981:** *Computer Chess: Master Level Play in 1981?* With Ben Mittman, Tony Marsland, Monroe Newborn, and Kathe Spracklen, a conference contribution. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/acm/MittmanMNST81))
- **1983:** *The UNIX Time-Sharing System (Reprint)*. With Dennis Ritchie, a republication of the 1974 article; it is an editorial manifestation and not a distinct underlying work. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/RitchieT83))
- **1983:** *Letter to David Levy*. A published letter concerning computer chess, a situated communication rather than a research paper. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson83))
- **1983:** *Fourth World Computer-Chess Championship*. With Benjamin Mittman, a championship report. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/MittmanT83))
- **1983:** *1983 United States Open and Fredkin Incentive Matches*. A report on computer-chess competitions. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson83a))
- **1984:** *Reflections on Trusting Trust*. The published manifestation of Thompson’s Turing Award lecture and compiler-subversion demonstration. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/Thompson84))
- **1985:** *The ACM’s 16th North American Computer-Chess Championship*. A short championship report. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson85))
- **1985:** *US Open Computer-Chess Championship*. A tournament report. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson85a))
- **1985:** *ACM’s Sixteenth North American Computer-Chess Championship*. With David Welsh, a second account of the same numbered championship; bibliographically distinct, but not evidence of a second event. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/WelshT85))
- **1986:** *The Fifth World Computer-Chess Championship*. With David Levy, a championship report. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/LevyT86))
- **1986:** *Retrograde Analysis of Certain Endgames*. A research article describing computational endgame analysis. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson86))
- **1986:** *An Example of QPvQ*. A technical chess-endgame study generated from Thompson’s computational work. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson86a))
- **1989:** *The Sixth World Computer-Chess Championship: Results and Games*. A championship record and game report. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson89))
- **1989:** *Results and Games*. A separate tournament-results article; its generic title does not justify inventing a more specific one. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson89a))
- **1990:** *KQPKQ and KRPKR Endings*. A technical report on two classes of computer-analyzed chess endings. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson90))
- **1990:** *Results and Games*. A tournament-results article distinct from the identically titled 1989 item. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson90a))
- **1990:** *Reading Chess*. With Henry S. Baird, a research paper on recognizing printed chess positions by machine. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/pami/BairdT90))
- **1991:** *New Results for KNPKB and KNPKN Endgames*. A short report of computer-generated endgame results. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson91))
- **1991:** *Chess Endgames Vol. 1*. A one-page bibliographic notice or short contribution; DBLP’s record alone does not establish that it is the underlying endgame-data volume itself. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson91a))
- **1992:** *Chess Endgames Vol. 2*. A one-page journal item associated with Thompson’s endgame series; its precise relation to the distributed databases is not resolved by the bibliographic record. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson92))
- **1992:** *The Use of Name Spaces in Plan 9*. With Rob Pike, David Presotto, Howard Trickey, and Phil Winterbottom, the conference manifestation of the namespace paper. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sigopsE/PikePTTW92))
- **1993:** *Chess Endgames Vol. 3*. A one-page journal item in the endgame series, not safely identifiable from the citation alone as the complete database object. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson93))
- **1993:** *The Use of Name Spaces in Plan 9*. With Rob Pike, David Presotto, Howard Trickey, and Phil Winterbottom, the journal manifestation of the 1992 conference work. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/sigops/PikePTTW93))
- **1993:** *Hello World*. With Rob Pike, a conference paper about UTF-8 and international text in Plan 9. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/usenix/PikeT93))
- **1995:** *Plan 9 from Bell Labs*. With Rob Pike, David Presotto, Sean Dorward, Bob Flandrena, Howard Trickey, and Phil Winterbottom, a systems article. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/csys/PikePDFTT95))
- **1995:** *Tributes to Tony Scherzer*. With Jonathan Schaeffer, a memorial text rather than a technical research article. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/ThompsonS95))
- **1996:** *6-Piece Endgames*. A research report on Thompson’s exhaustive six-piece chess databases. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson96))
- **1999:** *Unix and Beyond: An Interview with Ken Thompson*. An interview conducted by Daniel Cooke, Joseph Urban, and Scott Hamilton; Thompson is its speaking subject and participant, but interviewer framing and editorial manufacture make sole authorship inappropriate. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/computer/CookeUHT99))
- **2000:** *The Longest: Krnknn in 262*. A short computer-chess endgame result. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/icga/Thompson00))
- **2012:** *Systems Architecture, Design, Engineering, and Verification—The Practice in Research and Research in Practice*. With Dahlia Malkhi, Fernando Corbató, E. Allen Emerson, and Joseph Sifakis, a Turing-centenary panel contribution rather than a conventional jointly argued paper. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/acm/MalkhiCEST12))
- **2022:** *The Go Programming Language and Environment*. With Russ Cox, Robert Griesemer, Rob Pike, and Ian Lance Taylor, a retrospective journal article on the jointly made language and its tools. ([DBLP](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/cacm/CoxGPTT22))

### Sources

- [DBLP: Ken Thompson—curated computer-science bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/t/KenThompson.html). Used as the standard bibliographic spine; reprints, interviews, notices, and alternate manifestations are identified rather than silently counted as new underlying works.
- [Nokia Bell Labs: “The Creation of the UNIX Operating System—Ken Thompson Biography”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/unix-history/thompsonbio.html). Institutional chronology for Unix, B, Belle, and Thompson’s Bell Labs work.
- [Dennis M. Ritchie, “Ken, Unix and Games”](https://www.nokia.com/bell-labs/about/dennis-m-ritchie/ken-games.html). First-hand historical account of Space Travel, chess software, Belle, and the wire-wrap machinery.
- [Douglas McIlroy, *A Research Unix Reader: Annotated Excerpts from the Programmer’s Manual, 1971–1986*](https://www.cs.dartmouth.edu/~doug/reader.pdf). Historical collected-system guide used for Thompson’s Unix commands, compilers, and utilities.
- [Computer History Museum, *Oral History of Ken Thompson*](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/text/Oral_History/Thompson_Ken/thompson.oral_history_transcript.2005.102657921.pdf). Thompson’s first-person account of languages, Unix, chess hardware, and related projects.
- [Plan 9 from Bell Labs: project overview and release history](https://plan9.io/plan9/about.html). Project-authored account of Plan 9’s original designers, constituent software, and continuing collective development.
- [The Go Programming Language: FAQ and project history](https://go.dev/doc/faq). Official chronology and attribution for Go.
- [Andrew Gerrand, “The First Go Program”](https://go.dev/blog/first-go-program). Official Go project account identifying Thompson’s first working Go compiler.
- [Russ Cox, “Go, Open Source, Community”](https://go.dev/blog/open-source). Official account of Go’s three initiators and subsequent collective development.
