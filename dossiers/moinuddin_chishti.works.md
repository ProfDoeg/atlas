## Works by Khwaja Mu'in al-Din Hasan Chishti

### Living project

- **Late twelfth century–1236:** *The Chishti teaching community at Ajmer*. A spiritual and institutional project—teaching, discipleship, devotional practice, and the transmission of a lineage—rather than a bounded text. Its historical existence is clear, but its earliest doctrines survive principally through later oral and hagiographical transmission, so it resists treatment as a single authored obra ([Ernst and Lawrence, pp. 1–3, 64–84](https://sufi.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/sufi-martyrs-of-love-chishti-sufism-in-south-asia-and-beyond-by-carl-ernst-and-bruce-lawrence.pdf); [Böwering](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cestiya/)).

### Securely attributable writings

- **No securely attributable item:** No surviving book, treatise, letter collection, or poetic corpus is accepted by critical scholarship as Muʿin al-Din’s own composition. This absence matters: the early Chishti tradition represented its masters as transmitting teaching through presence and speech, while the texts bearing his name appeared later ([Currie, pp. 22–24](https://dokumen.pub/the-shrine-and-cult-of-muin-al-din-chishti-of-ajmer-9780195683295-0195683293.html)).

### Apocryphal records of teaching

- **c. 1324–1355:** *Anīs al-arwāḥ*. Ostensibly Muʿin al-Din’s record of the discourses of his master, ʿUsman Harwani: thus a disciple’s notebook or teaching archive rather than an independent doctrinal book. Currie concludes from internal anachronisms and the testimony of early Chishti sources that it is a posthumous fabrication, composed roughly a century after Muʿin al-Din’s death ([Currie, pp. 22–24](https://dokumen.pub/the-shrine-and-cult-of-muin-al-din-chishti-of-ajmer-9780195683295-0195683293.html)).

- **c. 1355–1385:** *Dalīl al-ʿārifīn*. A collection of conversations or sayings attributed to Muʿin al-Din and traditionally said to have been compiled by his disciple Qutb al-Din Bakhtiyar Kaki; it is therefore a purported manifestation of speech, not a text Muʿin al-Din claimed to write. Currie finds no evidence that its sayings are authentic and dates the fabrication between *Khayr al-majālis* and *Siyar al-awliyāʾ* ([Currie, pp. 23–24, 52–57](https://dokumen.pub/the-shrine-and-cult-of-muin-al-din-chishti-of-ajmer-9780195683295-0195683293.html); [IGNOU survey, p. 9](https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/84178/3/Unit-9.pdf)).

### Speculative manuscript attributions

- **Date unknown:** *Ganj al-asrār* (“Treasury of Secrets”). A Sufi work represented by at least seven manuscripts under Muʿin al-Din’s name, but classified by Ernst and Lawrence as “speculative” and explicitly attributed rather than authenticated; it cannot safely be counted as his obra ([Ernst and Lawrence, p. 231](https://sufi.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/sufi-martyrs-of-love-chishti-sufism-in-south-asia-and-beyond-by-carl-ernst-and-bruce-lawrence.pdf)).

- **Date unknown:** *Maktūbāt*. A collected body of letters transmitted in at least twelve manuscripts, not one demonstrably authorial book. The standard bibliography places it among speculative attributions, and no individual letter is securely established as Muʿin al-Din’s composition ([Ernst and Lawrence, p. 231](https://sufi.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/sufi-martyrs-of-love-chishti-sufism-in-south-asia-and-beyond-by-carl-ernst-and-bruce-lawrence.pdf)).

- **Date and fixed title unknown:** *Risāla* (Monzavi III, no. 2681). An otherwise untitled treatise preserved in a single recorded manuscript and attributed to Muʿin al-Din. Its catalogue label means simply “treatise,” so it resists identification as a securely titled or securely authored work ([Ernst and Lawrence, p. 231](https://sufi.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/sufi-martyrs-of-love-chishti-sufism-in-south-asia-and-beyond-by-carl-ernst-and-bruce-lawrence.pdf)).

- **Date and fixed title unknown:** *Risāla* (Monzavi III, no. 2682). A second, distinct, otherwise untitled treatise known from one recorded manuscript; like the preceding item, it survives only as a speculative attribution and cannot be assigned to Muʿin al-Din without qualification ([Ernst and Lawrence, p. 231](https://sufi.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/sufi-martyrs-of-love-chishti-sufism-in-south-asia-and-beyond-by-carl-ernst-and-bruce-lawrence.pdf)).

- **Date unknown:** *Wujūdiyya*. A speculative corpus or treatise on “being,” represented by ten catalogued manuscripts; Ernst and Lawrence note that most concern yoga. That subject and the late, uncertain transmission make it particularly resistant to classification as Muʿin al-Din’s own protocol or doctrinal obra ([Ernst and Lawrence, p. 231](https://sufi.co.za/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/sufi-martyrs-of-love-chishti-sufism-in-south-asia-and-beyond-by-carl-ernst-and-bruce-lawrence.pdf)).

### Falsely attributed poetry

- **Date unknown:** *Dīvān-i Khwāja Muʿin al-Din Chishtī*. A collected poetic corpus circulating under the saint’s name, not a securely constituted authorial collection. Hafiz Mahmud Sherani’s textual investigation identified it as the work of the later namesake Maulana Muʿin al-Din Farahi; it should therefore be recorded as pseudepigraphic reception, not as something Chishti made ([Dawn account of Sherani’s study](https://www.dawn.com/news/1333419); [National Archives of Pakistan manuscript record](https://www.chughtailibrary.com/digital_library/repository_detail.php?id=96825)).
