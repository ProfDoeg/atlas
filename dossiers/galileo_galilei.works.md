## Works by Galileo Galilei

### Mathematical, mechanical, and military treatises

- **c. 1584–1588:** *Iuvenilia*. A manuscript body of scholastic notes and disputations from Galileo’s student years; it is a dossier rather than a deliberately unified book. [EN I]
- **c. 1586:** *Theoremata circa centrum gravitatis solidorum*. A group of Latin geometrical propositions on the centers of gravity of solids, circulated in manuscript and revised over time. [EN I]
- **1586:** *La bilancetta*. A short protocol explaining a hydrostatic balance for determining the proportions of metals in alloys; it remained unpublished during Galileo’s lifetime. [EN I]
- **c. 1586:** *Tavola delle proporzioni della gravità in specie de i metalli e delle gioie pesate in aria e in acqua*. A working table accompanying Galileo’s hydrostatic investigations, closer to a calculating instrument in textual form than to a treatise. [EN I]
- **late 1580s:** *Postille ai libri De sphaera et cylindro di Archimede*. Marginal annotations to Archimedes; these are acts performed upon another author’s book and resist classification as an independent work. [EN I]
- **c. 1589–1592:** *De motu* (also called *De motu antiquiora*). An unfinished Latin project on motion, preserved as several related manuscript drafts rather than a completed publication. [EN I]
- **c. 1592–1593:** *Breve instruzione all’architettura militare*. A concise instructional text on military architecture, apparently produced for teaching and surviving in manuscript. [EN II]
- **c. 1593–1594:** *Trattato di fortificazione*. A more extensive teaching treatise on designing and attacking fortifications; manuscript witnesses do not constitute a single authorially published book. [EN II]
- **c. 1593–1594:** *Le matematiche nell’arte militare*. Notes or lectures applying mathematics to warfare; an instructional dossier rather than a finished publication. [EN II]
- **c. 1593–1600:** *Le meccaniche*. A treatise on simple machines and mechanical advantage, known through distinct manuscript versions and copies; its textual plurality makes “the work” a developing project. [EN II]
- **c. 1597:** *Trattato della sfera ovvero cosmografia*. A course text on traditional spherical astronomy and cosmography, probably made for Galileo’s private students. [EN II]
- **c. 1604:** *De motu accelerato*. A short manuscript treatment of accelerated motion; it belongs to Galileo’s evolving research rather than to a completed book. [EN II]
- **before 1606:** *Del compasso geometrico e militare: scritture preparatorie*. Drafts and preliminary explanations for the proportional compass, preserved as a textual project preceding the printed manual. [EN II]
- **1606:** *Le operazioni del compasso geometrico e militare*. A printed protocol teaching users how to operate Galileo’s calculating instrument; Galileo financed a small edition for purchasers and pupils. [EN II]
- **1607:** *Difesa di Galileo Galilei contro alle calunnie et imposture di Baldessar Capra*. A printed forensic and polemical defense asserting Galileo’s priority in the proportional compass after Capra published an imitation. [EN II]
- **c. 1630s:** *Scritture attenenti all’idraulica*. Notes, calculations, and fragments concerning water and hydraulic machines; the collected edition groups them editorially, so they resist treatment as one authorially constituted work. [EN VI]

### Astronomy, observation, and calculation

- **1604–1605:** *Frammenti di lezioni e di studi sulla nuova stella dell’ottobre 1604*. Surviving fragments from Galileo’s lectures and investigations of the supernova; they document a project without preserving a finished treatise. [EN II]
- **1605:** *Considerazioni astronomiche circa la stella nova dell’anno 1604*. A polemical astronomical treatise issued under the name “Alimberto Mauri.” Its attribution was long contested, but recent archival and textual research has renewed and strengthened the attribution to Galileo. [EN II; Cosci]
- **1605:** *Dialogo de Cecco di Ronchitti da Bruzene in perpuosito de la stella nuova*. A comic-scientific dialogue in Paduan dialect published pseudonymously; Favaro treated it as collaboration between Galileo and Girolamo Spinelli, while later scholars have variously assigned Galileo the arguments, joint authorship, or primary authorship. [EN II; Bucciantini]
- **1605:** *Poem in ottava rima on the new star*. An anonymous poem appended only to the first issue of the *Dialogo de Cecco* and removed from the next; Galileo’s authorship has been proposed but is not established. [Granada]
- **1610:** *Sidereus nuncius*. A rapidly produced illustrated astronomical report announcing telescopic observations of the Moon, the Milky Way, innumerable stars, and four satellites of Jupiter. It is both scientific report and priority-claiming manifesto. [EN III.1]
- **1610–1613:** *Osservazioni astronomiche*. Dated observational records, drawings, and calculations, especially concerning Jupiter’s satellites; they are working traces of repeated observation rather than a single literary text. [EN III.2]
- **1611–1617:** *Tavole dei moti medi dei satelliti di Giove*. Computational tables made to predict the motions of Jupiter’s satellites, repeatedly revised rather than definitively completed. [EN III.2]
- **1611–1619:** *Calcoli e prostaferesi dei Pianeti Medicei*. A changing archive of calculations and comparison methods for Jupiter’s satellites; the National Edition subdivides it by year, but it is better understood as one sustained computational project. [EN III.2]
- **c. 1611–1637:** *Giovilabii*. Drawings and designs for instruments modeling the configurations of Jupiter’s satellites; surviving sheets and later instruments do not establish that every physical specimen was made by Galileo himself. [EN III.2; Museo Galileo]
- **c. 1611–1630s:** *Analecta astronomica*. Miscellaneous astronomical notes assembled under an editorial title; they resist the status of a discrete work because Galileo did not constitute them as one. [EN III.2]
- **c. 1612:** *Theorica speculi concavi sphaerici*. A manuscript geometrical investigation of the spherical concave mirror, situated between optical calculation and instrument design. [EN III.2]
- **1613:** *Istoria e dimostrazioni intorno alle macchie solari e loro accidenti*. Three printed letters to Mark Welser on sunspots, solar rotation, and celestial change; the publication incorporates both serial correspondence and a sustained astronomical argument. [EN V]
- **1612–1613:** *Frammenti attenenti alle lettere sulle macchie solari*. Drafts and working material for the sunspot letters; these are remains of composition, not another finished treatise. [EN V]
- **1616:** *Discorso del flusso e reflusso del mare*. A manuscript discourse presenting tides as evidence of Earth’s motions; written for Cardinal Alessandro Orsini, it later became central to the *Dialogo*. [EN V]
- **1616–1641:** *Proposte per la determinazione della longitudine*. Memoranda, tables, designs, and negotiations proposing Jupiter’s satellites as a celestial clock for finding longitude, first to Spain and later to the Dutch States General. This is a long, repeatedly reformulated project rather than one text. [EN V; Museo Galileo]
- **1618–1619:** *Discorso delle comete*. A polemical discourse published under Mario Guiducci’s name and substantially shaped or written by Galileo; because the named author, speaker, and principal intellectual maker differ, it resists simple single authorship. [EN VI]
- **1624–1630:** *Fragments and drafts for the Dialogo sopra i due massimi sistemi del mondo*. Preparatory arguments, revisions, and discarded passages belonging to the making of the *Dialogo*, not a separate completed book. [EN VII]
- **1632:** *Dialogo sopra i due massimi sistemi del mondo, tolemaico e copernicano*. A four-day philosophical dialogue comparing Ptolemaic and Copernican cosmologies; formally framed as an even-handed discussion, it acts polemically and helped precipitate Galileo’s trial. [EN VII]
- **c. 1638:** *Le operazioni astronomiche*. An incomplete or fragmentary instructional text on astronomical operations, printed by the National Edition among Galileo’s late writings. [EN VIII]

### Natural philosophy, matter, and motion

- **1611–1612:** *Frammenti attenenti al trattato delle cose che stanno in su l’acqua*. Draft arguments and experiments leading to the work on floating bodies; a compositional dossier rather than an autonomous publication. [EN IV]
- **1612:** *Discorso intorno alle cose che stanno in su l’acqua o che in quella si muovono*. A printed intervention in the Florentine controversy over flotation, combining geometrical demonstration, experiment, and polemic. [EN IV]
- **1612–1615:** *Risposta alle opposizioni di Lodovico delle Colombe e di Vincenzio di Grazia*. An unfinished or unpublished response defending the work on floating bodies; surviving fragments make its boundaries uncertain. [EN IV]
- **c. 1612:** *Correzioni e aggiunte agli Errori di Giorgio Coresio raccolti da Benedetto Castelli*. Galileo’s revisions to Castelli’s polemical text; a collaborative editorial intervention rather than an independently authored work. [EN IV]
- **1623:** *Il saggiatore*. A printed polemical treatise replying to Orazio Grassi on comets and articulating a mathematical conception of natural knowledge; its wit and argumentative performance make it partly a manifesto for a way of doing philosophy. [EN VI]
- **c. 1633–1638:** *Fragments for the Discorsi e dimostrazioni matematiche intorno a due nuove scienze*. Drafts, proofs, diagrams, and variant passages for Galileo’s last major publication; their status is preparatory and sometimes unfinished. [EN VIII]
- **1638:** *Discorsi e dimostrazioni matematiche intorno a due nuove scienze*. A dialogue uniting earlier and later research on the strength of materials and local motion; published at Leiden while Galileo remained under house arrest. [EN VIII]
- **late 1630s:** *Sopra le definizioni delle proporzioni di Euclide* (“Fifth Day”). A projected additional day for the *Two New Sciences*, addressing proportion theory; left incomplete and published posthumously. [EN VIII]
- **late 1630s:** *Della forza della percossa* (“Sixth Day”). A projected additional dialogue on impact or percussion, unfinished at Galileo’s death and therefore a project rather than a completed book. [EN VIII]
- **undated, chiefly late:** *Scritture e frammenti di data incerta*. Scientific and philosophical notes whose dates and intended relationships cannot be securely recovered; this is an editorial residue, not an obra made under that title. [EN VIII]

### Theology, Copernican advocacy, and philosophical letters

- **1597:** *Letter to Jacopo Mazzoni*. A substantial philosophical letter discussing cosmology and physical arguments; it is correspondence but also a composed intellectual intervention. [EN II]
- **21 December 1613:** *Letter to Benedetto Castelli*. A manifesto-like letter on Scripture, natural knowledge, and Copernican astronomy; manuscript transmission produced materially consequential textual variants. [EN V]
- **16 February and 23 March 1615:** *Letters to Piero Dini*. Two extended letters refining Galileo’s account of Copernicanism and biblical interpretation; together they form stages in a developing apologetic project. [EN V]
- **1615:** *Lettera a Madama Cristina di Lorena, Granduchessa di Toscana*. A long open letter on the relationship between biblical interpretation and natural philosophy; though addressed as correspondence, it was constructed for wider circulation and acts as a theological-scientific manifesto. [EN V]
- **1615:** *Considerazioni circa l’opinione copernicana*. An unpublished memorandum assembling arguments about the status and defense of Copernican astronomy. [EN V]
- **1624:** *Lettera a Francesco Ingoli in risposta alla Disputatio de situ et quiete terrae*. A delayed, extensive answer to Ingoli’s 1616 objections to Earth’s motion; formally a letter, substantively a treatise. [EN VI]
- **1640:** *Letter to Prince Leopoldo of Tuscany*. A late philosophical letter, with related fragments, concerning physical questions; its accompanying drafts show that the “letter” was a worked composition. [EN VIII]

### Lectures, literary criticism, drama, and verse

- **1588:** *Due lezioni all’Accademia Fiorentina circa la figura, sito e grandezza dell’Inferno di Dante*. Two public lectures using geometry to reconstruct Dante’s Hell; they are performed lectures surviving as texts, not a book conceived as such. [EN IX]
- **c. 1589–1590:** *Considerazioni al Tasso*. Critical annotations and observations on Torquato Tasso’s poetry; an extended act of reading rather than a freestanding literary work. [EN IX]
- **c. 1590:** *Postille all’Ariosto*. Marginal notes on Ludovico Ariosto; their existence as a “work” is editorial, since they were made upon another text. [EN IX]
- **undated:** *Argomento e traccia d’una commedia*. A scenario and outline for an unrealized comedy; the project, not a completed play, is the made thing that survives. [EN IX]
- **various dates:** *Poesie e frammenti*. A small, heterogeneous body of verse and poetic fragments gathered by the National Edition; individual dating and completeness are often uncertain, and the collection itself is editorial rather than authorial. [EN IX]
- **1610:** *Corrections to Andrea Salvadori’s Canzone per le stelle Medicee*. A manuscript intervention in another poet’s celebratory song, written and corrected in Galileo’s hand; collaboration and revision, not sole authorship, define the object. [EN IX]
- **student years:** *Esercitazioni scolastiche*. School exercises attributed to Galileo and printed as a sample in the National Edition; they survive as evidence of training and resist elevation into deliberately authored works. [EN IX]

### Correspondence and documentary acts

- **1574–1642:** *Carteggio*. The surviving correspondence to, from, and around Galileo occupies volumes X–XVIII of the National Edition. Galileo’s own letters are individually made texts, but the *Carteggio* is an editorial corpus—not one work—and includes many texts he did not author. [EN X–XVIII]
- **1610–1641:** *Priority claims, petitions, memoranda, and negotiating papers*. Galileo made numerous documentary acts concerning appointments, telescopic discoveries, longitude proposals, publication, patronage, and disputes; the National Edition disperses them among correspondence and documents, and they cannot honestly be converted into a tidy list of titled works. [EN X–XIX]
- **1633:** *Depositions and abjuration associated with Galileo’s trial*. These texts bear Galileo’s speech, signature, or compelled assent but were produced within inquisitorial procedure. They strongly resist being called his works: institutional coercion and scribal mediation are part of what they are. [EN XV; EN XIX]

### Instruments, machines, and material projects

- **from c. 1597:** *Geometric and military compasses*. A family of brass proportional calculators designed by Galileo and made in multiple examples, often with craftsmen’s assistance; the surviving Museo Galileo instrument is dated about 1606, but it is one manifestation of the design rather than the whole obra. [Museo Galileo]
- **traditionally 1597:** *Thermoscope*. A device using thermal expansion of air to move water; Vincenzo Viviani credited its development to Galileo, but no original instrument survives and the museum examples are later, so the attribution concerns a design or experiment rather than an extant object made by his hand. [Museo Galileo]
- **from 1609:** *Telescopes*. Galileo developed and constructed multiple refracting telescopes after learning of the Dutch device; two surviving examples in Florence are catalogued as built by him, while the many lost examples form a serial instrument-making practice rather than one work. [Museo Galileo]
- **c. 1610–1612:** *Micrometer for telescopic observations*. An attachment or method devised to measure angular separations, especially among Jupiter’s satellites; it survives principally through descriptions and associated observational practice. [Museo Galileo]
- **c. 1611 onward:** *Jovilabe*. A calculating and demonstrational instrument for representing the configurations of Jupiter’s satellites. The surviving brass example has no date or known maker, so Galileo’s secure contribution is the project or design, not necessarily the physical object. [Museo Galileo]
- **early seventeenth century:** *Armed lodestones*. Galileo experimented with and promoted strongly armed magnets, but the Museo Galileo specimens have unknown makers; they belong to his experimental environment without all being safely classifiable as things he personally made. [Museo Galileo]
- **c. 1624:** *Occhialino* (compound “small eyeglass”). Galileo sent examples of a compound magnifying instrument and Johannes Faber coined *microscope* for it in 1625. The celebrated surviving “Galilean microscope” is now more plausibly attributed to Giuseppe Campani and therefore should not be listed as a secure Galileo-made object. [Museo Galileo]
- **by 1637:** *Celatone*. A helmet-mounted telescope designed to permit observation of Jupiter’s satellites from a moving ship as part of the longitude project; it is securely documented as a design, while no original survives. [Museo Galileo]
- **1637–1641:** *Pendulum-clock project*. Galileo proposed applying a pendulum to a clock and communicated a design late in life; the surviving drawing was made by Vincenzo Viviani and Vincenzo Galilei, and the physical working models are nineteenth-century manifestations, not objects made by Galileo. [Museo Galileo]
- **uncertain dates:** *Experimental inclined planes*. Galileo described inclined-plane experiments, but no original apparatus survives. The familiar bell-equipped inclined plane is a later didactic reconstruction, and the museum explicitly notes that no document proves he performed that particular experiment. [Museo Galileo]
