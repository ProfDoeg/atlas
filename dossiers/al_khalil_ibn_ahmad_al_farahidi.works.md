## Works by Al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi

### Lexicographical obra

- **Eighth century:** *Kitāb al-ʿAyn* (“Book of ʿAyn”). The earliest comprehensive Arabic lexicon, organized phonologically rather than alphabetically and constructed through permutations of consonantal roots. Its received text is composite: medieval testimony variously assigns al-Khalīl the plan, an initial text, or the whole work, while crediting his pupil al-Layth ibn al-Muẓaffar with transmission, completion, or redaction; consequently it resists treatment as a securely bounded single-author book ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf); [Talmon](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C); [Oxford scholarly discussion](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A2f210f1e-a598-455f-a2cc-853ad402a35b/files/mfc88dbf7133783b83a0a40b08d943c07)).

- **Eighth century; lost:** *Kitāb Fāʾit al-ʿAyn* (“What Escaped al-ʿAyn”). A supplement or collection of lexical material omitted from *Kitāb al-ʿAyn*, known from Ibn al-Nadīm’s bio-bibliographical list rather than from a surviving independent text ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf)).

### Prosodic project and its text

- **Eighth century:** *The system of ʿarūḍ*. A conceptual and diagrammatic apparatus for analyzing Arabic poetic metre: fifteen metres arranged through five generative circles. This is better classified as a made analytical system than simply as a book; later theorists reformulated it, and the sixteenth canonical metre is generally associated with al-Akhfash rather than al-Khalīl ([Ryding, ed.](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C); [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/al-Khalil-ibn-Ahmad)).

- **Eighth century; lost as an independent work:** *Kitāb al-ʿArūḍ* (“Book of Prosody”). The textual exposition traditionally associated with that metrical system; it is named by the early bio-bibliographical tradition, but no securely identifiable independent copy survives ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf); [Ryding, ed.](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C)).

- **Eighth century; lost:** *Kitāb al-Shawāhid* (“Book of Evidentiary Citations”). Apparently a collection of poetic attestations supporting linguistic or prosodic analysis; its precise arrangement and completeness cannot now be recovered ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf); [Haywood study reproduced by IGNCA](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/12555.pdf)).

### Writing-system intervention and protocol

- **Eighth century:** *The vowel-and-recitation diacritic system*. Al-Khalīl is credited with replacing the earlier colored-dot vocalization method with differentiated signs derived from letter forms and with using a miniature *shīn* as the ancestor of the *shadda*. This is a graphic protocol embedded in writing practice, not merely a text, and its received standard form may reflect subsequent development ([Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/al-Khalil-ibn-Ahmad); [Ryding, ed.](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C)).

- **Eighth century; lost:** *Kitāb al-Nuqaṭ wa-l-Shakl* (“Book of Points and Vocalization”). A technical protocol concerning Arabic pointing and vowel signs, reported in the classical book-list; it does not survive as an independently verifiable text ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf); [Haywood study reproduced by IGNCA](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/12555.pdf)).

### Music-theoretical works

- **Eighth century; lost:** *Kitāb al-Nagham* (“Book of Melody” or “Modes”). A music-theoretical treatise known through medieval bibliographical notices and later references; without the text, its exact object—melodic modes, song, or intonation—cannot be fixed securely ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf); [Ryding, ed.](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C)).

- **Eighth century; lost:** *Kitāb al-Īqāʿ* (“Book of Rhythm”). A treatise on musical rhythm or rhythmic cycles; the title is transmitted by Ibn al-Nadīm, but its contents and relationship to al-Khalīl’s prosodic system remain unrecoverable ([Ibn al-Nadīm](https://www.almutadaber.com/books/book1_473.pdf); [Haywood study reproduced by IGNCA](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/12555.pdf)).

### Cryptanalytic project

- **Eighth century; lost and attribution-dependent:** *Kitāb al-Muʿammā* (“Book of Enciphered Messages”). A treatise traditionally credited with applying combinatorial reasoning and linguistic knowledge to the making and solution of cryptograms. It is not included in Ibn al-Nadīm’s immediate list of al-Khalīl’s books, so it should be retained as a later-attributed lost work rather than treated as securely attested on the same footing as that list ([Cogent Arts & Humanities study](https://doi.org/10.1080/23311983.2023.2196842)).

### Grammatical texts of disputed authorship

- **Uncertain date; extant but disputed:** *Kitāb al-Jumal fī al-Naḥw* (“Book of Sentences in Grammar”). A compact grammatical treatise transmitted and printed under al-Khalīl’s name, but also attributed to the tenth-century grammarian Ibn Shuqayr; modern linguistic dating has not produced consensus, so this is an attributed obra rather than a secure component of al-Khalīl’s corpus ([Martins thesis record](http://hdl.handle.net/2152/26416); [Perseus Catalog](https://catalog.perseus.tufts.edu/catalog/urn%3Acts%3AarabicLit%3A0170KhalilFarahidi.JumalFiNahw); [Cogent Arts & Humanities study](https://doi.org/10.1080/23311983.2023.2196842)).

- **Eighth century; lost or possibly only a variant title:** *Kitāb al-ʿAwāmil* (“Book of Grammatical Governors”). Later biographers ascribe this grammatical work to al-Khalīl, but it is absent from Ibn al-Nadīm’s core list and no securely attributable text survives; it may represent material subsequently transmitted under another title ([Haywood study reproduced by IGNCA](https://ignca.gov.in/Asi_data/12555.pdf)).

- **Eighth century; attribution uncertain:** *Kitāb Maʿānī al-Ḥurūf* (“Book on the Meanings of Particles/Letters”). A grammatical-lexical treatise attributed to al-Khalīl in later bibliographies and modern editions, but not securely anchored in Ibn al-Nadīm’s list; its textual identity and authorship therefore remain uncertain ([Al-Azhar biographical study](https://azhar.eg/details-multi-portal/PgrID/3501/PageID/1309/ArtMID/3500/ArticleID/10171); [CiNii bibliographic record](https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BC1184726X.amp?l=en)).

- **Eighth century; lost and weakly attested:** *Jumlat Ālāt al-Iʿrāb* (“The Aggregate of the Instruments of Inflection”). A grammatical title assigned to al-Khalīl by later sources; neither an independent early witness nor a surviving text establishes whether it was a separate obra, an alternate title, or a retrospective attribution ([Al-Azhar biographical study](https://azhar.eg/details-multi-portal/PgrID/3501/PageID/1309/ArtMID/3500/ArticleID/10171)).

- **Eighth century; lost and weakly attested:** *Tafsīr Ḥurūf al-Lugha* (“Explanation of the Letters of Language”). Another title in later lists, apparently concerning letters, sounds, or grammatical particles; it may overlap with *Maʿānī al-Ḥurūf*, and the evidence does not justify silently turning the two titles into two certainly distinct books ([Al-Azhar biographical study](https://azhar.eg/details-multi-portal/PgrID/3501/PageID/1309/ArtMID/3500/ArticleID/10171)).

### Poetry and transmitted sayings

- **Eighth century; fragmentary, without a securely authorial collection:** *Poems attributed to al-Khalīl*. Biographical and literary sources preserve scattered verses, but no author-arranged *dīwān* is securely attested; modern collections are retrospective assemblies and therefore manifestations of a fragmentary poetic remainder rather than a book he demonstrably made ([Ryding, ed.](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C)).

- **Eighth century; fragmentary oral-teaching corpus:** *Grammatical analyses transmitted through Sībawayh’s Kitāb*. Sībawayh repeatedly records answers and doctrines received from his teacher, making al-Khalīl’s teaching a substantial intellectual creation embedded inside another author’s obra. It resists classification as an independent work because al-Khalīl neither titled nor delimited it as a text ([Ryding, ed.](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C); [Talmon](https://books.google.com/books?id=ebB6ROootW4C)).
