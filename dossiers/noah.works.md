## Works by Noah son of Lamech

### Constructed obra

- **Before the Flood, traditionally c. 2349 BC:** *The Ark*. A vast pitch-coated wooden vessel made by Noah according to divine specifications; Genesis presents the design as God’s project but the construction as Noah’s obedient work. The wood species translated “gopher wood” remains unidentified, and no historical or archaeological attribution can be established ([Genesis 6:14–22](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.6.14-22?lang=bi)).

- **By the Flood, traditionally c. 2349–2348 BC:** *The Ark’s Window*. An architectural component explicitly described as the window Noah had made; whether it is identical with the difficult Hebrew *tzohar* prescribed in Genesis 6:16 is disputed ([Genesis 8:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.8.6?lang=bi)).

- **After the Flood, traditionally c. 2348 BC:** *Altar to the LORD*. A ritual structure built after Noah left the ark and used for burnt offerings; it is untitled and known only by its function ([Genesis 8:20](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.8.20?lang=bi)).

### Cultivated and produced things

- **After the Flood:** *Noah’s Vineyard*. A cultivated landscape rather than a freestanding object: Genesis says Noah planted it, while *Jubilees* locates the planting on Mount Lubar and supplies its own chronology, incompatible with simply assigning it a secure modern BC date ([Genesis 9:20](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.9.20-27?lang=bi); [*Jubilees* 7:1–2](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th6314/content/VanderKam%281989%29Jubilees.pdf)).

- **After the vineyard’s first harvest:** *Noah’s Wine*. Genesis says only that Noah drank wine and does not expressly say he made it; *Jubilees* 7:2 explicitly credits him with making wine from his grapes and storing it in a vessel. It is a consumable product and ritual medium, not a “work” in the usual art-historical sense ([*Jubilees* 7:1–6](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th6314/content/VanderKam%281989%29Jubilees.pdf)).

### Ritual manifestations

- **After the Flood:** *Burnt Offerings of Clean Animals and Birds*. Noah “made” these in the ritual sense: the animals and birds were not his creations, but their transformation into offerings was his performed religious act. The offering survives only as a narrated manifestation, not as an object separable from the altar and ceremony ([Genesis 8:20–21](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.8.20-21?lang=bi); [*Jubilees* 6:1–4](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th6314/content/VanderKam%281989%29Jubilees.pdf)).

### Manifestos and protocols

- **After the Flood:** *Curse upon Canaan and Blessings of Shem and Japheth*. An untitled set of performative utterances: the words purport to establish the future relations of Noah’s descendants and therefore act rather than merely describe. Genesis attributes the speech to Noah, but its historical composition and its relationship to Israelite ethnographic traditions cannot be assigned to the traditional patriarch ([Genesis 9:24–27](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.9.24-27?lang=bi)).

- **In the later tradition of Jubilees:** *Noah’s Admonitions to His Sons*. A protocol of justice, sexual conduct, avoidance of blood, and resistance to lawlessness delivered orally in *Jubilees* 7:20–39. It resists classification as an independent work: it is a speech embedded in *Jubilees*, not an extant document authored by the historical Noah ([*Jubilees* 7:20–39](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th6314/content/VanderKam%281989%29Jubilees.pdf)).

- **In the later tradition of Jubilees:** *Prayer against the Evil Spirits*. A petition asking God to restrain the spirits afflicting Noah’s descendants. This is a narrated oral composition, not evidence for a separately circulating text by Noah ([*Jubilees* 10:1–6](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th6314/content/VanderKam%281989%29Jubilees.pdf)).

### Attributed writings and cartographic projects

- **In the later tradition of Jubilees:** *Book of the Division of the Earth*. A written territorial project from which Noah’s sons take their allotted regions; it functions simultaneously as map, title deed, and geopolitical protocol. *Jubilees* never securely supplies it with a distinct title, and scholarship distinguishes this allotment document from other writings attributed to Noah ([*Jubilees* 8:10–9:15](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/division-earths-portions-noahs-sons); [Jacques T. A. G. M. van Ruiten, “Space, Time and Group Identity in Jubilees 8–9”](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?pid=S0259-94222008000100032&script=sci_arttext)).

- **In the later tradition of Jubilees:** *Book of Remedies*. A medical protocol said to record angelically taught remedies against diseases caused by evil spirits. *Jubilees* attributes its writing to Noah, but no independent manuscript bearing this title survives, and scholars dispute whether references to Noah’s books presuppose one lost work, several works, or pseudepigraphic literary fictions ([*Jubilees* 10:10–14](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th6314/content/VanderKam%281989%29Jubilees.pdf); [Chontel Syfox, “Israel’s First Physician and Apothecary”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0951820718805635)).

- **Ancient attribution; no secure date or complete text:** *Book(s) of Noah*. This is not a securely recoverable single obra: ancient sources mention writings of Noah, while modern scholars have variously assigned “Noachic” passages in *1 Enoch*, the Genesis Apocryphon, the Aramaic Levi Document, and Qumran fragments to one or more hypothetical source-books. Michael E. Stone stresses the distinction between texts that explicitly mention a Noah book and passages reconstructed as Noachic by modern scholarship; none can responsibly be listed as Noah’s historically authenticated composition ([Michael E. Stone, “Noah and His Book(s)”](https://apocryphalstone.com/uploads/bibliography/362_Book%28s%29%20attributed.pdf); [Cana Werman, “Qumran and the Book of Noah”](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/qumran-and-the-book-of-noah/)).

### Things not securely classifiable as Noah’s works

- **After the Flood:** *Noah’s Tent*. Genesis calls it his tent but never says that he made it; ownership is not authorship, so it cannot securely enter the catalogue as an obra ([Genesis 9:21](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.9.21?lang=bi)).

- **Traditional lifetime:** *Shem, Ham, and Japheth*. Genesis names Noah as their father, but persons are not catalogued here as his individual “works”: their making is biological, relational, and shared with their unnamed mother, not sole fabrication by Noah ([Genesis 5:32](https://www.sefaria.org/Genesis.5.32?lang=bi)).
