## Works by José Gabriel Condorcanqui Noguera

### Legal projects and petitions

- **1777–1778:** *Representación de Túpac Amaru sobre la mita de Potosí*. Petition presented to the Real Audiencia of Lima on behalf of the communities of Tinta, asking that their compulsory labor at Potosí be ended; it is an advocacy document whose surviving form may incorporate the work of legal representatives rather than Condorcanqui’s unaided prose. [[1](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_2/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-2.pdf), [2](https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/362/420/1474)]

- **1777–1778:** *Escrito de Túpac Amaru a la Audiencia de Lima*. A further legal submission defending the communities’ claims after the visitador José Antonio de Areche rejected the representation; attribution here means that Condorcanqui was the litigant and authorizing voice, not necessarily the professional draftsman. [[1](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_2/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-2.pdf), [2](https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/362/420/1474)]

- **before February 1779:** *Genealogical and legal case for recognition as descendant of Túpac Amaru I*. A documentary project—petitions, proofs, testimony, and counsel’s argument—rather than a single authored book; Condorcanqui pursued it against a rival claim to the lineage and associated cacicazgo, but much of its language belongs to lawyers, witnesses, and earlier family papers. [[2](https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/362/420/1474)]

- **February 1779:** *Power granted to José Antonio García*. A notarized instrument authorizing the Lima lawyer to defend Condorcanqui’s genealogical claim in Spain; its status is juridical action rather than personal composition. [[2](https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/362/420/1474)]

### Surviving personal and administrative correspondence

- **3 October 1779:** *Letter to José Palacios concerning Mateo Quispe*. An administrative letter asking Condorcanqui’s cousin and secretary to assist an Indigenous subordinate in a legal and debt dispute; the documentary edition reproduces it under an editorial descriptive title. [[3](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_2/files/basic-html/page116.html)]

- **1780–1781:** *Correspondence with Micaela Bastidas*. A dispersed body of operational and family letters concerning recruits, weapons, provisions, money, prisoners, intelligence, and military movements. These are individually transmitted documents, often preserved as copies in prosecution files; they resist treatment as a closed “work,” and the extant set cannot safely be assumed complete. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf), [5](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/files/basic-html/page801.html)]

- **1780–1781:** *Letters, commissions, and orders to rebel commanders, curacas, clergy, towns, and provincial authorities*. This is an open administrative corpus rather than one work: surviving originals, copies, extracts, and testimony preserve Condorcanqui’s appointments, mobilization orders, demands for supplies, instructions concerning prisoners, and political appeals. Secretaries participated in drafting and copying, so signature, authorization, dictation, and verbal command do not always amount to the same kind of authorship. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf), [6](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_6/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-6.pdf)]

### Manifestos, edicts, and protocols of insurgent government

- **10 November 1780:** *Proclamation following the execution of Antonio de Arriaga*. A public political act announcing the removal of oppressive offices and exactions and directing Indigenous and Spanish inhabitants to live peaceably. Its wording survives through copies and witness reports, so the exact relationship between the proclaimed words and any autograph text is uncertain. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf)]

- **15 November 1780:** *Edict for the arrest of the corregidor and lieutenant of Lampa*. A protocol delegating authority to Bernardo Sucacagua to arrest the officials, inventory their property, and mobilize provincial forces; it presents insurgent action as execution of royal justice. [[7](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/files/basic-html/page47.html)]

- **16 November 1780:** *Commission to arrest the corregidor of Paruro, detain Europeans, and publish an edict*. An executive commission combining appointment, arrest warrant, and publication order. It belongs to Condorcanqui’s governmental apparatus, although the surviving text is transmitted in a criminal dossier rather than as an autonomous publication. [[6](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_6/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-6.pdf)]

- **16 November 1780:** *Bando de libertad de los esclavos*. A manifesto and operative decree offering freedom to enslaved people who joined the uprising—the best-attested antislavery text associated with Condorcanqui. It was issued at the sanctuary of Tungasuca, but surviving copies and later editorial titles vary, making “abolition of slavery throughout America” a broader retrospective description than the document itself securely warrants. [[2](https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/362/420/1474), [8](https://www.radionacional.gob.pe/noticias/cultural/a-241-anos-de-la-revolucion-de-tupac-amaru-y-micaela-bastidas)]

- **18–19 November 1780:** *Proclamation after the battle of Sangarará*. A manifesto addressed to the peoples of the region, presenting the rebellion as directed against abusive officials rather than the Catholic faith, and seeking adherents after the insurgent victory. Copies differ in headings and wording, and it should not be confused with every later proclamation circulated under Condorcanqui’s authority. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf)]

- **25 November 1780:** *Edict to the inhabitants of Lampa*. A public summons and manifesto designed to bring the province under insurgent authority; the Biblioteca Nacional del Perú’s documentary record identifies the surviving unit and date. [[9](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02199/)]

- **29 November 1780:** *Bando ordering provincial representatives to assemble at Coporaque*. A protocol requiring designated districts to appear at Coporaque on 3 December; it is signed in Condorcanqui’s sovereign style, but survives as a certified copy within later proceedings. [[10](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_6/files/basic-html/page142.html)]

- **December 1780:** *Edict to the province of Chichas*. A manifesto calling Indigenous people, mestizos, criollos, and American-born Spaniards into a common cause against oppressive government. Its precise date and textual form vary between copies and nineteenth-century publication, so “December 1780” is safer than an unsupported exact day. [[11](https://elhistoriador.com.ar/edicto-de-tupac-amaru-ii-manifestando-su-determinacion-de-sacudir-el-yugo-espanol/), [12](https://www.lluisvives.com/obra-visor/relacion-historica-de-los-sucesos-de-la-rebelion-de-jose-gabriel-tupacamaru-en-las-provincias-del-peru-el-ano-de-1780--0/html/)]

- **late 1780–early 1781:** *Appointments, safe-conducts, tax orders, confiscation warrants, and military commissions issued as “José Gabriel Túpac Amaru Inca”*. Collectively these documents made an insurgent government: they assigned offices, regulated resources, authorized coercion, and asserted jurisdiction. They resist enumeration as a stable set because many survive only as copies, enclosures, paraphrases, or references in trials, while others were produced by secretaries in Condorcanqui’s name. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf), [6](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_6/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-6.pdf)]

### Diplomatic and justificatory letters

- **27 November 1780:** *Letter to Bishop Juan Manuel Moscoso y Peralta*. A diplomatic manifesto in epistolary form, defending the rebellion as compatible with Catholic allegiance and directed against abusive corregidores and fiscal impositions. Like other insurgent state papers, it may have been dictated or secretary-drafted under Condorcanqui’s authority. [[13](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/files/basic-html/page240.html)]

- **December 1780–January 1781:** *Letters and capitulatory proposals to the cabildo, clergy, and authorities of Cuzco*. A political project seeking negotiation, entry into Cuzco, and recognition of the insurgents’ reforms while alternating persuasion with military pressure. The documentary record contains several related messages rather than a single fixed “proposal,” and some survive only through hostile copies. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf)]

- **before 6 April 1781:** *Letter to visitador José Antonio de Areche*. A justificatory and negotiating letter sent shortly before Condorcanqui’s capture, explaining the death of Arriaga, presenting demands as conditions for peace, and contesting the officials’ description of the uprising. The heading by which it is now known is editorial rather than an authorial title. [[14](https://www3.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/files/basic-html/page717.html)]

### Contested and materially exceptional texts

- **31 December 1780:** *Message written in blood on a fragment of taffeta*. A material message as much as a text: the Archivo General de Indias catalogues it as a communication by José Gabriel Túpac Amaru written in blood. Its unusual medium is integral to the act, but the archival description alone does not establish whether the surviving hand, blood, and wording are all personally his rather than produced under his direction. [[15](https://pares.mcu.es/ParesBusquedas20/catalogo/find?idA=10&idD=S&idN=20&texto=T%C3%BApac+Amaru)]

- **attributed to 18 March 1781:** *Edicto de coronación*. A manifesto of sovereign rupture, traditionally said to have been found among Condorcanqui’s papers and signed “José I”; unlike the more cautious royalist language of many authenticated proclamations, it declares the kings of Castile usurpers and claims an extensive Inca monarchy. Its authenticity, date, and even status as a document actually promulgated by Condorcanqui remain disputed, so it must be listed as an attribution, not silently accepted as an autograph work. [[2](https://revistas.cultura.gob.pe/index.php/historiaycultura/article/download/362/420/1474), [16](https://profeolazar.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/revolucion-en-los-andes.-la-era-de-tupac-amaru-ii.pdf)]

### Collective obra and political project

- **4 November 1780–6 April 1781:** *The insurgent government and rebellion conventionally called the Rebellion of Túpac Amaru II*. This was Condorcanqui’s largest obra: a collective political, military, ceremonial, administrative, and communicative project made with Micaela Bastidas, commanders, secretaries, messengers, communities, and allied leaders. Calling it his individual “work” would erase those makers; calling it only an event would erase the deliberate institutions, offices, proclamations, symbols, armies, and alternative sovereignty they created. [[4](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/Tupac_Amaru_3/Tupac-Amaru-volumen-3.pdf), [17](https://www.congreso.gob.pe/Docs/FondoEditorial/bicentenario/index.html)]
