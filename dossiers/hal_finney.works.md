## Works by Harold Thomas Finney II

### Video games

- **1979:** *Space Battle*. An Intellivision strategy/action game programmed by Finney; later Atari adaptations should not be mistaken for editions of the same software object. ([MobyGames catalogue](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/))
- **1981:** *Star Strike*. An Intellivision action game for which Finney received a programming credit. ([MobyGames catalogue](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/))
- **1982:** *Armor Battle / Armor Ambush*. Finney programmed the Atari 2600 realization, issued as *Armor Ambush*, of the earlier Intellivision design *Armor Battle*; catalogues vary between the source-game and release titles, so the naming is not silently resolved here. ([MobyGames catalogue](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/), [release history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armor_Ambush))
- **1982:** *Astrosmash / Astroblast*. Finney received the programming credit for the Atari 2600 adaptation, published as *Astroblast*; the catalogue records it under the source title *Astrosmash*. ([MobyGames catalogue](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/))
- **1982:** *Night Stalker / Dark Cavern*. Finney programmed the Atari 2600 adaptation, issued as *Dark Cavern*; again, the catalogue files the credit under the Intellivision source title. ([MobyGames catalogue](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/))
- **1982:** *Space Battle / Space Attack*. Finney received the programming credit for the Atari 2600 adaptation, published as *Space Attack*; it is a distinct software manifestation from the 1979 Intellivision game. ([MobyGames catalogue](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/))
- **1983:** *Adventures of Tron*. A jointly credited Atari 2600 game designed by Finney and programmed with G. Hightower; the hidden title-screen credit names both, while a planned Intellivision version was cancelled and therefore remains an unrealized project rather than a released game. ([MobyGames catalogue and hidden-credit record](https://www.mobygames.com/game/13803/adventures-of-tron/), [catalogue summary](https://www.mobygames.com/person/102253/hal-finney/credits/))

### Privacy software and cryptographic systems

- **1991–1992:** *Pretty Good Privacy 2.0*. A collaborative encryption program rather than Finney’s sole obra: he identifies himself as one of its original programmers working with principal author Philip Zimmermann, so authorship must remain shared. ([Finney’s archived homepage](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/home.html))
- **1992:** *Cypherpunk anonymous remailer*. An operational privacy service and software implementation assembled from Eric Hughes’s evolving remailer design, to which Finney added features; sources consequently describe the first Cypherpunk remailer as the work of Hughes, Finney, and others rather than a securely bounded single-author program. ([Finney’s 1992 messages](https://cryptome.wikileaks.org/2014/09/hal-finney-cpunks-1992.htm), [historical survey](https://www.freehaven.net/~nickm/slides/AnonHistory.pdf))
- **2004:** *RPOW—Reusable Proofs of Work*. A prototype digital-cash system and protocol—client, server, secure-coprocessor code, tokens, and explanatory website—that deliberately joins software, monetary object, and trust architecture. It was functional but remained experimental; Finney’s family later supplied the original source, which the archival repository distinguishes from subsequent maintenance changes. ([archived project and source catalogue](https://github.com/NakamotoInstitute/RPOW), [Finney’s dated project text](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/rpow/))
- **2009:** *Bitcoin testing and corrections*. Finney ran the early client, mined blocks, received its first person-to-person transaction, and reported bugs to Satoshi Nakamoto; this is collaborative technical labor inside another author’s project, not evidence that Finney authored Bitcoin, and no securely delimited “Finney version” survives as an independent work. ([Finney’s retrospective](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin-and-me/))
- **2013–2014:** *bcflick*. An unfinished experimental project using Trusted Computing to protect Bitcoin wallets. It resists classification as a completed work: Finney described continuing to write it despite advanced ALS, but no standard source-code catalogue establishes a finished public release. ([Finney’s retrospective](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin-and-me/), [biographical project record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Finney_%28computer_scientist%29))

### Standards and protocols

- **1998:** *OpenPGP Message Format* (RFC 2440). A collaboratively authored Internet standard specifying the OpenPGP message protocol, written with Jon Callas, Lutz Donnerhacke, and Rodney Thayer; it is a protocol in the strong sense—a text that directs interoperable implementations. ([IETF author bibliography](https://datatracker.ietf.org/person/hal%40finney.org))
- **2007:** *OpenPGP Message Format* (RFC 4880). A revised protocol co-authored with Jon Callas, Lutz Donnerhacke, David Shaw, and Rodney Thayer, superseding RFC 2440 and later itself superseded; it is a new standards document, not merely a reprint. ([RFC Editor](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc4880/), [IETF author bibliography](https://datatracker.ietf.org/person/hal%40finney.org))

### Cryptanalytic demonstrations

- **1995:** *SSL RC4 Challenge*. A performative cryptographic challenge consisting of captured SSL traffic, annotations, and an invitation to recover its export-strength key; it acts as an experiment rather than merely describing one. The first challenge was independently broken by two efforts, and Finney issued a second challenge in August that was also solved. ([contemporary challenge chronology](https://pauillac.inria.fr/~doligez/ssl/), [archived mailing-list dossier](https://bitcoin-institute.pages.dev/entries/threads/emails/cypherpunks/ssl-challenge/), [Finney’s project index](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/home.html))

### Technical expositions and proposals

- **1993, revised 1996:** *Detecting Double-Spending*. A technical exposition of Chaum–Fiat–Naor electronic cash, first posted on 15 October 1993 and revised on 13 March 1996; the revision belongs to the same evolving text rather than constituting an unrelated work. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/chcash2.html))
- **1993:** *Digital Cash & Privacy*. An argumentative essay proposing electronic cash as protection against transaction surveillance. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/dig_cash_priv.html))
- **1993:** *Down with Observers!*. A critique of electronic-cash protocols dependent on tamper-resistant “observer” hardware. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/anti_observers.html))
- **1993:** *First Reaction to Medvinsky NetCash Proposal*. A technical review arguing that the proposed NetCash system fails to provide meaningful transactional anonymity. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/netcash_crit.html))
- **1993:** *Is-A-Person Credentials*. A protocol proposal for proving that a pseudonym represents one distinct person without disclosing that person’s civil identity. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/is_a_person.html))
- **1993:** *PGP Multi-Precision Library Functions*. Technical documentation and a practical protocol for compiling and using PGP’s multiple-precision mathematics library; it documents copyrighted collaborative software rather than claiming that library as Finney’s sole creation. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/pgp_math_lib.html))
- **1994:** *PGP Web of Trust Misconceptions*. A technical essay correcting the common description of PGP trust as indefinitely transitive. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/web_of_trust.html))
- **1994:** *Remailer Abuse Prevention*. A protocol proposal using anonymous, delayed-renewal credentials to discourage remailer abuse without identifying senders. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/remailer_abuse.html))
- **1994:** *For-Pay Remailers*. A combined service-design and economic protocol proposing payment mechanisms for sustainable anonymous remailers. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/pay_remail.html))
- **1994, revised 1995:** *Truly Stealthy PGP*. A cryptographic algorithm note proposing PGP output indistinguishable from random data, first dated 6 March 1994 and revised 7 March 1995. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/stealth_pgp.html))
- **Undated:** *Patent Search on Blind Signatures*. An annotated search result and practical research aid concerning electronic-cash patents; Finney’s collected-essay page preserves it but supplies no composition date, so none is inferred. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/chaum_patents.html), [Finney’s collected-essay index](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/home.html))

### Essays on anonymity, politics, and digital objects

- **1992:** *Why Remailers I*. An essay explaining anonymous remailers as an applied realization of cryptographic privacy ideas. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/why_rem1.html))
- **1993:** *Why Remailers II*. A second, separately dated essay expanding the social and commercial uses of anonymous communication. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/why_rem2.html))
- **1993:** *Democracy vs. Libertarianism*. A short political note arguing that durable libertarian government still requires popular acceptance. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/liberty_demo.html))
- **1993:** *Steganography No Solution*. A political-technical argument that steganography cannot by itself overcome legal suppression of cryptography. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/steg_no_soln.html))
- **1993:** *Protecting Privacy with Electronic Cash*. A published article advocating privacy-preserving electronic payment; it overlaps in subject and date with *Digital Cash & Privacy* but is catalogued as a separate titled text, not silently collapsed into it. ([Satoshi Nakamoto Institute author catalogue](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/authors/hal-finney/))
- **1994:** *Politics vs Technology*. An argument against treating code as a substitute for political action; it is polemic, but not a manifesto in the strict performative sense. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/pol_v_tech.html))
- **1994:** *The Beauty of ECash*. A speculative essay treating uniquely numbered electronic notes as collectible digital objects; its playfulness complicates classification as either technical proposal or fiction. ([Finney archive](https://fennetic.net/irc/finney.org/~hal/beauty_ecash.html))
- **2013:** *Bitcoin and Me*. An autobiographical forum essay recounting Finney’s cryptographic work, early Bitcoin participation, illness, and unfinished programming; it is testimony and retrospective rather than a technical specification. ([Satoshi Nakamoto Institute catalogue](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin-and-me/))
