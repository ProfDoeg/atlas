## Works by Policarpa Salavarrieta

### Needlework

- **Circa 1804–1817:** Untitled garments and sewing. Sources describe Salavarrieta as a working seamstress, but no individually identifiable textile is documented or known to survive; even the occupation is cautiously reported (“parece que”), so this is a lost body of material work rather than a catalogue of attributable objects. Her sewing also furnished the social cover under which she gathered intelligence. ([Banco de la República](https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/index.php/Salavarrieta,_Policarpa), lines 44, 48; [González Stephan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/45381318_LA_AGUJA_SUBVERSIVA_EL_DES-BORDE_DE_LA_CIUDAD_LETRADA), pp. 177–78)

### Clandestine texts and information objects

- **1817:** Untitled messages to and from the Llanos guerrillas. This was a dispersed clandestine correspondence rather than a titled work: Salavarrieta received and sent messages, but the available scholarly accounts provide neither a complete inventory nor surviving texts, and do not establish which messages she wrote, dictated, copied, carried, or transmitted orally. ([Banco de la República](https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/index.php/Salavarrieta,_Policarpa), lines 46–51)

- **1817:** Untitled intelligence reports on royalist forces. These reports comprised information about troop numbers, movements, weapons, orders, and suspected participants, gathered for use in guerrilla ambushes. They resist classification as her authored “works”: the intelligence was operational, probably moved between speech and writing, and no discrete reports securely attributable to her are identified as surviving. ([Banco de la República](https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/index.php/Salavarrieta,_Policarpa), lines 47–50; [González Stephan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/45381318_LA_AGUJA_SUBVERSIVA_EL_DES-BORDE_DE_LA_CIUDAD_LETRADA), pp. 177–78)

- **1817:** Untitled list of royalists and patriots delivered to Alejo Sabaraín. This was an operational information object found in Sabaraín’s possession and said to have been given to him by Salavarrieta; the evidence establishes transmission more securely than physical authorship, and the document is not reported as extant. ([Banco de la República](https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/index.php/Salavarrieta,_Policarpa), line 51)

### Revolutionary project

- **By 1817:** Clandestine support network linking Santafé and the Llanos guerrillas. This was a collective political project rather than a bounded obra: Salavarrieta gathered intelligence, moved messages, bought war matériel, recruited young men, and helped them join patriot forces. Its distributed and secret character prevents a complete itemization, while the sources also stress that she worked with Bibiano Salavarrieta, Alejo Sabaraín, Andrea Ricaurte, and others rather than as a solitary maker. ([Banco de la República](https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/index.php/Salavarrieta,_Policarpa), lines 45–51)

### Oral manifestation

- **14 November 1817:** Untitled scaffold denunciations. Contemporary remembrance attributes defiant anti-royalist speech to Salavarrieta on the way to and at her execution; as an oral manifestation, it acted politically rather than merely describing. It resists treatment as a fixed text or securely recoverable manifesto because no authorial manuscript exists and later accounts preserve differing formulations. ([Banco de la República](https://enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org/index.php/Salavarrieta,_Policarpa), lines 53–57; [González Stephan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/45381318_LA_AGUJA_SUBVERSIVA_EL_DES-BORDE_DE_LA_CIUDAD_LETRADA), p. 178)

Publicaciones del Archivo Nacional de Colombia, vol. XXI, 1949. This archival report is the principal documentary study underlying the later standard biography.
