## Works by G. I. Gurdjieff

### Literary projects, scenarios, manifestos, and authored books

- **1914–c. 1920:** *The Struggle of the Magicians*. An unrealized ballet and Gesamtkunstwerk combining dramatic scenario, music, costume, scenery, and sacred dance; advertised in Moscow in 1914 and subsequently developed with P. D. Ouspensky and Thomas de Hartmann, it survives as a scenario rather than a completed production. ([Gurdjieff chronology](https://www.gurdjieff.org/chronology.htm); [contemporary notice](https://www.gurdjieff.org/voice.htm))

- **1914:** *Glimpses of Truth*. An anonymous pupil’s reconstruction of a conversation with Gurdjieff, prepared under his supervision and used to introduce newcomers to his teaching. Its authorship is therefore collective and uncertain: it is evidence of Gurdjieff’s teaching activity, but resists classification as a text he wrote. ([Driscoll bibliography, pp. 2–3](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf))

- **1922:** *The Aphorisms of the Study House*. Thirty-eight short injunctions or maxims codified by Gurdjieff and inscribed in cipher script on the walls of the Prieuré Study House; collectively they are both manifesto and architectural inscription rather than an ordinary book. ([Basarab Nicolescu, “Gurdjieff’s Philosophy of Nature”](https://www.gurdjieff.org/nicolescu4.htm); [Institute archive](https://www.gurdjieff.org/institute.htm))

- **1923:** *Prospectus Number 1 of the Institute for the Harmonious Development of Man*. A privately printed institutional manifesto and protocol, issued in English, French, and German, that presents the Institute’s history, purpose, curriculum, and methods; although issued in Gurdjieff’s name, its precise compositional process was collaborative. ([Gurdjieff International Review](https://www.gurdjieff.org/prospectus.htm))

- **1924–1935:** *All and Everything: Ten Books in Three Series*. Gurdjieff’s encompassing literary project, conceived after his 1924 automobile accident as ten books serving three distinct transformative purposes. Only three series-titles became established works, and the Third Series remained fragmentary, so the advertised ten-book architecture is an unfinished project rather than ten recoverable books. ([J. Walter Driscoll, “An Introduction to the Writings of G. I. Gurdjieff”](https://www.gurdjieff.org/driscoll3.htm); [Paul Beekman Taylor, “The Making of All and Everything”](https://gurdjieffclub.com/en/articles-essay-P-B-Taylor-The-Making-of-All-and-Everything-1924-o-1950/))

- **1924–1949; published 1950:** *Beelzebub’s Tales to His Grandson: An Objectively Impartial Criticism of the Life of Man*. The First Series of *All and Everything*: an immense philosophical-cosmological fiction, teaching narrative, and deliberately obstructive literary instrument, dictated principally in Russian and Armenian and repeatedly revised in response to readings before pupils. The first English edition appeared just after Gurdjieff’s death, and later editorial revision of the English text has generated continuing textual disputes. ([Driscoll bibliography, pp. 1–2](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf); [Driscoll introduction](https://www.gurdjieff.org/driscoll3.htm))

- **1927–1935; published 1963:** *Meetings with Remarkable Men*. The Second Series of *All and Everything*, combining memoir, travel narrative, parable, and teaching story; its people and journeys cannot safely be treated as straightforward biography, and the published text was reworked from Gurdjieff’s Russian manuscript and A. R. Orage’s English translation. ([Driscoll bibliography, pp. 2–3](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf))

- **early 1930s–May 1935; published 1975:** *Life Is Real Only Then, When “I Am”*. The unfinished Third Series of *All and Everything*, comprising a prologue, introduction, talks, and an incomplete final chapter that stops in mid-paragraph. Gurdjieff ceased writing it in 1935; its use in readings intended to produce effects in particular listeners makes it partly an operative text rather than a conventional memoir or treatise. ([Driscoll bibliography, p. 3](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf))

- **1933; withdrawn 1934:** *The Herald of Coming Good: First Appeal to Contemporary Humanity*. A self-published appeal, prospectus, autobiography, and manifesto announcing Gurdjieff’s plans and soliciting support; he recalled it shortly afterward and instructed pupils not to read it, making withdrawal part of the work’s contested status. ([Driscoll bibliography, p. 1](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf); [Andrew Rawlinson’s review of Driscoll](https://www.gurdjieff.org/rawlinson.htm))

### Spoken teaching and reconstructed texts

- **1914–1931:** *Early talks, lectures, and conversations*. A dispersed body of oral teaching subsequently reconstructed from pupils’ memories and notes in such editorial compilations as *Views from the Real World* and *Gurdjieff’s Early Talks 1914–1931*. These books are posthumous manifestations assembled by others—not books made by Gurdjieff—and their wording is incomplete, non-verbatim, and sometimes contextless. ([Driscoll bibliography, pp. 2–4](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf))

- **1941–1946:** *Wartime Paris meetings*. A body of question-and-answer teaching, practical exercises, and individually directed protocols preserved in pupils’ transcripts and later issued as *Transcripts of Gurdjieff’s Meetings 1941–1946*. Gurdjieff made the pedagogical encounters, but not the posthumous book-form, and the surviving record is neither a verbatim nor necessarily complete text. ([Driscoll bibliography, pp. 3–4](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/Bibliography.pdf))

### Music composed with Thomas de Hartmann

- **1919–1924:** *Music for Movements and sacred-dance demonstrations*. A collaborative corpus in which Gurdjieff supplied, recalled, sang, whistled, played, or dictated melodies and rhythmic requirements while Thomas de Hartmann realized and notated them; it includes music for *The Struggle of the Magicians* and the Paris and American demonstrations. Because inspiration, transcription, harmonization, and orchestration were divided between the two men, sole authorship cannot be assigned securely. ([Schott definitive-edition preface](https://pocketbook.de/en/downloadable/download/sample/sample_id/4023168/?bookId=MTMzODM1MTc%3D); [Gurdjieff Heritage Society](https://www.howarthgurdjieffarchive.org/dushka-2006-website/music.htm))

- **1923–1924:** *Oriental Suite: Complete Orchestrations*. The surviving orchestral and reduced-ensemble scores prepared for the Paris and American Movement demonstrations, written out or dictated by Gurdjieff in collaboration with de Hartmann. The modern title belongs to the archival recording project, not necessarily to a single suite named by Gurdjieff. ([Gurdjieff Heritage Society](https://www.howarthgurdjieffarchive.org/dushka-2006-website/music.htm); [music survey](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/Music.htm))

- **1925–1927:** *Asian Songs and Rhythms*. The first division of the Gurdjieff/de Hartmann piano corpus: notated miniatures derived from melodies, rhythms, and musical impressions Gurdjieff brought to de Hartmann, who supplied their pianistic realization. It is preserved as Volume I of the definitive four-volume edition, whose manuscript archive is the nearest thing to a catalogue raisonné of the collaboration. ([Wergo/Schott account](https://www.gurdjieff.org/wergo.htm); [Gurdjieff Foundation music catalogue](https://gurdjieff-foundation-philadelphia.org/music/))

- **1925–1927:** *Music of the Sayyids and the Dervishes*. The second division of the collaborative piano corpus, consisting of pieces presented as Sayyid, dervish, and related sacred or regional music. Those cultural labels record Gurdjieff’s attributions and compositional framing; they should not be mistaken for independently verified ethnographic provenances. ([Gurdjieff Foundation music catalogue](https://gurdjieff-foundation-philadelphia.org/music/); [Wergo/Schott account](https://www.gurdjieff.org/wergo.htm))

- **1925–1927:** *Hymns, Prayers and Rituals*. The third division of the collaborative piano corpus, conceived less as autonomous concert music than as contemplative, ritual, and pedagogical material within Gurdjieff’s teaching. Its individual pieces remain jointly attributed to Gurdjieff and de Hartmann. ([Gurdjieff Foundation music catalogue](https://gurdjieff-foundation-philadelphia.org/music/); [Wergo/Schott account](https://www.gurdjieff.org/wergo.htm))

- **1919–1927:** *Hymns from a Great Temple and Other Selected Works*. The fourth division of the definitive piano edition, gathering the *Hymns from a Great Temple*, fragments of *The Struggle of the Magicians*, four early pieces, *The Essentuki Prayer*, *Return from a Journey*, *The Initiation of the Priestess*, and *The Bokharian Dervish, Hadji-Asvath-Troov*. It is an editorially constituted remainder and selection, not a single composition made under that collective title. ([Schott Music catalogue](https://www.schott-music.com/de/music-for-the-piano-noq17892.html))

### Recorded music and sonic manifestations

- **25 December 1948–early February 1949:** *New York harmonium improvisations*. Improvised harmonium performances recorded on wire at the Wellington Hotel, sometimes together with Gurdjieff’s spoken stories and talks. They are sonic events rather than written compositions, and several survive without stable individual titles. ([Gert-Jan Blom’s catalogue description](https://www.soundohm.com/product/improvisations))

- **1 April–16 October 1949:** *Paris harmonium recordings*. Gurdjieff’s recorded improvisations, organized on the original tapes as the First, Mesoteric, American, and Second Series; with the New York wires and untitled addenda, the restored catalogue contains 136 tracks and more than nineteen hours. The tracks resist treatment as 136 conventionally composed works because many are improvised, numbered rather than titled, or contain speech as well as music. ([Gert-Jan Blom’s catalogue description](https://www.soundohm.com/product/improvisations); [Gurdjieff Heritage Society](https://www.howarthgurdjieffarchive.org/dushka-2006-website/music.htm))

### Movements, dances, and embodied protocols

- **1918–1924:** *The early Movements and sacred dances*. A large, changing body of choreographed exercises—including Obligatories, occupational rhythms, dervish exercises, women’s dances, prayer rituals, and mathematical “Multiplications”—created or adapted for instruction and public demonstration. Gurdjieff attributed some material to Central Asian, Tibetan, Afghan, Turkish, and other traditions, but those origins lack independent ethnographic verification; some Movements disappeared because he prohibited notation, so no complete catalogue is possible. ([“Gurdjieff’s Movements and Sacred Dances,” pp. 3–8](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/movements.pdf))

- **1919–1924:** *Movement demonstrations*. Staged presentations in Tiflis, Paris, and the United States combining dancers, exercises, music, costume, lighting, spoken explanation, and theatrical arrangement. Each was a temporary Gesamtkunstwerk and pedagogical manifestation rather than merely a performance of separable dances. ([“Gurdjieff’s Movements and Sacred Dances,” pp. 3–5](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/movements.pdf))

- **1948–1949:** *The Thirty-nine*. A fixed series of thirty-nine late Movement exercises worked out in Paris, combining exact postures, contrapuntal bodily actions, counting, attention, and sometimes prayer. They are embodied protocols whose full identity depends on performance and transmission, not solely on choreography or notation. ([“Gurdjieff’s Movements and Sacred Dances,” pp. 5–7](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/movements.pdf))

- **1949:** *The American Series*. Seven additional late Movements developed in New York and accepted by Gurdjieff as complete; together with *The Thirty-nine*, they constitute the forty-six late Movements he regarded as fixed. Other exercises from the same period were rejected, altered, or abandoned, so “hundreds created” does not mean hundreds of completed works. ([“Gurdjieff’s Movements and Sacred Dances,” pp. 5–6](https://www.gurdjieffandfourthway.org/pdf/movements.pdf))

### Institutions, environments, and the teaching-work

- **c. 1917–1949:** *The Work* or *the Fourth Way*. A developing system of collective and individual practice involving attention, self-observation, physical labor, music, dance, reading, oral instruction, and deliberately arranged conditions. It resists being called a discrete work: it is better understood as Gurdjieff’s overarching pedagogical project, manifested differently for particular people, places, and times rather than fixed in a single authoritative text. ([Gurdjieff International Review overview](https://www.gurdjieff.org/contents.en.htm); [Institute archive](https://www.gurdjieff.org/institute.htm))

- **1919–1924:** *Institute for the Harmonious Development of Man*. An educational, experimental, and communal project first publicly organized in Tiflis and subsequently re-established at the Prieuré near Fontainebleau in 1922. Its “made thing” was not simply an organization but an arranged way of life joining study, labor, performance, diet, interpersonal experiment, and spiritual discipline. ([Prospectus Number 1](https://www.gurdjieff.org/prospectus.htm); [Institute archive](https://www.gurdjieff.org/institute.htm))

- **1923:** *The Study House at the Prieuré*. A collaboratively constructed and decorated Movement hall made from sections of a former aircraft hangar under Gurdjieff’s direction, with Alexandre de Salzmann responsible for much of its design. Combining architecture, textiles, light, scent, fountains, instruments, cipher inscriptions, performance, and teaching, it is properly a Gesamtkunstwerk; attributing the physical building to Gurdjieff alone would erase the labor of de Salzmann and the Institute’s pupils. ([Basarab Nicolescu](https://www.gurdjieff.org/nicolescu4.htm))
