## Works by Yolande of Aragon

### Commissioned illuminated manuscripts

- **c. 1410–1420:** *Hours of René of Anjou (Paris Hours)*. An illuminated book of hours, now BnF MS Latin 1156A, probably commissioned for Yolande’s son René; assigning the commission specifically to Yolande rather than jointly to her and Louis II remains conjectural. Its calendar and decoration were subsequently altered, so the surviving manuscript is a layered object rather than an intact realization of one patron’s project. ([BnF/Biblissima catalogue](https://iiif.biblissima.fr/collections/manifest/5ff3fa3f6c0cb4032b1566c698e22317e65b905b); [L’Estrange’s study](https://books.google.com/books/about/Holy_motherhood.html?id=TSo4AQAAIAAJ))

- **after 1417–1431:** *Hours of Isabella Stuart (Fitzwilliam Hours)*. A lavish book of hours with more than 500 figural scenes, made in Angers by the Giac, Rohan, and Madonna Masters and their assistants; the Fitzwilliam Museum judges that Yolande most probably commissioned it for herself or for her daughter Yolande of Anjou, perhaps as a marriage gift in 1431. Later Breton owners extensively modified it, making the extant codex a continuing dynastic creation rather than solely Yolande’s finished work. ([Fitzwilliam Museum](https://fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/illuminated/manuscript/discover/the-hours-of-isabella-stuart/folio/folio-2r-206))

- **c. 1430–1435 (dating disputed):** *Grandes Heures de Rohan (Rohan Hours)*. An illuminated book of hours, BnF MS Latin 9471, generally attributed to Yolande’s patronage but not documented by a surviving commission: proposed dates range from about 1418 to the early 1430s, and proposed intended recipients include Charles VII, Yolande of Anjou, and a member of the Rohan family connected with an unrealized Angevin–Rohan marriage. It is therefore best classified as an attributed commission and dynastic project, not as a securely documented personal creation. ([Millard Meiss and Marcel Thomas bibliography and dating discussion](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781915837042_A49465702/preview-9781915837042_A49465702.pdf); [Jean Porcher](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bsnaf_0081-1181_1950_num_1945_1_4041); [Elizabeth L’Estrange review](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/16813))

### Monumental patronage

- **c. 1425–early 1430s:** *Stained-glass program in the north transept of Le Mans Cathedral*. A monumental cycle financed by Yolande and completed in the early 1430s, including princely donor portraits; it is an architectural manifestation made through patronage, design, and workshop labor, not an object physically authored by Yolande. The precise division of responsibility between Yolande, Charles VII, other donors, and the participating artists is not completely recoverable. ([Fitzwilliam Museum](https://www.fitzmuseum.cam.ac.uk/illuminated/manuscript/discover/the-hours-of-isabella-stuart/owner/yolande-of-aragon/folio/folio-139v-227); [Louis Grodecki bibliography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Mans_Cathedral))

### Diplomatic and juridical acts

- **3 July 1417:** *Agreement for the marriage of Louis III of Anjou and Isabelle of Brittany*. A sealed diplomatic protocol made jointly by Yolande, Jean V of Brittany, and Louis III to preserve friendship between their houses and arrange the marriage. Because chancery personnel formulated and engrossed such instruments, it resists treatment as a personally written “work,” but it is an operative text issued under Yolande’s authority. ([Actes princiers, critical digital edition](https://actesprinciers.huma-num.fr/corpus/Anjou/Yolande_i/1417_07_03a))

- **1417:** *Alliance with Jean V, Duke of Brittany*. A sealed political compact known through the French national sigillographic inventory; it is an enacted agreement rather than a literary composition, and its documentary survival should not be confused with sole authorship by Yolande. ([Sigilla, act Ad 44–E 179/4](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/17091))

- **1424:** *Letters patent concerning the proposed marriage of Louis III and Isabelle of Brittany*. A chancery instrument through which Yolande acted in a dynastic marriage project. The surviving catalogue record establishes the act and its seal but does not justify assigning its wording personally to her. ([Sigilla, Archives nationales J 409 no. 49](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/17091))

- **1431:** *Marriage settlement for Yolande of Anjou and Francis of Brittany*. A juridical and dynastic protocol providing Yolande’s daughter with rights in Beaufort-en-Vallée and Château-Fromont and specifying her dower. The instrument records a negotiated project involving several parties, so it is Yolande’s act without being straightforwardly her authored text. ([Sigilla, act Ad 44–E 10/7](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/17091))

- **16 February 1440:** *Donation of Yolande’s Iberian claims and possessions to Marie of Anjou*. An act granting her daughter claims to Cocentaina, Borja, Magallón, and other inherited Aragonese and Catalan holdings; it survives through a later vidimus rather than as an uncontested original. It is a performative juridical text—a manifesto in the literal sense of making a transfer operative—rather than a descriptive work. ([Archives nationales, J 877–903 inventory, item 32](https://www.archivesnationales.culture.gouv.fr/chan/chan/fonds/EGF/SA/InvSAPDF/SA_index_J/J0877_0903.pdf))

### Sigillographic manifestation

- **documented 1417–1431:** *Great seal of Yolande of Aragon*. A round, approximately 50-mm armorial seal combining the emblems of Jerusalem, Anjou-Sicily, and Aragon, with a fragmentarily preserved French legend; four recorded impressions authenticated her acts. It is a repeated manifestation of sovereign identity rather than a uniquely titled artwork, and the surviving evidence does not establish that Yolande personally designed or cut its matrix. ([Sigilla](https://sigilla.irht.cnrs.fr/17091))

### Unfixed documentary corpus

- **c. 1400–1442:** *Letters, mandates, financial orders, and diplomatic instructions issued in Yolande’s name*. These administrative texts constitute a distributed chancery corpus rather than a fixed, authorially controlled collected work: Yolande authorized or dictated them, while clerks normally drafted and copied them, and no complete critical edition or catalogue raisonné of the corpus exists. Zita Eva Rohr’s scholarly biography reconstructs her activity from dispersed archival letters and acts; consequently, presenting a handful of surviving documents as “everything she wrote” would create a false canon. ([Rohr, *Yolande of Aragon (1381–1442): Family and Power*](https://books.google.com/books?id=b-sYDAAAQBAJ))
