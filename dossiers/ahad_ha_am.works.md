## Works by Ahad Ha'am

### Collected obra

- **1902–1913:** *כל כתבי אחד העם* (*Kol kitve Aḥad Ha-‘am*; “Complete Writings of Ahad Ha’am”). The author-supervised collected obra published by Aḥi’asaf in Warsaw; its architecture—essays, small polemical pieces, memoirs, and additions—is the basis of the inventory below. “Complete” here means the contents admitted to this edition, not everything he ever wrote.
- **1895–1925:** *על פרשת דרכים* (*‘Al parashat derakhim*; “At the Crossroads”). A four-part, repeatedly enlarged collected obra of essays rather than a single continuous book; revisions, supplements, and paired articles make its boundaries porous.
- **1923–1925:** *אגרות אחד העם* (*Igrot Aḥad Ha-‘am*; “Letters of Ahad Ha’am”). A six-volume edited correspondence corpus. Its thousands of situational letters resist treatment as independently intended “works,” while the selected and arranged corpus is nevertheless a substantial post hoc obra.

### Programmatic and Zionist essays

- **1889:** *לא זה הדרך!* (*Lo zeh ha-derekh!*; “This Is Not the Way!”), first article. A programmatic intervention that helped initiate cultural Zionism; it operates partly as manifesto by rejecting immediate colonizing enthusiasm without cultural preparation.
- *לא זה הדרך*, second article. A continuation or reconsideration of the first intervention, not an autonomous book.
- *הכהנים והעם* (“The Priests and the People”). A political-cultural essay distinguishing leadership from the nation it claims to lead.
- *פצעי אוהב* (“Wounds of a Friend”). A critical Zionist essay whose rebuke is framed as internal, committed criticism.
- **1891:** *אמת מארץ ישראל*, first article (“Truth from the Land of Israel”). A report-essay made from Ahad Ha’am’s first journey to Ottoman Palestine; travel observation becomes political diagnosis.
- **1893:** *אמת מארץ ישראל*, second article. A second journey-report and reassessment; together the two articles form a serial obra rather than one travel book.
- *מלואים ל״אמת מארץ־ישראל״* (“Supplement to ‘Truth from the Land of Israel’”). A corrective or extension whose dependent status resists classification as a wholly separate work.
- *מילואים ל״אמת מארץ־ישראל״ למאמר ראשון* (“Supplement to the First ‘Truth from the Land of Israel’ Article”). Another attached textual layer, later also incorporated among the letters.
- *בית הספר ביפו ושאלת הפועלים* (“The School in Jaffa and the Workers’ Question”). A public-policy essay concerning settlement institutions, education, and labor.
- *הישוב ואפיטרופסיו* (“The Yishuv and Its Guardians”), second article. A polemical analysis of patronage and authority in the Jewish settlement; the surviving title explicitly marks it as part of a sequence.
- *שאלות ארץ ישראל: בתי הספר ביפו*, first article (“Questions of the Land of Israel: The Schools in Jaffa”). An educational-policy essay and first installment of an incompletely represented sequence.
- *מדינת־היהודים ו״צרת־היהודים״* (“The Jewish State and the Jewish Problem”). A major critique of political Zionism: a conceptual essay that also acts as a counter-program.
- *ה״מדיניות״ וישוב ארץ ישראל* (“‘Politics’ and the Settlement of the Land of Israel”). The second component of the political-Zionism sequence.
- *ה״מדיניות״ ואוצר הישוב* (“‘Politics’ and the Settlement Fund”). The third component of that sequence, directed at Zionist institutional finance.
- *הקונגרס הציוני הראשון* (“The First Zionist Congress”). A report and critical political reading of the congress.
- *הקונגרס ויוצרו* (“The Congress and Its Creator”). A polemical assessment of Theodor Herzl’s authorship of the congress movement.
- *לפני הקונגרס היהודי השני* (“Before the Second Jewish Congress”). An anticipatory political intervention.
- *הקונגרס הציוני השני* (“The Second Zionist Congress”). A critical report-essay.
- *הקונגרס הציוני השלישי* (“The Third Zionist Congress”). A critical report-essay continuing the congress sequence.
- *אספה של חובבי ציון* (“An Assembly of Ḥovevei Zion”). An occasional report whose subject is organizational practice.
- *שלוחי עם עני* (“Emissaries of a Poor People”). A polemical reflection on Zionist representatives and representation.
- *הגיעה השעה!* (“The Time Has Come!”). A paired-letter intervention: its epistolary form and public argumentative purpose make it both correspondence and manifesto.
- *סך הכל* (“Sum Total”). A retrospective reckoning with the Zionist movement.
- *הוספה ל״סך הכל״* (“Supplement to ‘Sum Total’”). A dependent after-text, meaningful precisely because it reopens an apparently conclusive essay.
- *האחרון* (“The Last”). A late public essay whose title asserts finality, although later texts and supplements complicate that status.
- *דרך החיים* (“The Way of Life”). A late programmatic essay.
- *מלואים ל״דרך החיים״* (“Supplement to ‘The Way of Life’”). An attached clarification rather than a fully independent treatise.
- *הצעד הראשון על ״דרך החיים״* (“The First Step on ‘The Way of Life’”). A practical sequel to the preceding program.
- *הציוניות ותיקון העולם* (“Zionism and the Reform of the World”). A conceptual essay on the reach and limits of the Zionist project.
- *״שלילת הגלות״* (“The Negation of Exile”). A critical examination of a Zionist slogan and the concept embedded in it.
- *״בעקבות משיחא״* (“In the Footsteps of the Messiah”). A diagnosis of messianic political expectation.
- *תורה מציון* (“Teaching from Zion”). A short programmatic intervention on the cultural authority of Palestine.
- *אחר עשר שנים* (“After Ten Years”). A retrospective essay measuring a movement or argument against elapsed time.
- *בנין* (“Building”). A brief programmatic text in which construction is cultural and institutional as well as material.
- *קל מבשר* (“A Herald’s Voice”). A public letter or essay occupying the border between announcement and argument.
- *הבוכים* (“Those Who Weep”). A polemical occasional essay.
- *קול מן החוץ* (“A Voice from Outside”). An intervention whose title foregrounds the author’s deliberately marginal position.
- *אמת מרה* (“A Bitter Truth”). A compressed polemical essay.
- *גואל חדש* (“A New Redeemer”). A skeptical response to political or ideological deliverance.
- *החטא וענשו* (“The Sin and Its Punishment”). A polemical diagnosis cast in moral-causal form.
- *מצוה הבאה בעבירה* (“A Commandment Fulfilled through a Transgression”). A short ethical-political argument.
- *החלוקה* (“The Ḥalukkah”). A critical treatment of the traditional system of charitable support in Palestine.
- *״הידעתם הארץ״?* (“Do You Know the Land?”). A short polemic against abstract or uninformed Palestine-talk.
- *״מעשינו״* (“Our Deeds”). A critical note on the discrepancy between Zionist rhetoric and practice.
- *לאומיות חדשה* (“A New Nationalism”). A short conceptual intervention.
- *כוח המעשים* (“The Power of Deeds”). A political-ethical miniature on action and its authority.
- *סם חיים* (“Elixir of Life”). A brief ideological essay.
- *״הוכוח״* (“The ‘Debate’”). A polemical miniature whose quotation-marked title questions whether genuine debate is occurring.
- *״קול ישראל״* (“The ‘Voice of Israel’”). A brief publicistic response.
- *שבת וציוניות* (“Sabbath and Zionism”). A short essay testing national ideology against inherited religious practice.
- *״סבלנות״ של קנאים* (“The ‘Tolerance’ of Zealots”). A polemic in which the quoted term is itself under dispute.
- *יחיד ורבים* (“Individual and Many”). A political-ethical essay on individual and collective authority.
- *שאלה של עורך* (“An Editor’s Question”). An editorial intervention rather than a detached essay.
- *״מלה חדשה״* (“A ‘New Word’”). A brief response to an ideological coinage or claim.
- **1902:** *״אלטניילנד״* (“Altneuland”). A critical review-essay on Herzl’s utopian novel, not a work titled *Altneuland* authored by Ahad Ha’am.
- *על שתי הסעפים* (“Between Two Branches”). A late diagnostic essay.
- *על שתי הסעיפים*, supplement. An attached continuation whose variant spelling and subordinate designation should not be silently normalized into a separate stable title.
- *שלטון השכל* (“The Rule of Reason”). A late philosophical-political essay.
- *נסיון שלא הצליח* (“An Experiment That Did Not Succeed”). A retrospective account of an institutional or ideological attempt.

### Essays on Judaism, ethics, history, and national culture

- *רבי מרדכי עליאשברג* (“Rabbi Mordecai Eliasberg”). An intellectual portrait and historical-critical essay.
- *ד״ר פינסקר ומחברתו* (“Dr. Pinsker and His Pamphlet”). A public letter or essay about Leon Pinsker and *Auto-Emancipation*.
- *סבלנות!* (“Patience!”). An open-letter intervention whose exclamatory address makes it an act in a controversy.
- *האדם באהל* (“The Person in the Tent”). An epistolary-philosophical essay.
- *תורה שבלב* (“Teaching of the Heart”). An epistolary essay on inward conviction and inherited doctrine.
- *דברי שלום* (“Words of Peace”). A conciliatory intervention seeking a modus vivendi between religious and secular Jewish camps.
- *חשבון הנפש* (“An Accounting of the Soul”). A collective moral audit rather than private confession.
- *עבדות בתוך חירות* (“Slavery amid Freedom”). A philosophical-cultural essay on external emancipation and internal dependence.
- *חצי־נחמה* (“Half-Consolation”). A historical-cultural essay whose title deliberately withholds full resolution.
- *בין קודש לחול* (“Between Sacred and Profane”). One of the *Perurim* fragments: a compact philosophical essay.
- *מדת הדין ומדת הרחמים* (“The Attribute of Justice and the Attribute of Mercy”). A *Perurim* fragment on competing ethical dispositions.
- *לתולדות החיוב והשלילה* (“Toward a History of Affirmation and Negation”). A short conceptual history.
- *מוקדם ומאוחר בחיים* (“Earlier and Later in Life”). A philosophical-cultural miniature.
- *עבר ועתיד* (“Past and Future”). A fragment on historical consciousness and national continuity.
- *שתי רשויות* (“Two Authorities”). A philosophical fragment on divided authority.
- *חקוי והתבוללות* (“Imitation and Assimilation”). A cultural-theoretical essay distinguishing borrowing from loss of identity.
- *כהן ונביא* (“Priest and Prophet”). A typological essay contrasting conserving institutions with transformative moral vision.
- *שלוש מדרגות* (“Three Degrees”). A philosophical miniature.
- *שנוי הערכין* (“Transvaluation of Values”). A conceptual essay; the collected edition also preserves a supplement tied to the preceding “Three Degrees.”
- *שלש מדרגות*, alternate or revised first component. The edition’s duplication indicates textual revision or variant arrangement rather than two securely independent works.
- *שינוי הערכין*, supplement to the preceding article. A dependent addition, not a freestanding book.
- *המוסר הלאומי* (“National Morality”). A sustained philosophical inquiry listed by the digital edition as “study” rather than ordinary essay.
- *התורה והעבודה* (“Teaching and Labor”). A cultural-ethical essay.
- *תחית הרוח* (“Revival of the Spirit”). A programmatic cultural essay.
- **1904:** *משה* (“Moses”). An interpretive essay about Moses as a formative national type rather than a historical biography in the ordinary sense.
- *בשר ורוח* (“Flesh and Spirit”). A philosophical essay on material and spiritual dimensions of national existence.
- *היובל* (“The Jubilee”). An occasional commemorative essay.
- *השומרונים* (“The Samaritans”). A historical-cultural miniature.
- *שמות שנתרוקנו מתכנם* (“Names Emptied of Their Content”). A conceptual critique of inherited terms that survive after their meanings have shifted.
- *רב לאומי* (“A National Rabbi”). A short institutional and cultural argument.
- *דור תהפוכות* (“A Generation of Upheavals”). A diagnosis of generational transformation.
- *חקוי של התבטלות* (“Imitation through Self-Effacement”). A companion in theme to “Imitation and Assimilation.”
- *נחלת אבות* (“Ancestral Inheritance”). A cultural essay on tradition as inheritance.
- *איוב ופרומיתיוס* (“Job and Prometheus”). A comparative mythic-philosophical essay.
- *הפרוגרס ושנאת ישראל* (“Progress and Hatred of Israel”). A historical-political critique of the assumption that modern progress necessarily abolishes antisemitism.
- *בעלי תשובה* (“Penitents” or “Returnees”). A public letter or essay on ideological return.
- *מזרח ומערב* (“East and West”). A public letter or essay on cultural difference.
- *מלים ומושגים* (“Words and Concepts”). A conceptual essay distinguishing terminology from the ideas it purports to carry.
- *נשכחות* (“Forgotten Things”). A late essay of historical-cultural recovery.
- *שתי מצבות* (“Two Memorial Stones”). A memorial diptych, internally divided into “The First” and “The Last”; those labels are sections, not secure independent titles.
- *״סימן השאלה״* (“The ‘Question Mark’”). A brief conceptual-polemical essay.
- *ד״ר יצחק קמינר* (“Dr. Isaac Kaminer”). A memorial or intellectual portrait included among the late gleanings.
- *רמזים* (“Hints”). A short reflective piece whose deliberately slight title resists elevation into a treatise.
- *י״ל פינסקר* (“Y. L. Pinsker”). A commemorative or interpretive miniature.
- *א״ז רבינוביץ* (“A. Z. Rabinowitz”). A commemorative address or short portrait.
- *חג הרוח* (“Festival of the Spirit”). An occasional cultural address.
- *״היהדות העותומנית״* (“‘Ottoman Jewry’”). A short publicistic essay.
- *עד מתי?* (“Until When?”). A late polemical appeal.

### Essays on language, literature, education, and editing

- *הלשון וספרותה* (“The Language and Its Literature”). The first component of *On the Language Question*.
- *הלשון ודקדוקה* (“The Language and Its Grammar”). The second component of *On the Language Question*.
- *על דבר אוצר היהדות בלשון עברית* (“Concerning a Treasury of Judaism in Hebrew”). A proposal for a Hebrew-language reference enterprise: at once essay and unrealized or only partly realized project.
- *אל הקוראים* (“To the Readers”). An editorial address whose function is performative: it establishes relations and expectations between periodical and readership.
- *תעודת ״השילוח״* (“The Mission of *Ha-Shiloaḥ*”). The first essay in *On the Question of Hebrew Literature* and effectively an editorial manifesto.
- *צורך ויכולת* (“Need and Capacity”). The second essay in that sequence, testing literary aspiration against available cultural resources.
- *עצה טובה* (“Good Advice”). The third essay in the sequence, framed as counsel but functioning as editorial policy.
- *ספרי עם* (“Books for the People”). A programmatic essay on popular literature.
- *סופרינו וקוראינו* (“Our Writers and Our Readers”). An essay on the literary system rather than on isolated books.
- *מבקרינו* (“Our Critics”). A polemical essay about the institution of criticism.
- *מהפכה ספרותית* (“A Literary Revolution”). A short diagnosis of literary change.
- *הצעות ספרותיות* (“Literary Proposals”). A protocol-like text: it advances practical measures for Hebrew literary culture.
- *דיבור ושתיקה* (“Speech and Silence”). A literary-ethical miniature.
- *תחיה ובריאה* (“Revival and Creation”). An essay distinguishing restoration from genuinely new cultural making.
- *פרידה* (“Farewell”). A public letter marking an editorial or institutional departure.
- *ריב לשונות* (“The Language Conflict”). A publicistic intervention in the struggle over the language of Jewish education.
- *החנוך הלאומי* (“National Education”). A policy address or essay; it also appeared in the jointly issued volume *Language and Education*.
- *על הגימנסיה העברית ביפו* (“On the Hebrew Gymnasium in Jaffa”). An educational-policy intervention.
- *לשאלת הלשון בבתי־הספר בארץ ישראל* (“On the Language Question in the Schools of the Land of Israel”). A protocol-like policy text concerning the language of instruction.
- *לביאליק* (“To Bialik”). A public or semi-public letter whose addressee and occasion remain part of the object.
- *לפתיחת ״המכון למדעי היהדות״ בירושלים* (“For the Opening of the Institute of Jewish Studies in Jerusalem”). A ceremonial address that performs institutional inauguration.
- *לפתיחת הטכניקום בחיפה* (“For the Opening of the Technikum in Haifa”). A ceremonial address attached to the making of an educational institution.
- **1907:** *הקדמה ל״הלשון והחנוך: נאומים בחברת ‘מפיצי השכלה בישראל’״* (“Preface to *Language and Education*”). A jointly authored or jointly attributed prefatory text associated with Ahad Ha’am, Judah Leib Katzenelson, and Simon Dubnow; sole authorship should not be assumed.
- *הקדמה למהדורה ראשונה* (“Preface to the First Edition”). An authorial framing text for *At the Crossroads*.
- *הקדמה למהדורה שניה* (“Preface to the Second Edition”). A revised framing text documenting the collection’s changing status.
- *הקדמה למהדורה חדשה* (“Preface to a New Edition”). Another layer in the author’s reshaping of the collected obra.
- *הקדמה לחלק השלישי* (“Preface to Part Three”). A structural and interpretive threshold within *At the Crossroads*.

### Small writings and public letters

- *״מכתבי יהודי פולני״* (“Letters of a Polish Jew”). A publicistic letter-sequence; the quotation-marked collective title is more stable than its individual components.
- *עוללות* (“Gleanings”). An author-arranged late collection of eleven previously printed small writings. Ahad Ha’am himself stressed that not all are letters; its resistance to a single genre is part of the obra.
- *הקונגרס הראשון* (“The First Congress”). A small retrospective piece distinct in the collected edition from the longer essay titled “The First Zionist Congress.”
- *מכתב לאספה* (“Letter to an Assembly”). An occasional intervention whose identity depends on its institutional occasion.
- *שני מכתבים אל עורך ״הארץ״* (“Two Letters to the Editor of *Haaretz*”). A paired public correspondence unit rather than a single essay.
- *מגלת־סתרים של אחד העם* (“Ahad Ha’am’s Secret Scroll”). A small satirical or private-public text; the collected title should not be taken as evidence of an independently issued book.
- *שונות* (“Miscellanea”). A residual category in the collected edition, not a securely unified intentional work.
- *מכתבים* (“Letters”). A supplementary correspondence grouping; individual letters are made texts, but the untitled grouping resists treatment as one author-designed work.

### Memoir and journey writing

- *פרקי זכרונות* (“Chapters of Memoirs”). A memoir sequence, episodic rather than a continuous autobiography.
- *מסקנות* (“Conclusions”). A reflective memoir-text or retrospective appendix.
- *על ראש הכרמל* (“On the Summit of Carmel”). A place-based journey or memoir sketch.
- *ממסעותי בא״י* (“From My Journeys in the Land of Israel”). A travel-memory text; it makes journeys into retrospective narrative rather than serving as a guidebook.
- *רשימות חדשות* (“New Notes”). A notebook-like memoir grouping whose generic incompleteness is signaled by the title.

### Translation

- **1921; collected 1925:** *אבטואימנציפציא* (“Auto-Emancipation”). Ahad Ha’am’s Hebrew translation of Leon Pinsker’s German pamphlet; the argument is Pinsker’s obra, while the Hebrew verbal object is Ahad Ha’am’s translational making. Ahad Ha’am stated that it first appeared in the 1921 *Pinsker Book* and was later appended to his collected writings.

### Editorial, institutional, and collective projects

- **1889–1897:** *בני משה* (*Bene Moshe*; “Sons of Moses”). A secret cultural-Zionist association founded and led by Ahad Ha’am. It is better classified as a collective project and organizational protocol than as his sole obra; its membership, practices, and failures were made collaboratively.
- **1893 onward:** *אחיאסף* (*Aḥi’asaf*). A Hebrew publishing enterprise that Ahad Ha’am helped establish and for which he worked as editor and literary authority. The press is a collective institutional creation, not a book authored by him.
- **1896–1902:** *השילוח* (*Ha-Shiloaḥ*), Ahad Ha’am’s founding editorship. He founded and served as chief editor of the Hebrew monthly, shaping its name, divisions, selection policy, prose norms, and intellectual program. The journal under his editorship is a collaborative serial Gesamtkunstwerk of essays, scholarship, criticism, and literature, although its contributors’ texts are not thereby his works.
- **Late nineteenth–early twentieth century:** The cultural or spiritual Zionist program. This is a dispersed project manifested through essays, editorial decisions, organizations, and mentorship rather than a single titled text; calling it an individual “work” risks erasing its collective realization.
- **Early twentieth century:** Hebrew educational and cultural infrastructure in Palestine. Ahad Ha’am advocated Hebrew schools and participated in efforts connected with institutions including the Technion; these are collective projects to which he contributed, not buildings or institutions he alone made.
- **Before 1927, unrealized:** Proposed book on Jewish ethics. A much-anticipated project that Ahad Ha’am never completed; scholarship reports uncertainty over whether composition even began. It must therefore be recorded as an intention, not silently promoted into a lost book.
