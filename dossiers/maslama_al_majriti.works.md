## Works by Maslama ibn Ahmad al-Majriti

### Mathematical and astronomical protocols

- **Undated; before 1007:** *Kitāb al-Muʿāmalāt* (“Book of Commercial Transactions”). A lost computational protocol for problems of trade, land measurement, and taxation, reportedly combining arithmetic, geometry, and algebra. Its title and contents are known only through later testimony, especially Ibn Khaldūn; Juan Vernet includes it among the works reasonably accepted as genuine. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))

- **Undated; before 1007:** *Treatise on the Astrolabe*. A very short protocol for constructing and using the instrument, preserved in Arabic and distinct from the much longer astrolabe treatise of his pupil Ibn al-Ṣaffār that was also circulated under al-Majrīṭī’s name. Vernet and María Asunción Catalá edited it among his mathematical works. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit); [Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

- **Undated; before 1007:** *Notes on the Theorem of Menelaus*. Mathematical annotations concerning the principal trigonometric tool then used in spherical astronomy; the standard biographical bibliographies treat them as genuine, but do not supply a secure independent title or date. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit); [Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

### Revisions, commentaries, and collaborative astronomical tables

- **About 979–before 1007:** *Zīj of al-Khwārizmī–Maslama* (revision of al-Khwārizmī’s *Sindhind zīj*). This is not an independently authored “book” so much as a collaborative remaking of an astronomical handbook: al-Majrīṭī and his pupil Ibn al-Ṣaffār replaced or supplemented tables, converted the chronology to the Islamic lunar calendar, and adapted observer-dependent material to Córdoba. Al-Khwārizmī’s Arabic original and the Andalusian Arabic recension are lost; the composite survives principally through Adelard of Bath’s Latin translation, making the exact division of labor resistant to attribution. ([Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm); [Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))

- **Undated; before 1007:** *Revision of astronomical tables by al-Battānī*. Vernet’s standard work-list credits al-Majrīṭī with revising some of al-Battānī’s tables, but gives neither a fixed title nor a surviving, clearly bounded text. It therefore resists treatment as a separate authored book and is best classified as technical redaction. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))

- **Undated; before 1007:** *Taʿālīq ʿalā Kitāb Baṭlamyūs fī Tasṭīḥ basīṭ al-kura* (“Notes on Ptolemy’s Book on the Planispheric Projection of the Sphere”). Eleven mathematical annotations on propositions in Ptolemy’s *Planisphaerium*, surviving in one Arabic manuscript and incorporated into the work’s Latin transmission. Earlier accounts called al-Majrīṭī the Arabic translator, but current scholarship instead regards him as the reviser and commentator of an existing eastern Arabic version. ([Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus, work C.6.1](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/work/206); [Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

- **978; with undated accompanying material:** *Faṣl laysa min al-kitāb min kalām Maslama b. Aḥmad* (“A Chapter Not in the Book, from the Words of Maslama ibn Aḥmad”). An additional cluster attached to the *Planisphaerium* commentary: discussions of projecting the zodiac and horizon, a protocol for locating stars on an astrolabe’s rete, instructions for constructing an astrolabe plate, and a star table dated 367 AH/978 and based on observations attributed to al-Majrīṭī. Because the manuscript presents it as supplementary material rather than a freestanding composition, its unity as a “work” is itself uncertain. ([Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus, work C.6.2](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/work/207))

### Observational and astrological creations

- **979:** *Observation of Regulus and determination of stellar precession*. An observational result rather than a text: al-Majrīṭī measured Regulus at ecliptic longitude 135°40′ and used it to derive longitudes for the fixed stars and a precessional displacement of 13°10′ relative to Ptolemy’s catalogue. The result entered the star table accompanying his *Planisphaerium* material. ([Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm); [Ptolemaeus Arabus et Latinus, work C.6.2](https://ptolemaeus.badw.de/work/207))

- **1006–1007:** *Prognostication for the conjunction of Saturn and Jupiter*. An astrological forecast—possibly once embodied in a horoscope or spoken judgment, but not securely preserved as a separately titled text—predicting dynastic change, destruction, killing, and famine. It resists classification as a surviving work because the sources attest the prediction, not an extant document containing it. ([Casulleras, *Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers*](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/biography/Majriti_BEA.htm))

### Traditional attributions now rejected or seriously doubtful

- **950–953, according to the earlier manuscript chronology:** *Rutbat al-ḥakīm* (“The Rank of the Sage”). An alchemical protocol and theoretical compilation long attributed to al-Majrīṭī; some manuscripts instead give 1047–1051, dates after his death. Maribel Fierro’s now widely accepted identification assigns both this work and the *Ghāya* to the earlier Andalusian scholar Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī (d. 964), so it should not be counted as something Maslama ibn Aḥmad made, though its long false attribution belongs in his reception history. ([Fierro, bibliographic record and attribution](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/bibcite/contributor/8851); [2017 scholarly overview of the attribution](https://classiques-garnier.com/cahiers-de-recherches-medievales-et-humanistes-journal-of-medieval-and-human-studies-2017-1-n-33-varia-la-version-arabe-ghyt-al-akm-et-la-version-latine-du-picatrix-en.html?displaymode=full); [Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))

- **954–959:** *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm* (“The Aim of the Sage”; later Latin *Picatrix*). A compilation of astral magic, cosmology, talismanic procedures, and esoteric learning, traditionally coupled with the *Rutba* and attributed to al-Majrīṭī. Modern scholarship likewise assigns it, very probably, to Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī; it is therefore an important manifestation under al-Majrīṭī’s name, but not an accepted work by him. ([Fierro, bibliographic record and attribution](https://ismi.mpiwg-berlin.mpg.de/bibcite/contributor/8851); [2017 scholarly overview of the attribution](https://classiques-garnier.com/cahiers-de-recherches-medievales-et-humanistes-journal-of-medieval-and-human-studies-2017-1-n-33-varia-la-version-arabe-ghyt-al-akm-et-la-version-latine-du-picatrix-en.html?displaymode=full); [Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))

- **Date uncertain:** *al-Risāla al-jāmiʿa* (“The Comprehensive Epistle”). An encyclopedic-esoteric text associated with the corpus of the Brethren of Purity and published under al-Majrīṭī’s name, but rejected by most scholars as his creation. It is better understood as a mobile attribution within the Brethren’s textual tradition than as part of his œuvre. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit); [Institute of Ismaili Studies, “The Comprehensive Epistle and its Useful Benefits”](https://www.iis.ac.uk/events/the-comprehensive-epistle/))

- **Dates and titles uncertain:** *Zoological and alchemical opuscules attributed to al-Majrīṭī*. These pieces resist being called independent works: Vernet identifies them as extracts from, or writings closely related to, the *Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ* rather than original compositions by al-Majrīṭī. The evidence does not support inventing separate titles or dates for them. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))

- **Undated:** *Long treatise on the astrolabe attributed in Latin to al-Majrīṭī*. This construction-and-use protocol belongs to his pupil Aḥmad ibn al-Ṣaffār, despite part of its Latin transmission naming the master. It must be distinguished from al-Majrīṭī’s genuinely accepted short astrolabe treatise. ([Vernet, *Complete Dictionary of Scientific Biography*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/science/dictionaries-thesauruses-pictures-and-press-releases/al-majrit))
