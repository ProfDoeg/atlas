## Works by Johan Palmstruch

### Projects and proposals

- **Before 1652:** *Plan for a Swedish silk-manufacturing enterprise*. An unrealized industrial project intended to produce silk for export to Germany and Russia. The surviving account does not identify a fixed title or show that Palmstruch actually established the manufacture, so this is a project rather than a completed obra. ([Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf))

- **1652:** *First proposal for a Swedish bank* [descriptive title]. A German-language manuscript submitted to Queen Christina and subsequently referred to Stockholm’s mayor and council; it argues from the examples of Venice, Amsterdam, and Hamburg for a bank that would improve payments, credit, and the handling of Sweden’s copper currency. It was rejected or allowed to lapse, and no securely established authorial title is reported. ([Nathorst-Böös, “The Advantage of Banking,” bibliographic record](https://doi.org/10.1080/03585522.1961.10411442); [Bindseil, *Early Central Banks*](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/207620/1/1679156152.pdf); [Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf))

- **20 February 1655:** *Proposal for a municipal exchange bank and loan bank* [descriptive title]. An unsuccessful manuscript project presented to Stockholm’s civic administration, dividing the proposed institution into a deposit-and-transfer bank and a secured-lending bank. It survives in the scholarly record as one of Palmstruch’s three banking manuscripts, not as a separately published book. ([Nathorst-Böös, “The Advantage of Banking,” cited in the *Svenskt biografiskt lexikon* bibliography](https://sok.riksarkivet.se/sbl/Presentation.aspx?id=7989); [Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf))

- **1656:** *Proposal for an exchange bank and a loan bank under royal privilege* [descriptive title]. Palmstruch’s third banking project, addressed directly to Karl X Gustav, succeeded where the earlier proposals had failed and led to two royal charters dated 30 November 1656. The charters themselves were royal instruments rather than Palmstruch’s sole-authored texts, so the made thing attributable to him is the institutional proposal behind them. ([Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf); [Ögren, “Johan Palmstrüch, a Copper Money Doctor”](https://www.uu.se/institution/ekonomisk-historiska/nyheter/arkiv/2024-07-18-bokkapitel-johan-palmstruch-a-copper-money-doctor-stockholm-banco-and-the-emergence-of-banknotes-in-seventeenth-century-sweden))

### Institutional obra

- **1656–1668:** *Stockholms Banco*. A functioning financial institution devised and founded by Palmstruch under royal privilege, comprising an exchange bank and a loan bank; it opened on 29 July 1657 and later established branches at Åbo, Falun, and Gothenburg. It resists classification as Palmstruch’s work alone: the Crown, Stockholm municipality, commissioners, officials, and investors participated, and recent scholarship cautions that the later name “Palmstruch’s Bank” helped make him the sole author—and sole culprit—of what was formally a collegially governed enterprise. ([Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf); [Ericson and Winton, “Mastering the Narrative and the Dirty Tricks of Trade”](https://doi.org/10.1080/00076791.2021.2025221))

- **1657–1664:** *Stockholms Banco’s deposit-and-credit system*. An operational financial design joining giro deposits to secured lending and, contrary to the formal separation of the bank’s departments, lending deposited funds. It is better understood as a protocol embodied in accounts, receipts, payment orders, and institutional practice than as a single text; responsibility was shared, and the precise extent to which the practice was Palmstruch’s personal design is not uncontested. ([Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf); [Ögren, “Johan Palmstrüch, a Copper Money Doctor”](https://www.uu.se/institution/ekonomisk-historiska/nyheter/arkiv/2024-07-18-bokkapitel-johan-palmstruch-a-copper-money-doctor-stockholm-banco-and-the-emergence-of-banknotes-in-seventeenth-century-sweden))

### Monetary instruments

- **1661–1664:** *Kreditivsedlar* [first issue]. A family of printed, fixed-denomination bearer credit notes issued by Stockholms Banco in copper daler, with some silver-daler notes; unlike ordinary deposit receipts, they were not tied to a particular deposit and circulated by possession. They are commonly described as Europe’s first modern banknotes, but not as the world’s first paper money; each was a collective bank document signed by Palmstruch and other officials, and the notes had the approval of the bank’s inspector and Council, so “made by Palmstruch” means designed and issued under his direction rather than solely authored. ([Sveriges Riksbank, “First Banknotes in Europe”](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/about-the-riksbank/history/historical-timeline/1600-1699/first-banknotes-in-europe); [Nationalencyklopedin, “kreditivsedel”](https://www.ne.se/uppslagsverk/encyklopedi/l%C3%A5ng/kreditivsedel); [Ericson and Winton, “Mastering the Narrative and the Dirty Tricks of Trade”](https://doi.org/10.1080/00076791.2021.2025221))

- **1666:** *Palmstruchare* [second issue of *Kreditivsedlar*]. A redesigned series of bearer banknotes denominated in 10, 25, 50, and 100 daler silvermynt, individually numbered, sealed, and signed by Palmstruch with other bank officers. Surviving examples bear different issue dates, so 1666 dates the series rather than every physical note; the series was withdrawn during the bank’s liquidation. ([Nationalencyklopedin, “kreditivsedel”](https://www.ne.se/uppslagsverk/encyklopedi/l%C3%A5ng/kreditivsedel); [Gustavianum, “Banknotes”](https://www.uu.se/en/gustavianum/about-the-museum/our-collections/the-coin-cabinet/banknotes); [Sveriges Riksbank, *Stockholms Banco*](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/riksbanken-350-ar/tidslinjen/stockholm-banco/24-45-stockholms-banco_eng.pdf))

### Commissioned built work

- **Date not established, before 1671:** *Gallery and Palmstruch family burial place in Täby Church* [descriptive title]. Palmstruch had a gallery constructed in connection with his burial place near the altar; this is a commissioned architectural intervention, not securely documented as something he designed or physically built himself. The associated grave slab and arms survive, but their makers are not identified in the consulted sources, and the later history of the burial arrangement is uncertain. ([Täby parish, “Johan Palmstruchs grav och vapensköld i Täby kyrka”](https://www.svenskakyrkan.se/taby/johan-palmstruchs-grav-och-vapenskold-i-taby-kyrka); [Swedish House of Nobility genealogical account](https://kvikr.se/sveriges-adel/atter/adliga-atten-palmstruch-nr-657/))
