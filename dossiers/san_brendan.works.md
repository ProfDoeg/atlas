## Works by San Brendan de Clonfert

### Monastic foundations — institutional *obras*

- **Sixth century; exact date unknown:** *Annaghdown (Enach Dúin)*. A monastic community traditionally counted among Brendan’s earliest foundations; the surviving medieval buildings are later creations and should not be mistaken for structures made by Brendan himself. The attribution rests principally on hagiographical tradition rather than contemporary documentation. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922))

- **Sixth century; exact date unknown:** *Ardfert (Ard Ferta)*. A monastery or ecclesiastical community in Kerry, identified by modern scholarship as Brendan’s most important foundation in his native region. The extant cathedral is substantially medieval, while the earliest surviving historical references are eleventh-century, so Brendan’s *obra* is the institution and sacred site—not the present building. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922); [Corpus of Romanesque Sculpture in Britain and Ireland](https://www.crsbi.ac.uk/view-item?i=14380))

- **Sixth century; exact date unknown:** *Shankeel (Seana Cill, near Brandon Hill)*. An early monastic foundation attributed to Brendan. Its status is principally traditional: the sources identify a foundation but do not securely reconstruct its original buildings, organization, or date. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922))

- **Sixth century; exact date and identification unknown:** *A monastery in Brittany*. Brendan’s Lives say that, following Íte’s advice, he travelled to Brittany and founded a monastery there. Because the institution is unnamed and the evidence hagiographical, this is better classified as a reported project or foundation than as a securely identifiable surviving *obra*. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922))

- **558–564:** *Cluain Ferta Brénaind (Clonfert)*. Brendan’s best-attested monastic foundation: an institution, settlement, and school rather than a single surviving building. The dates vary within the tradition; the existing cathedral is a later medieval manifestation of the site, with its principal fabric dating centuries after Brendan. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922); [Clonfert Cathedral excavation report](https://excavations.ie/report/2001/Galway/0006435/); [Clonfert conservation plan](https://www.heritagecouncil.ie/content/files/saint_brendans_cathedral_clonfert_conservation_plan_2004_6mb.pdf))

### Text attributed to Brendan

- **Attributed to Brendan; probably eighth century:** *Oratio Sancti Brendani*. A long Latin devotional prayer or *lorica*—a protective, performative text closer to a ritual invocation than to a descriptive book. It survives in an eleventh-century manuscript and is ascribed to Brendan, but its proposed eighth-century composition places it after his death; it therefore resists inclusion as something he personally made and is best recorded as a later creation under his authorial name. ([Pontifical Institute of Mediaeval Studies](https://pims.ca/publication/isbn-978-0-88844-312-0/); [The Medieval Review](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/journals/index.php/tmr/article/view/41555))

### Later Brendanian creations—not works made by Brendan

- **Late eighth century or later:** *Navigatio Sancti Brendani abbatis*. An anonymous Latin voyage narrative that makes Brendan its protagonist; scholarship places its composition roughly two centuries after his lifetime, possibly in Ireland or among Irish writers on the Continent. It is a creation of the Brendan tradition, not Brendan’s own travel account or authored *obra*. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922); [Dickinson College Commentaries](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/navigatio-brendani/intro/introduction))

- **Medieval; surviving in multiple, textually contaminated recensions:** *Vita Sancti Brendani* / *Betha Brenainn Clúana Ferta*. Latin and Irish Lives about Brendan, produced and repeatedly reshaped by later hagiographers. They preserve traditions concerning his foundations and journeys but are manifestations of his posthumous cult, not works authored by him; the surviving Latin versions are abbreviated from, or contaminated by, other Brendanian narratives. ([Dictionary of Irish Biography](https://www.dib.ie/biography/brendan-brenainn-a0922); [University of Glasgow, *Brendan—a summary*](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/4509/2/2007ButterV2.pdf))
