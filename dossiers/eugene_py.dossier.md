# Eugène Py: Research Dossier

## Scope, identity, and evidentiary method

This dossier concerns the French-born photographer, mechanic, laboratory technician, cinematographer, director, and actuality-film operator known in Argentina as **Eugenio Py**.

The surviving record is unusually fragmentary. Most films attributed to Py are lost; several standard biographical claims derive from retrospective histories rather than contemporary records; and early Argentine filmography often credits the employing company, Casa Lepage, rather than an individual operator. Accordingly:

- **Documented fact** means supported by civil, consular, census, catalog, or contemporary press evidence identified by a specialist source.
- **Reported fact** means repeated in credible historical scholarship but not independently verified here in the underlying record.
- **Traditional attribution** means accepted in much film historiography but lacking secure contemporary confirmation.
- **Disputed claim** means materially challenged by later research.
- **Unverified claim** means plausible but presently unsupported by sufficient documentary evidence.
- **Myth or legend** means a culturally influential story whose evidentiary basis is doubtful or absent.

The principal corrective source is Jean-Claude Seguin Vergara’s GRIMH research profile, which incorporates French civil records, censuses, French consular registrations, contemporary newspapers, and Casa Lepage catalogs. It conflicts with much older secondary literature on Py’s birth date, entry into photography, the date of his first films, and the existence of *La bandera argentina*.

### Birth-date conflict

**Documented fact:** GRIMH gives **19 March 1859** and supplies Py’s fuller civil name, Guillaume Eugène Py. Its genealogy is evidently derived from French civil and population records.

**Reported fact:** Most databases and twentieth-century Argentine histories give **19 May 1859**.

The March date has the stronger stated documentary foundation. The May date should not be silently discarded, however, because it became the dominant commemorative date in Argentine film historiography.

## Family and Ancestry

### Paternal family

The GRIMH reconstruction identifies Py’s paternal grandparents as:

- **Guillaume Lupin Py**
- **Claire Dalbés**

Their known children included:

- **Marie Py**, born at Trèbes on 10 March 1821; married Charles Guiraud at Carcassonne on 17 August 1843.
- **Jacques Py**, born at Carcassonne on 14 May 1831 and died there on 19 May 1878.

### Parents

Py was the son of:

- **Jacques Py** (1831–1878)
- **Antoinette Baptistine Rullac**, born at Aragon, Aude, on 12 December 1835 and died at Carcassonne on 19 December 1877.

They married at Aragon on 20 October 1857.

GRIMH describes the parents as agricultural workers or people from a farming background. Both died while Eugène was still young: his mother in December 1877 and his father five months later. He was therefore orphaned at about eighteen or nineteen.

### Aunt and foster household

After losing his parents, Py reportedly lived with his aunt **Élisabeth/Marie Py**, wife of **Charles Guiraud/Giraud**. The sources vary slightly in her forename and the spelling of her husband’s surname, likely reflecting abbreviated records or transcription.

### Sibling

A known younger sister was:

- **Marguerite Justine Py**, born at Carcassonne on 17 February 1874.
- She married **Louis Alexandre Alexis Laborie** at Saint-Paul-de-Fenouillet on 23 November 1897.

No other siblings were identified in the consulted sources.

### Marriage

On **18 January 1882**, at Trèbes, Py married:

- **Marie-Louise Amélie Antoinette Revel**, born at Trèbes on 22 November 1856.

After marriage the couple lived in the Trèbes household of **Napoléon Revel** (1 December 1835–21 March 1901), described as the uncle of Py’s wife.

### Child

Their documented daughter was:

- **Elisabeth Anne Jeanne Antoinette Py**, born at Trèbes on **5 October 1882**.

A later Argentine teaching text says that Elisabeth learned filming, developing, enlarging, inspecting, and editing and helped transmit these skills. This is a **reported fact requiring corroboration**: the account supplies no contemporary employment record or screen credit.

No additional children were identified. Accounts saying Py emigrated with “his whole family, including his parents-in-law” appear in popular retellings, but the precise composition and itinerary of the migrant party have not been independently established.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1859–1882

Py was born in Carcassonne into a modest agricultural family. No evidence of formal secondary or higher education was found.

He initially worked as a **mason**. As the eldest son of orphaned children, he reportedly did not perform French military service. This is presented by GRIMH as a finding from the relevant administrative record, not as evidence of evasion or political objection.

Nothing reliable was located concerning:

- Childhood schooling
- Religious formation
- Political beliefs
- Early artistic interests
- Apprenticeship to a photographer
- Juvenile illness or injury

His early working life appears manual and technical rather than artistic.

## Education, Technical Formation, and Formative Influences

### Mechanical training

After his 1882 marriage, Py lived with Napoléon Revel at Trèbes and reportedly acquired knowledge of **precision mechanics** from him. The 1886 census still placed Py at Trèbes.

This mechanical formation is significant because early cinematography required an operator to maintain, adjust, load, and sometimes modify hand-cranked mechanisms; expose and develop unstable film stock; repair perforation and transport problems; and improvise in difficult locations.

### Photography

Older biographies often say Py opened a photographic studio at San Martín in **1893** and joined Casa Lepage in **1895**. The census evidence complicates that account:

- On **10 May 1895**, Py was recorded in San Martín with his wife and daughter.
- His occupation was still **ajustador**, meaning fitter or mechanical adjuster.
- GRIMH therefore concludes that he had not yet become a professional photographer by that date.
- His supposed studio at **Villa Luján 200, San Martín**, if real, more plausibly opened in 1896 or 1897.

How he learned photography is unknown. No teacher, academy, apprenticeship, or surviving technical notebook has been identified.

### Institutional formation

Py’s decisive education was probably occupational:

- Purchasing supplies from Casa Lepage
- Handling imported photographic equipment
- Learning cinematographic machinery manufactured by Gaumont-Demenÿ and Pathé
- Developing and printing film in Casa Lepage’s laboratory
- Working with Henri Lepage and Max Glücksmann
- Solving exposure, mobility, projection, and synchronization problems in practice

He was not documented as an employee or licensed operator of the Lumière organization.

## Migration and Early Argentine Employment, 1887–1897

### Arrival in Argentina

**Documented fact:** Py was in Argentina by **18 June 1888**, when he appeared at the French consulate in Buenos Aires and stated that he had returned from Córdoba.

This provides a firmer terminus than the vague “late 1880s” found in reference works.

### Córdoba and railway employment

At the 1888 consular appearance Py described himself as a **house painter**. His presence in Córdoba may indicate employment connected with the Buenos Aires–Rosario railway, but GRIMH labels this as a possibility rather than a certainty.

Popular accounts say:

1. Py settled first at Rosario.
2. He worked for a railway.
3. He later moved to San Martín.
4. He became an amateur photographer there.

The railway connection is plausible and widely reported, but the consulted sources do not identify the precise company, dates, rank, wages, or personnel file.

### The “casier judiciaire” consular entry

The 18 June 1888 consular registration states the administrative purpose as **“casier judiciaire”**—criminal-record or police-record documentation.

This is a **documented notation but an unresolved question**. It does **not**, by itself, establish:

- An arrest
- A criminal accusation
- A prosecution
- The nature of any alleged offense
- A conviction
- Imprisonment
- Fraud or political activity

No underlying police dossier, judgment, or sentence was located. It would be inaccurate either to suppress the notation or to call Py a convicted criminal on this evidence.

### Campana

On **9 March 1892**, Py again declared himself to the French consulate. He was then:

- Living in **Campana**
- Working as an **ajusteur**, or mechanical fitter

Campana was an important railway and industrial town. This record reinforces the technical-worker phase of his career.

### San Martín

By the Argentine census of **10 May 1895**, Py, his wife, and daughter lived at San Martín. He remained identified as a fitter.

San Martín became his enduring home. He later operated a photographic workshop there and died there in 1924.

## Entry into Photography and Casa Lepage

### Photographic studio

The frequently repeated chronology places Py’s San Martín studio in 1893. GRIMH considers that dating incompatible with the 1895 occupational census and proposes **1896–1897** as the likelier period.

The address traditionally associated with the studio is **Villa Luján 200**. Its exact opening date, business name, ownership structure, clientele, and financial records remain unknown.

### Casa Lepage

Casa Lepage was a Buenos Aires photographic-supply business run by the Belgian-born **Henri/Enrique Lepage**. Its best-documented early address was **Bolívar 375**, near Belgrano and opposite or near the Colegio Nacional de Buenos Aires. One National Library publication gives Bolívar 335; the specialist catalog evidence favors 375.

Py apparently became known to the firm by purchasing photographic materials. Max Glücksmann is traditionally said to have recommended hiring him because of his technical knowledge.

Older accounts date his entry to **1895**, but this is difficult to reconcile with the census. A date after May 1895, probably in 1896 or 1897, is more defensible.

### The Teatro Odeón exhibition

A foundational story says that Lepage, Glücksmann, and Py attended the Buenos Aires motion-picture presentation at the **Teatro Odeón on 18 July 1896**, organized by journalist **Eustaquio Pellicer** and theater owner or impresario **Francisco Pastor**.

Two qualifications are necessary:

- Some secondary sources give **28 July**, not 18 July.
- Although long called Argentina’s first “Lumière cinematograph” show, GRIMH notes that the apparatus has not been securely identified as a Lumière Cinématographe.

The three men’s attendance is a **traditional and plausible report**, not conclusively documented in the material consulted.

### Failed Lumière acquisition and Gaumont equipment

The conventional narrative says Lepage attempted to buy a Lumière apparatus but could not obtain one. Lumière commercial policy favored territorial concessions, and the Southern Cone concession involved Camille Cerf and Mallmann y Compañía.

Casa Lepage instead acquired equipment associated with:

- **Gaumont**
- **Georges Demenÿ**
- **Pathé**

The celebrated Elgé 35 mm model became commercially available only in **September 1897**. GRIMH therefore argues that Casa Lepage’s indigenous production and Py’s activity as a cinematographer cannot securely be placed before **1898**.

## Chronological Life History

## 1898–1900: First Secure Film Work

### *La bandera argentina*

Traditional histories say Py exposed seventeen metres of film showing the Argentine flag flying in Plaza de Mayo in **1897**. It became canonized as Argentina’s first domestically produced film.

Later research raises serious problems:

- No securely identified copy survives.
- GRIMH has not confirmed its appearance in contemporary Casa Lepage catalogs or programs.
- The Gaumont camera supposedly used was not commercially available before late 1897.
- It may be a later confusion with Casa Lepage allegories such as *España y la Argentina* or *Italia y la Argentina*, made for propagandist Alfredo B. Martínez.
- Federico Figner exhibited locally photographed Buenos Aires views—usually identified as *Vistas de Palermo*, *Avenida de Mayo*, and *Plaza de Mayo*—on 24 November 1896, before the traditional date of Py’s film.

Thus *La bandera argentina* is best classified as a **traditional attribution of unconfirmed existence**, not as a securely documented surviving production.

### Earliest cataloged or reported actualities

Possible early Py/Casa Lepage films include:

- *Exposición Rural*, shown at Teatro Casino on 29 April 1898
- *Premio Internacional en el Hipódromo Argentino*, shown at Cine Pabellón Argentino on 4 November 1899
- *Revista de tropas argentinas el 25 de mayo*, shown at Teatro Casino on 9 July 1900

These are **reported or catalog-associated attributions**. Early company practice makes individual authorship difficult to prove.

### The Posadas surgical films

Between **1899 and 1900**, surgeon **Alejandro Posadas** hired or invited Py to record two operations at the old Hospital de Clínicas in Buenos Aires. The surviving material is generally called:

- *Operaciones del doctor Posadas*
- *Operación de quiste hidatídico de pulmón*

One operation involved the removal of a pulmonary hydatid cyst; another reportedly concerned an inguinal hernia.

Technical and medical details reconstructed by anesthesiology historians include:

- Camera or apparatus: Elgé/Gaumont
- Approximate combined or variant-copy duration: about 3 minutes 40 seconds, although shorter and longer durations appear in secondary accounts
- Lighting: natural daylight, either beside a large window or in an internal courtyard
- Surgeon: Alejandro Posadas
- Assistant: a practitioner named Viale
- Anesthesia: administered by Rodolfo S. Roccatagliata, probably chloroform through a Schimmelbusch mask
- Nurse: Ramón Vázquez
- Patient: adult male in the hydatid-cyst sequence

The films were intended as medical teaching material. Historical studies identify them as the earliest known moving-image record of surgery and of general anesthesia.

The negative or film material was rediscovered before demolition of the old Hospital de Clínicas. Florentino Sanguinetti reportedly recognized Posadas; Guillermo Fernández Jurado of the Fundación Cinemateca Argentina helped arrange copying. Copies or versions were subsequently consulted at the Cinemateca Argentina, INCAA, Museo del Cine Pablo C. Ducrós Hicken, Archivo General de la Nación, and the University of Buenos Aires medical faculty.

Unlike most films attributed to Py, this work survives in some form and is the firmest basis for assessing his camera practice.

### Campos Salles visit, October 1900

The earliest securely cataloged major Casa Lepage production attributed to Py is the three-part *Viaje del doctor Campos Salles a Buenos Aires*, photographed on **25 October 1900**.

It recorded the state visit of Brazilian president **Manuel Ferraz de Campos Salles**, including his meeting or embrace with Argentine president **Julio Argentino Roca**, with former president **Bartolomé Mitre** present.

The film’s status as “Argentina’s first documentary” is conventional but imprecise: earlier local actualities existed. Its importance lies in being one of the earliest securely documented, systematically produced Argentine news records.

### Tango films

*Tango argentino* is usually dated to **1900**. A related or possibly differently titled film, *Tango criollo*, is variously dated 1901 or 1906 and reportedly showed a couple dancing, the man known as “el Negro Agapito.”

Because the films are lost and titles migrate between retrospective lists, it is uncertain whether these references denote one film, variants, or separate works.

## 1901: Naval, Political, and Popular Actualities

Py’s Casa Lepage output functioned as a moving-image newspaper closely paralleling the illustrated weekly *Caras y Caretas*.

### Puerto Belgrano naval series

On **17 May 1901**, Py photographed naval maneuvers at Puerto Belgrano. Contemporary reporting describes:

- Eight to ten moving-picture scenes
- Troop landings
- A staged attack by the destroyer *Corrientes* on the armored cruiser *San Martín*
- Gunfire and smoke effects
- President Roca, Colonel Riccheri, Admiral Solier, and other officials
- Approximately 200 metres of film
- A total screening duration reported as about thirty minutes

Casa Lepage previewed the material to press and officials on **30 May 1901** and later showed it at the Teatro Victoria, including a charitable performance.

The film demonstrated Py’s ability to coordinate multiple viewpoints, military subjects, and large-scale actuality production.

### Other 1901 subjects

Catalog and historical sources attribute or associate Py with:

- *La revista de la Escuadra Argentina en mayo de 1901*
- *Visita del teniente general Bartolomé Mitre al Museo Histórico Nacional*
- *El general Mitre visitando la Casa Lepage*
- *Manifestación Radical del 25 de julio de 1901*
- *Pericón Nacional*, performed by the Podestá company
- *Club de Gimnasia y Esgrima*
- *Bohemia criolla*
- *El calotero* or *El carlotero*
- *En casa del fotógrafo*
- Views of Plaza de Mayo, tramways, public ceremonies, and rural festivities

Attribution varies between “Eugène Py,” “[Eugène Py],” and Casa Lepage. Brackets in GRIMH indicate inferred rather than explicitly printed credit.

## 1902: Andes Crossing and Chilean Mission

### Political setting

Argentina and Chile were in a period of severe boundary tension. In May 1902 their governments negotiated the agreements known as the **Pactos de Mayo**.

The Argentine government designated Py as an official film operator, according to recent scholarship. His employer’s commercial purpose and the government’s diplomatic purpose overlapped: the films were both news commodities and images of state ceremony.

### Departure

On **10 May 1902**, Py and Spanish journalist/operator **Enrique Casellas** left Buenos Aires from Retiro station. Max Glücksmann saw them off.

Their route was:

1. Buenos Aires to Mendoza by train
2. Mendoza to Cacheuta
3. Camera mounted at the front of a locomotive
4. Puente del Inca
5. Mule crossing through snow
6. Salto del Soldado
7. Santa Rosa de los Andes
8. Santiago de Chile

Photographs show Py with a Gaumont-Demenÿ camera on the locomotive and Py and Casellas crossing the mountains by mule.

### Films of the crossing

The journey produced:

- *Ferrocarril trasandino* — about 40 metres
- *En los Andes* — about 40 metres

These were subsequently exhibited at Casellas’s Salón París, owned by Manuel Malagrida.

### Work in Chile

With assistance from Chilean army major **Germán Fonsalida**, Py and Casellas remained for roughly a month, from mid-May to mid-June.

The most politically important film was:

- *Firma en Santiago de Chile de las actas de la paz*, photographed **20 May 1902**, approximately 20 metres

Other cataloged Chilean subjects included:

- *La calle Ahumada con el Paseo y sus aristocráticas damas*
- *Tranvías eléctricos en Santiago de Chile con señoritas que se desempeñan como mayorales*
- *Fiesta en el Club Hípico de Santiago de Chile*
- *Procesión de Corpus Christi en Santiago de Chile*
- *Cuartel de San Ignacio*
- *Desfile de lanceros y cazadores del ejército chileno*
- *Escuela Militar de Santiago de Chile y artillería*
- *La Alameda de Santiago de Chile*
- *El general Körner y su estado mayor*
- *Veterano chileno*
- *Llegada del presidente Riesco a La Moneda*
- *Salida del Congreso*
- *Salida del cuerpo diplomático*
- *El coronel Holdich*
- Numerous other views associated with the Pactos de Mayo

A Lepage catalog reportedly contained **32 Chile-related views** for 1902–1903; recent research estimates that Py made approximately twenty views during his Chilean stay.

### Encounter with a rival operator

While filming the diplomatic corps, Py saw another cameraman across the avenue using what appeared to be the same rare apparatus. Retrospective testimony identifies him as Spanish operator **Juan José Pont**, active with Pijoan Trías.

Py reportedly called it the greatest surprise of his life because the two possibly unique South American examples of that camera had converged on the same event.

This is a **reported recollection**, preserved in Carlos Barrios Barón’s later account rather than verified in a contemporaneous diary by Py.

### Return

Py ran out of unexposed negative stock and decided to leave. He and Casellas departed Valparaíso aboard the German steamer *Assuán* on **14 June 1902**, traveled through the Strait of Magellan, reached Montevideo on **2 July**, and continued to Buenos Aires.

### Political character of the films

The films concentrated on:

- Presidents
- Diplomats
- Military reviews
- Elite spectators
- Modern transport
- Urban infrastructure
- The public ritual of reconciliation

Recent scholarship classifies them within early governmental and diplomatic propaganda—not in the sense of a demonstrated covert operation, but as state-aligned images constructing Argentina and Chile as modern, orderly, peace-making nations. The surviving titles do not suggest coverage of dissenting, working-class, Indigenous, or anti-government perspectives.

## 1902–1905: News Events and Public Ritual

### *El soldado Sosa en capilla*

This 1902 film concerned conscript Sosa, who had been sentenced to death and later received a presidential commutation. Whether Py photographed the condemned man directly, staged elements, or only recorded surrounding events cannot be established from the surviving description.

### Antarctic expedition

In December 1903 Py/Casa Lepage recorded the return of the Argentine corvette *Uruguay*, which had rescued Otto Nordenskjöld’s Antarctic expedition.

The approximately 60-metre sequence included:

- Disembarkation of rescuers and survivors
- Crowd celebrations
- José María Sobral, Julián Irízar, Nordenskjöld, and other expedition figures

It was presented at the Politeama on **9 December 1903** during an event of the Instituto Geográfico Argentino. Contemporary reporting says the audience applauded the projected views enthusiastically.

### Argentine–Chilean ceremonies, 1903–1904

Py photographed ceremonies surrounding a Chilean delegation’s visit to Argentina in 1903, including:

- Arrival of the *Chacabuco*
- Officials leaving a Te Deum at the cathedral
- Return of the procession
- The Chilean delegation in Parque Lezama

In March 1904 he returned to the Andes to film the Argentine official party and the inauguration of the **Cristo Redentor de los Andes**, a monument symbolizing peace between Argentina and Chile.

### Garibaldi monument

On **19 June 1904**, Py photographed the inauguration of the monument to Giuseppe Garibaldi in Buenos Aires. The approximately 90-metre view recorded:

- Bartolomé Mitre
- President Julio Argentino Roca
- Municipal officials
- The unveiling
- Garibaldian veterans
- Italian and French societies with banners
- Crowds and speeches

The subject was the monument and ceremony; it does not constitute a personal connection with Garibaldi, who had died in 1882.

### Other productions

Reported titles in this period include:

- *Pica, pica compadrito* (1903)
- *Los políticos* (1904)
- *Abajo la careta* (1904)
- *Escuelas de Buenos Aires: Juegos de niñas* (before 30 April 1904)
- *Ensalada criolla* (1905)
- *Jubileo del general Mitre* (1905)

The boundaries between staged sketch, filmed theatrical performance, song film, and actuality are often unclear.

## 1906–1911: Staged Films and Synchronized Sound Experiments

Py increasingly worked on short comic, theatrical, and popular-musical subjects. Conventional director credits include:

- *El soldado de la Independencia* (1906)
- *Gabino, el Mayora* (1906)
- *El pechador* (1906)
- *Noticia telefónica angustiosa* (1906)
- *Los tocayos* (1907)
- *Mister Wiskey* (1907)
- *Dejá é jugar, ché, ché* (1907)
- *Los carreros* (1908)
- *La beata* (1909)
- *Cochero de tranvía* (1909)
- *Justicia criolla* (1910)
- *La trilla* (1910)
- *Los escruchantes* (1911)

### Chronophone-style synchronization

From about **1907**, Py and Casa Lepage made films synchronized to phonograph records, following systems comparable to Gaumont’s Chronophone method.

The scenes were filmed:

- On the roof of Casa Lepage
- At the Teatro San Martín

Titles associated with the experiments include:

- *Abajo la careta*
- *Ensalada criolla*
- *La reina mora*
- *La beata*
- *El soldado de la Independencia*

Synchronization was mechanical rather than sound-on-film. A record played while the projected performer’s image attempted to remain in step. No evidence was found that Py patented the method, invented its core mechanism, or owned the underlying technology. His importance was as a local experimenter and technician.

### Fiction and authorship

Many database listings call Py “director,” but early credit practices were unstable. Some works may have been:

- Photographed theatrical turns
- Produced by Lepage or Glücksmann
- Directed collectively
- Technically supervised by Py
- Retrospectively assigned to him because he was the house camera operator

Individual director credit is therefore strongest where a contemporary program or catalog names him and weaker where only a later filmography does so.

---

## 1908–1915: Glücksmann Organization and Feature Production

In **1908**, Henri Lepage sold the firm to Max Glücksmann. Py continued in the successor organization.

Glücksmann developed a vertically connected business involving:

- Equipment and film importation
- Production
- Developing and printing
- Distribution
- Exhibition in Argentina
- Exhibition and commercial connections in Uruguay and Chile
- Later phonograph and record operations

Py was a key technician and camera operator, but no evidence was found that he held equity in the company, owned theaters, shared directly in distribution profits, or accumulated significant personal wealth.

### Laboratory work and training

Later in his career Py concentrated on laboratory operations and reportedly trained younger technicians in different specialties. Their names are not consistently preserved in the consulted sources.

### *Amalia*

The feature adaptation of José Mármol’s novel is generally dated **1914**, though some summaries give 1912.

The better-supported credits are:

- Director: **Enrique García Velloso**
- Production context: Max Glücksmann and a charitable or society-sponsored undertaking
- Camera/photography: **Eugenio Py**
- Py’s role: cinematographer and camera operator, not sole director

### *Mariano Moreno y la Revolución de Mayo*

Made in **1915**, this historical production was:

- Directed or scripted by Enrique García Velloso
- Produced within Glücksmann’s organization
- Photographed by Py
- Performed by professional actors

Database and title variation includes *Mariano Moreno y la Revolución de Mayo*. It should not be confused with Mario Gallo’s earlier *La Revolución de Mayo*.

---

## Later Career, 1916–1924

The detailed record after 1915 is thin.

One history states that Py’s **last work** was filming the visit of **Prince Umberto of Savoy** to Argentina. The prince visited in 1924; if this identification is correct, Py remained professionally active near the end of his life. No complete contemporary credit, title, footage description, or exact filming date was located.

No reliable evidence was found concerning:

- Retirement
- Dismissal
- Bankruptcy
- Property holdings
- Pension
- Final salary
- Major illness
- Addiction
- Psychological crisis
- Accident
- Medical cause of death

Py died at **San Martín on 26 August 1924**.

## Companies, Institutions, Technologies, Projects, and Financial Interests

## Casa Lepage

Py’s central institutional affiliation was Casa Lepage. Its business combined photography retail with early film importation, processing, production, and demonstration.

Py’s functions reportedly included:

- Camera operation
- Location photography
- Film development
- Laboratory supervision
- Technical adjustment and repair
- Cinematography
- Short-film direction
- Training assistants

He was an employee and senior technician, not demonstrably an owner.

## Max Glücksmann organization

After Glücksmann acquired Lepage’s business, Py continued to supply actualities and photography. In later years, Glücksmann’s companies commissioned films and news material from Py and assistants.

No contract between Py and Glücksmann was located. Their exact financial arrangement is unknown.

## Equipment

Equipment associated with Py includes:

- Gaumont Elgé chronophotographic or cinematographic apparatus
- Gaumont-Demenÿ equipment
- Pathé apparatus
- Hand-cranked 35 mm cameras
- Phonograph-disc synchronization systems
- Laboratory developing and printing equipment

Claims that Py **built Argentina’s first cameras himself** appear in local commemorative writing but are **unverified**. His mechanical background makes repairs or modifications plausible; no patent, technical drawing, manufacturing record, or surviving Py-built camera was located.

## Patents and inventions

No patent registered in Py’s name was identified.

His innovations were practical and organizational:

- Mobile actuality filming
- Mounting a camera on a locomotive
- High-altitude expedition photography
- Rapid processing and exhibition of current events
- Medical cinematography
- Early disc-and-image synchronization
- Laboratory training

## Wealth and property

No probate file, property register, shareholding, theater ownership, investment portfolio, or estate valuation was found. Py’s long employment and technical seniority do not establish personal wealth.

## Political, Religious, Intellectual, and Artistic Development

### Politics

No evidence identifies Py as a member of an Argentine or French political party.

His films nevertheless had political functions. He recorded:

- Presidents Roca, Campos Salles, and Germán Riesco
- Bartolomé Mitre
- Military maneuvers
- Diplomatic negotiations
- The Radical demonstration of 25 July 1901
- Patriotic and historical theatrical scenes
- National centenary ceremonies
- The Chile–Argentina peace process

These subjects show proximity to state ritual and elite institutions but do not prove Py’s personal ideological allegiance.

### National identity

The lost or possibly legendary *La bandera argentina* became a model story of immigrant identification with Argentina. Later writers interpreted Py’s choice of the national flag as gratitude or faith in his adopted country. No surviving statement by Py establishes that motive.

### News-film aesthetics

His documented work emphasized:

- Public ceremony
- Movement
- Crowds
- Transport
- Warships and trains
- Political leaders
- Modern urban life
- Staged demonstrations for the camera

Recent scholars describe this as an early “rituality of power.” That is an analysis of subject selection and visual function, not proof of hidden political direction.

### Religion

Py filmed Te Deums, religious processions, and the Cristo Redentor ceremony, but these were public events. No evidence establishes his personal faith, denomination, anticlericalism, or religious practice.

### Artistic development

Py’s trajectory moved from static photography and mechanical expertise through actuality reporting toward:

- Staged popular sketches
- Musical and theatrical records
- Primitive synchronized sound
- Feature-film cinematography

No aesthetic manifesto, theoretical essay, diary, or interview explaining his artistic principles was located.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Henri/Enrique Lepage

**Documented professional relationship:** Employer and owner of Casa Lepage. Lepage provided equipment, premises, commercial infrastructure, and access to imported photographic and cinematographic technology.

### Max Glücksmann

**Documented professional relationship:** Colleague, manager, later proprietor and producer. Glücksmann commissioned, distributed, and exhibited films photographed by Py. Their collaboration joined Py’s technical labor to a transnational commercial network.

### Marie-Louise Revel

**Documented family relationship:** Wife from 1882; migrated and lived with Py in Argentina.

### Elisabeth Py

**Documented family relationship:** Daughter. Her alleged technical work in film processing and instruction is reported but insufficiently documented.

### Napoléon Revel

**Reported formative relationship:** Wife’s uncle, in whose Trèbes household Py learned precision mechanics.

### Enrique Casellas

**Documented collaborator:** Journalist and operator who accompanied Py across the Andes and through Chile in 1902; shared credit on *Firma en Santiago de Chile de las actas de la paz*.

### Manuel Malagrida

**Documented business contact:** Owner associated with Casellas’s Salón París, where Py’s Andes and Chile films were exhibited.

### Alejandro Posadas

**Documented collaborator/client:** Surgeon whose operations Py filmed at the Hospital de Clínicas.

### Rodolfo S. Roccatagliata, Viale, and Ramón Vázquez

**Documented participants:** Medical personnel visible or identified in the Posadas surgical film.

### Enrique García Velloso

**Documented collaborator:** Director and writer of feature projects photographed by Py, including *Amalia* and *Mariano Moreno y la Revolución de Mayo*.

### Podestá theatrical company

**Reported professional connection:** Py filmed the company’s *Pericón Nacional* and worked in the theater-to-film environment from which several early performances were drawn.

### Juan José Pont and Pijoan Trías

**Reported competitors and counterparts:** Spanish operators active in Chile. Py encountered Pont filming the same diplomatic event in Santiago in 1902.

### Germán Fonsalida

**Reported patron or facilitator:** Chilean army major who helped Py and Casellas obtain access during their 1902 stay.

### Alfredo B. Martínez

**Reported client or propagandist:** Associated with allegorical Casa Lepage films, possibly relevant to the later *La bandera argentina* tradition.

### Federico Figner

**Historiographical rival rather than proven personal rival:** Figner’s 1896 Buenos Aires views displaced Py’s supposed *La bandera argentina* from the position of “first Argentine film.” No documented meeting or personal conflict between the two was found.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, Crimes, and Financial Questions

## 1. Unexplained criminal-record notation

**Documented fact:** Py’s 18 June 1888 French consular registration was entered for the purpose “casier judiciaire.”

**Unresolved:** Whether this concerned routine transmission of a record, an employment or immigration requirement, a prior conviction, or a new proceeding.

**Not established:** Any crime, charge, trial, conviction, sentence, or imprisonment. No responsible conclusion about criminality can be made without the underlying dossier.

## 2. Was *La bandera argentina* real?

**Traditional claim:** Py filmed seventeen metres of the Argentine flag in Plaza de Mayo in 1897.

**Evidence for:**

- Repeated attribution in Argentine film histories
- Inclusion in modern film databases
- Long-standing commemorative tradition
- Later descriptions of subject, location, length, and apparatus

**Evidence against or limiting:**

- No known surviving print
- No secure contemporary catalog or program located by GRIMH
- Technical dating problem concerning the Gaumont apparatus
- Earliest documented Lepage production appears later
- Possible confusion with flag-themed allegorical films

**Status:** Disputed traditional attribution; existence presently unconfirmed.

## 3. “First Argentine film”

Even if *La bandera argentina* existed in 1897, Federico Figner had already exhibited locally photographed Buenos Aires views in November 1896.

Some scholars preserve Py’s priority by distinguishing:

- Film photographed for a kinetoscope or another device
- Film made with a cinematograph camera
- First systematic domestic production
- First explicitly national subject
- First film processed in Argentina

These are different claims. “Py made the first Argentine film” is therefore a historiographical convention, not an uncontested fact.

## 4. “Lumière operator”

Some biographies call Py one of the Lumière brothers’ operators.

No employment record, operator register, correspondence, or concession document establishing that status was found. Casa Lepage could not obtain the Lumière machine and turned to Gaumont equipment. The label is best treated as an **unsupported or erroneous claim**.

## 5. Entry into photography and Casa Lepage

**Older narrative:** Studio in San Martín by 1893; Casa Lepage employee in 1895.

**Documentary problem:** The May 1895 census still listed Py as a fitter.

**Likeliest reconstruction:** He entered professional photography and then Casa Lepage between mid-1895 and 1897.

## 6. State propaganda

Recent scholarship calls the 1902 Chile films governmental propaganda because Py traveled as an official operator and concentrated on diplomatic peace, military order, elite ceremony, and modern infrastructure.

This is a documented institutional context and scholarly interpretation. There is no evidence that Py was an intelligence officer, covert propagandist, or political agent beyond his camera assignment.

## 7. Authorship inflation

Later filmographies often turn the house operator into the “director” of every Casa Lepage production. Contemporary materials sometimes credit only Lepage, Glücksmann, or the company.

Individual authorship must therefore be graded:

- Explicit contemporary Py credit
- Shared credit
- Inferred operator attribution
- Retrospective director attribution

## 8. Medical ethics

The surgical films show an identifiable operation and patient at a time before modern film-consent standards. No consent record has been found.

It would be anachronistic to claim a proven legal violation under later standards, but the absence of known patient documentation is a legitimate unresolved ethical and archival question.

## 9. No evidence located for other misconduct

The dedicated searches using Py’s name with scandal, allegations, lawsuit, fraud, conspiracy, accusation, and criticism—including 2024–2026 material—found no substantiated record of:

- Fraud
- Corruption
- Money laundering
- Civil lawsuit
- Financial scandal
- Political violence
- Abuse
- Espionage
- Imprisonment
- Addiction
- Sexual scandal

The meaningful contested issues concern historical priority, authorship, official propaganda, and the unexplained consular “casier judiciaire” notation.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

## The immigrant saluting his new nation

**Story:** Py used the first available strip of virgin film to photograph the Argentine flag as a salute to his adopted country.

**Appearance and circulation:** Repeated throughout Argentine cinema histories, encyclopedias, commemorative journalism, and institutional publications.

**Evidence:** No identified statement by Py; no confirmed print or contemporary program.

**Effect:** It turned a French immigrant technician into a symbolic founder of national cinema and linked Argentine cinema’s origin to the flag rather than to foreign urban views.

**Status:** Hagiographic national-origin narrative built around a disputed film.

## “Seventeen metres of flag”

**Story:** The first Argentine film consisted of exactly seventeen metres of the flag waving over Plaza de Mayo.

**Evidence:** Precise length and location occur in retrospective accounts, but the film and contemporary catalog entry have not been verified.

**Effect:** The numerical detail gives the story documentary texture and remains central to Py’s popular image.

**Status:** Traditional detail attached to an unconfirmed production.

## The train panic at the Odeón

**Story:** During an early Buenos Aires projection of a train approaching the camera, a terrified spectator jumped from an upper level toward the stalls and was injured.

**Evidence:** Variants of the same legend circulate internationally around Lumière’s *L’Arrivée d’un train*. The apparatus and exact program of the Buenos Aires event are themselves uncertain.

**Effect:** It dramatizes the supposed shock of cinema’s arrival and places Py at a technological revelation.

**Status:** Cinema-origin folklore unless corroborated by a contemporary Buenos Aires report.

## Py as a Lumière emissary

**Story:** Py was one of the Lumière brothers’ official operators.

**Evidence against:** His documented equipment and business context were Gaumont/Lepage; Lumière’s concession system was held elsewhere.

**Effect:** The story connects Argentina’s cinema directly to the canonical Paris origin.

**Status:** Unsupported attribution.

## The first cinematic gag in Argentina

**Story:** While Py filmed aviation pioneer Alberto Santos-Dumont, a man repeatedly blocked the camera until someone spat in his face, ending the scene.

**Evidence:** Repeated in popular biographies, but no securely identified film, contemporary report, or firsthand testimony was located.

**Effect:** It supplies Argentine film history with a comic “first.”

**Status:** Reported anecdote bordering on legend.

## The ingenious camera-builder

**Story:** Py manufactured the primitive cameras with which he filmed Argentina’s first newsreels.

**Evidence:** He was demonstrably mechanically trained and capable of adjustment and laboratory work. The cameras securely associated with him were commercial Gaumont-Demenÿ or Pathé machines. No Py patent or surviving self-built camera was found.

**Status:** Possible exaggeration of genuine mechanical competence.

## Sunday screenings for neighborhood children

**Story:** Py gathered children from San Martín on Sundays and showed them films.

**Evidence:** Appears in later popular accounts without a cited contemporary record.

**Effect:** Supports a local image of Py as community educator and benevolent pioneer.

**Status:** Unverified local recollection.

## Successes and Failures

### Significant successes

- Transition from manual trades to precision mechanics, photography, and cinematography
- Establishment as Casa Lepage’s principal technical operator
- Surviving record of the Posadas operations
- Rapid production of topical films for local audiences
- Large-scale naval filming in 1901
- Difficult Andes expedition and Chilean diplomatic coverage in 1902
- Early transnational circulation in Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay
- Early experiments synchronizing film and phonograph records
- Cinematography on longer fiction and historical productions
- Training of laboratory personnel
- Lasting recognition as a foundational Argentine film technician

A 1902 catalog reportedly listed about fifty very short films made by Py or close collaborators. A later historical account says Casa Lepage’s body of work received a **gold medal at the Paris International Exposition in 1901**. Because the great Paris Exposition was held in 1900, the award year or exhibition identification requires further verification.

### Limitations and failures

- Most of the filmography is lost
- Individual credits were poorly preserved
- Synchronization experiments did not establish a lasting sound system
- No durable personal production company is documented
- The signature *La bandera argentina* may never have existed in the form later described
- Film histories often subordinated Py’s technical labor to the names of Lepage and Glücksmann
- No personal archive, memoir, or body of correspondence has surfaced

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

Py’s reputation developed in several layers.

### National founder

Twentieth-century histories used *La bandera argentina* to place a national emblem at the birth of Argentine cinema. This created a clear founder and a patriotic starting date, 1897.

### Technical pioneer

More recent research presents a stronger, if less mythic, achievement: Py was among Argentina’s earliest sustained professional camera and laboratory workers. Even if the flag film disappears from the record, his documented activity from 1898–1900 onward remains foundational.

### News-film pioneer

Py’s catalog constituted a moving chronicle of public Argentina:

- Political ceremonies
- State visits
- Military exercises
- Transport
- Theater
- Popular entertainment
- Urban modernization
- Foreign relations

### Medical-film pioneer

The Posadas footage is his most important surviving legacy. It joins film history, surgical history, anesthesia history, and medical education.

### Transnational pioneer

The 1902 expedition complicates a purely national account. Py was a French migrant working for a Belgian- and Austro-Hungarian-led business, using French equipment, filming Argentine–Chilean diplomacy, competing with Spanish operators, and distributing images through Argentina, Chile, and Uruguay.

### Archival absence

Most of Py’s output has disappeared through nitrate decomposition, neglect, reuse, disposal, and the broader weakness of early Argentine film preservation. His reputation therefore rests on catalogs, newspapers, still photographs, institutional memory, and a small surviving film corpus.

## Atlas Connections

Only documented or directly reported crossings with names in the supplied atlas roster are included.

### Max Glücksmann

**Evidentiary label: documented fact.**

Py and Max Glücksmann worked together at Casa Lepage in Buenos Aires from the later 1890s. Glücksmann managed the commercial side, distributed Py’s films, saw Py and Enrique Casellas off at Retiro on 10 May 1902, acquired Lepage’s business in 1908, and continued employing or commissioning Py. Py photographed *Amalia* and *Mariano Moreno y la Revolución de Mayo* within Glücksmann’s production structure.

### Julio Argentino Roca

**Evidentiary label: documented fact.**

Py filmed President Roca in several official contexts:

- Buenos Aires, 25 October 1900, during the visit of Brazilian president Campos Salles.
- Puerto Belgrano, May 1901, in the naval-review series.
- Buenos Aires, 19 June 1904, during the inauguration of the Garibaldi monument.

This was a camera-subject and official-event relationship; no private friendship or correspondence is documented.

### Giuseppe Maria Garibaldi

**Evidentiary label: documented commemorative association, not a personal meeting.**

Py filmed the inauguration of Garibaldi’s Buenos Aires monument on 19 June 1904, including Garibaldian veterans and political officials. Garibaldi died in 1882, so no personal crossing is established.

### José Rafael Hernández

**Evidentiary label: absence of documented connection.**

Py lived and worked in San Martín, a locality strongly associated with José Hernández, but no meeting, correspondence, influence, or institutional collaboration between them was found. Shared association with San Martín is insufficient to infer a relationship.

### Other roster members

No source-supported personal crossing was found with Carlos Gardel, Domingo Faustino Sarmiento, José de San Martín, Bartolomé Mitre being absent from the supplied roster, or the many other listed figures. Py photographed numerous political and cultural personalities, but connections have not been forced from mere contemporaneity, common nationality, or shared geography.
