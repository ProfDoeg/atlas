## Works by Maslama ibn Qasim al-Qurtubi

### Alchemical protocol

- **339–342/950–953:** *Rutbat al-ḥakīm* (“The Rank of the Sage”). A four-part alchemical treatise and practical-philosophical protocol, conceived together with the *Ghāya* as one of the two culminating stages of the sage’s education. Most manuscripts instead give 439–442/1047–1051, but specialists regard these dates as a scribal displacement by one century; the work was formerly attributed to Maslama al-Majrīṭī, while modern scholarship identifies Ibn Qāsim as its author. ([de Callataÿ and Moureau](https://doi.org/10.1163/2212943X-00501004); [Fierro](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1595996); [Madelung, critical edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=mD6JAQAACAAJ))

### Astral-magical protocol

- **343–348/954–960:** *Ghāyat al-ḥakīm* (“The Aim of the Sage”). A four-part handbook of astral magic—organizing cosmology, planetary correspondences, talismans, suffumigations, invocations, and inherited magical authorities—and the companion culmination to the *Rutba*; its later Latin translation is known as *Picatrix*, but that translation is not a separate work made by Maslama. Medieval and early-modern witnesses commonly assigned it to Maslama al-Majrīṭī; the attribution to Ibn Qāsim is the now-prevalent scholarly reconstruction, though it remains inferential rather than an uncontested manuscript declaration. ([Fierro](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1595996); [de Callataÿ](https://doi.org/10.3989/alqantara.2013.011); [Sciència.cat work record](https://www.sciencia.cat/scienciacat-db?obra=3020))

### Disputed collective creation and transmission project

- **Before 339/950; precise date and extent unknown:** *Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ* (“Epistles of the Brethren of Purity”). This philosophical encyclopedia resists classification as Maslama’s obra: some recent scholarship identifies him as its author, redactor, or appropriator, while other reconstructions make him only a copyist and early transmitter of an already developing collective corpus from the East to al-Andalus; even the claim that he belonged to the Brethren has been judged speculative. It is therefore included as a disputed editorial/transmission project, not as a securely authored book, and no surviving manuscript has been shown to be his personal copy. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ikhwan-al-safa/); [de Callataÿ](https://doi.org/10.3989/alqantara.2013.011); [de Callataÿ and Moureau](https://doi.org/10.1163/2212943X-00501004))
