## Works by Antonio Mamerto Gil Núñez

### Attributed oral manifestation

- **Traditionally 8 January 1878:** *Untitled prophecy concerning his executioner’s sick son*. In the devotional narrative, Gil foretells that the executioner’s son is gravely ill and promises to intercede if the man invokes him; the utterance therefore functions as a manifesto or performative promise rather than as a descriptive text. No contemporaneous record preserves it: its wording, date, and authorship survive only in divergent oral traditions, while Gil’s historical existence and biography are themselves imperfectly documented ([Ferraro](https://repositorio.uca.edu.ar/bitstream/123456789/17386/1/religiosidad-popular-memoria.pdf), pp. 321–23; [Sousa](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/4326190.pdf), pp. 150–52).

### No securely attributable material or written works

- No scholarly catalogue, collected-works edition, or source-critical study identifies a book, song, prayer, image, shrine, or other material *obra* made by Gil. The prayers, printed legends, canonical image, crosses, ex-votos, and sanctuaries associated with “Gauchito Gil” are later creations of devotees, publishers, artists, and custodians—not surviving works by the purported nineteenth-century man; even the original roadside cross is attributed in the legend to his executioner ([Gentile](https://api.naturalis.fcnym.unlp.edu.ar/server/api/core/bitstreams/4375ff0a-264c-44d3-a19f-4b3befdfb32d/content); [Barrios Cristaldo](https://revistas.javeriana.edu.co/index.php/memoysociedad/article/view/15703); [Ferraro](https://repositorio.uca.edu.ar/bitstream/123456789/17386/1/religiosidad-popular-memoria.pdf), pp. 321–23). Consequently, those objects resist inclusion as his works: they constitute the collective, evolving manifestation of his cult.
