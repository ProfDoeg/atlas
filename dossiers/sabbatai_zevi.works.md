## Works by Sabbatai Zevi

### Manifestos, decrees, and protocols

- **December 1665:** *Royal appointments for the kingdoms of the world*. A body of handwritten patents by which Sabbatai assigned thirty-eight followers imaginary kingships and territories: administrative fictions that acted as instruments of his proclaimed messianic government. The surviving testimony does not establish how much of their wording was his rather than his secretary Samuel Primo’s. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/shabbetai-zevi); [Gershom Scholem](https://books.google.com/books?id=S2pAq_Og2AsC))

- **1666:** *Decree summoning Nathan of Gaza and named companions to Constantinople*. A royal-style command rather than a descriptive text; contemporary testimony says it ordered Nathan and specified followers, including four men from Hebron, to appear before Sabbatai. Its wording survives only through hostile or retrospective witnesses, so its textual form and scribal authorship remain uncertain. ([David J. Halperin, *Sabbatai Zevi: Testimonies to a Fallen Messiah*](https://dokumen.pub/sabbatai-zevi-testimonies-to-a-fallen-messiah-9781904113256-1904113257-9781906764241.html))

- **Summer 1666:** *Letter concerning the Festival of Comforts*. A manifesto-protocol sent to Jewish communities, commanding that the Ninth of Av—traditionally a fast mourning the destroyed Temples and also Sabbatai’s asserted birthday—become a full festival with festive prayer, food, lights, music, and ordinary work suspended. It is transmitted as Sabbatai’s command, although Primo probably served as secretary and may have drafted its written form. ([Posen Library](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/letter-0); [Gershom Scholem](https://books.google.com/books?id=S2pAq_Og2AsC))

### Letters and circular epistles

- **After September 1666; exact date unknown:** *Letter on Conversion*. An open doctrinal letter presenting Sabbatai’s conversion to Islam as divinely required and instructing those whose souls accorded with his to follow him; it therefore functions simultaneously as confession, manifesto, and conversion protocol. It survives through a later manuscript publication, and no autograph is identified in the scholarly edition. ([Paweł Maciejko, ed., *Sabbatian Heresy*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctv102bcv4.13); [Abraham Amarillo source notice](https://noahbickart.fastmail.fm.user.fm/TRS%20312/Habib_Testimony.pdf))

- **During the Dulcigno exile, 1673–1676; attribution disputed:** *Bury My Faith!* A circular letter ordering believers to suppress public Sabbatian observance, restore the abolished fasts, cease calling him king or messiah, and return home. Avraham Elqayam and Paweł Maciejko accept it as Sabbatai’s, while translator David Halperin regards it as a forgery designed to reintegrate believers into Jewish society; it consequently resists classification as an authenticated obra. ([Avraham Elqayam’s edition record](https://cris.biu.ac.il/en/publications/%D7%A7%D7%91%D7%A8%D7%95-%D7%90%D7%9E%D7%95%D7%A0%D7%AA%D7%99-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%92%D7%A8%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%AA%D7%99-%D7%A6%D7%91%D7%99-%D7%9E%D7%9E%D7%A7%D7%95%D7%9D-%D7%92%D7%9C%D7%95%D7%AA%D7%95-%D7%90%D7%99%D7%92%D7%A8%D7%AA-%D7%9E%D7%90%D7%AA-%D7%A9%D7%91%D7%AA%D7%99-%D7%A6%D7%91%D7%99-%D7%9E%D7%9E%D7%A7/); [Maciejko/Halperin attribution note](https://noahbickart.fastmail.fm.user.fm/TRS%20312/Bury_My_Faith.pdf))

- **6 April 1676:** *Letter to the faithful of Sofia / Arnaut-Belgrade*. A messianic circular authorizing its bearer to speak and command in Sabbatai’s name. Three versions survive, addressed or adapted for different recipients; the Arnaut-Belgrade version may be in Sabbatai’s own hand, while Baruch of Arezzo’s transmitted version suppresses some of its most audacious claims. ([Halperin, “Epistles of Sabbatai Zevi”](https://dokumen.pub/sabbatai-zevi-testimonies-to-a-fallen-messiah-9781904113256-1904113257-9781906764241.html))

- **1676; exact date unknown:** *Letter to Joseph Filosoff*. A private but ceremonially styled epistle announcing Sabbatai’s intended arrival with Filosoff’s daughter Esther and her children. Its dense reassignment of biblical identities makes it less ordinary correspondence than a performed messianic genealogy. ([Halperin, “Epistles of Sabbatai Zevi”](https://dokumen.pub/sabbatai-zevi-testimonies-to-a-fallen-messiah-9781904113256-1904113257-9781906764241.html))

- **1676; exact date unknown:** *Letter to Joseph Karillo*. A summons commanding Karillo to come with two named companions, Isaac Haver and Abraham Ohev; Halperin cautions that the companions’ conveniently symbolic surnames may mean they never existed. The letter is thus a command-document whose human referents, not merely its dating, remain uncertain. ([Halperin, “Epistles of Sabbatai Zevi”](https://dokumen.pub/sabbatai-zevi-testimonies-to-a-fallen-messiah-9781904113256-1904113257-9781906764241.html))

### Dictated or attributed theological texts

- **About 1668:** *Raza de-Mehemanuta* (*The Mystery/Secret of the Faith*). Scholem treated this short theological tract as the first written exposition of Sabbatai’s post-conversion system, orally dictated by him to a disciple rather than written in his hand. Yehuda Liebes subsequently identified Abraham Miguel Cardozo as its actual author, so it belongs here only as a traditionally attributed and now strongly disputed obra. ([Scholem, “The Holiness of Sin”](https://www.commentary.org/articles/gershom-scholem/the-holiness-of-sin/); [New York Public Library manuscript description and Liebes attribution](https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/collections/collection-of-texts-on-shabbethai-tzevi-and-the-sabbatean-movement?filters%5Bname%5D=Cardozo%2C+Abraham+Miguel%2C+-1706&filters%5Btopic%5D%5B%5D=Sabbathaians&keywords=))

- **After 1666; exact date unknown:** *Sahaduta de-Mehemanuta* (*Testimony Concerning Faith*). An anonymous Sabbatian theological tract sometimes conflated with *Raza de-Mehemanuta* and sometimes attributed to Sabbatai; the manuscript catalogue describes it as anonymous, while the specialist primary-source bibliography assigns it to him. That unresolved conflict makes it an attributed manifestation of his doctrine, not a securely authored work. ([New York Public Library manuscript description](https://old-digitalcollections.nypl.org/collections/collection-of-texts-on-shabbethai-tzevi-and-the-sabbatean-movement?filters%5Bname%5D=Cardozo%2C+Abraham+Miguel%2C+-1706&filters%5Btopic%5D%5B%5D=Sabbathaians&keywords=); [Society for Sabbatean Studies](https://www.sabbateanstudies.org/primasy-sources))

### Ritual and institutional creations

- **1665–1666:** *The messianic festival calendar*. A dispersed ritual project rather than one stable text: Sabbatai and his chancery abolished established fasts, most conspicuously the Ninth of Av, and replaced mourning with messianic feast days. Its surviving letters are protocols for enactment, while the calendar itself existed through repeated communal performance and therefore resists being reduced to a single written work. ([Posen Library](https://www.posenlibrary.com/entry/letter-0); [Gershom Scholem](https://books.google.com/books?id=S2pAq_Og2AsC))

- **1665–1676:** *Messianic kingship and the “Faith”*. Sabbatai made no systematic book bearing this title; rather, through proclamations, titles, ceremonies, appointments, conversion, and private teaching he created an unstable religious-political project centered on his person and his personal “God of faith.” It is closer to a Gesamtkunstwerk of body, ritual, government, and utterance than to a separable text, and much of its written theology was actually made by Nathan of Gaza, Cardozo, and later followers. ([Maciejko, introduction to *Sabbatian Heresy*](https://noahbickart.fastmail.fm.user.fm/TRS%20312/Maciejko_Introduction.pdf); [Scholem](https://books.google.com/books?id=S2pAq_Og2AsC))
