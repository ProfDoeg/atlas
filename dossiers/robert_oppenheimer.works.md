### Wartime project, laboratory, and collective technological creations

- **1943–1945:** *Los Alamos Laboratory wartime project*. Oppenheimer directed the site and organized its scientific program; this is an institutional project and collective obra rather than a personally authored object. Its boundaries include organization, theory, experiments, engineering, and weapons development, so it resists attribution to any single maker. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2119); [NAS, pp. 195–200](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))
- **1945:** *The Gadget and the Trinity test*. The first nuclear explosive and its test were creations of the Los Alamos project under Oppenheimer’s direction, not sole works by him; “work” here names a vast collaborative technological event as much as an artifact. ([U.S. Department of Energy, Manhattan Project history](https://www.osti.gov/opennet/manhattan-project-history/Events/1945/trinity.htm))
- **1942–1945:** *Little Boy and Fat Man*. Atomic weapons made by the Manhattan Project, with Los Alamos under Oppenheimer responsible for weapon design and assembly. They belong in his body of work only in the qualified sense of directed collective creations; their authorship cannot responsibly be collapsed into his name. ([U.S. Department of Energy, Manhattan Project history](https://www.osti.gov/opennet/manhattan-project-history/Events/1945/hiroshima.htm))
- **1945:** *Los Alamos “Final Report” / Los Alamos technical report*. A collective scientific report on wartime bomb development, signed by Oppenheimer and numerous other participants; it is evidence of institutional production, not a sole-authored text. It is sometimes confused with Henry DeWolf Smyth’s separately authored public report. ([Atomic Heritage Foundation](https://ahf.nuclearmuseum.org/ahf/history/smyth-report/))

### Policy projects, reports, and protocols

- **1946:** *Report on the International Control of Atomic Energy* (“Acheson–Lilienthal Report”). A policy project developed by a board of consultants chaired by David Lilienthal, on which Oppenheimer was the principal scientific figure; it proposes an international system for controlling atomic energy and therefore functions partly as a protocol. Its collective authorship must be retained. ([U.S. Department of State, Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1946v01/d282))
- **1946–1952:** *General Advisory Committee recommendations to the Atomic Energy Commission*. A body of collective advisory work produced while Oppenheimer chaired the committee. Individual memoranda and recommendations resist treatment as his personal works because they were committee acts, although his intellectual and editorial influence was substantial. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2119))
- **1949:** *General Advisory Committee report on the “super”*. A collective policy memorandum opposing an immediate crash program for the hydrogen bomb; it acts as advice to government and thus approaches a manifesto, but it is a committee document rather than Oppenheimer’s sole declaration. ([Atomic Archive](https://www.atomicarchive.com/resources/documents/hydrogen/gac-report.html))

### Books and sustained lecture-obras

- **1953–1954:** *Science and the Common Understanding*. First delivered as the BBC Reith Lectures and then made into a book; the lecture series and book are manifestations of one sustained explanatory obra, not unrelated works. ([NAS, p. 214](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))
- **1955:** *The Open Mind*. A book assembling eight lectures and essays on science, public affairs, and intellectual freedom; because most constituent texts had earlier lives as speeches or articles, the volume is both a new arrangement and a collected manifestation. ([NAS, p. 214](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))
- **1959–1960:** *Some Reflections on Science and Culture*. The 1959 McNair Lectures, published in 1960 as a pamphlet-sized book; later variants should not be counted automatically as separate obras. ([NAS, p. 216](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))
- **1962:** *The Flying Trapeze: Three Crises for Physicists*. The Whidden Lectures issued as a book. The National Academy bibliography misprints the title as *The Flyer Trapeze*; contemporary bibliographic records support *The Flying Trapeze*. ([NAS, p. 217](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf); [WorldCat authority record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/409767))
- **1964:** *Oppenheimer*. A televised autobiographical interview or documentary made for NBC’s *White Paper* series, rather than a conventional authored text; Oppenheimer’s spoken testimony is one component of a collaboratively produced broadcast. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms998007/ms998007.pdf))
- **1965:** *The Decision to Drop the Bomb*. A televised interview/documentary later issued in book form; the broadcast and book are related manifestations, and editorial production prevents simple sole authorship. ([NAS, p. 218](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))

### Archival creations that resist enumeration as discrete works

- **1921–1967:** *Correspondence, memoranda, speeches, lecture manuscripts, scientific notes, desk books, statements, and working papers*. These survive as a large archival body rather than a fixed sequence of titled obras; many items are unpublished, untitled, collaborative, duplicated, or only preparatory. The Library of Congress finding aid is therefore the appropriate catalogue-level description, and pretending that every leaf is an autonomous “work” would manufacture distinctions the archive does not make. ([Library of Congress finding aid](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/gdc/gdcfindingaidpdfs/ms998007/ms998007.pdf))
- **1920s–1940s:** *Teaching and the Berkeley school of theoretical physics*. Oppenheimer made a research culture through seminars, supervision, problems, and intellectual collaboration, but it has no single title, date, or material boundary. It is an obra in the sense of a body of pedagogical practice, not a text that can be itemized without converting students’ work into his. ([NAS, pp. 185–194](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))
- **1947–1966:** *Directorship of the Institute for Advanced Study*. An institutional and intellectual project under which Oppenheimer shaped appointments, programs, lectures, and interdisciplinary exchange. It resists being called a personal work because its substance consists of an institution and the labor of many other people. ([NAS, pp. 203–209](https://www.nasonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/oppenheimer-j-robert.pdf))

### Posthumous manifestations, not new works made by Oppenheimer

- **1969:** *Robert Oppenheimer: Letters and Recollections*. A posthumous editorial construction from Oppenheimer’s correspondence and others’ recollections; the constituent letters are his, but the book as arranged was made after his death and should not be treated as a book he authored.
- **1980:** *Letters and Recollections*. A later expanded or differently issued editorial manifestation of Oppenheimer’s letters, not a new lifetime obra.
- **1984:** *Uncommon Sense*. A posthumous selection of Oppenheimer’s lectures and essays edited by N. Metropolis, Gian-Carlo Rota, and David Sharp; its contents derive from earlier works, while the collection as such belongs to its editors.
- **1989:** *Atom and Void: Essays on Science and Community*. A posthumous edited selection; it is a new compilation of existing writings, not a newly discovered book composed by Oppenheimer.
