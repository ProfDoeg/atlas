## Works by José Julián Martí Pérez

### Poetry and poetic projects

- **1862:** *A Micaela*. Martí’s earliest surviving poem, written for the wife of Rafael María de Mendive; it belongs to the juvenile manuscripts rather than to a book planned by Martí. [Ripoll, *Índice universal*, chronological entry for 1862](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm).

- **1868:** *¡10 de octubre!*. Patriotic poem occasioned by the beginning of Cuba’s Ten Years’ War; it survives as an individual periodical text, not as part of an author-organized collection. [Ripoll, *Índice universal*, entries for 1868](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm).

- **1869:** *Abdala*. A dramatic poem or one-act verse drama published in the sole issue of *La Patria Libre*; its fusion of poetry, theatre, and anticolonial allegory resists classification as merely a poem or play. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/diccionario-de-la-literatura-cubana--0/html/254p.htm).

- **1882:** *Ismaelillo*. A book of fifteen poems addressed to Martí’s son, José Francisco; it was conceived and published by Martí as a unified poetic book. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **approximately 1878–1882; unpublished in Martí’s lifetime:** *Versos libres*. A projected poetic book preserved in manuscripts and drafts. The title and project are Martí’s, but its final contents and ordering cannot be treated as completely settled because he never prepared a definitive edition. [UNED](https://portal.uned.es/portal/page?_dad=portal&_pageid=93%2C69900155&_schema=PORTAL); [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO, critical-edition catalogue](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **1891:** *Versos sencillos*. A book of forty-six numbered poems, composed as a sequence and published by Martí in New York. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **principally the 1880s; posthumously assembled:** *Flores del destierro*. This is not securely an author-created book: later editors gathered poems under the title, but Martí did not identify such a volume in his accounts of his poetic projects. The modern critical edition therefore returns the poems to their manuscript and chronological contexts. [“El libro inexistente”](https://centroconvivencia.org/el-libro-inexistente-notas-para-leer-la-poesia-reunida-en-flores-del-destierro/).

- **1862–1895:** *Uncollected poems, poetic drafts, fragments, and dedications*. Hundreds of poems and fragments survive outside *Ismaelillo*, *Versos libres*, and *Versos sencillos*. They constitute a manuscript and periodical corpus rather than a single obra with an authorial title or stable boundary. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO, *Obras completas*, vols. 14–15](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/); [Ripoll, *Índice universal*](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm).

### Theatre and performance texts

- **1874:** *Adúltera*. A drama composed in Spain and left unpublished and unperformed during Martí’s lifetime; surviving preliminary notes make its status partly that of an unfinished theatrical project. [Centro de Estudios Martianos, *Obras completas: edición crítica*](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/); [Ripoll, *Índice universal*](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm).

- **1875:** *Amor con amor se paga*. A one-act “dramatic proverb” in verse, written for performance and premiered at Mexico City’s Teatro Principal on 19 December 1875; it was printed the following year. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1877:** *Patria y libertad (Drama indio)*. A verse drama written in Guatemala around an Indigenous anticolonial subject. It is both theatre and political allegory, and its surviving textual history is less settled than that of the plays Martí published himself. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO, *Obras completas: edición crítica*](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

### Narrative, books, and extended prose

- **1871:** *El presidio político en Cuba*. A testimonial-political pamphlet about colonial imprisonment and forced labour; its purpose is simultaneously documentary, accusatory, and propagandistic. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1873:** *La República Española ante la Revolución Cubana*. A political pamphlet addressed to republican Spain, arguing that Spanish republican principles require recognition of Cuban independence. It acts as an intervention in contemporary politics and can properly be called a manifesto as well as an essay. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1878:** *Guatemala*. A book-length political and cultural portrait of Guatemala, published in Mexico; it combines travel observation, national interpretation, portraiture, and advocacy rather than conforming to a single genre. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1879:** *Rasgos biográficos de Alfredo Torroella*. A commemorative biographical address subsequently printed as a pamphlet; it is an act of public mourning as well as literary portraiture. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1885:** *Amistad funesta*. Martí’s only completed novel, first serialized under the pseudonym “Adelaida Ral.” Later editions often call it *Lucía Jerez*, but that was not the title of its original serial manifestation. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **before 1879; unfinished or lost:** *History of the first years of the Cuban Revolution*. Martí told Manuel Mercado that this projected book was nearly finished, but no complete text is known. It should be recorded as a project, not silently promoted into a surviving book. [Chronology of Martí’s life](https://accjosemarti.wordpress.com/jose-marti/cronologia-de-la-vida-de-jose-marti/).

### Periodicals as editorial obras

- **19 January 1869:** *El Diablo Cojuelo*. A one-number satirical newspaper made collaboratively by Martí and Fermín Valdés Domínguez. Accounts that call Martí its sole founder or director are disputed; the safer attribution is participation in its conception and writing. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/diccionario-de-la-literatura-cubana--0/html/254p.htm); [Universidade de Brasília](https://livros.unb.br/index.php/portal/catalog/download/374/590/2894?inline=1).

- **23 January 1869:** *La Patria Libre*. A short-lived political newspaper edited with Martí’s participation and represented by one surviving issue, containing *Abdala*. The newspaper is a collective editorial manifestation, not simply another title for Martí’s poem. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/diccionario-de-la-literatura-cubana--0/html/254p.htm).

- **1–15 July 1881:** *Revista Venezolana*. A periodical project founded, directed, and largely written by Martí in Caracas; only two numbers appeared. Its brevity does not make it unfinished in the same way as a draft, but the intended continuing magazine was abruptly terminated. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **July–October 1889:** *La Edad de Oro*. Four monthly issues of an illustrated magazine written and edited by Martí for children in the Americas. Later binding as a book obscures its original identity as a serial Gesamtkunstwerk of stories, poems, historical explanation, science, art commentary, typography, and images. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9); [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **from 14 March 1892:** *Patria*. The revolutionary newspaper Martí founded in New York and helped edit and write. It was a collective political instrument rather than his private publication, so the newspaper as a whole should not be attributed to him alone. [Sociedad Cultural José Martí](https://martianoscuba.wordpress.com/2026/03/14/patria-de-jose-marti-punto-culminante-del-periodismo-como-mision-por-marlene-vazquez-perez/).

### Essays, chronicles, criticism, and journalistic corpora

- **1881–1891:** *Escenas norteamericanas* / *Cartas de Nueva York*. An extensive, dispersed series of chronicles sent from the United States to newspapers across Spanish America. The collective titles are editorial and generic designations rather than a single book fixed by Martí. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1882:** *Prólogo a “El poema del Niágara”*. A literary-critical prologue to Juan Antonio Pérez Bonalde’s poem that became an autonomous statement about poetry and modernity, although materially it first existed as part of another author’s book. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1882:** *Sección constante*. A recurring journalistic column in Caracas’s *La Opinión Nacional*, composed of short reports and commentaries. It is better understood as a serial textual practice than as one essay or book. [Ripoll, *Índice universal*](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm).

- **1883–1884:** *La América*. Martí’s articles, reviews, notices, and editorials for the New York magazine form an editorial-journalistic corpus; he worked first as a contributor and editor and later directed the publication, but did not author all of its contents. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **25 March 1889:** *Vindicación de Cuba*. A public letter first printed in English in the *New-York Evening Post*, answering disparaging North American representations of Cubans; it is both polemic and political action. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1889:** *Cuba y los Estados Unidos*. A pamphlet assembling the hostile articles answered by Martí and his response, *Vindicación de Cuba*. As an object it is partly compilation and counter-publication, not a wholly new continuous work. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1891:** *Nuestra América*. A political-cultural essay first published in periodicals, not originally a book. Its prescriptions for self-government and continental solidarity give it the force of a manifesto, although its form is argumentative prose. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **1893:** *Mi raza*. An antiracist political article published in *Patria*; it argues against both racism and racially separatist politics. It is an intervention within the newspaper project rather than an independently issued book. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **1875–1895:** *Periodical articles, reviews, portraits, correspondence columns, notices, and unsigned editorial matter*. Martí produced thousands of individual newspaper and magazine texts across the Americas. No short title-list is genuinely complete: Carlos Ripoll’s standard index registers 2,620 chronologically arranged items through 1895 plus undated writings and a title index, while the still-expanding critical edition incorporates newly located and reattributed material. [Ripoll, *Índice universal*](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm); [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

### Speeches, lectures, and commemorative performances

- **24 January 1880:** *Asuntos cubanos*. A lecture delivered at Steck Hall in New York and issued as a pamphlet; it is political analysis designed for collective deliberation. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **30 November 1889:** *Heredia*. A commemorative address delivered at Hardman Hall on the Cuban poet José María Heredia and printed separately. Its obra is both the performed occasion and the surviving text. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **19 December 1889:** *Madre América*. A ceremonial and political speech to delegates attending the International American Conference. The familiar title is conventional; the original obra was an oratorical event before it became a printed text. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **30 March 1891:** *Informe sobre la Comisión Monetaria Internacional Americana*. A bilingual diplomatic-economic report on monetary policy, delivered before the commission in Washington; it is institutional testimony rather than literary essay alone. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **26 November 1891:** *Con todos y para el bien de todos*. A political speech delivered in Tampa to Cuban emigrants. Its received title derives from its defining formulation and should not be mistaken for a book title supplied on an original title page. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **27 November 1891:** *Los pinos nuevos*. A commemorative and mobilizing speech delivered in Tampa. Like the preceding address, it is simultaneously a performed event, a political act, and a subsequently stabilized text. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1891:** *Por Cuba y para Cuba*. A pamphlet containing the two Tampa speeches; it is a documentary publication made from performances, not a third independent speech. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **17 February 1892:** *Oración de Tampa y Cayo Hueso*. A New York address celebrating the political unity achieved among émigré organizations; the title is conventional and the work’s first form was oral. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1893:** *Simón Bolívar*. A commemorative address delivered at the Sociedad Literaria Hispano-Americana and issued separately. It is historical portraiture shaped for public performance. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1875–1894:** *Other speeches, lectures, toasts, commemorations, and classroom addresses*. Some survive as authorial manuscripts, some as contemporary reports, and some only fragmentarily; consequently the spoken event and the recoverable text do not always coincide. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/); [Ripoll, *Índice universal*](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm).

### Manifestos, protocols, and revolutionary organization

- **28 November 1891:** *Resoluciones de Tampa*. Resolutions drafted by Martí and approved by Cuban émigré organizations. They are a protocol for political union and revolutionary preparation, not merely an essay expressing Martí’s opinions. [Chronology of Martí’s life](https://accjosemarti.wordpress.com/jose-marti/cronologia-de-la-vida-de-jose-marti/).

- **5 January 1892:** *Bases del Partido Revolucionario Cubano*. The foundational public principles of the party Martí organized. This is a manifesto and constitutive instrument: its language creates a political organization and defines its purpose. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **5 January 1892:** *Estatutos secretos del Partido Revolucionario Cubano*. An operational protocol governing the party’s clandestine structure and conduct. It belongs to a collective political institution even though Martí drafted it. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1892–1895:** *Circulars, club communications, credentials, instructions, proclamations, and official correspondence of the Partido Revolucionario Cubano*. These texts often act administratively—authorizing, appointing, coordinating, or instructing—and many are co-signed or institutional, so exclusive personal authorship cannot always be assumed. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **1894:** *Plan de Fernandina*. A coordinated revolutionary expedition organized by Martí and his associates rather than a single bounded text. It is properly an unfinished political-logistical project whose material manifestation included vessels, arms, contracts, instructions, and correspondence; United States authorities stopped it before departure. [Center for a Free Cuba, Martí chronology](https://www.josemarti.info/chronology.html).

- **25 March 1895:** *Manifiesto de Montecristi*. A manifesto signed by José Martí and Máximo Gómez setting out the purposes and principles of Cuba’s renewed war of independence. Its joint authorship and performative political function are essential parts of what the text is. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **1895:** *El Partido Revolucionario Cubano a Cuba*. A revolutionary proclamation issued at Montecristi as a broadside. It is a collective political utterance associated with Martí, not simply a personal literary composition. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

### Translations

- **1875:** *Mis hijos*. Martí’s Spanish translation of Victor Hugo’s *Mes fils*. It is a made text by Martí but not an independently authored narrative: both Hugo’s source authorship and Martí’s translational authorship must remain visible. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO, *Obras completas*, “Traducciones I”](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **1886:** *Misterio*. Martí’s Spanish translation of Hugh Conway’s novel *Called Back*, accompanied by a translator’s prologue. The Spanish text is a translational obra, distinct from both Conway’s novel and Martí’s original fiction. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO, *Obras completas*, “Traducciones I”](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **1887:** *Ramona*. Martí’s Spanish translation of Helen Hunt Jackson’s novel, also supplied with a prologue. It is both commercial translation and an intervention in the representation of Indigenous dispossession. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9); [Centro de Estudios Martianos, *Anuario* 38](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/5343/1/Anuario_38.pdf).

- **date uncertain; not located:** *Lalla Rookh*. Martí worked on a Spanish translation of Thomas Moore’s poem, but the translation has not been found. It survives as an attested project, not as an extant work that editors can reconstruct. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **various dates:** *Other literary and utilitarian translations*. The critical edition devotes two volumes to translations, including complete works, excerpts, drafts, and translated matter embedded in journalism. Their boundaries and degree of Martí’s intervention vary, so they resist reduction to a simple list of “books by” him. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO, *Obras completas*, vols. 20–21](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

### Letters, diaries, notebooks, and graphic traces

- **approximately the 1860s–1895:** *Epistolario*. Martí’s thousands of private, political, editorial, diplomatic, and organizational letters form a distributed corpus rather than a book he authored as such. Some are collaborative or institutional communications, and the surviving corpus is necessarily incomplete. [Ripoll, *Índice universal*, epistolary index](https://www.ismaelillo.com/indice/index.htm); [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **various dates:** *Cuadernos de apuntes*. Manuscript notebooks containing observations, reading notes, drafts, plans, linguistic material, drawings, and fragments. They resist being called finished works: their value lies precisely in preserving making-in-progress. [Centro de Estudios Martianos, *Obras completas*, vol. 21](https://biblioteca.clacso.edu.ar/Cuba/cem-cu/20150115031418/Vol21.pdf).

- **1881:** *Diario de viaje de Caracas a Nueva York*. Travel notes recording Martí’s return from Venezuela to the United States; the designation “diary” describes a manuscript practice rather than a book prepared for publication. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **9 April–6 May 1895:** *Diario de Cabo Haitiano a Dos Ríos*. A campaign diary recording the revolutionary expedition from Hispaniola to Cuba and its first weeks in the field. It ends shortly before Martí’s death and is complete only in the circumstantial sense that no later continuation could be made. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **18 May 1895; unfinished:** *Carta a Manuel Mercado*. Martí’s final surviving letter, interrupted on the eve of his death. It is private correspondence with the scope of a political testament, but its unfinished state must not be concealed by treating it as a completed essay. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

- **various dates:** *Drawings, marginal sketches, calligraphic designs, and visual annotations*. The critical edition includes Martí’s drawings alongside manuscripts and notebooks. These are graphic manifestations—sometimes autonomous, more often inseparable from note-taking—and should not be mislabeled as literary texts. [Centro de Estudios Martianos/CLACSO](https://libreria.clacso.org/biblioteca_jose_marti/).

### Posthumous constructions that are not Martí-made books

- **from 1900:** *Obras completas*. Successive collected editions beginning with Gonzalo de Quesada y Aróstegui’s fifteen-volume undertaking preserve Martí’s production, but their divisions, titles, and arrangements are editorial creations made after his death. They must not be counted as books Martí himself composed. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).

- **posthumous:** *Escenas norteamericanas*, *Nuestra América*, *En los Estados Unidos*, *Crítica y libros*, *Flor y lava*, *Páginas escogidas*, and numerous similarly titled selections. These editions often name genuine textual corpora or individual essays, but as books and tables of contents they are generally works of later editors rather than discrete Martí-made objects. [Diccionario de la literatura cubana, bibliography](https://buho.guru/dict/literatura_cubana/MART%C3%8D,_Jos%C3%A9).
