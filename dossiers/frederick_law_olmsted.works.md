## Works by Frederick Law Olmsted

### Books and sustained works of reportage

- **1852:** *Walks and Talks of an American Farmer in England*. A book of travel observation, agricultural inquiry, and social criticism derived from Olmsted’s 1850 walking tour; it is his first published book. [Olmsted Network bibliography](https://olmsted.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Works-by-FLO.pdf)

- **1856:** *A Journey in the Seaboard Slave States, with Remarks on Their Economy*. A book assembled from Olmsted’s commissioned reporting on slavery, labor, and Southern society for the *New-York Daily Times*. [Olmsted Network bibliography](https://olmsted.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Works-by-FLO.pdf)

- **1857:** *A Journey through Texas; or, A Saddle-Trip on the Southwestern Frontier, with a Statistical Appendix*. A travel narrative and antislavery investigation substantially indebted to the observations and writing of Olmsted’s brother John Hull Olmsted; sole attribution to Frederick therefore obscures a collaborative authorship. [Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/olmsted-frederick-law)

- **1857:** *Introduction and Chapter XXVI to The Englishman in Kansas; or, Squatter Life and Border Warfare*. Contributions to Thomas H. Gladstone’s book rather than an autonomous book by Olmsted; the object resists treatment as a separate work because it survives as part of another author’s publication. [Olmsted Network bibliography](https://olmsted.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Works-by-FLO.pdf)

- **1860:** *A Journey in the Back Country*. The third book generated by Olmsted’s investigation of slavery and the Southern economy. [Olmsted Network bibliography](https://olmsted.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Works-by-FLO.pdf)

- **1861:** *The Cotton Kingdom: A Traveller’s Observations on Cotton and Slavery in the American Slave States*. A two-volume recomposition and abridgment of the three preceding Southern journeys, rather than wholly new reportage; its British manifestation appeared as *Journeys and Explorations in the Cotton Kingdom*. [Library of Congress chronology and finding aid](https://www.loc.gov/collections/frederick-law-olmsted-papers/articles-and-essays/timeline/), [Handbook of Texas](https://www.tshaonline.org/handbook/entries/olmsted-frederick-law)

- **1863:** *Hospital Transports: A Memoir of the Embarkation of the Sick and Wounded from the Peninsula of Virginia in the Summer of 1862*. An institutional memoir and documentary account associated with Olmsted’s executive work for the United States Sanitary Commission. [Olmsted Network bibliography](https://olmsted.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Works-by-FLO.pdf)

### Manifestos, reports, plans, and protocols

- **1858:** *Greensward*. Olmsted and Calvert Vaux’s competition report and plan for Central Park: simultaneously a design project, a set of instructions for making a landscape, and a manifesto for an urban park; the complete original report and accompanying views are preserved in the scholarly *Papers*. [Olmsted Papers Project](https://olmsted.org/olmsted-papers-project/), [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1865:** *Yosemite and the Mariposa Grove: A Preliminary Report*. A conservation report drafted for the California commissioners, arguing that spectacular scenery should be preserved for public use; it acts as policy advocacy and thus approaches a manifesto. [Olmsted Network bibliography](https://olmsted.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/Works-by-FLO.pdf)

- **1868:** *A Report on the Park and City of Brooklyn*. A planning report by Olmsted and Vaux that treats parks, parkways, streets, public health, and urban growth as a single civic system. It is both text and operative planning project. [Library of Congress firm timeline](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1870:** *Public Parks and the Enlargement of Towns*. A published address advancing parks and parkways as instruments of metropolitan planning and democratic social life; it functions as a professional and civic manifesto. [Digital edition of the *Papers of Frederick Law Olmsted*](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/OLMS.html)

- **1881:** *A Consideration of the Justifying Value of a Public Park*. An argumentative park report, not merely a description: it specifies the social effects by which a public landscape should be judged. [Digital edition of the *Papers of Frederick Law Olmsted*](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/OLMS.html)

- **1882:** *The Spoils of the Park*. A polemical text about political patronage and Central Park administration; its title and separate publication are documented in the Library of Congress finding aid. [Library of Congress finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2365)

- **1890:** *Governmental Preservation of Natural Scenery*. A privately printed conservation argument whose purpose is governmental action, making it closer to a manifesto than a neutral essay. [Chronology in *The Papers of Frederick Law Olmsted*, vol. 8](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/default.xqy?keys=OLMS-print-01-08-03-0001&mode=deref&printable=yes)

- **1838–1895:** *Speeches, articles, letters, reports, memoranda, journals, and unrealized book manuscripts*. This is a dispersed textual obra rather than a single work: the archive includes drafts, lectures, professional reports, correspondence, maps, and notes, including material for an unfinished history of civilization. No honest itemization can convert every archival document into a separately titled “work”; the twelve-volume editorial project selects significant writings rather than claiming to reproduce every paper. [Library of Congress collection description](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2365), [Olmsted Papers Project](https://olmsted.org/olmsted-papers-project/)

### Public parks, parkways, and scenic reservations

- **1858–1876:** *Central Park / Greensward Plan, New York*. A Gesamtkunstwerk of terrain, planting, water, circulation, bridges, architecture, and social program, designed with Calvert Vaux and realized by a large administrative and construction workforce. The master chronology records later firm work extending beyond Olmsted’s own period of control. [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/), [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1861:** *Hartford Retreat for the Insane grounds*. A therapeutic institutional landscape involving Olmsted, Vaux, George E. Waring Jr., and others; its divided authorship resists attribution to Olmsted alone. [Library of Congress firm timeline](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1861:** *Bloomingdale Asylum grounds*. An institutional-landscape design prepared with Calvert Vaux. [Library of Congress firm timeline](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1861:** *Hillside Cemetery, Middletown, New York*. A cemetery-landscape project by Olmsted and Vaux. [Library of Congress firm timeline](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1868–1901:** *Prospect Park, Brooklyn*. A public landscape designed with Vaux and subsequently modified and administered across decades; it is a collaborative, evolving place rather than a fixed object completed on one date. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1868–c. 1905:** *Brooklyn park and parkway system*. A metropolitan network encompassing Prospect Park, parkways, and related public grounds; firm work after Olmsted’s direct participation means the whole system cannot be assigned to him without qualification. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1868–1873:** *Eastern Parkway*. A parkway by Olmsted and Vaux connecting Prospect Park with the developing city: road, linear park, circulation protocol, and urban-growth instrument at once. [Library of Congress firm timeline](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1868–1876:** *Ocean Parkway*. A landscaped metropolitan route by Olmsted and Vaux joining Prospect Park to the ocean; it is infrastructure made scenic rather than simply a park. [Library of Congress firm timeline](https://www.loc.gov/collections/olmsted-associates-records/articles-and-essays/timelines/evolution-of-olmsted-firm-1822-to-1900/)

- **1869–c. 1905:** *Buffalo park and parkway system*. An interconnected civic landscape initiated by Olmsted and Vaux, including The Park, The Parade, The Front, and linking parkways; subsequent work by the firm complicates both its terminal date and individual authorship. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/parks-parkways-recreations-areas-scenic-reservations-cont.htm)

- **1871–1889:** *Morningside Park, New York*. A steep-site public park project begun by Olmsted and Vaux but altered and completed through later designers and administrations; “the Olmsted work” is therefore partly an intention and partly a transformed manifestation. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1873–1917:** *Belle Isle, Detroit*. A public-park project whose long firm chronology extends well past Olmsted’s direct work, so the full realized park is not exclusively his obra. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1876–1905:** *Mount Royal Park, Montreal*. A mountain park and circulation system begun under Olmsted but implemented and modified over decades; its realized form does not perfectly coincide with his project. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1877–1895:** *Boston and Brookline park system, later called the Emerald Necklace*. A regional Gesamtkunstwerk of parks, parkways, waterways, drainage engineering, sanitation, and neighborhoods; the wider firm chronology continues beyond Olmsted’s active authorship. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/flo.htm)

- **1879–1897:** *Arnold Arboretum*. A public scenic landscape and scientific tree collection designed as part of Boston’s park system; its dual recreational and research purposes prevent it from being only a “park” or only a “garden.” [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/gardens.htm)

- **1880–1915:** *South Park system—Washington Park, Jackson Park, and Midway Plaisance, Chicago*. A linked park system that later furnished the site and armature of the World’s Columbian Exposition; work and alteration continued after Olmsted. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1888 onward:** *Rochester park system*. A system organized around the Genesee River and varied local scenery, including Genesee Valley, Highland, and Seneca/Maplewood parks; later development by the firm makes it a continuing project rather than a single completed object. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/parks.htm)

- **1889–1905:** *National Zoological Park, Washington, D.C.* A zoological, scientific, recreational, and landscape project begun by Olmsted and carried forward by his successors. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1890–1893:** *Milwaukee park system*. Olmsted’s interconnected scheme included Lake, Riverside, and Washington parks and the connecting Newberry Boulevard; later firm work continued from 1907 to 1911. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1891–1948:** *Louisville park and parkway system*. A citywide project initiated under Olmsted, with Cherokee, Iroquois, and Shawnee parks and their parkways; its half-century implementation makes it both Olmsted’s project and a multigenerational firm work. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1893–c. 1930:** *Boston Metropolitan Park System*. A regional preservation and recreation project associated with Olmsted, Charles Eliot, and the successor firm; the distributed authorship and long realization resist designation as Olmsted’s alone. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1895–1959:** *Essex County Park System, New Jersey*. A county-scale park project begun during Olmsted’s final active years and developed principally over the successor firm’s long practice; attribution to the elder Olmsted applies to the project’s inception, not its total realized form. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1896–1905:** *New York State Reservation at Niagara*. A scenic-preservation landscape associated with Olmsted and Vaux but carried through later firm phases; it preserves an existing natural manifestation through deliberately designed access and restraint. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/people.htm)

- **1896–1920:** *Dayton park system*. A municipal park project initiated at the end of Olmsted’s working life and largely developed by successors; it should be classified as a firm project with limited elder-Olmsted authorship. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

### Communities, urban plans, and regional projects

- **1866–1870:** *Staten Island regional plan*. A regional-planning project addressing roads, settlement, and landscape; the dates in the firm chronology distinguish the planning episode from later physical development. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1868–1882:** *Riverside, Illinois*. A planned suburban community by Olmsted and Vaux combining curvilinear streets, parkland, drainage, planting, and rail access; implementation and later alterations continued after the original design. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/flo.htm)

- **1875:** *Plan for the Twenty-third and Twenty-fourth Wards, New York*. A street and open-space plan for the annexed Bronx prepared for the New York Department of Public Parks; as only parts governed later growth, it is principally a project—an intention recorded in plans—rather than a wholly realized work. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/city-planning.htm)

- **1892–1905:** *Druid Hills, Atlanta*. A residential-community plan of curving roads and linear parks begun by Olmsted and completed or revised by the successor firm; the built neighborhood is a later manifestation of an incompletely realized original project. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

### Campuses and institutional grounds

- **1872–1875:** *McLean Asylum grounds, Belmont*. A therapeutic campus-landscape project whose later work continued under the firm. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1872 onward:** *Trinity College campus, Hartford*. A campus-relocation and planning project prompted by Hartford’s expansion; later construction and institutional change mean the present campus is not identical with Olmsted’s plan. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/college-campuses.htm)

- **1876–1877:** *Buffalo State Hospital grounds*. A therapeutic institutional landscape accompanying H. H. Richardson’s hospital architecture; it is a collaborative environment rather than a separable garden. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1883–1901:** *Lawrenceville School campus*. A school-campus landscape developed through a long institutional commission, with work continuing beyond Olmsted’s direct authorship. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1883–1891:** *Leland Stanford Junior University campus plan*. A university plan establishing the general site and organizing conception; the Stanfords, architects, and Olmsted disagreed over monumentality and setting, and later construction departed from aspects of his preferred project. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/college-campuses.htm), [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1890–1893:** *Princeton University campus*. A landscape-planning consultation and early campus project, subsequently continued and transformed by the institution and Olmsted firm. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1890–1915:** *United States Military Academy, West Point*. A campus and military-post landscape project whose firm chronology extends two decades beyond Olmsted’s retirement. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1891–1965:** *Phillips Academy campus, Andover*. A project commissioned from Olmsted but initially answered by John Charles Olmsted and later worked on by Frederick Law Olmsted Jr. and assistants; it is improperly treated as the elder Olmsted’s individual design. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/college-campuses.htm)

### Civic and governmental grounds

- **1875–1898:** *New York State Capitol grounds, Albany*. A civic-ground project developed intermittently and later continued by the firm; the long dates distinguish the project from any single plan or finished manifestation. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1876 and 1892:** *Buffalo City Hall grounds*. Two episodes of civic-landscape consultation or design rather than one continuously executed work. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1876–1889:** *United States Capitol grounds*. A comprehensive setting for the Capitol integrating terraces, circulation, planting, approaches, and architectural features; later firm work in 1894 and 1903–1904 lies outside Olmsted’s sole authorship. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/publicbuildings.htm), [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

### Private estates, domestic landscapes, and working land

- **1880–1881:** *Moraine Farm*. A private-estate landscape for the Phillips/Batchelder property in Beverly, Massachusetts; later firm interventions make the surviving place a layered work. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline)

- **1880–1895:** *Fairsted*. Olmsted’s Brookline home, grounds, and professional office: domestic landscape, experimental design ground, workplace, archive, and institutional seed of the successor firm. It is therefore closer to a Gesamtkunstwerk than to a conventional private garden. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/estates.htm)

- **1886–1889:** *Shelburne Farms*. A working estate organized by Olmsted into farm, forest, and park; agricultural production and scenic composition form one designed system. [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/estates.htm)

- **1889–1895:** *Biltmore Estate grounds*. A vast estate landscape uniting approach road, gardens, arboretum, village, working farm, and forest management; Richard Morris Hunt’s architecture, Vanderbilt patronage, extensive staff work, and later continuation by Olmsted’s sons make it a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than Olmsted’s solitary object. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [Biltmore estate history](https://www.biltmore.com/our-story/biltmore-history/estate-timeline/)

### Expositions, infrastructure, and composite environments

- **1880–1884:** *Boston and Albany Railroad landscape improvements*. A railway-corridor project represented by approximately ninety-five plans and later completed by Olmsted, Olmsted & Eliot; it joins infrastructure, station grounds, planting, and coordinated visual experience. [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/industrial.htm)

- **1891–1893:** *Landscape of the World’s Columbian Exposition, Chicago*. A temporary Gesamtkunstwerk of lagoons, islands, terraces, planting, circulation, architecture, and spectacle, produced with Daniel Burnham’s organization and numerous architects and engineers. Olmsted’s landscape was materially altered or removed after the fair, so the historical work survives partly as site, documentation, and remembered manifestation. [OlmstedOnline chronology](https://olmstedonline.org/Home/Timeline), [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/flo.htm)

- **1857–1897:** *The professional design practice founded by Olmsted*. This is a collective obra encompassing thousands of commissions, plans, drawings, reports, and consultations under changing names and partnerships; it cannot responsibly be collapsed into a list of objects made by one hand. The standard *Master List* catalogues firm projects from 1857 to 1979, while the archives preserve more than 200,000 plans and drawings; entries after Olmsted’s illness and retirement belong principally to partners and successors even when filed under the Olmsted name. [Olmsted Network, “Design Projects”](https://olmsted.org/design-projects/), [National Park Service biography](https://www.nps.gov/frla/learn/historyculture/flo.htm)
