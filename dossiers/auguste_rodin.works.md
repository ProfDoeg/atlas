## Works by Auguste Rodin

### Sculptural œuvres and independent figures

- **1860–1865:** *Madame Cruchet (Tête de femme)*. A terracotta portrait head, among Rodin’s earliest surviving independent sculptures; the dating remains approximate. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1863:** *Le Père Eymard*. A portrait bust of the priest Pierre-Julien Eymard, made in plaster and subsequently cast in bronze. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1863–1864:** *L’Homme au nez cassé*. A deliberately weathered portrait mask rejected by the Salon in 1864; Rodin later exhibited a marble version as a bust, so “the work” comprises a changing model and its manifestations rather than one unique object. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/man-broken-nose).
- **1864:** *Jeune femme et enfant*. A modeled sculptural group, known through plaster and bronze manifestations. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1864:** *Jeune femme au chapeau fleuri de roses*. A terracotta portrait or ideal bust; its descriptive title is conventional rather than necessarily authorial. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1865:** *Jean-Baptiste Rodin, père de l’artiste*. A portrait bust of Rodin’s father, modeled in plaster and later cast in bronze. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1865–1870:** *Buste de jeune femme aux roses dans les cheveux*. A terracotta bust surviving under a descriptive museum title. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **c. 1865–1870:** *Diane chasseresse*. A small mythological figure in marble; its execution belongs to Rodin’s early decorative practice. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1870–1872:** *Suzon*. An idealized young woman’s bust conceived during Rodin’s Belgian period and issued in terracotta, plaster, marble, porcelain, and bronze; the proliferating versions resist treatment as a single fixed object. [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/191803).
- **1875–1877:** *L’Âge d’airain*. A life-size male nude first called *Le Vaincu*; its naturalism prompted the false accusation that Rodin had cast directly from the model. The obra is the modeled figure, while its plasters and bronzes are separate manifestations. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/age-bronze).
- **1878–1880:** *Saint Jean-Baptiste prêchant*. A larger-than-life walking male figure, conceived without narrative accessories and subsequently made in several scales and materials. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/saint-john-baptist).
- **c. 1880–1882:** *Adam*. A monumental male figure devised for *La Porte de l’Enfer* and subsequently treated as an independent sculpture. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **c. 1881:** *Ève*. A monumental female counterpart to *Adam*, initially associated with *La Porte de l’Enfer*; Rodin left the first large figure incompletely worked when its pregnant model ceased posing. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1881–1882:** *Le Penseur*. Originally the small figure commonly identified as Dante above *La Porte de l’Enfer*, then enlarged and made autonomous; “the work” therefore changes function and scale across its manifestations. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/thinker).
- **c. 1881–1882:** *Le Baiser*. A pair first conceived for *La Porte de l’Enfer*, traditionally identified as Paolo and Francesca, but removed from the portal and developed as an independent marble and bronze group. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/kiss).
- **c. 1881–1882:** *Ugolino et ses enfants*. A sculptural group derived from Dante and developed for *La Porte de l’Enfer*, later enlarged and exhibited independently. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1881–1886:** *La Danaïde*. A female figure related to Rodin’s work on *La Porte de l’Enfer*, subsequently realized as an autonomous marble with assistance from the practitioner Jean Escoula. The distinction between Rodin’s model and a practitioner’s carving makes sole manual authorship misleading. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/danaid).
- **c. 1881–1899:** *Fugit Amor*. A pair of intertwined bodies originating in the portal project and repeatedly recombined, reversed, cast, and carved. It is as much a reusable sculptural configuration as a unique object. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1882:** *Je suis belle*. An assemblage joining the figures later known independently as *L’Homme qui tombe* and *La Femme accroupie*; its title comes from Baudelaire. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1882:** *La Femme accroupie*. A compact female figure made for *La Porte de l’Enfer* and later detached, enlarged, and exhibited independently. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1882:** *L’Homme qui tombe*. A falling male body designed for *La Porte de l’Enfer* and repeatedly reused in other groups, including *Je suis belle*. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1882–1885:** *Cariatide à la pierre*. A crouching female figure bearing a block, conceived amid the portal sculptures and issued independently in several materials and scales. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **c. 1884:** *L’Éternel Printemps*. An embracing couple assembled from figures developed around *La Porte de l’Enfer* but excluded from its final composition. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1884–1885:** *L’Éternelle Idole*. A kneeling man and upright woman forming an erotic group; its exact date and relationship to *La Porte de l’Enfer* are conventionally reconstructed rather than documented by Rodin. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1885:** *Le Fils prodigue*. An arched, supplicating male figure derived from the portal project and later enlarged. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1885–1886:** *La Martyre*. A recumbent female figure from the orbit of *La Porte de l’Enfer*, subsequently treated as an independent sculpture. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1885–1886:** *La Méditation, ou La Voix intérieure*. A female figure lacking part of its arms, later incorporated into the Victor Hugo monument; the fragmentary body is intentional rather than merely unfinished. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1886:** *Les Trois Ombres*. Three casts or repetitions of one altered male figure placed over *La Porte de l’Enfer* and also produced as an independent group. Its identity lies in repetition and assembly, not three separately modeled bodies. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/three-shades).
- **c. 1887:** *La Douleur*. A female head and upper body associated with the portal, also known through independent casts and variants. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1889:** *Le Sommeil*. A female head conceived as a sculptural study and realized in plaster, marble, and bronze manifestations. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1890–1892:** *La Muse tragique*. A bowed female figure used independently and in versions of the Victor Hugo monument. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1891:** *Iris, messagère des dieux*, later *Iris*. A winged figure designed for the Victor Hugo project, then reoriented, enlarged, and stripped of wings and one arm as an autonomous fragment. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/iris-messenger-gods).
- **c. 1895:** *L’Aurore*. A woman’s face emerging from an incompletely carved marble block; the tension between finished flesh and raw stone is part of the work’s form. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1895:** *La Convalescente*. A female head resting on a support, known through marble and bronze versions. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1895:** *Assemblage : Masque de Camille Claudel et main gauche de Pierre de Wissant*. An intentionally heterogeneous assemblage of components borrowed from two earlier works; it resists the idea of sculpture as one body modeled for one title. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **c. 1895–1900:** *Assemblage : Nu féminin dans une coupe antique*. A small modeled body installed in an antique vessel from Rodin’s collection. Neither sculpture nor found object alone constitutes the obra. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **c. 1895–1900:** *Assemblage : torse féminin à tête de femme au chignon et serpent lové*. A recombination of separately generated sculptural fragments; its status is explicitly that of an assemblage rather than a unitary modeled figure. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **c. 1896:** *La Terre et la Lune*. A marble group of two female bodies, derived from earlier figures and joined within an incompletely worked block. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1896:** *L’Illusion, sœur d’Icare*. A falling female figure made independently and incorporated into later compositions. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1896–1900:** *L’Homme et sa pensée*. A female head emerging from a block beneath a male figure, generally interpreted as a material allegory of artistic creation. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1897–1898:** *La Main de Dieu*. A colossal hand cradling an entwined male and female pair within unworked stone; it is simultaneously sculpture and an image of sculpture being created. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/hand-god).
- **c. 1898:** *La Main du diable*. A hand holding a female figure, conceived as a counterpart in theme rather than a literal companion commission to *La Main de Dieu*. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1899–1902:** *Le Génie du repos éternel*. A winged male figure devised as a funerary sculpture and known in plaster, stone, and bronze manifestations. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1900:** *Avant la création*. An assemblage of hands and a female figure that stages artistic emergence; its composite construction makes it an obra about making rather than a representation of one stable body. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/creation).
- **c. 1900:** *La Tentation de saint Antoine*. A marble assemblage of a reclining woman and an inscribed rock, executed from Rodin’s model with studio assistance. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1900:** *Les Illusions reçues par la terre*. A marble group combining figures devised elsewhere in Rodin’s sculptural vocabulary. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1900–1905:** *La Cathédrale*. Two right hands, each derived from a different model, joined to form an architectural void; the later title turns an anatomical assemblage into an architectural metaphor. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/cathedral).
- **c. 1905:** *L’Athlète américain*. A portrait figure modeled from the bodybuilder Samuel Stockton White III; versions differ in scale and completeness. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1907:** *La Prière*. A fragmentary female torso whose amputated appearance was deliberately retained and exhibited as a complete artistic statement. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1907:** *Torse d’Adèle*. A female torso presented without head or limbs; it exemplifies Rodin’s conversion of the study or fragment into an autonomous obra. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1908:** *La Grande Ombre*. An enlarged autonomous version of the figure used three times in *Les Trois Ombres*. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1910:** *La Sculpture grecque*. A small modeled demonstration of the formal principles Rodin attributed to ancient Greek sculpture, made during his conversations on art. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **c. 1910:** *La Sculpture de Michel-Ange*. A companion modeled demonstration contrasting Michelangelo’s sculptural principles with those of Greek art. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).

### Monumental projects and public commissions

- **1880–1917:** *La Porte de l’Enfer*. A monumental portal commissioned for a projected museum of decorative arts and continually revised until Rodin’s death. It is an unfinished project and a generative body of work—source of numerous independent figures—not a single conclusively finished object; the bronze casts were made posthumously. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/gates-hell).
- **1884–1895:** *Monument aux Bourgeois de Calais*. A civic monument comprising six individually modeled burghers—Eustache de Saint Pierre, Jean d’Aire, Pierre de Wissant, Jacques de Wissant, Andrieu d’Andres, and Jean de Fiennes—conceived without the conventional pyramidal hierarchy. Later casts are authorized manifestations of the group, not newly modeled works by Rodin. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/burghers-calais).
- **1885–1889:** *Monument à Jules Bastien-Lepage*. A public memorial at Damvillers combining a portrait bust with a standing allegorical figure; its final material execution involved ordinary studio and foundry collaboration. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1889–1892:** *Monument à Claude Lorrain*. A public monument for Nancy combining Claude’s figure with allegorical and animal components; the commission underwent substantial revision. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1889–1897; installed 1909:** *Monument à Victor Hugo*. A state commission that generated several incompatible arrangements, including seated, standing, nude, clothed, and muse-accompanied Hugos. The project resists reduction to the version eventually installed at the Palais-Royal. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/monument-victor-hugo).
- **1891–1898:** *Monument à Balzac*. A commissioned monument developed through portrait heads, nude studies, clothed studies, and dressing-gown forms; the commissioners rejected the exhibited plaster in 1898. Rodin regarded the rejected project as complete, while the familiar bronzes are posthumous manifestations. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/monument-balzac).
- **1894–1900:** *Monument au président Domingo Faustino Sarmiento*. A public monument for Buenos Aires combining Sarmiento’s figure with an allegorical base; its unconventional portrait was contested after installation. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1899–1917:** *Monument à Puvis de Chavannes*. An uncompleted memorial project involving a portrait figure, allegory, and multiple compositional studies. Its unfinished project-state is itself the obra; no final public monument was realized. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1905–1917:** *Monument à James McNeill Whistler*. An unfinished commemorative project centered on a climbing female allegory sometimes called *Muse nue* or *La Renommée*. Whistler’s portrait never became part of a completed monument. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1908–1912:** *Monument au général Lynch*. A commission for Santiago de Chile that remained unrealized; surviving studies document an intention rather than a completed public object. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).

### Portrait sculptures

- **1879:** *Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse*. A portrait bust of Rodin’s former employer, modeled after their professional association had ended. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1883:** *Victor Hugo*. A portrait bust modeled from sittings with the writer; Rodin subsequently developed several variants and incorporated the likeness into monument projects. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1883:** *Jean-Paul Laurens*. A portrait bust of the painter, known in plaster, marble, and bronze manifestations. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1883:** *Antonin Proust*. A portrait bust of the politician and arts administrator. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1884:** *Camille Claudel au bonnet*. A portrait of Claudel; titles and dates among Rodin’s several heads of her are not always consistently assigned across collections. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1884:** *Camille Claudel aux cheveux courts*. A portrait head surviving through variants and casts; it should not be confused with Claudel’s own sculpture. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1885:** *Dalou*. A portrait bust of the sculptor Aimé-Jules Dalou. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1886:** *Henri Rochefort*. A portrait bust of the journalist and politician, modeled with emphatically irregular surfaces. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1886:** *Madame Vicuna*. A society portrait bust known in marble and bronze versions. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1888:** *Madame Russell*. A portrait bust of Marianna Mattiocco Russell, made in several versions. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1891:** *Madame Fenaille*. A portrait bust of Mary Fenaille, produced in plaster, marble, and bronze. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1892:** *Puvis de Chavannes*. A portrait bust later serving as a point of departure for the unfinished monument. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1897:** *Cécile Renault*. A portrait bust commissioned by Baron Thyssen; multiple material versions survive. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1898:** *Henry Becque*. A portrait bust of the playwright, modeled after Becque’s death from photographs and other evidence rather than from life. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/musee/collections/oeuvres).
- **1899:** *Gustav Mahler*. A portrait bust modeled during sittings in Paris and transmitted through plasters, bronzes, and marble. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1902:** *George Bernard Shaw*. A portrait bust modeled during sittings; Shaw also documented Rodin and his studio photographically. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1905–1906:** *Eve Fairfax*. A portrait bust of the English sitter, made in several versions and materials. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1906:** *Bernard Shaw*. A second portrait conception, including a clothed bust, distinct from the earlier nude head-and-shoulders treatment. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1906–1907:** *Lady Sackville*. A portrait bust of Victoria Sackville-West, made in variant states. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1907:** *Clemenceau*. A portrait bust of Georges Clemenceau; the sitter disliked Rodin’s interpretation, and several states survive. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1908:** *La Duchesse de Choiseul*. A portrait bust of Claire Coudert, Duchess of Choiseul, Rodin’s American patron. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1908–1909:** *Comtesse de Noailles*. A portrait head and bust of the poet Anna de Noailles; disputes between sitter and sculptor affected which version was accepted. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1911:** *Pope Benedict XV*. A portrait bust modeled in Rome; the sittings ended before Rodin considered the work fully resolved, making completeness debatable. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1912:** *Hanako*. One of numerous masks and heads modeled from the Japanese performer Ōta Hisa, known as Hanako; the individual titles largely distinguish states within a sustained portrait project. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).

### Decorative, architectural, and collaborative production

- **1864–1870:** *Decorative models for Albert-Ernest Carrier-Belleuse*. Rodin produced figures, ornaments, and architectural decoration as an employee or collaborator. Many objects cannot securely be separated from Carrier-Belleuse’s workshop production, so treating each as a solely authored Rodin obra would overstate the evidence. [Musée Rodin biography](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/rodin/biography/auguste-rodin).
- **1871–1877:** *Architectural sculpture for the Brussels Stock Exchange and other Belgian buildings*. Rodin worked under Carrier-Belleuse and the sculptor Antoine-Joseph Van Rasbourgh; documentary and stylistic attributions of individual components remain disputed. [Musée Rodin biography](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/rodin/biography/auguste-rodin).
- **1879–1882:** *Models for the Manufacture nationale de Sèvres*. Decorative masks, figures, and vase designs made while Rodin worked for the porcelain manufactory. These are collaborative design-and-production objects rather than sculptures wholly executed by one hand. [Musée Rodin biography](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/rodin/biography/auguste-rodin).
- **1900:** *Pavillon de l’Alma installation*. A purpose-built exhibition environment in which Rodin arranged sculpture, drawings, and photographs around a stripped, figureless state of *La Porte de l’Enfer*. As a temporary Gesamtkunstwerk of architecture, objects, and display, it cannot be reduced to its individual exhibits. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).

### Drawings, watercolours, cut-outs, and albums

- **1855–1865:** *Formation drawings*. A corpus of academic, anatomical, ornamental, and architectural sheets rather than one titled work. Rodin rarely dated drawings, so the museum’s periodization is reconstructed. [Musée Rodin, “Dessins”](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/recherche/fonds-patrimoniaux-et-documentaires-du-musee/collections-oeuvres-du-musee/dessins).
- **c. 1870–1880:** *Early and Belgian-period drawings*. A heterogeneous corpus of figures, ornament, landscapes, and architectural studies. Individual sheets often have only descriptive catalogue titles and approximate dates. [Musée Rodin, “Dessins”](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/recherche/fonds-patrimoniaux-et-documentaires-du-musee/collections-oeuvres-du-musee/dessins).
- **c. 1880–1890:** *Dessins noirs*. A body of dark Dantean and visionary drawings associated with *La Porte de l’Enfer*. It is a scholarly corpus, not a single album planned by Rodin. [Musée Rodin, “Dessins”](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/recherche/fonds-patrimoniaux-et-documentaires-du-musee/collections-oeuvres-du-musee/dessins).
- **1890s–1917:** *Movement drawings from the living model*. Thousands of rapidly made figure drawings, often subsequently traced, simplified, coloured, cut out, or pasted onto other sheets. Their serial and experimental character resists classification as separately titled finished works. [Musée Rodin, “Dessins”](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/recherche/fonds-patrimoniaux-et-documentaires-du-musee/collections-oeuvres-du-musee/dessins).
- **1896–1897:** *Les Dessins d’Auguste Rodin*. An album of reproductions with a preface by Octave Mirbeau; Rodin supplied the graphic obra, while the publication as text-object is collaborative. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **c. 1900–1910:** *Erotic drawings*. A corpus of several hundred direct studies of female sexuality, generally untitled by Rodin and catalogued under later descriptive labels. It is a body of work rather than a fixed album. [Musée Rodin, “Before the Creation”](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/collections/oeuvres/creation).
- **1906:** *Cambodian-dancer drawings*. A series of pencil and watercolour studies made in response to the Royal Ballet of Cambodia’s visit to France. The series is reconstructed from individual sheets rather than constituted by Rodin as a closed album. [Musée Rodin](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/rodin/rodin-and-dance).
- **c. 1906–1917:** *Cut-outs and pasted figures*. Drawn bodies cut from their original sheets and repositioned as independent silhouettes or collages. These objects deliberately unsettle the distinction between drawing, fragment, assemblage, and mobile sculptural figure. [Musée Rodin, “Drawings Unbound”](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/musee/expositions/rodin-drawings-unbound).
- **c. 1890–1914:** *Cathedral and architectural drawings*. Approximately two thousand sketches made during Rodin’s visits to French churches and related monuments; only a selection entered *Les Cathédrales de France*. Dates and depicted sites are often reconstructed. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).

### Prints and graphic manifestations

- **1881 onward:** *Drypoints and etchings*. A relatively small graphic corpus initiated after Rodin learned printmaking from Alphonse Legros. States, impressions, and plates are manifestations of repeatable images rather than unique works. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1884:** *Victor Hugo, de trois quarts*. A drypoint portrait derived from Rodin’s studies of Hugo and printed in multiple states. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1884:** *Henri Becque*. A drypoint portrait, distinct as a print from Rodin’s later sculptural bust of Becque. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **c. 1885:** *Antonin Proust*. A drypoint portrait translated from Rodin’s portrait practice into a reproducible graphic medium. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1898:** *Le Printemps*. A lithographic image after one of Rodin’s drawings, produced through printer-mediated transfer; authorship belongs to Rodin’s design but not necessarily every material step. [Musée Rodin catalogue](https://collections.musee-rodin.fr/).
- **1902:** *Le Jardin des supplices illustrations*. Twenty lithographs after Rodin’s drawings for Octave Mirbeau’s book. The images are Rodin’s contribution, while the book itself is a collaborative publication rather than his sole obra. [Bibliothèque nationale de France](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb31234265j).
- **1907:** *Rodin in Rime illustrations*. Seven lithographs printed by Auguste Clot after Rodin’s watercolours for Aleister Crowley’s volume. These are mediated graphic manifestations, not autograph watercolours in the book. [Musée Rodin bibliography](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/).

### Writings, interviews, and textual works

- **1904:** *La Leçon de l’antique*. An article or recorded discourse signed by Rodin, first published in *Le Musée* and later expanded. Its exact textual authorship is mediated by editorial transcription, as were many published “propos” attributed to him. [INHA bibliography](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1904:** *Une statue de femme au musée de Naples*. An article on ancient sculpture published under Rodin’s name in *Le Musée*. [INHA bibliography](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1904:** *La Tête Warren*. An article on an ancient marble head, published in *Le Musée*. [INHA bibliography](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1905:** *Le Parthénon et les Cathédrales*. A comparative essay published in *Le Musée*. [INHA bibliography](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1909:** *Un sacrilège national. Nous laissons mourir nos cathédrales*. A polemical newspaper article opposing destructive restoration and neglect; it functions partly as a manifesto for preservation. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1910:** *Notre-Dame-du-Fort à Étampes*. A brief newspaper text on the church and its architecture, later related to the cathedral writings. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1910:** *Vénus. À la Vénus de Milo*. A lyrical critical essay addressed to the ancient statue and published in *L’Art et les Artistes*. [INHA bibliography](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1911:** *L’Art: entretiens réunis par Paul Gsell*. A book assembled by Gsell from conversations with Rodin. It records Rodin’s thought but is a collaborative, editorially shaped manifestation rather than an autograph treatise written solely by him. [Musée Rodin critical edition](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/research/musee-rodin-research-center/publications/series-rodin-textes-et-recherches).
- **1911:** *Testament*. A short declarative address to young artists appended to *L’Art*; it functions as a manifesto, though its transmission is bound to Gsell’s edited volume. [Musée Rodin critical edition](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/en/research/musee-rodin-research-center/publications/series-rodin-textes-et-recherches).
- **1912:** *La Rénovation de la danse: Loïe Fuller, Isadora Duncan, Nijinski*. A newspaper text about modern dance, published under Rodin’s name. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1914:** *Les Cathédrales de France*. A book made from Rodin’s notes, manuscripts, and architectural drawings, put into publishable form with substantial assistance from Charles Morice and Louis Gillet and illustrated with facsimiles printed by Auguste Clot. It is a composite textual-and-graphic Gesamtkunstwerk whose sole authorship by Rodin must be qualified. [INHA](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **written before 1917; published 1921:** *La Danse de Çiva*. An essay on Indian sculpture published posthumously in *Sculptures çivaïtes de l’Inde*. The date of composition is not securely fixed by the publication date. [INHA bibliography](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/).
- **1860–1917:** *Correspondance*. Thousands of letters and notes constitute a documentary and epistolary body of work, not a single authored book; survival is incomplete, and many correspondents’ incoming letters belong to them rather than to Rodin. A four-volume scholarly edition covers Rodin’s correspondence. [Musée Rodin archival description](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/recherche/fonds-patrimoniaux-et-documentaires-du-musee/fonds-historiques-et-archives-institutionnelles/fonds-historique/correspondants-de-rodin).

### Institutional and curatorial project

- **1909–1917:** *Musée Rodin project and donations to the French state*. Rodin pursued the conversion of the Hôtel Biron into a museum and in 1916 donated his works, collections, property, and archives to France. This is a legal, curatorial, and institutional project rather than a conventional artwork, but it materially shaped the posthumous existence and authorized multiplication of his œuvre. [INHA chronology](https://www.inha.fr/dictionnaire-critique-des-historiens-de-lart-actifs-en-france-de-la-revolution-a-la-premiere-guerre-mondiale/rodin-auguste-inha/); [Musée Rodin collections history](https://www.musee-rodin.fr/node/577).
