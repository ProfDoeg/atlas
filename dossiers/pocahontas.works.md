## Works by Pocahontas

### Corpus status

- No surviving written, visual, musical, or material obra can be securely attributed to Pocahontas. The standard scholarly biographies reconstruct her life from English colonial records rather than from works preserved under her name; presumed everyday Powhatan women’s work—such as agriculture, cooking, mat-making, basketry, clothing, and pottery—cannot be assigned to her as individual objects ([Townsend](https://www.si.edu/object/pocahontas-and-powhatan-dilemma-camilla-townsend%3Asiris_sil_762942); [Rountree](https://www.upress.virginia.edu/title/2284/)).

### Oral and pedagogical acts

- **c. 1608–1609:** Untitled language lessons or exchanges with John Smith. This was an oral, collaborative teaching practice rather than a fixed text: Pocahontas and Smith probably taught one another words and phrases, but Smith’s subsequently published Virginia Algonquian vocabulary does not identify individual contributions, so none of its entries can securely be called her composition ([Townsend interview](https://time.com/5039077/pocahontas-history/); [Smith’s vocabulary](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/the-maner-of-their-language-an-excerpt-from-map-of-virginia-with-a-description-of-the-countrey-the-commodities-people-government-and-religion-by-john-smith-1612/)).

### Manifesto-like speech acts

- **1614:** Untitled public profession of Christian faith. Colonial accounts describe Pocahontas as publicly declaring Christianity before her baptism as Rebecca, but preserve none of her words; the only “text” is therefore a reported speech act, transmitted by colonial promoters while she was a captive, and their assertion that it was voluntary cannot be independently verified ([Hamor’s *True Discourse*](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/2293hpr-aa0ba4c2b68477f/); [Pocahontas biography](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/pocahontas-d-1617/)).

### Political creation that resists classification as a work

- **1614:** The peace associated with her marriage to John Rolfe. The marriage helped end the First Anglo-Powhatan War and thus produced a political condition, but the peace was collective and negotiated among Powhatan and English authorities; scholarship specifically cautions that Pocahontas probably did not negotiate it herself. It is consequently a historical effect in which she participated, not a securely attributable personal obra ([First Anglo-Powhatan War](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/first-anglo-powhatan-war-1609-1614/); [Pocahontas biography](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/entries/pocahontas-d-1617/)).

### Manifestation made with, and of, her

- **1616:** *Matoaka als Rebecka*. Pocahontas sat for the image from which Simon van de Passe made the only surviving lifetime portrait; the engraving and its propagandistic costume, inscription, and publication belong to Van de Passe and publisher Compton Holland, not to Pocahontas. Her sitting may be understood as an embodied manifestation or constrained collaborative performance, but it resists inclusion as a work “by” her because the record does not establish her control over its presentation ([National Portrait Gallery, London](https://www.npg.org.uk/collections/search/portrait/mw39924/Pocahontas); [National Portrait Gallery, Smithsonian](https://npg.si.edu/blog/collection-pocahontas)).

### Exclusions and disputed attributions

- **1616:** John Smith’s untitled letter to Queen Anne is not a work by Pocahontas. Smith claimed in 1624 that he had sent such a letter on her behalf, but no contemporary copy is known and its surviving text is Smith’s later publication ([Encyclopedia Virginia](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/john-smiths-letter-to-queen-anne-an-excerpt-fromthe-generall-historie-of-virginia-new-england-and-the-summer-isles-by-john-smith-1624/)).

- **1607–1616:** Reported rescues, warnings, food deliveries, interpretation, and diplomatic appearances are consequential acts rather than stable works. Most survive only in John Smith’s retrospective, self-interested narration, which itself acknowledges uncertainty over whether Pocahontas acted independently, under Powhatan’s direction, or as part of a political protocol; they therefore cannot responsibly be catalogued as separately authored obras ([Smith’s account](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/john-smiths-letter-to-queen-anne-an-excerpt-fromthe-generall-historie-of-virginia-new-england-and-the-summer-isles-by-john-smith-1624/); [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/jame/learn/historyculture/pocahontas-her-life-and-legend.htm)).
