## Works by René Descartes

### Published obras

- **1616:** *Theses ex utroque iure de testamentis ordinandis* (the “1616 Law Thesis”). A printed broadsheet announcing Descartes’s oral defense in canon and civil law, with a dedication and forty propositions; its surviving copy, authenticated after discovery in 1981, lacks a corner containing parts of the final propositions. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://seop.illc.uva.nl/entries/descartes-works/history.html))

- **1637:** *Discours de la méthode pour bien conduire sa raison, et chercher la vérité dans les sciences*. A methodological and autobiographical discourse published anonymously as the preface to three scientific “essays”; it is one component of a deliberately composite obra, not originally a freestanding book. ([Corpus Descartes](https://www.unicaen.fr/puc/sources/prodescartes/presentation.html); [Adam–Tannery VI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery))

- **1637:** *La Dioptrique*. An essay or demonstrative treatise on vision, refraction, telescopes, and the cutting of lenses, published as the first material trial of the method. Its protocols include designs for instruments and a lens-grinding machine, but the text does not establish that Descartes personally fabricated a successful machine. ([Adam–Tannery VI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery); [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/ss/a/KHs8zwnJbBXzDnzVtQWvFhq/?lang=pt))

- **1637:** *Les Météores*. A natural-philosophical essay on terrestrial and atmospheric phenomena, including clouds, precipitation, and the rainbow; it was published as the second trial of the method. ([Corpus Descartes](https://www.unicaen.fr/puc/sources/prodescartes/presentation.html); [CCFr](https://ccfr.bnf.fr/portailccfr/ark%3A/16871/0018098479))

- **1637:** *La Géométrie*. A mathematical essay joining algebraic notation to geometrical construction; its curve-tracing devices and “new compasses” are designs and operations embedded in the text, not securely surviving artifacts made by Descartes’s hands. ([Adam–Tannery VI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-mathematics/))

- **1641; enlarged 1642:** *Meditationes de prima philosophia*. A sequence of six metaphysical meditations published with objections supplied by other readers and Descartes’s replies; the seven-part objections-and-replies apparatus in the 1642 edition is therefore a collaborative manifestation, not wholly his text. ([Corpus Descartes](https://www.unicaen.fr/puc/sources/prodescartes/presentation.html); [Adam–Tannery VII](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery))

- **1642:** *Epistola ad Patrem Dinet*. A public justificatory letter appended to the second Latin edition of the *Meditationes*, defending Descartes and his philosophy amid the Utrecht controversy. ([Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

- **1643:** *Epistola Renati Des-Cartes ad celeberrimum virum D. Gisbertum Voetium*. A published open letter and polemical intervention against Gisbertus Voetius: closer to a manifesto or forensic act than to a neutral philosophical treatise. ([Corpus Descartes](https://www.unicaen.fr/puc/sources/prodescartes/presentation.html); [Adam–Tannery VIII-2](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_VIII.djvu))

- **1643:** *Brief van René Descartes aan den vermaarden D. Gisbertus Voetius*. The authorized Dutch manifestation of the *Epistola ad Voetium*; because it is a translation produced for the Utrecht dispute, it is not simply a second independent obra by Descartes. ([Descartes correspondence bibliography](https://philotextes.info/spip/IMG/pdf/1643.pdf))

- **1643:** *Antwoord van René Descartes*. A Dutch-language public answer connected with the Utrecht controversy; its status is polemical and occasional rather than that of a systematic treatise. ([Descartes correspondence bibliography](https://philotextes.info/spip/IMG/pdf/1643.pdf))

- **1644:** *Principia philosophiae*. A four-part Latin textbook-system of metaphysics, physics, cosmology, and terrestrial phenomena, designed to replace scholastic manuals. ([Corpus Descartes](https://www.unicaen.fr/puc/sources/prodescartes/presentation.html); [Adam–Tannery VIII-1](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery))

- **1645:** *Lettre apologétique aux magistrats d’Utrecht*. A petitionary defense addressed to Utrecht’s civic authorities; Adam–Tannery prints it among the works, but its operative legal-political purpose makes it resist classification as an autonomous philosophical work. ([Adam–Tannery VIII-2](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_VIII.djvu))

- **1647:** *Les Méditations métaphysiques*. The authorized French manifestation of the *Meditationes*, translated chiefly by the duc de Luynes and accompanied by translated objections and replies, revisions, and new prefatory material; authorship of the whole French object is therefore distributed. ([Adam–Tannery IX-1](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery); [Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

- **1647:** *Avertissement de l’auteur touchant les cinquièmes objections*. A brief authorial intervention in the French *Méditations*, modifying how Gassendi’s objections were presented. ([Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

- **1646; published 1647:** *Lettre de Monsieur Descartes à Monsieur C. L. R.*. A polemical reply to further objections by Gassendi, incorporated into the French *Méditations*. ([Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

- **1647:** *Les Principes de la philosophie*. An authorized French translation by Claude Picot of the *Principia*, carrying substantive additions and alterations associated with Descartes; it is a revised collaborative manifestation rather than merely a reprint. ([Adam–Tannery IX-2](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/%C5%92uvres_de_Descartes/%C3%89dition_Adam_et_Tannery); [Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

- **1647:** *Lettre de l’auteur à celui qui a traduit le livre, laquelle peut ici servir de préface*. Descartes’s letter-preface to the French *Principes*, presenting philosophy through the image of a tree and prescribing an order of study; it functions partly as a pedagogical protocol. ([Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

- **1648:** *Notae in programma quoddam*. A published answer to Henricus Regius’s broadsheet on the human mind, combining doctrinal correction with public polemic; some bibliographies date it from the broadsheet of late 1647, but the work itself appeared in 1648. ([Corpus Descartes](https://www.unicaen.fr/puc/sources/prodescartes/presentation.html); [Adam–Tannery VIII-2](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_VIII.djvu))

- **1649:** *Les Passions de l’âme*. A published treatise in 212 articles on embodiment, emotion, conduct, and the passions; its prefatory exchange of four letters makes the printed object partly dialogical. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu); [Bulletin cartésien account](https://studylibfr.com/doc/3110607/bulletin-cart%C3%A9sien-xl-publi%C3%A9-par-le-centre-d-%C3%A9tudes))

### Manuscript obras and unfinished projects

- **1618:** *Compendium musicae*. A manuscript compendium of musical theory made for Isaac Beeckman, treating sound, consonance, rhythm, and modes; it circulated privately and was first printed posthumously in 1650. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **1618–1619:** *Physico-mathematica*. A small group of demonstrations on hydrostatics and accelerated fall preserved through Beeckman’s journal; because Beeckman recorded the material, the surviving text resists identification with an autonomous Descartes manuscript. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **1618–1619:** *Varia* in Beeckman’s journal. Notes and problems on music, mechanics, falling bodies, mathematics, and Lullian art attributed to Descartes in Beeckman’s record; “Varia” is an editorial grouping, not a title bestowed by Descartes. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1619–1621:** *Olympica*. A lost notebook or sequence of reflections known principally from Adrien Baillet’s excerpts, including Descartes’s dream narrative; its boundaries, wording, and status as one work are irrecoverable. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1619–1621:** *Experimenta*. A lost youthful notebook known from Baillet’s extracts; the surviving evidence is too fragmentary to decide whether it was a finished work rather than a working dossier. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1619–1621:** *Studium bonae mentis*. A lost, unfinished youthful project on cultivating the mind, reconstructed from Baillet’s account; the conventional Latin title names a project more securely than a recoverable text. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1619–1621:** *Cogitationes privatae*. Private mathematical, physical, and methodological notes preserved only in Leibniz’s selections from a now-lost manuscript; the modern title and corpus are mediated editorially and resist treatment as a finished obra. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-mathematics/))

- **c. 1620:** *De solidorum elementis*. A mathematical manuscript on polyhedra and their elements, surviving through Leibniz’s copy; its exact date and intended completeness are uncertain. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1619–1628:** *Excerpta mathematica*. Eleven mathematical pieces concerning polygons, polygonal numbers, aliquot parts, cube roots, quadrature, cycloids, equations, and optical ovals, first printed from manuscript excerpts in 1701; whether Descartes conceived them as a single work is doubtful. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1628–1629:** Mathematical and optical demonstrations entered in Beeckman’s journal. These include algebraic examples, refraction investigations, musical-string ratios, burning mirrors, conic lenses, mean proportionals, equation-solving devices, and a proposal for signs readable on the Moon; they survive as Beeckman’s records and therefore resist clean attribution as individually authored texts. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1620s:** *Thaumantis regia*. A projected “palace of marvels” or mathematical-mechanical entertainment mentioned by early biographical evidence; no securely identifiable text survives, so this is an intention rather than an extant work.

- **c. 1619–1620s:** *Art de l’escrime*. A lost or abandoned protocol on fencing, represented only by fragments and reports printed by Adam–Tannery; its original extent and title are uncertain. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **c. 1627–1628:** *Regulae ad directionem ingenii*. An unfinished set of rules for directing inquiry: twenty-one rules survive, but exposition breaks off during Rule XXI, and both its stages of composition and precise dating remain disputed. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

- **1629–1633:** *Le Monde*, or *Traité de la lumière*. An unfinished and withheld natural-philosophical project explaining a hypothetical world through matter and motion; Descartes abandoned publication after learning of Galileo’s condemnation, and the surviving text was printed posthumously. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **c. 1632–1633:** *L’Homme*. A physiological and mechanical account of an artificial human body intended as part of *Le Monde*; its separation as an independent work is largely a consequence of its posthumous publication history. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **1630s–1640s:** *Anatomica*. A body of anatomical observations, dissections, diagrams, and working notes preserved incompletely through copies and excerpts; “Anatomica” is an editorial corpus rather than a finished treatise with stable boundaries. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **1630s–1640s:** *Generatio animalium*. Notes toward an account of animal generation and embryology, left unfinished and published posthumously; their relation to the broader anatomical notebooks is not wholly fixed. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Undated, probably 1630s–1640s:** *De saporibus*. A short physiological note on tastes printed with the material on animal generation; it resists elevation into a separate finished work. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Uncertain, probably 1630s–1640s:** *Problemata*. A collection of scientific problems among the posthumous *Varia*; its title and unity are editorial, and its precise date is unknown. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Uncertain:** *De magnete*. A fragment on magnetism included among the posthumous *Varia*; no fixed date or evidence of completion survives. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Uncertain:** *Remedia et vires medicamentorum*. Medical and pharmacological notes included among the *Varia*; they are working material, not demonstrably a work Descartes prepared for circulation. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Uncertain:** *De refractione*. An optical fragment preserved in manuscript copies; its date, completeness, and precise relation to *La Dioptrique* remain uncertain. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Uncertain:** *Annotationes in Principia*. Annotations associated with the *Principia philosophiae*, surviving among posthumous papers; they are marginal working matter rather than a securely independent work. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **c. 1641–1649, date disputed:** *La Recherche de la vérité par la lumière naturelle*. An unfinished philosophical dialogue originally written in French; the French manuscript was lost, while a Latin translation and a partial French copy preserve different portions, making the recoverable text a composite manifestation. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu); [history of the text](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Search_for_Truth_by_Natural_Light))

- **1647–1648:** *La Description du corps humain*. An unfinished physiological treatise on bodily functions and generation, conceived as a supplement or correction to earlier work on the human machine and printed posthumously. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **Undated:** *Calcul de Monsieur Descartes* (“Introduction à la Géométrie”). An incomplete mathematical manuscript or pedagogical calculation preserved without a secure authorial title; Adam–Tannery’s label should not be mistaken for a title supplied by Descartes. ([Adam–Tannery X](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_X.djvu))

### Designs, protocols, and institutional projects

- **1619–1637:** The “new compasses,” including the mesolabe or mean-proportionals compass. These are geometrical instrument designs for generating curves and solving equations, developed in private notes and *La Géométrie*; surviving evidence securely establishes invention and description, not an extant instrument fabricated by Descartes. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/descartes-mathematics/))

- **1630s; published 1637:** Hyperbolic-lens cutting and polishing apparatus. This is a machine design and construction protocol in *La Dioptrique*, intended to mechanize the production of optical surfaces; attempts by artisans belong to the project’s history, while a successful physical Descartes-made machine is not established. ([SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/ss/a/KHs8zwnJbBXzDnzVtQWvFhq/?lang=pt))

- **1640s:** Project for an *École des Arts et Métiers*. An institutional and educational scheme rather than a book; it survives only through documentary traces collected by Adam–Tannery and was not realized. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **1640s:** Project for a comedy. Adam–Tannery records the intention among “Projets divers,” but no completed dramatic text is securely extant; the project therefore resists being called a work at all. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

- **1649–1650:** Project for an academy at Stockholm. An unrealized institutional design associated with Queen Christina’s court, surviving as proposals rather than as a completed institution or stable treatise. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))

### Correspondence as a distributed body of work

- **1619–1650:** *Correspondance*. Hundreds of letters to and from Beeckman, Mersenne, Princess Elisabeth, Huygens, Chanut, Queen Christina, and many others form a distributed intellectual practice encompassing proofs, medical counsel, experimental protocols, polemics, and drafts; Adam–Tannery’s five-volume corpus also includes incoming letters and later copies, so it is neither a single authored book nor wholly Descartes’s creation. ([Adam–Tannery I–V](https://www.classiques.uqam.ca/classiques/Descartes/correspondance/correspondance.html); [CNRS Éditions](https://www.cnrseditions.fr/serie/oeuvres-completes-de-descartes/))

### Disputed or rejected attributions

- **1649:** *La Naissance de la Paix*. A Stockholm court-ballet libretto long attributed to Descartes and therefore sometimes counted among his last works; modern discussion rejects or substantially doubts his authorship, so it should not be silently admitted into the œuvre. ([Washington University report](https://source.washu.edu/2008/04/campus-author-richard-a-watson/); [Indiana University Press discussion](https://publish.iupress.indiana.edu/read/through-the-eyes-of-descartes/section/fff76010-cc66-419c-b290-59962eeb5d5f))

- **Undated:** *Cartesius*. A short item printed under that heading among Adam–Tannery’s *Varia*; the heading, authorship, and status as a distinct work are too indeterminate for confident classification. ([Adam–Tannery XI](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Livre:Descartes_-_%C5%92uvres,_%C3%A9d._Adam_et_Tannery,_XI.djvu))
