## Works by Paul Jules Barbier

### Spoken dramas, comedies, vaudevilles, and theatrical interludes

- **1847:** *L’Ombre de Molière*. Verse interlude first performed at the Théâtre-Français; Barbier’s earliest documented theatrical work. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1847:** *Un poète*. Five-act verse drama. Contemporary criticism disputed whether its loose construction could properly be called drama at all—an unusually explicit resistance to classification as a theatrical work. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1848:** *Amour et bergerie*. One-act verse comedy. Some later bibliographies give the title as *Amour et Bergère* and date it 1849; the documented première was at the Odéon on 16 January 1848. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1848:** *Les Premières Coquetteries*. One-act comédie-vaudeville. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1848:** *Oscar XXVIII*. Two-act comédie-vaudeville made with Eugène Labiche and Adrien Decourcelle. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1848:** *Les Femmes fortes*. Stage work attributed to Barbier in the performance record; the readily available repertory sources do not establish its form or collaborators, so it resists finer classification here. ([Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1849:** *Bon gré mal gré*. One-act prose comedy. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1849:** *André Chénier, ou 90, 92, 94*. Verse drama in three historical “epochs.” It is also catalogued more briefly as *André Chénier*. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1849:** *Un drame de famille*. Five-act drama made with Michel Carré. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1849:** *Le Feu de paille*. One-act comédie-vaudeville made with Chapelle. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1849:** *Graziella*. One-act drama made with Michel Carré from Lamartine’s *Confidences*; it is distinct from the later operatic libretto of the same title. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1850:** *Laurence*. Two-act drama made with Théodore Barrière and Michel Carré. Some bibliographies mention only Barrière beside Barbier. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1850:** *Henriette Deschamps*. Three-act drama made with Michel Carré and A. Dumesnil. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1850:** *L’Amour mouillé*. One-act comédie-vaudeville made with Michel Carré and Arthur de Beauplan; its dramatic premise was later remade as a musical work. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1850:** *Les Amoureux sans le savoir*. One-act verse comedy made with Michel Carré. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1850:** *Jenny l’ouvrière*. Five-act drama made with Adrien Decourcelle. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1851:** *Les Contes d’Hoffmann*. Five-act fantastic drama made with Michel Carré from stories by E. T. A. Hoffmann. It is a separate obra from the later Offenbach libretto, although that libretto remade it. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1851:** *Un Roi de la mode*. Three-act comedy with songs, made with Adrien Decourcelle and Théodore Barrière. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1851:** *Les Derniers Adieux*. One-act prose comedy made with Michel Carré. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1851:** *La Fileuse*. Five-act drama made with Michel Carré. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1851:** *Les Marionnettes du docteur*. Five-act drama made with Michel Carré and incorporating music by Ancessy; as a staged conjunction of spoken drama and music it is more than a freestanding “text,” though not a full Gesamtkunstwerk in the stronger sense. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1852:** *Le Mémorial de Sainte-Hélène*. Historical drama in three parts and eighteen tableaux, including prologue and epilogue, made with Michel Carré and music by Artus. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1852:** *Voyage autour d’une jolie femme*. One-act “tableau de mœurs” made with Michel Carré. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1854:** *Les Antipodes*. One-act vaudeville made with Michel Carré, with music by G. Hirt. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1855:** *Une épreuve avant la lettre*. One-act comédie-vaudeville made with Cordellier-Delanoue. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1861:** *Cora, ou l’Esclavage*. Five-act drama in seven tableaux. Later references sometimes misdate it 1866; the documented première was 21 August 1861. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1865:** *Princesse et favorite*. Five-act drama, also recorded as *Princesse ou favorite*. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1866:** *Le Maître de la maison*. Comedy made with Édouard Foussier. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1867:** *Maxwell*. Five-act drama with a prologue, made with Michel Carré according to the contemporary bibliography. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1868:** *La Loterie du mariage*. Two-act verse comedy. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1872:** *Sous le même toit*. One-act comedy. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1873:** *Jeanne d’Arc*. Five-act verse drama in seven tableaux, designed with choruses, incidental music, and dance music by Charles Gounod. Its performed manifestation is a deliberately composite theatrical object rather than merely a printed play. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier))
- **1879:** *Un homme à plaindre*. Stage work documented in performance and in Barbier’s collected verse theatre; the available repertory record does not securely specify its dramatic subtype. ([Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier); [Google Books record for volume II of *Théâtre en vers*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Jeanne_d_Arc_Un_retour_de_jeunesse_Un_ho.html?id=o1RKAQAAMAAJ))
- **1879:** *Un retour de jeunesse*. Dramatic work included with *Jeanne d’Arc* and *Un homme à plaindre* in the second volume of *Théâtre en vers*; the collection, not a separate première record, is the secure manifestation traced here. ([Google Books record for volume II of *Théâtre en vers*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Jeanne_d_Arc_Un_retour_de_jeunesse_Un_ho.html?id=o1RKAQAAMAAJ))
- **1896:** *Lucile Desmoulins*. Five-act historical drama, variously described as having six tableaux; its première venue is reported with uncertainty in secondary listings. ([Les Archives du spectacle](https://lesarchivesduspectacle.net/p/34850-Jules-Barbier); [Jules Barbier overview](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Barbier))

### Opera, opéra-comique, operetta, and dramatic-music texts

- **1847; published 1888:** *Manfred*. Text for a five-part dramatic symphony by Louis Lacombe, made with de Château-Renaud and A. Queyroy; the long interval between performance and publication makes it both musical-dramatic creation and later textual manifestation. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1852:** *Galathée*. Two-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Victor Massé. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1853:** *Les Noces de Jeannette*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Victor Massé. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1853:** *Les Papillotes de Monsieur Benoît*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Henri Reber. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1854:** *Les Sabots de la marquise*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Ernest Boulanger. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1854:** *Le Roman de la rose*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Jules Delahaye, music by Prosper Pascal. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1855:** *Miss Fauvette*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Victor Massé. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1855:** *L’Anneau d’argent*. One-act opéra-comique libretto. The repertory sources disagree on Barbier’s collaborator: the specialist French chronology names Léon Battu, while the music encyclopedia names Michel Carré. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1855:** *Deucalion et Pyrrha*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Alexandre Montfort; the encyclopedia alternatively spells the second name “Pyrrhe.” ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1855:** *Les Saisons*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Victor Massé. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1856:** *Valentine d’Aubigny*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Fromental Halévy. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1856:** *Le Chercheur d’esprit*. Opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré and Édouard Foussier, music by F. Besanzoni. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1856:** *La Parade*. Operetta libretto made with Brésil, music by Émile Jonas. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1857; revised 1878:** *Psyché*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Ambroise Thomas; a substantially revised manifestation appeared in 1878. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1858:** *Le Médecin malgré lui*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré after Molière, music by Charles Gounod. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1859; revised 1869:** *Faust*. Five-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré after Goethe, music by Charles Gounod; the 1869 recitative version is a later manifestation of the same evolving theatrical object. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1859:** *Le Pardon de Ploërmel*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré from Carré’s *Les Chercheurs de trésor*, music by Giacomo Meyerbeer; later productions renamed it *Dinorah*, so that title is a manifestation, not a separate Barbier work. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1860; revised 1876:** *Philémon et Baucis*. Libretto made with Michel Carré after La Fontaine, music by Charles Gounod; originally three acts, then remade in two. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1860:** *Gil-Blas*. Five-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Théophile Semet. The specialist sources disagree by one day on the première date, 23 or 24 March. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1860:** *La Colombe*. Two-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré after La Fontaine’s *Le Faucon*, music by Charles Gounod. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1860:** *L’Éventail*. One-act opéra-comique, music by Ernest Boulanger. The music encyclopedia credits Barbier and Carré, but the French repertory chronology calls it Carré’s libretto alone; Barbier’s authorship is therefore disputed. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1861:** *La Statue*. Three-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Ernest Reyer. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1861:** *La Nuit aux gondoles*. One-act opéra-comique libretto, music by Prosper Pascal; one source credits Carré jointly and another lists Barbier alone, so collaboration is uncertain. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1861:** *Les Amours de Silvio, ou Le Fruit défendu*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by François Schwab. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1862:** *La Reine de Saba*. Four- or five-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré after Gérard de Nerval, music by Charles Gounod; sources differ over the act count. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1862:** *La Fille d’Égypte*. Two-act opéra-comique libretto made with Philippe Gille, music by Jules Beer. Some repertory summaries omit Gille. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1862:** *Le Cabaret des amours*. Opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Prosper Pascal. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1862–1873:** *La Guzla de l’émir*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré: Bizet composed a setting in 1862 that was never performed and is probably destroyed, while Théodore Dubois’s setting appeared in 1873. The libretto is therefore a surviving obra across two musical projects, not two independently written texts. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1864:** *La Fleur de lotus*. One-act opéra-comique libretto, music by Prosper Pascal. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1865:** *Le Mariage de Don Lope*. Opéra-comique libretto, music by Édouard de Hartog. The sources variously credit Hector Salomon or no collaborator beside Barbier, so the textual authorship is not settled here. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1866:** *Les Dragées de Suzette*. One-act opéra-comique libretto made with Jules Delahaye, music by Hector Salomon. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1866:** *Mignon*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré after Goethe’s *Wilhelm Meisters Lehrjahre*, music by Ambroise Thomas. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1867:** *Le Magnifique*. One-act opéra-bouffe libretto, music by Jules Philipot; published or composed in 1867 but not staged until 1876. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1867:** *Roméo et Juliette*. Five-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré after Shakespeare, music by Charles Gounod. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1867:** *Loetice*. Opera libretto for Victor Massé; known in the repertory source only through a piano reduction, so its performed status and textual completeness remain uncertain. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1868:** *Hamlet*. Five-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré after Shakespeare, music by Ambroise Thomas. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1868; retitled 1872:** *L’Amour mouillé / L’Amour et son hôte*. One-act opéra-comique or operetta text made with Arthur de Beauplan and, according to one source, Michel Carré, music by Édouard de Hartog. The Brussels title represents a revised or retitled manifestation rather than a securely separate work. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1869:** *Don Quichotte*. Opéra-comique libretto made with Michel Carré after Cervantes, music by Ernest Boulanger. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1875:** *Don Mucarade*. One-act opéra-bouffe libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Ernest Boulanger. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1876:** *Les Amoureux de Catherine*. One-act opéra-comique libretto after Erckmann and Chatrian, music by Henri Maréchal. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1876:** *Paul et Virginie*. Three-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré after Bernardin de Saint-Pierre, music by Victor Massé. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1877:** *Le Timbre d’argent*. Four-act fantastic opera or drame lyrique libretto made with Michel Carré, music by Camille Saint-Saëns. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1877:** *Graziella*. Two-act drame lyrique libretto after Lamartine, music by Antony Choudens. The encyclopedia credits Michel Carré jointly, while the French chronology lists Barbier alone; it is distinct from their 1849 spoken drama. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1878:** *Polyeucte*. Five-act opera libretto made with Michel Carré after Corneille, music by Charles Gounod. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1878:** *La Reine Berthe*. Two-act opera libretto, music by Victorin Joncières. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1879; French manifestation 1884:** *Néron*. Four-act opera libretto, music by Anton Rubinstein; Richard Pohl’s German version was performed first, while Barbier’s French-language manifestation followed at Antwerp. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1881:** *Les Contes d’Hoffmann*. Opera libretto by Barbier, remade from the 1851 play he wrote with Michel Carré and from Hoffmann’s tales; Offenbach died before completing the score, which Ernest Guiraud prepared for performance. Its unstable act structure and multiple posthumous musical editions make the performed opera a continuing creation rather than one fixed text. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1881:** *La Taverne des Trabans*. Three-act opéra-comique libretto made with Émile Erckmann and Alexandre Chatrian, music by Henri Maréchal. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1882:** *Françoise de Rimini*. Opera libretto with prologue and epilogue, made with Michel Carré after Dante’s *Inferno*, music by Ambroise Thomas. Sources variously count four or five acts. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1885:** *Une Nuit de Cléopâtre*. Three-act drame lyrique or opera libretto after Théophile Gautier, music by Victor Massé. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1886:** *Bianca Capello*. Five-act opera libretto made with Émile Mestépès, music by Hector Salomon. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1888:** *Les Fiançailles de Pasquin*. One-act operetta libretto made with Léon de Maupertuis, music by Eva Dell’Acqua. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1893:** *Jehan de Saintré*. Two-act opéra-comique libretto made with Pierre Barbier, music by Camille Erlanger. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1898:** *Lovelace*. Four-act opera libretto made with Paul de Choudens, music by Henri Hirschmann. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1899:** *Daphnis et Chloé*. Opéra lyrique or comédie lyrique libretto made with Pierre Barbier, music by Henri Maréchal; sources disagree whether it has two or three acts. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1903, posthumous:** *Le Tasse*. Opera libretto made with Pierre Barbier, music by Eugène d’Harcourt. Sources disagree between three and four acts; its posthumous première means the performed manifestation cannot have been finalized under Jules Barbier’s supervision. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **Undated; unperformed:** *Renza*. Three-act operetta project made with Pierre Barbier, music by Charles Lecocq. Its failure to reach performance does not erase the libretto-project, but the accessible repertory gives no secure date or completeness statement. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **Undated; unperformed:** *Circé*. Grand-opera project made with Pierre Barbier, intended for music by Théodore Dubois. Its textual and musical completion are not established. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **Undated; unperformed:** *Danubia*. Musical-stage project made with Pierre Barbier and the composer Feinsinger. The source supplies neither genre nor evidence of completion, so calling it a finished work would overstate what survives. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))

### French adaptations of pre-existing operas

- **1858:** *Les Noces de Figaro*. French four-act adaptation, made with Michel Carré, of Mozart and Lorenzo Da Ponte’s *Le nozze di Figaro*. It is an adaptive manifestation of an existing opera, not an original Barbier plot. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1860:** *Fidelio*. French three-act adaptation made with Michel Carré of Beethoven’s opera. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1863:** *Peines d’amour perdues*. Four-act French reworking, made with Michel Carré, of Mozart and Da Ponte’s *Così fan tutte*; it is not merely a translation, since it relocates the pre-existing music into a differently titled dramatic frame. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1864 or 1866:** *Les Joyeuses Commères de Windsor*. French three-act adaptation of Otto Nicolai and Salomon Hermann Mosenthal’s *Die lustigen Weiber von Windsor*. Specialist sources disagree on whether the relevant première was at Strasbourg in 1864 or the Théâtre-Lyrique in 1866. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html); [Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1865:** *Lisbeth, ou La Cinquantaine*. French two-act adaptation of music by Felix Mendelssohn. The available repertory describes it as a “version française” but does not identify a prior dramatic text precisely enough to call it a simple translation. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **circa 1873:** *Biondina*. French version of Giuseppe Zaffira’s Italian words for Gounod’s cycle of twelve songs. It is a translated song-cycle manifestation rather than twelve securely independent Barbier originals. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **Undated:** *Mélodies*—including *Les Amours du poète* and *L’Amour et la vie d’une femme*. A corpus of approximately fifty French versions of Schumann songs. The repertory source does not enumerate all fifty titles, so the corpus resists honest item-by-item reconstruction without a dedicated critical song-text catalogue. ([Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))

### Ballet scenarios

- **1876:** *Sylvia, ou La Nymphe de Diane*. Three-act ballet scenario made with Jacques de Reinach and choreographer Louis Mérante, music by Léo Delibes. This is a choreographic-dramatic protocol for movement and staging, not a book or an opera libretto. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))
- **1889:** *La Tempête*. Three-act fantastic-ballet scenario made with choreographer Joseph Hansen, music by Ambroise Thomas. It is a protocol for a composite stage creation rather than an autonomous spoken text. ([Polska Biblioteka Muzyczna](https://polskabibliotekamuzyczna.pl/encyklopedia/barbier-jules/?lang=en))

### Poetry and author-organized collections

- **1871; later edition 1875:** *Le Franc-Tireur: chants de guerre*. Collection of wartime poems or songs. The conflicting dates appear to reflect first appearance and later book manifestation, not two distinct works. ([Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001); [Art lyrique français](https://artlyrique.fr/personnages/Barbier%20Jules.html))
- **1879:** *Théâtre en vers*. Two-volume authorial collection of Barbier’s verse dramas. It is both a made book and a manifestation gathering works that also existed independently; it should not be counted as another première of each constituent play. ([Catulle Mendès, bibliography](https://obtic.huma-num.fr/obvie/critique/compdoc.jsp?fromdoc=5870&fromscore=3.013753&id=mendes_mouvement-poetique-francais-02_039&refid=diderot_salon-1763_001))
- **1890:** *Fleur blessée; Tableaux; Mosaïque*. Poetry collection or tripartite poetic book; the available evidence establishes the grouped publication but not whether its three headings were previously autonomous cycles. ([Mollat bibliographic notice](https://www.mollat.com/Recherche/Auteur/0-1176872/jules-barbier))
- **1904, posthumous:** *Petites fantaisies poétiques*. Posthumous poetry collection. Because Barbier died in 1901 and the accessible record does not identify the editor or manuscript basis, it is securely a publication-manifestation but not securely an author-finalized obra. ([Mollat bibliographic notice](https://www.mollat.com/Recherche/Auteur/0-1176872/jules-barbier))

### Archival remainder—not securely separable works

- **Undated:** *Échos de la tour Clovis*. Titled item preserved among Barbier’s manuscript papers; the archival description does not determine whether it is a poem, cycle, draft, or completed work, so its resistance to work-status must be retained. ([BnF manuscript description](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb39757231c))
- **Undated:** *Chanson espagnole*. Title attached to two manuscript leaves of notated music in Barbier’s papers. Authorship of the music, completion, and intended medium are not established by the archival description. ([BnF manuscript description](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb39757231c))
- **Undated:** Unnamed poems addressed to Caroline Carvalho. Manuscript poems survive, but the archive does not supply stable individual titles or dates; they are textual remains rather than safely enumerable works. ([BnF manuscript description](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb39757231c))
- **Undated:** Fragments of libretti, poems, notes, artistic and political reflections, correspondence, a genealogical sketch, and a drawing. These are things Barbier made, but the holding describes an archival aggregate rather than a set of completed, separately titled obras; inventing titles or dates would falsify its structure. ([BnF manuscript description](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb39757231c))
