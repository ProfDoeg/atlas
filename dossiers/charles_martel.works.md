## Works by Charles Martel

### Documentary acts preserved as authentic

- **23 February 718:** [Donation of property at Bollendorf to Echternach Abbey] (MGH *D Arnulf.* 9). A dispositive charter—an *obra* of government rather than a personally handwritten composition—by which Charles transferred property inherited from his father Pippin; it survives only in later cartulary copies.

- **5 December 720:** [Judgment for Stavelot-Malmedy] (MGH *D Arnulf.* 10). A *placitum*, or performative judicial text, deciding that certain estates had been granted to Rotgisus only for life and belonged to the monastery; its dating formula is corrupt, although the editor assigns it securely to 720.

- **c. 723:** [Protection letter for Boniface] (MGH *D Arnulf.* 11). A protective mandate placing the missionary and his companions under Charles’s *mundeburdium*; the precise date is not preserved and is reconstructed from Boniface’s movements and his papal commission.

- **1 January 723:** [Endowment of Willibrord’s community at Utrecht] (MGH *D Arnulf.* 12). A grant of fiscal property within and outside the former Roman fort at Utrecht, together with Vechten, establishing the material basis of Willibrord’s monastery and the later bishopric.

- **9 June 726:** [Donation of Eberhard’s confiscated property at Elst and Merm to the Utrecht church] (MGH *D Arnulf.* 13). A dispositive charter transferring forfeited estates to the church dedicated to the Saviour and other saints at Utrecht.

- **17 September 741:** [Donation of Clichy and Saint-Ouen to Saint-Denis] (MGH *D Arnulf.* 14). Charles’s last surviving charter, made five weeks before his death; it is also a political manifestation because its consent clauses name his wife Swanahild and their son Grifo, but not Carloman or Pippin.

### Medieval constructions in Charles’s name

- **Undated; purportedly 8th century:** [Donation of the church of Velzen and property at Adrichem to Echternach] (MGH *D Arnulf.* †32). A forged charter assembled from traditions about distinct gifts; the underlying donation may preserve a historical memory, but this text is not an authentic work of Charles’s administration.

- **Purportedly 724:** [First Reichenau foundation charter] (MGH *D Arnulf.* †33). A medieval forgery presenting Charles as a founder or endower of Reichenau. It resists classification as his *obra*: it is a later institutional claim made through his name.

- **Purportedly 724:** [Second Reichenau foundation charter] (MGH *D Arnulf.* †34). Another forged manifestation of Reichenau’s Carolingian foundation tradition, confusing or overlaying Charles Martel with later rulers named Charles.

- **Purportedly 724:** [Third Reichenau foundation charter] (MGH *D Arnulf.* †35). A further forged privilege for Reichenau; the critical edition does not accept it as evidence that Charles composed or issued the surviving text.

### Lost or indirectly reported acts

- **Date unknown:** [Judgment between Frodoinus and Geruntus] (MGH *Dep.* 62). A lost judicial act known only through later notice; its existence is catalogued, but neither its complete wording nor a secure date survives.

- **After 717/718:** [Transfer involving Chaumont-devant-Damvillers and Bishop Peppo of Verdun] (MGH *Dep.* 63). A reported property act preserved in narrative evidence rather than an extant charter; the source does not make entirely clear whether a written instrument once existed.

- **After 717/718:** [Sale of Chaumont-devant-Damvillers to Bishop Peppo] (MGH *Dep.* 64). A related transaction reportedly made by Charles and another nobleman. The *Gesta episcoporum Virdunensium* reports the transaction without explicitly attesting a charter, so it may resist being called a surviving textual work at all.

- **717/718–741:** [Gifts to Echternach, including the villa Adrichem] (MGH *Dep.* 65). A lost or reported grant remembered in Echternach’s *Liber aureus* and Thiofrid’s *Life of Willibrord*; the latter assigns the act to 12 May at Trier but supplies no year.

- **Probably 737:** [Benefice granted to Charles’s brother Childebrand] (MGH *Dep.* 66). A lost property grant associated with Charles’s Burgundian campaign; the recipient’s identification as Childebrand is probable rather than certain.

- **Between 731 and 741:** [Judgment concerning the churches of Trier, St Maximin, and St Victor of Marseille] (MGH *Dep.* 67). A lost *placitum* reconstructed from later evidence. Its unusually broad institutional setting makes it a governmental event recoverable only imperfectly as a “text.”

- **Date unknown:** [Act concerning Bishop Milo of Trier and Reims] (MGH *Dep.* 68). A reported but lost disposition whose precise legal content and date cannot be recovered securely.

- **Before 22 October 741:** [Earlier act for Saint-Denis] (MGH *Dep.* 69). A lost grant or privilege distinct from the surviving charter of 17 September 741; later Saint-Denis tradition establishes the claim but not its complete original form.

- **About 19 July 723:** [Judgment for Abbot Benignus of St Wandrille against Count Bertharius] (MGH *Dep.* 70). A lost *placitum* summarized in the *Gesta abbatum Fontanellensium*; the date is reconstructed from the assembly and the approaching feast of the monastery’s patron.

- **Date unknown:** [Act for Abbot Lando and St Wandrille] (MGH *Dep.* 71). A lost institutional disposition known through the abbey’s later record; its wording, exact legal form, and date are not preserved.

- **Purportedly 724:** [Protection or endowment of Pirmin and his monasteries] (MGH *Dep.* †72). A lost notice judged false or doubtful. It belongs to later traditions that projected Carolingian sponsorship backward onto Pirmin’s foundations and may confuse Charles Martel with Charlemagne.

- **Purportedly 8th century:** [Protection of St Gall] (MGH *Dep.* †73). A lost and critically rejected attribution: conflicting traditions assign the monastery’s protection either to Charles or to his son Pippin, so it cannot securely be treated as Charles’s work.

- **Purportedly before 741:** [Grant to Fulda] (MGH *Dep.* †74). A lost notice regarded as false, probably created to extend Fulda’s Carolingian patronage into Charles’s lifetime; it is a later historical construction, not an authenticated act by him.
