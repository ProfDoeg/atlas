## Works by Matteo Ricci

### Missionary protocols, prayers, and catechetical texts

- **1584:** *Zuchuan Tianzhu shijie* (祖傳天主十誡, “The Ten Commandments of the Lord of Heaven, Transmitted by the Ancestors”). A Chinese Decalogue made with Michele Ruggieri and printed at Zhaoqing; collaborative authorship prevents treating it as Ricci’s alone. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **1584:** *Pater noster*, *Ave Maria*, and *Credo* in Chinese. These were separately printed or circulated prayer-sheets translated with Ruggieri, not a single book; the surviving bibliography describes their Chinese as still experimental. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf); [“Opere di Matteo Ricci”](https://www.yumpu.com/it/document/view/16120642/opere-di-matteo-ricci-lassociation-ricci))
- **1584:** *Tianzhu shilu* (天主實錄, “True Record of the Lord of Heaven”). The first Chinese Jesuit catechism was principally composed by Ruggieri but emerged from the two missionaries’ shared Zhaoqing project; Ricci’s degree of authorship is consequently disputed, and it should not be assigned to him without qualification. ([“Opere di Matteo Ricci”](https://www.yumpu.com/it/document/view/16120642/opere-di-matteo-ricci-lassociation-ricci))
- **1594–1603:** *Tianzhu shiyi* (天主實義, “The True Meaning of the Lord of Heaven”). A Chinese philosophical-theological dialogue, repeatedly revised with Chinese literary assistance and first printed at Beijing in 1603; it is closer to an apologetic manifesto of Ricci’s accommodation project than to a basic catechism. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w))
- **1605:** *Tianzhu jiaoyao* (天主教要, “Compendium of the Teaching of the Lord of Heaven”). A revised Chinese prayer-and-doctrine manual, printed at Beijing after ecclesiastical review; unlike *Tianzhu shiyi*, it functioned as a practical missionary protocol. ([“Notizie sugli scritti di Matteo Ricci”](https://it.scribd.com/document/74757218/Notizie-Sugli-Scritti-Di-Matteo-Ricci); [Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **1605–1608; published posthumously in 1629:** *Bianxue yidu* (辯學遺牘, “Testament/Documents in Defence of the Learning”). A small dossier of replies exchanged with anti-Christian critics rather than a deliberately unified book; its later editors made the “work” by gathering documents Ricci had produced in controversy. ([Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w); [Chinese Christian Studies Network](https://ccsnet.org/688.html))

### Ethical, mnemonic, and intercultural compositions

- **1595:** *Jiaoyou lun* (交友論, “On Friendship”). A Chinese collection of one hundred adapted sayings on friendship, made from European classical and humanist materials for Prince Jian’an; it is generally regarded as Ricci’s first independently composed Chinese work, although Chinese friends helped refine its language and successive editions altered its paratexts. ([Wiley, *Renaissance Studies*](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/rest.12863); [Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w))
- **1596:** *Xiguo jifa* (西國記法, “Western Art of Memory” or “Occidental Mnemonics”). A six-part protocol for constructing and using mnemonic images and places, written in Chinese for Lu Wangai’s sons; it survives as a manuscript tradition and was not printed in Ricci’s lifetime. ([Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w); [Korean Citation Index](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART001671260))
- **1604:** *Ershiwu yan* (二十五言, “Twenty-Five Sayings”). A Chinese moral treatise adapted chiefly from Epictetus’s *Enchiridion*, revised with Chinese collaborators; sources sometimes date it 1605, reflecting the difference between composition and publication. ([Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w); [Fondazione Intorcetta](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/I-chapter-1.pdf))
- **1605:** *Xizi qiji* (西字奇蹟, “The Miracle of Western Letters”). A very short pedagogical specimen showing Chinese texts and sounds through Roman letters, accompanied by Christian images; it is simultaneously a devotional object, demonstration of alphabetic writing, and early romanization experiment rather than an ordinary treatise. ([Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w); [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/15/8/905))
- **1608:** *Jiren shipian* (畸人十篇, “Ten Discourses of a Strange/Paradoxical Man”). Ten Chinese dialogues on moral and religious life, shaped from conversations with literati and printed with their prefaces; its conversational and collaborative formation makes it both obra and record of an intellectual network. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w))

### Mathematics, astronomy, and instruments in words

- **1599–1600:** *Siyuan xing lun* (四元行論, “Treatise on the Four Elements”). A short Chinese natural-philosophical text associated with Ricci’s Nanjing period; material under this title was later absorbed into *Qiankun tiyi*, while scholarship warns that parts of the received compilation are not in Ricci’s hand. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [“Notizie sugli scritti”](https://it.scribd.com/document/74757218/Notizie-Sugli-Scritti-Di-Matteo-Ricci))
- **1601:** *Jingtian gai* (經天該, approximately “Compendium of the Celestial Circles”). A brief astronomical or cosmographical work associated with Ricci’s Beijing teaching; its exact textual identity and relationship to his later compilations remain uncertain. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **1607:** *Jihe yuanben* (幾何原本, “Elements of Geometry”). The first six books of Euclid rendered into Chinese from Christopher Clavius’s Latin edition: Ricci orally explained the mathematics and Xu Guangqi formulated and edited the Chinese, so this is a collaborative translation-project, not Ricci’s sole-authored book. ([Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf); [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1607:** *Yi Jihe yuanben yin* (譯幾何原本引, “Introduction to the Translation of Euclid’s Elements”). Ricci’s prefatory account of the translation, separable as a short text even though it ordinarily manifests as part of the collaborative *Jihe yuanben*. ([Springer corpus study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42803-025-00104-w))
- **1607:** *Celiang fayi* (測量法義, “Principles of Measurement”). A protocol for practical surveying and measurement, transmitted by Ricci and written or edited by Xu Guangqi; its divided intellectual labor makes sole authorship inappropriate. ([Chinese Christian Studies Network](https://ccsnet.org/688.html); [Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf))
- **1607:** *Celiang yitong* (測量異同, “Similarities and Differences in Measurement”). A comparative mathematical supplement associated with the Ricci–Xu Guangqi workshop; some bibliographies catalogue it under Ricci while others place the textual authorship chiefly with Xu. ([Chinese Christian Studies Network](https://ccsnet.org/688.html); [Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf))
- **1607:** *Gougu yi* (勾股義, “Principles of Right-Triangle Calculation”). A mathematical explanation of the relation now called the Pythagorean theorem, produced in the Ricci–Xu circle; received catalogues attribute it to Ricci, but its Chinese redaction was collaborative. ([Chinese Christian Studies Network](https://ccsnet.org/688.html); [Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf))
- **1607:** *Yuanrong jiaoyi* (圜容較義, “Principles for Comparing Figures Contained in Circles”). A geometry manual adapted from Clavius and formulated with Li Zhizao; it is better understood as a collaborative translation-and-demonstration project than as an original monograph. ([Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf); [China Review International discussion](https://paperzz.com/doc/8138210/china-review-interna-tional))
- **1607:** *Xiqin quyi* (西琴曲意, “Meaning of the Western Keyboard/Clavichord”). A short account of a European keyboard instrument and its musical meanings, sometimes treated as a prefatory or explanatory text rather than an independent book. ([Routledge preview bibliography](https://s3-euw1-ap-pe-df-pch-content-store-p.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/9781003490159/daf30fbd-dd13-4cad-ab1e-b1f7c3cbc02b/preview.pdf))
- **1607–1608:** *Hungai tongxian tushuo* (渾蓋通憲圖說, “Illustrated Explanation of the Astrolabic Sphere”). An illustrated astronomical protocol explaining an instrument for representing and observing the heavens, made with Li Zhizao from European sources; text, diagrams, and intended instrument together approach a scientific Gesamtkunstwerk. ([Korean historical study](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART001517342); [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **after 1606–1607:** *Riqiu dayu diqiu, diqiu dayu yueqiu* (日球大於地球，地球大於月球, “The Sun Is Larger than the Earth, and the Earth Larger than the Moon”). A short astronomical demonstration whose title states its proposition; it is known bibliographically but is difficult to delimit from Ricci’s related cosmographical writings. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **c. 1608–1610; printed posthumously, probably 1614:** *Qiankun tiyi* (乾坤體義, “Structure and Meaning of Heaven and Earth”). A composite cosmographical obra combining explanations, diagrams, measurements, and writings of unequal provenance; later elaboration by Bi Maokang and posthumous printing mean that the received book resists identification with a single finished Ricci manuscript. ([University of Hong Kong Scholars Hub](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/190542); [Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf))
- **before 1610; printed 1613:** *Tongwen suanzhi* (同文算指, “Arithmetic Guidance in a Common Language”). A Chinese adaptation of Clavius’s practical arithmetic, transmitted by Ricci and compiled by Li Zhizao; Li completed and published it after Ricci’s death, so it is an unfinished-at-death collaborative project rather than a book Ricci personally brought to press. ([Chinese Christian Studies Network](https://ccsnet.org/688.html); [Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf))

### Maps and cartographic manifestations

- **1584:** *Yudi shanhai quantu* (輿地山海全圖, “Complete Map of the Mountains and Seas of the Earth”). Ricci’s first Chinese world map, printed at Zhaoqing with Chinese assistance; no copy is known to survive, so the obra persists through testimony and later revisions rather than as an extant object. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf); [Grand Ricci bibliography](https://www.grandricci.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/histoire_bibliographie.pdf))
- **1584–1608:** *The Chinese world-map project*. Ricci repeatedly enlarged, corrected, retitled, and reprinted his world image in at least six manifestations; because several editions are lost or known only indirectly, the continuing project is more historically defensible than pretending every state was a wholly independent work. ([Grand Ricci bibliography](https://www.grandricci.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/histoire_bibliographie.pdf); [Springer, “Mapping the Ambiguous”](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-67339-3_11))
- **1596:** *Shanhai yudi tu* or a revised world map made at Nanchang. This manifestation is reported in the cartographic literature but is lost, and its precise title, format, and distinction from the 1584 map are not securely recoverable. ([Grand Ricci bibliography](https://www.grandricci.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/histoire_bibliographie.pdf))
- **1600:** *Shanhai yudi quantu* (山海輿地全圖, “Complete Terrestrial Map of Mountains and Seas”). A substantially revised Nanjing world map; it is another state of Ricci’s evolving cartographic project rather than merely a reprint. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **1601:** *Jingtian gai* celestial or hemispherical diagram. Some bibliographies treat this as a map or diagram rather than the astronomical text of the same title; because the surviving references do not securely establish whether these are one object or two, it resists separate-work status. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **1602:** *Kunyu wanguo quantu* (坤輿萬國全圖, “Complete Geographical Map of the Myriad Countries of the World”). A six-panel woodblock world map made at Beijing with Li Zhizao, Zhang Wentao, and other Chinese collaborators; its images, descriptive notices, transliterations, and ideological repositioning of the globe make it cartography, encyclopedic text, and intercultural manifesto at once. ([Springer, “Mapping the Ambiguous”](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-67339-3_11); [Pontifical Gregorian University catalogue](https://oseegenius.unigre.it/pug/resource%3Bjsessionid%3DCC63CF3D9B99B8D9F9B7F35B7E338A39?uri=418195BIB&v=l))
- **1603:** *Liangyi xuanlan tu* (兩儀玄覽圖, “Profound Survey of the Two Principles”). A large hemispherical world map produced by Li Yingshi from Ricci’s cartography and teaching; Ricci’s contribution is formative but shared, so attribution ranges from “by Ricci” to “after Ricci.” ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **1608:** *Imperial presentation world map*. A final enlarged or revised manifestation prepared for presentation to the Wanli emperor, sometimes called the sixth edition of Ricci’s map; it appears not to survive, and its precise title and relation to the 1602 *Kunyu wanguo quantu* remain uncertain. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf); [Grand Ricci bibliography](https://www.grandricci.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/histoire_bibliographie.pdf))

### Language tools and translations of Chinese learning

- **1583–1588:** *Portuguese–Chinese Dictionary*. A manuscript lexicographical tool made jointly by Ricci and Michele Ruggieri, with Chinese words romanized for European learners; because it was a working mission instrument rather than a published book, its unstable collaborative manuscript is the obra. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf); [Sources of Chinese Tradition](https://studylib.net/doc/28104331/sources-of-chinese-tradition-2nd-ed-9780231109383-0231109...))
- **c. 1591–1595:** *Latin translation of the Four Books*. Ricci translated the Confucian *Four Books*, probably with explanatory material, for Jesuit use; the complete work is lost or has not been securely identified, so it survives principally as a reported translation-project and possible fragments rather than a stable text. ([Chinese Christian Studies Network](https://ccsnet.org/688.html); [Sources of Chinese Tradition](https://studylib.net/doc/28104331/sources-of-chinese-tradition-2nd-ed-9780231109383-0231109...))

### Music and court presentation

- **1601:** *Xiqin quyi bazhang* (西琴曲意八章, “Eight Songs/Meanings for the Western Keyboard”). Eight Chinese Christian song-texts prepared for performance with a European keyboard instrument at court and later appended to *Jiren shipian*; the melodies appear to have been adapted rather than newly composed by Ricci, so his secure contribution is chiefly the Chinese verbal and conceptual program. ([Criveller, appendix](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf); [“Notizie sugli scritti”](https://it.scribd.com/document/74757218/Notizie-Sugli-Scritti-Di-Matteo-Ricci))

### Historical narrative, correspondence, and unfinished documentary bodies

- **1580s–1610:** *Letters from India and China*. Fifty-four surviving letters are presently counted, addressed to Jesuit superiors, relatives, and colleagues; they are individual acts of communication later made into a corpus, not a single work planned by Ricci. ([Brendan Gottschall, *Matteo Ricci: Letters from China* bibliographical notice](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_der_Schriften_von_Matteo_Ricci); [Treccani bibliography](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1608–1610:** *Della entrata della Compagnia di Giesù e Christianità nella Cina* (“On the Entry of the Society of Jesus and Christianity into China”). An unfinished Italian manuscript history and ethnography of China and the Jesuit mission, left incomplete at Ricci’s death; Nicolas Trigault subsequently edited, translated, rearranged, and expanded it as *De Christiana expeditione apud Sinas* (1615), which therefore is not simply Ricci’s original text. ([*Fonti Ricciane* review](https://www.jstor.org/stable/23570071); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/ricci-matteo))

### Projects and practices that resist work-status

- **from the 1590s:** *The accommodation project*. Ricci’s adoption of Chinese scholarly dress, Confucian vocabulary, book-centered persuasion, and selective recovery of the classical terms *Shangdi* and *Tian* formed an intentional missionary-cultural project manifested across maps, conversations, rituals, and texts; it is a body of practice, not a separately bounded obra. ([Criveller, “The Missionary Method of Matteo Ricci”](https://hsstudyc.org.hk/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/T158_11.pdf))
- **c. 1595 onward:** *The Chinese memory palace*. Ricci taught interlocutors to construct imaginary architectural spaces populated by mnemonic images; beyond the protocol recorded in *Xiguo jifa*, each palace existed as a mental practice, so calling it a freestanding work would falsely turn an instructed process into a surviving object. ([Korean Citation Index](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART001671260))
- **1601 onward:** *The Beijing scientific workshop*. Ricci’s oral explanations, diagrams, instruments, and joint terminology-making with Xu Guangqi and Li Zhizao generated several mathematical and astronomical books; the workshop itself is a productive network, and it resists attribution as either one work or Ricci’s creation alone. ([Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/matteo-ricci_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Ricci Institute article](https://www.riccimac.org/doc/monographs/1/exchange_of_learning_yet_failed_encounter.pdf))
