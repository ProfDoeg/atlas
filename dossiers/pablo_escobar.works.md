## Works by Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria

### Built environments and commissioned constructions

- **1978–1993:** *Hacienda Nápoles*. A jointly assembled estate and evolving Gesamtkunstwerk commissioned by Escobar and his cousin Gustavo Gaviria: residence, transport infrastructure, recreational landscape, collection display, and private zoo. Escobar did not personally design or build its components, while the animals’ dispersal—especially the introduced hippopotamuses—made the project an unintended ecological creation that continued after him. ([Historia, Ciências, Saúde—Manguinhos](https://www.scielo.br/j/hcsm/a/K6knWNPp5x5t4TDgxdKSJPw/?format=html&lang=en); [Design Journal study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/14606925.2017.1352968))

- **c. 1982–1984:** *Medellín sin Tugurios*, subsequently known by residents as *Barrio Pablo Escobar*. A housing and political-clientelist project that relocated families living near the Moravia dump; construction depended on a corporation, clergy, organizers, laborers, and residents, so it is not accurately described as Escobar’s individual architectural work. Contemporary and later accounts disagree about the number of houses and the precise completion date. ([Universidad del Rosario study](https://repository.urosario.edu.co/bitstream/10336/5706/4/CallejasOchoa-Simon-2013.pdf); [Colombian Truth Commission](https://babel.banrepcultural.org/digital/api/collection/comision-col/id/406/download))

- **1979–1984:** *Sports grounds, parks, lighting, roads, schools, and neighborhood improvements made through Civismo en Marcha*. This is a distributed body of commissioned or subsidized interventions rather than a securely catalogued set of individually authored works; one study counts more than forty improved sports grounds, while contemporary reporting records disputes over unauthorized work on public property. ([Universidad del Rosario study](https://repository.urosario.edu.co/bitstream/10336/5706/4/CallejasOchoa-Simon-2013.pdf); [CINEP press archive](https://biblioarchivo.bogota.gov.co/opac-tmpl/IMG_CINEP1/BC0705-1983-3T-1.pdf))

- **c. 1984–1986:** *Edificio Mónaco*. A fortified residential building commissioned for Escobar and his family, not a building designed by him; construction required several acquired lots and professional builders, and its façade incorporated a sculpture by Rodrigo Arenas Betancourt. It was bombed in 1988, expropriated, and demolished by Medellín in 2019. ([Universidad del Rosario, *El Mónaco, una última pregunta*](https://repository.urosario.edu.co/handle/10336/24462); [El Colombiano historical dossier](https://oom.elcolombiano.com/medellin-es-mas-que-pablo-escobar/edificio-monaco))

- **1990–1991:** *La Catedral*. A prison complex built by the Colombian state under terms substantially dictated by Escobar, with security arrangements and amenities shaped for his benefit; it therefore resists classification as either his private building or an ordinary public prison. Scholarly accounts describe his organization supplying materials and controlling its internal systems, but do not establish Escobar as its architect. ([University of Maryland study](https://www.gotech.spp.umd.edu/sites/default/files/2019-07/lamb_umd_0117e_11062.pdf); [FLACSO thesis](https://repositorio.flacsoandes.edu.ec/bitstream/10469/21051/2/TFLACSO-2024VFRP.pdf))

### Civic, political, and media projects

- **1979–1984:** *Civismo en Marcha*. A civic-political project founded and directed by Escobar that combined tree planting, neighborhood meetings, radio programming, public entertainment, social services, and construction work; it acted simultaneously as philanthropy, participatory urban program, publicity apparatus, and electoral machine. Its collective execution prevents the project’s many manifestations from being treated as Escobar’s individually authored works. ([National Centre for Historical Memory](https://www.centrodememoriahistorica.gov.co/descargas/informes-accesibles/medellin-una-guerra-urbana_accesible.pdf); [Universidad del Rosario study](https://repository.urosario.edu.co/bitstream/10336/5706/4/CallejasOchoa-Simon-2013.pdf))

- **from 1979:** *Casa de las Negritudes*. A medical, dental, cultural, and community-service institution presented as part of *Civismo en Marcha*. The surviving descriptions come substantially from the movement’s own publicity, so its duration, institutional independence, and complete program remain uncertain. ([study citing the January 1984 issue of *Medellín Cívico*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/354620463_Localizacion_y_movimiento_estrategias_de_territorializacion_de_la_identidad_afrodescendiente_en_Medellin))

- **c. 1979–1984:** *Medellín Cívico*. A periodical used to publicize Escobar, *Civismo en Marcha*, and its social projects; it is a financed and controlled media platform, not a newspaper wholly written or originally created by him. The title had in fact been established in 1957 by his uncle Hernando Gaviria Berrio, who later directed it, and Escobar’s public speeches were also reportedly written by others. ([Proyecto Pablo Escobar documentation](https://www.proyectopabloescobar.com/2020/01/periodico-medellin-civico-1957.html); [Alonso Salazar interview](https://elpais.com/america-colombia/2023-12-01/alonso-salazar-si-pablo-escobar-hubiera-tenido-una-bomba-atomica-nos-la-habria-tirado.html))

- **c. 1979–1984:** *“Cartas a los medellinenses”*. A recurring group of signed newspaper columns or open letters associated with Escobar in *Medellín Cívico*. No critical edition or complete scholarly inventory has been identified, and editorial or ghostwriting practices at the newspaper make individual authorship uncertain; the corpus therefore cannot responsibly be expanded into invented item-level titles and dates. ([Proyecto Pablo Escobar documentation](https://www.proyectopabloescobar.com/search?by-date=false&m=0&max-results=500&start=39&updated-max=2012-08-25T09%3A12%3A00-07%3A00); [Alonso Salazar interview](https://elpais.com/america-colombia/2023-12-01/alonso-salazar-si-pablo-escobar-hubiera-tenido-una-bomba-atomica-nos-la-habria-tirado.html))

### Compiled publication

- **1992:** *Pablo Escobar en caricaturas, 1983–1991*. A privately printed scrapbook or “libro de oro” compiling press cartoons about Escobar, reportedly assembled at La Catedral with Edgar Roberto Escobar Taborda and completed on 2 July 1992. Escobar was its subject, patron, and possible compiler—not the creator of the reproduced cartoons—and contemporary provenance accounts conflict over whether he personally assembled it. ([El Tiempo, 2004](https://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/mam-1637702); [El Tiempo, 2016](https://www.eltiempo.com/archivo/documento/cms-16490796))

### Collective manifestos and coercive texts

- **c. 1984–1991:** *Communiqués of Los Extraditables*. An uncollected corpus of anonymous or collectively signed declarations, threats, claims of responsibility, negotiation statements, and anti-extradition manifestos; they were texts intended to alter state conduct rather than merely describe a position. Sources identify Escobar as a principal organizer and drafter, but the collective signature, participation of other traffickers, and absence of a critical collected edition make exclusive or item-by-item attribution unsafe. ([International Drug Policy Consortium study](https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/151938/Extradicion%20Info.15.pdf); [CLACSO study](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/249022/1/Literatura-y-narcotrafico.pdf))

- **23 August 1989:** *Letter to public opinion declaring “total war”*. A manifesto issued under the collective identity of Los Extraditables in response to the Colombian government’s emergency anti-narcotics measures. Escobar’s leadership makes his participation probable, but the collective authorship does not justify silently converting it into an individually authored Escobar text. ([historical account of extradition in Colombia](https://www.historiacolombiana.com/ideas/extradicion); [International Drug Policy Consortium study](https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/151938/Extradicion%20Info.15.pdf))

- **19 June 1991:** *Declaration upon surrender*. A performative legal-political statement accompanying Escobar’s submission to Colombian authorities and the end announced by Los Extraditables after the new constitution prohibited extradition of Colombian nationals. It functioned as a negotiation instrument and self-exculpatory public narrative; surviving descriptions do not establish whether every word was composed by Escobar himself. ([CLACSO study](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/249022/1/Literatura-y-narcotrafico.pdf); [Medellín Abrazas su Historia](https://medellinabrazasuhistoria.co/los-extraditables/))

### Organizations and systems that resist being called works

- **c. 1976–1993:** *The trafficking network conventionally called the Medellín Cartel*. Escobar helped construct and lead an international system of production, transport, finance, corruption, and coercion, but “cartel” is an externally stabilized name for a changing alliance rather than a single organization authored by one person. Calling it an obra risks both falsely individualizing collective criminal activity and aestheticizing exploitation; it belongs here only as a deliberately made operational system. ([Colombian Truth Commission](https://babel.banrepcultural.org/digital/api/collection/comision-col/id/406/download); [National Centre for Historical Memory](https://www.centrodememoriahistorica.gov.co/descargas/informes-accesibles/medellin-una-guerra-urbana_accesible.pdf))

- **1981 onward:** *Muerte a Secuestradores (MAS)*. A paramilitary project publicly announced after the kidnapping of Martha Nieves Ochoa and backed by numerous traffickers and allied actors. Escobar is repeatedly associated with its formation, but it was collective, its membership and later continuity are contested, and its killings are acts and crimes—not “works” in any celebratory or conventional bibliographic sense. ([Colombian Truth Commission](https://babel.banrepcultural.org/digital/api/collection/comision-col/id/406/download); [International Drug Policy Consortium study](https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/151938/Extradicion%20Info.15.pdf))

- **c. 1984–1991:** *Los Extraditables*. A clandestine coalition and coercive political project organized to prevent extradition to the United States by propaganda, negotiation, bribery, kidnapping, and terror. Escobar was its central strategist but not its sole maker; the organization’s deliberately impersonal name and changing membership resist individual attribution. ([International Drug Policy Consortium study](https://www.files.ethz.ch/isn/151938/Extradicion%20Info.15.pdf); [Medellín Abrazas su Historia](https://medellinabrazasuhistoria.co/los-extraditables/))

- **1984–1991:** *Campaign of narcoterrorism against extradition and the Colombian state*. A coordinated but evolving project comprising threats, assassinations, kidnappings, bombings, and public communiqués. These are historically produced actions rather than an obra, and responsibility for particular attacks varies from judicially established orders to allegations and unresolved disputes; a works list should therefore neither aestheticize the campaign nor assign every act personally to Escobar. ([National Centre for Historical Memory](https://www.centrodememoriahistorica.gov.co/descargas/informes-accesibles/medellin-una-guerra-urbana_accesible.pdf); [CLACSO study](https://biblioteca-repositorio.clacso.edu.ar/bitstream/CLACSO/249022/1/Literatura-y-narcotrafico.pdf))
