## Works by Giulio Camillo Delminio

### Encyclopedic architecture and memory project

- **c. 1520s–1544:** *Teatro della memoria* (also called *Teatro della sapientia* or *Casa della sapienza*). A lifelong project for arranging all knowledge through forty-nine image-bearing “places,” organized by seven planetary sectors and seven levels. Reports of a wooden construction exist, but no securely identifiable object survives: scholarship therefore cannot determine whether the obra was an architectural structure, a model, a changing body of manuscripts and diagrams, a mental system, or a Gesamtkunstwerk comprising all of these. ([Bolzoni/Adelphi](https://www.adelphi.it/libro/9788845929823); [Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **1530s–1544; unfinished:** *L’idea dell’eloquenza*. A manuscript manifestation of the Theatre as an ordered system of rhetoric and invention, preserved in more than one redaction and published critically only in 2015. It resists treatment as an autonomous “book”: it belongs to the evolving documentary galaxy of the Theatre. ([Bolzoni/Adelphi](https://www.adelphi.it/libro/9788845929823))
- **January 1544; first printed 1550:** *L’idea del Theatro*. A compact exposition dictated to Girolamo Muzio in seven early-morning sessions, rather than a complete specification of the Theatre; the circumstances of its mediation cast some uncertainty on the printed text’s status as Camillo’s unfiltered wording. It is simultaneously description, mnemonic protocol, and surviving fragment of a much larger project. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Wolfenbüttel digital edition](https://diglib.hab.de/content.php?dir=edoc%2Fed000188&distype=optional&metsID=edoc_ed000188_introduction&xml=tei-introduction.xml&xsl=http%3A%2F%2Fdiglib.hab.de%2Frules%2Fprojekte%2Ftheatra%2Ftei-introduction2.xsl))
- **before 1544; first printed 1552:** *Discorso in materia del suo Theatro*. A supplementary discourse concerning the Theatre, transmitted posthumously in the first collected edition; it is another textual manifestation of the larger, incomplete obra rather than the Theatre itself. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Rhetorical and linguistic protocols

- **before 1544; first printed 1544:** *Trattato delle materie che possono venire sotto lo stile dell’eloquente*. A classificatory protocol for organizing the subjects available to an orator or writer; its early printing included a red-and-black diagram of Camillo’s “secondo artificio,” making the work partly diagrammatic rather than prose alone. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Govi Rare Books study](https://www.govirarebooks.com/autori/camillo-giulio))
- **before 1544; first printed 1544:** *Della imitatione*. A rhetorical treatise and practical protocol arguing that imitation should recover and recombine a model’s organizing principles, not merely copy its words. It was first issued together with the *Trattato delle materie*. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Govi Rare Books study](https://www.govirarebooks.com/autori/camillo-giulio))
- **before 1544; first printed 1560:** *La Topica, overo dell’elocutione*. A systematic treatise classifying forms and ornaments of expression, conceived as a complement to the *Trattato delle materie*. Its posthumous editors transmitted it in both partial and collected forms. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Petrarch Exegesis in Renaissance Italy](https://petrarch.mml.ox.ac.uk/printed-editions/il-secondo-tomo-dellopere-di-messer-givlio-camillo-manchester-john-rylands-1))
- **before 1544; first printed 1560:** *Discorso sopra l’idee di Hermogene*. A rhetorical exposition adapting Hermogenes’ “ideas,” or forms of style, into Camillo’s classificatory system; later editors coupled or conflated it with the *Artificio della Bucolica*. ([Petrarch Exegesis in Renaissance Italy](https://petrarch.mml.ox.ac.uk/printed-editions/il-secondo-tomo-dellopere-di-messer-givlio-camillo-manchester-john-rylands-1); [Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))
- **before 1544; first printed 1560:** *Grammatica*. A grammatical treatise preserved through the posthumous second volume of Camillo’s works. Its belated editorial appearance makes its original form and date uncertain. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Petrarch Exegesis in Renaissance Italy](https://petrarch.mml.ox.ac.uk/printed-editions/il-secondo-tomo-dellopere-di-messer-givlio-camillo-manchester-john-rylands-1))
- **before 1544; first printed 1560:** *De’ verbi semplici*. A linguistic treatise on simple verbs, added posthumously to the enlarged first volume of Camillo’s works; no secure composition date is known. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1544; first printed 1594:** *Artificio della Bucolica di Virgilio*. A protocol for analyzing and composing discourse through Camillo’s reading of Virgil’s *Eclogues*. It survives as a late posthumous publication, subsequently combined editorially with his treatment of Hermogenes. ([Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))

### Petrarchan reading apparatus

- **1533; unrealized:** *Petrarca novo con l’artificio di Iulio Camillo*. A projected edition and reusable analytical apparatus for Petrarch, documented by a ten-year Venetian printing privilege but never completed in its intended form. As a project, its unrealized design—not a hypothetical lost book—is the obra. ([Govi Rare Books study](https://www.govirarebooks.com/autori/camillo-giulio))
- **before 1544; first printed 1553:** *Avertimenti* or *Annotationi sopra le Rime del Petrarca*. Brief Petrarchan annotations incorporated posthumously into Lodovico Dolce’s editions and also issued separately; the printed matter is only partly Camillo’s, since Dolce’s tables and indexes occupy much of the apparatus. Scholars have disputed their originality, one judgment treating them as a partial, corrupt derivative of Bembo’s annotations. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1544; first printed 1560:** *Espositione sopra il primo et secondo sonetto del Petrarca*. A close prose commentary on the first two poems of Petrarch’s *Rerum vulgarium fragmenta*. It is distinct from the shorter *Avertimenti*, though both belong to Camillo’s broader, incompletely realized Petrarch project. ([Petrarch Exegesis in Renaissance Italy](https://petrarch.mml.ox.ac.uk/printed-editions/il-secondo-tomo-dellopere-di-messer-givlio-camillo-manchester-john-rylands-1))
- **date uncertain:** *Rime del Petrarca con le annotazioni di Giulio Camillo*. An interleaved Aldine Petrarch bearing extensive manuscript annotations in two late-sixteenth-century hands. The headings attribute the notes to Camillo, but their handwriting, relation to Bembo’s annotations, and exact authorial status remain disputed; it therefore resists classification as a securely autograph work. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Orations and courtly works

- **before 1544; first printed 1551:** *Due orationi di Giulio Camillo al re christianissimo*. Two ceremonial speeches composed for Cosimo Pallavicino to deliver before Francis I of France on behalf of Pallavicino’s bishop brother. They are commissioned acts of courtly advocacy—texts designed to perform an intervention—not simply descriptive essays. ([Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio); [Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1544; first printed 1587:** *Pro suo de eloquentiae Theatro ad Gallos oratio*. A Latin oration advocating Camillo’s Theatre before a French audience—a manifesto-like performance intended to secure belief and patronage for the project. The late edition precedes it with eighty-one hexameters addressed to Pietro Bembo. ([Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))
- **before 1544:** *Davalus*. A Latin poem made in honor of Alfonso d’Avalos, marquis del Vasto, surviving in manuscript and later printed by Francesco Altan. Its precise date and original title-status are not securely established. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1544:** *In Ciceronis orationes argumenta*. A manuscript collection of Latin summaries or argumentative schemata for Cicero’s speeches, surviving in Ferrara and Milan witnesses; it is best understood as a pedagogical and rhetorical tool. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Religious, philosophical, and alchemical writings

- **before 1544; first printed 1552:** *Lettera del rivolgimento dell’huomo a Dio*. A spiritual letter on the human turn toward God, subsequently treated by editors as a discrete work. Its epistolary form complicates the boundary between private communication, devotional protocol, and authored treatise. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1544; first printed 1571:** *Sermoni della cena di Nostro Signore Giesu Christo*. A sequence of religious sermons on the Last Supper. The posthumous title page incorrectly calls Camillo a Florentine, a warning that the edition is not transparent evidence for the work’s original circumstances. ([Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))
- **before 1544; first published in a modern edition in 1984:** *De l’humana deificatione*. A theological-philosophical manuscript on human deification, identified and edited by Cesare Vasoli. Its surviving state belongs to Camillo’s heterogeneous manuscript legacy rather than to the early printed canon. ([Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))
- **before 1544; first published critically 2015:** *De transmutatione*. An alchemical treatise concerned with transformation and natural secrets. Its manuscript transmission places it near the Theatre’s cosmological ambitions, but it remains a distinct alchemical text rather than merely a chapter of *L’idea del Theatro*. ([Bolzoni/Adelphi](https://www.adelphi.it/libro/9788845929823))
- **before 1544; manuscripts:** Untitled or variably titled Cabalistic letters and alchemical notebooks. These are surviving clusters of working documents rather than securely bounded works: scholarship records them as part of Camillo’s manuscript corpus, but their fluid, miscellaneous form resists conversion into invented individual titles. ([Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))

### Poetry and correspondence

- **before 1544; first collected 1552 and enlarged 1560:** *Rime*. A posthumously assembled poetic corpus: the 1552 collection contains twenty-one sonnets and an ode, while the 1560 edition adds eleven sonnets and an ode to Tullia d’Aragona. Because the corpus was editorially enlarged after Camillo’s death and many additional vernacular and Latin poems remain dispersed in manuscript, *Rime* names an unstable body of work, not one authorially fixed book. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
- **before 1544; first collected 1560–1567:** *Lettere*. A dispersed correspondence rather than a single work: seven letters entered the first volume of the 1560 collection, two accompanied its second volume, and another was added in 1566–67. The collective title is editorial, and additional manuscript letters—including Cabalistic material—prevent the corpus from being treated as complete. ([Stabile, DBI](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/); [Turello, DBF](https://www.dizionariobiograficodeifriulani.it/camillo-giulio-detto-delminio))
