## Works by Saint Barbara of Heliopolis

### Corpus status

- **No historically secure date:** *No authenticated obra survives*. Barbara is absent from the earliest historical authorities, and even her existence, place of martyrdom, and chronology are unverified; the standard hagiographic repertories catalogue anonymous narratives about her—BHG 213–218, BHO 132–134, and BHSE 1624—not works demonstrably made by her. Everything below is therefore an object, action, or utterance attributed to Barbara inside evolving legends, not an authenticated historical oeuvre. ([Bibliotheca Hagiographica Arabica](https://www.bha.uob-dh.org/?q=node/65849); [Kirsten Wolf, *The Old Norse-Icelandic Legend of Saint Barbara*](https://www.brepols.net/products/IS-9780888441348-1); [Catholic Encyclopedia](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02284d.htm))

### Legendary architectural project and protocol

- **Legendary, under Maximian (late third or early fourth century):** *The third window of the bathhouse*. Barbara does not build the bathhouse or tower: her father commissions them and workmen execute them, while she changes the protocol by ordering a third window in place of the intended two, turning architecture into a Trinitarian manifesto. The episode is prominent in later Greek, Arabic, and Western traditions but is absent from the surviving summary of the apparently earlier BHG 213 recension, so it cannot be treated as a stable element of an original biography. ([Bibliotheca Hagiographica Arabica, Sin. ar. 416](https://www.bha.uob-dh.org/?q=node/65849); [University of Madrid medieval-iconography database](https://www.ucm.es/bdiconografiamedieval/santabarbara); [Kirsch](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02284d.htm))

### Legendary ritual and material manifestations

- **Legendary, under Maximian:** *The self-baptism in the bath*. BHG 213 presents Barbara’s immersion as an initiatory ritual rather than a manufactured object: recent scholarship reads it as self-baptism, although the narrative supplies no spoken baptismal formula. Other recensions instead have her baptized by John the Baptist or by Origen’s emissary, so both agency and form are disputed. ([Ángel Narro Sánchez, “Self-Baptism in Late Antique Hagiography of Female Saints,” pp. 11–13](https://journals.uco.es/astarte/article/download/17224/15822/47496); [Louis Goosen, “Barbara”](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/goos020vana03_01/goos020vana03_01_0018.php))

- **Legendary, under Maximian:** *Cross traced in the bathhouse marble* [editorial title]. BHG 213 attributes to Barbara a cross drawn with her finger that miraculously enters the stone and remains visible: simultaneously an inscription, sacred image, and manifestation, since its material execution exceeds ordinary making. ([Narro Sánchez, pp. 11–13](https://journals.uco.es/astarte/article/download/17224/15822/47496))

- **Legendary, under Maximian:** *Footprint in the bathhouse pool* [editorial title]. BHG 213 says her foot leaves a healing imprint in the stone; it resists classification as a deliberate work because the narrative treats it as an involuntary, divinely efficacious trace rather than an artifact consciously designed by Barbara. ([Narro Sánchez, pp. 11–13](https://journals.uco.es/astarte/article/download/17224/15822/47496))

### Legendary texts and performative utterances

- **Later medieval legend; purportedly composed during her confinement:** *Letter to Origen* [no transmitted fixed title]. This is a reported message requesting Christian instruction or baptism, not an independently preserved letter: it belongs to an expanded tradition associated with Jean of Wakkerzeel and later adaptations, while BHG 213 explains Barbara’s conversion without Origen. Its status as a text by Barbara is therefore especially resistant—only the narrative claim that she wrote exists. ([Louis Goosen, “Barbara”](https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/goos020vana03_01/goos020vana03_01_0018.php); [Wolf’s critical edition and study](https://www.brepols.net/products/IS-9780888441348-1); [Narro Sánchez, pp. 11–13](https://journals.uco.es/astarte/article/download/17224/15822/47496))

- **Legendary, shortly before martyrdom:** *Final intercessory prayer* [no fixed title]. This spoken text acts rather than merely describes: Barbara asks protection and mercy for those who remember her martyrdom, establishing the legendary protocol behind her patronage against sudden death. Its wording and promised benefits vary among Greek and Western recensions and belong textually to anonymous hagiographers, not to a recoverable historical authorial text. ([*Golden Legend*, “Life of Saint Barbara”](https://christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/barbara.htm); [John of Damascus, *Praise of Saint Barbara*](https://catholiclibrary.org/library/view?chunk.id=00000021&docId=Fathers-Synchronized-EN%2FJohn_of_Damascus__Laudatio_sanctae_Barbarae.en.html))

### Legendary counter-creation

- **Legendary, under Maximian:** *Defacement of Dioscorus’s idols* [editorial title]. BHG 213 has Barbara spit upon the images; amplified Western versions say she scratches their faces. This is deliberate unmaking rather than a conventional obra, and the physical alteration itself is recension-dependent. ([Narro Sánchez, pp. 11–13](https://journals.uco.es/astarte/article/download/17224/15822/47496); [Caxton’s *Golden Legend*, vol. VI](https://ccel.org/ccel/v/voragine/goldleg6/cache/goldleg6.pdf))

### 1893 coda: a manifestation not made by Barbara

- **1893:** *Woman (Louise Guiney) in Saint Barbara costume with laurel wreath and penciled-in halo*. This platinum portrait is a modern manifestation of Barbara performed by Louise Imogen Guiney and photographed by F. Holland Day; it is not an obra by the saint and belongs here only as the specified coda, preserving the distinction between a figure’s work and her later embodiment by others. ([Library of Congress catalogue record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2005679124/); [Jan M. Ziolkowski, *The Juggler of Notre Dame and the Medievalizing of Modernity*, vol. 3, fig. 4.18](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/19/45/29/72/2019452972/2019452972.pdf))
