## Works by Loïe Fuller

### Solo dances and light-choreographic Gesamtkunstwerke

- **1892:** *Serpentine Dance* (*Danse serpentine*; also billed as *The Serpentine*). Fuller’s foundational solo joined improvised skirt movement, a silk costume, concealed wands, darkness, and changing electric light. She sought copyright protection, but *Fuller v. Bemis* held that an abstract dance without story or character was not copyrightable; competing “Loïe Fuller” films generally show imitators, not Fuller herself. [S1, S2, S3]
- **1892:** *XXXX* (*La Danse blanche*). An early abstract solo whose cryptic billing resists interpretation as a conventional title; the standard dance reference identifies *XXXX* and *La Danse blanche* as the same choreography. [S4]
- **1892:** *La Violette* (*Violet*). A solo from Fuller’s earliest Paris repertory, using costume and light to turn the dancer into a floral apparition. [S1, S5]
- **1892:** *Le Papillon* (*The Butterfly Dance*). A solo first documented at the Folies-Bergère on 5 November; its successive costumes included painted wings and, later, white or phosphorescent silk shaped and coloured by light. [S1, S6]
- **1893:** *La Fleur*. A solo documented at New York’s Garden Theatre on 26 August; it belongs to Fuller’s mutable repertory of floral transformations and should not automatically be conflated with the later group *Danse des fleurs*. [S1]
- **1895:** *Le Firmament*. An astronomical light-dance in which Fuller projected lunar photographs over her costume and employed a faceted or reflective setting; it remained in her repertory at the 1900 Exposition Universelle. [S5]
- **1895:** *Salomé*. A full-length “lyrical pantomime,” first performed at the Comédie-Parisienne on 4 March with music by Gabriel Pierné. Later independent dances, especially *La Danse du feu* and *Le Lys*, reused material from it, so its borders as a single work are porous. [S1, S5]
- **1896:** *La Nuit* (*Night*). A solo first documented at Koster and Bial’s Music Hall in New York on 6 March. [S1]
- **c. 1896–1902:** *La Danse phosphorescente* (*The Phosphorescent Dance*; later associated with *La Dame phosphorescente*). A light-dance made from silk dotted or treated with phosphorescent salts so that points of light continued moving after ordinary illumination vanished. Sources variously date and title its manifestations: Fuller began the experiments after visiting Edison in 1896, while repertory lists place *Danses fluorescentes* in 1902 and *La Dame phosphorescente* in 1903. [S4, S5, S7]
- **1897:** *La Danse du feu* (*Fire Dance*). A solo, premiered at the Folies-Bergère on 14 September and commonly performed to Wagner, that made projected and reflected light appear to surround the dancer with flames; it derives from *Salomé* but became an independent repertory work. [S1, S5]
- **1897:** *Le Lys* (*La Danse du lys*; *Lily Dance*). A solo premiered at the Folies-Bergère on 14 September, in which long wand-supported silk rose above the concealed body as a monumental flower; like *La Danse du feu*, it was extracted from *Salomé*. [S1, S5]
- **1900:** *Lumières et ténèbres* (*Lumière et ténèbres*). A mirror-and-light dance presented in Fuller’s theatre at the Exposition Universelle; angled mirrors multiplied the illuminated dancer into an apparently limitless architectural space. Sources fluctuate between singular and plural in the title. [S5]
- **1901:** *L’Archange*. A winged light-dance documented in the standard dance repertory; the surviving references establish its existence more securely than its precise première circumstances. [S4]
- **1901:** *La Tempête*. A solo first documented at the Théâtre de l’Athénée on 14 September, translating a storm into agitated fabric, movement, and light rather than narrative character. [S1]
- **1902:** *Danses fluorescentes*. A repertory designation for Fuller’s experiments with fluorescence rather than necessarily one stable choreography; it therefore resists treatment as a single fixed work. [S4]
- **1903:** *La Dame phosphorescente*. A named manifestation of Fuller’s phosphorescent-dance project; its relation to *La Danse phosphorescente* and *Danses fluorescentes* is not consistently distinguished by the sources. [S4, S7]
- **1904:** *Danse du radium* (*Dance of Radium*). Fuller intended a costume with radium-bearing “butterfly wings,” but the Curies declined to supply radium; the realized dance used phosphorescent salts instead. It is thus both an unrealized scientific project and a substituted stage manifestation. [S5]
- **1907:** *La Tragédie de Salomé*. A two-act dramatic pantomime created at the Théâtre des Arts on 9 November with music by Florent Schmitt. Robert d’Humières conceived the scenario and directed the production, while Fuller created and performed its movement and light imagery; authorship is therefore collaborative, not hers alone. [S1, S8]
- **1909:** *Le Ballet de la lumière*. A light-based choreographic creation documented in the standard repertory; its title names the medium—changing illumination—as the principal performer and makes it closer to a “theatre of light” project than an ordinary ballet. [S4]
- **1911:** *Danses ultra-violettes* (*La Danse ultra-violette*). A family of dances made visible through ultraviolet light and fluorescent substances, not securely one invariant choreography. Contemporary descriptions include an apparition called *L’Oiseau bleu*. [S4, S6, S7]
- **1914:** *Le Phalène noir* (*The Black Moth*). A dark, winged solo or apparition documented in contemporary criticism; the title continues Fuller’s butterfly imagery while reversing its usual luminosity. [S6]
- **1914:** *Danse d’acier* (*Dance of Steel*). An abstract projection work to music by Florent Schmitt, using geometric forms, silver light, laboratory images of cancer cells, and fish skeletons. Dance, scientific image, costume, screen, and lighting form a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than separable scenery and choreography. [S5]

### Group dances, ballets, and staged spectacles

- **1898:** *La Danse des fleurs*. A group or ensemble choreography first documented at a private performance in Paris in May; it should be distinguished from the 1893 solo *La Fleur*. [S1]
- **1900:** *The programme of the Théâtre Loïe Fuller*. Fuller’s Exposition Universelle spectacle combined *La Danse du feu*, *Le Lys*, *Le Firmament*, and *Lumières et ténèbres* inside a purpose-built theatre. As an obra it is both an assembled repertory programme and an architectural-technological Gesamtkunstwerk, rather than a single new dance. [S5, S9]
- **after 1908:** *Les Petits Riens*. A company ballet staged by Fuller to Mozart; available repertory summaries confirm the production but do not securely establish its première date or how much movement was newly choreographed rather than arranged. [S1]
- **after 1908:** *Peer Gynt*. A company staging to Grieg, incorporating Fuller’s light, veil, and ensemble techniques. Its surviving identification is repertorial rather than a complete production record. [S1]
- **after 1908:** *Feu d’artifice* (*Fireworks*). A company light-ballet to Stravinsky; scholarly indexing records it, but the accessible sources do not establish a safe première date. [S1, S5]
- **date uncertain:** *Le Ballet des sylphes*. An ensemble work by Fuller’s company used in André Antoine’s staging of *Faust*. It survives as an embedded theatrical number rather than an independently documented première. [S6]
- **1913:** *Nocturnes* (*Nuages*, *Fêtes*, *Sirènes*). A three-part group choreography to Debussy, premiered in December according to contemporary review evidence; projected clouds and groups of veiled dancers made human bodies function as mobile atmosphere. [S5]
- **1913:** *Une nuit sur le mont Chauve* (*Night on Bald Mountain*). An ensemble choreography to Mussorgsky, first documented at the Odéon on 13 December; some later discussion dates a giant-veil version to 1915, indicating revision or revival rather than a securely single form. [S1, S5]
- **1913–1914:** *Les Mille et Une Nuits*. An ensemble spectacle with music by Armande de Polignac. Sources disagree between a 13 December 1913 Odéon première and a 1914 date, so the chronology remains unsettled. [S1, S6]
- **date uncertain, before 1925:** *Sirènes* (*Sur la mer immense*; also called *La Mer*). A collective giant-veil dance derived from the third movement of *Nocturnes* and repeatedly reworked under several titles. It resists division into separate works because the sources explicitly treat the names as variants of an evolving project. [S5]
- **date uncertain, 1910s–1920s:** *Land of Visions*. A projection dance using astronomical imagery; the scholarly repertory identifies it but does not provide a secure première date. [S5]
- **1920:** *Le Voile magique*. A group dance at the Olympia in which twelve dancers animated a single circular veil while projected colours and designs transformed it. The collective fabric, rather than any one body, was the visible protagonist. [S5]
- **1920:** *Le Lys de la vie*. A large fairy-tale stage spectacle presented at the Paris Opéra on 1 July, adapted from Queen Marie of Romania’s story and assembled from music by several composers. Fuller’s role embraced adaptation, choreography, lighting, design, and production, making the result a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an authored dramatic text. [S1, S10]
- **1920:** *Danse de la grande opale* (*Dance of the Great Opal*). A collective-veil number within *Le Lys de la vie*: dancers raised an internally lit white veil into an opalescent hemisphere. It is both a component of the larger spectacle and a named choreographic object. [S5]
- **1922:** *Ombres gigantesques*. A comic shadow ballet, generally indexed with music by Debussy, in which backlighting enlarged and reduced dancers’ silhouettes and an isolated hand became a giant threatening figure. [S5]
- **date uncertain, c. 1920s:** *Soldats du Brésil*. A shadow dance to music by Darius Milhaud in which paired black and grey silhouettes moved behind a screen amid coloured projections. The available scholarly account does not supply a secure première date. [S5]
- **1925:** *La Mer*. Fuller’s most extensive realization of the evolving *Sirènes/Sur la mer immense* project, performed on 16 June at the Exposition Internationale des Arts Décoratifs. Seventy-five dancers moved beneath some 4,000 square metres of iridescent silk on the Grand Palais staircase: architecture, wind, bodies, fabric, Debussy’s music, and coloured light became one Gesamtkunstwerk. [S5]
- **1925:** *L’Homme au sable* (*Coppelius and the Sandman*). A dark stage adaptation of E. T. A. Hoffmann, presented at the Théâtre des Champs-Élysées on 4 December with music by Arthur Honegger and developed with Gab Sorère. It was Fuller’s last stage piece registered with the Société des auteurs et compositeurs dramatiques; sources also use *Coppelius and the Sandman*, creating ambiguity between this stage work and the later film project. [S1, S5]

### Films and unrealized film projects

- **1920–1921:** *Le Lys de la vie*. A silent fantasy film adapted from Queen Marie of Romania and made as a counterpart to Fuller’s stage spectacle. Fuller is credited with production and choreography and often with direction, but Gab Sorère’s creative participation complicates single authorship; the surviving film is incomplete. [S4, S5, S10]
- **1924–1925:** *Visions des rêves* (*Vision des rêves*). A film project made with Gab Sorère; it is listed in Fuller filmographies but is lost, and its completion or public release is not securely established. It is best classified as a project rather than an extant film. [S1, S5]
- **1927–1928:** *Les Incertitudes de Coppelius* (*Coppelius and the Sandman*). An unfinished film made with Gab Sorère at Gaumont from Hoffmann’s *The Sandman*, planned around dreamlike illumination, optical distortions, and Fuller herself as Coppelius. No footage is known to survive, so what remains is an unrealized or incomplete project rather than a recoverable film. [S1, S5]

### Patented protocols, costumes, and stage machines

- **1893–1894:** *Mechanism for the Production of Stage Effects* (French patent no. 227,105; British no. 10,301; U.S. no. 513,102). A protocol and machine for transparent or pierced stage floors, concealed illumination, mirrored supports, and the illusion that a dancer floats in space. The national patents are legal manifestations of one invention, not three separate obras. [S11]
- **1893–1894:** *Garment for Dancers* (French patent no. 227,107; British no. 10,296; U.S. no. 518,347). A protocol for the head-mounted silk garment and internal hand-held wands that allowed a dancer to produce waves, wings, and sculptural poses; it materially codifies the instrument of the *Serpentine Dance*. [S12]
- **1893–1895:** *Theatrical Stage Mechanism* (French patent no. 227,106; British no. 10,221; U.S. no. 533,167). A mirrored floor, ceiling, and angled background with vertical coloured lights, designed to multiply one dancer into many figures among apparent pillars of fire. [S13]
- **1893:** *A fourth French patent application associated with Fuller’s stage systems*. Contemporary chronologies say that Fuller lodged four French applications, whereas the three patent families traceable through the U.S. specifications are nos. 227,105–227,107. Without a securely identified fourth title or number in the consulted records, it cannot responsibly be promoted to a named work. [S2, S11, S12, S13]

### Theatre, school, and company projects

- **1889:** *Caprice*. Fuller leased London’s Globe Theatre to mount and star in this stage production; it was a financial and artistic failure. The play itself was not authored by Fuller, so her made thing here was the production and entrepreneurial project, not the dramatic text. [S2, S3]
- **1900:** *Théâtre Loïe Fuller*. A purpose-built theatre at the Paris Exposition Universelle, designed as the apparatus and container for Fuller’s light performances. Because its architecture, stage machinery, repertory, and public identity were inseparable, it is a Gesamtkunstwerk and institutional project rather than merely a building. [S5, S9]
- **c. 1908 onward:** *The school and Les Ballets fantastiques de Loïe Fuller*. Fuller’s troupe and pedagogical laboratory trained young dancers without a fixed codified vocabulary and generated changing ensemble works. It is a continuing body of work and institutional project, not one choreography with stable boundaries. [S2, S5]

### Autobiography, lectures, and theoretical texts

- **1908:** *Quinze ans de ma vie*. Fuller’s autobiographical obra, written from her recollections, translated into French by Prince Bojidar Karageorgevitch, and introduced by Anatole France. Scholars caution that its accounts are strategically shaped and sometimes fictionalized; it is testimony and self-creation, not a neutral chronicle. [S3, S14]
- **1911:** *Prelude to Light*. An unpublished lecture or essay preserved in Fuller’s papers, setting out her ideas about illumination and the future “symphony of light.” Its archival status makes it a theoretical text without an authorially fixed published edition. [S5]
- **1911:** *Radium*. An unpublished lecture preserved in Fuller’s papers, concerned with radium’s capacity to render invisible forces perceptible. It is a speculative scientific-aesthetic text, not a technical account of the realized *Danse du radium*. [S5]
- **date uncertain:** *My Laboratory*. An unpublished autobiographical essay in Fuller’s papers about her chemical and lighting experiments. Its date and final status are not securely fixed, so it may be a draft rather than a completed work. [S5]
- **1913:** *Fifteen Years of a Dancer’s Life, with Some Account of Her Distinguished Friends*. The English autobiography is not merely an edition mechanically identical to the 1908 French book: Fuller drafted her memoirs again in English, and a nearly complete manuscript survives. Its relation to *Quinze ans de ma vie* is therefore revision and new manifestation within one autobiographical project. [S3, S15]
- **date uncertain; published posthumously in 2002:** *La Danse*. A theoretical text collected by Giovanni Lista in *Ma vie et la danse*; the consulted edition identifies it as previously unpublished but does not supply a secure composition date. [S16]
- **date uncertain; published posthumously in 2002:** *Danse ultra violette*. A text on Fuller’s ultraviolet work, collected by Giovanni Lista. It accompanies the performance project but is a distinct discursive manifestation. [S16]
- **date uncertain; published posthumously in 2002:** *Le Langage de la danse*. A theoretical text collected by Giovanni Lista, treating dance as a communicative medium. Its surviving editorial form is posthumous, and no secure date is supplied. [S16]
- **date uncertain; published posthumously in 2002:** *L’Oiseau noir*. A previously unpublished text collected by Giovanni Lista; its exact genre and date remain uncertain in the accessible bibliographic description. [S16]
- **date uncertain; published posthumously in 2002:** *Théorie de la danse*. Fuller’s theoretical writing on dance, collected posthumously by Giovanni Lista. It should be understood as an edited archival text rather than a book Fuller herself issued. [S16]
