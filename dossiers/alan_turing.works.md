## Works by Alan Mathison Turing

### Early philosophical writing

- **c. 1932:** *Nature of Spirit*. An unpublished handwritten essay joining questions of mind, matter, free will, and survival after death to contemporary physics; the precise date is inferred rather than written on the manuscript. ([Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts-amtc/amt-c-29))

### Mathematical logic and computability

- **1936–1937:** *On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem*. A journal paper defining what became the Turing machine and proving limits on mechanical computation; its two parts appeared in 1936, followed by a correction in 1937. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **1937:** *Computability and λ-Definability*. A mathematical paper proving the equivalence of Turing computability and Church’s lambda-definability. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **1937:** *The p-Function in λ-K-Conversion*. A short technical note in symbolic logic. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **1938–1939:** *Systems of Logic Based on Ordinals*. Turing’s Princeton doctoral thesis, completed in 1938 and published as a paper in 1939; it develops “ordinal logics” and oracle machines rather than presenting a closed formal system of the ordinary kind. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **1941:** *Some Theorems about Church’s System*. A group of three unpublished manuscripts, partly surviving in continuations without independent titles. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

- **1942:** *A Formal Theorem in Church’s Theory of Types*. A jointly authored paper by Turing and M. H. A. Newman. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **1942:** *The Use of Dots as Brackets in Church’s System*. A paper proposing notational machinery for symbolic logic. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **1943–1944:** *Practical Forms of Type-Theory II*. An unpublished continuation project; the archive catalogue instead dates the surviving typescript approximately to 1948, so its dating is unsettled. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

- **1944–1945:** *The Reform of Mathematical Notation and Phraseology*. An unpublished project proposing reforms to mathematical expression; the archival title includes “and Phraseology,” which is sometimes omitted in bibliographies. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

- **1948:** *Practical Forms of Type-Theory*. A published paper on usable formulations of type theory. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

### Pure mathematics and statistics

- **1935:** *On the Gaussian Error Function*. Turing’s unpublished King’s College fellowship dissertation; only its preface entered the *Collected Works*, although the complete dissertation survives in the archive. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1935:** *Equivalence of Left and Right Almost Periodicity*. Turing’s first published mathematical paper, a concise result in analysis. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1938:** *Finite Approximations to Lie Groups*. A published research paper in group theory. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1938:** *The Extensions of a Group*. A published group-theory paper. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1939–1943:** *A Method for the Calculation of the Zeta-Function*. A paper submitted in 1939 but not published until 1943, associated with Turing’s projected mechanical calculator for evaluating the Riemann zeta function. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1948:** *Rounding-Off Errors in Matrix Processes*. A published numerical-analysis paper containing what became known as Turing’s method or decomposition. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1950:** *The Word Problem in Semi-Groups with Cancellation*. A published paper whose unfinished associated work was later analyzed and completed by W. W. Boone; Boone’s 1958 paper is not itself a work by Turing. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1953:** *Some Calculations of the Riemann Zeta-Function*. A published report of computations made with the Manchester computer. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **1954:** *Solvable and Unsolvable Problems*. An expository article about decidability and computation, included in two volumes of the *Collected Works* but constituting one work. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *A Note on Normal Numbers*. An unpublished typescript and a slightly different manuscript; the manuscript was written on the backs of surviving sheets from *On Computable Numbers*, and later analysis found the proposed construction nearly, but not completely, correct. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *The Word Problem in Compact Groups*. An unpublished and incomplete mathematical manuscript; its fragmentary state makes it more accurately a research project than a finished paper. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *On Permutation Groups*. An unpublished mathematical manuscript subsequently edited for the *Collected Works*. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *The Difference ψ(x) − x*. An unpublished typescript with handwritten calculations and corrections. Related results were completed and published after Turing’s death by A. M. Cohen and M. J. E. Mayhew, whose paper should not be silently counted as Turing’s own. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

- **Undated:** *On a Theorem of Littlewood*. An unpublished joint manuscript by Turing and Stanley Skewes. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *Minimum Cost Sequential Analysis*. An unpublished statistical paper preserving part of the theory Turing developed for wartime cryptanalysis; only an excerpt was printed in the *Collected Works*. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *Notes on Game Theory*. Surviving research notes rather than a settled publication; the *Collected Works* bibliography acknowledges them but does not include them. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

- **Undated:** *Notes on Tensors and Spinors*. Archival mathematical notes excluded from the *Collected Works*; they resist treatment as a single finished work. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html))

### Cryptanalytic protocols, methods, and reports

- **1939–1940:** *The British Bombe*. A cryptanalytic-machine design developed by Turing from foundations supplied by Polish cryptanalysts; Harold “Doc” Keen and the British Tabulating Machine Company translated the conception into engineering and production machines, so the physical bombes were collaborative manifestations rather than objects made by Turing alone. ([GCHQ](https://www.gchq.gov.uk/person/alan-turing); [GCHQ on the Polish contribution](https://www.gchq.gov.uk/information/the-pyry-forest-meeting))

- **c. 1940:** *Banburismus*. A statistical cryptanalytic protocol for reducing the possible rotor orders of Naval Enigma. It was developed by Turing and operated collectively in Hut 8, notably by Joan Clarke and Hugh Alexander, so it is an authored method rather than a single material object. ([Bletchley Park](https://bletchleypark.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/record_attachments/1800.pdf); [GCHQ](https://www.gchq.gov.uk/information/joan-clarke))

- **1940:** *Turing’s Treatise on the Enigma*, also known as *The Prof’s Book* and *Mathematical Theory of ENIGMA Machine*. A secret cryptanalytic treatise and practical protocol whose differing archival titles are manifestations of the same work. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **c. 1942:** *Turingery*. A statistical method for attacking the German Lorenz teleprinter cipher; later machinery used derivatives of the method, but Turing did not build Colossus and should not be credited with that machine. ([Bletchley Park](https://bletchleypark.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/record_attachments/1800.pdf))

- **November 1942:** *Report on U.S. Navy Cryptanalytic Work and Their Machinery*. A classified report written after Turing’s wartime mission to the United States. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **December 1942:** *Visit to National Cash Register Corporation of Dayton, Ohio*. A report on the factory where American bombes were being constructed; it was later published with historical annotation. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **Undated, wartime:** *The Applications of Probability to Cryptography*. A classified mathematical report setting out probability theory underlying Turing’s cryptanalytic practice; it was not released by GCHQ until 2012. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **Undated, wartime:** *Paper on Statistics of Repetitions*. A classified statistical paper connected with the Bayesian basis of Banburismus; it too remained secret until 2012. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **Wartime, dates not fully catalogued:** *Further Cryptanalytic Memoranda*. Hodges records additional memoranda by Turing that have not been comprehensively listed and possibly further postwar GCHQ reports that remain unreleased. This is an acknowledged but incomplete body of work, not a safely enumerable set of titles. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

### Secure-speech Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1943–1945:** *Delilah*. A project by Turing and Donald Bayley combining cryptographic theory, electronic engineering, laboratory notebooks, speech recordings, and a working portable secure-voice prototype; its inseparable mixture of apparatus, signal, documentation, and performance makes it a small technical Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a machine or text. ([Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/amt-h))

- **6 June 1944:** *Speech System “Delilah”—Report on Progress*. A three-page internal progress report: a documentary manifestation of the larger Delilah project rather than the project itself. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

- **c. 1945:** *Report on Speech Secrecy System DELILAH*. An approximately eighty-page technical report compiled jointly by Turing and Lieutenant Donald Bayley. ([Hodges, Mathematical Logic and Cryptology](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html))

### Calculating machines, computer designs, and programs

- **1939:** *Zeta-Function Machine*. A blueprint and partially constructed special-purpose mechanical calculator designed with assistance from Donald McPhail; war stopped construction, no completed machine or components survive, and the unfinished project is the work. ([Turing’s zeta-function machine](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/zetamachine.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

- **1945–1946:** *Proposed Electronic Calculator*. Turing’s detailed design report for the Automatic Computing Engine, produced as an internal National Physical Laboratory typescript in early 1946. The eventual Pilot ACE was built by an NPL team after Turing’s departure and is a realization of the project, not a machine made solely by him. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **1946–1947:** *The Automatic Computing Engine*. A lecture series given by Turing and J. H. Wilkinson at the Ministry of Supply; what survives includes T. H. Marshall’s report and D. R. Hartree’s notes, so the documentary text is partly an auditor’s manifestation rather than a wholly authored Turing manuscript. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **20 February 1947:** *Lecture to the London Mathematical Society*. A typescript and delivered lecture explaining Turing’s computer design and anticipating machine intelligence. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **1948:** *Long-Division Program for the Manchester “Baby”*. A program submitted by Turing for the Small-Scale Experimental Machine; it is software rather than an article, and the computer itself was chiefly the work of Frederic Williams, Tom Kilburn, and their Manchester team. ([Manchester history](https://www.cs.manchester.ac.uk/about/history-and-heritage/); [Manchester Baby account](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_Baby))

- **c. 1948:** *Turochamp*. A chess-playing procedure devised with David Champernowne for a not-yet-available computer. It was worked through by hand but not successfully implemented on a contemporary machine, so it is an unfinished program-project rather than surviving executable software. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html); [Copeland and Proudfoot](https://sidoli.w.waseda.jp/Copeland_Proudfoot_2005_Turing_Computer.pdf))

- **1948–1950:** *Manchester Computer Programming System*. Turing devised operating conventions, a base-32 encoding scheme, programs, and a handbook for the Manchester machines, but he did not make the Manchester Baby, Mark 1, or Ferranti Mark 1 alone; those machines were collective institutional constructions. ([Manchester archive catalogue](https://www.alanturing.net/turing_archive/archive/index/manchesterindex.html); [Manchester history](https://www.cs.manchester.ac.uk/about/history-and-heritage/))

- **1950:** *Programmers’ Handbook for the Manchester Electronic Computer*. A 119-page protocol for using and programming the Manchester machine; the catalogue calls its copy undated, while the scholarly bibliography assigns it to 1950. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html); [Manchester archive catalogue](https://www.alanturing.net/turing_archive/archive/index/manchesterindex.html))

- **1950:** *Riemann Zeta-Function Program*. A Manchester-computer program applying Turing’s earlier numerical work; it helped produce the calculations subsequently reported in his 1953 paper. ([Hodges, Pure Mathematics](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html); [Manchester Computers](https://www.turing.org.uk/scrapbook/manmach.html))

- **1951:** *Local Programming Methods and Conventions*. A conference paper and practical protocol presented at the Manchester University Computer Inaugural Conference. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

### Machine intelligence: reports, papers, and performances

- **1948:** *Intelligent Machinery*. An internal National Physical Laboratory report describing unorganized machines, learning, and evolutionary search; it was not publicly printed until 1968. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **24 June 1949:** *Checking a Large Routine*. A conference paper presenting a method for reasoning about program correctness, later republished with corrections and annotations. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **27 October 1949:** *Discussion on the Mind and the Computing Machine*. A collaborative seminar performance involving Turing, Newman, Polanyi, Jefferson, Emmet, Young, and others; the surviving object is a rough record supplied by Wolfe Mays, not a sole-authored paper by Turing. ([Manchester archive catalogue](https://www.alanturing.net/turing_archive/archive/index/manchesterindex.html))

- **1950:** *Computing Machinery and Intelligence*. A philosophical and technical paper that replaces the question of whether machines think with the operational “imitation game”; it acts partly as a protocol because it specifies a test rather than merely describing one. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **1951:** *Can Digital Computers Think?* A BBC radio talk preserved as Turing’s typescript and subsequently published from the archive. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **1951:** *Intelligent Machinery: A Heretical Theory*. A Manchester talk surviving as a typescript; it was omitted from the original *Collected Works* but published in later scholarly collections. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **1952:** *Can Automatic Calculating Machines Be Said to Think?* A BBC radio discussion with M. H. A. Newman, Geoffrey Jefferson, and R. B. Braithwaite. It is a collaborative spoken event preserved through a BBC typescript, not a sole-authored essay. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

- **1953:** *Chess*. Turing’s subsection of the composite chapter “Digital Computers Applied to Games” in *Faster than Thought*. The chapter’s other sections and introductory matter have different or uncertain authorship and must not all be assigned to Turing. ([Hodges, Mechanical Intelligence](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html))

### Mathematical biology and morphogenesis

- **1952:** *The Chemical Basis of Morphogenesis*. Turing’s only published paper in mathematical biology, presenting a reaction–diffusion account of biological pattern formation. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html))

- **Before 1954:** *A Diffusion Reaction Theory of Morphogenesis in Plants*. An unpublished collaborative typescript by Turing and botanist C. W. Wardlaw; Wardlaw’s later commentary is a different work. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

- **c. 1952–1954:** *Morphogen Theory of Phyllotaxis, Part I: Geometrical and Descriptive Phyllotaxis*. An unfinished manuscript edited after Turing’s death by P. T. Saunders; the published arrangement is therefore partly an editorial construction. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html))

- **c. 1952–1954:** *Morphogen Theory of Phyllotaxis, Part II: Chemical Theory of Morphogenesis*. An unfinished continuation surviving among Turing’s last research papers. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html))

- **c. 1952–1954:** *Morphogen Theory of Phyllotaxis, Part III: A Solution of the Morphogenetical Equations for the Case of Spherical Symmetry*. An unfinished collaborative manuscript with B. Richards, later shaped for publication by an editor. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html))

- **c. 1952–1954:** *Outline of the Development of the Daisy*. An unfinished morphological study found among Turing’s papers and edited posthumously. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html))

- **1952–1954:** *Morphogenesis and Phyllotaxis Research Papers*. A pile of calculations, diagrams, programs, typescripts, and incomplete work, some produced with research students N. E. Hoskin and B. Richards. It resists being called one finished work: the later *Collected Works* sequence is an editorial manifestation of an interrupted research project. ([Hodges, Morphogenesis](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html); [Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0))

### Sources

- Andrew Hodges, [“Alan Turing’s Scientific Papers: Introduction and Overview”](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio.html), a bibliography organized around the four-volume *Collected Works of A. M. Turing*.

- Andrew Hodges, [“Mechanical Intelligence”](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio1.html), full listing corresponding to the computing and machine-intelligence volume of the *Collected Works*.

- Andrew Hodges, [“Pure Mathematics”](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio2.html), listing of published papers, unpublished manuscripts, and incompletely edited mathematical work.

- Andrew Hodges, [“Morphogenesis”](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio3.html), listing of the published paper and posthumously edited biological manuscripts.

- Andrew Hodges, [“Mathematical Logic plus Cryptology”](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/biblio4.html), listing of published and unpublished logical papers and then-released cryptographic reports.

- King’s College, Cambridge, [The Turing Digital Archive: unpublished manuscripts and drafts](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/browse-unpublished-manuscripts-and-drafts?page=0), the archival catalogue used to distinguish manuscripts, typescripts, continuations, and blueprints.

- King’s College, Cambridge, [The Turing Digital Archive: archive index](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/archive-index?page=0), describing the archive’s manuscript, publication, correspondence, personal, and Delilah divisions.

- King’s College, Cambridge, [Hanslope Park Delilah Papers](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/amt-h), catalogue description of the joint Turing–Bayley project, notebook, and working prototype.

- Jack Copeland, ed., [AlanTuring.net: Manchester Computer archive catalogue](https://www.alanturing.net/turing_archive/archive/index/manchesterindex.html), facsimile catalogue including the *Programmers’ Handbook* and the 1949 Manchester discussion.

- Andrew Hodges, [“Turing’s Zeta-Function Machine, 1939”](https://www.turing.org.uk/sources/zetamachine.html), account of its blueprint, partial construction, abandonment, and non-survival.

- Bletchley Park, [Alan Turing biographical record](https://bletchleypark.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/record_attachments/1800.pdf), used for Banburismus, Turingery, and the boundary between Turing’s methods and later cryptanalytic machinery.

- GCHQ, [Alan Turing](https://www.gchq.gov.uk/person/alan-turing) and [The Pyry Forest Meeting](https://www.gchq.gov.uk/information/the-pyry-forest-meeting), used for the qualified attribution of the British bombe and the Polish contribution on which its design was built.

- University of Manchester, [History and Heritage](https://www.cs.manchester.ac.uk/about/history-and-heritage/), used to distinguish Turing’s Manchester work from the machines made by the wider university team.

- B. Jack Copeland and Diane Proudfoot, [*Alan Turing’s Automatic Computing Engine*](https://sidoli.w.waseda.jp/Copeland_Proudfoot_2005_Turing_Computer.pdf), used for Turochamp and the distinction between Turing’s computer projects and later implementations.
