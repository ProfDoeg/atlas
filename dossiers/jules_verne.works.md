## Works by Jules Gabriel Verne

### *Voyages extraordinaires*: novels published during Verne’s lifetime

- **1863:** *Cinq Semaines en ballon*. Novel and inaugural Verne–Hetzel collaboration; later incorporated into the *Voyages extraordinaires*.
- **1864:** *Voyage au centre de la Terre*. Scientific-adventure novel, substantially enlarged for its 1867 edition.
- **1864–1866:** *Voyages et aventures du capitaine Hatteras*. Polar novel first serialized in two parts, *Les Anglais au pôle Nord* and *Le Désert de glace*.
- **1865:** *De la Terre à la Lune*. Lunar-project novel; the journey continues in *Autour de la Lune*.
- **1865–1868:** *Les Enfants du capitaine Grant*. Circumnavigation novel serialized and issued in three parts.
- **1869–1870:** *Vingt Mille Lieues sous les mers*. Submarine voyage novel published in two parts.
- **1870:** *Autour de la Lune*. Novel completing the lunar project begun in *De la Terre à la Lune*.
- **1871:** *Une ville flottante*. Transatlantic travel novel drawing upon Verne’s 1867 voyage aboard the *Great Eastern*.
- **1872:** *Aventures de trois Russes et de trois Anglais dans l’Afrique australe*. Geodetic expedition novel.
- **1873:** *Le Pays des fourrures*. Arctic exploration novel published in two parts.
- **1873:** *Le Tour du monde en quatre-vingts jours*. Circumnavigation-and-wager novel, subsequently remade as a spectacular stage work.
- **1874–1875:** *L’Île mystérieuse*. Robinsonade and sequel-like convergence of the Nemo and Grant narratives, published in three parts.
- **1875:** *Le Chancellor*. Maritime survival novel presented as the diary of passenger J. R. Kazallon.
- **1876:** *Michel Strogoff*. Historical adventure and journey novel set across imperial Russia.
- **1877:** *Hector Servadac*. Astronomical fantasy published in two parts.
- **1877:** *Les Indes noires*. Industrial and subterranean novel set in a Scottish coal mine.
- **1878:** *Un capitaine de quinze ans*. Maritime and African adventure novel published in two parts.
- **1879:** *Les Cinq Cents Millions de la Bégum*. Utopian-dystopian novel developed from Paschal Grousset’s manuscript *L’Héritage de Langevol*; Verne’s degree of transformation makes authorship collaborative and contractually complicated.
- **1879:** *Les Tribulations d’un Chinois en Chine*. Comic journey and pursuit novel.
- **1880:** *La Maison à vapeur*. Indian travel novel whose vehicle is an elephant-shaped steam machine.
- **1881:** *La Jangada*. Amazonian river-journey novel published in two parts.
- **1882:** *L’École des Robinsons*. Parodic Robinsonade.
- **1882:** *Le Rayon vert*. Scottish travel and courtship novel organized around an optical phenomenon.
- **1883:** *Kéraban-le-Têtu*. Comic circum-Black Sea journey novel, later adapted by Verne for the stage.
- **1884:** *L’Étoile du Sud*. South African diamond novel developed from a manuscript by Paschal Grousset; its authorship therefore resists treatment as Verne’s unaided creation.
- **1884:** *L’Archipel en feu*. Historical adventure novel set during the Greek War of Independence.
- **1885:** *Mathias Sandorf*. Mediterranean revenge-and-science novel conceived in homage to Alexandre Dumas.
- **1886:** *Un billet de loterie*. Norwegian journey and domestic-adventure novel.
- **1886:** *Robur-le-Conquérant*. Heavier-than-air aviation novel.
- **1887:** *Nord contre Sud*. American Civil War adventure novel.
- **1887:** *Le Chemin de France*. Historical journey novel set during the revolutionary wars.
- **1888:** *Deux Ans de vacances*. Island Robinsonade about a group of schoolboys, published in two parts.
- **1889:** *Famille-sans-nom*. Historical novel about the Lower Canada rebellion, published in two parts.
- **1889:** *Sans dessus dessous*. Satirical sequel to the Gun Club’s lunar project.
- **1890:** *César Cascabel*. Overland circus-family journey from North America to France.
- **1891:** *Mistress Branican*. Search-and-rescue circumnavigation novel.
- **1892:** *Le Château des Carpathes*. Gothic-technological novel.
- **1892–1893:** *Claudius Bombarnac*. Central Asian railway-journey novel presented as a reporter’s notebook.
- **1893:** *P’tit-Bonhomme*. Irish orphan narrative, also published as *Fils d’Irlande*.
- **1894:** *Mirifiques Aventures de maître Antifer*. Comic treasure-hunt and maritime journey novel.
- **1895:** *L’Île à hélice*. Satirical novel about a mobile artificial island.
- **1896:** *Face au drapeau*. Political-technological novel about a superweapon.
- **1896:** *Clovis Dardentor*. Comic Mediterranean travel novel.
- **1897:** *Le Sphinx des glaces*. Antarctic continuation of Edgar Allan Poe’s *The Narrative of Arthur Gordon Pym*.
- **1898:** *Le Superbe Orénoque*. River-expedition novel.
- **1899:** *Le Testament d’un excentrique*. Game-structured journey across the United States.
- **1900:** *Seconde Patrie*. Continuation and rewriting of Wyss’s *Swiss Family Robinson*.
- **1901:** *Le Village aérien*. African expedition novel, initially serialized as *La Grande Forêt*.
- **1901:** *Les Histoires de Jean-Marie Cabidoulin*. Maritime fantastic novel, also known as *Le Serpent de mer*.
- **1902:** *Les Frères Kip*. Maritime and judicial-error novel.
- **1903:** *Bourses de voyage*. School-and-sea voyage novel.
- **1904:** *Un drame en Livonie*. Detective and judicial novel written years before publication.
- **1904:** *Maître du monde*. Technological pursuit novel returning to Robur.
- **1905:** *L’Invasion de la mer*. Saharan engineering and colonial-conflict novel, the last *Voyage extraordinaire* issued while Verne lived.

### Novels and novel-projects outside the lifetime *Voyages extraordinaires*

- **1845–1848:** *Un prêtre en 1835*. Unfinished youthful novel-project; its posthumous title *Un prêtre en 1839* arose from a misreading of the manuscript date.
- **1847:** *Jédédias Jamet, ou l’Histoire d’une succession*. Unfinished comic novel-project, first published in 1993.
- **1860:** *Voyage en Angleterre et en Écosse*. Travel novel rejected by Hetzel and first published in 1989, initially under the editorial title *Voyage à reculons en Angleterre et en Écosse*.
- **1861:** *Joyeuses Misères de trois voyageurs en Scandinavie*. Unfinished autobiographical travel narrative written with Aristide Hignard and Émile Lorois; only the opening survives.
- **1863:** *Paris au XXe siècle*. Dystopian future novel rejected by Hetzel and first published in 1994.
- **c. 1869–1870:** *L’Oncle Robinson*. Abandoned Robinsonade and compositional precursor to *L’Île mystérieuse*, first published in 1991.
- **1885:** *L’Épave du “Cynthia”*. Novel formally associated with Verne but substantially written by André Laurie; Verne revised or authorized it, so it resists classification as his sole obra.
- **c. 1899–1900:** *Le Phare du bout du monde*. Novel manuscript published in 1905 only after substantial rewriting by Michel Verne; Jules’s original text appeared in 1999.
- **c. 1899–1900:** *En Magellanie*. Political novel manuscript transformed by Michel Verne into *Les Naufragés du “Jonathan”* (1909); the original was published in limited form in 1985 and commercially in 1987.
- **c. 1899–1900:** *Le Beau Danube jaune*. Danube journey novel transformed by Michel Verne into *Le Pilote du Danube* (1908); the original text appeared in 1988.
- **c. 1899–1900:** *Le Volcan d’or*. Klondike novel manuscript altered and expanded by Michel Verne for the 1906 publication; Jules’s text appeared in 1989.
- **c. 1901:** *La Chasse au météore*. Satirical astronomical novel altered by Michel Verne for publication in 1908; the original manuscript version appeared in 1986.
- **c. 1897–1898:** *Le Secret de Wilhelm Storitz*. Invisibility novel whose tense, ending, and other features were changed by Michel Verne for the 1910 edition; the manuscript text received a critical publication in 1985 and a later general edition.
- **1903–1904:** *Voyage d’études*. Unfinished African novel-project associated with Verne’s interest in Esperanto; Michel Verne used its surviving chapters as the beginning of *L’Étonnante Aventure de la mission Barsac*.
- **1919:** *L’Étonnante Aventure de la mission Barsac*. Posthumous novel completed and largely written by Michel Verne from Jules’s unfinished *Voyage d’études*; it is a manifestation of the family archive, not securely a Jules Verne work in its published totality.

### Short fiction and hybrid narratives

- **1851:** *Les Premiers Navires de la marine mexicaine*. Historical adventure tale later revised and retitled *Un drame au Mexique*.
- **1851:** *Un voyage en ballon*. Aeronautical tale later revised as *Un drame dans les airs*.
- **1852:** *Martin Paz*. Peruvian historical novella.
- **c. 1852:** *Pierre-Jean*. Unpublished-in-life story later reworked as *La Destinée de Jean Morénas* by Michel Verne.
- **1854:** *Maître Zacharius, ou l’Horloger qui avait perdu son âme*. Fantastic novella.
- **c. 1854:** *Le Siège de Rome*. Historical story first published in 1993.
- **1855:** *Un hivernage dans les glaces*. Polar rescue novella.
- **c. 1855:** *Le Mariage de M. Anselme des Tilleuls*. Comic story first published in 1982.
- **c. 1856:** *San Carlos*. Unpublished-in-life smuggling story, first published in 1993.
- **1864:** *Le Comte de Chanteleine*. Historical novella about the Vendée; sometimes catalogued as a novel.
- **1865–1871:** *Les Forceurs de blocus*. American Civil War maritime novella; its publication dating varies because serialization and later collection are distinct manifestations.
- **1872:** *Une fantaisie du docteur Ox*. Scientific-satirical novella.
- **c. 1870:** *Le Humbug*. Unfinished or lightly concluded American satire, first published from the manuscript in the twentieth century.
- **1875:** *Une ville idéale*. Satirical future-city narrative first delivered as an academic reading; it is simultaneously story and public performance.
- **1879:** *Les Révoltés de la Bounty*. Historical narrative based on research and a draft by Gabriel Marcel; Verne’s authorship is collaborative rather than exclusive.
- **1881–1882:** *Dix Heures en chasse*. Comic autobiographical hunting narrative, first read publicly and then published.
- **1884:** *Frritt-Flacc*. Fantastic story.
- **1887:** *Gil Braltar*. Satirical military story.
- **1886–1891:** *Aventures de la famille Raton*. Fairy tale, written earlier than its publication.
- **1889–1891:** *La Journée d’un journaliste américain en 2889*. Jules’s French reworking of Michel Verne’s English-language *In the Year 2889*; the underlying conception and authorship are disputed between father and son.
- **1892–1893:** *Monsieur Ré-Dièze et Mademoiselle Mi-Bémol*. Musical fantasy tale.
- **c. 1900:** *Edom*. Philosophical future-history story by Jules Verne, heavily rewritten and published by Michel Verne in 1910 as *L’Éternel Adam*; the original appeared only in 1991.

### Geographies, histories, criticism, memoir, and other prose

- **1857:** *Portraits d’artistes. XVIII*. Critical portrait of the composer Victor Massé.
- **1857:** *Salon de 1857*. Sequence of seven art-critical articles; the sequence, rather than a later book binding, is the made object.
- **1863:** *À propos du Géant*. Article on Nadar’s balloon and the future of heavier-than-air flight.
- **1864:** *Edgar Poe et ses œuvres*. Literary-critical study.
- **1866–1868:** *Géographie illustrée de la France et de ses colonies*. Geographical reference work begun by Théophile Lavallée and completed or revised by Verne after Lavallée’s death.
- **1868:** *Les Méridiens et le calendrier*. Geographical and calendrical essay.
- **1868–1880:** *Histoire générale des grands voyages et des grands voyageurs*. Multi-volume history of exploration made with research assistance from Gabriel Marcel; its parts appeared as *Découverte de la Terre*, *Les Grands Navigateurs du XVIIIe siècle*, and *Les Voyageurs du XIXe siècle*.
- **1873:** *Vingt-quatre minutes en ballon*. Account of a balloon ascent; sometimes printed as *24 minutes en ballon*.
- **1876:** *Note pour l’affaire J. Verne contre Pont-Jest*. Advocacy document in a plagiarism dispute: closer to a legal manifesto or intervention than to a literary work.
- **1881–1888:** *Le Nouveau Monde*. Geographical-historical reference project printed privately or experimentally but never commercially issued; planned illustrations survive, and its unpublished status makes it a project rather than a stable public book.
- **c. 1890:** *Souvenirs d’enfance et de jeunesse*. Autobiographical memoir, first manifested in English in 1891 as *The Story of My Boyhood* and first published in French in 1974.
- **1893:** *M. Victor Hugo*. Commemorative literary text.
- **1894:** *Le Comte de Paris*. Political-biographical article whose attribution and publication context require bibliographical caution.
- **1897:** *Théophile Gautier*. Literary remembrance.
- **1898:** *Amiens en l’an 2000*. Future-city text related to Verne’s civic imagination; title and generic placement vary in bibliographies.
- **1899:** *La Navigation sous-marine*. Short technological commentary rather than a self-standing book.

### Plays, libretti, scenarios, and theatrical projects

- **1846:** *La Conspiration des poudres*. Five-act verse drama; an unpublished youthful manuscript.
- **1847:** *Alexandre VI*. Five-act verse drama, unpublished during Verne’s life.
- **1847:** *Un drame sous Louis XV*. Five-act verse drama.
- **1847:** *Don Galaor*. Synopsis for a two-act play; the project survives without a completed drama.
- **1847:** *Le Quart d’heure de Rabelais*. One-act play.
- **1847:** *Une promenade en mer*. One-act vaudeville.
- **1849:** *Le Coq de bruyère*. Synopsis for a one-act play.
- **1849:** *On a souvent besoin d’un plus petit que soi*. Theatrical synopsis rather than a completed play.
- **1849:** *Abd’Allah*. Two-act vaudeville.
- **1850:** *La Mille et Deuxième Nuit*. One-act lyric play with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1850:** *Quiridine et Quidinerit*. Three-act “Italian comedy.”
- **1850:** *La Guimard*. Two-act play.
- **1850:** *Les Pailles rompues*. One-act verse comedy; Alexandre Dumas fils probably assisted, though the degree of collaboration remains uncertain.
- **1851:** *De Charybde en Scylla*. One-act verse comedy.
- **1852:** *La Tour de Montlhéry*. Five-act drama with a prologue, written with Charles Wallut.
- **1852:** *Les Châteaux en Californie, ou Pierre qui roule n’amasse pas mousse*. One-act prose comedy-proverb written with Pitre-Chevalier.
- **1853:** *Les Heureux du jour*. Five-act verse comedy.
- **1853:** *Un fils adoptif*. One-act comedy written with Charles Wallut.
- **1853:** *Le Colin-Maillard*. One-act opéra-comique libretto written with Michel Carré, with music by Aristide Hignard; the Gesamtkunstwerk is collaborative and cannot be reduced to Verne’s words.
- **1854:** *Guerre aux tyrans*. One-act verse play.
- **1855:** *Au bord de l’Adour*. One-act verse play.
- **1855:** *Les Compagnons de la Marjolaine*. One-act opéra-comique libretto written with Michel Carré, with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1855:** *Monna Lisa*. One-act verse comedy; later revised, read publicly in 1874, and published only posthumously.
- **1858:** *Le Page de Madame Malborough*. One-act operetta with music by Frédéric Barbier; attribution of the libretto to Verne is disputed.
- **1858:** *Monsieur de Chimpanzé*. One-act operetta libretto with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1860:** *L’Auberge des Ardennes*. One-act opéra-comique libretto written with Michel Carré, with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1861:** *Onze jours de siège*. Three-act prose comedy with Charles Wallut and probably Victorien Sardou; the precise division of authorship is uncertain.
- **1867:** *Les Sabines*. Unfinished opéra-bouffe project with Charles Wallut.
- **1871:** *Le Pôle Nord*. Synopsis for a large theatrical adaptation of *Hatteras*: six acts, a prologue, and fourteen tableaux.
- **1873:** *Un neveu d’Amérique, ou Les Deux Frontignac*. Three-act comedy with Charles Wallut; Édouard Cadol also participated in its theatrical development.
- **1874:** *Untitled second act*. Surviving act of an otherwise unidentified play; it resists being called a complete work because neither a stable title nor a whole drama survives.
- **1874:** *Le Tour du monde en quatre-vingts jours*. Spectacular stage adaptation with Adolphe d’Ennery; a theatrical machine of text, scenery, music, animals, and effects rather than merely a rewritten novel.
- **1877:** *Le Docteur Ox*. Opéra-bouffe by Jacques Offenbach with libretto credited to Philippe Gille and Arnold Mortier; Verne may have collaborated, but the nature and extent of his contribution remain uncertain.
- **1878:** *Les Enfants du capitaine Grant*. Spectacular stage adaptation with Adolphe d’Ennery.
- **1880:** *Michel Strogoff*. Spectacular stage adaptation with Adolphe d’Ennery.
- **1882:** *Voyage à travers l’impossible*. Three-act, seventeen-tableau fantasy play with Adolphe d’Ennery, combining figures and machines from several Verne narratives.
- **1883:** *Kéraban-le-Têtu*. Five-act stage adaptation written by Verne from his novel.
- **1887:** *Mathias Sandorf*. Stage adaptation with William Busnach; its exact textual relationship to Verne’s own work is collaborative and bibliographically complex.

### Poetry, songs, and musical manifestations

- **1842–c. 1855:** *First manuscript poetry notebook*. A corpus of youthful poems rather than a single designed book; dates attached to many individual pieces are conjectural, and the notebook includes love poems, satires, ballads, sonnets, acrostics, and occasional verse.
- **c. 1847–c. 1860:** *Second manuscript poetry notebook*. A further manuscript corpus containing revised and variant poems; because titles and versions recur, counting each heading as an independent obra would falsely stabilize a fluid working archive.
- **1847:** *À ma chère mère*. Early occasional poem; its dating is generally supplied conjecturally by editors.
- **1847:** *Hésitation*. Poem addressed to an unnamed young woman; approximate date.
- **1847:** *Paraphrase du Psaume 129*. Devotional paraphrase; approximate date.
- **1847:** *Damoiselle et damoiseau*. Ballad; approximate date.
- **1847:** *Acrostiche*. Love poem associated with Herminie Arnault de La Grossetière; approximate date.
- **1847:** *J’ai donc mal entendu…*. Lyric poem; approximate date.
- **1847:** *Le Cancan*. Sonnet; approximate date.
- **1847:** *L’Attente du simoun*. Ballad; approximate date.
- **1847:** *Jupiter et Léda*. Mythological poem; approximate date.
- **1847:** *La Vapeur*. Sonnet on modern technology; approximate date.
- **1847:** *L’Oméopathie*. Comic sonnet whose manuscript deliberately or mistakenly spells the title without an initial “h.”
- **1847:** *La Fille de l’air*. Poem addressed to Herminie; approximate date.
- **1847:** *L’Attente*. Villanelle later revised as *La Douce Attente* and incorporated into *Monna Lisa*; these are variant manifestations, not securely separate creations.
- **1847–1848:** *La Sixième Ville de France*. Satirical poem attacking Nantes; dating is approximate.
- **1847–1848:** *Madame C…*. Satirical poem directed at an unidentified or partially identified Nantes woman; its abbreviated title preserves that uncertainty.
- **1850:** *Les Gabiers*. Song text set to music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1850:** *Les Deux Troupeaux*. Song text with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1850:** *La Douce Attente*. Song or lyric manifestation of the earlier villanelle, with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1850:** *Notre étoile*. Song text with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1850:** *Chanson scandinave*. Song text with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1850:** *La Vapeur*. Song manifestation with music by Aristide Hignard, distinct in medium from the manuscript sonnet.
- **1857–1863:** *Rimes et Mélodies*. Two-part body of published songs pairing Verne’s words with music chiefly by Aristide Hignard; it is a collaborative musical-literary manifestation, not simply a poetry book.
- **1860:** *Chanson turque*. Song associated with *L’Auberge des Ardennes*, with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1860:** *Les Clairons de l’armée*. Song text with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1860:** *Souvenirs d’Écosse*. Song text with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1863:** *La Tankadère*. Song text with music by Aristide Hignard.
- **1886:** *Le Retour*. Poem or song written for a family occasion; surviving catalogues differ over the exact status of some late occasional verses.
- **1888:** *Aux marins du Saint-Michel*. Occasional maritime verse; its manuscript existence is more secure than any autonomous publication status.
- **1889:** *À la morphine*. Late poem responding to Verne’s medical treatment.
- **1897:** *À Alexandre Dumas fils*. Commemorative poem.
- **1842–1905:** *Poésies inédites / Poésies*. Posthumous editorial collections assembling approximately 184 known poems and songs from manuscripts and printed music. These collections are manifestations constructed by editors, not single books designed by Verne, and dates and boundaries remain provisional as manuscripts and variants are reassessed.

### Speeches, civic interventions, and performed texts

- **3 May 1867:** *Untitled report on the Atlantic crossing aboard the Great Eastern*. Oral report to the Société de géographie; mentioned in its proceedings but apparently unpublished.
- **20 March 1868:** *Untitled review of Cortambert and de Tranaltos’s Histoire de la guerre civile*. Oral intervention at the Société de géographie; no published text is known.
- **8 January 1875:** *Réponse au discours de réception de Gustave Dubois*. Academic response speech, printed in the *Journal d’Amiens*.
- **25 June 1875:** *Réponse au discours de réception de Gédéon Baril*. Academic response speech, printed in the *Journal d’Amiens*.
- **12 December 1875:** *Une ville idéale*. Public reading before the Académie d’Amiens; the same made text is also classifiable as speculative short fiction.
- **25 March 1881:** *Réponse au discours de réception de M. Pacaut*. Academic response speech.
- **1884:** *Untitled account of the Mediterranean cruise*. Oral communication to the Académie d’Amiens; never published and known through institutional record.
- **10 June 1892:** *Réponse au discours de réception d’Émile Ricquier*. Academic response speech.
- **23 June 1889:** *Discours lors de l’inauguration du Cirque municipal d’Amiens*. Civic ceremonial speech: an utterance acting to inaugurate a building, and therefore close to a manifesto or performative civic text.
- **1888–1903:** *Interventions au Conseil municipal d’Amiens*. Numerous recorded statements on theatre, streets, public works, fairs, and municipal administration. They resist treatment as individually authored literary works: most survive only as minutes, summaries, or institutional speech acts rather than fixed Verne texts.

### Correspondence and things not securely classifiable as works

- **1840s–1905:** *Correspondance de Jules Verne*. Thousands of private and professional letters, notably exchanges with his family, Pierre-Jules Hetzel, Louis-Jules Hetzel, Nadar, and collaborators. The correspondence is a documentary corpus rather than a single obra, and published editions remain selective.
- **1873–1905:** *Recorded interviews with Jules Verne*. These are creations of the interviewing journalists, though they preserve Verne’s spoken answers; they should not silently be counted as texts made solely by Verne.
- **Post-1905:** *Posthumous Hetzel novels credited to Jules Verne*. Their title pages constitute editorial manifestations, but several contain extensive rewriting or entirely new material by Michel Verne; each must be tested against the surviving manuscript rather than accepted as an undivided paternal work.
- **Various dates:** *Apocrypha attributed to Jules Verne*. Bibliographies record falsely attributed continuations, translations, adaptations, and anonymous works, but attribution alone does not make them things Verne made; they are therefore evidence about the Verne name, not additions to his oeuvre.
