## Works by Louis Vuitton

### Trunk forms and systems

- **1854–1892:** *Bespoke trunks, packing cases, and travelling wardrobes*. This is Louis Vuitton’s principal obra: an undocumented number of individually fitted objects made by the workshops under his name. No public catalogue raisonné or complete production register enumerates them, so the corpus resists item-by-item listing; surviving trunks are often workshop products rather than securely documented objects made by Louis’s own hands. [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf); [Pasols](https://books.google.com/books/about/Louis_Vuitton_The_Birth_of_Modern_Luxury.html?id=2JhbuAAACAAJ)

- **1854 or 1858–c. 1876:** *Malle plate en toile Gris Trianon* (flat-topped Trianon-grey canvas trunk). A lightweight wooden trunk covered with varnished grey canvas, made waterproof and stackable by its flat lid; sources variously date the Trianon covering to the firm’s opening in 1854 and the definitive flat trunk to 1858 or about 1860, so it is better understood as an evolving design than a single securely dated object. [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf); [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage)

- **c. 1865:** *Poupée, trousseau et sa malle* (doll, trousseau, and trunk). This surviving ensemble is preserved by the Musée des Arts Décoratifs, but only the fitted trunk is attributable to Vuitton; the doll and clothes are contents by other makers, making the whole a commissioned assemblage rather than a single-author work. [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf)

- **1869–1871:** *Malle Louis Vuitton* (untitled trunk in the Musée des Arts Décoratifs). A surviving workshop-made travelling trunk dated by the museum to a range rather than a single year; its individual designer or maker is not identified, so personal authorship by Louis cannot be claimed beyond the house attribution. [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf)

- **1875:** *Malle Wardrobe* (Wardrobe Trunk). A vertically opening portable wardrobe fitted for hanging and folded clothes; it is a type or project repeatedly manufactured and adapted, not one unique object. The house chronology dates its introduction to 1875, while some secondary histories give 1876. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage); [*100 Legendary Trunks*](https://uk.louisvuitton.com/eng-gb/products/louis-vuitton-100-legendary-trunks-002593/R07147)

- **1888:** *Malle Explorer en laiton* (Brass Explorer Trunk). A brass-clad trunk intended to withstand extreme climates; an example dated 1888 survives on the art market, but—as with other production pieces—the attribution is to the Vuitton workshop rather than demonstrably to Louis’s hands. [Christie’s](https://press.christies.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/4ffef1c80e3145ed3de38d162a0b6a85.pdf)

- **1888–1890:** *Malle Cabine en toile Damier* (Damier Cabin Trunk). A low trunk designed to fit beneath a ship-cabin bed; the cited surviving example is dated to a range and represents a workshop type, not a uniquely titled work. [Di Mano in Mano catalogue entry](https://fineart.dimanoinmano.co.uk/louis-vuitton-trunk-malle-cabine-paris-1888-1890/)

### Surface designs and visual identity

- **1854 or 1858:** *Toile Gris Trianon*. A varnished grey covering developed to lighten and waterproof Vuitton’s wooden trunks. It is a material system manifested across many objects, not an autonomous picture or textile work; the sources disagree over whether to date its introduction to 1854 or associate it with the 1858 flat trunk. [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf); [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage)

- **1872:** *Toile Rayée rouge et beige* (Red-and-beige Striped Canvas). A repeat-pattern trunk covering introduced in response to imitations of Gris Trianon. It is simultaneously a functional material, a brand-identification system, and a design disseminated through an indefinite series of trunks. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://fr.louisvuitton.com/fra-fr/magazine/articles/heritage)

- **1876:** *Toile Rayée beige et brune* (Beige-and-brown Striped Canvas). Secondary histories identify this as a later striped covering, but the current official chronology discusses only the red-and-beige design of 1872; its exact status as a separately authored Louis Vuitton design is consequently less secure. [LV Trunks historical chronology](https://www.lvtrunks.com/history); [Bonhams catalogue](https://images1.bonhams.com/original?src=Images%2Flive%2F2024-02%2F16/S-29725-0-1.pdf)

- **1888:** *Toile Damier*. A beige-and-brown checked canvas bearing “Marque L. Vuitton déposée,” the first Vuitton trunk covering to display the name externally. The house attributes its introduction to Louis, although manufacture was collective and Georges Vuitton was already active in the business; it is therefore both an authored design and a workshop identity system. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage); [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf)

### Patented mechanisms and collaborative works

- **1877:** *Protection for Vuitton trunk patterns and designs*. The house chronology records a patent filing protecting the firm’s patterns and designs. This is a legal protocol attached to a family of manufactured objects, not itself a trunk; the available summary does not supply a patent number or enough information to treat it as a separately titled invention. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage)

- **1885:** *Malle-lit* (Bed Trunk). A trunk unfolding into a camp bed, patented in 1885 and subsequently made in variants for travellers and explorers. It is often absorbed into “Louis Vuitton” as a brand history, but the house’s Spanish chronology expressly attributes the patent to Georges Vuitton; it should therefore not be silently assigned to Louis personally, though it was a manifestation of the family workshop during his lifetime. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology, Spanish edition](https://es.louisvuitton.com/esp-es/magazine/articulos/heritage); [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology, English edition](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage)

- **1886:** *Système de serrure à gorges multiples* (multi-tumbler trunk lock). A lock devised for Vuitton trunks and later capable of keying several pieces of luggage alike. Sources variously call it Georges Vuitton’s invention or a collaboration between Louis and Georges, so sole authorship is disputed and should not be resolved in Louis’s favor without the underlying patent record. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://us.louisvuitton.com/eng-us/magazine/articles/heritage); [Musée des Arts Décoratifs](https://madparis.fr/IMG/pdf/157-ADC-aide-visite-LVMJ-web01-fr.pdf)

### Exhibition manifestations

- **1867:** *Exhibit at the Exposition universelle de Paris*. Vuitton presented patented travelling trunks as an industrial-art display and received a bronze medal. The constituent objects are not completely identified in the accessible chronology, so this is best recorded as an exhibition manifestation rather than supplied with an invented work title. [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://en.louisvuitton.com/eng-nl/magazine/articles/heritage)

- **1889:** *Exhibit at the Exposition universelle de Paris*. The firm displayed its travel goods and advertised the resulting gold medal, but the accessible contemporary material does not provide a complete object list. This collective commercial presentation therefore resists classification as a single personally authored work. [Contemporary Louis Vuitton advertisement in the Library of Congress](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdc/00010162439/00010162439.pdf); [Louis Vuitton heritage chronology](https://en.louisvuitton.com/eng-nl/magazine/articles/heritage)
