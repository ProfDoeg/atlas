## Works by Nicolaus Copernicus

### Astronomical works and working papers

- **c. 1495 onward:** *The Uppsala Notebook*. A sixteen-folio working notebook bound into Copernicus’s copies of astronomical tables, containing observations, calculations, planetary parameters, and adapted latitude tables. Some entries are unquestionably his, but whether every hand and every table is Copernican remains disputed. ([Nicolaus Copernicus Gesamtausgabe, vol. IV](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [Rosińska, study of the notebook](https://kpbc.umk.pl/Content/44476/PDF/Copernicana_001_02.pdf))

- **before 1514:** *Nicolai Copernici de hypothesibus motuum caelestium a se constitutis commentariolus* (“Little Commentary”). A compact manuscript treatise announcing the project of a moving Earth and a Sun-centered planetary system; Copernicus circulated copies privately but did not publish it. Its composition is usually placed around 1510–14, not at a securely known single date. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/copernicus/); [Gesamtausgabe, vol. IV](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html))

- **1513–1516, now lost:** *Response on calendar reform*. A contribution requested by Paul of Middelburg for the Fifth Lateran Council’s calendar project. Its existence is documented, but the response itself does not survive; it therefore persists as evidence of a project rather than as an examinable text. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf); [Graßhoff and Fischer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01875))

- **3 June 1524:** *Letter to Bernard Wapowski on Johannes Werner’s “Motion of the Eighth Sphere”*. A letter in address but a mathematical-critical essay in function: Copernicus tests and rejects Werner’s treatment of precession and trepidation. The collected edition accordingly classifies it among the scientific *opera minora*, not ordinary correspondence. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. IV](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

- **undated:** *Table of the Risings and Settings of the Sun*. A practical astronomical table preserved among Copernicus’s minor scientific writings; no secure date should be supplied merely to make it fit a chronology. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. IV](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html))

- **c. 1515–1543:** *De revolutionibus libri sex* (“Six Books on the Revolutions”). Copernicus’s principal astronomical obra, repeatedly revised in manuscript and printed at Nuremberg in 1543 as *De revolutionibus orbium coelestium*. Andreas Osiander anonymously added an unsigned prefatory note without Copernicus’s authorization, so that paratext is not part of Copernicus’s authorship. ([LMU Nicolaus-Copernicus-Edition](https://www.gn.geschichte.uni-muenchen.de/forschung/datenbanken_editionen/copernic/cedition/index.html); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/copernicus/))

- **1542:** *De lateribus et angulis triangulorum* (“On the Sides and Angles of Triangles”). A separately printed mathematical text extracted from the still-unpublished *De revolutionibus*; Rheticus arranged its publication. It is therefore both a manifestation of part of the larger obra and a historically separate printed object, not an independently conceived second book. ([Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118565273.html?language=de); [Gesamtausgabe, vol. IV](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html))

- **various dates:** *Mathematical and astronomical annotations in books from Copernicus’s library*. These calculations, corrections, and glosses are surviving acts of scholarly making, but many resist the status of autonomous “works”; handwriting and attribution are disputed in some cases. The critical edition includes them precisely without pretending that every marginal mark is a titled composition. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. IV](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [Goddu, “Reflections on the Origin of Copernicus’s Cosmology”](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002182860603700104))

### Translation and verse

- **1509:** *Theophylacti scholastici Simocati epistolae morales, rurales et amatoriae, interpretatione latina*. Copernicus’s Latin translation of Theophylactus Simocatta’s Greek moral, rustic, and amatory letters, printed at Kraków and dedicated to his uncle Lucas Watzenrode. The letters are Theophylactus’s creations; Copernicus made this Latin translation and its publication. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. V](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [LMU Nicolaus-Copernicus-Edition](https://www.gn.geschichte.uni-muenchen.de/forschung/datenbanken_editionen/copernic/cedition/index.html))

- **1512:** *Greek epigram for Johannes Dantiscus’s Epithalamium*. A brief occasional poem contributed to Dantiscus’s publication celebrating the marriage of King Sigismund I and Barbara Zápolya. It is a poem, not a book, and survives as a component of another author’s printed work. ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2024/entries/copernicus/))

### Monetary-reform projects and protocols

- **15 August 1517:** *Meditata de estimatione monetae*. The first surviving formulation of Copernicus’s monetary-reform project: an analytical memorandum intended to guide policy, and thus partly a protocol rather than merely a descriptive essay. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. V](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [Sommerfeld edition bibliographical record](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnzdenkschrift))

- **1519:** *Modus cudendi monetam* / *Tractatus de monetis*. A revised treatment of the currency problem, associated with a German-language presentation for the Prussian estates. Sources disagree over whether the surviving Latin and German forms should be counted as separate works, translations, or stages of the same evolving project. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. V](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [Akademia Kopernikańska](https://akademiakopernikanska.gov.pl/en/on-the-minting-of-coin-a-treatise-by-nicolaus-copernicus/))

- **1522:** *De estimatione monetae*. A further redaction prepared for deliberations at the Prussian diet in Grudziądz. It is best understood as another manifestation of the continuing monetary-reform project, not as an unrelated treatise. ([Gumowski, study of the monetary writings](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/files/Komunikaty_Mazursko-Warminskie/Komunikaty_Mazursko-Warminskie-r1968-t-n4/Komunikaty_Mazursko-Warminskie-r1968-t-n4-s621-660/Komunikaty_Mazursko-Warminskie-r1968-t-n4-s621-660.pdf))

- **1526–1528:** *Monete cudende ratio* (“On the Minting of Coin”). The fullest redaction of Copernicus’s monetary analysis and reform proposals; sources variously date the principal version to 1526 or its final revision to 1528. The fluctuation reflects a developing dossier rather than a securely fixed, once-for-all publication date. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. V](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [Akademia Kopernikańska](https://akademiakopernikanska.gov.pl/en/on-the-minting-of-coin-a-treatise-by-nicolaus-copernicus/))

- **1526:** *Letter of the Prussian councillors to Jost Ludwig Dietz concerning restoration of the Prussian currency*. An official collective instrument closely connected with Copernicus’s proposals. His participation is recognized in the monetary dossier, but its corporate voice prevents uncomplicated classification as a solely authored work. ([Sommerfeld edition bibliographical record](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnzdenkschrift))

- **19 April 1528:** *Letter to Felix Reich on the currency memorandum*. Formally correspondence, substantively an explanatory supplement to *Monete cudende ratio*. The scholarly letter edition retains it because it also belongs to the institutional debate over monetary reform. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

### Administrative records and correspondence

- **1516–1521:** *Locationes mansorum desertorum* entries (“Leases of Abandoned Holdings”). Administrative records made during Copernicus’s governance of the Warmian chapter’s estates, documenting the resettlement and leasing of deserted farms. The manuscript is an institutional register rather than a literary work: Copernicus made entries within it, not necessarily the codex as a whole. ([Copernican Trail documentation](https://szlakkopernikowski.pl/images/pdf/Map_Copernicus_trail_ENG_2016.pdf); [Nicolaus Copernicus Gesamtausgabe overview](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html))

- **c. 1520–1521:** *Wartime and defensive correspondence from Olsztyn*. Letters, requisitions, reports, and instructions produced while Copernicus administered and helped defend chapter territory during the war with the Teutonic Order. Some chapter letters were written or conceived by him, some are merely in a corporate voice, and several attributions based on handwriting remain uncertain; they resist conversion into a tidy list of individually authored “works.” ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf); [LMU edition bibliography](https://www.gn.geschichte.uni-muenchen.de/personen/apl__professoren/kuehne/publikationen/index.html))

- **late 1520, lost:** *Letter to Alexander Scultetus requesting firearms*. A wartime instruction intercepted by Teutonic forces; only indirect testimony of it survives. It is a documented act and protocol, but no recoverable textual object remains. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

- **18 July 1534, lost:** *Letter to Achatius von Zehmen*. A lost letter known only through surviving evidence and fragmentary information about its contents. Its absence makes it a documented communication rather than an extant work. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

- **1 July 1540, lost:** *Letter to Andreas Osiander*. A lost communication concerning presentation of the astronomical theory; only fragmentary evidence of its contents survives. It belongs to the publication project of *De revolutionibus*, but cannot be reconstructed as a complete text. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

- **sixteenth century, chiefly 1520s–1540s:** *Surviving outgoing correspondence*. The critical *Documenta Copernicana* identifies a core of twenty letters or notices of letters written by or attributed to Copernicus, alongside twelve surviving letters addressed to him. The corpus is not one authored obra: it combines private messages, learned essays, administrative acts, collective chapter documents, and lost items known only by report. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

### Medical and pharmaceutical texts

- **undated:** *Medical prescriptions and pharmaceutical recipes*. Brief practical formulas surviving in manuscripts and books associated with Copernicus’s medical activity. They are protocols for treatment rather than a medical treatise, and the critical edition distinguishes authenticated writing from doubtful annotations. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. V](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html); [LMU Nicolaus-Copernicus-Edition](https://www.gn.geschichte.uni-muenchen.de/forschung/datenbanken_editionen/copernic/cedition/index.html))

- **various dates:** *Medical annotations in books from Copernicus’s library*. Marginal comments, corrections, and recipe notes gathered by the complete edition as evidence of medical reading and practice. Individual notes resist being called works, and not every annotation in a formerly Copernican volume is necessarily in his hand. ([Gesamtausgabe, vol. V](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/serial/ncg-b/html))

### Maps and astronomical apparatus

- **1509–1510, lost and uncertain:** *Map of Warmia and western Prussia*. Contemporary correspondence says that Copernicus was charged with making a regional map and that another canon sought access to it for copying. The object is lost, and both its exact extent and Copernicus’s sole authorship remain uncertain. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf); [History of Cartography](https://press.uchicago.edu/books/hoc/HOC_V3_Pt2/HOC_VOLUME3_Part2_chapter61.pdf))

- **1519, lost:** *Map of the southwestern Vistula Lagoon*. A map made for a boundary dispute involving the bishopric of Warmia and Elbląg. It is known through documentation rather than a surviving sheet, and accounts differ over its precise coverage and whether collaborators participated. ([Olsztyn exhibition publication](https://zamkigotyckie.org.pl/archiwalne/orgpl/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Kopernik_Kant_wer_ang.pdf); [History of Cartography](https://press.uchicago.edu/books/hoc/HOC_V3_Pt2/HOC_VOLUME3_Part2_chapter61.pdf))

- **c. 1517:** *Astronomical observing panel at Olsztyn Castle*. A large plastered wall panel carrying geometrically constructed solar day-lines and hour-lines, originally used with a reflecting mirror. Traditionally called a sundial, it is now interpreted as a heliograph for measuring solar longitude; because it combines architecture, diagram, instrument, inscription, and observational protocol, it approaches a scientific Gesamtkunstwerk more closely than a “text.” ([Graßhoff and Fischer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01875))

- **before 1543, lost:** *Quadrant, parallactic instrument, and armillary sphere*. Copernicus reports using instruments of these types, but the evidence does not establish that he personally manufactured them rather than commissioned, acquired, or assembled them. They therefore resist inclusion as securely “made by Copernicus,” although his observational project depended upon them. ([Graßhoff and Fischer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01875))

### Rejected or seriously disputed attributions

- **1 January 1514, probably spurious:** *Letter to “the noble youth Georgius”*. Included by the critical correspondence edition to document the attribution, but judged overwhelmingly inauthentic: its references to a book supposedly withheld for thirty-six years and dedicated to Paul III are incompatible with the ostensible date. It should not silently be counted as a Copernican work. ([Documenta Copernicana: Briefe](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9783050047980_A21712719/preview-9783050047980_A21712719.pdf))

- **formerly assigned to Copernicus:** *Septem sidera*. A religious poem-cycle once printed under Copernicus’s name but now attributed to an early-seventeenth-century Jesuit. It is an important false manifestation of a Copernican oeuvre, not something Copernicus made. ([Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118565273.html?language=de))

- **22 July 1516, rejected attribution:** *Warmian chapter’s letter to the king of Poland*. Earlier scholarship sometimes assigned the document to Copernicus, but handwriting analysis rejects that assignment. It belongs to his administrative environment, not securely to his production. ([Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118565273.html?language=de))

- **July 1521, rejected attribution:** *Warmian chapter’s grievance against the Teutonic Order*. Another institutional document formerly credited to Copernicus but rejected on palaeographical grounds. Its corporate setting and subject are insufficient evidence of authorship. ([Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/gnd118565273.html?language=de))
