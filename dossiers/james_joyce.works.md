## Works by James Augustine Aloysius Joyce

### Lost juvenilia and abandoned projects

- **1891:** *Et Tu, Healy*. A political poem on the death of Charles Stewart Parnell, privately printed by Joyce’s father; no copy is known to survive, so its text and exact material form remain uncertain. [James Joyce Centre](https://jamesjoyce.ie/timeline/)

- **c. 1898:** *Silhouettes*. A lost collection of prose sketches or “prose poems.” Its status rests on biographical and manuscript evidence rather than a surviving finished book. [Collected Epiphanies of James Joyce](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/AA/00/09/69/78/00001/9780813070674_OA.pdf)

- **c. 1898–99:** *Moods*. A lost manuscript collection of poems, not a published book. [James Joyce Centre](https://jamesjoyce.ie/timeline/)

- **c. 1899–1901:** *Shine and Dark*. A lost manuscript collection of poems; neither its contents nor its final intended shape can now be established. [James Joyce Centre](https://jamesjoyce.ie/timeline/)

- **1900:** *A Brilliant Career*. A four-act play dedicated by Joyce “to my own soul”; rejected by William Archer and later destroyed, it survives only as a documented project. [Cambridge Introduction to James Joyce](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805215/49653/excerpt/9780521549653_excerpt.pdf)

- **c. 1900:** *Dream Stuff*. A lost dramatic project known from the record of Joyce’s early writing; it resists classification as a completed play because no recoverable text establishes its extent. [Collected Epiphanies of James Joyce](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/AA/00/09/69/78/00001/9780813070674_OA.pdf)

### Prose fiction and book-length projects

- **1900–04:** *Epiphanies*. A projected sequence of brief dramatic and lyrical prose manifestations rather than a conventional book: Joyce is thought to have written at least seventy, while forty survive. Their boundaries, numbering and intended completeness remain editorial questions. [Collected Epiphanies of James Joyce](https://ufdcimages.uflib.ufl.edu/AA/00/09/69/78/00001/9780813070674_OA.pdf)

- **1904:** *A Portrait of the Artist*. A short autobiographical essay-story submitted to *Dana* and rejected; it is an independent textual experiment as well as an ancestor of *Stephen Hero* and *A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man*. [University at Buffalo Joyce catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog.html)

- **1904–06:** *Stephen Hero*. An abandoned autobiographical novel project, originally conceived on a much larger scale; Joyce destroyed or lost much of it, and the surviving manuscript fragment was published posthumously in 1944. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1904–07; published 1914:** *Dubliners*. An obra comprising fifteen interdependent short stories; repeated censorship and publishing failures made its decade-long struggle into part of the object’s history. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1904:** *The Sisters*. A short story first printed under the name Stephen Daedalus and extensively rewritten for *Dubliners*; the newspaper and book texts are substantially different manifestations. [James Joyce Centre timeline](https://jamesjoyce.ie/timeline/)

- **1904:** *Eveline*. A short story first published under the name Stephen Daedalus and later revised for *Dubliners*. [James Joyce Centre timeline](https://jamesjoyce.ie/timeline/)

- **1904:** *After the Race*. A short story first published under the name Stephen Daedalus and subsequently revised for *Dubliners*. [James Joyce Centre timeline](https://jamesjoyce.ie/timeline/)

- **1905:** *An Encounter*. A short story belonging to the designed sequence later called *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *Araby*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *The Boarding House*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *A Little Cloud*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *Counterparts*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *Clay*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *A Painful Case*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905:** *Ivy Day in the Committee Room*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905–06:** *Two Gallants*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1905–06:** *A Mother*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1906:** *Grace*. A short story belonging to *Dubliners*. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1907:** *The Dead*. The final and longest story of *Dubliners*, written after the other fourteen and functioning almost as a novella-sized coda to the sequence. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1907–14; serialized 1914–15; book 1916:** *A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man*. A novel made by radically recomposing, rather than merely completing, the abandoned *Stephen Hero* project. Its serial and book appearances are manifestations of the same obra. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **c. 1911–14; published 1968:** *Giacomo Joyce*. An untitled-in-the-manuscript sequence of fifty fragments written in Trieste; “Giacomo Joyce” is its editorially accepted title, and whether the fair-copy notebook constitutes a completed work remains disputed. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1914–21; serialized 1918–20; book 1922:** *Ulysses*. A novel, typographic construction and encyclopedic city-object organized around one Dublin day. Its serial instalments, fair copy, proofs and competing printed texts are distinct manifestations of an unusually unstable textual obra. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1923:** *Finn’s Hotel*. A name applied posthumously by Danis Rose to a group of “epiclets” or sketches at the threshold of *Finnegans Wake*. Rose and John O’Hanlon argue that it was a distinct abandoned project; Terence Killeen and other scholars contend that Joyce never constituted the pieces as a separate work, so its resistance to work-status is essential to the entry. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/f/ff/fdra/fhda.htm), [Irish Times account of the dispute](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/publication-of-james-joyce-collection-divides-scholars-1.1430440)

- **1923–39; book 1939:** *Finnegans Wake*. Joyce’s final novel—or, more adequately, a multilingual verbal Gesamtkunstwerk—developed publicly for sixteen years under the provisional designation *Work in Progress*. Drafts, notebooks, transition texts, magazine episodes and limited-edition fragments are stages and manifestations, not automatically independent works. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1936:** *The Cat and the Devil*. An illustrated children’s tale first made as a letter to Joyce’s grandson Stephen and published posthumously in 1964; its epistolary origin complicates the boundary between correspondence and fiction. [International James Joyce Foundation bibliography](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1936:** *The Cats of Copenhagen*. A children’s tale written in a letter to Stephen Joyce and published posthumously in 2012. Like *The Cat and the Devil*, it became a book only through later editorial extraction from correspondence. [James Joyce Centre resources](https://jamesjoyce.ie/resources/)

### Drama

- **1914–15; published 1918:** *Exiles*. Joyce’s only surviving complete play, a three-act prose drama; its performance history and surviving drafts do not convert it into a novelistic work despite its close thematic relation to his fiction. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/), [University at Buffalo Joyce catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog.html)

### Poetry and verse sequences

- **1904–05:** *The Holy Office*. A satirical verse manifesto attacking the Dublin literary establishment; privately circulated as a broadside or pamphlet and therefore an intervention as much as a poem. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1907:** *Chamber Music*. A deliberately ordered sequence of thirty-six short lyrics, many conceived in relation to song; it is a poetic cycle rather than thirty-six separately titled books. [James Joyce Centre](https://jamesjoyce.ie/work/)

- **1912:** *Gas from a Burner*. A satirical broadside prompted by the destruction of the Dublin sheets of *Dubliners*; it acts as polemic, curse and bibliographical record as well as poem. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **1904–24; published 1927:** *Pomes Penyeach*. A designed collection of thirteen titled poems, offered as twelve purchasable “pomes” plus a bonus “tilly”; the book’s pricing pun is part of the made object. [Pomes Penyeach bibliography](https://jamesjoycebibliography.wordpress.com/the-prototype/pomes-penyeach-1927/)

- **1904; published 1927:** *Tilly*. The bonus or “tilly” poem opening *Pomes Penyeach*, originally associated with the title “Cabra.” [Pomes Penyeach bibliography](https://jamesjoycebibliography.wordpress.com/the-prototype/pomes-penyeach-1927/)

- **1904; published 1927:** *Watching the Needleboats at San Sabba*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1913; published 1927:** *A Flower Given to My Daughter*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1913; published 1927:** *She Weeps over Rahoon*. An elegiac lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1914; published 1927:** *Tutto è sciolto*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1914; published 1927:** *On the Beach at Fontana*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1914; published 1927:** *Simples*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1915; published 1927:** *Flood*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1915; published 1927:** *Nightpiece*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1916; published 1927:** *Alone*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1917; published 1927:** *A Memory of the Players in a Mirror at Midnight*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*; manuscript evidence records its replacement of an earlier poem in the planned collection. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1918; published 1927:** *Bahnhofstrasse*. A lyric included in *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1924; published 1927:** *A Prayer*. The closing lyric of *Pomes Penyeach*. [University at Buffalo verse catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog/iv-verses/iva1.html)

- **1932; published 1933:** *Ecce Puer*. A poem joining the birth of Joyce’s grandson to the death of his father. [James Joyce Centre](https://jamesjoyce.ie/work/)

- **1936:** *Collected Poems*. An author-supervised collection bringing together *Chamber Music*, *Pomes Penyeach* and selected occasional verse; it is principally a retrospective manifestation, not a wholly new poetic work. [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

### Translation projects

- **1901:** *Vor Sonnenaufgang / Before Sunrise*. Joyce’s English translation of Gerhart Hauptmann’s play, preserved in manuscript but not published by him; Joyce later judged his early German translations harshly. [Yale Modernism Lab](https://campuspress.yale.edu/modernismlab/vor-sonnenaufgang/)

- **1901:** *Michael Kramer*. Joyce’s English translation of Hauptmann’s play; it survives as a documented manuscript project and is echoed through the fictional translation attributed to Mr Duffy in “A Painful Case.” [Yale Modernism Lab](https://campuspress.yale.edu/modernismlab/vor-sonnenaufgang/)

- **1909:** *Riders to the Sea*. An Italian translation of J. M. Synge’s play made collaboratively by Joyce and Nicolò Vidacovich; because responsibility was shared, it should not be silently converted into a sole-authored Joyce work. [Slocum and Cahoon bibliography description](https://rbms.info/scf/?scf_entries=slocum-j-j-bibliography-of-james-joyce-1882-1941)

- **1913:** *The Countess Cathleen*. An Italian translation of W. B. Yeats’s play made collaboratively by Joyce and Nicolò Vidacovich; its collaborative authorship is part of the object’s identity. [Slocum and Cahoon bibliography description](https://rbms.info/scf/?scf_entries=slocum-j-j-bibliography-of-james-joyce-1882-1941)

- **1937–38:** *Anna Livia Plurabelle*. An Italian translation of part of *Work in Progress*, made by Joyce with Nino Frank; it is simultaneously translation, radical rewriting and a new multilingual manifestation of material later incorporated into *Finnegans Wake*. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

### Manifestations of *Work in Progress*

- **1924:** *From Work in Progress*. Early fragments printed in periodicals under the project designation *Work in Progress*; these are public manifestations of the developing *Finnegans Wake*, not securely independent works. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1928:** *Anna Livia Plurabelle*. A separately issued version of the river episode from *Work in Progress*, subsequently revised for *Finnegans Wake*. Its fine-press objecthood is real, but its textual independence is provisional. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1929:** *Tales Told of Shem and Shaun*. A book-form selection from *Work in Progress* containing three developing episodes; it is a designed interim manifestation rather than another novel. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1930:** *Haveth Childers Everywhere*. A separately published fragment from *Work in Progress*, later transformed within *Finnegans Wake*. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1932:** *Two Tales of Shem and Shaun*. A further selected manifestation of *Work in Progress*, assembled from already developing material rather than conceived as a separate narrative obra. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1934:** *The Mime of Mick, Nick and the Maggies*. A separately printed “fragment from Work in Progress,” later revised as part of *Finnegans Wake*. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

- **1937:** *Storiella as She Is Syung*. A separately printed fragment from *Work in Progress*, functioning as an interim performance of material later fixed differently in *Finnegans Wake*. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/)

### Correspondence, notebooks and compositional bodies

- **c. 1900–41:** *Letters and postcards*. Thousands of private, familial, erotic, commercial and literary communications survive. They constitute a body of writing made by Joyce, but not a single author-designed obra; the posthumous *Letters* and *Selected Letters* are editorial collections, not books Joyce made. [University at Buffalo Joyce catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog.html), [International James Joyce Foundation](https://joycefoundation.utulsa.edu/resource-center/bibliography-works-by-james-joyce/)

- **c. 1903–39:** *Working notebooks, notesheets, drafts, fair copies, typescripts and proofs*. These are material acts in the making of Joyce’s works and sometimes contain otherwise independent fragments, but the archive should not be falsely atomized into thousands of titled works. The James Joyce Digital Archive treats the documents as linked compositional levels for *Ulysses* and *Finnegans Wake*. [James Joyce Digital Archive](https://jjda.ie/), [University at Buffalo Joyce catalogue](https://library.buffalo.edu/jamesjoyce/catalog.html)

- **c. 1923–39; published posthumously 1961:** *Scribbledehobble*. The editorial title given to an early workbook for *Finnegans Wake*; it is an ur-workbook or compositional object, not a finished book authorized under that title by Joyce. [James Joyce Digital Archive notebook description](https://jjda.ie/f/ff/fdra/fnbdetails.htm)
