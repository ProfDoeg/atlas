## Works by Anton Fugger

### Legal and testamentary texts

- **23 May 1517:** *Testament*. Anton Fugger’s earliest surviving will is a legal instrument rather than a literary work; it is separately catalogued in the collected scholarly edition of the sixteenth-century Fugger testaments. [Edition contents](https://www.eurobuch.de/buch/isbn/9783874373241.html)

- **22 March 1550:** *Testament*. This later will reorganized the transmission of property, commercial interests, religious obligations, and charitable provision; its “authorship” is juridical and collaborative, since a notarial instrument embodies Fugger’s decisions without necessarily preserving his own wording. [Edition description](https://www.konrad-verlag.de/programm/titel/die-fuggertestamente-des-16-jahrhunderts-1.html)

- **26 October 1554:** *First codicil*. A supplement to the 1550 testament, preserved and edited as a distinct legal text rather than an independent literary composition. [Edition contents](https://www.eurobuch.de/buch/isbn/9783874373241.html)

- **12 March 1556:** *Ricordo*. An instructional memorandum associated with Fugger’s testamentary settlement; it is best classified as a family and succession protocol, although the edition’s title does not establish that every word is autograph. [Edition contents](https://www.eurobuch.de/buch/isbn/9783874373241.html)

- **11 July 1560:** *Second codicil*. A further testamentary amendment, juridically effective but dependent upon the larger testamentary complex. [Edition contents](https://www.eurobuch.de/buch/isbn/9783874373241.html)

- **26 July 1560:** *Third codicil*. Fugger’s final codicil ordered fifty-seven legacies totaling 10,900 florins and an additional 1,000 florins in alms; an archival presentation calls it his “third will,” while the collected edition classifies it as the third codicil, so the nomenclature is not uniform. [Paper in Motion, Fugger Archive FA 19.2](https://www.paperinmotion.org/paper/last-will-and-testament-of-anton-fugger/)

### Institutional protocols and settlements

- **July 1548:** *Renewed foundation charter for the Fugger foundations*. This protocol reconfirmed Jakob Fugger’s 1521 settlement, specified the male family representatives responsible for governance, and provided alternatives should Augsburg’s confessional circumstances threaten the institutions; Anton therefore reorganized an inherited obra rather than creating it wholly anew. [Historisches Lexikon Bayerns](https://www.historisches-lexikon-bayerns.de/Lexikon/Artikel_45825)

- **November 1548:** *Fugger family entail (Familienfideikommiss)*. A dynastic property protocol intended to preserve the family estate and regulate inheritance; the scholarly edition stresses that its rules remained consequential into the twentieth century. [Anton H. Konrad Verlag](https://www.konrad-verlag.de/programm/titel/die-fuggertestamente-des-16-jahrhunderts-1.html)

### Charitable and educational foundations

- **1548:** *Holz- und Blatternhaus Foundation*. A medical foundation that converted an already operating treatment house into an endowed institution supporting buildings, medicines, physicians, nursing, and aftercare; it is an institutional obra, not simply the surviving house. [Fugger Foundation Administration](https://www.fugger.de/en/history/a-versatile-institution-with-500-years-of-history)

- **1548:** *Waltenhausen Hospital Foundation*. An endowed hospital for fifty beneficiaries near Babenhausen, created under Fugger in the same year as his general reorganization of the family foundations. [Fugger Foundation Administration](https://www.fugger.de/stiftungen/staerkung-fuer-die-stiftungen)

- **1554:** *Latin school at Babenhausen*. An educational foundation established by Fugger; the available municipal account identifies the foundation and year but not a stable contemporary title for the institution. [Markt Babenhausen](https://www.babenhausen-schwaben.de/unsere-gemeinde/geschichte-und-tradition/babenhausen-historisch)

- **1560, testamentary:** *Schneidhaus Foundation*. A project for free surgical treatment of poor patients, ordered through Fugger’s last dispositions and put into operation after his death; because the institution appeared posthumously, it is simultaneously his project and his executors’ realization. [Fugger Foundation Administration](https://www.fugger.de/en/history/a-versatile-institution-with-500-years-of-history)

### Commissioned buildings and architectural complexes

- **1535–1546:** *Oberndorf moated castle*. A four-winged residence commissioned by Anton Fugger; later destruction and rebuilding mean that the present two-wing complex is not identical with his architectural obra. [Gemeinde Oberndorf am Lech](https://www.oberndorf-am-lech.de/freizeit-und-kultur/tourismus-and-kultur/fuggerschloss)

- **1537–1539:** *Pfleghaus, Donauwörth* (later called the Fuggerhaus). A representative Renaissance administrative residence commissioned after Fugger obtained the imperial bailiwick, with plans attributed to master builder Quirin Knoll; subsequent wartime damage and restoration complicate identification of the surviving building with the sixteenth-century work. [City-planning history of Donauwörth](https://www.donauwoerth.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Leben_in_Donauwoerth/Bauen_und_Wohnen/Stadtplanung/231026_ISEK_Donauwoerth_Bericht_gesamt.pdf)

- **1539–1546:** *First Fugger building campaign at Schloss Babenhausen*. After acquiring the lordship, Fugger had the older Rechberg castle radically remodeled and new wings erected, probably under Quirin Knoll; this is a layered architectural project rather than a building attributable to one maker or moment. Later campaigns by Fugger’s sons further altered the complex. [Dehio, Schloss Babenhausen](https://de.dehio.org/ausstattungsobjekt/babenhausen-schloss-denkmal?facet%5Bobject_types_browsable%5D%5Bindex%5D=G&filter%5Bcontent_type%5D%5B0%5D=Ausstattungsobjekt&filter%5Bcontent_type%5D%5B1%5D=Ortsgeschichte&filter%5Blocations%5D%5B0%5D=BABENHAUSEN%2C+Kr.+Unterallg%C3%A4u&filter%5Bvolume%5D%5B0%5D=Hamburg%2C+Schleswig-Holstein&filter%5Bvolume%5D%5B1%5D=Bayern+III.+Schwaben&filter%5Bvolume%5D%5B2%5D=Sachsen+I.+Regierungsbezirk+Dresden&position=60&start=56&widget%5Bobject_types_browsable_facet%5D%5Bshow%5D=1&widget%5Bpersons_browsable_facet%5D%5Bshow%5D=1)

- **1550–1560:** *Schloss Glött*. A three-wing castle attributed in the Bavarian monument inventory to the builder Veit Widenmann during Fugger’s ownership and patronage; a 1932 enlargement and added east wing prevent the present fabric from being treated as an untouched Anton Fugger work. [Bavarian monument-list transcription](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_der_Baudenkm%C3%A4ler_in_Gl%C3%B6tt)

### Commercial and territorial projects

- **1525–1560:** *Direction and reorientation of the Fugger company*. This was not a newly created, individually authored work: Fugger inherited leadership of an existing partnership and conducted it through associates, factors, accountants, and relatives. It nevertheless constitutes a long-duration collective project encompassing finance, mining, trade, land acquisition, and planned ventures reaching from Hungary and the Balkans to Venice and the Levant. [Neue Deutsche Biographie](https://www.deutsche-biographie.de/sfz17997.html)

- **1532, unfinished:** *Proposed colonial lease with the Spanish Crown*. A reportedly signature-ready agreement between Fugger and the Crown never became an operating colonial enterprise; it therefore resists classification as a completed work and belongs instead among unrealized commercial-political projects. [History of the Fugger von der Lilie](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugger_von_der_Lilie)

- **1538–1560:** *Babenhausen territorial complex*. Fugger assembled the lordship through purchase and subsequent acquisitions and made it the center of a new family line; this was a territorial and dynastic project, not an authored “text,” and its boundaries continued changing after his death. [Historisches Lexikon Bayerns](https://www.historisches-lexikon-bayerns.de/Lexikon/Artikel_45396)

### Exclusions and disputed “works”

- **1542/1543–1549:** *Geheimes Ehrenbuch der Fugger*. This illuminated genealogical manuscript is often associated generally with Fugger family self-fashioning, but scholarship identifies Hans Jakob Fugger—not Anton—as its commissioner; it is therefore not included among Anton Fugger’s works. [H-Soz-Kult review of Gregor Rohmann’s critical edition](https://www.hsozkult.de/publicationreview/id/reb-7287)

- **No fixed date:** *Anton Fugger’s correspondence and business records*. Letters, accounts, contracts, and instructions unquestionably survive, but no consulted catalogue raisonné or collected-works edition defines a complete Anton Fugger corpus; many records were produced by the firm’s “golden writing room” and cannot safely be converted into individually authored works without document-level study. This documentary mass resists treatment as a single obra or as a list of titled texts. [Ekkehard Westermann on the Fugger company’s written administration](https://www.bergbaumuseum.de/fileadmin/forschung/zeitschriften/der-anschnitt/2011/2011-02-03/anschnitt-2-3-2011-westermann-fugger.pdf)
