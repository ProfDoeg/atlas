## Works by The Lady of the Lake

### Enchantment

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** Merlin’s enchanted imprisonment. This is the Lady’s clearest obra: magic learned from Merlin and turned into an enclosure that keeps its teacher alive but inaccessible. Its manifestation varies radically—an invisible tower or walls of air in the Vulgate continuation, a stone tomb in the Post-Vulgate *Suite du Merlin*, and an enclosure beneath a rock or tree in later retellings—so it is better classified as a continuing enchantment than as a building. ([*Merlin: A Casebook*](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135583408_A25041886/preview-9781135583408_A25041886.pdf); [Servier, *La représentation des fées dans le roman arthurien*](https://pepite-depot.univ-lille.fr/LIBRE/EDSHS/2017/SERVIER_Alicia_Texte.pdf))

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** The sleep or paralysis laid upon Merlin. In the Post-Vulgate account this is a distinct preparatory spell: Niniane incapacitates Merlin so that attendants can place him beneath the tombstone. Other witnesses collapse the sleep and the enclosure into one act, making its independence as an obra uncertain. ([*Merlin: A Casebook*](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781135583408_A25041886/preview-9781135583408_A25041886.pdf); [Servier](https://pepite-depot.univ-lille.fr/LIBRE/EDSHS/2017/SERVIER_Alicia_Texte.pdf))

### Formation project

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** Lancelot’s chivalric formation. The Lady abducts or rescues the infant, raises him in her otherworldly household, arranges his instruction, equips him, and brings him to Arthur for knighting; the resulting knight is therefore a long-duration pedagogical and social project, not a body she literally created. Chrétien supplies only the earlier fairy-foster-mother tradition, Ulrich’s *Lanzelet* gives it an extensive independent form, and the Vulgate *Lancelot* identifies the foster mother with the Lady of the Lake. ([University of Rochester, “Lancelot”](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/theme/lancelot.html); [William Morris Archive, *Lancelot of the Lake*](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/transcription-lancelot))

### Protocol

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** The Lady’s instruction for becoming a knight. Before Lancelot enters Arthur’s world, she delivers an extended account of the conduct expected of knighthood. It is an embedded oral text that acts upon its recipient—a protocol, and arguably a chivalric manifesto—rather than a separately circulating book; its exact wording belongs to the medieval romance and is not reproduced here. ([William Morris Archive, *Lancelot of the Lake*](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/transcription-lancelot); [Lacy, *The Lancelot-Grail Reader*](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Lancelot_Grail_Reader.html?id=JwBKPQAACAAJ))

### Disputed or derivative creation

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** Lancelot’s love for Guinevere. A late continuation preserved in BnF MS fr. 24400 has Lancelot attribute his compulsive love chiefly to the Lady’s enchantments. This would make the affair an engineered affective manifestation, but the claim is not standard in the principal Vulgate narrative and should remain an isolated, disputed attribution rather than be harmonized with it. ([BnF catalogue, MS français 24400](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb40536419v); [Lacy, ed., *Lancelot-Grail*](https://www.routledge.com/Lancelot-Grail-Volume-1-Routledge-Revivals-The-Old-French-Arthurian-Vulgate-and-Post-Vulgate-in-Translation/Lacy/p/book/9780415877220))

### Objects she transmits but is not said to have made

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** *Excalibur and its scabbard*. The unnamed Lady possesses or transmits these objects to Arthur in the Post-Vulgate–Malory tradition, but the texts do not securely identify her as their maker; earlier tradition instead associates Arthur’s sword with Avalon. They therefore resist inclusion as her works and belong here only to prevent gift, custody, and manufacture from being conflated. ([University of Rochester, “Frequently Asked Questions”](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/text/frequently-asked-questions.html); [Lupack, *The Oxford Guide to Arthurian Literature and Legend*](https://books.google.com/books?id=KigTAQAAIAAJ))

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** Lancelot’s ring, armor, weapons, tent, and other marvelous equipment. The Vulgate *Lancelot* presents these as things the Lady gives or supplies; neither their fabrication nor every object’s enchantment is consistently assigned to her. They are a commissioned or curated knightly apparatus, not securely an oeuvre of artifacts made by her. ([Lacy, *The Lancelot-Grail Reader*](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Lancelot_Grail_Reader.html?id=JwBKPQAACAAJ); [William Morris Archive, *Lancelot of the Lake*](https://morrisarchive.lib.uiowa.edu/transcription-lancelot))

- **c. 500–537 (traditional chronology):** The apparent lake and the hidden household beneath it. The “lake” is an illusion concealing a terrestrial residence, but the Post-Vulgate tradition attributes the concealing enchantment to Merlin, not to the Lady; the palace itself likewise has no secure maker. Her title records occupancy and rule, not authorship. ([Servier](https://pepite-depot.univ-lille.fr/LIBRE/EDSHS/2017/SERVIER_Alicia_Texte.pdf); [Lacy, ed., *Lancelot-Grail*](https://www.routledge.com/Lancelot-Grail-Volume-1-Routledge-Revivals-The-Old-French-Arthurian-Vulgate-and-Post-Vulgate-in-Translation/Lacy/p/book/9780415877220))

- **c. 537 (traditional chronology):** The barge that receives Arthur after Camlann. Malory places the “chief Lady of the Lake” among the women aboard, but does not say that she made or conjured the vessel. The voyage is a collaborative manifestation involving several queens and ladies, including Morgan le Fay, rather than an attributable work by a single Lady. ([University of Rochester, Malory synopsis and commentary](https://d.lib.rochester.edu/camelot/text/collins-king-arthur-and-his-round-table.html); [Lupack, *The Oxford Guide to Arthurian Literature and Legend*](https://books.google.com/books?id=KigTAQAAIAAJ))
