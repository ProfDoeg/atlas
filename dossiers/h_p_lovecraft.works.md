## Works by Howard Phillips Lovecraft

### Original fiction and prose poems

- **1897?:** *The Noble Eavesdropper*. Juvenile tale known by title but no longer extant; its date is conjectural.
- **1897:** *The Little Glass Bottle*. Surviving juvenile adventure tale.
- **1898:** *The Secret Cave; or, John Lees Adventure*. Surviving juvenile tale; the irregular title is Lovecraft’s.
- **1898:** *The Mystery of the Grave-Yard; or, “A Dead Man’s Revenge.”* Surviving juvenile mystery tale.
- **1898–1902?:** *The Haunted House*. Lost juvenile fiction, known only from later evidence; both date and contents remain uncertain.
- **1898–1902?:** *The Secret of the Grave*. Lost juvenile fiction.
- **1898–1902?:** *John, the Detective*. Lost juvenile detective story.
- **1902:** *The Mysterious Ship*. Juvenile adventure tale surviving in two versions.
- **21 April 1905:** *The Beast in the Cave*. Early weird tale, revised before its first publication.
- **1907:** *The Picture*. Lost story, reportedly destroyed by Lovecraft.
- **1908:** *The Alchemist*. Early weird tale.
- **June 1917:** *The Tomb*. Weird tale framed as a disturbed narrator’s testimony.
- **July 1917:** *Dagon*. Weird tale presented as a suicide manuscript.
- **1917:** *A Reminiscence of Dr. Samuel Johnson*. Comic pseudo-memoir written in an invented eighteenth-century persona.
- **1917:** *Sweet Ermengarde; or, The Heart of a Country Girl*. Parodic melodrama; its precise composition date is uncertain.
- **May 1918?:** *Polaris*. Dream fantasy; the month is uncertain.
- **1918:** *The Mystery of Murdon Grange*. Nonextant story whose status and contents are poorly documented.
- **1919:** *Beyond the Wall of Sleep*. Weird-science tale.
- **1919:** *Memory*. Very short prose poem or allegorical fantasy rather than a conventional story.
- **1919:** *Old Bugs*. Temperance satire written for a private audience.
- **16 September 1919:** *The Transition of Juan Romero*. Weird tale that Lovecraft withheld during his lifetime.
- **November 1919:** *The White Ship*. Dream fantasy.
- **3 December 1919:** *The Doom That Came to Sarnath*. Pseudo-legend or prose chronicle.
- **December 1919:** *The Statement of Randolph Carter*. Weird tale cast as an official deposition.
- **28 January 1920:** *The Terrible Old Man*. Short weird tale.
- **1920:** *The Tree*. Classical fantasy or prose fable.
- **15 June 1920:** *The Cats of Ulthar*. Dreamland fable.
- **1920:** *The Temple*. Submarine weird tale presented as a recovered manuscript.
- **1920:** *Facts Concerning the Late Arthur Jermyn and His Family*. Genealogical horror tale, later published under editorially altered titles Lovecraft disliked.
- **1920?:** *The Street*. Allegorical prose narrative; the date is approximate.
- **1920?:** *Life and Death*. Lost work, too poorly documented to classify securely beyond probable fiction.
- **early November 1920:** *Celephaïs*. Dream fantasy.
- **16 November 1920:** *From Beyond*. Weird-science tale.
- **early December 1920:** *Nyarlathotep*. Prose poem or apocalyptic vision rather than an ordinarily plotted story.
- **12 December 1920:** *The Picture in the House*. New England horror tale.
- **1920–21:** *Ex Oblivione*. Prose poem or dream allegory.
- **January 1921:** *The Nameless City*. Archaeological weird tale.
- **28 February 1921:** *The Quest of Iranon*. Dream fantasy and allegory.
- **March 1921:** *The Moon-Bog*. Weird tale.
- **1921:** *The Outsider*. Weird tale structured as a first-person revelation.
- **14 August 1921:** *The Other Gods*. Dreamland myth or fable.
- **December 1921:** *The Music of Erich Zann*. Weird tale.
- **September 1921–mid-1922:** *Herbert West—Reanimator*. Serial horror fiction in six episodes; Lovecraft disliked the commissioned cliff-hanger structure.
- **March 1922:** *Hypnos*. Dream tale.
- **5 June 1922:** *What the Moon Brings*. Prose poem or dream vision.
- **June 1922:** *Azathoth*. Unfinished fragment, apparently the opening of an abandoned longer fiction; its incompleteness is part of the surviving obra.
- **September 1922:** *The Hound*. Weird tale.
- **November 1922:** *The Lurking Fear*. Serial horror story in four installments.
- **August–September 1923:** *The Rats in the Walls*. Gothic and genealogical horror tale.
- **September 1923:** *The Unnamable*. Metafictional weird tale.
- **October 1923:** *The Festival*. Weird tale and invented antiquarian observance.
- **16–19 October 1924:** *The Shunned House*. Long New England horror tale.
- **1–2 August 1925:** *The Horror at Red Hook*. Urban horror tale whose xenophobia is integral to, and troubling within, the object made.
- **11 August 1925:** *He*. Urban antiquarian fantasy.
- **18 September 1925:** *In the Vault*. Weird tale.
- **1926?:** *The Descendant*. Unfinished fictional fragment; the date is uncertain.
- **March 1926:** *Cool Air*. Weird-science tale.
- **summer 1926:** *The Call of Cthulhu*. Documentary collage and weird tale assembled from nested records.
- **1926:** *Pickman’s Model*. Weird tale structured as an oral account.
- **1926:** *The Silver Key*. Dream fantasy and sequel to the Randolph Carter stories.
- **9 November 1926:** *The Strange High House in the Mist*. New England dream fantasy.
- **autumn 1926–22 January 1927:** *The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath*. Short novel or novella; unpublished in Lovecraft’s lifetime.
- **January–1 March 1927:** *The Case of Charles Dexter Ward*. Short novel; Lovecraft left it unpublished and without a fully settled presentation.
- **March 1927:** *The Colour out of Space*. Science-fictional weird tale.
- **2 November 1927:** *The Very Old Folk*. Dream narrative first embedded in a letter and later treated as an independent text; it therefore resists a clean division between fiction and correspondence.
- **25 November 1927:** *The Thing in the Moonlight*. Not a Lovecraft work in its published form: J. Chapman Miske extracted and framed a dream passage from a Lovecraft letter, making its traditional attribution spurious.
- **1927:** *History of the Necronomicon*. Pseudo-scholarly chronology of an invented book; simultaneously fiction, bibliographical hoax, and apparatus for other tales.
- **1928?:** *Ibid*. Brief comic tale; the date is uncertain.
- **summer 1928:** *The Dunwich Horror*. New England weird tale.
- **24 February–26 September 1930:** *The Whisperer in Darkness*. Epistolary and documentary weird novella.
- **February–22 March 1931:** *At the Mountains of Madness*. Science-fiction and weird novella framed as a scientific warning.
- **1931:** *Discarded Draft of “The Shadow over Innsmouth.”* Abandoned draft fragment; not a separate finished story, but materially distinct evidence of the project.
- **November–3 December 1931:** *The Shadow over Innsmouth*. Weird novella combining investigation, pseudo-history, and confession.
- **January–28 February 1932:** *The Dreams in the Witch House*. Weird-science tale.
- **21–24 August 1933:** *The Thing on the Doorstep*. Weird tale.
- **October 1933:** *The Evil Clergyman*. Dream account later published as fiction; like *The Very Old Folk*, it originated in correspondence and resists firm generic separation.
- **late 1933?:** *The Book*. Unfinished fictional fragment, possibly related to the projected work sometimes called *The Book of Azathoth*; the connection and date are uncertain.
- **November 1934–March 1935:** *The Shadow out of Time*. Science-fictional weird novella.
- **November 1935:** *The Haunter of the Dark*. Weird tale and response to Robert Bloch’s fiction.

### Collaborations, revisions, and commissioned ghostwriting

- **1918–19:** *The Green Meadow*. Collaborative dream fantasy with Winifred V. Jackson, published under the joint pseudonym “Elizabeth Berkeley”; the precise division of authorship is not recoverable.
- **1920:** *Poetry and the Gods*. Collaborative fiction with Anna Helen Crofts, published as by “Henry Paget-Lowe”; Lovecraft’s exact share is debated.
- **1920–21:** *The Crawling Chaos*. Collaborative dream narrative with Winifred V. Jackson, published as by “Elizabeth Berkeley” and “Lewis Theobald, Jun.”
- **June 1922:** *The Horror at Martin’s Beach* / *The Invisible Monster*. Revision-collaboration with Sonia H. Greene; the published title was editorially changed, and the shares of the two writers cannot be reduced to ordinary joint authorship.
- **1923:** *Ashes*. Revision or collaboration with C. M. Eddy, Jr.; Lovecraft’s intervention was substantial, but the surviving evidence does not fix every authorial boundary.
- **1923:** *The Ghost-Eater*. Revision-collaboration with C. M. Eddy, Jr.
- **1923:** *The Loved Dead*. Revision-collaboration with C. M. Eddy, Jr.; its necrophilic subject caused controversy on publication.
- **1924?:** *Deaf, Dumb, and Blind*. Revision-collaboration with C. M. Eddy, Jr.; the date is approximate.
- **February–March 1924:** *Under the Pyramids* / *Imprisoned with the Pharaohs*. Commissioned ghostwritten tale for Harry Houdini, based on a purported experience supplied by Houdini; Lovecraft wrote the narrative, but it first appeared under Houdini’s name.
- **July–October 1926:** *Two Black Bottles*. Collaboration with Wilfred Blanch Talman; scholars regard Talman as the primary author and Lovecraft’s contribution as limited.
- **1927:** *The Last Test*. Extensive revision for Adolphe de Castro of de Castro’s earlier story “A Sacrifice to Science”; classification as simple collaboration conceals the derivative and commissioned nature of the text.
- **1928:** *The Curse of Yig*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Zealia Bishop from a supplied synopsis.
- **1929?:** *The Electric Executioner*. Extensive revision for Adolphe de Castro of an earlier de Castro story; the date is uncertain.
- **December 1929–early 1930:** *The Mound*. Ghostwritten novella for Zealia Bishop from a brief premise; Lovecraft created most of the finished narrative.
- **May 1930:** *Medusa’s Coil*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Zealia Bishop.
- **late 1931:** *The Trap*. Collaboration or revision with Henry S. Whitehead; the exact division of labor is uncertain.
- **1932:** *The Man of Stone*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Hazel Heald.
- **October 1932:** *The Horror in the Museum*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Hazel Heald.
- **October 1932–April 1933:** *Through the Gates of the Silver Key*. Collaboration with E. Hoffmann Price based on Price’s draft and Lovecraft’s extensive rewriting; it also continues Lovecraft’s Randolph Carter project.
- **1933:** *Winged Death*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Hazel Heald.
- **1933:** *Out of the Aeons*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Hazel Heald.
- **1933–35:** *The Horror in the Burying-Ground*. Ghostwritten or extensively revised tale for Hazel Heald; its range of possible dates reflects uncertain composition evidence.
- **1933:** *The Hoard of the Wizard-Beast*. Collaboration with R. H. Barlow.
- **1933:** *The Slaying of the Monster*. Collaboration with R. H. Barlow.
- **May 1934:** *The Tree on the Hill*. Collaboration or extensive revision with Duane W. Rimel.
- **June 1934:** *The Battle That Ended the Century*. Topical parody with R. H. Barlow, circulated pseudonymously; it is closer to an amateur-journalist prank than to conventional fiction.
- **January 1935:** *“Till A’ the Seas”*. Collaboration with R. H. Barlow.
- **June 1935:** *Collapsing Cosmoses*. Comic science-fiction collaboration with R. H. Barlow.
- **August 1935:** *The Challenge from Beyond*. Round-robin story with C. L. Moore, A. Merritt, Robert E. Howard, and Frank Belknap Long; Lovecraft made only one segment, so the whole cannot be assigned to him alone.
- **September 1935:** *The Disinterment*. Collaboration or extensive revision with Duane W. Rimel.
- **October 1935:** *The Diary of Alonzo Typer*. Extensive revision of a story by William Lumley.
- **January 1936:** *In the Walls of Eryx*. Collaboration with Kenneth Sterling; Sterling supplied the originating draft and Lovecraft substantially reworked it.
- **autumn 1936?:** *The Night Ocean*. Collaboration with R. H. Barlow; scholarship has disputed how much Lovecraft contributed, and the date is approximate.
- **undated:** *The Sorcery of Aphlar*. Proposed or fragmentary collaboration associated with Duane W. Rimel; it resists treatment as a completed work.
- **undated:** *The Clarendon Mysterious Disappearance*. Revision associated with Arthur Harris; attribution and extent of Lovecraft’s contribution remain uncertain.
- **undated:** *The Automatic Executioner*. Lost or unlocated revision project associated with Adolphe de Castro; its relationship to *The Electric Executioner* is not wholly secure.
- **1920s:** *The Hoax of the Spirit Lover*. Sonia H. Greene’s text has sometimes entered the Lovecraft revision corpus, but later scholarship judges Lovecraft’s role to have been, at most, minor stylistic advice; it therefore resists inclusion as his obra.

### Poetry and poetic projects

- **1897–1937:** *The poetical corpus*. More than three hundred surviving poems, fragments, translations, satires, elegies, birthday verses, epistles, and jointly revised poems are established in S. T. Joshi’s *The Ancient Track: The Complete Poetical Works of H. P. Lovecraft*. Many are private occasional artifacts rather than autonomous literary “works,” and several survive untitled under editorial bracketed descriptions; treating the corpus as one obra preserves that resistance instead of manufacturing fixed titles.
- **8 November 1897:** *The Poem of Ulysses; or, The Odyssey*. Juvenile verse narrative and the earliest surviving dated poem.
- **1898–1902:** *Ovid’s Metamorphoses*. Juvenile verse adaptation or project; the broad date reflects uncertain composition.
- **1901:** *H. Lovecraft’s Attempted Journey betwixt Providence & Fall River on the N.Y.N.H. & H.R.R.* Juvenile comic verse narrative.
- **1902:** *Poemata Minora, Volume II*. Handmade juvenile poetic sequence or booklet containing “Ode to Selene or Diana,” “To the Old Pagan Religion,” “On the Ruin of Rome,” “To Pan,” and “On the Vanity of Human Ambition”; it is simultaneously manuscript object and composite work.
- **1902:** *C.S.A. 1861–1865: To the Starry Cross of the South*. Juvenile historical poem.
- **July 1905:** *De Triumpho Naturae*. Philosophical poem.
- **1912:** *On the Creation of Niggers*. Racist satirical poem; its slur is retained only because it is the historical title, not reproduced as poetic text.
- **c. 17 December 1914:** *The Teuton’s Battle-Song*. Martial poem.
- **January 1916:** *An American to Mother England*. Anglophile political poem.
- **1916:** *The Poe-et’s Nightmare*. Comic and literary-satirical poem.
- **late 1917–summer 1918:** *Psychopompos: A Tale in Rhyme*. Long narrative poem.
- **1919:** *On Reading Lord Dunsany’s Book of Wonder*. Literary homage in verse.
- **1919:** *To Edward John Moreton Drax Plunkett, Eighteenth Baron Dunsany*. Verse address to Lord Dunsany.
- **December 1921:** *Sir Thomas Tryout*. Comic verse narrative associated with amateur journalism.
- **26 September 1924:** *Providence*. Topographical and autobiographical poem.
- **15 February 1925:** *The Cats*. Poem on Lovecraft’s favored animal.
- **1927:** *Nathicana*. Fantasy poem.
- **26 November 1929:** *The Outpost*. Historical-fantastic poem.
- **26 November 1929:** *The Ancient Track*. Weird poem.
- **30 November 1929:** *The Messenger*. Weird sonnet.
- **27 December 1929–4 January 1930:** *Fungi from Yuggoth*. Cycle of thirty-six sonnets; individual sonnets also circulated separately, so the cycle is both a Gesamtkunstwerk-like sequence and a container of independently manifested poems.
- **1934:** *To Clark Ashton Smith, Esq., upon His Phantastick Tales, Verses, Pictures, and Sculptures*. Verse tribute that explicitly addresses Smith’s work across media.
- **1936:** *The Cats of Ulthar*. Poetic adaptation distinct from the 1920 prose tale.
- **1937:** *On the Death of Robert E. Howard*. Memorial poem, among Lovecraft’s last poetic works.

### Essays, criticism, manifestos, and protocols of writing

- **1903–37:** *The nonfiction corpus*. Hundreds of essays, reviews, editorials, scientific articles, polemics, autobiographical sketches, and amateur-journalism columns are collected in S. T. Joshi’s five-volume *Collected Essays*. Many began as letters, club papers, unsigned notices, or serial departments, so boundaries between independent text, editorial act, and correspondence remain unstable.
- **1917–18:** *The United Amateur* editorial essays. A sustained body of unsigned and signed amateur-journalism criticism made while Lovecraft served as an official editor; individual notices often resist secure separation from the periodical he assembled.
- **1918:** *The Literature of Rome*. Historical-literary survey.
- **1919:** *The Allowable Rhyme*. Prescriptive essay on versification; it functions partly as a protocol for poets.
- **1919:** *Metrical Regularity*. Prescriptive literary-critical essay.
- **1919:** *The Despised Pastoral*. Defense of pastoral poetry.
- **1919:** *At the Root*. Literary-critical essay.
- **1919:** *Literary Composition*. Instructional essay; explicitly a protocol for beginning writers.
- **1920–21:** *In Defence of Dagon*. Sequence of polemical replies defending Lovecraft’s story and aesthetic principles; less a single descriptive essay than an argumentative intervention.
- **1921:** *The Defence Remains Open!*. Polemical continuation of the “Dagon” controversy.
- **1921:** *Nietzscheism and Realism*. Philosophical and political essay.
- **1921:** *The Vivisector*. Critical column or serial reviewing amateur publications rather than a single stable essay.
- **1921:** *The Conservative*. Editorial and essayistic body of work housed in Lovecraft’s self-published amateur paper; authorship of individual matter varies.
- **1922:** *Lord Dunsany and His Work*. Critical essay.
- **1923:** *The Materialist Today*. Philosophical essay.
- **1925:** *Cats and Dogs*. Autobiographical, polemical, and comic essay rather than a neutral comparison.
- **1925:** *The Omnipresent Philistine*. Cultural and literary polemic.
- **1926:** *The Dignity of Journalism*. Amateur-journalism manifesto asserting standards and purposes for the activity.
- **1926–27; revised 1933–34:** *Supernatural Horror in Literature*. Historical-critical survey composed serially and later revised; the revisions make it a continuing scholarly project rather than one immutable text.
- **1927:** *Notes on Writing Weird Fiction*. Autobiographical craft essay and practical protocol for composing weird tales.
- **1928:** *Some Notes on Interplanetary Fiction*. Critical essay and writing protocol for speculative fiction.
- **1933:** *Weird Story Plots*. Notes or protocol for constructing weird fiction; closer to working apparatus than polished essay.
- **1934:** *Some Notes on a Nonentity*. Autobiographical sketch.
- **1936:** *In Memoriam: Robert Ervin Howard*. Memorial and critical essay.
- **undated:** *A Confession of Unfaith*. Autobiographical account of Lovecraft’s loss of religious belief; dating is not securely fixed.
- **undated:** *Idealism and Materialism—A Reflection*. Philosophical essay.
- **undated:** *The Case for Classicism*. Literary manifesto for formal and stylistic traditionalism.
- **undated:** *Notes on Verse Technique*. Instructional protocol for poetic composition.

### Scientific publications and manuscript scientific projects

- **1899–1902?:** *The Scientific Gazette*. Juvenile handwritten or hectographed science periodical; survival is incomplete and dating approximate.
- **1903–09:** *The Rhode Island Journal of Astronomy*. Handmade astronomical periodical written and duplicated by Lovecraft; sixty-nine issues survive, making it both a serial publication and a sustained scientific obra.
- **1904:** *The Annual Report on the Science of Astronomy and Its Progress During 1903*. Handwritten astronomical report; the surviving title contains Lovecraft’s grammatical error in “It’s” in some cataloguing.
- **1906–08:** *Astronomical articles for the Providence Tribune*. Newspaper science column or series, not a single book-like work.
- **1906–08:** *Astronomical articles for the Pawtuxet Valley Gleaner*. Newspaper series whose individual manifestations form a continuing public-education project.
- **1906–08:** *Astronomical articles for the Providence Evening News*. Newspaper science writing; survival and bibliographical identification are not wholly complete.
- **1909–15:** *Astronomy articles and almanac material*. A dispersed body of astronomical forecasts, observing notes, and explanatory pieces for newspapers and amateur periodicals; it resists enumeration as wholly independent titled works.
- **1914:** *Science versus Charlatanry*. Anti-astrology polemic and public scientific manifesto.
- **1914:** *The Falsity of Astrology*. Scientific polemic.
- **1914:** *Astrology and the Future*. Anti-astrology essay.
- **1914:** *Delavan’s Comet and Astrology*. Topical astronomical polemic.
- **1914:** *The Fall of Astrology*. Anti-astrology essay.
- **1915:** *The Truth about Mars*. Popular-science article.
- **1915:** *Can the Moon Be Reached by Man?* Speculative scientific essay.
- **1930:** *Some Backgrounds of Fairyland*. Scientific and imaginative note concerning astronomical settings; it sits between reference apparatus and literary essay.

### Travel writing and topographical obra

- **summer 1927:** *The Trip of Theobald*. Comic-antiquarian travelogue written in a persona.
- **29 September 1927:** *Vermont—A First Impression*. Travel essay.
- **summer 1928:** *Observations on Several Parts of America*. Extended travel narrative, unpublished until after Lovecraft’s death.
- **summer 1928:** *A Descent to Avernus*. Travel essay.
- **summer 1928:** *Sleepy Hollow To-day*. Literary-topographical travel essay.
- **after 18 May 1929:** *Travels in the Provinces of America*. Extended antiquarian travelogue, posthumously published.
- **summer 1929:** *An Account of a Trip to the Antient Fairbanks House, in Dedham, and to the Red Horse Tavern in Sudbury, in the Province of the Massachusetts-Bay*. Antiquarian excursion narrative.
- **28 April–9 May 1930:** *Account of a Visit to Charleston, S.C.* Travel account.
- **17 July 1930:** *An Account of Charleston, in His Majesty’s Province of South-Carolina*. Antiquarian travelogue written in deliberately archaic form.
- **1930–31:** *A Description of the Town of Quebeck in New-France, Lately Added to His Britannick Majesty’s Dominions*. Historical-topographical travel essay.
- **19 December 1932:** *European Glimpses*. Ghostwritten travel narrative based on Sonia H. Greene’s European journey; Greene supplied the experience and materials, while Lovecraft shaped the prose, making authorship composite.
- **July 1933:** *Some Dutch Footprints in New England*. Historical and topographical essay.
- **July 1934:** *Homes and Shrines of Poe*. Literary pilgrimage and travel essay.
- **September 1934:** *The Unknown City in the Ocean*. Travel sketch.
- **January 1936:** *Charleston*. Condensed or reworked travel account.

### Amateur-journalism periodicals and editorial creations

- **1915–23:** *The Conservative*. Lovecraft’s amateur periodical, issued irregularly in thirteen numbers; it is a composite obra containing his essays, poems, editorials, reprints, and contributions by others.
- **1917–18:** *The United Amateur*. Official organ edited by Lovecraft during his UAPA presidency; the issues are collective objects, not works authored solely by him.
- **1918–19:** *The United Cooperative*. Amateur periodical edited with collaborators; editorial responsibility does not make every contained text Lovecraft’s.
- **1921:** *The Rainbow*. Amateur paper edited for or with Sonia H. Greene; a collaborative periodical object.
- **1923–25:** *The Conservative* revival material and related amateur editorial projects. These are editorial manifestations dispersed across issues rather than a single fixed work.
- **1920s–30s:** *Revision work for amateur writers*. A large, partly unrecoverable body of corrections, rewritings, poems, essays, and stories prepared for paying or friendly clients; many pieces resist the category “Lovecraft work” because his contribution ranges from proofreading to near-total ghostwriting.

### Notebooks, invented documents, and working apparatus

- **1919–35:** *Commonplace Book*. Notebook of 221 numbered story ideas and motifs, some originating with other writers or dreams; it is a project-generator and working protocol, not a collection of completed stories.
- **1920s–30s:** *Dream records*. Accounts preserved chiefly inside letters, some later extracted and published as independent texts; their later manifestation as “stories” does not erase their epistolary origin.
- **1927:** *History of the Necronomicon*. In addition to being fiction, this is an invented bibliographical record designed to coordinate the pseudo-history of a nonexistent book.
- **1931:** *Notes and sketches for At the Mountains of Madness*. Plotting notes, maps, and drawings constituting working apparatus for the novella rather than a separate finished literary work.
- **1933:** *Instructions in Case of Decease*. Practical testamentary memorandum concerning manuscripts and papers; a protocol rather than literary description.
- **1934:** *Suggestions for a Reading Guide*. Bibliographical or pedagogical protocol associated with R. H. Barlow.
- **1930s:** *Genealogical charts and antiquarian notes*. Manuscript research objects made for himself and correspondents; their boundaries and titles are often archival rather than authorial.

### Drawings, maps, and hybrid visual-textual objects

- **childhood–1937:** *Drawings and manuscript illustrations*. A dispersed corpus of astronomical diagrams, architectural sketches, creatures, heraldic devices, maps, and comic images embedded in manuscripts and letters; many have no fixed titles and resist treatment as autonomous artworks.
- **c. 1931:** *Map and creature sketches for At the Mountains of Madness*. Visual working documents integral to the fictional project, crossing the boundary between illustration, compositional aid, and imaginary scientific evidence.
- **1933:** *Sketch of Cthulhu*. Small drawing accompanying a letter to R. H. Barlow; a visual manifestation of the fictional entity rather than a literary text.
- **1934:** *Plan of R’lyeh*. Diagrammatic drawing associated with Lovecraft’s invented geography; its existence as letter-enclosure and world-building apparatus makes it neither simply illustration nor independent artwork.
- **various dates:** *Maps of Arkham and other imaginary New England places*. Collaborative and revisable diagrams, sometimes made with correspondents; attribution of particular features is not always exclusively Lovecraft’s.

### Correspondence as life-work

- **c. 1906–15 March 1937:** *Correspondence*. An estimated 100,000 letters were written, of which only a fraction survives; the surviving letters form a vast epistolary obra containing criticism, autobiography, travel narrative, dreams, politics, philosophy, scientific discussion, sketches, and sometimes texts later published independently. Completeness is impossible, individual letters frequently lack settled titles, and treating every letter as a conventional “work” would falsify the social and serial nature of the project.
- **1919–21:** *The Gallomo correspondence*. Multi-person correspondence among Lovecraft, Alfred Galpin, Samuel Loveman, and Maurice W. Moe; a conversational creation whose constituent letters belong to several makers.
- **1922–37:** *Letters to Clark Ashton Smith*. Long-running literary and personal correspondence; editorial collections are later manifestations, not works Lovecraft himself assembled.
- **1926–37:** *Letters to August Derleth*. Correspondence combining personal exchange, criticism, politics, and collaborative myth-making.
- **1927–37:** *Letters to Donald Wandrei*. Literary correspondence.
- **1930–36:** *Correspondence with Robert E. Howard*. Extended debate on history, civilization, economics, and literature; the exchange is a jointly made intellectual project rather than Lovecraft’s solitary text.
- **1931–37:** *Letters to J. Vernon Shea*. Philosophical, political, and autobiographical correspondence.
- **1931–37:** *Letters to R. H. Barlow*. Correspondence incorporating mentorship, collaboration, textual preservation, drawings, and estate planning.
- **1936–37:** *Letters to Fritz Leiber*. Critical mentorship and discussion of weird fiction near the end of Lovecraft’s life.
