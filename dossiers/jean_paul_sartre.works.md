## Works by Jean-Paul Charles Aymard Sartre

### Juvenilia, student works, and early projects

- **1912:** *Lettre à Georges Courteline*. A childhood letter later published in *Le Figaro littéraire*; it survives more as documentary juvenilia than as a deliberately constituted work. [Contat–Rybalka bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_%C3%89crits_de_Sartre)
- **1922:** *Complainte de deux khâgneux qui travaillent fort*. A comic verse made with Paul Nizan; joint authorship prevents treating it as Sartre’s alone. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1922:** *La Belle et la Bête*. An early literary exercise, unpublished until *Écrits de jeunesse*; its unfinished or private status makes it juvenilia rather than a public work. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1922:** *Fragments sur le jazz*. Fragmentary youthful writing on music, first published posthumously; the title describes surviving pieces rather than a finished composition. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1922:** *Saturnin Picquot*. A youthful prose text published only in *Écrits de jeunesse*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1923:** *Anatole France — le Conducteur*. A youthful critical or parodic text, posthumously collected. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1923:** *L’Ange du morbide*. A short literary text published in *La Revue sans titre*, Sartre’s first known periodical publication. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1923:** *Jésus la Chouette, professeur de province*. A serial satirical tale published across three issues of *La Revue sans titre*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1924:** *Nelly ou De l’inconvénient des proverbes*. A youthful fiction, unpublished during Sartre’s lifetime. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1924:** *Andrée*. An early fictional text first made public posthumously. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1924:** *Carnet Midy*. A notebook rather than a finished book: its status as working material is part of the obra. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1924:** *Apologie du cinéma: Défense et illustration d’un art international*. A youthful manifesto-like defence of cinema, unpublished until *Écrits de jeunesse*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1926:** *Les Maranes*. A collaborative text by Sartre, Paul Nizan, and René Maheu; it cannot be assigned exclusively to Sartre. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1926:** *Pour les 21 ans d’Ugène mélancolique*. A collective occasional creation by Sartre, Nizan, Maheu, and Pierre Guille. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1926:** *Ho hé ho (je suis un petit garçon qui ne veut pas grandir)*. A youthful literary piece published posthumously. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1926:** *Une défaite*. An early novella or novelistic project, unpublished during Sartre’s lifetime. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1927:** *Er l’Arménien*. A youthful fictional text first published posthumously. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1927:** *La Théorie de l’État dans la pensée française aujourd’hui*. A student scholarly article, rediscovered and republished in 1997. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1927:** *L’Image dans la vie psychologique: rôle et nature*. Sartre’s postgraduate thesis, supervised by Henri Delacroix; it is an academic project and precursor of his later books on imagination, not originally a commercial book.
- **1929:** *Fragment d’une lettre dans « Enquête auprès des étudiants d’aujourd’hui »*. A published response whose fragmentary and editorially mediated character resists classification as an autonomous work. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1931:** *Légende de la vérité*. A philosophical-literary fragment published in *Bifur*; the larger projected work was not completed. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1931:** *L’Art cinématographique*. A ceremonial lecture or brochure on film, not a book-length study. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)

### Fiction and fictional projects

- **1937:** *Le Mur*. A short story first published independently and later made the title story of the 1939 collection. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1938:** *La Nausée*. A novel, extensively revised from the project successively called *Factum sur la contingence* and *Melancholia*; the published title was suggested by Gallimard. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1938:** *La Chambre*. A short story later collected in *Le Mur*. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1938:** *Intimité*. A short story later collected in *Le Mur*. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1939:** *Érostrate*. A short story published as part of *Le Mur*. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1939:** *L’Enfance d’un chef*. A long story or novella published in *Le Mur*. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1939:** *Le Mur*. A collection comprising five distinct fictional works; the collection is itself a deliberately made arrangement, not merely an edition. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **written before 1939; published 1981:** *Dépaysement*. A story removed from *Le Mur* before publication and issued posthumously; it remains a rejected component rather than an authorized sixth story. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1945:** *L’Âge de raison*. A novel and the first volume of *Les Chemins de la liberté*. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1945:** *Le Sursis*. A novel and the second volume of *Les Chemins de la liberté*, formally constructed through simultaneous, shifting narratives. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1949:** *La Mort dans l’âme*. A novel and the third published volume of *Les Chemins de la liberté*. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1945–1949:** *Les Chemins de la liberté*. A projected four-volume novel cycle; three volumes appeared, so the project—not a nonexistent completed tetralogy—is the obra. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1949; published posthumously:** *Drôle d’amitié*. Two chapters of the projected fourth volume, first published in periodical form and later incorporated into the critical edition. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **late 1940s; published posthumously:** *La Dernière Chance*. Fragments belonging to the abandoned fourth volume of *Les Chemins de la liberté*; editors distinguish them from *Drôle d’amitié*, and neither constitutes a completed novel. [Gallimard’s critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/OEuvres-romanesques4)
- **1951–1952; published 1991:** *La Reine Albemarle ou le dernier touriste*. An unfinished travel-fiction/autobiographical project on Italy; its posthumous title identifies surviving drafts, not a book completed by Sartre. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)

### Plays, adaptations, and theatrical manifestations

- **1940:** *Bariona, ou le Fils du tonnerre*. A Christmas mystery play written and staged for fellow prisoners in Stalag XII-D; it combines drama, music, collective performance, and camp circumstance, approaching a modest Gesamtkunstwerk. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1943:** *Les Mouches*. A play reworking the Oresteia myth and first staged under German occupation. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1944:** *Huis clos*. A one-act play; its first production is a theatrical manifestation distinct from the printed text. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1946:** *Morts sans sépulture*. A play about captured Resistance fighters, written earlier and first staged in 1946. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1946:** *La Putain respectueuse*. A play, subsequently revised and adapted; the theatrical text and later film should not be collapsed into one object. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1948:** *Les Mains sales*. A political drama whose later performance history repeatedly became politically contested. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1951:** *Le Diable et le Bon Dieu*. A large historical drama in eleven tableaux. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1952:** *L’Engrenage*. A screenplay written earlier and transformed for the stage without Sartre’s initial intention that it be a play; it therefore resists a single-medium classification. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1953–1954:** *Kean*. Sartre’s radical adaptation of Alexandre Dumas’s play; Sartre expressly presented it as an adaptation rather than an entirely original drama. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1955:** *Nekrassov*. A satirical political play first staged in 1955 and published in 1956. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1959:** *Les Séquestrés d’Altona*. A five-act drama, first staged in 1959 although some bibliographies date the book publication to 1960. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1965:** *Les Troyennes*. An adaptation of Euripides that recasts the ancient play as an anti-colonial drama; authorship is consequently both Euripidean and Sartrian. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1943–1965; collected 2005:** *Théâtre complet*. The critical corpus includes finished plays, adaptations, deleted scenes, unfinished tableaux, performance documents, and statements; it is an editorially reconstructed body of theatrical work, not a collection designed by Sartre. [Gallimard critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/Theatre-complet14)

### Screenplays, film, radio, and spoken works

- **1943–1944; published 2007:** *Typhus*. An unfilmed screenplay commissioned by Pathé; its existence is that of a cinematic project whose intended film was never made.
- **1947:** *Les Jeux sont faits*. A screenplay made into a film directed by Jean Delannoy; the scenario and the resulting film are related but non-identical works. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1947:** *Les Faux Nez*. A screenplay published in *La Revue du cinéma* but not realized as a film. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1947:** *La Tribune des Temps modernes*. A short-lived series of radio broadcasts and political discussions made collectively by Sartre and the journal’s circle; it is a broadcast project rather than a single authored text. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1958–1959; published 1984:** *Le Scénario Freud*. A screenplay commissioned by John Huston for the film *Freud*; Sartre’s very long versions were rejected or radically altered, and he removed his name from the finished film, so the screenplay—not Huston’s film—is his obra. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1965:** *Huis clos: préface parlée*. A recorded spoken introduction to the play; this is a phonographic manifestation rather than another written play. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1972–1976:** *Sartre par lui-même*. A filmed self-portrait directed by Alexandre Astruc and Michel Contat, built from Sartre’s interviews and participation; it is collaborative, and directorial authorship belongs to Astruc and Contat rather than Sartre alone. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1977:** *Sartre*. The published transcript of *Sartre par lui-même*; it is an editorial transformation of a film and spoken testimony, not simply a book written by Sartre. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)

### Phenomenology, ontology, ethics, and philosophical projects

- **1936:** *L’Imagination*. A philosophical study of theories of imagination and the first published book arising from Sartre’s academic research. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1936–1937:** *La Transcendance de l’Ego*. A phenomenological essay first published in a journal in two parts. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1938:** *Structure intentionnelle de l’image*. A philosophical article subsequently incorporated into *L’Imaginaire*; it is both an earlier autonomous manifestation and material absorbed into another obra. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1939:** *Esquisse d’une théorie des émotions*. A philosophical monograph expressly presented as an extract from the abandoned larger project *La Psyché*. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1930s:** *La Psyché*. A projected comprehensive work on phenomenological psychology; Sartre drafted much of it but abandoned it, and its project-status—not a complete lost book—must be preserved.
- **1940:** *L’Imaginaire: Psychologie phénoménologique de l’imagination*. A philosophical monograph completing and revising Sartre’s early work on images. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1943:** *L’Être et le Néant: Essai d’ontologie phénoménologique*. A major philosophical treatise; its concluding promise of a future ethics makes it also one part of an unfinished philosophical project. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1945–1946:** *L’existentialisme est un humanisme*. A public lecture, stenographic publication, and philosophical manifesto; Sartre did not prepare its first book form as a conventional authored manuscript and later expressed reservations about it. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1947–1948; published 1983:** *Cahiers pour une morale*. Working notebooks for the promised ethics; they are an unfinished project published by an editor, not the completed moral treatise Sartre intended. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1948; published 1989:** *Vérité et existence*. An unfinished philosophical manuscript on truth, knowledge, and history; its posthumous edition gives provisional material the appearance of a book. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1957:** *Questions de méthode*. A methodological essay first published in a Polish journal and *Les Temps modernes*, then revised as the opening of the *Critique de la raison dialectique*. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1960:** *Critique de la raison dialectique, tome I: Théorie des ensembles pratiques*. A philosophical-historical treatise seeking to make existentialism and Marxism intelligible together. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1958–1962; published 1985:** *Critique de la raison dialectique, tome II: L’Intelligibilité de l’histoire*. An unfinished continuation reconstructed and published posthumously; it must not be represented as a completed second volume authorized by Sartre. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1964:** *Détermination et liberté*. A lecture or philosophical intervention rather than a completed treatise.
- **1966:** *L’Universel singulier*. A philosophical conference paper on Kierkegaard, later collected in *Situations IX*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1966:** *Morale et société*. A conference contribution at the Gramsci Institute; it belongs to Sartre’s continuing but never completed moral project. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1964–1965; published in part posthumously:** *Morale et histoire*. An unfinished lecture-and-manuscript project; portions appeared separately, including “Kennedy and West Virginia,” so no single definitive completed text exists.

### Literary criticism, artist studies, and existential biographies

- **1944:** *L’Homme et les choses*. An essay on Francis Ponge, first published under a different title and later collected in *Situations I*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1944–1947:** *Qu’est-ce que la littérature?* A sequence of essays, first serialized and then published as a book; it is both literary theory and a manifesto for committed writing. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1947:** *Baudelaire*. An existential psychoanalytic study developed from a projected longer work; biography, criticism, and philosophy are deliberately inseparable. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1947:** *Pour un théâtre de situations*. A programmatic essay functioning as a theatrical manifesto. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1947:** *La Responsabilité de l’écrivain*. A UNESCO lecture on literary action and responsibility, published separately much later. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1948–1952:** *Saint Genet, comédien et martyr*. A vast existential biography and critical study, initially conceived as a preface to Genet’s works but expanding into an autonomous book. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1948–1953; published 1979:** *Mallarmé: La lucidité et sa face d’ombre*. An unfinished study assembled posthumously; it should not be mistaken for the shorter 1953 preface “Mallarmé (1842–1898).”
- **1953:** *Mallarmé (1842–1898)*. A concise critical portrait written as a preface to Mallarmé’s poetry and later collected independently. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1957–1964:** *Le Séquestré de Venise*. A series of essays on Tintoretto rather than a completed single book; later editors assembled related texts under this title.
- **1961:** *Le Peintre sans privilèges*. An essay on Robert Lapoujade, combining art criticism with Sartre’s theory of the imaginary.
- **1964:** *Les Mots*. An autobiographical narrative and critique of Sartre’s childhood vocation as a writer, first serialized in 1963. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1966:** *La Conscience de classe chez Flaubert*. A serialized portion of the developing Flaubert project, later absorbed into *L’Idiot de la famille*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1966:** *Flaubert: du poète à l’artiste*. Another serialized manifestation of the Flaubert project, later incorporated into *L’Idiot de la famille*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1971:** *L’Idiot de la famille, tomes I–II*. The first two volumes of Sartre’s enormous study of Gustave Flaubert, simultaneously biography, psychoanalysis, social history, and philosophy.
- **1972:** *L’Idiot de la famille, tome III*. The third published volume; Sartre did not complete the projected continuation on *Madame Bovary*, so the overarching project remains unfinished. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)

### Political essays, manifestos, protocols, and collective interventions

- **1944:** *Un promeneur dans Paris insurgé*. A seven-part eyewitness report on the liberation of Paris; reportage rather than retrospective history. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1944:** *La République du silence*. A Resistance essay whose rhetoric also performs a post-Liberation act of collective self-definition. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1945:** *Portrait de l’antisémite*. The first portion of the work later published as *Réflexions sur la question juive*.
- **1946:** *Réflexions sur la question juive*. A political-philosophical essay on antisemitism, Jewish identity, and social freedom. [BnF Sartre exhibition](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/liste.htm)
- **1946:** *Matérialisme et révolution*. A political-philosophical essay challenging orthodox materialism while defending revolutionary action. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1948:** *Appel du Comité pour le Rassemblement démocratique révolutionnaire*. A collective manifesto associated with the RDR; it acts by soliciting political organization, and Sartre’s authorship is shared. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1948–1949:** *Rassemblement démocratique révolutionnaire*. A political project co-founded with David Rousset and others; it is an organization and attempted political practice, not a text.
- **1950–1954:** *Les Communistes et la paix*. A three-part political essay defending and interrogating the French Communist Party; it was published serially and later collected.
- **1953:** *L’Affaire Henri Martin*. A collectively produced dossier and campaign book edited around the imprisoned sailor Henri Martin; Sartre supplied major texts but was not its sole maker. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1956:** *Le Réformisme et les fétiches*. A political critique of Pierre Hervé and Communist orthodoxy, later collected in *Situations*. [Gallimard Situations bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situations_V)
- **1956:** *Le Fantôme de Staline*. An essay responding to the Soviet invasion of Hungary and breaking publicly with Soviet communism.
- **1957:** *Le Colonialisme est un système*. An anti-colonial analysis first delivered as a public intervention and later collected in *Situations V*.
- **1960:** *Préface à Aden Arabie*. A political and memorial essay on Paul Nizan, functioning both as preface and rehabilitation.
- **1961:** *Préface aux Damnés de la terre*. An anti-colonial manifesto written for Frantz Fanon’s book; its status is inseparable from, but not identical to, Fanon’s work.
- **1964:** *Refus du prix Nobel*. Sartre’s public refusal is an action accompanied by explanatory statements: a manifesto enacted through non-acceptance rather than a conventional literary work.
- **1967:** *Tribunal Russell sur le Vietnam*. Sartre’s presidency, statements, and judgments formed part of a collective political-juridical performance; this “tribunal” had moral rather than state authority.
- **1968:** *On a raison de se révolter*. Dialogues with Pierre Victor and Philippe Gavi, produced orally and collaboratively; it is political conversation rather than sole-authored treatise.
- **1970:** *Tribunal populaire de Lens*. Sartre’s participation in a staged people’s tribunal was a political manifestation whose declarations acted as accusation and judgment.
- **1971:** *Préface au Procès de Burgos*. A political preface to Gisèle Halimi’s dossier, later collected in *Situations X*. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1972:** *Les Maos en France*. A preface to Michèle Manceaux’s book and a statement on French Maoism, later incorporated into *Situations*. [Gallimard Situations bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situations_IX)
- **1973:** *On a raison de se révolter*. The book-form manifestation of Sartre’s discussions with Pierre Victor and Philippe Gavi; all three are makers.
- **1974:** *La Révolte*. Conversations with Pierre Victor; an orally generated collaborative political work.
- **1980:** *L’Espoir maintenant*. Interviews with Benny Lévy, first serialized shortly before Sartre’s death; Beauvoir and others disputed how far failing health and Lévy’s influence affected the positions attributed to Sartre, so authorship and authority remain contested. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)

### Prefaces, art texts, reportage, and short interventions

- **1945:** *Reportages aux États-Unis*. Several newspaper series for *Le Figaro* and *Combat*; they form a journalistic travel corpus rather than a single book. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1946:** *New York, ville coloniale*. An essay-reportage on New York, first published in English as “Manhattan: The Great American Desert.”
- **1946:** *Les Mobiles de Calder*. An essay on Alexander Calder’s kinetic sculpture; art criticism written in response to objects in motion. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1946:** *Présence noire*. A short intervention on Black writing and anticolonial culture. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1947:** *Jean-Paul Sartre présente Gjon Mili*. A photographic-book introduction whose object is a collaboration between Sartre’s text and Mili’s images.
- **1947:** *Nick’s Bar, New York City*. A prose response to a photograph by Gjon Mili; text and image form the intended object together. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1947:** *Sculptures à n dimensions*. An exhibition introduction for David Hare, made for the apparatus of an art exhibition. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1948:** *Orphée noir*. A preface to Léopold Sédar Senghor’s anthology of Black and Malagasy poetry; it became an autonomous essay but remains structurally attached to another maker’s anthology.
- **1954:** *D’une Chine à l’autre*. A preface to Henri Cartier-Bresson’s photographic book; words and photographs make a composite object.
- **1960:** *Ouragan sur le sucre*. A series of reports on revolutionary Cuba, later collected in *Situations VI*.
- **1967:** *Préface aux Temps des assassins*. A political preface to Mohamed Boudia’s writings.
- **1968:** *The Burgos Trial / declarations on political prisoners*. Short statements and appeals whose primary form is political action rather than durable literary composition.
- **1970–1974:** *La Cause du peuple* and *J’accuse*. Editorial statements, reports, interviews, and signed appeals made within collective militant newspapers; individual authorship is often uncertain or shared, so the corpus resists conversion into a clean list of sole-authored works.
- **1973:** *Libération*. Sartre helped found and publicly embody the newspaper but did not “author” the collective institution; it belongs here as a political-media project rather than a text.

### Periodicals and editorial projects

- **1945–1980:** *Les Temps modernes*. A review founded by Sartre with Simone de Beauvoir, Maurice Merleau-Ponty, Raymond Aron, Michel Leiris, Jean Paulhan, and others. Sartre’s founding presentation acts as a manifesto, while the journal itself is a continuing collective project rather than his sole work.
- **1945:** *Présentation des Temps modernes*. The journal’s founding manifesto, defining literature as situated action and setting a protocol for intellectual engagement.
- **1970–1974:** *La Cause du peuple*. Sartre served as a legally responsible director during state repression; the role was itself a political act, but the newspaper remained collectively made.
- **1973–1974:** *Libération*. Sartre was a co-founding public figure and contributor; the newspaper is an institutional creation involving many makers, not a Sartre text.

### The *Situations* obra

- **1947:** *Situations I: Essais critiques*. A made collection of philosophical and literary criticism; later “new editions” alter its contents and chronology, so they are editorial reconstructions rather than simple reprints.
- **1948:** *Situations II: Littérature et engagement*. A collection centred on committed literature, including *Qu’est-ce que la littérature?*
- **1949:** *Situations III: Lendemains de guerre*. A collection of political, travel, and postwar essays.
- **1964:** *Situations IV: Portraits*. A collection of studies and memorial portraits.
- **1964:** *Situations V: Colonialisme et néo-colonialisme*. A collection of anti-colonial essays and reportage.
- **1964:** *Situations VI: Problèmes du marxisme, 1*. A collection of political essays including work on communism and Cuba.
- **1965:** *Situations VII: Problèmes du marxisme, 2*. A further collection of political and philosophical interventions.
- **1972:** *Situations VIII: Autour de 68*. A collection concerned with intellectuals, revolt, and the events surrounding May 1968.
- **1972:** *Situations IX: Mélanges*. A heterogeneous collection whose refusal of a single subject is declared by its subtitle.
- **1976:** *Situations X: Politique et autobiographie*. A collection joining political interventions to spoken self-portraiture.
- **1947–1976:** *Situations I–X*. The ten-volume series is itself an obra: Sartre repeatedly remade ephemeral articles, lectures, prefaces, interviews, and manifestos into a retrospective body of work. The later chronological edition redistributes and adds texts and therefore should not be confused with Sartre’s own ten-volume arrangement. [BnF account of all ten volumes](https://expositions.bnf.fr/sartre/reperes/oeuvres/situations.htm)

### Diaries, notebooks, correspondence, and autobiographical speech

- **1939–1940; published 1983 and enlarged 1995:** *Carnets de la drôle de guerre*. Wartime notebooks, incomplete because several original notebooks are missing; posthumous editions differ as additional material was recovered. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1926–1963; published 1983:** *Lettres au Castor et à quelques autres*. Sartre’s correspondence edited by Simone de Beauvoir in two volumes; omissions and editorial selection mean it is not the complete correspondence and not a book designed by Sartre. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1926–1939; published 1983:** *Lettres au Castor et à quelques autres, tome I*. The first editorially constituted volume of the correspondence. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1940–1963; published 1983:** *Lettres au Castor et à quelques autres, tome II*. The second editorially constituted volume; surviving letters extend beyond a single addressee. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1926–1939; published 1985:** *Lettres à Simone Jollivet*. A posthumously edited correspondence, personal writing not originally made as a public book.
- **1938–1940; published 1986 and 1990:** *Lettres à Wanda Kosakiewicz*. A posthumously published correspondence whose surviving and selected form is editorially determined.
- **1974; published 1981:** *Entretiens avec Jean-Paul Sartre, août–septembre 1974*. Extended spoken autobiography elicited and edited by Simone de Beauvoir; it is jointly produced testimony, not a manuscript authored by Sartre alone.
- **1975:** *Autoportrait à soixante-dix ans*. A long interview with Michel Contat; because failing eyesight had prevented sustained writing, the work exists as dialogic speech shaped by interviewer and editor.
- **1978; published 1990:** *Entretiens Sartre–Arlette Elkaïm-Sartre–Benny Lévy*. Conversations rejected for publication at the time and issued posthumously; their status and authority are consequently contested. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)

### Posthumously constituted collections

- **1983:** *Cahiers pour une morale*. An editor-made publication of unfinished ethical notebooks, not Sartre’s completed promised ethics. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1983:** *Carnets de la drôle de guerre*. The first posthumous book-form construction of Sartre’s surviving wartime notebooks. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1984:** *Le Scénario Freud*. The posthumous publication of Sartre’s unfilmed versions of the screenplay. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1985:** *Critique de la raison dialectique, tome II: L’Intelligibilité de l’histoire*. A posthumously edited unfinished manuscript. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1989:** *Vérité et existence*. A posthumously established philosophical text written in 1948. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1990:** *Écrits de jeunesse*. An editorial collection of early texts, fragments, collaborations, and notebooks; the collection is not itself an arrangement made by Sartre. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **1991:** *La Reine Albemarle ou le dernier touriste*. An editorial selection from an unfinished Italian travel project. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1991:** *L’Espoir maintenant*. The book-form reissue of Sartre’s disputed final conversations with Benny Lévy. [Chronological bibliography](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liste_des_%C5%93uvres_de_Jean-Paul_Sartre)
- **1995:** *Carnets de la drôle de guerre: nouvelle édition augmentée*. An enlarged reconstruction incorporating an additional recovered notebook; its difference from the 1983 edition demonstrates that the surviving obra is materially incomplete. [Stanford Encyclopedia bibliography](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sartre/index.html)
- **2005:** *Théâtre complet*. A scholarly critical edition establishing Sartre’s theatrical corpus from publications, manuscripts, variants, fragments, and production documents. [Gallimard critical edition](https://www.cercle-enseignement.com/Ouvrages/Gallimard/Bibliotheque-de-la-Pleiade/Theatre-complet14)
- **2010:** *Les Mots et autres écrits autobiographiques*. A critical edition combining published autobiography, posthumous drafts, notebooks, portraits, and spoken materials; it is an editorial map of autobiographical manifestations rather than a book Sartre made under that title.
- **2010–present:** *Situations, nouvelle édition revue, augmentée et chronologique*. A scholarly reconstitution intended to gather Sartre’s articles and interventions chronologically, including texts excluded from his original volumes; it is evidence for the corpus but not itself Sartre’s own arrangement. [Gallimard edition description](https://www.lemonde.fr/livres/article/2024/12/06/situations-ix-janvier-1970-juillet-1975-de-jean-paul-sartre-la-chronique-philosophie-de-roger-pol-droit_6433856_3260.html)
