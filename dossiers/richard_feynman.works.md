## Works by Richard Phillips Feynman

### Early notebooks and theses

- **1933:** *The Calculus for the Practical Man*. A handwritten high-school notebook preserved in the Caltech and Niels Bohr Library archives; it is an unpublished working object rather than a conventional publication.
- **1939:** *Forces and Stresses in Molecules*. Feynman’s thirty-page MIT bachelor’s thesis, supervised by John C. Slater.
- **1942:** *The Principle of Least Action in Quantum Mechanics*. His Princeton doctoral dissertation; the later title *Feynman’s Thesis: A New Approach to Quantum Theory* is a 2005 editorial manifestation, not its original title.

### Scientific papers and research communications

- **1939:** *The Scattering of Cosmic Rays by the Stars of a Galaxy*. A co-authored scientific note with Manuel Sandoval Vallarta.
- **1939:** *Forces in Molecules*. A research paper derived from Feynman’s undergraduate work; it independently obtained what became known as the Hellmann–Feynman theorem, whose priority also belongs to Hans Hellmann.
- **1941:** *Reaction of the Absorber as the Mechanism of Radiative Damping*. A one-page conference abstract made with John A. Wheeler.
- **1945:** *Interaction with the Absorber as the Mechanism of Radiation*. A full research paper made with John A. Wheeler.
- **1948:** *Pocono Conference*. A short report on the conference at which Feynman presented his developing formulation of quantum electrodynamics.
- **1948:** *Space-Time Approach to Non-Relativistic Quantum Mechanics*. A research paper establishing the path-integral formulation.
- **1948:** *A Relativistic Cut-Off for Classical Electrodynamics*. A research paper.
- **1948:** *Relativistic Cut-Off for Quantum Electrodynamics*. A research paper.
- **1949:** *Equations of State of Elements Based on the Generalized Fermi-Thomas Theory*. A research paper with Nicholas Metropolis and Edward Teller, arising from previously classified work.
- **1949:** *The Theory of Positrons*. A research paper giving Feynman’s space-time interpretation of positrons.
- **1949:** *Space-Time Approach to Quantum Electrodynamics*. A research paper presenting his diagrammatic method and formulation of quantum electrodynamics.
- **1949:** *Classical Electrodynamics in Terms of Direct Interparticle Action*. A research paper with John A. Wheeler.
- **1950:** *Mathematical Formulation of the Quantum Theory of Electromagnetic Interaction*. A research paper completing the formal presentation of Feynman’s quantum-electrodynamical method.
- **1951:** *Estado atual dos conhecimentos sobre os mésons* (*Current State of Knowledge about Mesons*). A Portuguese-language conference abstract; the bibliography records only the abstract, not a recoverable full paper.
- **1951:** *Uma nova notação para o cálculo operacional* (*A New Notation for the Operational Calculus*). A Portuguese-language conference abstract.
- **1951:** *An Operator Calculus Having Applications in Quantum Electrodynamics*. A research paper introducing an ordering notation for noncommuting operators.
- **1952:** *Radiative Corrections to Compton Scattering*. A research paper with Laurie M. Brown.
- **1953:** *Relativistic Correction to the Lamb Shift*. A research paper with Michel Baranger and Hans Bethe.
- **1953:** *The λ-Transition in Liquid Helium*. A brief scientific communication; some databases mistranscribe λ as “gamma.”
- **1953:** *Atomic Theory of the λ Transition in Helium*. A research paper.
- **1953:** *Atomic Theory of Liquid Helium Near Absolute Zero*. A research paper.
- **1954:** *Atomic Theory of the Two-Fluid Model of Liquid Helium*. A research paper.
- **1954:** *Proton-Neutron Mass Difference*. A brief research communication with G. Speisman.
- **1955:** *Slow Electrons in a Polar Crystal*. A research paper developing Feynman’s variational treatment of the polaron.
- **1956:** *Energy Spectrum of the Excitations in Liquid Helium*. A research paper with Michael Cohen.
- **1956:** *Physical Conditions for Ferromagnetic Resonance*. A brief communication with J. E. Mercereau.
- **1957:** *Theory of Inelastic Scattering of Cold Neutrons from Liquid Helium*. A research paper with Michael Cohen.
- **1957:** *Superfluidity and Superconductivity*. A review and comparative theoretical essay.
- **1958:** *Theory of the Fermi Interaction*. A research paper with Murray Gell-Mann proposing the vector-minus-axial-vector form of weak interaction.
- **1962:** *Mobility of Slow Electrons in a Polar Crystal*. A research paper with Robert Hellwarth, C. K. Iddings, and Philip Platzman.
- **1964:** *Group U(6) × U(6) Generated by Current Components*. A research communication with Murray Gell-Mann and George Zweig.
- **1966:** *Development of the Space-Time View of Quantum Electrodynamics*. Feynman’s Nobel lecture, functioning both as scientific autobiography and retrospective account of a method.
- **1969:** *Very High-Energy Collisions of Hadrons*. A short research paper associated with the parton model.
- **1970:** *Velocity Acquired by an Electron in a Finite Electric Field in a Polar Crystal*. A research paper with Karl Thornber.
- **1970:** *Some Comments on Baryonic States*. A research paper with Sandip Pakvasa and S. F. Tuan.
- **1971:** *Current Matrix Elements from a Relativistic Quark Model*. A research paper with Michael Kislinger and F. Ravndal.
- **1974:** *Structure of the Proton*. A review article and synthesis of the parton picture.
- **1977:** *Quark Elastic Scattering as a Source of High-Transverse-Momentum Mesons*. A research paper with Richard Field.
- **1977:** *Correlations Among Particles and Jets Produced with Large Transverse Momenta*. A research paper with Richard Field and Geoffrey Fox.
- **1978:** *A Parametrization of the Properties of Quark Jets*. A research paper with Richard Field.
- **1978:** *Quantum-Chromodynamic Approach for the Large-Transverse-Momentum Production of Particles and Jets*. A research paper with Richard Field and Geoffrey Fox.
- **1981:** *The Qualitative Behavior of Yang-Mills Theory in 2+1 Dimensions*. A research paper.
- **1982:** *Simulating Physics with Computers*. A conference paper that formulates the project of quantum simulation.
- **1986:** *Quantum Mechanical Computers*. A lecture-paper elaborating quantum computation.
- **1986:** *Effective Classical Partition Functions*. A research paper with Hagen Kleinert.
- **1988:** *An Outsider’s Inside View of the Challenger Inquiry*. An article derived from Feynman’s work on the Rogers Commission; it is investigative testimony as well as memoir.
- **1963; republished 2000:** *The Theory of a General Quantum System Interacting with a Linear Dissipative System*. A research paper with Frank Vernon Jr.; the 2000 journal appearance is a republication, not a new work.

### Los Alamos reports

- **1943:** *Absolute Measurement of γ₂₅ with Long Counter*. A three-page Los Alamos report with Joseph L. McKibben and Thomas M. Snyder.
- **1944:** *Intensity Fluctuations of a Neutron Chain Reactor*. A Los Alamos report with Frederic de Hoffmann and Robert Serber.
- **1946:** *Introduction to Atomic Energy*. An edited and declassified Manhattan Project instructional report associated with Robert Bacher and other Los Alamos contributors; bibliographic records disagree about its exact author order.
- **1946:** *Statistical Behavior of Neutron Chains*. A Los Alamos technical report.
- **1946:** *Neutron Diffusion in a Space Lattice of Fissionable and Absorbing Materials*. A Los Alamos technical report with Theodore Welton.
- **1946:** *A Theorem and Its Application to Finite Tampers*. A Los Alamos technical report.
- **1947:** *The Calculation of Critical Masses Including the Effects of the Distribution of Neutron Energies*. A Los Alamos report with Theodore Welton.
- **1947 or 1949:** *Equations of State of Elements Based on the Generalized Fermi-Thomas Theory*. A report with Nicholas Metropolis and Edward Teller. The bibliography explicitly disputes whether 1947 or 1949 is the correct report date and whether differently numbered records represent the same object; it later became the 1949 journal paper.

### Lecture courses, pedagogical obra, and books made from speech

- **1951:** *High Energy Phenomena and Meson Theories*. Notes made with Carl Helstrom, Malvin Ruderman, and William Karzas from Feynman’s January–March Caltech course; the recorded author order is uncertain.
- **1953:** *Física Nuclear Teórica* (*Theoretical Nuclear Physics*). Portuguese lecture notes edited by Guido Rawitscher from Feynman’s Brazilian teaching; this is a mediated transcript rather than a book written directly by Feynman.
- **1961:** *Theory of Fundamental Processes*. A book manifestation of Cornell lectures transcribed and edited by Peter Carruthers and Michael Nauenberg.
- **1961:** *Quantum Electrodynamics*. A book made from lectures recorded and edited by Albert Hibbs; it should not be mistaken for a continuously authored monograph.
- **1961–1963; published 1963–1965:** *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, Volumes I–III. A three-volume pedagogical obra based on Feynman’s Caltech lectures and made into books by Robert B. Leighton and Matthew Sands; the lectures are Feynman’s, but the written manifestation is explicitly collaborative and editorial.
- **1962–1963; published 1995:** *Feynman Lectures on Gravitation*. Caltech lecture notes reconstructed and edited posthumously by Fernando Morínigo, William Wagner, and Brian Hatfield. It resists simple authorship because the surviving book is an editorial reconstruction of lectures rather than a manuscript Feynman prepared for publication.
- **1964; published 1965:** *The Character of Physical Law*. The Messenger Lectures delivered at Cornell and converted into a book; the lecture-series and book are manifestations of the same pedagogical obra.
- **1965:** *Quantum Mechanics and Path Integrals*. A technical monograph made with Albert Hibbs.
- **1972:** *Statistical Mechanics: A Set of Lectures*. A graduate-level lecture book prepared with assistance from Jacob Shaham.
- **1979; published 1985:** *QED: The Strange Theory of Light and Matter*. Four Douglas Robb Memorial Lectures transformed into a book with editorial assistance from Ralph Leighton.
- **1986; published 1987:** *Elementary Particles and the Laws of Physics: The 1986 Dirac Memorial Lectures*. A shared lecture volume containing Feynman’s *The Reason for Antiparticles* and Steven Weinberg’s separate lecture; the volume as a whole is not solely Feynman’s work.
- **1964; published 1996:** *Feynman’s Lost Lecture: The Motion of Planets Around the Sun*. A Caltech lecture recovered from notes and audio and reconstructed by David and Judith Goodstein. The posthumous book is partly their archival project, not simply an authored Feynman book.
- **1962–1963; published 2005:** *Feynman’s Tips on Physics*. Four previously unpublished problem-solving lectures supplemented and edited by Michael Gottlieb and Ralph Leighton. The volume includes material by other hands and is a late editorial manifestation of the Caltech course.
- **Lectures delivered principally in the 1980s; published 1996:** *Lectures on Computation*. A posthumously edited set of lectures prepared by Anthony Hey and Robin Allen; its book form was not completed by Feynman.
- **1964–1966; published 1998:** *The Meaning of It All: Thoughts of a Citizen-Scientist*. Three public lectures, originally titled “A Scientist Looks at Society,” issued posthumously; the book title and arrangement are editorial.
- **Lectures delivered 1960s–1980s; published 1994:** *Six Easy Pieces*. An editorial selection of six chapters from *The Feynman Lectures on Physics*, not a new work by Feynman.
- **Lectures delivered 1960s–1980s; published 1997:** *Six Not-So-Easy Pieces*. Another editorial selection from the larger lecture obra, not a separately composed book.
- **Various dates; published 1999:** *The Pleasure of Finding Things Out*. Jeffrey Robbins’s posthumous anthology of interviews, speeches, and essays. It is a new editorial creation assembled from older Feynman manifestations rather than a book Feynman planned.

### Public lectures, protocols, manifestos, and investigations

- **1947:** *What Will the Fundamental Particles Turn Out to Be?*. A reported contribution to a Princeton conference volume. The bibliography warns that the title is uncertain and that the cited volume could not be located in several catalogs, so this item’s bibliographical existence remains unresolved.
- **1959:** *There’s Plenty of Room at the Bottom*. A lecture proposing atom-scale fabrication. It operates partly as a manifesto and partly as a protocol, because it not only imagines a field but issues two concrete prize challenges.
- **1966:** *Address to Far Rockaway High School Audience*. A speech preserved in the Caltech Archives rather than a conventional publication.
- **1974:** *Cargo Cult Science*. Feynman’s Caltech commencement address, later printed in *Engineering and Science*; it is an ethical manifesto for scientific integrity.
- **1975:** *Los Alamos from Below*. An autobiographical public talk recorded at the University of California, Santa Barbara; later print and audio manifestations derive from the performance.
- **1979:** *The Douglas Robb Memorial Lectures*. Four public lectures on quantum electrodynamics; these are the performed obra later manifested as *QED*.
- **1981:** *The Pleasure of Finding Things Out*. A BBC Horizon interview-film. It is a distinct audiovisual work from the later anthology carrying the same title.
- **1983:** *Fun to Imagine*. A BBC series of filmed conversations about physical phenomena; its several broadcast segments form a conversational audiovisual obra rather than a written book.
- **1983:** *Quantum Mechanical View of Reality*. A recorded workshop at Esalen; the event and its surviving recording are different manifestations.
- **1984:** *Tiny Machines: The Feynman Talk on Nanotechnology*. A filmed lecture revisiting atom-scale machines.
- **1985:** *Idiosyncratic Thinking Workshop*. A recorded workshop or conversational performance.
- **1986:** *Personal Observations on the Reliability of the Shuttle*. Feynman’s appendix to the Rogers Commission report. It is simultaneously a technical finding, dissenting intervention, and protocol for thinking about engineering risk.
- **1987:** *Mr. Feynman Goes to Washington*. An article about the Challenger inquiry and a precursor or alternate manifestation of material later used in his memoir.
- **1988:** *It’s as Simple as One, Two, Three…*. A posthumously published lecture-text on computation and physical law.

### Autobiographical recordings and edited story-books

- **Stories recorded principally in the 1970s–1980s; published 1985:** *“Surely You’re Joking, Mr. Feynman!”: Adventures of a Curious Character*. A collaborative oral-history object: Feynman told the stories, Ralph Leighton recorded and edited them, and Edward Hutchings assisted in shaping the book. It resists the category of conventionally authored autobiography.
- **Stories and documents of various dates; published 1988:** *“What Do You Care What Other People Think?”: Further Adventures of a Curious Character*. A second Ralph Leighton-edited collection combining oral narratives, letters, and the Challenger account; Feynman died before publication.
- **Letters written 1939–1987; published 2005:** *Perfectly Reasonable Deviations from the Beaten Track: The Letters of Richard P. Feynman*. A posthumous selection edited by Michelle Feynman. The individual letters are Feynman’s made things; the collection, title, and arrangement are his daughter’s.
- **Published 2006:** *Classic Feynman: All the Adventures of a Curious Character*. Ralph Leighton’s reordered omnibus of the two earlier story-books, with an audio disc. It is an editorial recombination rather than a new Feynman obra.

### Recorded performances and audiovisual collaborations

- **1972:** *Take the World from Another Point of View*. A filmed conversation with Feynman; its dialogic form makes authorship shared rather than singular.
- **Date not fixed:** *Safecracker Suite*. A recording that intersperses Feynman’s drumming with spoken safecracking stories; it is a hybrid musical and autobiographical object.
- **Date not fixed:** *Nature of Matter*. A recorded lecture series attributed to Feynman; the available bibliographies do not establish a single authoritative production date.
- **Date not fixed:** *Computers from the Inside Out*. A filmed lecture or instructional recording; surviving listings do not supply a secure date.
- **Recorded before 1988; released 1999:** *Tuva Groove (Bolur Daa-Bol, Bolbas Daa-Bol)*. A musical collage by Kongar-ool Ondar incorporating samples of Feynman’s drumming, chanting, and speech. Feynman contributed recorded material, but the finished track is not solely his composition.
- **Recorded before 1988; released 1999:** *Kargyraa Rap (Dürgen Chugaa)*. Another Ondar track sampling Feynman. It belongs here as a collaborative manifestation, not as a song authored by Feynman.

### Drawings and paintings

- **1962–1987:** *Approximately one hundred catalogued drawings and paintings*. Feynman made portraits, figure studies, black-and-white drawings, and a smaller number of color works, sometimes signing them “Ofey.” *The Art of Richard P. Feynman: Images by a Curious Character* is a selected posthumous catalogue compiled by Michelle Feynman, not a catalogue raisonné; consequently the individual surviving artworks cannot honestly be presented as a complete, securely titled list.
- **Dates and titles variably recorded:** *Uncatalogued sketches, studies, and drawings*. These objects resist treatment as fixed textual “works”: many lack stable titles, dates, or an authoritative inventory, and the published art volume explicitly selects rather than exhausts them.

### Conceptual and notational inventions

- **Late 1940s:** *Feynman diagrams*. A graphical calculational protocol developed for quantum electrodynamics. No single drawing constitutes the whole work: the obra is a repeatable notation and method embodied across papers, talks, and countless working diagrams.
- **1942–1948:** *The path-integral formulation of quantum mechanics*. A theoretical and mathematical project developed from the dissertation through the 1948 paper; it is a method distributed across several texts rather than a separately bounded object.
- **Late 1960s:** *The parton model*. A conceptual model of high-energy hadrons developed through lectures and papers. It likewise resists classification as one text or one dated artifact.
- **1981–1986:** *Quantum simulation and quantum computation*. A research project articulated through *Simulating Physics with Computers* and *Quantum Mechanical Computers*, rather than a single finished machine or book.
