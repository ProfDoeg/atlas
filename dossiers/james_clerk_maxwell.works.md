## Works by James Clerk Maxwell

### Books, treatises, and edited volumes

- **1859:** *On the Stability of the Motion of Saturn’s Rings*. An Adams Prize essay issued as a monograph; a shorter notice and related paper are manifestations of the same investigation rather than separate books. [B; SP]
- **1870:** *Theory of Heat*. A scientific textbook repeatedly revised by Maxwell through the fifth edition of 1877; the famous “sorting demon” appears as a conceptual creation within this obra, not as a separately titled work. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism*. A two-volume theoretical treatise and Maxwell’s principal electromagnetic synthesis; he began revising it, but the second edition was completed posthumously by W. D. Niven. [B]
- **1873:** *Matter and Motion*. A compact instructional treatise in the “Manuals of Elementary Science” series; later dates commonly encountered are reissues and translations, not new obras. [B]
- **1879:** *The Electrical Researches of the Honourable Henry Cavendish, F.R.S.* An edition reconstructed by Maxwell from Cavendish’s manuscripts, supplemented by Maxwell’s experiments and commentary; it is an editorial-scholarly obra rather than an authored Cavendish text. [B; EC]
- **unfinished at death; published 1881:** *An Elementary Treatise on Electricity*. An incomplete textbook project edited and supplied with material by William Garnett after Maxwell’s death; its mixed status must not be concealed by treating the 1881 volume as wholly completed by Maxwell. [B]

### Mathematical and mechanical papers

- **1846; published 1851:** *On the Description of Oval Curves, and Those Having a Plurality of Foci*. Maxwell’s first paper to be read, written while he was fourteen; Professor James Forbes presented it, and the delayed printing included Forbes’s remarks. [B]
- **1849:** *On the Theory of Rolling Curves*. A mathematical memoir and Maxwell’s first printed paper. [B]
- **1850; published 1853:** *On the Equilibrium of Elastic Solids*. A substantial memoir on elasticity, later printed alongside related problem solutions that the bibliography attributes to Charles Anthony Swainson rather than Maxwell. [B]
- **1853; published 1854:** *On a Particular Case of the Descent of a Heavy Body in a Resisting Medium*. A short mathematical-mechanical paper. [B]
- **1854; published 1856:** *On the Transformation of Surfaces by Bending*. A differential-geometrical memoir, also manifested as a brief notice in the Cambridge Philosophical Society proceedings. [B; SP]
- **1856:** *On an Instrument to Illustrate Poinsot’s Theory of Rotation*. A short communication describing an experimental-mechanical visualization. [SP]
- **1857:** *On a Dynamical Top for Illustrating the Phenomena of the Motion of a System of Invariable Form about a Fixed Point, with Some Suggestions as to the Earth’s Motion*. Both a theoretical paper and a protocol for using Maxwell’s dynamical top. [SP; NMS]
- **1864:** *On Reciprocal Figures and Diagrams of Forces*. A paper in graphical statics, later enlarged into the 1872 memoir. [B; SP]
- **1864:** *On the Calculation of the Equilibrium and Stiffness of Frames*. A structural-mechanics paper. [B; SP]
- **1867:** *On the Equilibrium of a Spherical Envelope*. A mathematical paper on a mechanical surface. [B; SP]
- **1868:** *On the Cyclide*. A geometrical memoir. [SP]
- **1868:** *On Governors*. A stability analysis of feedback regulators, now treated as a foundational work in control theory; the historical label “cybernetics” is retrospective. [SP]
- **1869:** *The Construction of Stereograms of Surfaces*. A very short communication on representing surfaces stereographically. [B; SP]
- **1869:** *On Reciprocal Diagrams in Space and Their Relation to Airy’s Function of Stress*. A paper extending graphical statics into three dimensions. [B; SP]
- **1869:** *On J. B. Listing’s Paper “On the Kinds of Cyclosis in Lines, Surfaces, and Regions of Space”*. A mathematical comment on Listing’s classification. [B]
- **1870:** *On Hills and Dales*. A topological-mathematical essay on critical points of surfaces; a further notice appeared in 1871. [B; SP]
- **1870:** *On the Displacement in a Case of Fluid Motion*. A mathematical paper on fluid displacement. [SP]
- **1870–1871:** *Remarks on Mr Hanlon’s Paper on the Vena Contracta*. A brief response concerning contracted fluid jets. [SP]
- **1871–1873:** *On the Theory of a System of Electrical Conductors, and Other Physical Theories Involving Homogeneous Quadratic Functions*. A mathematical-physical paper; it is also associated with Maxwell’s introduction of “curl” into English vector terminology. [SP; MF]
- **1872:** *On Reciprocal Figures, Frames, and Diagrams of Forces*. A major memoir in graphical statics, incorporating a girder diagram supplied by Fleeming Jenkin; the abstract is a manifestation of this work, not another obra. [B; SP]
- **1872:** *On the Geometrical Mean Distance of Two Figures on a Plane*. A mathematical memoir. [B; SP]
- **1872:** *On the Condition That, in the Transformation of Any Figure by Curvilinear Coordinates in Three Dimensions, Every Angle in the New Figure Shall Be Equal to the Corresponding Angle in the Original Figure*. A short paper on conformal transformation. [SP]
- **1873:** *On the Proof of the Equations of Motion of a Connected System*. A short mechanics paper, printed in 1876. [SP]
- **1873; published 1876:** *On a Problem in the Calculus of Variations in Which the Solution Is Discontinuous*. A one-page mathematical communication. [B; SP]
- **1874:** *Note to Mr Röhr’s Paper on “Spheric and Cylindric Motion in Viscous Fluid”*. A brief mathematical comment, dependent on Röhr’s paper and therefore resistant to treatment as a wholly autonomous work. [SP]
- **1876:** *On Bow’s Method of Drawing Diagrams in Graphical Statics, with Illustrations from Peaucellier’s Linkage*. A paper combining geometrical construction, mechanism, and graphical protocol. [B; SP]
- **1877; published 1880:** *On Approximate Multiple Integration between Limits of Summation*. A posthumously printed mathematical paper. [SP]
- **1877; published 1880:** *On a Paradox in the Theory of Attraction*. A posthumously printed mathematical paper. [B; SP]
- **written by 1879; published 1880:** *Harmonic Analysis*. An encyclopaedia article published after Maxwell’s death. [B; SP]

### Colour, vision, and optical works

- **1855:** *On the Theory of Colours in Relation to Colour-Blindness*. A letter-paper first printed within George Wilson’s *Researches on Colour-Blindness* and reprinted independently in 1856; these are two manifestations of one text. [B]
- **1855; published 1857:** *Experiments on Colour, as Perceived by the Eye, with Remarks on Colour-Blindness*. An experimental memoir establishing quantitative colour-mixture relations. [SP]
- **1856:** *On a Method of Drawing the Theoretical Forms of Faraday’s Lines of Force without Calculation*. A one-page graphical-method communication. [SP]
- **1856; published 1857:** *On the Theory of Compound Colours with Reference to Mixtures of Blue and Yellow Light*. A short colour-science paper. [B; SP]
- **1856; published 1857:** *On the Unequal Sensibility of the Foramen Centrale to Light of Different Colours*. A short physiological-optics paper. [B; SP]
- **1857:** *On the Perception of Colour*. An expository scientific paper on visual colour perception. [SP]
- **1858:** *On the General Laws of Optical Instruments*. A mathematical-optics paper. [B; SP]
- **1859:** *On the Mixture of the Colours of the Spectrum*. A brief conference communication. [B]
- **1859–1860:** *On the Theory of Compound Colours, and the Relations of the Colours of the Spectrum*. A major colour-science memoir; a postscript and proceedings notice are supplementary manifestations. [B; SP]
- **1860:** *On an Instrument for Exhibiting Any Mixture of the Colours of the Spectrum*. A communication about Maxwell’s colour-mixing apparatus. [B]
- **1861:** *On the Theory of Three Primary Colours*. A lecture-paper and demonstration protocol for additive colour synthesis. [B; SP]
- **1868; published 1869:** *On the Best Arrangement for Producing a Pure Spectrum on a Screen*. An experimental-optics paper. [B; SP]
- **1870:** *On Colour Vision at Different Points of the Retina*. A short report in physiological optics. [SP]
- **1871; published 1872:** *On Colour Vision*. A Royal Institution lecture-paper. [B; SP]
- **1872:** *Addition to a Paper by Francis Deas on Spectra Formed by the Passage of Polarised Light through Double-Refracting Crystals*. A supplement to Deas’s paper, not a fully independent memoir. [B]
- **1873:** *On the Focal Lines of a Refracted Pencil*. A geometrical-optics paper. [SP]
- **1873:** *On Double Refraction in a Viscous Fluid in Motion*. A brief paper coupling optics and fluid mechanics. [SP]
- **1874:** *On Hamilton’s Characteristic Function for a Narrow Beam of Light*. A mathematical-optics paper published in 1875. [B; SP]
- **1874; published 1876:** *On the Relation of Geometrical Optics to Other Parts of Mathematics and Physics*. A short connective or programmatic paper. [SP]
- **1875:** *On the Application of Hamilton’s Characteristic Function to the Theory of an Optical Instrument Symmetrical about Its Axis*. A mathematical-optics paper. [B; SP]
- **1875; published 1876:** *On the Centre of Motion of the Eye*. A short physiological-optics communication. [SP]

### Electromagnetism and electrical measurement

- **1855–1856; published in full 1864:** *On Faraday’s Lines of Force*. A major theoretical memoir first circulated as an abstract in 1856; the abstract, full printing, and later reprint are manifestations of one work. [B; SP]
- **1861–1862:** *On Physical Lines of Force*. A four-part project applying a mechanical-vortex model successively to magnetism, currents, electrostatics, and magneto-optics. [B; SP]
- **1864–1865:** *A Dynamical Theory of the Electromagnetic Field*. A theoretical synthesis first abstracted in 1864 and printed in full in 1865; the later four-equation formulation conventionally called “Maxwell’s equations” is not a separately authored text. [B; SP]
- **1863–1864:** *Description of an Experimental Measurement of Electrical Resistance, Made at King’s College*. A collaborative report with Fleeming Jenkin embedded in the British Association committee report; Maxwell wrote parts I, III, and IV, while Jenkin wrote part II. [B]
- **1864–1865:** *Description of a Further Experimental Measurement of Electrical Resistance Made at King’s College*. A collaborative experimental report by Maxwell, Fleeming Jenkin, and Charles Hockin. [B; SP]
- **1867:** *On the Theory of the Maintenance of Electric Currents by Mechanical Work without the Use of Permanent Magnets*. A paper on electromagnetic generation. [B; SP]
- **1868:** *On Mr Grove’s “Experiment in Electro-Magnetic Induction”*. A short critical or explanatory note. [SP]
- **1868–1869:** *On a Method of Making a Direct Comparison of Electrostatic with Electromagnetic Force; with a Note on the Electromagnetic Theory of Light*. An experimental-theoretical paper with several abstract manifestations. [B; SP]
- **1869–1870:** *Experiments on the Value of v, the Ratio of the Electromagnetic to the Electrostatic Unit of Electricity*. An experimental report closely related to the preceding paper, but catalogued separately. [B; SP]
- **1869–1871:** *Can the Potential of a Uniform Circular Disk at Any Point Be Expressed by Means of Elliptic Integrals?* A one-page mathematical-electrical question or note. [SP]
- **1872:** *On the Induction of Electric Currents in an Infinite Plane Sheet of Uniform Conductivity*. An electromagnetic paper. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *On Action at a Distance*. A lecture-paper arguing about the conceptual form of physical action; it is expository and philosophical as well as scientific. [B; SP]
- **1874–1875:** *On the Application of Kirchhoff’s Rules for Electric Circuits to the Solution of a Geometrical Problem*. A one-page electrical-mathematical note. [B; SP]
- **1876:** *On the Solution of Electrical Problems by the Transformation of Conjugate Functions*. A one-page mathematical method. [SP]
- **1876:** *Theory of Electrical Induction*. A brief explanatory article in *Nature*. [SP]
- **1876; published 1878:** *Note on Mr George Forbes’s Paper “On Diamagnetic Rotation”*. A short comment on another author’s paper. [SP]
- **1876–1877:** *Report of the Committee for Testing Experimentally Ohm’s Law*. A collaborative experimental report with J. D. Everett and Arthur Schuster; Niven’s collected edition misleadingly retitled the excerpt *On Ohm’s Law*. [B; SP]
- **1878:** *On the Electrical Capacity of a Long Narrow Cylinder, and of a Disk of Sensible Thickness*. A mathematical-electrical paper. [B; SP]
- **1879; published 1880:** *On a Possible Mode of Detecting a Motion of the Solar System through the Luminiferous Ether*. A posthumous experimental proposal, not a completed experiment. [SP]

### Heat, gases, molecules, and statistical physics

- **1859–1860:** *Illustrations of the Dynamical Theory of Gases*. A two-part memoir—on collisions of elastic spheres and on diffusion—which introduced the velocity distribution now associated with Maxwell. [B; SP]
- **1859; published 1860:** *On the Dynamical Theory of Gases*. A brief preliminary report, distinct from but preparatory to *Illustrations*. [B]
- **1860–1861:** *On the Results of Bernoulli’s Theory of Gases as Applied to Their Internal Friction, Their Diffusion, and Their Conductivity for Heat*. A conference report on molecular transport. [B]
- **1866:** *On the Viscosity or Internal Friction of Air and Other Gases*. A Bakerian Lecture and experimental-theoretical memoir; its proceedings abstract is another manifestation. [B; SP]
- **1866–1868:** *On the Dynamical Theory of Gases*. A major later memoir, first abstracted in 1867 and printed in full in 1868. The apparent title *On the Final Distribution of Velocity among the Molecules of Two Systems…* is only a subheading, not a separate paper. [B]
- **1873:** *On Loschmidt’s Experiments on Diffusion in Relation to the Kinetic Theory of Gases*. A critical scientific note. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *On the Final State of a System of Molecules in Motion Subject to Forces of Any Kind*. A short statistical-mechanical paper. [SP]
- **1873:** *Molecules* / *A Discourse on Molecules*. A lecture circulated under variant titles in several journals; these are manifestations of one discourse. [B; SP]
- **1874:** *Van der Waals on the Continuity of the Gaseous and Liquid States*. A review and exposition rather than an original memoir. [SP]
- **1875:** *On the Dynamical Evidence of the Molecular Constitution of Bodies*. A lecture-paper on molecular theory. [B; SP]
- **1876:** *Diffusion of Gases through Absorbing Substances*. A review of research on gas diffusion, not a new experimental report by Maxwell. [B; SP]
- **1876–1877:** *On the Equilibrium of Heterogeneous Substances*. A brief exposition of J. Willard Gibbs’s work rather than Maxwell’s independent theory. [B; SP]
- **1878–1879:** *On Stresses in Rarefied Gases Arising from Inequalities of Temperature*. A memoir, with an appendix added in May 1879; its 1878 abstract is a preliminary manifestation. The contemporary priority of explanations of radiometer forces involving Maxwell and P. G. Tait is historically disputed. [B; SP]
- **1878; published 1879:** *On Boltzmann’s Theorem on the Average Distribution of Energy in a System of Material Points*. A statistical-mechanical memoir. [B; SP]

### Astronomy and terrestrial physics

- **1856–1859:** *On the Stability of the Motion of Saturn’s Rings*. Besides the book-length Adams Prize essay, Maxwell made a shorter astronomical-society communication under the same title; it is best treated as a manifestation of the larger project. [B; SP]
- **1858; published 1862:** *On Theories of the Constitution of Saturn’s Rings*. A short related paper arguing that the rings must consist of numerous discrete bodies. [B; SP]
- **1859; published 1860:** *On an Instrument for Exhibiting the Motions of Saturn’s Rings*. A report on a working mechanical model rather than another theoretical treatise. [B]
- **1867; published 1869:** *On the Influence of the Motions of the Heavenly Bodies on the Index of Refraction of Light*. A short contribution printed inside William Huggins’s paper; its embedded status makes it bibliographically easy to overlook. [B]
- **1868–1878:** *Reports of the Committee for Investigating the Rate of Increase of Underground Temperature Downwards*. A collaborative, multi-year British Association research project with successive reports; Maxwell’s individual share is not separable from the committee authorship in the published bibliography. [B]

### Scientific instruments, models, and images

- **by 1855:** *New Form of the Platometer*. A physical planimeter for measuring areas of plane figures and a paper describing its construction and use; “instrument” and “text” are two manifestations of one design project. [B; SP]
- **1850s:** *Colour top*. A rotating-disc apparatus devised to make colour equations experimentally; surviving examples were constructed by instrument makers, so Maxwell is securely the designer but not necessarily the fabricator. [SP; NMS]
- **1850s:** *Colour box*. A long optical apparatus for mixing and matching spectral colours; it is an experimental device and protocol rather than a textual work. [SP]
- **1856–1857:** *Dynamical top*. A brass adjustable top devised by Maxwell to visualize rigid-body rotation; examples were made by instrument makers, and the coloured disc of the National Museums Scotland example is missing. [NMS; DT]
- **1859–1860:** *Instrument for exhibiting the motions of Saturn’s rings*. A mechanical model reported to the British Association; the survival and maker of the specific apparatus are not established in the bibliography. [B]
- **1860–1861:** *Instrument for exhibiting any mixture of the colours of the spectrum*. An optical colour-mixing apparatus associated with Maxwell’s colour experiments. [B]
- **1861:** *Tartan-ribbon three-colour projection*. An experimental image-system comprising three monochrome separation photographs, lantern slides, filters, projectors, and their superimposed screen manifestation. Thomas Sutton exposed the plates under Maxwell’s direction, so it resists attribution as a photograph “made by Maxwell” alone; the familiar colour print was produced only in the 1930s. [CP; TR]
- **date uncertain:** *Fish-eye optical design*. Maxwell is credited in the scientific-work survey with devising a wide-field “fish-eye” lens concept, but no independently titled contemporary publication or securely identified Maxwell-made camera is supplied there; it is better classified as an attributed design than as a finished object. [SP]
- **1874:** *Thermodynamic surface model*. A sculptural plaster representation of Gibbs’s thermodynamic surface made by Maxwell and sent to Gibbs; it is a mathematical object and teaching model rather than a publication. [EC]
- **1876:** *General Considerations Concerning Scientific Apparatus*. An essay for the South Kensington Special Loan Collection handbook, functioning partly as a curatorial statement about instrument design. [B; SP]
- **1876:** *Molecular Physics—Instruments Connected with Fluids*. A handbook article classifying and explaining fluid apparatus; Niven shortened its title to *Instruments Connected with Fluids*. [B; SP]

### Standards, engineering protocols, and committee works

- **1863–1868:** *Reports of the Committee on Standards of Electrical Resistance*. A sequence of collaborative British Association reports involving Maxwell, William Thomson, James Joule, Fleeming Jenkin, Charles Hockin, and others; individual experimental sections can be assigned, but the reports as wholes resist single authorship. [B]
- **1867–1868:** *On the Theory of Diagrams of Forces as Applied to Roofs and Bridges*. A short engineering communication. [B]
- **1873:** *Reports of the Committee on Electrical Standards*. A separately issued collaborative volume by the British Association committee, consolidating work on electrical units and resistance standards. [SP]
- **1873–1875:** *Reports of the Committee for the Selection and Nomenclature of Dynamical and Electrical Units*. Collaborative standard-making texts: protocols in the strong sense, because they prescribe the names and relations by which measurements are to be made. [B]
- **1873–1875:** *Reports of the Committee on Siemens’s Electrical Pyrometer*. Collaborative reports on evaluating a measurement instrument; Maxwell’s exact individual textual share is not identified. [B]
- **1875:** *Lightning Conductors*. An engineering article on protective systems. [SP]
- **1875–1876:** *Reports of the Committee for Testing the New Pyrometer of Mr Siemens*. Collaborative experimental protocols and findings. [B]
- **1876:** *On the Protection of Buildings from Lightning*. A practical protocol printed in both the British Association report and *Symons’s Meteorological Magazine*. [SP]

### Encyclopaedia articles, lectures, and scientific exposition

- **date uncertain, 1856–1860:** *Inaugural Address at Marischal College, Aberdeen*. A lecture whose bibliographical reference was already reported as unknown; its existence is recorded, but its text and exact date resist secure cataloguing as a finished publication. [SP]
- **1860:** *Inaugural Lecture at King’s College London*. An academic address published only much later; it is an occasional lecture rather than a contemporary printed paper. [SP]
- **1870–1871:** *Address to the Mathematical and Physical Sections of the British Association*. A presidential address first abstracted in *Nature* and then printed in full. [B; SP]
- **1871:** *Introductory Lecture on Experimental Physics*. Maxwell’s inaugural lecture as Cavendish Professor, laying out a project for experimental teaching and the new laboratory. [SP]
- **1875:** *Atom*. An extensive *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. [B; SP]
- **1875:** *Attraction*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. [B; SP]
- **1876:** *Capillary Action*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. [B; SP]
- **1877:** *Constitution of Bodies*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. [B; SP]
- **1877:** *Diagrams*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* article about visual-mathematical representation. [B; SP]
- **1877:** *Diffusion*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. [B; SP]
- **1878:** *Ether*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* article. [B; SP]
- **1878:** *The Telephone*. The Rede Lecture, subsequently printed in *Nature* and elsewhere. [B; SP]
- **1879:** *Faraday*. An *Encyclopædia Britannica* biography, distinct from Maxwell’s 1873 *Nature* article of the same subject. [B; SP]
- **1879:** *Reports on Special Branches of Science*. A short British Association committee text whose date and source troubled later bibliographers; Niven may have printed it from a secondary source. [B]
- **manuscript by 1879; published 1885:** *Remarks on the Classification of the Physical Sciences* / *Physical Sciences*. A posthumous encyclopaedia article overlooked by earlier accounts; the manuscript and printed titles differ. [B]

### Reviews, biographies, and philosophical essays

- **1856; published posthumously 1882:** *Are There Real Analogies in Nature?* A Cambridge Apostles essay; it was an orally presented philosophical text before becoming a posthumous publication. [B]
- **1873:** *Reprint of Papers on Electrostatics and Magnetism, by Sir William Thomson*. A review. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *Elements of Natural Philosophy, by Professors Sir W. Thomson and P. G. Tait*. A review. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *An Essay on the Mathematical Principles of Physics, by the Rev. James Challis*. A review. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *Faraday*. A scientific-biographical article in *Nature*. [B; SP]
- **1873:** *Does the Progress of Physical Science Tend to Give Any Advantage to the Opinion of Necessity (or Determinism) over That of the Contingency of Events and the Freedom of the Will?* A Cambridge Apostles essay, published posthumously; it is philosophical argument, not a scientific paper. [B; SP]
- **1874:** *Plateau on Soap Bubbles*. A review and exposition of Joseph Plateau’s work. [SP]
- **1874:** *Grove’s “Correlation of Physical Forces”*. A review. [SP]
- **1876:** *Diffusion of Gases through Absorbing Substances*. A scientific review, also classifiable under molecular physics. [B]
- **1876:** *Whewell’s Writings and Correspondence*. A review of Isaac Todhunter’s study and edition. [B; SP]
- **1876; published posthumously 1882:** *On Modified Aspects of Pain*. A Cambridge Apostles essay; its first publication was in the Maxwell biography. [B]
- **1877:** *Hermann Ludwig Ferdinand Helmholtz*. A scientific-biographical article. [B; SP]
- **1877; published 1880:** *On the Unpublished Electrical Papers of the Hon. Henry Cavendish*. A report on Maxwell’s editorial project, printed posthumously. [B; SP]
- **1878:** *Tait’s Thermodynamics*. A substantial review. [B; SP]
- **1878:** *Paradoxical Philosophy*. A review. [B; SP]
- **1878; published posthumously 1882:** *Psychophysik*. A Cambridge Apostles essay; its posthumous title is German, but the text is an English philosophical-psychological paper. [B]
- **1879:** *Practical Physics, Molecular Physics, and Sound, by Frederick Guthrie*. A review. [B]
- **1879:** *Thomson and Tait’s Natural Philosophy*. A review published near the end of Maxwell’s life. [B; SP]

### Verse and occasional verbal works

- **1840s–1879:** *Poems, songs, parodies, and occasional verses*. Maxwell made numerous individually titled and untitled verses—scientific parodies, valentines, college songs, devotional poems, and commemorative pieces—preserved principally in Campbell and Garnett’s biographical selection and later in Harman’s collected letters and papers. This is a dispersed body of work rather than a book Maxwell assembled, and the surviving corpus cannot safely be called complete because much of it remained in letters or private circulation. [CG; LP]
- **1874:** *To the Chief Musician upon Nabla: A Tyndallic Ode*. A scientific parody circulated in connection with the British Association meeting; it acts simultaneously as poem, satire, and intervention in a controversy. [CG; LP]
- **1874:** *A Paradoxical Ode*. A satirical scientific poem associated with Maxwell’s criticism of paradoxical philosophy. [CG; LP]
- **1874:** *Rigid Body Sings*. A comic scientific song treating rotational dynamics as lyric persona; it is a song-text, not a scientific paper. [CG; LP]
- **1874:** *Valentine by a Telegraph Clerk*. An occasional comic poem using electrical and telegraphic language. [CG; LP]
- **1878:** *To the Committee of the Cayley Portrait Fund*. A commemorative poem made for the portrait of mathematician Arthur Cayley; it belongs to the social event and image as much as to literary publication. [CG; LP]
- **date uncertain:** *A Student’s Evening Hymn*. A devotional poem preserved among Maxwell’s occasional writings; sources do not establish a secure composition date. [CG; LP]
- **date uncertain:** *Notes on the President’s Address*. A satirical verse response to a British Association address; its performance and circulation make it resist classification as a stable published “work.” [CG; LP]
