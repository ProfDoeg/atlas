## Works by The Queen of Sheba

### Historically attested obra

- **No secure date:** No surviving text, inscription, building, artwork, or other obra can be securely attributed to the Queen of Sheba. Saba itself was historical, but no contemporary inscription confirms this queen or even a female ruler of Saba; consequently, “c. 966 BC” is a traditional narrative date, not a documented floruit ([British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/BIOG78480)).

### Questions and riddle cycles

- **c. 966 BC (traditional); recorded centuries later:** Untitled “hard questions” addressed to Solomon. This is an oral intellectual performance rather than a book: 1 Kings 10:1–3 asserts that the queen devised and delivered questions but preserves none of their wording, so neither their number nor their original form is recoverable ([1 Kings 10](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.10?lang=bi)).

- **Medieval textual attestations:** The Queen of Sheba’s four riddles in *Midrash Mishle*. These are a named-attribution corpus only in retrospect: the medieval midrash supplies four compositions absent from Kings, while their actual authorship and date remain unknown ([Jewish Encyclopedia, “Midrash to Proverbs”](https://sefaria.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10808-midrash-mishle); Jacob Lassner, *Demonizing the Queen of Sheba*, pp. 161–63, cited in [“Coming of (S)Age”](https://heiup.uni-heidelberg.de/journals/mial/article/download/24769/18784/67866)).

- **By c. 1430 in the surviving attribution:** The nineteen riddles and tests of *Midrash ha-Ḥefez*. This expanded cycle is not securely her obra: several questions depend upon Israelite traditions or people later than the queen’s supposed lifetime, indicating that anonymous transmitters made at least part of the collection and placed it in her mouth ([“Queen of Sheba and Biblical Scholarship,” pp. 77–79](https://free-minds.org/sites/default/files/Sheba.pdf); [Jewish Encyclopedia, “Midrash to Proverbs”](https://sefaria.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10808-midrash-mishle)).

- **Uncertain medieval date:** The riddles and tests in the *Targum Sheni* to Esther. They are another literary manifestation of the riddling queen, not evidence for a recoverable tenth-century composition; the relationship and chronology among the Qurʾanic, targumic, and other late-antique or medieval versions remain disputed ([Jacob Lassner, *Demonizing the Queen of Sheba*](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/D/bo3629684.html); Jillian Stinchcomb, “The Queen of Sheba in the Qurʾān and Late Antique Midrash,” in [*The Study of Islamic Origins*](https://almuslih.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Mortensen-The-Study-of-Islamic-Origins.pdf)).

### Journey-project and diplomatic assemblage

- **c. 966 BC (traditional):** Untitled journey to Jerusalem and test of Solomon. The journey is a project and performed diplomatic encounter, not a freestanding work: Kings narrates her intention, travel, examination of Solomon, and return but supplies no contemporary corroboration ([1 Kings 10:1–13](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.10?lang=bi); [British Museum](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/term/BIOG78480)).

- **c. 966 BC (traditional):** Untitled diplomatic gift of gold, spices, and precious stones. This is best classified as a curated offering or diplomatic assemblage rather than an object manufactured by the queen; Kings attributes its presentation to her but not the making of its components ([1 Kings 10:2, 10](https://www.sefaria.org/I_Kings.10?lang=bi)).

- **Narrated in the seventh-century Qurʾān; event undated:** Untitled gift-embassy sent to Solomon. This is a political project meant to test his intentions, not a titled obra; the Qurʾānic account does not identify its contents or claim that the queen manufactured them ([Qurʾān 27:29–37](https://corpus.quran.com/translation.jsp?chapter=27&verse=35)).

### Political creations attributed in Ethiopian tradition

- **Legendary event; recorded c. 1314–1322:** Menelik I, also called Bayna-Leḥkem or David. The *Kebra Nagast* makes Makeda and Solomon his parents and thereby makes her maternity the origin of Ethiopia’s Solomonic line. A person resists classification as an obra: moreover, this is a medieval dynastic foundation story, not verified tenth-century biography ([Abyssinian Orthodox University, “The Kebra Nagast”](https://abyssinianorthodox.university/open-education/canon/kebra-nagast); E. A. Wallis Budge, trans., [*The Queen of Sheba and Her Only Son Menyelek*](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c2/The_Queen_of_Sheba_%26_her_only_son_Menyelek_%28IA_queenofshebahero00budgrich%29.pdf)).

- **Legendary event; recorded c. 1314–1322:** Untitled coronation and endowment of Menelik. In chapters 86–87 of the *Kebra Nagast*, Makeda transfers the kingdom and royal resources to her son and obtains the nobles’ oath; this is an institutional creation or succession project rather than a material object ([*Kebra Nagast*, contents and chapters 86–87](https://www.tewahedo.dk/litt/cached/www.sacred-texts.com/chr/kn/index.htm); Budge’s [introduction and synopsis](https://original.sacred-texts.com/chr/kn/kn000-5.htm)).

- **Legendary event; recorded c. 1314–1322:** Untitled male-line succession oath. This is a protocol with manifesto-like force: Makeda orders that no woman should again occupy Ethiopia’s throne and restricts the succession to Menelik’s male line. Its attribution belongs to the medieval *Kebra Nagast* and served that text’s legitimating project; it cannot be treated as an authentic decree from c. 966 BC ([*Kebra Nagast*, ch. 87](https://mylordsbible.vercel.app/read/kebra-nagast/87); [Abyssinian Orthodox University](https://abyssinianorthodox.university/open-education/canon/kebra-nagast)).
