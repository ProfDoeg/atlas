## Works by Maimonides

### Rabbinic obra: commentaries, digests, and codes

- **c. 1158:** *Treatise on the Jewish Calendar* (*Maʾamar ha-ʿIbbur*). An astronomical protocol for calculating lunar conjunctions and seasons; it survives through a medieval Hebrew translation, while its exact original title and date are not secure. [The standard classified bibliography identifies its two-part structure](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **c. 1158–1168:** *Commentary on the Mishnah* (*Kitāb al-Sirāj*, “Book of the Lamp”). A complete Judeo-Arabic commentary, legal digest, and pedagogical project, repeatedly revised by Maimonides; its general introduction, “Eight Chapters,” and introduction to *Ḥeleq* later circulated as apparently independent texts but belong to this obra. [The scholarly survey dates the project to 1158–68](https://jewishmedicalethics.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maimonides-rabbi-physician-and-philosopher.pdf), and the [classified bibliography documents its component introductions and transmission](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **before 1168:** *Commentaries on the Babylonian Talmud*. An unfinished or subsequently lost exegetical project covering much of the orders Moʿed, Nashim, and Neziqin and the tractate Ḥullin. Only a purported commentary on Rosh ha-Shanah has been reported, and its authenticity is doubtful, so this obra now chiefly survives as Maimonides’ own reference to what he had made. [The standard account specifies the coverage, loss, and disputed fragment](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **before 1168:** *Hilkhot ha-Yerushalmi* (“Laws of the Jerusalem Talmud”). An early selective digest and commentary rather than a commentary on the whole Palestinian Talmud; Genizah fragments preserve passages copied in Maimonides’ hand, but most of the work is lost. [Cambridge University Library identifies and describes the surviving autograph fragments](https://www.lib.cam.ac.uk/collections/departments/taylor-schechter-genizah-research-unit/fragment-month/fragment-month-14-5).

- **c. 1168–1170:** *Book of the Commandments* (*Kitāb al-Farāʾiḍ*; Hebrew *Sefer ha-Mitzvot*). A classificatory protocol establishing fourteen rules and enumerating the 613 commandments, conceived as groundwork for the larger legal code. [The standard bibliography records the Arabic work and its translations](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni), while the [halakhic survey explains its architectural function](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **c. 1168–1178, with later revisions:** *Mishneh Torah*. A fourteen-book Hebrew legal code intended as a comprehensive, systematically arranged manifestation of the Oral Law rather than a commentary or anthology; Maimonides continued correcting copies after its initial completion. [A scholarly overview dates its composition to 1168–78 and describes its fourteen-book structure](https://jewishmedicalethics.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maimonides-rabbi-physician-and-philosopher.pdf); the [standard account explains its codificatory design](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

### Philosophical and theological obra

- **c. 1185–1191:** *Guide of the Perplexed* (*Dalālat al-Ḥāʾirīn*). A three-part Judeo-Arabic philosophical-theological obra addressed to Joseph ben Judah, designed to guide an educated reader through apparent conflicts between revelation and philosophy. The terminal dates vary among scholarly reconstructions; one overview gives 1187–91. [The work and chronology are summarized here](https://jewishmedicalethics.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maimonides-rabbi-physician-and-philosopher.pdf), and its [Arabic title and textual transmission are documented in the classified bibliography](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

### Manifestos and public epistles

- **c. 1160–1165:** *Epistle on Martyrdom* (*Iggeret ha-Shemad*; *Maʾamar Qiddush ha-Shem*). A manifesto of practical religious resistance arguing how Jews compelled to profess Islam should live and act; its date is approximate, and older scholarship questioned Maimonides’ authorship, although it is included in the modern scholarly collection of his three major public epistles. [The authorship dispute is recorded here](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni), and [Halkin and Hartman’s collected edition includes it](https://books.google.com/books/about/Epistles_of_Maimonides.html?id=7zAlxRzcUoEC).

- **1172:** *Epistle to Yemen* (*Iggeret Teiman*). A pastoral manifesto addressed to Jacob ben Netanel al-Fayyumi and the persecuted Yemenite Jewish community, combining consolation, polemic, practical counsel, and a messianic calculation. [The date, addressee, and circumstances are documented in the standard bibliography](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni); it is also one of the [three works in Halkin and Hartman’s scholarly edition](https://books.google.com/books/about/Epistles_of_Maimonides.html?id=7zAlxRzcUoEC).

- **1191:** *Essay on Resurrection* (*Maʾamar Teḥiyyat ha-Metim*). A public theological defense written in response to accusations that Maimonides denied bodily resurrection; it is therefore an intervention in a controversy as much as a descriptive treatise. [The work is documented in the classified bibliography](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni) and included in the [standard modern edition of the major epistles](https://books.google.com/books/about/Epistles_of_Maimonides.html?id=7zAlxRzcUoEC).

- **1194:** *Letter on Astrology*. A reasoned ruling addressed to scholars of southern France, rejecting astrology as unsupported by demonstration, sense experience, or trustworthy tradition. It resists classification as a “book”: it is simultaneously correspondence, philosophical argument, and communal legal counsel. [The circumstances and argument are summarized in the standard account](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

### Responsa, letters, and administrative texts

- **c. 1165–1204:** *Responsa*. A dispersed corpus of hundreds of rulings answering concrete legal and communal questions, not a single author-designed book; manuscripts, translations, later collections, duplicate answers, and uncertain attributions make its exact boundary unstable. [The standard bibliography records the early collected editions](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni), while a biographical survey reports a corpus of more than 600 responsa](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3721660/).

- **c. 1165–1204:** *Private, scholarly, and communal correspondence*. Letters to family, pupils, translators, officials, congregations, and individual petitioners form an open archival corpus rather than a finished obra; some survive as autograph Genizah documents, others only through copies or translations, and their borders overlap with the responsa. [The Encyclopaedia Judaica survey distinguishes the numerous letters and responsa](https://jewishmedicalethics.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maimonides-rabbi-physician-and-philosopher.pdf), and the [standard bibliography records their collective transmission](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

### Medical compilations, protocols, and consultations

- **c. 1190–1204:** *Extracts from Galen* (*Al-Mukhtaṣarāt*; often called *The Art of Cure: Extracts from Galen*). A teaching compendium distilled from Galen rather than an original medical system; only part of the project survives, so its status is that of a fragmentary pedagogical obra. [Encyclopaedia Judaica identifies three surviving Arabic portions](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html), and the [medical bibliography describes its instructional purpose](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3721660/).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *Commentary on Hippocrates’ Aphorisms*. A critical commentary mediated through Galen’s interpretation, preserved only partially in two defective Arabic manuscripts. Its incomplete survival makes the extant object a fragment of the made commentary, not the whole obra. [Its condition and sources are documented by Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *Medical Aphorisms* (*Kitāb al-Fuṣūl fī al-Ṭibb*; *Fuṣūl Mūsā*). A twenty-five-treatise compilation of roughly 1,500 medical propositions drawn principally from Galen but critically organized and supplemented by Maimonides; it is both compilation and independent medical obra. [The modern critical-edition series documents its component treatises](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/series/BYU-MWMM.html), and [Encyclopaedia Judaica identifies its sources and significance](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *Glossary of Drug Names* (*Sharḥ Asmāʾ al-ʿUqqār*). A multilingual pharmacological concordance identifying approximately 350 remedies through Arabic, Greek, Persian, Spanish, Moroccan, Egyptian, and Berber names—a reference instrument rather than a continuous treatise. [The manuscript, organization, and languages are described here](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *On Hemorrhoids* (*Fī al-Bawāsīr*). A seven-chapter therapeutic protocol composed for a young nobleman, treating digestion, diet, and local and general therapies. It appears in the modern critical-edition series as an independent medical work. [The critical edition is listed by the University of Chicago Press](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/series/BYU-MWMM.html), and its original setting is recorded in [Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *On Coitus* (*Fī al-Jimāʿ*). A three-part medical protocol on sexual intercourse, written for an Ayyubid ruler identified in the sources as ʿUmar, son of Nūr al-Dīn; it is a commissioned regimen rather than a general philosophical work. [The classified bibliography documents its dedication and manuscript transmission](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni), and it is included among the [six minor works in the modern critical collection](https://brill.com/downloadpdf/display/title/61150.pdf).

- **1190:** *On Asthma* (*Maqāla fī al-Rabw*). A medical monograph and regimen treating asthma through environment, diet, physical care, and psychological well-being. [Encyclopaedia Judaica supplies the date and describes its therapeutic scope](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html); the work also has a [modern critical edition](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/series/BYU-MWMM.html).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *On Poisons and Protection against Lethal Drugs* (*Kitāb al-Sumūm wa-l-Mutaḥarriz min al-Adwiya al-Qitāla*). A two-part emergency protocol describing poisons, bites, symptoms, antidotes, and immediate treatment. [The standard bibliography records its structure and alternate title](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni), and the [critical-edition series confirms it as a principal medical treatise](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/series/BYU-MWMM.html).

- **1198:** *On the Regimen of Health* (*Fī Tadbīr al-Ṣiḥḥa*). A commissioned regimen for Sultan al-Afḍal, integrating diet, exercise, environment, emotional health, and preventive medicine; it is simultaneously protocol, consultation, and courtly medical obra. [The date, patron, and purpose are documented by Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html).

- **c. 1200:** *On the Elucidation of Some Symptoms and the Response to Them* (*Maqāla fī Bayān al-Aʿrāḍ*). A twenty-two-chapter follow-up consultation for al-Afḍal concerning persistent physical and depressive symptoms, and therefore closer to an extended case report than a universal medical textbook. [Encyclopaedia Judaica describes its occasion and form](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html), while a [medical survey dates it to about 1200](https://jewishmedicalethics.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Maimonides-rabbi-physician-and-philosopher.pdf).

- **c. 1190–1204:** *On Rules Regarding the Practical Part of the Medical Art*. A concise protocol of practical medical rules. It is recognized and separately edited in the modern critical series, although older ten-work enumerations sometimes subsume or omit it, demonstrating that the boundary of the “medical works” corpus is itself bibliographically unstable. [The critical edition is listed in the collected series](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/series/BYU-MWMM.html).

### Works of uncertain or rejected attribution

- **undated; traditionally assigned to Maimonides’ youth:** *Treatise on the Art of Logic* (*Maqāla fī Ṣināʿat al-Manṭiq*; Hebrew *Millot ha-Higgayon*). A fourteen-chapter technical lexicon of philosophical and logical terminology traditionally said to have been written when Maimonides was sixteen; Herbert Davidson rejects the attribution, so it cannot securely be counted as something Maimonides made. [The historical attribution appears in the classified bibliography](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni), while the [University of Pennsylvania manuscript description records Davidson’s contrary judgment](https://openn.library.upenn.edu/Data/0021/html/HebrewMss30.html).

- **undated:** *Essay on the Unity of God* (*Maqāla fī al-Tawḥīd*). A short work transmitted under Maimonides’ name, but the attribution is not secure enough for inclusion among his authenticated writings; it belongs to the history of the Maimonidean corpus rather than confidently to his obra. [The traditional attribution and Hebrew title are recorded here](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **undated:** *Essay on Felicity* (*Maqāla fī al-Saʿāda*; Hebrew *Peraqim be-Haẓlaḥah*). A two-chapter philosophical text transmitted under Maimonides’ name, but not securely authenticated; its resistance to being called his “work” is therefore essential catalogue information. [The text’s manuscript and publication history are recorded in the classified bibliography](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **undated:** *Commentary on Rosh ha-Shanah*. A surviving text once presented as part of Maimonides’ lost Talmudic commentaries, but its authenticity is doubtful. It should be catalogued as an attribution, not silently absorbed into his works. [The authenticity problem is explicitly stated in the standard account](https://www.jewishencyclopedia.com/articles/10302-maimonides-maimuni).

- **eighteenth century:** *Prayer of a Physician* (“Prayer of Maimonides”). Not an obra by Maimonides: the prayer was composed many centuries after his death and only subsequently attached to his name. [Both Encyclopaedia Judaica](https://jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/judaica/ejud_0002_0013_0_13047.html) and a [medical-historical review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3721660/) reject the attribution.
