## Works by Sam Bankman-Fried

### Corporate obras and financial platforms

- **2017–2022:** *Alameda Research*. A quantitative cryptocurrency-trading firm co-founded by Bankman-Fried, Gary Wang, and others in November 2017; sources that call Bankman-Fried its sole “founder” simplify disputed collective authorship. It became inseparable from FTX’s financial machinery and entered bankruptcy in November 2022.
- **2018–2019:** *FTX*. A project begun by Bankman-Fried and other Alameda employees in late 2018 and launched as a centralized digital-asset exchange by May 2019; Bankman-Fried and Wang are ordinarily identified as its co-founders. This is less a single object than a corporate-technological assemblage—companies, websites, software, markets, custody claims, and financial relationships—and therefore approaches a commercial *Gesamtkunstwerk*.
- **2019–2022:** *FTT*. FTX’s exchange token: 350 million units were minted in April 2019 and the token was publicly launched on July 29. It was collectively produced by FTX rather than demonstrably authored by Bankman-Fried alone; its market price and use as collateral became integral to the FTX–Alameda structure.
- **2020–2022:** *FTX US*. A United States digital-asset exchange, legally separate from FTX.com but sharing its founders, ownership structure, branding, and technological model. It incorporated acquired operations such as LedgerX/FTX US Derivatives, so it resists classification as an entirely new, singly authored work.
- **2020–2022:** *Serum*. A decentralized-exchange and liquidity-infrastructure project initiated by FTX, Alameda Research, and collaborating developers on the Solana blockchain. It is attributable to Bankman-Fried’s corporate network, not securely to him as sole maker; after FTX’s collapse, outside developers forked its open-source code because its controlling upgrade keys were associated with FTX.
- **2021–2022:** *FTX Foundation*. FTX’s philanthropic apparatus, established to direct a portion of exchange revenue toward charitable and effective-altruist causes. It was an institutional project rather than Bankman-Fried’s personal work and ceased operating with the FTX collapse.
- **2022:** *FTX Future Fund*. A grantmaking project launched by the FTX Foundation in February 2022, funded principally by Bankman-Fried and intended to support long-term and existential-risk work. Its team resigned in November 2022; the unfinished project had distributed more than $160 million, and its dependence on funds originating in the collapsed corporate system makes its status as philanthropy contested.

### Protocols, policy texts, and testimony

- **2019:** *Alameda Research White Paper*. A corporate account of Alameda’s trading and market-making operation, dated June 29. Regulators attribute its representations to Alameda, not securely to Bankman-Fried alone; it should therefore be treated as a collectively issued corporate text rather than an individually authored essay.
- **2019:** *FTT White Paper*. A protocol-like corporate text specifying the exchange token’s supply, distribution, utility, and repurchase-and-destruction mechanism. It belongs to the FTX project; no reliable consulted source establishes Bankman-Fried as its sole writer.
- **2021:** *Testimony of Sam Bankman-Fried: “Digital Assets and the Future of Finance.”* Written testimony submitted to the U.S. House Committee on Financial Services for its December 8 hearing. It is both descriptive corporate advocacy and a performative regulatory text, presenting FTX’s structure and asking government to recognize a preferred model of digital-asset markets.
- **2022:** *Written Testimony of Sam Bankman-Fried before the Senate Committee on Agriculture, Nutrition, and Forestry*. Testimony prepared for the February 9 hearing on digital assets. The document incorporates FTX policy recommendations and thus has mixed authorship: Bankman-Fried signed and presented it, but it speaks institutionally for FTX.
- **2022:** *Possible Digital Asset Industry Standards*. A draft regulatory protocol published October 19 under Bankman-Fried’s name, proposing industry practices for sanctions, token classification, customer protection, stablecoins, DeFi, and responses to exploits. Its prescriptive purpose makes “protocol” more accurate than essay, although Bankman-Fried partially retreated from it following criticism.
- **2022:** *Prepared congressional testimony for December 13, 2022*. An unfinished and undelivered account of FTX’s collapse, prepared with members of FTX’s policy team for a House hearing that Bankman-Fried could not attend after his arrest. It resists treatment as a finished authored work: only portions entered the trial record, and Bankman-Fried testified that other people helped prepare the submission.

### Public self-account and unfinished writings

- **2022:** *Tweets and public statements during the FTX collapse*. A dispersed, subsequently altered or deleted body of platform writing rather than a stable titled work. Some statements became evidence in criminal and regulatory proceedings; because the corpus has no authoritative collected edition, individual posts cannot responsibly be presented here as a complete series of works.
- **2022–2023:** *Unpublished writings on the collapse of FTX*. Approximately 250 pages of drafts, reflections, proposed public statements, and an unposted roughly 15,000-word social-media thread reportedly composed while Bankman-Fried was under house arrest. These are unfinished project materials, not a published book or settled text; their titles, boundaries, and completeness are not publicly fixed.
- **2023:** *FTX Pre-Mortem Overview*. A January 12 Substack post presenting Bankman-Fried’s account of the failures of Alameda and FTX. It is an apologetic self-account rather than an independent audit, and its factual claims conflict with the prosecution’s evidence and the jury’s subsequent verdict.
- **2023:** *FTX US Balance Update 2023-01-17*. A January 18 Substack memorandum arguing that FTX US remained solvent. Its form is part balance-sheet narrative and part public defense; it was not an audited financial statement, and its conclusions were disputed by FTX’s post-bankruptcy management.

### Designed system of concealment and privileged access

- **2019–2022:** *The FTX–Alameda customer-funds arrangement*. This resists being called a “work”: it was an operational system, implemented collectively through corporate accounts, misleading representations, and software privileges, through which Alameda obtained extraordinary access to FTX customer assets. The CFTC alleged that Bankman-Fried directed the creation of an effectively unlimited credit line and exceptions to automatic liquidation; a jury convicted him in 2023 of fraud and conspiracy, and the Justice Department describes him as having orchestrated the schemes.
