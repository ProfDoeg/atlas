## Works by Lucilio Vanini

### Extant philosophical works

- **1614–1615; published 1615:** *Amphitheatrum aeternae providentiae divino-magicum, christiano-physicum, nec non astrologo-catholicum adversus veteres philosophos, atheos, epicureos, peripateticos et stoicos*. A Latin philosophical-theological treatise in fifty *exercitationes*, outwardly an apology for providence but structured so ambiguously that scholars describe it as pseudo-apologetic and covertly heterodox; it survives complete and is one of the two books included in the standard modern collected editions. ([ILIESI–CNR](https://www.iliesi.cnr.it/AGCV/Scheda-Amphitheatrum.php), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Il-Contributo-italiano-alla-storia-del-Pensiero%3A-Filosofia%29/))

- **1616:** *De admirandis naturae reginae deaeque mortalium arcanis libri quatuor*. A Latin natural-philosophical work cast as dialogues between “Iulius Caesar” and “Alexander,” treating cosmology, generation, sensation, human nature, and religion; although issued as a finished four-book obra, its sequence jumps from Dialogue XXXIV to XXXVI, so the absent XXXV makes its numerical completeness uncertain. ([ILIESI–CNR](https://www.iliesi.cnr.it/AGCV/Scheda-De-admirandis.php), [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Il-Contributo-italiano-alla-storia-del-Pensiero%3A-Filosofia%29/))

### Lost philosophical and apologetic obras

- **1601–1610:** *De generatione et corruptione*. A lost work of natural philosophy assigned by the modern critical biography to Vanini’s Neapolitan period; no surviving text permits its extent, completion, or precise date to be established. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **1601–1610:** *De contemnenda gloria*. A lost philosophical or moral work from the Neapolitan period. Its title is known, but neither its form nor whether Vanini completed it can now be verified. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **1601–1610:** *Liber physico-magicus*. A lost natural-philosophical or “physico-magical” book associated with Vanini’s early production; because no manuscript survives, later attempts to identify its contents in other texts remain conjectural. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/), [ILIESI–CNR bibliography](https://www.iliesi.cnr.it/Vanini/biblio/1981-1990.pdf))

- **1601–1610:** *Apologia pro Mosaica et Christiana Lege*. A lost apologetic work that Vanini himself cites in the *Amphitheatrum*, where he says he had treated a question more fully in it; that self-reference supports its existence but cannot establish its original extent or finished state. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/), [ILIESI–CNR, *Amphitheatrum*, p. 43](https://www.iliesi.cnr.it/AGCV/main.php?f=..%2FVanini%2Ftesti%2FAmph%2FVanAmphP043.php&lk=Amph&n=VanAmphP043.gif&t=&tx=1))

- **1614:** *Apologia pro Concilio Tridentino*. A reported eighteen-book defense of the Council of Trent for which Vanini sought permission to publish; it is lost, and the surviving evidence does not settle whether all eighteen books existed as a completed manuscript or represented the declared scope of a project. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Letters and documentary texts

- **9 October 1612:** *Letter to Dudley Carleton*. An extant personal-political letter from Lambeth reporting Vanini’s situation under Archbishop George Abbot; it is correspondence rather than an autonomous philosophical obra. A purported letter dated 24 February 1612 may be a non-autograph, misdated variant of this one rather than a separate text. ([Francesco Paolo Raimondi, critical biography](https://www.studivaniniani.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Biografia_Giulio_Cesare_Vanini.pdf))

- **9 October 1612:** *Letter to Isaac Wake*. An extant companion letter from Lambeth, addressed to Carleton’s secretary and conveying substantially the same self-presentation; it belongs to Vanini’s correspondence, not to the two-book philosophical corpus. ([Francesco Paolo Raimondi, critical biography](https://www.studivaniniani.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Biografia_Giulio_Cesare_Vanini.pdf))

- **March 1613:** *Memorial to Pope Paul V*. A lost petition, apparently made jointly with Giovanni Maria Ginocchio, seeking legal absolution, release from Carmelite vows, and permission to live as secular clerics. Its contents survive only through a Holy Office minute, so it resists treatment as a securely recoverable Vanini text and may be a collaborative juridical act rather than an individually authored obra. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

- **15 February 1614:** *Declaratio before the English High Commission*. A confessional and legal declaration appended to Vanini’s interrogation, reaffirming Catholic allegiance and explaining his flight from Carmelite discipline. It is a performative documentary text—an act of self-defense recorded within proceedings—rather than a freestanding authored work. ([Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))

### Performative manifestations

- **8 July 1612:** *Sermon and abjuration at the Mercers’ Chapel*. A spoken manifesto-act in which Vanini publicly renounced Roman Catholic allegiance and professed adherence to the English church; reports establish its themes and favorable reception, but no complete authorial text is known to survive, and its simultaneous character as sermon, ritual, and politically supervised declaration makes “work” an unstable category. ([Francesco Paolo Raimondi, critical biography](https://www.studivaniniani.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/04/Biografia_Giulio_Cesare_Vanini.pdf), [Treccani, *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/giulio-cesare-vanini_%28Dizionario-Biografico%29/))
