## Works by Moshé Feldenkrais

### Juvenilia, translation, and martial-arts protocols

- **c. 1915:** *The Collected Writings of M. P. Feldenkrais at 11 Years: A General Notebook*. An unpublished Hebrew childhood notebook, known through an unpublished English translation; its status is archival juvenilia rather than a formally issued work. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1929:** *The Practice of Autosuggestion by the Method of Émile Coué*. Feldenkrais translated C. Harry Brooks’s book into Hebrew and added two chapters of his own; it is therefore partly translation and partly an original theoretical work, not wholly his obra. ([publication history](https://www.feldenkraistorontowest.com/publications-by-dr-feldenkrais))
- **1929:** *Thinking and Doing*. The original essay formed Feldenkrais’s two added chapters to the autosuggestion translation; its present English title belongs to the posthumous 2013 edition, so the essay and that edition should not be mistaken for two works. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1930:** *Jiu-Jitsu and Self Defense*. A Hebrew illustrated self-defence protocol; Feldenkrais subsequently translated and reworked it in French as *Science versus brute force, or Jiu-jitsu for intellectuals* (1933) and *Manuel pratique de jiu-jitsu: la défense du faible contre l’agresseur* (1944), manifestations of substantially the same project rather than securely distinct obras. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1941/1944:** *Judo: The Art of Defence and Attack*. An illustrated instructional protocol for standing and throwing techniques. Bibliographies disagree over whether 1941 or 1944 should be treated as its first publication date. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography), [comparative bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1942:** *Practical Unarmed Combat*. A wartime close-combat protocol derived from the courses Feldenkrais taught in Britain; some bibliographies date it 1941, while the more detailed bibliographical account gives 1942. ([comparative bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/), [Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1952:** *Higher Judo: Ground Work (Katame-Waza)*. An advanced illustrated protocol devoted to groundwork; although a martial-arts manual, its account of learning and coordinated action also anticipates Feldenkrais’s later method. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))

### Physics and engineering

- **1936:** *Emploi du tétrachlorure de carbone pour l’élévation de la tension des générateurs électrostatiques du type Van de Graaff*. A jointly authored experimental paper by Frédéric Joliot, Feldenkrais, and André Lazard; its collaborative authorship must be retained. ([contemporary bibliographic record](https://www.nature.com/articles/157481b0), [title record](https://www.fortefeldenkrais.it/ricerca/feldenkrais-nel-mondo/))
- **1945:** *Sur la mesure de très hautes tensions des générateurs électrostatiques du type Van de Graaff*. Feldenkrais’s engineer-doctor thesis: a scientific monograph and examination submission describing several high-voltage measuring devices, not a work about his movement method. ([thesis transcription and title page](https://www.arlingtonacupuncturewellness.com/feldenkraisthesis.html))
- **1946:** *“Industrial” Separation of Isotopes*. A short scientific letter in *Nature* proposing an application of the electro-affinity phenomenon investigated in his earlier work. ([Nature record and DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/157481b0))
- **1940–1945:** *Admiralty anti-submarine and sonar work*. A classified engineering project rather than a stable public text: Feldenkrais helped develop anti-submarine equipment in Scotland, but the frequently repeated claim that this produced “several patents” is not accompanied by identifiable patent numbers in the consulted standard bibliography and should not be converted into invented entries. ([Mark Reese’s concise biography](https://feldenkrais.com/a-concise-biography-of-moshe-feldenkrais/))

### Books and book-length projects on human functioning

- **1949:** *Body and Mature Behavior: A Study of Anxiety, Sex, Gravitation and Learning*. A theoretical monograph joining physics, developmental psychology, martial practice, and bodily learning; it is the first published book-length formulation of the project later called the Feldenkrais Method. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **c. 1940s–early 1950s; published 1985:** *The Potent Self: A Guide to Spontaneity*. A book-length manuscript written near the period of *Body and Mature Behavior* but left unpublished by Feldenkrais; the posthumous edition is editorially mediated, and later appeared as *The Potent Self: A Study of Spontaneity and Compulsion*. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1967:** *Improving Ability: Theory and Practice*. A Hebrew book combining an account of learning with twelve movement protocols; the substantially corresponding English manifestation appeared in 1972 as *Awareness Through Movement: Health Exercises for Personal Growth*. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1971:** *Twenty-five Lessons by Dr. Moshe Feldenkrais*. Twenty-five movement lessons rendered by Noa Eshkol in Eshkol–Wachman Movement Notation. The movements and teaching are Feldenkrais’s, but the written object is Eshkol’s notational mediation and thus a collaborative manifestation rather than an ordinary authored book. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1977:** *The Case of Nora: Adventures in the Jungle of the Brain*. A case narrative about Feldenkrais’s work with one pupil, crossing clinical report, memoir, and theoretical demonstration; later editions retitled it *Body Awareness as Healing Therapy: The Case of Nora*. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1980:** *50 Lessons by Dr. Moshe Feldenkrais*. An expanded Eshkol–Wachman notation corpus prepared by Noa Eshkol; as with the 1971 volume, authorship is divided between Feldenkrais’s lessons and Eshkol’s inscription of them. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1981:** *The Elusive Obvious; or, Basic Feldenkrais*. A compact theoretical book explaining the premises of his method; later subtitles belong to reissues rather than to a separate obra. ([bibliography](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))
- **1979; published 1984/1985:** *The Master Moves*. An edited transcription of a five-day Mann Ranch workshop, interweaving spoken theory and enacted movement lessons. Sources variously give 1984 and 1985 for publication, and the book’s editors transformed a live event into the textual object. ([publication account](https://www.feldenkraistorontowest.com/publications-by-dr-feldenkrais), [bibliographical disagreement](https://feldynotebook.com/bibliography/))

### Essays, lectures made into texts, and interviews

- **1964:** *Mind and Body*. Two lectures published together in *Systematics*; a theoretical manifesto against treating mental and physical life as separable domains. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1966:** *Image, Movement, and Actor: Restoration of Potentiality*. A published interview with Richard and Helen Schechner rather than a conventionally authored essay; later bibliographies sometimes shorten or alter its title. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1968:** *Die Muskulatur der Seele*. A German-language Swiss Radio presentation written by Feldenkrais and edited by Franz Wurm; it is simultaneously script, broadcast lecture, and later audiovisual manifestation. ([IFF Archive](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))
- **1975:** *Awareness Through Movement*. A chapter or lecture-text published in the *Annual Handbook for Group Facilitators*, distinct as a short text from the book bearing the same English phrase. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1975; published 1991:** *A Non-Specific Treatment*. A San Francisco training lecture edited by Mark Reese after Feldenkrais’s death; the surviving publication is therefore a posthumous textual manifestation of speech. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1976:** *On the Primacy of Hearing*. An essay in the first issue of *Somatics* on hearing, orientation, and nervous-system development. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1976:** *The Forebrain: Sleep, Consciousness, Awareness and Learning*. An interview conducted by Edward Rosenfeld and published in *Interface*; dialogic testimony rather than a solely authored work. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1979:** *Man and the World*. A theoretical essay published in *Somatics* and reprinted in *Explorers of Humankind*. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1979:** *On Health*. A short essay in *Dromenon* treating health as the capacity to recover and act, rather than merely the absence of illness. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1980:** *Learn to Learn*. A booklet functioning as both manifesto and protocol: it tells the reader how to approach learning and movement lessons rather than supplying a descriptive theory alone. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1980:** *Movement and the Mind*. An interview with Will Schutz published in *The New Healers*; its dialogic authorship resists treatment as an ordinary Feldenkrais essay. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1981:** *Physics and My Method*. An autobiographical lecture connecting Feldenkrais’s scientific work with the development of his somatic practice; the available text is a later edited transcript. ([CERN-hosted transcript](https://cds.cern.ch/record/2316931/files/Feldenkrais.pdf))
- **1981; published 1982:** *Self-Fulfillment Through Organic Learning*. A Mandala Conference lecture edited by Mark Reese for the *Journal of Holistic Health*, hence an editorially stabilized version of an event. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **date uncertain; published 1988–1989:** *Bodily Expression*. A French text or lecture translated by Thomas Hanna and issued posthumously in two parts in *Somatics*. The bibliography establishes the publication dates but not a secure date for Feldenkrais’s original composition. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1981; released later:** *Moshé Feldenkrais: Biographical Interview*. Mark Reese’s filmed Amherst interview is the principal extended first-person account of Feldenkrais’s life; it is a jointly produced audiovisual document rather than an authored text. ([IFF Archive](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))
- **date uncertain; published 1986:** *Moshé on Moshé on the Martial Arts*. An interview published after Feldenkrais’s death by Dennis Leri; the bibliography does not securely date the original conversation. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))

### The method as Gesamtkunstwerk

- **c. 1940s–1984:** *The Feldenkrais Method*. Feldenkrais’s overarching obra: a developing practice of learning through movement, touch, speech, observation, and self-directed attention. It is not reducible to any book or fixed protocol and is best understood as a Gesamtkunstwerk distributed across texts, bodies, recordings, demonstrations, and teacher–pupil encounters. ([IFF Archive description](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/), [biographical account](https://feldenkrais.com/about-moshe-feldenkrais/))
- **c. 1940s–1984:** *Awareness Through Movement*. The group-taught branch of the method, made as spoken movement sequences whose exact realization changes with a class. The same phrase also names a book and several recorded series, so it denotes a practice, not one stable text. ([IFF Archive](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))
- **c. 1940s–1984:** *Functional Integration*. The one-to-one, chiefly tactile branch of the method. Each lesson is an improvised bodily encounter and resists being called a reproducible work at all; film records manifestations of it, not the lesson itself in its entirety. ([IFF Amherst documentation](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))

### Lesson corpora, workshops, and teacher-training projects

- **c. 1951–1975:** *Alexander Yanai Lessons*. A corpus of 550 preserved Awareness Through Movement lessons taught and recorded in Tel Aviv; individual lesson titles were often supplied or normalized by later editors, and the collection is a body of spoken protocols rather than a single book. Sources give somewhat different outer dates, reflecting uncertain recording chronology. ([collection history](https://feldenkraistreasury.com/login/introductionay), [collection index](https://feldynotebook.com/sources-of-feldenkraisa-atm-lessons/))
- **1969–1971:** *Tel Aviv Professional Training*. Feldenkrais’s first project for transmitting the method to teachers, conducted for a small group of approximately twelve or thirteen pupils. Its work was the formation of practitioners, not a single authored object. ([official biography](https://feldenkrais.com/about-moshe-feldenkrais/))
- **1972:** *Esalen Workshop*. A 23-day American teaching project comprising approximately 43–46 Awareness Through Movement lessons, depending on whether one counts surviving notes, recordings, or taught sessions; that numerical disagreement is part of the archive. ([workshop chronology](https://aynmaltaeglich.org/zeitstrahl.html), [Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1973:** *Berkeley Workshop*. A recorded public lesson series whose surviving audio, rather than a Feldenkrais-prepared publication, defines the present object. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1973:** *Vancouver Workshop*. A recorded workshop corpus of roughly forty-five hours; its boundaries are archival and commercial rather than those of an authored book. ([IFF-materials library inventory](https://cdn.ymaws.com/feldenkrais.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/pdf/ny_region_library.pdf))
- **1974:** *Israeli Defense Force Radio Series*. A broadcast series of spoken movement lessons; it is listed in the lesson-source index, but the consulted bibliography does not establish a definitive episode count. ([lesson-source index](https://feldynotebook.com/sources-of-feldenkraisa-atm-lessons/))
- **1974:** *London Workshop*. Thirty movement lessons and six lectures preserved through recordings and transcription; the transcript is a later textual manifestation of live teaching. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1975:** *Feldenkrais–Pribram Discussions*. Recorded discussions with neuroscientist Karl Pribram; an intellectual dialogue rather than a solely authored Feldenkrais work. ([lesson-source index](https://feldynotebook.com/sources-of-feldenkraisa-atm-lessons/))
- **1975–1978:** *San Francisco Professional Training*. A four-year pedagogical project comprising lectures, demonstrations, movement lessons, and supervised practice. Its obra is simultaneously an event, an oral curriculum, a community of trained practitioners, and an extensive recording corpus. ([official biography](https://feldenkrais.com/about-moshe-feldenkrais/))
- **1976:** *Feldenkrais for the Elder Citizen*. A ten-lesson recorded Awareness Through Movement series designed for older participants; a spoken protocol collection rather than a print text. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1976:** *San Francisco Public Evening Class*. A corpus of twenty-six recorded or transcribed lessons; some catalogues divide its publication into three volumes, illustrating how later packaging changes the apparent object. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography), [lesson-source index](https://feldynotebook.com/sources-of-feldenkraisa-atm-lessons/))
- **1976:** *Rotterdam Workshop*. Approximately thirteen hours of surviving unedited recorded teaching; its title names an archival event rather than a Feldenkrais-authored publication. ([IFF-materials library inventory](https://cdn.ymaws.com/feldenkrais.site-ym.com/resource/resmgr/pdf/ny_region_library.pdf))
- **1978:** *Théâtre des Bouffes du Nord / Peter Brook Workshop*. Eight French-language movement lessons taught to Peter Brook’s actors and recorded informally by Myriam Pfeffer. The editors later supplied the lesson titles and acknowledge that the published order is not securely chronological. ([IFF edition](https://feldenkrais-method.org/media/s0hnbqfz/peterbrookcompleteenglish.pdf))
- **1978:** *Explorers of Humankind Conference*. A recorded Los Angeles lecture or interview later circulated on audiocassette; its bibliographic identity remains less stable than that of a print publication. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **date uncertain:** *Awareness Through Movement Basic Series*. Ten recorded lessons assembled as a named audio series; the standard bibliography supplies neither a reliable recording date nor evidence that Feldenkrais himself chose the commercial title. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **date uncertain:** *San Francisco Evening Class, Volumes I and II*. Two audio groupings of ten lessons each. The title and volume boundaries appear to be later distribution structures, so the series resists identification as a deliberately composed two-volume work. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1980–1983:** *Amherst Professional Training*. Feldenkrais’s final and unfinished teacher-training project: illness prevented him from personally teaching its whole scheduled course, while assistants completed the program. It survives as a large audiovisual and transcript corpus, but equating that archive with a finished authorial work would conceal its collaborative and interrupted status. ([official biography](https://feldenkrais.com/about-moshe-feldenkrais/), [IFF Archive](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))
- **1980–1981:** *Amherst Evening Functional Integration Lessons*. Individually improvised hands-on lessons given after training sessions and preserved on video. They are performances and interpersonal events; the recordings document them but are not identical with them. ([IFF Archive](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))
- **1981:** *Dallas, New York, and Washington workshops*. Three separately circulated recorded workshop projects from Feldenkrais’s last American teaching period; the standard bibliography identifies their locations and season but not a complete, stable lesson-level catalogue. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **date uncertain:** *Life and Human Nature*. A recorded dialogue with Will Schutz, circulated by the New Dimensions Foundation as tape 1170; it is a co-created conversation, and the consulted standard bibliography supplies no dependable recording date. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))

### Film and television manifestations

- **date uncertain:** *Medicine Man, Programs I and II*. Eight half-hour television programs showing Feldenkrais’s work. Their audiovisual form matters: these are performed demonstrations shaped by television production, not merely lectures that happen to have been filmed. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))
- **1980:** *The Work of Dr. Moshé Feldenkrais*. Two video volumes, each containing four half-hour segments, produced in Canada by Korentayer and Kolman. Production authorship is shared, while Feldenkrais supplies the teaching and demonstrations. ([Reese bibliography](https://www.feldenkraismethod.com/articles/moshe-feldenkrais-bibliography))

### Archives that resist item-by-item closure

- **c. 1950s–1984:** *Recorded public lessons, lectures, workshops, and Functional Integration sessions not yet fully catalogued*. The IFF preserves unpublished and incompletely catalogued recordings alongside more than 800 transferred archival objects; because no complete scholarly catalogue raisonné yet exists, claiming a definitive lesson-by-lesson list would be false precision. ([IFF Archive history](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/))
- **c. 1930s–1981:** *Judo and teaching photographs*. The IFF holds roughly 1,700 photographs of Feldenkrais practising judo and more than 1,500 photographs from the San Francisco training. Most were made by photographers rather than by Feldenkrais, so they are documentation of his embodied obra—not, without further evidence, works authored by him. ([IFF Archive history](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/), [collection description](https://feldenkrais-method.org/iff-archive/iff-archive/))
