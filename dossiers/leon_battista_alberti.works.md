## Works by Leon Battista Alberti

### Art theory, mathematics, architecture, and technical protocols

- **1434–1436:** *De pictura / Della pittura*. A three-book theory and protocol of painting, composed first in Latin or Italian—the priority of the two redactions remains debated—and then recast in the other language; perspective is treated as a reproducible geometrical procedure. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Probably 1430s:** *Elementa picturae / Elementi di pittura*. A compact geometrical protocol for painters, surviving in distinct Latin and Italian redactions rather than as a straightforward translation pair. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *De punctis et lineis apud pictores*. A short technical text about painters’ points and lines; its separate place in the corpus is recognized by the Edizione Nazionale, although its dating and relation to *Elementa picturae* remain unsettled. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **1434–early 1440s:** *Descriptio urbis Romae*. A mathematical description and coordinate survey of Rome: simultaneously text, measurement protocol, and recoverable map rather than a conventional literary work. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **1441:** *De equo animante*. A Latin treatise on the horse, its qualities, care, and training, dedicated to Leonello d’Este; it joins natural observation to practical protocol. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1452:** *Ex ludis rerum mathematicarum* (usually *Ludi rerum mathematicarum*). A vernacular collection of practical mathematical games and measurement procedures for heights, depths, distances, areas, weights, and mechanical problems, dedicated to Meliaduse d’Este. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html)

- **By 1452; revised afterward:** *De re aedificatoria*. A ten-book Latin treatise on building, cities, materials, ornament, infrastructure, and the architect’s knowledge; completed sufficiently to be presented to Nicholas V in 1452 but apparently revised later, it is both theoretical obra and building protocol. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain, probably mid-fifteenth century:** *De statua*. A Latin treatise and measurement protocol for sculpture, including instruments and procedures by which bodily form can be recorded and reproduced. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Around 1466:** *De componendis cifris / De cyfris*. A Latin cryptographic protocol proposing cipher systems based on movable concentric disks and frequency analysis; it turns writing into an instrument that acts by concealing information. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain; lost:** *Navis*. A technical work on ships, mentioned by Alberti and later known to Leonardo da Vinci but no longer extant; because only testimony survives, it is a lost obra rather than a recoverable text. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain; lost or unidentified:** *De literis et ceteris principiis grammaticae*. Alberti refers to a work on letters and grammatical principles in *De cifris*, but no securely identifiable copy survives; proposed identification with the *Grammatichetta vaticana* has not been proved. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain:** *Treatise on weights, levers, and hauling devices* (*Trattato sui pondi, leve e tirari*). A practical mechanics protocol traditionally included in Alberti’s technical corpus; its textual identity and title are less stable than those of his major treatises. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Moral, civic, and household works

- **Around 1430–1432:** *De commodis litterarum atque incommodis*. A Latin moral treatise weighing the advantages and hardships of a life devoted to learning. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **1433–1441:** *Libri della famiglia*. A four-book vernacular dialogue on family, education, marriage, household economy, friendship, and civic continuity, composed in stages. The separately circulated Pandolfini recasting of Book III was long attributed to Agnolo Pandolfini and is not simply Alberti’s original text. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain, probably 1430s:** *Theogenius*. A vernacular moral dialogue about wisdom, adversity, public life, and withdrawal; older chronologies often place it around 1440, but its precise date is uncertain. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Around 1441–1442:** *Profugiorum ab aerumna libri III*. A three-book vernacular dialogue, also known through the later title *Della tranquillità dell’animo*, on resistance to misfortune and the cultivation of inner steadiness. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Mid-fifteenth century:** *Cena familiaris*. A short vernacular household dialogue conceived as a pendant to *Libri della famiglia*, concerned with family conversation, conduct, and education. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Villa*. A short vernacular work on the country estate and rural life; unknown to modern scholarship until Cecil Grayson identified and edited it, it sits between moral dialogue and estate-management writing. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1468–1470:** *De iciarchia*. Alberti’s late vernacular civic dialogue on the *iciarco*, the exemplary head of household and leading citizen; the title is Latinizing, but the work itself is Italian. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1462:** *Sentenze pitagoriche*. A vernacular collection of moral maxims dedicated to Alberti’s young relatives; an ethical protocol in aphoristic form rather than a continuous treatise. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Comedy, satire, fable, and experimental prose

- **Around 1424; revised later:** *Philodoxeos fabula*. A Latin comedy about the pursuit of glory, first circulated under the fictive ancient author “Lepidus”; its false-antique authorship was part of the work’s humanist experiment rather than a later cataloguing mistake. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Probably 1430s:** *Apologi centum*. One hundred brief Latin fables or apologues, compact moral and comic thought-experiments rather than a single continuous narrative. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **From the 1430s; assembled and revised over many years:** *Intercenales*. A heterogeneous sequence of Latin dialogues, tales, dreams, satires, and philosophical miniatures arranged in books; its unstable transmission and repeated rewriting make it resist treatment as one fixed text. [Critical-edition description](https://www.lesbelleslettres.com/livre/9782251801339/propos-de-table-intercenales)

- **Around 1441–1443:** *Canis*. A short Latin comic encomium for Alberti’s dead dog, simultaneously epitaph, praise-piece, and parody. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1442–1443:** *Musca*. A Lucianic Latin encomium of the fly, reportedly composed during a fever and dedicated to Cristoforo Landino; mock praise is its operative form. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Before 1450:** *Momus, sive de principe*. A four-book Latin mythological satire or comic novel about rule, disorder, and the failures of gods and princes; its political allegory has never received a single accepted decoding. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain:** *Simiae* and a possibly intercenal fragment. Brief comic material preserved fragmentarily and listed separately by the Edizione Nazionale; whether the fragment belongs to the *Intercenales* remains uncertain. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Before 1443; lost:** *Passer*. A poem about a sparrow known through Cristoforo Landino’s contemporary allusion but no longer extant; its existence is attestable, while its text and exact form are not. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Love, marriage, and lyric works

- **Date uncertain:** *Amator*. A work on love transmitted in connection with questions omitted from Carlo Alberti’s *Ephebiis*; that textual setting makes it a distinct but relational obra, not Carlo’s work itself. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Probably 1420s–1430s:** *Deifira*. A vernacular dialogue on unhappy love. Older editions contaminated Alberti’s text with material from Boccaccio, so its received form must not be mistaken for an uncontested authorial text. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain:** *De amore*. A short work on love, recognized as an independent item in the national-edition plan although its date is not securely fixed. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Sofrona*. A vernacular dialogue or conduct text on marriage and female virtue; its precise date and generic boundaries remain uncertain. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Ecatonfilea*. A vernacular monologue or instruction in the art of love, voiced through a woman addressing other women; it behaves partly as a theatrical speech and partly as a protocol of courtship. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Dissuasio Valerii*. A brief anti-marriage or marriage-debate text transmitted in Alberti’s corpus, participating in a long rhetorical tradition rather than presenting itself as personal confession. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Various dates:** *Rime*. Alberti’s corpus of vernacular verse, including poems connected with love and poetic competition; it is a body of work rather than one intentionally unified book, and some older attributions have been rejected. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain; attribution doubtful:** *Istorietta amorosa* (also transmitted as *Ippolito e Leonora*). A short love narrative traditionally attached to Alberti, but anonymously transmitted and judged doubtful in the critical literature; it resists inclusion as a secure Alberti work. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Language, law, religion, politics, and occasional writings

- **Around 1437–1441:** *Grammatica della lingua toscana* (the *Grammatichetta vaticana*). A concise grammar and protocol for describing Tuscan, often called the first grammar of a modern European vernacular; attribution to Alberti is strong but historically contested, with Lorenzo de’ Medici and Cristoforo Landino also proposed. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **1441:** *Protesta*. Alberti’s public protest concerning the judges’ refusal to award the prize at the Certame Coronario; because it intervenes in an institutional dispute and demands recognition for vernacular poetry, it functions as a manifesto rather than mere description. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Vita Sancti Potiti*. A Latin saint’s life; a hagiographical narrative whose attribution and text entered modern scholarship through Cecil Grayson’s critical edition. [Edizioni della Normale](https://edizioni.sns.it/prodotto/opuscoli-inediti-di-leon-battista-alberti-musca-vita-s-potiti/)

- **1437:** *De iure*. A short Latin legal work composed at Bologna, preserved in manuscript and included among Alberti’s independent opuscula. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Pontifex*. A short Latin work concerned with the figure or office of the pontiff; its genre and date are not securely established. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Epistola consolatoria*. A consolatory letter fashioned as a literary work, standing between private correspondence and formal moral discourse. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Around 1453:** *Porcaria coniuratio*. A Latin historical account of Stefano Porcari’s conspiracy against the papal government; it is political historiography, not to be confused with the three speeches by Porcari once misattributed to Alberti. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Trivia senatoria*. A short Latin civic and rhetorical commentary associated with a passage of Cicero’s *De officiis*. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Epistolae septem Epimenidis nomine Diogeni inscriptae*. Seven Latin letters addressed to Diogenes under the assumed name of Epimenides; their pseudonymous epistolary situation is part of the work’s philosophical fiction. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain:** *Psalmi precationum*. A group of Latin prayer-psalms; devotional compositions forming a small corpus rather than a conventional authored book. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Throughout Alberti’s adult life:** *Corpus epistolare e documentario*. Alberti’s surviving letters and documentary writings constitute a dispersed body of made texts rather than one work conceived under a single title; the Edizione Nazionale accordingly treats them as a corpus. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Probably 1430s; attribution disputed:** *Vita Leonis Baptistae Alberti*. An anonymous Latin life of Alberti long read as an autobiography; the Edizione Nazionale retains “Autobiografia” as a corpus category, but scholars have repeatedly disputed whether Alberti wrote it, so it cannot be listed as secure self-authorship. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Literary and civic projects beyond a single text

- **22 October 1441:** *Certame Coronario*. A public vernacular poetry competition conceived and organized by Alberti in Florence, with a silver laurel crown as its unawarded prize; it was a civic-linguistic project and manifestation, not a book, although poems, rules, and Alberti’s *Protesta* survive around it. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **After 1441; unrealized:** *Second Certame Coronario on envy*. A projected continuation of the competition, announced around the theme of envy but never held; the intention is the work here, and its non-realization is part of its status. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

### Architecture and urban projects

- **1438; lost or unrealized:** *Monument for Niccolò III d’Este at Ferrara*. A small triumphal-arch support for an equestrian monument, designed for Leonello d’Este; its precise realization and Alberti’s degree of responsibility are uncertain, and the architectural object does not survive securely enough to reconstruct. [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Leon-Battista-Alberti)

- **From about 1447 to 1455; attribution uncertain:** *Architectural and restoration projects for Nicholas V in Rome*. Contemporary and later testimony associates Alberti with papal works at St Peter’s, the Vatican, and elsewhere, but documentation rarely permits individual buildings to be assigned to him as sole architect. This is best classified as a body of consultation and projects rather than a secure catalogue of executed buildings. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Around 1447–1455; unrealized and attribution inferential:** *Plan for rebuilding the Borgo Leonino*. An urban project attributed to Alberti from descriptions of Nicholas V’s plans and their correspondence with *De re aedificatoria*; it was not executed, and the attribution is not documented beyond dispute. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html)

- **Around 1450 onward:** *Tempio Malatestiano, Rimini*. Alberti’s project transformed the medieval church of San Francesco into Sigismondo Malatesta’s dynastic and commemorative monument; executed by others from his designs and never completed, it remains simultaneously church, memorial envelope, and unfinished architectural project. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html)

- **Before 1460; attribution generally accepted but execution collaborative:** *Palazzo Rucellai, Florence*. An urban palace façade and associated design commonly credited to Alberti, with Bernardo Rossellino often identified as executing architect; the documentary basis is indirect enough that the division of authorship remains debated. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html)

- **1457–1470:** *Façade of Santa Maria Novella, Florence*. Alberti’s marble façade project for Giovanni Rucellai joined a new classical upper composition to an already existing medieval lower façade; commissioned in 1457–58 and inscribed 1470, it is a designed reconciliation of two historical objects rather than a wholly new building. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/centro/l-b-alberti/)

- **1462–1467:** *Rucellai Sepulchre, San Pancrazio, Florence*. A marble-clad chapel monument modeled on the Holy Sepulchre in Jerusalem; architecture, inscription, colored stone, funerary object, and devotional image form a small Gesamtkunstwerk. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/centro/l-b-alberti/)

- **1463–1466:** *Loggia Rucellai, Florence*. An open ceremonial loggia designed for family celebrations and mourning; Alberti’s authorship is traditional and widely accepted, though construction involved other hands. [Repertorio delle Architetture Civili di Firenze](https://www.palazzospinelli.org/architetture/scheda.asp?ID=685)

- **From 1460; unfinished and altered:** *San Sebastiano, Mantua*. A centrally planned church commissioned by Ludovico Gonzaga and left incomplete at Alberti’s death; later building decisions and restorations make several important features of the intended design uncertain. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/centro/l-b-alberti/)

- **1460s–1470s; attribution and extent disputed:** *Tribune of Santissima Annunziata, Florence*. A rotunda or tribune traditionally connected with Alberti’s design, but the respective roles of Alberti, Michelozzo, and later executants are debated. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **By 1472; completed 1472–1478:** *Apse of San Martino a Gangalandi, Lastra a Signa*. A church enlargement recorded as begun and nearly finished in Alberti’s will, then completed after his death; the surviving fabric therefore embodies both his project and posthumous execution. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/centro/l-b-alberti/)

- **1470–1472; built posthumously and altered:** *Sant’Andrea, Mantua*. Alberti’s monumental basilica project for Ludovico Gonzaga, expressed through drawings and a surviving design letter shortly before his death; construction began after his death and continued for centuries, so the existing church is a manifestation of his project rather than an object he supervised to completion. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/centro/l-b-alberti/)

- **Mid-fifteenth century; attribution disputed:** *Villa Medici at Fiesole*. Sometimes assigned to Alberti because its form appears consonant with his writing on villas, but also attributed to Michelozzo or others; stylistic agreement is not proof of authorship, so it resists secure inclusion as an Alberti building. [Encyclopaedia Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Leon-Battista-Alberti)

- **Mid-fifteenth century; attribution doubtful:** *Palazzo Venezia, Rome*. Occasionally attributed to Alberti, but without decisive documentation and with other architects proposed; it belongs to the history of Alberti attribution, not to the secure architectural oeuvre. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html)

### Images, emblems, objects, and engineering manifestations

- **Probably 1430s–1440s:** *Self-Portrait medallion*. A cast bronze relief showing Alberti in profile and bearing his winged-eye device; museums identify Alberti as maker, but earlier scholarship also attributed it to Pisanello or Matteo de’ Pasti, so authorship is strong rather than uncontested. [Louvre](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010100290), [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/43845-self-portrait)

- **By the 1430s:** *Winged eye with the motto “Quid tum?”*. Alberti’s personal emblem or *impresa*, manifested in manuscripts and objects rather than as one uniquely fixed artifact; it is a repeatable sign-system and act of self-fashioning, not readily a single “work.” [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Around 1446–1450; attribution uncertain:** *Portrait bust or sculptural self-image of Alberti*. A terracotta or sculptural portrait type has been catalogued in connection with Alberti, but maker, date, and relationship to the bronze self-portrait remain disputed; it should not be treated as securely autograph. [Italian national cultural-property catalogue](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0900073459-1)

- **1440s:** *Attempt to recover the ancient ships from Lake Nemi*. An engineering and archaeological salvage project undertaken with flotation and lifting devices; it produced no enduring recovered ship, so the obra is the designed operation and experiment rather than a surviving object. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain; lost:** *Paintings and drawn portraits mentioned in early biographical testimony*. Alberti was credited with painted and drawn likenesses and other visual experiments, but no securely documented painting survives; this is evidence for a lost practice or body of work, not grounds for inventing individual titles. [National Gallery of Art](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/43845-self-portrait)

- **Attribution rejected:** *The Ideal City*, Urbino. This panel was formerly drawn into Alberti’s oeuvre because its city resembles his urban theory, but that attribution is now considered untenable; it is included here only to mark a famous work that should not be counted among the things he made. [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html)

### Works formerly or wrongly attributed to Alberti

- **Date uncertain; not by Alberti:** *Della prospettiva*. An anonymous perspective treatise once printed among Alberti’s writings but rejected by modern scholarship. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain; not by Alberti:** *Amiria*. A work by Carlo Alberti formerly included in Leon Battista’s corpus. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **Date uncertain; not by Alberti:** *Ephebiis / Ephoebia*. A work by Carlo Alberti formerly assigned to Leon Battista; the Edizione Nazionale prints it only as a related text. [Edizione Nazionale](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html)

- **1453; not by Alberti:** *Three speeches concerning Stefano Porcari*. These speeches are Porcari’s, not Alberti’s, although older collected editions placed them in Alberti’s oeuvre alongside his account of the conspiracy. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Sixteenth century; not by Alberti:** *Italian De statua*. The vernacular version once treated as Alberti’s is Cosimo Bartoli’s translation of the Latin treatise, not a second authorial redaction. [Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/)

- **Date uncertain; not by Alberti:** *Italian translation of the first three books of De re aedificatoria*. The vernacular text in manuscript Riccardiano 2520 was once assigned to Alberti but is the work of an anonymous translator. - [Edizione Nazionale delle opere di Leon Battista Alberti: editorial plan](https://www.leonardolibri.com/collana-256-ed-nazionale-opere-di-leon-battista-alberti-strumenti.html). The controlling scholarly corpus used to establish the textual works and their separations.

- [Centro di Studi sul Classicismo: Edizione Nazionale delle opere di Leon Battista Alberti](https://www.centrostudiclassicismo.it/alberti.htm). Institutional description of the national critical edition, its manuscript census, and the *Opere latine* project.

- [Cecil Grayson, “Alberti, Leon Battista,” *Dizionario Biografico degli Italiani*, Treccani](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/leon-battista-alberti_(Dizionario-Biografico)/). Consulted for chronology, lost works, architectural activity, and the history of disputed or false attributions.

- [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti, biographical and works survey](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/lba.html). Consulted especially for architectural projects, incompleteness, urban planning, and rejected attributions.

- [Fondazione Leon Battista Alberti, “L. B. Alberti”](https://www.fondazioneleonbattistaalberti.it/centro/l-b-alberti/). Consulted for Santa Maria Novella, the Rucellai Sepulchre, San Sebastiano, and Sant’Andrea.

- [Franco Borsi, *Leon Battista Alberti: The Complete Works*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Leon_Battista_Alberti.html?id=LO1OAAAAYAAJ). Standard synthetic catalogue and monograph used as a check on the cross-media scope of the oeuvre.

- [Roberto Cardini, ed., *Intercenales*: critical-edition description](https://www.lesbelleslettres.com/livre/9782251801339/propos-de-table-intercenales). Consulted for the work’s complex transmission, ordering, and redactional status.

- [National Gallery of Art, *Self-Portrait*](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/43845-self-portrait). Object record and detailed attribution history for the bronze self-portrait.

- [Musée du Louvre, *Médaillon: Autoportrait*](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010100290). Museum catalogue record for the self-portrait medallion and its scholarly bibliography.

- [Repertorio delle Architetture Civili di Firenze, *Loggia Rucellai*](https://www.palazzospinelli.org/architetture/scheda.asp?ID=685). Consulted for the loggia’s commission, function, date, and attribution.

- [Repertorio delle Architetture Civili di Firenze, *Palazzo Rucellai*](https://www.palazzospinelli.org/architetture/scheda.asp?ID=684). Consulted for the palace’s construction history and divided attribution.

- [Edizioni della Normale, *Opuscoli inediti di Leon Battista Alberti: Musca, Vita S. Potiti*](https://edizioni.sns.it/prodotto/opuscoli-inediti-di-leon-battista-alberti-musca-vita-s-potiti/). Consulted for Grayson’s critical recovery of the two opuscula.

- [Italian Ministry of Culture, national cultural-property catalogue: portrait bust of Alberti](https://catalogo.beniculturali.it/detail/HistoricOrArtisticProperty/0900073459-1). Consulted for the disputed sculptural portrait object.

- [Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Leon Battista Alberti”](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Leon-Battista-Alberti). Consulted as a chronological cross-check for Alberti’s early and attributed architectural projects.
