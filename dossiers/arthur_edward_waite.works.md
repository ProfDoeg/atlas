## Works by Arthur Edward Waite

### Poems, poetic sequences, and visionary dramas

- **1877:** *An Ode to Astronomy, and Other Poems*. Waite’s first published obra, a privately issued pamphlet of verse in an edition of one hundred copies. ([Gilbert, pp. 34–35, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1879:** *A Lyric of the Fairyland, and Other Poems*. A poetry collection developing the fairyland mythology that recurs throughout Waite’s literary work. ([Gilbert, pp. 36, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1886:** *Israfel: Letters, Visions and Poems*. A composite literary obra—prose letters, visionary pieces, and poems—rather than a single continuous text. Some catalogues misread the first word as “Israel.” ([Gilbert, pp. 44–45, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1887:** *A Soul’s Comedy*. A dramatic poem or verse drama, not a conventional stage play. Waite published it under the pseudonym “Austin Blake.” ([Gilbert, p. 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1890 or 1894:** *Lucasta: Parables and Poems*. A hybrid collection of poems and prose parables named for Waite’s wife; Gilbert’s chronological list gives 1890, while surviving bibliographical records commonly date the issued book to 1894, so the date is not silently normalized here. ([Gilbert, pp. 45, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Waite%2C%20Arthur%20Edward%2C%201857-1942))
- **1894:** *Belle and the Dragon: An Elfin Comedy*. A fairy drama or dramatic poem whose subtitle announces comedy but whose imaginative form resists a simple division between play, poem, and occult allegory. ([Gilbert, pp. 63–64, 199–200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1902:** *A Book of Mystery and Vision*. A collection of mystical and symbolic poetry. ([Gilbert, pp. 45, 99–100, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1906:** *Strange Houses of Sleep*. A limited collection of poems and initiatory dramatic pieces; its contents make it closer to a literary-mystical cycle than an ordinary verse miscellany. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1914:** *The Collected Poems of Arthur Edward Waite*. A two-volume retrospective poetic corpus, incorporating and revising earlier work rather than constituting an entirely new sequence. ([Gilbert, pp. 151, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1921:** *The Book of the Holy Graal*. A privately printed cycle of Grail poems: a poetic manifestation of the same mystical project pursued in Waite’s historical Grail studies. ([Gilbert, pp. 147, 151, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))

### Fairy tales, romances, and collaborative fiction

- **1889:** *Prince Starbeam: A Tale of Fairyland*. An allegorical fairy romance, published under Waite’s name but belonging as much to his continuing fairyland project as to the category of children’s fiction. ([Gilbert, pp. 53, 90, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *The Golden Stairs: Tales from the Wonder-World*. A collection of mystical fairy tales; its later transformation into *The Quest of the Golden Stairs* makes the project larger than either individual book. ([Gilbert, pp. 90, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1904:** *The House of the Hidden Light*. An epistolary initiatory romance made collaboratively with Arthur Machen and issued pseudonymously by the “High Fratres” Filius Aquarum and Elias Artista. Attribution of particular letters between the collaborators is not always recoverable, so it resists treatment as Waite’s sole work. ([Gilbert, pp. 69–72, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1927:** *The Quest of the Golden Stairs: A Mystery of Kinghood in Faerie*. A substantially recast manifestation of the 1893 fairy-tale project, presented as a continuous mystical quest or prose poem rather than merely a reprint. ([Gilbert, pp. 90, 200–201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [contemporary review](https://skirret.com/archive/builder/builder-1928-14-06.html))

### Occult history, criticism, and reference works

- **1887:** *The Real History of the Rosicrucians*. A documentary history constructed from Rosicrucian manifestos and related records; it is historical criticism, not itself a Rosicrucian manifesto. ([Gilbert, pp. 77–78, 109, 124, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1891:** *The Occult Sciences: A Compendium of Transcendental Doctrine and Experiment*. A synoptic reference work surveying occult doctrines and practices. ([Gilbert, pp. 80, 91, 125, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *A New Light of Mysticism: Azoth; or, The Star in the East*. A speculative mystical-alchemical treatise announcing Waite’s “new mysticism”; its rhetoric approaches a manifesto, although it remains formally expository. ([Gilbert, pp. 80, 92–94, 151, 172, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1896:** *Devil-Worship in France; or, The Question of Lucifer*. A critical exposé of the Taxil–Diana Vaughan anti-Masonic fabrication, written as polemical investigation rather than as occult instruction. ([Gilbert, pp. 83, 126, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1898:** *The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts*. A critical compilation and analysis of grimoires that also reproduces their ritual structures; consequently it sits uneasily between history, anthology, and protocol. ([Gilbert, pp. 11, 66, 83, 150, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1906:** *Studies in Mysticism and Certain Aspects of the Secret Tradition*. A collection of historical and interpretive essays unified by Waite’s developing idea of a Western “secret tradition.” ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1909:** *The Hidden Church of the Holy Graal*. A historical-symbolic study treating Grail romance as the outward manifestation of an interior Christian mystery. ([Gilbert, pp. 74, 98, 100, 102, 148, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1911:** *The Book of Ceremonial Magic*. A radically revised manifestation of *The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts*, not an independent first conception; it reframes the grimoire material around Waite’s thesis of a “secret tradition in Goëtia.” ([Gilbert, pp. 133, 150, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1911:** *The Secret Tradition in Freemasonry*. A two-volume historical and symbolic study of Craft Masonry and the higher degrees. ([Gilbert, pp. 200–201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1913:** *The Secret Doctrine in Israel: A Study of the Zohar and Its Connections*. A new Kabbalistic study that revises the position of Waite’s 1902 book rather than simply reprinting it. ([Gilbert, pp. 13, 45, 149, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1921:** *A New Encyclopaedia of Freemasonry (Ars Magna Latomorum) and of Cognate Instituted Mysteries*. A two-volume reference obra combining alphabetic encyclopaedia, institutional history, symbolism, and Waite’s own interpretive system. ([Gilbert, pp. 131, 146, 154, 160, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1924:** *The Brotherhood of the Rosy Cross*. A historical reconstruction of Rosicrucian traditions, distinct from but informed by Waite’s own initiatory institutions. ([Gilbert, pp. 131, 150, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1925:** *Emblematic Freemasonry and the Evolution of Its Deeper Issues*. A symbolic and historical Masonic study later absorbed in part into the 1937 revision of *The Secret Tradition in Freemasonry*. ([Gilbert, pp. 142–43, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1926:** *The Secret Tradition in Alchemy: Its Development and Records*. Waite’s mature historical reassessment of alchemy, correcting positions he had taken in earlier editorial and biographical work. ([Gilbert, pp. 150, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1929:** *The Holy Kabbalah*. Waite’s culminating synthetic study of Jewish mystical literature, incorporating and revising material from both *The Doctrine and Literature of the Kabalah* and *The Secret Doctrine in Israel*. ([Gilbert, pp. 151, 153, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1933:** *The Holy Grail: Its Legends and Symbolism*. A condensed and substantially reconsidered survey of the Grail corpus, related to but not identical with *The Hidden Church of the Holy Graal*. ([Gilbert, pp. 151, 159, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1937:** *The Secret Tradition in Freemasonry*. A one-volume revised synthesis incorporating much of *Emblematic Freemasonry*; it is a new manifestation of the 1911 project, not merely another impression. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1938:** *Shadows of Life and Thought: A Retrospective Review in the Form of Memoirs*. An autobiography whose deliberate reticence and pseudonymous naming make it a shaped self-portrait rather than a transparent documentary record. ([Gilbert, pp. 15–16, 160, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))

### Mystical theology, aphorisms, and spiritual biography

- **1901:** *The Life of Louis Claude de Saint-Martin, the Unknown Philosopher*. A biographical and doctrinal study; its historical life and exposition of Martinist teaching are inseparable. ([Gilbert, pp. 99, 127, 133, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1902:** *The Doctrine and Literature of the Kabalah*. An early synthetic study of Kabbalistic texts. Printing begun in 1899 was destroyed in the Ballantyne Press fire, although Waite retained proofs and had the book printed afresh in 1902; the destroyed setting is a failed manifestation, not a separate finished work. ([Gilbert, pp. 83, 97, 200](https://maestrosdelsaber.com/wp-content/uploads/ftp-files/Masoneria/a%20Waite%2C%20Arthur%20Edward/Magia/Maso%20Gilbert%2C%20R%20A%20-%20A%20E%20Waite%2C%20Magician%20of%20Many%20Parts.pdf))
- **1906:** *Steps to the Crown*. A sequence of mystical aphorisms: neither systematic treatise nor narrative, but a compact protocol of inward life. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1915:** *The Way of Divine Union*. A synthetic work of mystical theology describing an experiential path toward union with God. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1922:** *Raymund Lully: Illuminated Doctor, Alchemist and Christian Mystic*. A spiritual biography and historical study that distinguishes the historical Ramon Llull from alchemical works attributed to him. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Waite%2C%20Arthur%20Edward%2C%201857-1942))
- **1922:** *Saint-Martin: The French Mystic and the Story of Modern Martinism*. A shorter, revised manifestation of Waite’s Saint-Martin project, extended to the later history of Martinism. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1923:** *Lamps of Western Mysticism*. A collection of essays and contemplative instruction; its “Inner Doctrine of the Path” also became conceptual material for Waite’s second tarot project. ([Gilbert, pp. 151, 164, 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [Waite–Trinick study guide](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/Week2.html))

### Divinatory protocols and symbolic image systems

- **1889:** *A Handbook of Cartomancy, Fortune-Telling and Occult Divination*. An edited practical manual published under Waite’s pseudonym “Grand Orient”; later revisions appeared as *A Manual of Cartomancy*, making this an evolving protocol rather than a fixed text. ([Gilbert, pp. 62, 78–79, 138, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1909:** *The Key to the Tarot*. The first companion protocol for Waite’s tarot pack, explaining its symbolic system and divinatory use; it was superseded and enlarged by *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*. ([Waite biography, lines 195–197](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._E._Waite#Tarot_deck); [Warburg Institute catalogue](https://resources.warburg.sas.ac.uk/pdf/Innes_Catalogue.pdf))
- **1909:** *The Rider–Waite–Smith Tarot*. A 78-card symbolic and divinatory Gesamtkunstwerk conceived and directed by Waite, drawn and painted by Pamela Colman Smith, and published by William Rider & Son. “Rider–Waite” erases Smith’s material authorship; “Waite–Smith” or “Rider–Waite–Smith” better records the collaboration. ([Waite biography, lines 191–198](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._E._Waite#Tarot_deck); [deck history](https://www.divinityatlas.com/tarot-decks/rider-waite-smith))
- **1911:** *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*. An expanded illustrated companion to the tarot pack, simultaneously symbolic commentary, historical argument, and divinatory protocol. ([Gilbert, pp. 137–38, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1912:** *The Book of Destiny and the Art of Reading Therein*. A revised divination manual issued under “Grand Orient,” incorporating cartomancy and other fortune-telling systems; it belongs to the evolving *Handbook/Manual of Cartomancy* project rather than standing wholly apart from it. ([Gilbert, pp. 138, 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1917–1923:** *The Great Symbols of the Paths* (the Waite–Trinick tarot). An initiatory image-and-commentary project devised by Waite and executed visually by John Brahms Trinick for the Fellowship of the Rosy Cross. It resists being called a conventional tarot deck: the surviving large images concern the trumps and were intended for meditation and ritual display, not ordinary divination, and the archival album preserves sketches, finished designs, plates, and Waite’s commentary rather than a single standardized pack. ([Waite–Trinick research](https://www.tarotassociation.net/books/); [historical account](https://www.alchemywebsite.com/tarot/Tarot_weblog_Adam_McLean.html); [Fellowship account and attribution](https://hermeticgoldendawn.org/2011/09/19/great-symbols-of-paths/))

### Ritual protocols, orders, and institutional projects

- **1903–1914:** *The Holy Order of the Golden Dawn under the Obedience of the Independent and Rectified Rite*. An initiatory institution remade under Waite’s direction from the Isis-Urania Temple. As an obra it consists not in one book but in an organization, ceremonies, grades, administrative instruments, and a deliberately revised spiritual purpose. ([Gilbert, pp. 102–26, 202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1903–1908:** *Declaration of Independence, Preliminary Report, circular letters, and convocational addresses of the Independent and Rectified Rite*. Institutional manifestos and operative texts through which Waite constituted and governed his order; the archive preserves them collectively, and they resist reduction to one stable title. ([Gilbert, p. 202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **circa 1904–1906:** *Manuscript rituals of the Independent and Rectified Rite*. A two-volume ritual project subsequently manifested in seven printed ceremonies in 1910. Individual titles are not supplied in Gilbert’s principal inventory, so none are invented here. ([Gilbert, pp. 200, 202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1910:** *Rituals of “The Holy Order of the G∴D∴ under the Obedience of the Independent and Rectified Rite”*. Seven printed initiatory protocols. Their practical, restricted function makes them acts that produced members and grades, not merely texts describing ceremonies. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1915–1942:** *The Fellowship of the Rosy Cross*. Waite’s institutional and spiritual project succeeding the Independent and Rectified Rite; its obra includes rituals, teachings, visual symbols, minute books, professions, and continuing revisions rather than one bounded publication. ([Gilbert, pp. 133–59, 200–202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1916–1942:** *Rituals of the Fellowship of the Rosy Cross*. Eight principal initiatory protocols were prepared in 1916, with additional texts and revisions continuing until Waite’s death. The corpus therefore remains an evolving project whose completeness depends on date and archival state. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1926–1928:** *The Book of Life in the Rose*. A serial initiatory text issued in successive parts for the Fellowship of the Rosy Cross; simultaneously doctrine, meditation, and protocol rather than a public book in the usual sense. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1934:** *The Liturgy of the Rite of the Strict Observance*. A mimeographed Masonic ritual protocol; its restricted circulation and operative function make it closer to institutional performance-script than public authorship. ([Gilbert, “The Masonic Career of A. E. Waite,” Appendix B](https://freemasonrymatters.co.uk/masonic-career-e-waite/))

### Translations, textual reconstructions, and editorial creations

- **1886:** *The Mysteries of Magic: A Digest of the Writings of Éliphas Lévi*. A translated, selected, rearranged, and critically introduced digest; neither simply Lévi’s book nor wholly Waite’s independent treatise. ([Gilbert, pp. 77, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1888:** *Songs and Poems of Fairyland: An Anthology of English Fairy Poetry* (also issued as *Elfin Music*). An anthology selected and edited by Waite; the variant title represents the same editorial obra rather than another collection. ([Gilbert, pp. 91, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1888:** *Lives of Alchemystical Philosophers*. A reconstruction and expansion of an earlier compilation, supplemented with Waite’s research and bibliography; “edited” understates the extent of his remaking, while sole authorship would erase the inherited material. ([Gilbert, pp. 78, 93–94, 152, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [digitized title page](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/83/Lives_of_alchemystical_philosophers_..._To_which_is_added_a_bibliography_of_alchemy_and_hermetic_philosophy_%28IA_b21930478%29.pdf))
- **1888:** *The Magical Writings of Thomas Vaughan*. An edited selection from Vaughan’s writings and the first manifestation of Waite’s long textual project on Vaughan, later superseded by the 1919 collected edition. ([Gilbert, p. 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1892:** *A Lexicon of Alchemy; or, Alchemical Dictionary*, by Martinus Rulandus. Waite’s edited English version, printed in only six copies and not intended for public distribution; it therefore resists classification as an ordinary published book. ([Gilbert, pp. 81, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *The Hermetic Museum, Restored and Enlarged*. A two-volume English translation and editorial reconstruction of the *Musaeum Hermeticum*, with additional alchemical tracts. ([Gilbert, pp. 81, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *A Golden and Blessed Casket of Nature’s Marvels*, by Benedictus Figulus. An edited English alchemical text whose historical author remains Figulus; Waite made its English editorial manifestation. ([Gilbert, p. 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *The Triumphal Chariot of Antimony*, by Basilius Valentinus. An edited English alchemical text; both the historical identity of “Basil Valentine” and the underlying attribution are contested, and Waite did not resolve them by editing the work. ([Gilbert, p. 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *Collectanea Chemica*. A collection of alchemical tracts selected and edited by Waite, with only a brief editorial introduction; it should not be mistaken for a unitary work authored by him. ([Gilbert, pp. 189, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1893:** *The Alchemical Writings of Edward Kelly*. An edited collection with an extensive Waite introduction; the primary writings remain attributed to Edward Kelley/Kelly. ([Gilbert, pp. 189, 199](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1894:** *The New Pearl of Great Price*, attributed principally to Petrus Bonus of Ferrara. An English editorial compilation incorporating several alchemical authorities; authorship belongs to its historical sources, while the assembled English object is Waite’s editorial creation. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Waite%2C%20Arthur%20Edward%2C%201857-1942))
- **1894:** *The Hermetic and Alchemical Writings of Paracelsus*. A two-volume translated and edited corpus assembled from writings transmitted under Paracelsus’s name; the authenticity of individual source texts is not guaranteed by the collective attribution. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1894:** *Avalon: A Poetic Romance*, by Dora Stuart-Menteath. An editorial collaboration whose precise attribution is uncertain: Gilbert lists Waite as editor but explicitly directs readers to his discussion of Waite’s role, while the surviving original draft is in Waite’s hand. It therefore resists a clean division between another writer’s poem and Waite’s work. ([Gilbert, pp. 64, 200–201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1896:** *The Turba Philosophorum; or, Assembly of the Sages*. Waite’s translated and edited English manifestation of an anonymous medieval alchemical dialogue. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1896:** *Transcendental Magic: Its Doctrine and Ritual*, by Éliphas Lévi. Waite’s English translation and critical edition of Lévi’s *Dogme et rituel de la haute magie*. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1898:** *The Gift of the Spirit: A Selection from the Essays of Prentice Mulford*. An editorial selection through which Waite reshaped Mulford’s essays into a spiritual anthology. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1899:** *Braid on Hypnotism*. An edited reconstruction of James Braid’s writings, also known through the title of Braid’s principal text, *Neurypnology*. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1903:** *Obermann*, by Étienne Pivert de Senancour. Waite’s translated and edited English selection from an epistolary French novel; abridgment and framing make it an editorial object distinct from the full original. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/who/Waite%2C%20Arthur%20Edward%2C%201857-1942))
- **1903:** *The Cloud upon the Sanctuary*, by Karl von Eckartshausen. An edited English version of a Christian-theosophical text central to Waite’s doctrine of an interior church. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1907:** *The Gift of Understanding: A Second Series of Essays by Prentice Mulford*. A second Waite-selected anthology, continuing the editorial project begun with *The Gift of the Spirit*. ([editorial bibliography](https://pennyspoetry.fandom.com/wiki/Arthur_E._Waite))
- **1910:** *The Tarot of the Bohemians*, by Papus. An English editorial manifestation associated with Waite; some bibliographies call him translator, while Gilbert’s principal list cautiously says only “edited,” so the stronger attribution remains disputed. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1910:** *Lumen de Lumine; or, A New Magical Light*, by Thomas Vaughan. A separately edited Vaughan text and an intermediate manifestation of Waite’s Vaughan project. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1912:** *Some Characteristics of the Interior Church*, by Ivan Vladimirovich Lopukhin. An edited English mystical text selected for its relation to Waite’s idea of the hidden or interior church. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1913:** *The History of Magic*, by Éliphas Lévi. Waite’s translated and annotated English edition of Lévi’s history; some catalogues date later impressions 1922, but Gilbert records the first Waite edition under 1913. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1917:** *The Harmonial Philosophy: A Compendium and Digest of the Works of Andrew Jackson Davis*. A large editorial digest that reorganizes Davis’s spiritualist writings into a synthetic system. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1919:** *The Works of Thomas Vaughan: Eugenius Philalethes*. Waite’s collected, annotated, and introduced edition, the culminating manifestation of the Vaughan editorial project begun in 1888. ([Gilbert, p. 200](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf); [Alchemy Bibliography](https://alchemy-bibliography.co.uk/online.shtml))
- **1923:** *The Book of Formation (Sepher Yetzirah)*, attributed to Rabbi Akiba ben Joseph. An edited English manifestation of the foundational Kabbalistic text; the traditional attribution to Akiba is not accepted as secure historical authorship. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))

### Periodicals and serial editorial projects

- **1891:** *The Municipal Review*. A local-government periodical effectively edited by Waite. Because contributors and editors were generally anonymous, the boundaries of his authorship within it cannot be reconstructed securely. ([Gilbert, pp. 79–80](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1894–1895:** *The Unknown World*. An eleven-issue occult monthly conceived and edited by Waite as a non-sectarian forum for occult science and Western mysticism. It is a collective periodical creation, not a book by Waite, and individual unsigned contributions cannot all be assigned with certainty. ([Gilbert, pp. 81–82](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1904 onward:** *Horlick’s Magazine and Home Journal for Australia, India and the Colonies*. A commercial house magazine edited by Waite and filled partly with his pseudonymous essays and poems alongside work by other writers. Its mixed corporate, literary, and advertising identity makes it resistant to treatment as either his sole work or a merely neutral container. ([Gilbert, discussion of the magazine](https://www.scribd.com/document/499184412/A-E-Waite-A-Magician-of-Many-Parts))
- **1876–1938:** *Periodical poems, stories, essays, reviews, and Masonic papers*. Gilbert’s archive inventory preserves these as three volumes of early writings and twenty-three volumes of miscellaneous writings and reviews. They are unquestionably part of Waite’s made corpus, but anonymity, pseudonyms, clipping-based preservation, and the absence of an itemized open scholarly register make it unsafe to invent a definitive list of separate titles here. ([Gilbert, p. 202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))

### Unpublished and unfinished projects

- **circa 1880–1887:** *Collectanea Metaphysica*. A manuscript miscellany of notes, rejected or unpublished poems, and records of séances. Its heterogeneity resists being called one finished work. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **circa 1880–1900:** *The Secret Commonwealth of Rogues and Vagabonds*. A bound body of typescripts containing unpublished sensational fiction; it is a corpus or project rather than a settled book. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **circa 1893:** *Esoteric Freemasonry*. An unpublished typescript of notes on Masonic history, doctrine, symbols, and science. It is an unfinished research project, not a publication suppressed into nonexistence. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1897:** *Diana Vaughan and the Question of Modern Palladism*. An unpublished typescript conceived as a sequel to *Devil-Worship in France*. Its nonpublication does not cancel the project, but its surviving status should not be mistaken for a completed public work. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **circa 1900–1910:** *The Sodality of the Shadows*. Manuscript records of a society bound with two unpublished stories; simultaneously fictional, social, and documentary material, it resists classification as a single literary work. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1900–1906:** *Business Diaries*. Four manuscript volumes documenting Waite’s work for Horlick & Co., including carbon copies of business letters. They are records made in employment, not comfortably “works” in the literary sense, but their resistance belongs in the record. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1902–1903:** *Annus Mirabilis Redivivus*. A private diary incorporating records of the mock Rabelaisian Order of Tosspots and Arthur Machen’s “Hermetic Ritual”; the bound object is partly Waite’s creation and partly a container for collaborative or borrowed material. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1909–1942:** *Pocket diaries*. A nearly continuous series of small private diaries, with the volumes for 1911 and 1914 missing. This is an incomplete documentary corpus rather than a composed public oeuvre. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **1915:** *The Testimonies of Frater Finem Respice*. Waite’s manuscript transcript of conversations with Robert William Felkin conducted between 1906 and 1915 concerning the Third Order and Rosicrucian matters. It records a dialogic event and therefore cannot be attributed to Waite as sole intellectual author. ([Gilbert, p. 202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **circa 1923:** *Dealings in Bibliomania*. An unpublished corrected typescript. Gilbert’s inventory establishes the object but does not warrant inventing a firmer genre, completion status, or publication date. ([Gilbert, p. 201](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
- **undated:** *Concerning Malted Milk*. A typescript corpus of approximately two hundred promotional circular letters written for Horlick & Co. These are commissioned commercial copy and resist absorption into either Waite’s mystical oeuvre or an uncomplicated list of literary works. ([Gilbert, p. 202](https://tarothermeneutics.com/classes/waite-trinick/books/Gilbert-A-E-Waite-a-Magician-of-Many-Parts.pdf))
