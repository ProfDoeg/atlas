## Works by Ze'ev

### Novels and long fiction

- **1926–1927:** *Samson the Nazarite* (*Samson*; *Prelude to Delilah*). A historical-biblical novel, first serialized in Russian in 1926 and issued as a book in 1927; later titles belong to translations and editions, not to additional works. ([Jabotinsky Institute](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/life-story/literature-and-hebrew/); [Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/israeli-history-biographies/vladimir-jabotinsky))
- **1936:** *The Five* (*Pyatero*; Hebrew *Ḥameshtam*). An autobiographically inflected Russian novel about an assimilated Jewish family in Odessa; calling it autobiography would overstate the identification between narrator and author. ([Jabotinsky Institute](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/life-story/literature-and-hebrew/); [Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **Undated; unpublished:** *Galilean Romance*. A film scenario preserved in the Jabotinsky Institute Archives rather than a completed or released film; it therefore survives more securely as a project than as a cinematic manifestation. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **Undated:** *Gantzori*. A scenario for a silent film. Its status resists the word “work”: the collected-text tradition preserves a screenplay, but no corresponding finished film has been established. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))

### Plays

- **1901:** *Blood* (*Krov*; also published as *Minister Haim*). A verse drama performed at the Odessa Municipal Theater; the variant title denotes its publication history rather than a second play. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/israeli-history-biographies/vladimir-jabotinsky))
- **1902:** *All Right* (*Ladno*). A verse play staged at the Odessa Municipal Theater. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/israeli-history-biographies/vladimir-jabotinsky))
- **1908:** *In a Foreign Land* (*Chuzhbina*). A five-act satirical comedy about Russian Jewish life; tsarist censorship prevented contemporary publication, and it appeared in book form only in Berlin in 1922. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/israeli-history-biographies/vladimir-jabotinsky); [Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))

### Poems and movement songs

- **1899:** *City of Peace*. A Russian poem. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1902:** *Ballad*. A poem. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1903:** *Shaflokh*. A secular poem in which the Jabotinsky Institute identifies an early formulation of his individualism; transliteration varies. ([Jabotinsky Institute](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/life-story/literature-and-hebrew/))
- **1904:** *In Memory of Herzl*. A memorial poem. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1904:** *Poor Charlotte*. A narrative poem about Charlotte Corday; part of its print run was reportedly confiscated because authorities read it as revolutionary propaganda. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/israeli-history-biographies/vladimir-jabotinsky))
- **1920:** *Song of the Prisoners of Acre*. A Hebrew political song-poem produced during Jabotinsky’s imprisonment; as a sung collective object, it is both authored text and movement manifestation. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **1923:** *Song of Tel Hai*. A memorial and mobilizing song associated with Joseph Trumpeldor and the Betar tradition. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/catalog-of-files/?arc=341&itemId=151017&section=%D7%A4))
- **1926:** *The Left Bank of the Jordan* (also *Two Banks Has the Jordan*). A political song-poem that became a Revisionist slogan; alternate English titles represent translation choices, not separate poems. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **1932:** *Song of Betar*. A movement anthem: simultaneously poem, song, and performative political protocol for the movement’s ideal member. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **Date not securely established:** *Song of the Flag*. A movement song whose text belongs to the poetic corpus but whose communal performance exceeds the category of printed poem. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **Date not securely established:** *The Vow*. A movement poem functioning as a manifesto or spoken pledge as much as a literary lyric. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **Date not securely established:** *Upward Song*. A Zionist movement song. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **Date not securely established:** *It Is All Mine*. A poem included in the identified Jabotinsky poetic corpus. ([Project Ben-Yehuda author page](https://benyehuda.org/author/62))
- **1890s–1940:** *Songs of Zion*. Not one fixed poem but a body of Zionist verse later gathered under a retrospective rubric; it resists classification as a single obra because the unity belongs partly to editors and movement reception. ([Jabotinsky Institute](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/life-story/literature-and-hebrew/))

### Autobiography, memoir, and witness

- **1928:** *A Word about the Regiment* (*The Story of the Jewish Legion*; Hebrew *Megillat ha-Gedud*). A memoir-history of the Jewish Legion, written and proofed in Russian between approximately 1926 and 1928; the English *Story of the Jewish Legion* was a posthumous translation, not a new work by Jabotinsky. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/TermSearch/?page=9&term=T8114038))
- **1936:** *The Story of My Days*. An autobiography composed in Hebrew. Its later Russian and English forms are translations, while the work itself is an autobiographical narrative rather than a neutral chronicle. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034); [Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **Dates various:** *Memoirs of a Contemporary*. A body of memoir-sketches subsequently constituted as a collected-work volume; it has no single original date or wholly stable boundary and therefore resists treatment as one contemporaneously made book. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **Dates various:** *From the Diaries*. Diary material published in *Rassvet* around 1927; periodical selection and later collection mediate whatever original diary existed. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive search](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/TermSearch/?page=7&term=T8114092))

### Manifestos, programs, and political interventions

- **1903:** *To the Enemies of Zion*. A polemical Zionist pamphlet or grouped intervention, including “Without Patriotism” and “Forward”; it acts as persuasion and mobilization and thus fits “manifesto” better than neutral essay. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1903:** *Critics of Zionism*. A political pamphlet. Revised and expanded editions followed, but they are states of the same obra unless their textual differences are being catalogued. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1903:** *Hebrew Education*. A programmatic essay or pamphlet advocating national education. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1905:** *Doctor Herzl*. A commemorative political-biographical pamphlet. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1905:** *Zionism and Palestine*. A political pamphlet. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1905:** *On Territorialism*. A political argument written during the territorialist controversy. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1905:** *What Must We Do?*. A coordinated series comprising a preface, “Preparation for the Seventh Congress,” “Permanent Work,” “Work in Palestine,” and a conclusion; the project is the argumentative sequence, not merely its later binding. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1906:** *The Bund and Zionism*. A political pamphlet opposing the Bund’s national program. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1906:** *On the Question of Our Political Platform*. A programmatic pamphlet. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1908:** *What Is the Basel Program?*. An explanatory political pamphlet. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1909:** *A-Semitism*. A political-cultural essay distinguishing a desired absence of ethnic antagonism from assimilation. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1912:** *The Helsingfors Program*. A national-rights program associated with the Helsingfors Zionist deliberations; its nature is collective and political, so Jabotinsky’s authorship should not be read as solitary ownership of every formulation. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1915:** *Activism*. A political essay advocating active intervention during the First World War. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1917:** *Turkey and the War*. An English-language wartime political book arguing from the Ottoman question; it is analysis designed to affect policy. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1918:** *The Jewish Nation*. A partly published English manuscript; as preserved, it is an unfinished or incompletely manifested political project rather than a securely bounded published work. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive search](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/TermSearch/?page=4&term=T8114092))
- **1923:** *On the Iron Wall (We and the Arabs)*. A political manifesto arguing that Zionist settlement could proceed only behind power local Arab resistance could not overcome; it was the first of two distinct essays, not an interchangeable title for the pair. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/item/?itemId=114767); [Brian Horowitz, “The Iron Wall Revisited”](https://www.jabotinsky.org/media/158894/brian-horowitz-the-iron-wall-revisited.pdf))
- **1923:** *The Ethics of the Iron Wall*. A separate sequel published about a week after *On the Iron Wall*, defending the moral argument of the earlier manifesto; later translations sometimes obscure their separateness. ([Brian Horowitz, “The Iron Wall Revisited”](https://www.jabotinsky.org/media/158894/brian-horowitz-the-iron-wall-revisited.pdf))
- **1926:** *What We Stand For: An Outline of the Revisionist (Activist) Programme*. A manifesto or draft program defining Revisionist Zionist aims. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive search](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/TermSearch/?page=7&term=T8114092))
- **1927:** *The Revolt of the Elders*. A political-social essay. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **1928:** *Moral Decline in Zionism*. A polemical essay published in *The Jewish Chronicle*. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1928:** *Activism in Language*. A linguistic intervention joining language reform to political activism. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1930:** *The Active Voice*. A linguistic-political essay. ([Russian Wikisource author bibliography](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Автор:Владимир_Евгеньевич_Жаботинский))
- **1931:** *The Jewish State*. An article presenting statehood as the explicit Zionist objective; it should not be confused with the longer 1937 pamphlet of a similar title. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1932:** *The Meaning of Preparedness*. A Betar instructional-political essay: partly manifesto, partly protocol for organized readiness. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1934:** *The Betar Idea* (also *The Ideology of Betar*). A doctrinal text explaining Betar’s political and ethical formation; both manifesto and educational protocol. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive search](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/TermSearch/?page=4&term=T8114092))

- **1937:** *The Jewish State: A Solution of the Jewish Question*. A Russian-language political pamphlet or book-length manifesto; its title is also translated *A Jewish State—The Solution of the Jewish Question*. - **1937:** *The Threatened Partition of Palestine*. An address to British members of Parliament, subsequently printed as a pamphlet; it is an intervention in a specific policy process rather than a detached treatise. - **1938:** *Proposals and Decisions for the Third International Betar Conference*. ### Social and economic designs

- **Date not securely established:** *The Jubilee Idea*. A social-economic essay deriving a distributive program from the biblical Jubilee. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **Date not securely established:** *Chapters in the Social Philosophy of the Bible*. A sequence of interpretive social essays rather than a completed systematic treatise. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **Date not securely established:** *Introduction to the Theory of Economy*. An introductory economic text; the surviving title specifies a beginning and should not be expanded into a nonexistent complete system. ([Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))
- **Date not securely established:** *Class*. A short social-theoretical essay. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1926:** *Land Reform for Palestine*. A policy proposal published in *The Zionist*. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1930s:** *The Five Mems*. A social-welfare formula—food, shelter, clothing, education, and medicine—dispersed through Jabotinsky’s writings rather than a securely bounded standalone text. It is best treated as a project or conceptual protocol, not forced into the shape of a book.

### Language projects and protocols

- **1911 onward:** *Turgman*. A publishing and translation project founded by Jabotinsky to issue world literature in Hebrew; it is an institutional obra whose individual books were made collaboratively by authors, translators, editors, and printers. ([Encyclopaedia Judaica entry](https://www.encyclopedia.com/people/history/israeli-history-biographies/vladimir-jabotinsky))
- **1920s:** *The Hebrew Atlas*. A Hebrew school atlas produced with Shlomo Perlmann and published in London; authorship is collaborative, and the atlas is simultaneously book, pedagogical instrument, cartographic program, and language-making project. ([Project Ben-Yehuda lexicon](https://benyehuda.org/lexicon/03402.php))
- **1920s:** *Kol-Bo for the Student*. A Hebrew educational compendium associated with Jabotinsky and Shlomo Perlmann; as a collaborative reference object, it should not be reduced to individually authored prose. ([Project Ben-Yehuda lexicon](https://benyehuda.org/lexicon/03402.php))
- **1930:** *Hebrew Pronunciation*. A linguistic treatise or protocol prescribing a Sephardi-based system of modern Hebrew pronunciation. ([Project Ben-Yehuda lexicon](https://benyehuda.org/lexicon/03402.php))
- **Date not securely established; published posthumously:** *Taryag Millim: 613 Hebrew Words—Introduction into Spoken Hebrew in Latin Characters*. A practical language-learning protocol; posthumous editions dated 1949 or 1950 do not establish when Jabotinsky completed the underlying text. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/); [Jabotinsky Institute archive catalogue](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/search-archive/TermSearch/?page=13&term=T8114089))

### Editorial and periodical creations

- **1897–1940:** The newspaper and journal corpus. Jabotinsky made thousands of articles, reviews, feuilletons, unsigned leaders, speeches subsequently printed as articles, and multilingual versions under names including Vladimir Illirich and Altalena; the standard annotated bibliography records **2,467 items** in twenty languages, including books, poems, plays, speeches, articles, translations, and unpublished manuscripts. This corpus resists being called 2,467 separate obras without examining version-relations: many records are translations, republications, revised manifestations, or transcripts of spoken acts. ([Mina Graur, *The Writings of Ze’ev Jabotinsky: A Bibliography, 1897–1940*](https://www.jabotinsky.org/%D7%97%D7%A0%D7%95%D7%AA-1/%D7%96%D7%91%D7%95%D7%98%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%99/%D7%9B%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%99-%D7%96%D7%90%D7%91-%D7%96%D7%91%D7%95%D7%98%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%A1%D7%A7%D7%99-%D7%91%D7%99%D7%91%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%95%D7%92%D7%A8%D7%A4%D7%99%D7%94-1897-1940/))
- **1923–1932:** *Rassvet*. A Russian-language Zionist weekly edited by Jabotinsky in Berlin and Paris and used as the principal manifestation-space for his fiction, feuilletons, and political interventions. The periodical itself was collective, so it is an editorial project rather than an individually authored work. ([Brian Horowitz, “The Iron Wall Revisited”](https://www.jabotinsky.org/media/158894/brian-horowitz-the-iron-wall-revisited.pdf))
- **1920s–1930s:** Revisionist Zionist and Betar platforms. Jabotinsky drafted or shaped platforms, declarations, conference resolutions, oaths, curricula, and organizational instructions for the Revisionist movement and Betar. These are performative and frequently collective texts: manifestos and protocols whose authorship cannot always be reduced to the person whose handwriting survives. ([Jabotinsky Institute archive](https://en.jabotinsky.org/archive/))
- **1899–1940:** Correspondence. Approximately seven thousand letters survive or are reported in the literary archive; each is a made text, but many have no authorial title and the published collected volume is an editorial selection rather than a work Jabotinsky designed as a book. Consequently the correspondence resists honest enumeration as a title-list without reproducing an archival finding aid. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/); [Project Ben-Yehuda collected works](https://benyehuda.org/collections/20034))

### Posthumous editorial manifestations—not new works by Ze'ev

- **1947–1959:** *Collected Works*, eighteen volumes. A posthumous editorial Gesamtkunstwerk encompassing autobiography, essays, translations, memoirs, novels, speeches, drama, stories, criticism, feuilletons, sketches, poems, and letters; its volumes are indispensable witnesses but should not be mistaken for eighteen books conceived and made in this arrangement by Jabotinsky. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1941:** *From the Pen of Jabotinsky*. A posthumous English selection; an editorial manifestation, not a newly authored obra. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
- **1961:** *Nation and Society*. A posthumous selection of articles; the constituent essays are Jabotinsky’s, while their book-form grouping is editorial. ([Jabotinsky Institute bibliography](https://en.jabotinsky.org/zeev-jabotinsky/selected-bibliography/))
