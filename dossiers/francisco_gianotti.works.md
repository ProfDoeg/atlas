## Works by Francisco Gianotti

### Buildings and residences

- **1911:** *Vivienda colectiva para el Dr. Bancalari*. Apartment building at Montevideo 1018, Buenos Aires; demolished. It appears in Gianotti’s own 1964 register of works as transcribed by Horacio Caride. [Caride]
- **1911:** *Vivienda colectiva para Adela de Piratte*. Apartment building at Marcelo T. de Alvear 1317, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1912:** *Residencia del Dr. José Solá*. Private residence at Avenida del Libertador 4164, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1912:** *Vivienda colectiva, Marcelo T. de Alvear 1473/77*. Apartment building whose ground floor was subsequently altered. Caride added it to Gianotti’s own register, so its documentary basis differs from that of the other listed works. [Caride]
- **1912:** *Vivienda colectiva para la sucesión de Esteban Devoto*. Apartment building at Marcelo T. de Alvear 1449, Buenos Aires; its ground floor was later altered. [Caride]
- **1913:** *Residencia de Héctor Dellepiane*. Residence at Gaspar Campos and Melo, Vicente López, later occupied by the Fundación Raggio. [Caride]
- **1914:** *Vivienda colectiva para el Dr. Federico Pinedo*. Apartment building at Carlos Pellegrini and Arroyo, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1914:** *Mercado Central de Salta*. Municipal market commissioned by Emilio San Miguel, now generally called Mercado San Miguel. [Caride]
- **1914:** *Vivienda colectiva y comercio para Adolfo San Miguel*. Mixed apartment-and-commercial building at Lavalle 1517, Buenos Aires; the commercial portion was demolished. [Caride]
- **1915:** *Galería General Güemes*. Mixed-use passage, offices, residences, theatre, restaurants, cabaret, bank and shops between Florida and San Martín streets: less a single-purpose building than an urban microcosm or architectural Gesamtkunstwerk. Completed in 1915; the Florida façade and substantial interior fabric were later lost or altered. [Caride]
- **1915:** *Banco Supervielle*. Bank premises at San Martín 154/176, associated with the Galería Güemes complex; the original interior was demolished. [Caride]
- **1915:** *Vivienda de Federico Crovetto*. Residence at Córdoba 1480, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1915:** *Vivienda de Federico Usandivaras*. Private house in Salta, also identified in later summaries as the Chalet Usandivaras at Belgrano and Pasaje Castro. [Caride]
- **1916:** *Confitería del Molino*. A combined café-confectionery, rental building and ornamental interior at Callao 32 and Rivadavia 1815. Its architecture, furniture, metalwork, lighting and stained glass form an integrated decorative ensemble rather than merely a building shell. [Caride] [Conde Rodríguez and Cristofani]
- **1916:** *Vivienda colectiva y comercios de Almirante Brown y Pinzón*. Gianotti’s own mixed-use apartment building, recorded at Almirante Brown 938/989 and Pinzón 401/421. [Caride]
- **1917:** *Residencia Gianotti*. Gianotti’s residence at Ciudad de la Paz 965, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1922:** *Residencia Liberti*. Private residence at Echeverría and O’Higgins, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1922:** *Vivienda colectiva para el Dr. Ruiz*. Apartment building at Uruguay 669, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1923:** *Residencia Brach*. Private residence at Posadas 1064, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1925:** *Edificio para Ana Ortiz Basualdo de Olazábal*. Office-and-commercial building at Avenida Presidente Roque Sáenz Peña 647/651; its ground floor was extensively altered. [Caride]
- **1926:** *Edificio para Felisa Ortiz Basualdo de Alvear*. Office-and-commercial building at Avenida Presidente Roque Sáenz Peña 622; its ground floor was extensively altered. [Caride]
- **1926:** *Edificio Andes*. Mixed apartment-and-commercial building for Arnaldo Arabehety at Corrientes 1300. The ground-floor premises—formerly the Confitería Real and later Pizzería Banchero—were heavily modified. [Caride]
- **1927:** *Edificio de la Compañía de Navegación Italia-América*. Office building occupying the triangular block at Avenida Presidente Roque Sáenz Peña 660, Bartolomé Mitre and Maipú; later Banco di Napoli and Banco Comafi. Preliminary designs quoted the Venetian Gothic more literally than the executed building. [Caride]
- **1928:** *Vivienda del Dr. Isamendi*. Residence at Corrientes 608, Olivos, Province of Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1929:** *Residencia Gianotti*. Gianotti’s private house at Avenida del Libertador 5280, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1930:** *Vivienda colectiva para Alberto Tarasido*. Apartment building at Tucumán and Montevideo, Buenos Aires; its ground floor was subsequently altered. [Caride]
- **1932:** *Edificio de la Sociedad Anónima Industrial Schaffhausen*. Commercial or office building at Reconquista 336/356, completed early in 1932 and regarded by Caride as one of Gianotti’s three principal works. The surname also appears as “Shaffhausen” in later summaries. [Caride]
- **1935:** *Vivienda colectiva para la familia Varela*. Apartment building in the 1000 block of Riobamba, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1936:** *Vivienda colectiva para la familia Carrizo*. Apartment building at Juncal and Esmeralda, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1940:** *Mercado de Monroe y 11 de Septiembre*. Market building in Buenos Aires, commonly called Mercado Monroe. [Caride]
- **1942:** *Vivienda del ingeniero Marzo*. Single-family house on Arribeños street, Buenos Aires; the catalogue supplies no street number. [Caride]
- **1946:** *Vivienda colectiva de Avenida del Libertador 5176/5181*. Apartment building owned by Gianotti; demolished. Published summaries differ slightly over the terminal street number, also giving 5182. [Caride] [Gutiérrez]
- **1955:** *Edificio Vázquez Italia*. Building at Bogotá and Bolivia, Buenos Aires, designed with Gianotti’s son Pedro Gianotti. [Caride]
- **1957:** *Vivienda colectiva y comercio para Emilia Barros*. Mixed apartment-and-commercial building at Esmeralda 977/981, Buenos Aires. [Caride]
- **1959:** *Vivienda colectiva para la familia Correa Rosetti*. Apartment building at Arenales 2080, designed with Pedro Gianotti and identified as Francisco Gianotti’s last realized work. [Caride] [Gutiérrez]

### Interiors, alterations and architectural interventions

- **Before 1909, date uncertain:** *Decoración interior del Palazzo Casanova*. Interior decoration in Milan’s Piazza del Duomo. Caride says Gianotti executed several Milanese interiors and singles this one out, but neither a firm date nor a complete inventory is supplied; it therefore resists treatment as a securely bounded catalogue entry. [Caride]
- **1916:** *Ampliación de la antigua Facultad de Medicina*. Addition of a third floor to the building at Córdoba and Junín, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1917:** *Decoración interior del Club 3 de Febrero*. Interior decorative scheme for the club in Salta. It is an architectural interior rather than an autonomous building. [Caride]
- **1918:** *Confitería La París*. Café interior and related architectural work at Marcelo T. de Alvear and Libertad, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1930:** *Refección de la vivienda principal de la Estancia Ortiz Basualdo*. Alteration of Samuel Ortiz Basualdo’s principal estancia residence at Las Armas, Province of Buenos Aires. Because this was a refashioning of an existing house, Gianotti’s obra is the intervention rather than the entire building. [Caride]
- **1933:** *Remodelación del Gran Hotel Plaza*. Architectural remodeling of the hotel in Mendoza. [Caride]
- **1953:** *Ampliación del Banco Supervielle*. Enlargement of the bank premises on the San Martín side of the Galería Güemes; subsequently much altered. [Caride]
- **1954:** *Decoración del local de Exprinter S.A.*. Commercial interior on the San Martín side of the Galería Güemes; subsequently altered. [Caride]

### Pavilions, displays and temporary constructions

- **1906:** *Pabellones para la Exposición Internacional de Milán*. An unspecified group of exhibition pavilions. The attribution is qualified: Caride says Gianotti worked on them “probably” with Alfredo Melani and his brother Giovanni Battista Gianotti, while the later CEDODAL chronology says he designed some pavilions; neither source provides a complete set of titles. [Caride] [Gutiérrez]
- **1910:** *Pabellón de Italia, Exposición Internacional del Centenario*. Temporary exhibition building in Buenos Aires, later demolished. Gianotti and Mario Palanti participated in its erection, but Gaetano Moretti was the architect; calling the entire pavilion Gianotti’s independent work would therefore overstate his authorship. [Caride] [CEDODAL drawings catalogue]
- **1916:** *Panorama de la Batalla de Salta*. An immersive historical panorama designed for Salta and exhibited in Buenos Aires. The CEDODAL monograph’s synopsis attributes the project to Gianotti, but it is absent from his 1964 list as published by Caride; its exact physical form and survival are consequently uncertain. [Gutiérrez]
- **1917:** *Pabellón Circus*. Entertainment pavilion at Corrientes 480/496, Buenos Aires; demolished. [Caride]
- **1920:** *Pabellón del Parque 3 de Febrero, Salta*. Park pavilion, recorded by Caride as demolished. Later derivative lists identify a “Hostería Parque de los Lagos,” now a natural-sciences museum, so the identification and survival status are not entirely settled. [Caride] [Urbipedia]

### Projects and competition designs

- **1912:** *Pasaje Florida*. Architectural project that became the Galería General Güemes, completed under that later name in 1915. The 1912 project is the intention and design phase, not a second building. [Gutiérrez]
- **1928:** *Monumento a la Bandera Nacional, Rosario*. Competition project made with sculptor Troiano Troiani. Sources call it a first-prize design while also reporting that the competition was declared void; it was not the monument eventually built. [Caride] [Gutiérrez]
- **1928:** *Hotel en Villa Carlos Paz*. Unrealized hotel project for Villa Carlos Paz, Córdoba. The catalogue gives no client or fixed building name. [Caride]
- **1935:** *Hidrodeslizador para transporte de pasajeros*. Project for a passenger hydroglider: an industrial or transport design rather than architecture, and apparently unrealized. [Caride]
- **1936:** *Edificio de la Compañía Ítalo Argentina de Electricidad*. Winning competition design for an office building at Avenida Roque Sáenz Peña and Maipú, Buenos Aires; it was not constructed. [Caride]
- **1936:** *Proyecto Matarazzo, São Paulo*. Unrealized rationalist building project preserved through two architectural watercolours in the CEDODAL collection. It is omitted from Gianotti’s published 1964 list, but the archival catalogue explicitly attributes and dates the drawings. [CEDODAL drawings catalogue]
- **1952:** *Iglesia ortodoxa griega*. Unrealized project for a church on José Hernández street in Belgrano, Buenos Aires. The catalogue does not supply a formal dedication or parish name, so “Iglesia ortodoxa” is descriptive rather than necessarily a fixed title. [Caride]

### Transport and designed infrastructure

- **1921:** *Balsa para el transporte fluvial de maderas*. Design for a raft or barge to carry timber from Paraguay. Its status as a realized vessel is not made explicit by the catalogue, so it is safest treated as a transport-design project. [Caride]
- **1927:** *Emplazamiento del mástil de la Avenida de los Italianos*. Installation or siting of a flagpole designed by Gaetano Moretti. The made act attributable to Gianotti is the emplacement, not authorship of the mast itself, and thus it resists classification as an independent Gianotti work. [Caride]

### Collaborative and incompletely attributable work

- **1909–1911, approximate:** *Work for the studio of Arturo Prins and Oscar Ranzenhofer*. Gianotti worked as a draughtsman in the architects’ office after arriving in Buenos Aires. This is a body of professional production, not a securely separable obra, because surviving summaries do not identify every drawing or design he made there. [Caride]
- **1911:** *Proyecto de la nueva Facultad de Derecho*. Mario Palanti’s published chronology records his intervention in the project in collaboration with Gianotti while both were connected with the Prins–Ranzenhofer office. The precise division of authorship is unspecified, and the project should not be represented as Gianotti’s independent building. [IAA/Arquitectura en Línea: Palanti]
- **Date uncertain:** *Residencia urbana—boceto*. An early Beaux-Arts residential sketch reproduced by Caride as evidence of Gianotti’s first work in Argentina. No client, address or secure date is given, and it may be a study rather than a project intended for construction. [Caride]

### Writings and editorial work

- **1924–1926, at least:** *Arquitectura y Arte Decorativo*. Illustrated architectural periodical whose editorial council included Gianotti, Mario Palanti, Francisco De Pretis and Sebastiano Locati; it later became *Áurea*. This is collective editorial production, not a singly authored text, and its run and Gianotti’s exact contributions are not fully itemized by the consulted sources. [IAA/Arquitectura en Línea: Palanti] [Gutiérrez]
- **1927:** *La casa nueva*. Article named in the CEDODAL chronology. The accessible scholarly synopsis supplies the title and year but not publication details or text, so its bibliographic identity remains incomplete. [Gutiérrez]
- **1964:** *Memorias*. Gianotti’s memoirs, completed in 1964 and used as the basis for his own register of works. The consulted sources do not identify a published edition; it is therefore an archival autobiographical text rather than a conventional book publication. [Caride]
