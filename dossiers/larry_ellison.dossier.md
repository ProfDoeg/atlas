# Larry Ellison: Research Dossier

**Subject:** Lawrence Joseph Ellison  
**Coverage:** Birth to 20 August 2026  
**Status:** Living  
**Method:** Documentary reconstruction from corporate and securities filings, declassified government records, court opinions, official government releases, contemporaneous reporting, interviews, institutional histories, and published biographies. Monetary amounts are nominal unless stated otherwise.

## Evidentiary key

- **Documented fact** means supported by a primary record, official filing, judicial finding, or multiple independent contemporary sources.
- **Reported/alleged** means a named source published the claim, but it has not been established by a court or independently documented in the public record reviewed here.
- **Disputed** means a material denial or competing version exists.
- **Unverified** means the claim circulates but reliable public evidence is insufficient.
- Corporate conduct is not attributed personally to Ellison unless a source connects him to it. No public record reviewed for this dossier shows that Ellison has been criminally charged or convicted.

## Family and Ancestry

### Birth family and adoption

Ellison was born to **Florence Spellman**, a nineteen-year-old, unmarried Jewish woman. Biographies describe his biological father as an Italian-American U.S. Army Air Forces pilot stationed abroad; his name has not been reliably published, and Ellison said he never met him. When Ellison contracted pneumonia at about nine months, Florence arranged for her Chicago aunt and uncle, **Lillian Spellman Ellison** and **Louis Ellison**, to adopt him. He did not learn he was adopted until approximately age twelve and did not meet Florence again until he was forty-eight.

Louis Ellison was a government employee, commonly described as an auditor for the Chicago Housing Authority. Biographical accounts say that he accumulated and then lost money in Chicago real estate during the Depression. The frequently repeated story that Louis chose “Ellison” to commemorate Ellis Island is plausible family lore but is not supported by an immigration or name-change document in the sources reviewed. Larry remembered Lillian as loving and protective and Louis as austere, distant and persistently doubtful of his prospects. No siblings are reliably documented.

### Marriages, partners and children

The early marriages are consistently recorded:

1. **Adda Quinn**, married 1967, divorced 1974. They met through an employment agency in Chicago or shortly after Ellison’s move west, depending on the biographical account. They lived modestly in the Bay Area while he did contract programming.
2. **Nancy Wheeler Jenkins**, married in late 1976 or 1977, divorced 1978. Published biographies state that she relinquished her interest in the young Software Development Laboratories for $500 during the divorce. That often-retold amount comes from biography rather than a public divorce filing.
3. **Barbara Boothe**, an early Oracle employee, married 1983, divorced in 1986 or 1987. Their children are **David Ellison** (born 1983), founder of Skydance Media and, after the 2025 merger, chairman and CEO of Paramount Skydance, and **Margaret “Megan” Ellison** (born 1986), founder of Annapurna Pictures and an Academy Award-nominated producer.
4. **Melanie Craft**, novelist, married 18 December 2003, divorced 2010. U.S. Representative Tom Lantos officiated and Steve Jobs photographed the ceremony.

From roughly 2010, Ellison was publicly associated with Ukrainian-born model, actor and animal-welfare advocate **Nikita Kahn**. Some recent profiles call her his fifth wife and date a divorce to 2020; other contemporary coverage described her only as a girlfriend. No marriage record was produced by the sources reviewed, so the legal status of that relationship remains **unverified**.

By November 2024, University of Michigan football reporting identified **Jolin Zhu**, also known as **Keren Zhu** and now commonly styled **Jolin Ellison**, as Ellison’s wife. The university’s donor network thanked her and Ellison for helping assemble a name-image-likeness package for quarterback recruit Bryce Underwood. Public-record reporting says Zhu graduated from Michigan in 2012 and shared Florida and California addresses with Ellison. Later profiles report that the couple is raising four young children; names, birth dates and exact parentage have not been publicly established. Accordingly, recent claims that Ellison has “six children” are plausible but rest on press reporting rather than an official family statement.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1944–1962

Ellison was taken from New York to Chicago as an infant and grew up in a small apartment in **South Shore**, then a heavily Jewish lower-middle- and middle-class neighborhood. He attended Reform Jewish services with his adoptive parents but rejected a bar mitzvah at thirteen. He later described Jewish scripture as interesting mythology rather than literal truth and said he saw no evidence for dogma. This skepticism coexisted with a strong ethnic and political identification with Israel in later life.

Accounts agree that he was intellectually quick, argumentative and inattentive to subjects that did not interest him. Science, mathematics, astronomy and later computer programming did. His relationship with Louis supplied a durable personal narrative: the father said he would amount to nothing; the son organized his adult identity around disproving him. This story appears in sympathetic interviews and hostile biographies alike, but it is retrospective family testimony, not independently verifiable psychology.

Ellison attended **South Shore High School**. He was not known as a conventional academic high achiever. The later “college dropout genius” myth compresses a more mixed record: strong performance in science, an ability to learn technical material quickly, and repeated disengagement from formal curricula.

## Education and Formation, 1962–1966

Ellison entered the **University of Illinois at Urbana–Champaign** around 1962. Sources differ on whether his intended course was pre-medical study or a broader science program. The Academy of Achievement says he was named science student of the year. He left after his second year without taking final examinations following Lillian’s death.

After time in California, he enrolled for one term at the **University of Chicago** in 1966, studying physics and mathematics. He encountered computer design and programming there and found contract programming both more absorbing and better paid than academic work. He left without a degree. No specific professor has emerged as a documented personal mentor. His most important intellectual influence in computing was indirect: **Edgar F. “Ted” Codd**, whose 1970 paper “A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks” supplied the conceptual basis for the product Ellison later commercialized.

Ellison’s formative pattern was already visible: technical self-education, aversion to credentials, attraction to abstraction when linked to a practical market, and willingness to represent an unfinished idea as an imminent product.

## Early Career, 1966–1976

Ellison moved permanently to the **San Francisco Bay Area** in 1966. Early jobs are incompletely documented and sometimes appear in different order. The best-supported sequence includes programming or technical work for **Fireman’s Fund**, **Wells Fargo**, **Amdahl Corporation**, and then **Ampex**. At the University of Chicago and afterward, he learned that fixed-price programming let him earn more by finishing faster; he also consulted for local businesses.

At Ampex, Ellison met **Robert “Bob” Miner**, his manager and an accomplished programmer, and **Edward “Ed” Oates**. **Bruce Scott** joined the later start-up as its first employee and co-architect rather than an original equity founder in the strictest corporate histories. The men worked in the world of large-scale storage and database systems.

### Project ORACLE at Ampex

Declassified CIA memoranda document an Ampex mass-storage effort called **Project ORACLE** for the Agency’s Office of Joint Computer Support. The system was intended to store and retrieve large quantities of foreign-intelligence data. A 1975 acceptance report recorded serious failures transferring files between disk and tape-based memory. The documents establish the government project and its purpose, but the released pages reviewed do **not name Ellison**.

Company histories and Ellison’s later accounts place him and his colleagues on or adjacent to that Ampex work. This matters because two episodes are often collapsed:

- **Ampex’s Project ORACLE** was a CIA-sponsored mass-storage project before the company Oracle existed.
- **Software Development Laboratories**, founded afterward, sold an early relational database to the CIA. Ellison publicly called the CIA the start-up’s “very first customer.”

The record supports a formative CIA contract relationship and first-customer revenue. It does **not** show CIA equity ownership, a venture-capital investment, or an In-Q-Tel-style “seed round.” In-Q-Tel itself was not created until 1999. The phrase “CIA seed funding” is therefore defensible only in the loose sense of an early customer contract, not in its ordinary equity-financing sense.

## Chronological Life History

### 1977–1986: Founding and first ascent

In June 1977 Ellison, Miner and Oates formed **Software Development Laboratories (SDL)** in Santa Clara with approximately $2,000; institutional histories say Ellison supplied $1,200. Miner was the principal technical architect, Oates developed software and managed technical work, Scott became the earliest additional programmer, and Ellison became the salesman, strategist and public face.

Ellison and Oates recognized a market in Codd’s relational model and IBM’s experimental System R work. IBM was slow to turn the relational model into a portable commercial product. SDL built for Digital Equipment PDP-11 minicomputers and IBM mainframes, emphasized SQL, and—crucially—made the database portable across hardware and operating systems. This separated the database vendor’s fortunes from a single machine manufacturer.

The company’s public origin story says the CIA became its first customer for the product code-named Oracle. Some biographies put the first contract at about $50,000, but no contract document establishing that amount was found in the declassified material reviewed. Precision Instrument is often identified as the first private-sector customer; the U.S. Navy was another early government customer.

In 1979 the company, renamed **Relational Software, Inc. (RSI)**, released **Oracle Version 2**. There was no commercial Version 1. Ellison later said the number was a marketing device because customers would hesitate to buy a first version. In 1982 the company took the name **Oracle Systems Corporation**. Oracle Version 3, written in C, extended portability; later releases added transaction processing, distributed databases and networked client/server operation.

Oracle cultivated a highly aggressive sales culture: promises preceded completed features, quotas were severe, and products were pitched against designated enemies. Growth was extraordinary. Revenue rose from millions to hundreds of millions, and Oracle went public on **12 March 1986**, one day before Microsoft’s IPO. Contemporary accounts put the offering proceeds at about $31.5 million.

### 1987–1993: Hypergrowth, accounting crisis and recovery

Oracle expanded internationally and sold databases to banks, airlines, retailers, manufacturers and governments. Its platform independence helped it compete against IBM, Ingres, Sybase and Informix. Rapid hiring and decentralized sales practices outgrew financial controls.

In 1990 Oracle announced a major earnings miss and restated results. Securities litigation alleged premature and contingent revenue recognition; one federal opinion summarizes the claim that second-quarter 1990 net income was overstated by roughly 30 percent through about $15 million of contracts signed after quarter-end or containing contingencies. Oracle’s share price lost roughly four-fifths of its value at the trough, the company reported its first loss and cut hundreds of jobs. Oracle and Arthur Andersen later funded a class settlement without an admission that Ellison had committed criminal fraud.

Ellison later called the episode an “incredible business mistake”: he had focused on product and competition while neglecting sales administration, accounting and legal controls. He replaced senior managers, brought in professional financial leadership, and concentrated on engineering. **Oracle7**, released in 1992, stabilized the product, added stored procedures and triggers, and restored the company’s technical and commercial position. Bob Miner, suffering from cancer, withdrew from day-to-day product leadership and died in 1994.

In the early 1990s Ellison also suffered severe physical injuries. A Hawaiian surfing accident broke his neck and punctured a lung. During rehabilitation, a high-speed bicycle crash shattered his left elbow; he underwent four operations at UC Davis with metal bars and screws. The treatment led to a multimillion-dollar gift that established the Lawrence J. Ellison Musculoskeletal Research Center and later an ambulatory-care building. No chronic incapacity is publicly documented.

### 1994–2002: Database wars, the network-computer campaign and Microsoft

Oracle became the dominant independent relational-database supplier. Ellison’s public rivalries were part product strategy and part theater:

- **Phil White and Informix:** Informix briefly looked capable of overtaking Oracle. The companies litigated over alleged recruitment and trade secrets. Informix later restated earnings; White was convicted in an unrelated federal securities case. Ellison’s victory became part of Oracle folklore, but White’s later imprisonment was not caused by Ellison.
- **Bill Gates and Microsoft:** Ellison argued that networked servers and thin clients would displace expensive, decentralized Windows PCs. Microsoft’s rise in database servers and enterprise software made the rivalry commercial as well as symbolic.
- **SAP and PeopleSoft:** Oracle increasingly competed beyond databases in enterprise applications.

In 1995 Ellison promoted the **Network Computer**, a low-cost, centrally managed terminal drawing applications and data from servers. Oracle created Network Computer Inc. and attracted partners including Netscape, Nintendo, Sony and Sega. The device never displaced the PC: bandwidth, web software and pricing were not ready, and cheap PCs improved rapidly. The project failed as a product category but anticipated browser-based applications, software as a service, centrally managed endpoints and cloud computing.

Ellison joined Apple’s board in 1997 after his close friend **Steve Jobs** returned. He had publicly contemplated an Apple takeover that would restore Jobs but abandoned it. He resigned the board in September 2002, saying his schedule prevented adequate attendance, while continuing to advise Jobs and management.

In 2000 Oracle admitted hiring Investigative Group International to investigate organizations that publicly supported Microsoft during the federal antitrust case. Investigators obtained discarded documents and were accused of offering janitors cash for access to trash. Ellison called the tactics “unsavory” in hygiene terms but defended exposing undisclosed Microsoft funding as a public service. The operation was documented corporate opposition research; it was not an intelligence-agency operation.

After the 11 September 2001 attacks, Ellison advocated a consolidated federal database and uniform digital identification cards, and offered Oracle software to the government without charge. He argued that fragmented watch lists had prevented agencies from connecting information. Civil-liberties groups objected that a centralized identity system would enable tracking and abuse. Ellison later said the proposal had been misunderstood and backed away from the label “national ID,” but he continued to support government data integration. This was the clearest public statement of a long-running intellectual position: centralized data is, in his view, more accurate, secure and useful than fragmented systems.

### 2003–2010: Acquisition machine, litigation and integrated systems

Oracle turned from a primarily database company into a consolidated enterprise-software vendor. Ellison and presidents **Safra Catz** and **Charles Phillips** used Oracle’s cash flow to buy competitors and adjacent technologies. Major transactions included:

- **PeopleSoft**, agreed December 2004 for approximately **$10.3 billion**, after an eighteen-month hostile campaign. The U.S. Justice Department sued to block it and lost at trial.
- **Siebel Systems**, announced 2005 for approximately **$5.85 billion**, strengthening customer-relationship software.
- **BEA Systems**, 2008 for approximately **$8.5 billion**, adding middleware.
- **Sun Microsystems**, agreed 2009 and closed January 2010 for **$7.4 billion** gross value, bringing Java, Solaris, SPARC hardware and MySQL under Oracle.

The Sun transaction made Oracle a systems company that could design database, middleware, operating system and hardware together. It also generated long-running conflicts with open-source communities and Google. Several prominent Sun engineers left; OpenOffice contributors created LibreOffice; Oracle ended OpenSolaris; and Oracle’s ownership of Java set up a decade of litigation over Android.

Ellison’s personal trading also produced major litigation. Shareholder plaintiffs alleged that he sold almost $900 million of Oracle stock in January 2001 before disclosure of a weak quarter. A Delaware court refused to accept an Oracle special litigation committee’s independence because of dense Stanford University ties. Ellison denied wrongdoing and in 2005 agreed to donate $100 million to charities in Oracle’s name and pay roughly $22 million in plaintiffs’ fees. This was a civil settlement, not a finding or admission of insider trading.

In 2003 Matthew Symonds published *Softwar*, an authorized-access biography with unusually extensive marginal comments by Ellison that sometimes contradicted the author. It remains both a biography and a primary source for Ellison’s self-presentation.

### 2010–2014: Hardware, cloud and transition from CEO

Oracle marketed engineered systems such as Exadata and integrated Sun hardware with its database. Ellison initially mocked the imprecision of the term “cloud,” then repositioned Oracle around cloud applications and infrastructure as subscription computing became unavoidable.

Oracle sued Google in 2010, alleging Android infringed Java copyrights and patents acquired with Sun. The patent claims failed; litigation over Java API declaring code reached the U.S. Supreme Court. In 2021 the Court assumed copyrightability for argument’s sake and held Google’s copying a fair use. Ellison personally testified in the 2012 trial. The case was a strategic loss for Oracle and a major precedent for software interoperability.

Ellison’s BMW Oracle Racing team won the America’s Cup in Valencia in 2010 after prolonged litigation over race rules. Oracle Team USA defended the trophy in San Francisco in 2013, recovering from a nominal 1–8 deficit to win 9–8. The team had been penalized two points and three members were sanctioned for unauthorized modifications to smaller World Series boats. Ellison was not personally found to have ordered the misconduct.

In August 2013, amid Edward Snowden’s disclosures, Ellison called NSA surveillance “essential” and said it would become objectionable if used by one political party against another. His position aligned with his earlier support for integrated national databases and departed sharply from much of Silicon Valley’s public response.

On **18 September 2014**, Ellison stepped down as CEO after thirty-seven years. Catz and **Mark Hurd** became co-CEOs; Ellison remained full-time as executive chairman and CTO, responsible for technology, product direction and architecture.

### 2015–2022: Cloud transition, NetSuite, health data, Israel and Tesla

Oracle continued its acquisition program and struggled to close the cloud-infrastructure gap with Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud. Its later Oracle Cloud Infrastructure architecture emphasized high-performance databases, bare-metal computing and multicloud interconnection.

In 2016 Oracle acquired **NetSuite** for approximately **$9.3 billion**. Ellison owned about 35 percent of NetSuite, which he had helped finance at its founding, and received billions from the sale. Oracle shareholders alleged that he forced Oracle to overpay for an asset he controlled. Ellison recused himself from the special committee process. After five years of litigation and a ten-day trial, the Delaware Court of Chancery entered judgment for Ellison and Catz. The Delaware Supreme Court affirmed on 21 January 2025, finding that Ellison had influence or “clout” but did not exercise general or transaction-specific control, and that neither defendant materially misled the committee.

Ellison donated **$200 million** to USC in 2016 for a cancer-research and treatment institute led by oncologist **David Agus**. In parallel, Ellison, Agus and Dave Hodgson co-founded **Project Ronin**, intended to synthesize electronic medical records and other data for cancer decisions. Ronin, built on Oracle Cloud, closed in March 2024 after employing about 150 people; reporting cited difficulty finding sufficient paying customers.

In December 2018 Tesla appointed Ellison and Kathleen Wilson-Thompson as independent directors under a Securities and Exchange Commission settlement requiring more oversight of Elon Musk. Ellison disclosed a large Tesla holding, became a close adviser and friend to Musk, and served until August 2022. The relationship joined two founder-controlled technology networks and later included Musk’s stays on Lānaʻi.

Oracle agreed in December 2021 to buy electronic-health-record company **Cerner** for **$28.3 billion**, its largest acquisition; it closed 8 June 2022. The transaction put Oracle inside hospital records, public-health infrastructure and the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs modernization program. Ellison framed medical records as another fragmented-data problem that a unified database and cloud could solve.

Oracle relocated its corporate headquarters from Redwood Shores to Austin in 2020, though operations remained dispersed. Ellison announced that he had moved to Lānaʻi and would work through cloud systems. In 2022 he purchased a Manalapan, Florida, estate reportedly for $173 million; public registration later placed his residence there.

### 2023–20 August 2026: AI infrastructure, defense, media and state influence

Oracle’s late cloud position became an advantage in the AI infrastructure buildout because it could supply large GPU clusters and work with rival clouds. At the White House on 21 January 2025, President Donald Trump appeared with Ellison, OpenAI’s Sam Altman and SoftBank’s Masayoshi Son to announce **Stargate**, a proposed U.S. AI infrastructure program whose promoters said could invest up to $500 billion over four years. That figure was an announced ambition, not capital already spent.

Oracle also moved closer to the center of U.S. media and data policy:

- **TikTok:** From 2022, “Project Texas” placed U.S. TikTok user data on Oracle infrastructure and assigned Oracle review and monitoring roles. A new U.S. joint venture completed in January 2026 gave Oracle, Silver Lake and Abu Dhabi investor MGX 15 percent each; ByteDance retained 19.9 percent and other existing investors held the balance. Oracle stores protected U.S. data and has a role in inspecting, retraining, testing and updating the U.S. recommendation system. The structure gives Oracle technical oversight, not unilateral editorial control.
- **Paramount Skydance:** A Skydance-led transaction closed in August 2025 after FCC approval. Ellison family financing and trusts supplied much of the capital; David Ellison received operational and voting control of the merged company. Larry’s economic backing is documented, but “Larry Ellison personally runs CBS” overstates the governance record.

In September 2025 Oracle appointed Clay Magouyrk and Mike Sicilia co-CEOs; Catz became executive vice chair. Ellison remained executive chairman and CTO. Oracle’s fiscal 2026 Form 10-K reported total revenue of approximately **$67.4 billion** and research and development expense of about **$10.3 billion**. The business had shifted toward cloud infrastructure, while databases and enterprise applications remained foundational.

On 25 March 2026 Trump appointed Ellison to PCAST with Catz, Mark Zuckerberg, Sergey Brin, Jensen Huang, Lisa Su and other technology leaders. The formal advisory role placed him inside federal AI and science policy.

In July 2026 the Defense Department announced an enterprise software agreement with Oracle potentially worth nearly **$7 billion** over ten years, covering the Department of Defense, Coast Guard and intelligence community. The department projected at least $441 million in savings through consolidated terms. Contract ceilings are not guaranteed expenditures.

As of the cutoff date, Ellison remained an active product strategist. His fortune had become unusually concentrated: the 2025 proxy’s 40.6 percent Oracle stake made daily rankings highly volatile. On 10 September 2025 Bloomberg’s calculation briefly placed him above Elon Musk at about $393 billion after an Oracle share-price surge; later estimates fell sharply. No single net-worth figure should be treated as a bank balance.

## Companies, Institutions, Technologies, Projects, and Financial Interests

### Oracle as Ellison’s central instrument

Oracle has had several corporate names: Software Development Laboratories (1977–1979), Relational Software, Inc. (1979–1982), Oracle Systems Corporation (1982–1995), and Oracle Corporation thereafter. Ellison’s contributions were not principally those of a lone programmer. Miner, Scott and large engineering teams wrote the core systems. Ellison’s durable roles were to recognize the commercial opening in relational databases, insist on portability, sell before the product was finished, define competitors, recruit and remove executives, allocate capital, and return repeatedly to product architecture.

The technology sequence most associated with his leadership is:

- **Portable relational database and SQL:** Oracle’s decisive early product. Version 2 (1979) was the first commercially released Oracle database; Version 3’s C implementation widened platform support.
- **Client/server and distributed databases:** Oracle6 and Oracle7 made the database a standard enterprise back end during the 1980s–90s.
- **Internet computing:** Oracle8i and 9i marketed the database as infrastructure for web applications.
- **Network Computer:** A commercial failure in the late 1990s, but a precursor to thin clients and hosted applications.
- **Enterprise applications and middleware:** Built and acquired, especially through PeopleSoft, Siebel, BEA and later NetSuite.
- **Engineered systems:** Sun enabled Oracle to sell integrated servers, storage, operating systems and database appliances, including Exadata.
- **Cloud applications and OCI:** Oracle moved from licensing software on customer premises toward subscriptions and rented infrastructure.
- **Health-data platforms:** Cerner/Oracle Health, medical-record integration, research and proposed national health databases.
- **AI clusters and databases:** GPU infrastructure, vector search, database-integrated machine learning and large AI training contracts.

### Investments and affiliated projects

- **NetSuite:** Early backer and major shareholder before Oracle’s 2016 acquisition.
- **Salesforce:** Ellison invested early in protégé Marc Benioff’s company before it became a major Oracle rival.
- **Tesla:** Three million shares disclosed around his 2018 board appointment; later holdings changed through splits and sales. He remained a material investor after leaving the board.
- **Theranos:** Early investor. Public evidence does not establish that he participated in laboratory operations or the fraud for which Elizabeth Holmes and Ramesh Balwani were convicted.
- **Project Ronin:** Co-founder; closed in 2024.
- **Skydance/Paramount:** Family trusts and guarantees financed son David’s studio and the 2025 Paramount transaction. The asset bridges Ellison wealth into film, television and news.
- **SailGP and America’s Cup teams:** Founded and financed elite sailing competitions and technology development. These are relevant as engineering, media-rights and institutional projects, though secondary to the three central themes of this dossier.

### Wealth, leverage and property

Ellison’s wealth is overwhelmingly the market value of Oracle stock, not a diversified cash reserve. The 2025 proxy records 346 million Oracle shares pledged to secure personal indebtedness. Pledged shares create liquidity without a taxable sale but introduce collateral risk if the stock falls. The proxy states that Oracle’s board limits and monitors pledging; it does not disclose Ellison’s complete private debt.

He acquired approximately **98 percent of Lānaʻi** in 2012 from Castle & Cooke for a reported price near $300 million, including resorts, commercial properties and utilities. The State of Hawaiʻi retained the balance. Through Pūlama Lānaʻi and related entities, he became the island’s dominant private landowner and employer. Residents have credited investment in infrastructure and jobs and raised concerns about housing, water, rent, transparency and dependence on one owner. This is a concentrated local political economy, not simply a private vacation property.

His other publicized properties have included a Japanese-inspired Woodside estate, Malibu houses, a Lake Tahoe compound, a Rhode Island estate, a Kyoto villa and the Manalapan property. They are omitted from detailed treatment because they add little to the technology, intelligence and political networks at issue.

## Technology, Intelligence, Surveillance, and Government

### The three distinct intelligence relationships

**1. Origin:** Ellison’s Ampex work and Oracle’s first CIA sale. Declassified Project ORACLE records establish a CIA mass-storage program; Ellison’s later public statement establishes the CIA as his start-up’s first customer. This supplied revenue, credibility and a name.

**2. Long-term government vendor:** Oracle databases became infrastructure across civilian government, defense and intelligence agencies. The relationship is ordinary procurement in legal form but unusually consequential in scale because databases organize identities, operations, logistics, finance, health and classified reporting.

**3. Contemporary classified cloud and defense ecosystem:** Oracle now markets isolated cloud regions for U.S. Secret and Top Secret workloads. Its National Security Regions are designed for ICD 503/705 environments, DISA Impact Level 6 and sensitive compartmented facilities. In December 2022 Oracle joined Amazon, Google and Microsoft on the Pentagon’s **Joint Warfighting Cloud Capability (JWCC)** vehicle, whose aggregate ceiling is $9 billion through 2028 and which spans unclassified through Top Secret workloads from headquarters to the tactical edge. A contract ceiling is not a payment to each vendor.

In 2023 Oracle announced TS/SCI authorization for intelligence-community cloud workloads. In 2025 the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency awarded an Oracle unlimited-license agreement initially worth about $20.7 million, and GSA negotiated government-wide “OneGov” discounts. The July 2026 enterprise agreement potentially worth nearly $7 billion consolidated more DoD and intelligence-community software use.

### Palantir and defense-technology partners

Oracle and **Palantir Technologies** announced a formal partnership in April 2024. Palantir Foundry and Artificial Intelligence Platform can run on Oracle public cloud, dedicated regions, government regions, air-gapped environments and “Roving Edge” devices. The companies market joint systems for governments and regulated industries.

Oracle’s 2025 Defense Ecosystem also connected OCI with firms including **Anduril** and Israel’s **Rafael Advanced Defense Systems**. Oracle advertises Rafael’s IMILITE intelligence-analysis system and FIRE WEAVER networked targeting technology in this ecosystem.

Palantir was itself backed by the CIA’s In-Q-Tel. That history should not be retroactively assigned to Oracle: Oracle predates In-Q-Tel by twenty-two years. The documented connection is that two firms with different intelligence histories now integrate platforms for defense and security customers.

### National databases and surveillance doctrine

Ellison’s public statements form a consistent doctrine:

- Fragmented government databases cause duplication, errors and missed connections.
- Centralized records permit a “single version of the truth.”
- Identity and health systems should be national in scope.
- Government surveillance is a legitimate security tool, with the political misuse of data—not collection itself—as the boundary he has most often named.

The doctrine appeared in his 1995 Smithsonian discussion of a digital “Alexandrian library,” his 2001 national-ID campaign, his 2013 defense of NSA collection, Oracle’s electronic-health-record strategy and his 2024 advocacy of AI-assisted population-scale monitoring. At Oracle’s September 2024 financial-analyst meeting, he described AI continuously analyzing police body cameras, vehicle cameras, doorbells and other feeds: “Citizens will be on their best behavior because we are constantly recording and reporting everything that’s going on.” He presented constant recording as supervision of police as well as civilians and suggested drones could replace police cars in pursuits. It is both a technical worldview and a business model: the proposed state requires the databases, cloud regions and analytical systems Oracle sells.

This does not prove that every Oracle government deployment conducts surveillance or that Ellison directs agency operations. It does show that surveillance-adjacent uses are neither accidental nor alien to his expressed philosophy.

### Commercial data brokerage and privacy

Oracle assembled a large advertising-data business through **BlueKai**, **Datalogix**, **Moat**, AddThis and related acquisitions. Plaintiffs in *Katz-Lacabe v. Oracle America* alleged that Oracle created dossiers linking online browsing to offline purchases, location and other personal information without adequate consent. Oracle denied wrongdoing.

In 2024 Oracle agreed to a **$115 million** class settlement and restrictions on some data practices. The federal court gave final approval on 14 November 2024. Settlement is not an admission. Separately, Oracle closed its advertising business in 2024 after annual revenue had fallen to roughly $300 million from about $2 billion in 2022. The episode is the strongest documented basis for calling Oracle a commercial surveillance-data company, but the business was later discontinued.

### Security failures

In 2025 Oracle told health customers that attackers had accessed older Cerner servers and copied patient data; Reuters reported that the FBI was investigating an attempted extortion campaign. Public reporting did not establish the number of records or all affected providers. The incident concerned legacy servers not yet migrated to Oracle Cloud. It demonstrates that centralization can improve control but also concentrates consequences when security fails.

## Zionism, Israel, and Israeli State/Defense Networks

### Ellison’s stated position

Ellison’s support for Israel is ethnic, political and technological rather than conventionally theological. In 2017 he said:

> “For 2,000 years, we were a stateless people, but now we have a country we can call our own.”

He referred to IDF soldiers as defending “our home.” This is direct evidence of a Zionist identification in the ordinary sense of Jewish collective statehood and support for Israel. It does not by itself locate him within a particular Israeli party or religious tendency.

### Donations to Israeli military-linked causes

- **2007:** Reported pledge of **$500,000** to reinforce a community center in Sderot against rocket attacks.
- **2014:** **$10 million** to Friends of the Israel Defense Forces (FIDF).
- **2017:** **$16.6 million** to FIDF, then the organization’s largest single gift, for well-being facilities at a training campus for co-ed infantry units.

The publicly documented FIDF total is therefore at least **$26.6 million**, apart from the Sderot gift and other possible giving. FIDF is a U.S. nonprofit that supports soldiers’ welfare; it is not the Israeli Defense Ministry, though its beneficiary population is the IDF.

### Benjamin Netanyahu and the corruption-case orbit

Ellison and **Benjamin Netanyahu** have been repeatedly described as close friends. The relationship entered Israel’s judicial record indirectly:

- In December 2019 Israel’s attorney general listed Ellison among 333 possible prosecution witnesses in Netanyahu’s corruption trial. His relevance concerned media ownership and relationships around **Case 2000**, in which prosecutors allege Netanyahu negotiated favorable coverage from Yedioth Ahronoth publisher Arnon Mozes in return for legislation disadvantaging a rival paper. Being listed as a witness did not make Ellison a suspect or defendant.
- Israeli reporting says Netanyahu or intermediaries approached Ellison as a possible buyer of Yedioth Ahronoth or related media assets. No purchase occurred.
- Haaretz reported in 2020 that Ellison persuaded film producer Arnon Milchan to release lawyer Boaz Ben Tzur so Ben Tzur could represent Netanyahu. This is a press report about private lobbying, not a court finding.
- In September 2021 Haaretz reported that Ellison offered Netanyahu an Oracle board seat worth about $450,000 annually plus stock. Netanyahu denied receiving an offer; Oracle did not substantively confirm it. Netanyahu never joined the board. The claim remains **reported and disputed**.
- In August 2021 Netanyahu and his family vacationed on Lānaʻi while Ellison remained a potential witness. The Netanyahu family said it paid its own expenses. No public evidence reviewed shows Ellison paid for the trip or that it affected testimony.

The documentary conclusion is a close personal and political association that intersected with a criminal case as a witness relationship, not evidence that Ellison was charged in Netanyahu’s alleged offenses.

### Oracle in Israel

Oracle has operated in Israel for decades, employing local engineers and selling databases, applications and cloud services. In 2021 it opened an underground cloud facility in Jerusalem designed for government and regulated workloads. Oracle bid for the Israeli government’s **Project Nimbus** cloud contract but lost to Google and Amazon Web Services; an Israeli court rejected Oracle’s appeal. Claims that Oracle “runs Nimbus” are false.

Oracle’s published defense-partner pages establish work with Rafael technologies. Additional military relationships were reported by *The Intercept* in 2025 from internal Oracle Slack posts and reproduced by the Business & Human Rights Resource Centre:

- a four-year **Project Menta** with the Israeli Air Force, described internally as enabling unspecified “important military” work;
- a multi-year collaboration with an entity called **Unit 81** to accelerate soldiers’ procurement requests;
- employee work on **Words of Iron**, a social-media advocacy tool said to have been developed with Israeli ministries after 7 October 2023.

Oracle and the Israeli Defense Ministry did not publicly confirm the classified-project details in that reporting. The Slack material is therefore **documented investigative reporting based on leaked internal communications**, not an independently released contract file. Unit numbering is also ambiguous in public descriptions and should not be conflated automatically with the better-known IDF intelligence technology Unit 8200.

Digital Forensic Research Lab independently analyzed Words of Iron as a tool that generated and coordinated pro-Israel messages and encouraged reporting of opposing content. Its analysis documents the tool’s behavior; the specific Oracle role rests on the leaked Slack reporting.

After 7 October 2023, Oracle displayed an Israeli flag on its global site, matched donations, donated $1 million to Magen David Adom, assisted Israeli employees and reservists, and reportedly supplied equipment or support to IDF units. Oracle Israel and global CEO Safra Catz expressed unusually explicit political alignment. Catz told Calcalist that “if you are not for America or Israel, don’t work here,” framing the position as shared by her and Ellison. The quotation is Catz’s, not Ellison’s, but she publicly represented it as company leadership policy.

### Zionism, media ownership and TikTok

The 2025 Paramount transaction and 2026 TikTok joint venture generated a narrative that Ellison was assembling a pro-Israel media system. The underlying facts are:

- Ellison’s personal pro-Israel record and Netanyahu friendship are documented.
- Ellison family wealth financed Paramount Skydance; David Ellison has legal operational control.
- Oracle owns 15 percent of the TikTok U.S. venture and performs security and algorithm-related functions.
- Paramount acquired Bari Weiss’s *Free Press* and elevated Weiss inside CBS News after the Ellison-backed merger, adding a prominent pro-Israel editorial figure to the company.

What is **not established** is that Larry Ellison personally ordered CBS or TikTok to suppress Palestinian viewpoints, that Israel motivated the acquisitions, or that Oracle unilaterally controls TikTok moderation. Those are inferences or allegations, not demonstrated facts as of the cutoff date. The concentration of technical, financial and editorial influence makes scrutiny reasonable; it does not substitute for evidence of a specific instruction.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### Religion, Japanese aesthetics and worldview

Ellison was raised Reform Jewish, refused a bar mitzvah and has described himself in terms ranging from religious skeptic to atheist. He distinguishes respect for Jewish stories from literal belief. His attachment to Israel therefore cannot be explained simply by religious observance.

He developed a long interest in Japanese architecture, gardens, art, samurai armor and Zen-inflected aesthetics. He built and bought Japanese-style properties and named boats with Japanese references. Public profiles sometimes turn this into a “samurai” identity. The evidence supports sustained collecting and aesthetic influence; claims that he formally practiced Zen or lived by bushidō are mostly journalistic metaphor and self-branding.

His intellectual themes are unusually stable:

- competition reveals quality;
- institutions should be run by a small number of unusually capable people;
- centralized systems outperform fragmented ones;
- software should hide hardware complexity;
- personal autonomy matters, but competent government should be able to see and integrate data;
- mortality and disease are engineering problems worth attacking with information.

This combination explains apparent contradictions: an individualist who favors centralized databases; a religious skeptic who speaks of Israel as “our home”; a college dropout who funds elite research institutes; and a critic of the word “cloud” who built a cloud infrastructure company.

### U.S. political activity

Ellison’s earlier giving crossed party lines. Reports identify support for Bill Clinton-era Democrats as well as Republicans. His later record moved decisively rightward:

- Supported Mitt Romney in 2012.
- Gave roughly $5 million to support Marco Rubio’s 2016 presidential campaign through super PACs.
- Hosted a Trump fundraiser at his Rancho Mirage estate in February 2020, with admission levels reported at $100,000 and $250,000. About 300 Oracle employees staged a walkout; more than 1,000 signed a petition. Ellison reportedly did not attend the event in person.
- On 14 November 2020 joined a call with Senator Lindsey Graham, Sean Hannity, Trump lawyer Jay Sekulow, attorney James Bopp and True the Vote founder Catherine Engelbrecht discussing ways to contest Trump’s election loss. The call is documented through an email filed in litigation and participant reporting. No source reviewed shows Ellison committed a crime or directed the January 6 attack.
- Donated at least **$35 million** from 2020–23 to a super PAC supporting Senator Tim Scott; a January 2022 transfer of $15 million was then Ellison’s largest disclosed single political contribution.
- Reporting in 2026, based on unnamed people familiar with the donation, said Ellison gave about **$45 million** to a pro-Trump nonprofit in 2024. Because nonprofit “dark money” is not itemized in FEC filings, the amount is **reported but not independently visible in campaign-finance records**.
- Joined Trump’s PCAST in March 2026.

Ellison’s political access overlaps with Oracle’s federal interests—cloud procurement, AI regulation, TikTok, health data and defense licensing. Overlap establishes a potential conflict environment, not a quid pro quo unless a specific exchange is proven.

### Philanthropy and institutional influence

Ellison signed the Giving Pledge and wrote that he had put nearly all his assets into a trust intending to give away at least 95 percent. A pledge is an intention, not a completed distribution.

- **Ellison Medical Foundation:** Founded in 1997; gave about $430 million to roughly 600 researchers, chiefly in aging and infectious disease. It stopped accepting new aging and neuroscience grants in 2013 while honoring existing awards.
- **UC Davis:** Musculoskeletal research and ambulatory care gifts following his treatment.
- **Harvard:** Pledged $115 million for an institute on world health, then withdrew the unfulfilled pledge in 2006 after President Lawrence Summers resigned. Harvard expressed disappointment; Ellison’s spokesman said the project had been Summers’s creation.
- **USC / Ellison Medical Institute:** $200 million gift announced in 2016 for cancer research and treatment. The institute later became independent and expanded its mission.
- **Ellison Institute of Technology, Oxford:** A planned campus and partnership with the University of Oxford, with investment described as exceeding £1 billion, focused on health, food security, climate/clean energy and AI. Completion was projected for 2027.
- **Tony Blair Institute for Global Change (TBI):** Lighthouse Reports, based on financial records and interviews with twenty-nine current and former employees, reported that Ellison’s foundation gave about $130 million in 2021–23 and pledged another $218 million. The investigation alleged that TBI’s government consulting increasingly promoted Oracle-compatible technology and centralized public data. TBI said donations were ring-fenced, it remained independent and it did not advocate for any vendor. The funding is documented; the claim that TBI became an Oracle lobbying arm is an investigated allegation, not a judicial finding.

### Aggressive licensing and lock-in

Oracle’s enterprise model has long combined mission-critical databases with complex licenses and audits. Customers and rivals accuse it of using technical dependency and audit pressure to force larger purchases. Oracle describes audits as contract enforcement and emphasizes support, security and integration. The public record supports a reputation for hard bargaining and high switching costs, but “extortion” is rhetorical unless tied to a particular adjudicated case.

### Compensation and governance

For many years Oracle’s board faced shareholder criticism over Ellison’s very large option grants and weak responsiveness to advisory pay votes. His controlling-size ownership aligns him with the stock price while also limiting outside shareholders’ practical influence. The 2025 proxy’s 40.6 percent stake and pledged-share disclosure are the clearest current governance facts. Delaware’s NetSuite judgment is important counterevidence to the assumption that influence automatically amounted to legal control in every transaction.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### “Oracle was created by / owned by / seeded by the CIA”

1. **Story:** The CIA created Oracle, financed it as a covert front, or has controlled it since inception.
2. **Appearance and circulation:** Variants grew from biographies and resurfaced after Ellison’s 2014 OpenWorld statement; technology histories, anti-surveillance writing and social media often repeat the compressed form.
3. **Evidence for:** Ampex ran CIA Project ORACLE; Ellison and colleagues worked at Ampex; Oracle took the project name; the CIA was the company’s first customer. Early contract revenue and credibility plainly mattered.
4. **Evidence against:** No reviewed filing or declassified document shows CIA equity, a covert ownership stake or agency control. The released Ampex documents do not even name Ellison. “Seed funding” confuses customer procurement with venture investment.
5. **Reputational effect:** The story makes Oracle appear as a permanent privatized arm of U.S. intelligence. The factual relationship is substantial without that unsupported ownership claim.

### “Larry Ellison invented the relational database”

1. **Story:** Ellison invented relational databases or SQL.
2. **Origin:** Founder-centered Oracle marketing and simplified business profiles.
3. **Evidence for:** He recognized and commercialized the opportunity early, backed a portable SQL product and drove adoption.
4. **Evidence against:** Codd developed the relational model; IBM researchers developed System R and SQL/SEQUEL; Miner, Scott and other Oracle engineers built Oracle’s product.
5. **Effect:** It elevates commercial insight into lone technical invention and obscures collaborative engineering.

### “Oracle Version 2 proves there was a secret Version 1”

1. **Story:** Version 1 was classified for the CIA or hidden because of defects.
2. **Origin:** The missing number invited speculation; CIA associations amplified it.
3. **Evidence:** Ellison and the Computer History Museum state there was no commercial V1; “V2” was chosen to make the product look mature. Internal prototypes naturally preceded release, but that is not evidence of a secret classified commercial version.
4. **Effect:** Became a compact legend about Ellison’s audacious marketing.

### “He caused Phil White to go to prison”

1. **Story:** Ellison destroyed Informix and sent its CEO to prison.
2. **Origin:** 1990s database-war profiles and retrospective Oracle victory stories.
3. **Evidence:** Oracle and Informix fought fiercely and litigated; Informix later collapsed into an accounting scandal. White was prosecuted and convicted by federal authorities for securities fraud.
4. **Against:** The conviction arose from Informix’s conduct, not a prosecution controlled by Ellison.
5. **Effect:** Reinforced the image that Ellison’s enemies must fail literally, not merely competitively.

### “The Network Computer was a foolish failure” versus “Ellison invented the cloud”

The first version treats the 1990s thin client as mere hubris; the second treats later software-as-a-service as proof he was completely right. Both erase timing and implementation. The product failed in its market because networks, browsers, storage and prices were inadequate. Its centralized-management thesis anticipated parts of web and cloud computing but did not uniquely invent them.

### “He is an observant Jew” / “his Israel politics cannot be Jewish because he is an atheist”

Ellison’s own words sustain neither shortcut. He is Jewish by family and upbringing, rejects literal religious dogma, and makes explicitly collective Jewish arguments for Israel. Ethnic-national identification and theological skepticism coexist in the record.

### “Netanyahu was given an Oracle board seat”

Haaretz reported an offer; Netanyahu denied receiving it, and he never served. The established event is the report and denial, not an appointment.

### “Oracle runs Project Nimbus”

Oracle bid, was disqualified or lost, challenged the result and lost the appeal. Google and AWS won. Oracle has other Israeli government, cloud and defense relationships, but it is not a Nimbus prime contractor.

### “Ellison bought TikTok/CBS to censor Palestine”

The narrative arose in 2025–26 among pro-Palestinian activists and critics of media concentration. It draws on real facts—FIDF donations, Netanyahu ties, Oracle Israel’s politics, family financing of Paramount, and Oracle’s TikTok role. No public documentary evidence reviewed proves this motive or a personal censorship order. It is a politically influential hypothesis, not an established fact.

### Self-created mythology: dropout, outsider, samurai, survivor

Ellison repeatedly tells his life as a sequence of doubters defeated: adoptive father, universities, IBM, rival database companies, Microsoft, cloud incumbents and mortality. The narrative is grounded in real reversals—the 1990 near-collapse and later recovery are central—but edits out the institutional help of government customers, co-founders, engineers, capital markets and acquired companies. The myth helped Oracle recruit, sell and endure because the corporation’s identity became inseparable from the founder’s refusal to concede.

## Works by or Under the Name of Larry Ellison

Ellison has not published a conventional book solely authored by him. His corpus consists mainly of speeches, interviews, corporate keynotes, a co-credited/annotated biography, an op-ed, testimony and patent co-authorship.

## Illness, Injuries, and Psychological Claims

Documented physical events include infant pneumonia, a surfing accident involving a broken neck and punctured lung, the bicycle crash that shattered an elbow, and exposure to the fatal 1998 Sydney-to-Hobart storm while aboard *Sayonara*. Six sailors died in that race; Ellison’s crew survived and took line honors, finishing first across the line.

No reliable public evidence establishes an addiction, diagnosed psychiatric illness or hospitalization for a psychological crisis. Biographers have described insecurity, narcissism, fear of death, compulsive competition and risk-seeking. These are interpretations based on behavior and interviews, not clinical diagnoses. His funding of aging and cancer research is sometimes reduced to a personal terror of death; Ellison has indeed spoken about extending healthy life, but the single-cause psychological explanation remains speculative.

## Present Status and Later-Life Assessment

Ellison is alive and active. He has never truly retired from Oracle: the 2014 title change transferred operating management while preserving product and strategic authority. The 2025 management change again left him as executive chairman/CTO while installing co-CEOs.

His late-life position joins four forms of power:

1. **Technical infrastructure:** databases, health records, AI compute and classified cloud.
2. **Equity control:** more than 40 percent of Oracle voting equity in the latest reviewed proxy.
3. **Political access:** Trump, PCAST, federal procurement and major campaign/nonprofit giving.
4. **Media/data influence:** financing behind Paramount Skydance and Oracle’s role in TikTok U.S.

His Israel network cuts across all four: personal donations, Netanyahu, Oracle Israel, defense partnerships and public communications. These connections are strongest when described individually; claims of a single centrally directed intelligence-media project go beyond the available evidence.

## Reputation, Influence, and Legacy

Because Ellison is living, “legacy” remains provisional. The durable, documentable elements are:

- Commercialization of relational databases into a platform-independent enterprise standard.
- Creation of a founder-centered sales and acquisition machine that survived a near-fatal accounting crisis.
- A model of enterprise-software power based on customer dependence, recurring support, acquisitions and regulatory/legal combat.
- Early government and intelligence contracting that matured into classified cloud, defense AI partnerships and national-security procurement.
- Persistent advocacy for centralized identity, health and government data, now extended into AI.
- A pro-Israel political and philanthropic network unusually explicit for a major U.S. technology founder.
- Mentorship and capital links to later founders, especially Benioff and Musk, and family-financed expansion into Hollywood and news.
- Medical-research philanthropy that funded hundreds of investigators, alongside failed or withdrawn initiatives such as the Harvard pledge and Project Ronin.
- A corporate mythology in which audacity, enemy selection and survival are treated as technical virtues.

The historical argument over Ellison is unlikely to be a simple balance of philanthropy against controversy. It will concern who controls large databases, what states and firms can infer from them, and how technical infrastructure converts private ownership into governmental and cultural power.
