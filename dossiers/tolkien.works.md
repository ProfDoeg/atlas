## Works by J. R. R. Tolkien

### Lifelong projects and composite obras

- **c. 1905–1973:** *Invented languages and writing systems*. A lifelong linguistic project encompassing Nevbosh, Naffarin, Qenya/Quenya, Goldogrin/Gnomish, Noldorin/Sindarin, Adûnaic, Khuzdul, the Black Speech, and numerous lesser languages, together with scripts including Sarati, Tengwar and Cirth; boundaries between individual languages and successive developmental states are often uncertain. ([Tolkien Estate timeline](https://www.tolkienestate.com/life/timeline-1892-1949/); [chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1914–1973:** *The legendarium*. Tolkien’s central, never-completed project: an evolving assemblage of poems, tales, annals, genealogies, maps, invented documents and linguistic writings concerning Arda. *The Silmarillion*, *Unfinished Tales*, *The History of Middle-earth*, *The Children of Húrin*, *Beren and Lúthien*, *The Fall of Gondolin*, *The Nature of Middle-earth* and *The Fall of Númenor* are posthumous editorial manifestations of this project, not separate books completed by Tolkien in those published forms. ([Tolkien Estate, “The History of Middle-earth”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/david-bratman-the-history-of-middle-earth/); [Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **c. 1916–1920:** *The Book of Lost Tales*. The first sustained prose form of the legendarium, organized around a mariner hearing Elvish histories; unfinished and repeatedly reconceived, it survives as a project rather than a finished book. ([Tolkien Estate, “The History of Middle-earth”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/david-bratman-the-history-of-middle-earth/))

- **c. 1917–1925:** *The Lay of the Children of Húrin*. An unfinished alliterative narrative poem of the First Age, preserved in more than one state and published posthumously in *The Lays of Beleriand*. ([Tolkien Estate, “The Fall of Arthur”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/christopher-tolkien-the-fall-of-arthur/); [Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **1920s:** *The Lay of Leithian*. An unfinished narrative poem in rhyming couplets recounting Beren and Lúthien; Tolkien later began but abandoned a substantial revision. ([Tolkien Estate, “The History of Middle-earth”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/david-bratman-the-history-of-middle-earth/))

- **1920s–1973:** *The Silmarillion*. Not a single completed manuscript but successive “Sketch,” *Quenta*, annals and later revisions. The 1977 book is Christopher Tolkien’s editorial construction from this unfinished textual field. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html); [Tolkien Estate, “The History of Middle-earth”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/david-bratman-the-history-of-middle-earth/))

- **1936–1937:** *The Lost Road*. An unfinished time-travel novel devised partly as Tolkien’s contribution to a projected exchange with C. S. Lewis; it introduced the Númenórean catastrophe into the legendarium. ([Tolkien Estate, “The History of Middle-earth”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/david-bratman-the-history-of-middle-earth/))

- **1945–1946:** *The Notion Club Papers*. An unfinished fictional record of an Oxford discussion group whose dreams and linguistic experiences lead toward Númenor; it resists classification between novel, pseudo-document and philological experiment. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1950s–1973:** *Late writings on Middle-earth*. A body of unfinished narratives, philosophical dialogues, linguistic essays, chronologies and notes—including “Athrabeth Finrod ah Andreth,” “Laws and Customs among the Eldar,” “Myths Transformed” and “The Shibboleth of Fëanor”—rather than a book Tolkien prepared for publication. ([Tolkien Estate, “The History of Middle-earth”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/david-bratman-the-history-of-middle-earth/); [Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

### Published imaginative books and stories

- **1937; substantially revised 1951 and 1966:** *The Hobbit, or, There and Back Again*. A children’s fantasy novel that became attached to the legendarium during composition and revision; Tolkien also supplied maps, lettering and illustrations. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1945:** *Leaf by Niggle*. A short allegorical or “fairy” story about making, incompletion and grace, first published in *The Dublin Review*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1949:** *Farmer Giles of Ham*. A comic pseudo-medieval tale, presented as though translated from a Latin chronicle; an earlier manuscript and an unfinished sequel also survive. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1954–1955:** *The Lord of the Rings*. A single long romance issued for commercial reasons in three volumes—*The Fellowship of the Ring*, *The Two Towers* and *The Return of the King*—with poems, invented documents, maps, scripts and appendices making it approach a Gesamtkunstwerk of narrative and philology. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1967:** *Smith of Wootton Major*. A short fairy-story written in connection with an abandoned preface to George MacDonald’s *The Golden Key*, then developed as an independent tale. ([Tolkien Estate, “Writing”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/); [Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **c. 1925–1928:** *Roverandom*. A children’s story created for Tolkien’s son Michael after the loss of a toy dog; unpublished during Tolkien’s life and issued posthumously from manuscript in 1998. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html); [Tolkien Estate, “Writing”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/))

- **c. 1928–1932:** *Mr. Bliss*. A handwritten and fully illustrated children’s picture-book manuscript; publication was abandoned because colour reproduction was too costly, and a facsimile manifestation appeared in 1982. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1920–1943:** *The Father Christmas Letters*. Annual illustrated letters made for Tolkien’s children, with invented handwriting, stamps, characters and serial incidents; they are domestic performances and graphic objects as much as literary texts. Posthumous editions differ markedly in completeness. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1960:** *The Bovadium Fragments*. An unfinished satirical story about motor traffic, bureaucracy and an Oxford-like city; its fragmentary manuscript was first published as a book in 2025. ([Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

### Poetry and poetic projects

- **1910–1973:** *The poems*. A corpus of nearly two hundred poems and substantial variants—mythological, devotional, comic, occasional, philological and personal—now most comprehensively represented by *The Collected Poems of J.R.R. Tolkien* (2024). Individual drafts frequently change title or identity, so the corpus resists reduction to a stable checklist of “finished works.” ([Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography); [chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1911:** *The Battle of the Eastern Fields*. A long schoolboy narrative poem, Tolkien’s earliest known published imaginative work. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1913:** *From the many-willow’d margin of the immemorial Thames*. A published undergraduate poem, signed only “J.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1914:** *The Voyage of Éarendel the Evening Star*. A poem generally treated as the first germ of the legendarium; later versions and the Eärendil story make its identity developmental rather than fixed. ([Tolkien Estate timeline](https://www.tolkienestate.com/life/timeline-1892-1949/))

- **1915:** *Goblin Feet*. A fairy poem published in *Oxford Poetry 1915*; Tolkien later disliked its diminutive conception of fairies. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1915–1916:** *Narqelion*. An early poem in Tolkien’s invented Elvish, preserved in manuscript and first published in full in 1999. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1920:** *The Happy Mariners*. A poem associated with the emerging mythology, published in *The Stapeldon Magazine* and surviving in revised states. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1922:** *The Clerke’s Compleinte*. A comic poem written in a form of Middle English and originally published pseudonymously. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1923:** *The Cat and the Fiddle: A Nursery-Rhyme Undone and Its Scandalous Secret Unlocked*. A mock-explanatory poem later transformed into “The Man in the Moon Stayed Up Too Late.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1923:** *The City of the Gods*. A short mythological poem subsequently absorbed into the published record of the Lost Tales. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1923:** *Enigmata Saxonica Nuper Inventa Duo*. Two riddles composed in Old English. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1923:** *Iúmonna Gold Galdre Bewunden*. A poem repeatedly revised, ultimately becoming “The Hoard”; the versions should not be mistaken for simple reprints of one fixed text. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1923:** *Why the Man in the Moon Came Down Too Soon*. A poem later substantially revised under the title “The Man in the Moon Came Down Too Soon.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1924:** *An Evening in Tavrobel*. A poem connected with the Lost Tales’ frame narrative. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1924:** *The Lonely Isle*. A mythological poem about Tol Eressëa. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1924:** *The Princess Ní*. A poem later revised as “Princess Mee.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1925:** *Light as Leaf on Lindentree*. A lyric associated with the tale of Beren and Lúthien and later reworked within *The Lord of the Rings*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1927:** *Fastitocalon*. A mock-bestiary poem, first published as part of “The Freaks of Fisiologus” and later revised. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1927:** *Iumbo, or, Ye Kind of Ye Oliphaunt*. A mock-bestiary poem whose later descendant is the “Oliphaunt” verse. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1929–1930:** *The Lay of Aotrou and Itroun*. A long Breton-style narrative poem, first published in 1945; its early manuscript and published text differ. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1930–1934:** *The Fall of Arthur*. An unfinished alliterative Arthurian poem; it remains a project arrested in composition, not the finished 2013 book through which it appeared. ([Tolkien Estate, “The Fall of Arthur”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/christopher-tolkien-the-fall-of-arthur/); [chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **early 1930s:** *The New Lay of the Völsungs*. A completed alliterative retelling of the Sigurd legend, first published posthumously in 2009. ([Tolkien Estate, “The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/christopher-tolkien-the-legend-of-sigurd-and-gudrun/))

- **early 1930s:** *The New Lay of Gudrún*. A completed companion poem on Gudrún and the Niflungs, first published posthumously in 2009. ([Tolkien Estate, “The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/christopher-tolkien-the-legend-of-sigurd-and-gudrun/))

- **early 1930s:** *The Prophecy of the Sybil*. A short rhymed rendering inspired by *Völuspá*, published posthumously with the Sigurd and Gudrún poems. ([Tolkien Estate, “The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/christopher-tolkien-the-legend-of-sigurd-and-gudrun/))

- **early 1930s:** *Fragments of a heroic poem of Attila in Old English*. An incomplete poetic experiment surviving with Tolkien’s translation; the absence of a settled title and finished form is part of the object’s status. ([Tolkien Estate, “The Legend of Sigurd and Gudrún”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/christopher-tolkien-the-legend-of-sigurd-and-gudrun/))

- **1931:** *Progress in Bimble Town*. A satirical poem published under the name “K. Bagpuize.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1933:** *Errantry*. A formally intricate poem whose revision history eventually intersects with the Eärendil poem in *The Lord of the Rings*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1934:** *The Adventures of Tom Bombadil*. A narrative poem predating the character’s appearance in *The Lord of the Rings*; Tolkien revised it for the 1962 collection. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1934:** *Firiel*. A poem later revised as “The Last Ship.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1934:** *Looney*. A poem later transformed into “The Sea-Bell,” one of Tolkien’s darkest works. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1936:** *Songs for the Philologists*. A collaborative, privately printed booklet by Tolkien, E. V. Gordon and others; Tolkien contributed thirteen comic or philological songs and poems, including “Bagme Bloma,” “Éadig Béo þu!,” “Frenchmen Froth,” “From One to Five,” “I Sat upon a Bench,” “Ides Ælfscýne,” “La Húru,” “‘Lit’ and ‘Lang’,” “Natura Apis,” “Ofer Wídne Gársecg,” “The Root of the Boot,” “Ruddoc Hana” and “Syx Mynet.” Authorship of the volume as a whole is collective, and most copies were destroyed in a fire. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html); [Tolkien Estate timeline](https://www.tolkienestate.com/life/timeline-1892-1949/))

- **1936:** *Noel*. A devotional poem first published in a school annual. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1936:** *The Shadow Man*. A poem later revised as “Shadow-Bride.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1937:** *The Dragon’s Visit*. A comic poem published in *The Oxford Magazine* and substantially revised for republication in 1965. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1937:** *Knocking at the Door*. A comic poem published as “Oxymore,” later revised as “The Mewlips.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1931:** *Mythopoeia*. A philosophical poem defending myth-making; although circulated among the Inklings, it was published only after Tolkien’s death. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1955:** *Imram*. A narrative poem about Saint Brendan, developed from an earlier version embedded in *The Notion Club Papers*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1962:** *The Adventures of Tom Bombadil and Other Verses from the Red Book*. A deliberately framed collection of sixteen poems presented as translations from a fictional manuscript; it is both poetry book and extension of the documentary fiction of Middle-earth. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1965:** *Once Upon a Time*. A late fairy poem first published in *Winter’s Tales for Children*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1967:** *For W.H.A.*. An occasional poem for W. H. Auden made in paired Old English and modern-English forms. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1966; published 1974:** *Bilbo’s Last Song (At the Grey Havens)*. A valedictory poem first manifested commercially as a poster after Tolkien’s death; it was not part of *The Lord of the Rings*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

### Philology, criticism and editorial scholarship

- **1918:** *Prefatory note to A Spring Harvest*. Tolkien’s brief introduction to the posthumous poetry collection of his friend Geoffrey Bache Smith. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1922:** *A Middle English Vocabulary*. A scholarly glossary designed as a companion to Kenneth Sisam’s *Fourteenth Century Verse and Prose*. ([Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **1923:** *Henry Bradley: 3 Dec., 1845–23 May, 1923*. An obituary ending with an Old English memorial poem. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1923:** *Holy Maidenhood*. An unsigned review attributed to Tolkien; because it appeared unsigned, the bibliographical attribution must remain visible rather than being treated as self-evident. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1924 and 1926:** *Philology: General Works*. Two annual critical surveys for *The Year’s Work in English Studies*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1925:** *Sir Gawain and the Green Knight*. A scholarly edition made jointly with E. V. Gordon; later revision by Norman Davis is not Tolkien’s work alone. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1925:** *The Devil’s Coach-Horses*. A philological essay on an obscure Middle English expression. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1925:** *Some Contributions to Middle-English Lexicography*. A philological article correcting and supplementing dictionary evidence. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1928:** *Foreword to A New Glossary of the Dialect of the Huddersfield District*. A substantial prefatory essay on dialect study. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1929:** *Ancrene Wisse and Hali Meiðhad*. A textual and linguistic study of two Middle English religious works. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1930:** *The Oxford English School*. An essay on the organization and purpose of English studies at Oxford. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1931–1934:** *Chaucer as a Philologist*. A major lecture and essay on Chaucer’s dialectal and linguistic art; the published title differs from the lecture title, “Chaucer’s Use of Dialects.” ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1932:** *The Name “Nodens”*. A philological appendix commissioned for the archaeological report on Lydney Park. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1932 and 1934:** *Sigelwara Land*. A two-part etymological investigation whose conclusions remained exploratory rather than final. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1936; published 1937:** *Beowulf: The Monsters and the Critics*. A lecture revised into a foundational critical essay arguing that the poem’s monsters are central to its artistic meaning. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1933–1936; published 2002:** *Beowulf and the Critics*. The much longer lecture-text underlying “The Monsters and the Critics”; its surviving typescripts represent developmental states rather than a book Tolkien published. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1939–1947:** *On Fairy-Stories*. An Andrew Lang lecture expanded into an essay defining fairy-story, sub-creation, recovery, escape and consolation; Tolkien continued revising it for *Tree and Leaf*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1930s:** *A Secret Vice*. A lecture and associated notes on invented languages, illustrated by Tolkien’s own linguistic creations; posthumous editions reveal more than one textual state. ([Tolkien Estate, “Selected further reading”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/selected-further-reading/); [Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **1947:** *Iþþlen in Sawles Warde*. A philological article jointly made with Simonne d’Ardenne. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1948:** *MS Bodley 34: A Re-collation of a Collation*. A textual study jointly made with Simonne d’Ardenne. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1953:** *Form and Purpose*. Tolkien’s contribution to E. V. Gordon’s edition of *Pearl*, later incorporated into the introduction to Tolkien’s translation volume. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1953:** *A Fourteenth-Century Romance*. A short article written to accompany a radio performance of Tolkien’s *Sir Gawain* translation. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1953:** *The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth Beorhthelm’s Son*. A composite obra joining the essays “Beorhtnoth’s Death” and “Ofermod” to a verse dramatic dialogue; it deliberately crosses criticism, translation, drama and poetic invention. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1953:** *Middle English “Losenger”: Sketch of an Etymological and Semantic Enquiry*. A philological conference paper and published essay. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1959; published 1979:** *Valedictory Address to the University of Oxford*. Tolkien’s retrospective final university lecture, surviving in slightly different published forms. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1962:** *Ancrene Wisse: The English Text of the Ancrene Riwle*. Tolkien’s critical edition of the Corpus Christi College Cambridge manuscript, the result of decades of editorial work. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1963:** *English and Welsh*. An O’Donnell lecture on linguistic history, contact, naming and Tolkien’s personal response to Welsh. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1966:** *Tolkien on Tolkien*. A short autobiographical article. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1966–1967; published 1975:** *Nomenclature of The Lord of the Rings*. A protocol for translators, prescribing how names should be translated or retained; the initially published form, “Guide to the Names,” was revised editorially by Christopher Tolkien. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

### Translations and retellings

- **c. 1914–1915:** *The Story of Kullervo*. An unfinished prose-and-verse retelling of material from the *Kalevala*, accompanied by two versions of an essay on that work; its first complete scholarly publication came posthumously. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1920s–1940s; published 1975:** *Sir Gawain and the Green Knight*. A modern-English verse translation of the Middle English poem, made for oral as well as written performance. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1920s–1940s; published 1975:** *Pearl*. A modern-English verse translation preserving the original’s demanding stanzaic pattern. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **date uncertain; published 1975:** *Sir Orfeo*. A modern-English verse translation of the Middle English Breton lay. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1920s; published 1981:** *The Old English Exodus*. An edition, translation and commentary originating in Tolkien’s teaching; Joan Turville-Petre prepared the posthumous publication. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **lectures delivered principally in the 1920s–1930s; published 1982:** *Finn and Hengest*. Tolkien’s reconstruction and interpretation of the Finnsburg material, assembled posthumously by Alan Bliss from lecture notes. Its book form is editorial, while the underlying scholarly project is Tolkien’s. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1926:** *Beowulf*. A complete prose translation with commentary, made largely for teaching and published only in 2014; Tolkien did not prepare the surviving text as a finished book. ([Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **1950s; published 2014:** *Sellic Spell*. Tolkien’s modern-English reconstruction of a folktale underlying *Beowulf*, also rendered by him into Old English; it is creative reconstruction as much as scholarship. ([Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **1966:** *The Book of Jonah*. Tolkien’s translation for *The Jerusalem Bible*; it appeared anonymously and was editorially altered, while his manuscript version was published only in 2014. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

### Letters, reports and minor prose

- **1910–1911:** *King Edward’s School Chronicle reports and editorials*. Two 1910 debating-society reports, two further reports in 1911, the Latin “Acta Senatus,” and two editorials are unsigned and therefore bibliographically attributed rather than indisputably signed works. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1932:** *A Philologist on Esperanto*. Extracts from Tolkien’s letter to the British Esperanto Association; the manifestation is an editor’s selection from private correspondence. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1945:** *The Name Coventry*. A published letter explaining the place-name’s etymology. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1960:** *Letter concerning “No Monroe in Lothlorien!”*. A published response in *Triode*. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1971:** *Statement on books loved in adolescence*. An untitled paragraph contributed to *Attacks of Taste*; it resists treatment as an independently titled work. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1972:** *Beautiful Place because Trees Are Loved*. A published letter opposing destructive forestry; the title is editorial rather than necessarily Tolkien’s. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1910s–1973:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of private, professional and literary letters form a documentary corpus rather than a work Tolkien designed as a book. *The Letters of J.R.R. Tolkien* is a posthumous selection by Humphrey Carpenter and Christopher Tolkien, not the corpus itself. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html); [descriptive bibliography](https://www.tolkienbooks.us/hammond))

### Graphic works, maps and calligraphy

- **childhood–1973:** *Visual art*. A corpus of paintings, drawings, patterns, heraldic devices, decorated inscriptions, manuscript ornament and experimental calligraphy. It resists a closed work-list because many objects are untitled studies or components of manuscripts; Hammond and Scull’s catalogue and the Bodleian catalogues are the principal raisonnés. ([Tolkien Estate, “Studies on Tolkien”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/studies-on-tolkien/); [descriptive bibliography](https://www.tolkienbooks.us/hammond))

- **1910s–1920s:** *Visionary and mythological pictures*. Watercolours and drawings—including landscapes associated with the emerging mythology—whose relation to particular narratives is sometimes conjectural rather than documented. ([Tolkien: Maker of Middle-earth contents](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf03/z01_978-1-85124-497-3_01.pdf))

- **1920–1943:** *Father Christmas pictures, envelopes and invented postal matter*. Annual graphic creations inseparable from the letters they accompanied; together they form a domestic multimedia serial rather than merely illustrations to a later book. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **c. 1928–1932:** *Mr. Bliss illustrations and manuscript lettering*. The story’s pictures, layout and handwritten text constitute one integrated picture-book object—a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than separable prose and illustration. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1930s:** *The Hobbit maps and illustrations*. Maps, monochrome drawings, colour paintings, lettering and dust-jacket designs made as parts of the novel’s imagined documentary world; more than one hundred related images survive. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html); [Tolkien bibliography](https://tolkiengateway.net/wiki/Tolkien_bibliography))

- **1937–1955:** *Maps of Middle-earth for The Lord of the Rings*. Working and publication maps that actively governed composition—design instruments as well as representations of the fictional geography. ([Tolkien: Maker of Middle-earth contents](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf03/z01_978-1-85124-497-3_01.pdf))

- **1940s–1960s:** *The Lord of the Rings illustrations, jacket designs and calligraphy*. An unfinished publication-design project including proposed dust jackets, “facsimile” pages, inscriptions, seals and heraldic emblems; only part entered the first edition. ([Tolkien Estate, “Studies on Tolkien”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/writing/studies-on-tolkien/); [Tolkien: Maker of Middle-earth contents](https://toc.library.ethz.ch/objects/pdf03/z01_978-1-85124-497-3_01.pdf))

- **1967:** *Decorations for The Road Goes Ever On*. Calligraphic and emblematic designs made for the song-cycle volume. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1979:** *Pictures by J.R.R. Tolkien*. A posthumous curated manifestation of Tolkien’s graphic corpus, selected and annotated by Christopher Tolkien; it is not itself a collection designed by the artist. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

### Music, performance and recorded voice

- **1967:** *The Road Goes Ever On: A Song Cycle*. A collaborative work: Donald Swann composed the music, while Tolkien supplied poems, linguistic notes, translations, decorations and guidance, including direct involvement with the musical setting of “Namárië.” It is not solely Tolkien’s composition. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1952:** *Private tape recordings made with George Sayer*. Tolkien performed passages from the unpublished *Lord of the Rings*, “Riddles in the Dark,” songs and the Lord’s Prayer in Gothic; these are performances and sound manifestations, not new written works. ([Tolkien Estate, “Audio”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/es/audiovisual/audio/))

- **1953:** *The Homecoming of Beorhtnoth recording*. Tolkien recorded his verse drama, but it was not commercially issued; copies were later distributed privately at the 1992 Tolkien Centenary Conference. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1967:** *Poems and Songs of Middle Earth*. A commercial recording combining Tolkien’s readings of six poems with Donald Swann’s song settings performed by Swann and William Elvin; authorship and performance are collaborative. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1967:** *J.R.R. Tolkien Reads and Sings His The Hobbit and The Fellowship of the Ring*. A spoken and sung performance later issued by Caedmon; the record is a manifestation assembled from Tolkien’s readings, not a separately written text. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1967:** *J.R.R. Tolkien Reads and Sings His The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers/The Return of the King*. A companion recorded performance of selected prose, verse and songs. ([chronological bibliography](https://forodrim.org/bibliography/tbchron.html))

- **1968:** *Tolkien in Oxford*. A BBC television appearance filmed over five days: Tolkien performs, remembers and interprets his work on camera, but the documentary itself was made by producer Leslie Megahey and cannot be attributed to Tolkien as an authored film. ([Tolkien Estate, “Visual”](https://www.tolkienestate.com/audio-visual/visual/))
