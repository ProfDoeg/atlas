## Works by Ronald Linn Rivest

### Cryptographic systems, ciphers, and hash functions

- **1977–1978:** *RSA public-key cryptosystem*. A cryptographic protocol and mathematical construction made with Adi Shamir and Leonard Adleman; it supports public-key encryption and digital signatures. The foundational paper appeared in 1978, while the associated patent was filed in 1977 and issued in 1983, so “RSA” is simultaneously an algorithm, protocol, paper, and patented system. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [U.S. Patent 4,405,829](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/RSA83b.pdf))

- **1981:** *Mental Poker*. A cryptographic protocol made with Adi Shamir and Leonard Adleman for playing card games without a trusted dealer; it is a protocol rather than a physical game. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1986:** *RC2*. A proprietary symmetric block cipher designed for RSA Data Security. Its early history was partly confidential, making the algorithm’s creation distinct from its later public specification. ([Rivest FAQ](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/faq.html), [RFC 2268](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc2268))

- **1987:** *RC4*. A symmetric stream cipher originally kept as an RSA Security trade secret and later independently disclosed; Rivest confirms that “RC” originally meant “Ron’s Code.” The published descriptions are manifestations of a design whose original specification was not openly issued. ([Rivest FAQ](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/faq.html), [RFC 6229](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6229))

- **1988:** *MD2 Message-Digest Algorithm*. A cryptographic hash function, later specified as an Internet RFC; it is now obsolete for collision-resistant applications. ([RFC 1319](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1319), [Rivest FAQ](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/faq.html))

- **1990:** *MD4 Message-Digest Algorithm*. A cryptographic hash function and protocol specification whose RFC manifestation appeared in 1990; Rivest’s own bibliography treats that RFC as a work. It is now obsolete for collision-resistant applications. ([RFC 1186](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1186), [Rivest FAQ](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/faq.html))

- **1991–1992:** *MD5 Message-Digest Algorithm*. A cryptographic hash function, first presented in 1991 and formally specified in RFC 1321 in 1992. It resists classification as a single text because the algorithm, reference implementation, and RFC are distinct manifestations. ([RFC 1321](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc1321), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1994–1995:** *RC5*. A parameterized block cipher conceived as a family rather than one fixed algorithm; Rivest presented it in a workshop paper subsequently published in the proceedings. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [RC5 paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/Riv95.pdf))

- **1996:** *Time-lock Puzzles and Timed-release Crypto*. A cryptographic project and protocol made with Adi Shamir and David Wagner for requiring approximately sequential computation before information can be recovered. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1997:** *All-or-Nothing Encryption and the Package Transform*. A cryptographic transform that makes partial decryption of a package unhelpful; it is a construction or preprocessing mode, not itself an encryption cipher. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1997:** *Chaffing and Winnowing: Confidentiality without Encryption*. A protocol and conceptual demonstration for confidentiality through authentication and packet selection rather than conventional encryption. Its title names both a procedure and the paper describing it. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1997:** *S-boxes Incorporating Tree and Table Lookups*. A cryptographic component and patented design for data-dependent substitution operations, associated with Rivest’s work on block ciphers. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1998:** *RC6*. A block cipher made with Matt Robshaw, Ray Sidney, and Yiqun Lisa Yin as an AES competition candidate. It is only partly “Ron’s Code,” as Rivest explicitly notes. ([Rivest FAQ](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/faq.html), [RC6 submission](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/RRSY98.pdf))

- **2001:** *Ring Signatures*. A cryptographic signature construction made with Adi Shamir and Yael Tauman, permitting a signer to authenticate anonymously as one member of an ad hoc group. The conference paper was expanded into a 2006 chapter. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2005:** *Abelian Square-Free Dithering for Iterated Hash Functions*. A proposed hash-function strengthening technique accompanied by executable code; it is both a research text and an algorithmic artifact. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2013:** *Honeywords*. A password-security scheme made with Ari Juels in which decoy passwords make stolen password files detectably dangerous to use. It is a defensive construction and continuing project, not merely the paper *Honeywords: Making Password-Cracking Detectable*. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

### Algorithms and computational constructions

- **1972–1973:** *BFPRT median-of-medians selection algorithm*. A deterministic linear-time selection algorithm made with Manuel Blum, Robert Floyd, Vaughan Pratt, and Robert Tarjan. The 1972 conference paper was replaced by the fuller 1973 journal manifestation, so these are not two independent inventions. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1973–1975:** *Floyd–Rivest selection algorithm (SELECT)*. A randomized selection algorithm made with Robert W. Floyd; its analysis and its published “Algorithm 489” implementation are related but distinct textual manifestations of one computational construction. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1973:** *A Fast Stable Minimum-Storage Sorting Algorithm*. A sorting algorithm issued as a research report; it is an algorithmic object manifested through the report rather than a book. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1974–1976:** *Self-organizing sequential-search heuristics*. A family of adaptive list-search procedures studied first in a conference paper and then in a journal article that replaced it. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1974–1976:** *Partial-match retrieval algorithms*. A body of associative-retrieval algorithms developed through Rivest’s dissertation and subsequent papers; its boundaries resist treatment as one fixed algorithm. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1976:** *Simple union–find algorithm with linear expected time*. An algorithm and analysis made with Jon Doyle. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1977–1980:** *Binary search with unreliable comparisons*. A fault-tolerant search procedure made with Albert Meyer, Daniel Kleitman, Karl Winklmann, and Joel Spencer; the 1977 preliminary report was replaced by the 1980 journal work *Coping with Errors in Binary Search Procedures*. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1982–1983:** *Greedy channel router*. A VLSI-routing algorithm made with Charles Fiduccia, first presented at the Design Automation Conference and then published in *Computer-Aided Design*. Later reprinting does not constitute another work. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1987:** *Decision lists*. A machine-learning representation and learning method introduced in *Learning Decision Lists*. It is simultaneously a hypothesis class and an algorithmic project. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1987–1994:** *Diversity-based inference of finite automata*. A learning method made with Robert Schapire; the 1987 conference version was replaced by the expanded 1994 journal paper. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

### Computers, chips, and software systems

- **1979:** *BLIZZARD computer architecture*. A proposed computer architecture described in *ACM SIGARCH Computer Architecture News*. The surviving scholarly text documents a design; whether a complete physical machine was built is not established by the bibliography. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **circa 1980–1984:** *RSA chip*. A single-chip hardware implementation of RSA developed at MIT and documented across several reports and talks. It resists identification with any one publication: Rivest recalls that Lisp programs generated its masks, while the bibliography separately records descriptions of the chip. ([Rivest oral history](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/Riv16q.pdf), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1982:** *PI Placement and Interconnect System*. An advanced Lisp-based VLSI placement-and-routing system. At publication, its signal router was operational but the rest was still being coded, so this is explicitly an unfinished project as well as software. ([PI paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/Riv82a.pdf))

- **1996:** *SDSI—A Simple Distributed Security Infrastructure*. A distributed authorization and naming infrastructure made with Butler Lampson. Its paper, implementation ideas, and later SPKI/SDSI development form a project rather than one self-contained text. ([SDSI paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/RL96.ver-1.1.html), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1999–2019:** *LCS35 Time Capsule Crypto-Puzzle*. A time-lock puzzle created for MIT Laboratory for Computer Science’s thirty-fifth anniversary and solved twenty years later. It is a computational event-object—software, ciphertext, challenge, and delayed performance—not merely its written description. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

### Voting systems, ballots, and election-audit methods

- **2006:** *ThreeBallot Voting System*. A paper-ballot design intended chiefly as a pedagogical demonstration of end-to-end verifiability without cryptography, not as a practical deployment proposal. That deliberate impracticality is part of the project’s status. ([ThreeBallot paper](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/pubs/Riv06c.pdf), [Software Independence Revisited](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/RV16.pdf))

- **2006–2007:** *VAV voting system*. A paper-based voting protocol developed with Warren Smith as a variation intended to address weaknesses in ThreeBallot. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2007:** *Scratch & Vote*. An end-to-end verifiable optical-scan voting system made with Ben Adida; it combines printed ballots, cryptographic commitments, and audit procedures. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2007–2008:** *Twin*. A voting-system proposal related to ThreeBallot that uses paired ballots; it remained primarily a research design rather than a generally deployed election system. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2010–present:** *Bayesian post-election audits*. A continuing body of methods, explanations, demonstrations, and software for using Bayesian inference to audit election outcomes. It resists being called a single work because Rivest’s bibliography records multiple evolving papers and implementations. ([Bayes Post-Election Audit project](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/bayes/), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2015:** *DiffSum*. A simple risk-limiting post-election audit method, manifested as an unpublished paper and computational procedure. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00127))

- **2017:** *ClipAudit*. A risk-limiting ballot-polling audit method designed to be comparatively simple to administer and explain. ([ClipAudit paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.08312), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2018:** *Bayesian Tabulation Audits*. An election-audit method and explanatory paper extending Bayesian auditing from ballot polling to tabulation structures. ([arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.00528), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2019:** *Voting and Auditing with Ternary Plurality Trees*. An experimental election-tabulation and audit construction manifested as a Jupyter notebook and source repository; the executable notebook is part of the work. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2020:** *Double-Bubble Ballots*. An election-ballot design circulated as a short research document rather than a deployed system. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2022–2024:** *Scan, Shuffle, Rescan*. A machine-assisted/two-prover election-audit protocol made with Douglas Jones, Sunoo Park, and Adam Sealfon for obtaining trustworthy evidence from untrusted scanners. The 2022 e-print and 2024 conference version are successive manifestations, not separate projects. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [IACR ePrint 2022/960](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/960))

### Public-health and biological-security protocols

- **2020–2023:** *PACT—Private Automated Contact Tracing*. A multi-institutional protocol and research project for privacy-preserving exposure notification, co-created by Rivest and numerous collaborators. It includes a protocol specification, technical reports, talks, and implementations and therefore behaves more like a Gesamtkunstwerk of specification, software, policy, and public-health practice than a single text. ([PACT specification](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [PACT project](https://pact.mit.edu/))

- **2020:** *SonicPACT*. An ultrasonic-ranging component for PACT made with John Meklenburg, Michael Specter, Michael Wentz, Hari Balakrishnan, and others; it is both a measurement method and a technical report. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2019–present:** *SecureDNA cryptographic screening methods*. A collaborative technical project for privately screening DNA-synthesis orders, combining cryptographic protocols, threat models, software, and biological-safety infrastructure. Authorship is highly collective, so it should not be attributed to Rivest alone. ([SecureDNA](https://securedna.org/), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

### Books and edited works

- **1989:** *Proceedings of the Second Annual Workshop on Computational Learning Theory (COLT ’89)*. An edited conference volume made with David Haussler and Manfred Warmuth; Rivest was an editor rather than sole author. ([DBLP bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/r/RonaldLRivest.html), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1990–2022:** *Introduction to Algorithms*. A textbook made with Thomas Cormen and Charles Leiserson, with Clifford Stein joining from the second edition onward. The editions of 1990, 2001, 2009, and 2022 are substantial successive manifestations of one continuing obra rather than four unrelated books. ([MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262046305/introduction-to-algorithms/), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1994:** *Computational Learning Theory and Natural Learning Systems, Volume I: Constraints and Prospects*. An edited scholarly volume made with Stephen José Hanson and George Drastal; Rivest’s role was editorial and collaborative. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [MIT Press author catalogue](https://mitpress.mit.edu/author/ronald-l-rivest-5904/))

- **1994:** *Computational Learning Theory and Natural Learning Systems, Volume II: Intersections between Theory and Experiment*. An edited scholarly volume made with Stephen José Hanson, Thomas Petsche, and Michael Kearns. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html), [MIT Press author catalogue](https://mitpress.mit.edu/author/ronald-l-rivest-5904/))

- **1996:** *Research Directions in Computer Science: An MIT Perspective*. An edited collective volume associated with Rivest and fellow MIT computer scientists; it is an editorial construction rather than a sole-authored monograph. ([MIT Press author catalogue](https://mitpress.mit.edu/author/ronald-l-rivest-5904/), [Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

### Patented inventions

- **1977–1983:** *Cryptographic Communications System and Method*. The patented legal manifestation of RSA, made with Adi Shamir and Leonard Adleman as U.S. Patent 4,405,829. It is not a separate cryptosystem from RSA, but the patent is itself a consequential protocol-like legal text. ([U.S. Patent 4,405,829](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/RSA83b.pdf))

- **1987:** *Method and Apparatus for Reusing Non-Erasable Memory Media*. A patented write-once-memory construction made with Adi Shamir, U.S. Patent 4,691,299. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1990:** *Cryptographic Identification, Financial Transaction, and Credential Device*. A patented cryptographic apparatus made with Russell Pogue, U.S. Patent 5,144,667. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **1998:** *Block Encryption Algorithm with Data-Dependent Rotations*. Rivest’s patented block-cipher construction, U.S. Patent 5,835,600, associated with the RC5 design family. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2005:** *Method and Apparatus for Selective Blocking of Radio Frequency Identification Devices*. A patented RFID privacy mechanism made with Ari Juels and Michael Szydlo, U.S. Patent 6,970,070. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2006:** *System and Method for Authentication Seed Distribution*. A patented authentication construction made with John Brainard, Burton Kaliski, and Magnus Nyström, U.S. Patent 6,985,583. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2013:** *Remote Verification of File Protections for Cloud Data Storage*. A patented cloud-integrity mechanism made with Ari Juels, Marten van Dijk, Alina Oprea, and Emil Stefanov, U.S. Patent 8,346,742. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2013:** *User Authentication Based on Voucher Codes*. A patented authentication method made with John Brainard, Ari Juels, and Michael Szydlo, U.S. Patent 8,438,617. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2014:** *Key Update with Compromise Detection*. A patented key-management mechanism made with Ari Juels, U.S. Patent 8,699,713. ([U.S. Patent 8,699,713](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/RJ14.pdf))

- **2015:** *System and Method Providing Disconnected Authentication*. A collectively invented authentication system, including Rivest among its inventors, issued as U.S. Patent 8,966,276. ([U.S. Patent 8,966,276](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs/NABDx15.pdf))

- **2015:** *Method and System for Micropayment Transactions*. A patented micropayment construction made with Silvio Micali, U.S. Patent 8,983,874. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2016:** *Configurable One-Time Authentication Tokens with Improved Resilience to Attacks*. A patented authentication-token mechanism made with Ari Juels, Nikolaos Triandopoulos, Marten van Dijk, John Brainard, and Kevin Bowers, U.S. Patent 9,270,655. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

- **2020:** *Methods and Apparatus for Private Set Membership Using Aggregation for Reducing Communications*. A patented privacy-preserving set-membership construction made with Nikolaos Triandopoulos, Kevin Bowers, James Kelley, and Alina Oprea, U.S. Patent 10,635,824. ([Rivest bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))

### Textual and scholarly corpus

- **1972–2025:** *Publications, Talks, Drafts, Patents, and Other Miscellania*. Rivest’s self-maintained bibliography records 433 numbered manifestations as of its September 2025 generation date, ranging from formal papers and books to slides, testimony, code, interviews, unpublished drafts, patents, and political letters. This corpus resists being called one “work,” and the compiler calls it only “fairly complete”; preliminary papers, replacements, reprints, talks with repeated titles, and works signed as a group prevent a defensible claim that every record is a separate obra or that the list is a final catalogue raisonné. ([Rivest’s official bibliography](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rivest/pubs.html))
