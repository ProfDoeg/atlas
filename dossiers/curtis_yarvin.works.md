## Works by Curtis Guy Yarvin

### Software Gesamtkunstwerk

- **2002–present:** *Urbit*. A clean-slate personal-computing and peer-to-peer-network project begun by Yarvin as a solitary protocol experiment; it combines languages, an operating system, runtime, identity system, network, and social architecture, so it is better understood as a technological Gesamtkunstwerk than as one program. Yarvin worked alone until 2013, after which Urbit became a collaborative Tlon project; consequently, later components and revisions cannot be attributed to him alone. ([Urbit history](https://urbitguide.com/urbit-history/), [Yarvin’s “A Founder’s Farewell”](https://urbit.org/blog/a-founders-farewell))
- **2008:** *Nock*. The minimal functional virtual machine and frozen computational specification at the bottom of Urbit’s stack. It is simultaneously executable code, a language, and a protocol; the initial design is Yarvin’s, while implementations have subsequently been collective. ([Urbit history](https://urbitguide.com/urbit-history/), [Urbit white paper](https://media.urbit.org/whitepaper.pdf))
- **2012:** *Hoon*. Urbit’s high-level, self-compiling functional programming language, designed to compile into Nock. Yarvin created its original architecture and implementation, but Hoon has continued to change under other Urbit developers. ([Urbit history](https://urbitguide.com/urbit-history/), [Urbit documentation](https://docs.urbit.org/hoon/why-hoon))
- **2013:** *Arvo*. The event-driven operating-system kernel written in Hoon and executed as Nock. The first running system belongs to Yarvin’s original Urbit project, but modern Arvo—including its evolving vanes—resists attribution as a wholly individual work. ([Urbit history](https://urbitguide.com/urbit-history/), [Arvo documentation](https://docs.urbit.org/urbit-os/kernel/arvo))
- **2013:** *Urbit demo ~2013.9.23*. A command-line demonstration of the first real-time Urbit network and chat system; it is a recorded software manifestation rather than a separately maintained program. ([Urbit history](https://urbitguide.com/urbit-history/))
- **2016:** *The Urbit Address Space*. A designed hierarchy of planets, stars, and 256 galaxies that functions at once as namespace, identity protocol, routing structure, and property scheme. Yarvin created and initially allocated it, but its later technical implementation and governance are collective. ([Urbit address-space account](https://urbit.org/blog/the-urbit-address-space))
- **2016:** *Urbit: A Solid-State Interpreter*. A collaborative technical white paper by Yarvin, Philip Monk, Anton Dyudin, and Raymond Pasco, specifying Urbit’s virtual machine, language, operating system, and network architecture. It is documentation and protocol description, not sole-authored software. ([White paper](https://media.urbit.org/whitepaper.pdf))

### Earlier software invention

- **2001:** *Method and Apparatus for Dynamically Updating a Markup Language Based User Interface*. A patented software method that updates only invalidated portions of a markup-based interface; Yarvin is named as inventor and Openwave Systems as applicant/assignee. The patent application is a legal-technological protocol, not evidence that Yarvin alone made every implementation covered by its claims. ([US patent record](https://patents.justia.com/inventor/curtis-g-yarvin), [European application](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/9e/e7/ad/fc8d63fb1fcd9e/EP1221661A3.pdf))

### Serial textual obras

- **2007–2014:** *Unqualified Reservations*. The blog published under the name Mencius Moldbug: hundreds of essays, dialogues, reading notes, polemics, and serialized treatises intended collectively to construct a new reactionary political vocabulary. It is best treated as a serial obra or body of work; its archive’s later descriptive titles and ebook arrangements are editorial manifestations rather than additional compositions. ([Complete chronological and topical archive](https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/))
- **2020–present:** *Gray Mirror of the Nihilist Prince*. An ongoing subscription newsletter and projected book written as Curtis Yarvin, combining political theory, commentary, memoir, fiction, verse, and book-in-progress installments. Its borders remain unstable: some posts are autonomous essays, others drafts or previews, and the announced book is being issued separately in fascicles. ([Gray Mirror](https://graymirror.substack.com/), [publication plan](https://graymirror.substack.com/p/administrivia-0a5))

### Manifestos and political protocols

- **2007:** *A Formalist Manifesto*. An essay that names and advocates “formalism,” proposing that political power and property be made explicit. Because it attempts to found and recruit adherents to a position, it is a manifesto in the active rather than merely descriptive sense. ([Unqualified Reservations archive](https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/))
- **2008:** *Patchwork: A Political System for the 21st Century*. A serialized proposal for replacing territorial nation-states with numerous sovereign, privately governed jurisdictions. It is both speculative political design and protocol: it tells its imagined successor institutions how sovereignty should be organized. ([Collected-work description](https://passage.press/products/ur2), [Unqualified Reservations archive](https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/))
- **2008:** *An Open Letter to Open-Minded Progressives*. A thirteen-part epistolary treatise designed to move a presumed progressive reader through Yarvin’s revisionist account of history, government, and political restoration. The later PDF and print appearances collect a pre-existing web serial rather than constitute new works. ([Collected text and contents](https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/pdfs/open_letter.pdf))
- **2009:** *A Gentle Introduction to Unqualified Reservations*. A multipart introductory treatise that recasts the blog’s arguments as a guided course in Yarvin’s political system. It occupies an unstable category between primer, polemic, and conversion protocol. ([Unqualified Reservations archive](https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/), [collected-work description](https://passage.press/products/ur2))
- **2009–2010:** *Moldbug on Carlyle*. A multipart commentary and political reconstruction built around Thomas Carlyle. It is neither simply an edition of Carlyle nor a conventional monograph: extensive quotation and commentary are made to serve Yarvin’s own anti-egalitarian project. ([Unqualified Reservations archive](https://www.unqualified-reservations.org/), [collected-work description](https://passage.press/products/ur2))
- **2019:** *The Clear Pill*. A five-part political essay sequence presenting Yarvin’s theory of pervasive institutional error and regime change. Its numbered serial form makes it one composite work rather than five unrelated essays. ([Scholarly citation to the sequence](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/ws/files/309039011/0263276421999439.pdf))

### Books and collected manifestations

- **2023:** *Unqualified Reservations: Volume I*. A print selection from the earlier *Unqualified Reservations* corpus. It is a new editorial manifestation and material book, but not an entirely new textual obra; its contents were composed for the blog. ([Passage Publishing’s Yarvin catalogue](https://passage.press/collections/curtis-yarvin))
- **2024:** *Gray Mirror: Fascicle I: Disturbance*. The first of four announced novella-length fascicles intended eventually to compose the book *Gray Mirror*. The publisher describes it as Yarvin’s first work original to print; the larger project remains unfinished, so this is both a completed book-object and one quarter of a projected obra. ([Yarvin’s announcement](https://graymirror.substack.com/p/administrivia-0a5), [publisher catalogue](https://passage.press/collections/curtis-yarvin))
- **No publication date stated:** *Unqualified Reservations: Volume 2*. A print collection comprising *A Gentle Introduction to Unqualified Reservations*, *Patchwork*, and *Moldbug on Carlyle*. It is listed by the publisher as a preorder; because it recombines existing serial texts, it is a collected manifestation rather than three newly written works. ([Publisher’s contents and status](https://passage.press/products/ur2))

### Periodical essay

- **1995:** *Scripting Languages*. A short illustrated technology essay published on Wired’s “Geek Page,” arguing for programming languages accessible to non-specialists. It is an early named publication under Yarvin’s own name, predating both Urbit and Mencius Moldbug. ([Wired archive](https://www.wired.com/1995/09/geek-32/))
