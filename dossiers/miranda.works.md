## Works by Francisco de Miranda

### Self-archive: a documentary Gesamtkunstwerk

- **1771–1810:** *Colombeia*. Miranda’s deliberately assembled personal archive—diaries, correspondence, political projects, military papers, printed matter, maps and material written by others—survives as sixty-three bound volumes. It is simultaneously an obra, an autobiographical construction and a repository, so its contents cannot all be attributed to Miranda as author; the standard editions are the twenty-four-volume *Archivo del General Miranda* and the later, still incomplete, critical edition *Colombeia*. [UNESCO’s archival description](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mtd001/colombeia-generalissimo-francisco-de-miranda-s-archives-nomination-form.pdf), [Biblioteca Ayacucho’s collected selection](https://biblioteca.clacso.edu.ar/clacso/se/20191120054114/America_espera.pdf)

### Journals and travel records

- **1771:** *Diario de navegación del puerto de La Guaira al de Cádiz*. A shipboard log and observational journal of Miranda’s thirty-four-day Atlantic crossing, generally treated as his earliest surviving personal text and the beginning of the travel sequence incorporated into *Colombeia*. [Francisco de Miranda documentary edition](https://www.franciscodemiranda.info/es/documentos/laguaira-cadiz.htm)

- **1783–1784:** *Diario de viaje por los Estados Unidos de América*. A continuous travel journal—not a retrospectively composed travel book—recording Miranda’s North American itinerary, encounters and reading; its familiar title belongs to modern editions of the manuscript sequence in *Colombeia*. [Robertson’s scholarly edition, identified in his bibliography](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/People/Francisco_de_Miranda/ROBMIR/Bibliography%2A.html)

- **1785–1789:** *Diarios de viaje por Europa, el Imperio otomano y Rusia*. A many-part manuscript itinerary through Britain, the German states, Italy, Greece, the Ottoman lands, Russia, Scandinavia, the Low Countries, Switzerland and France. This is a serial practice of observation dispersed through the “Viajes” volumes of *Colombeia*, not one book with a stable authorial title. [UNESCO’s description of the archive](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mtd001/colombeia-generalissimo-francisco-de-miranda-s-archives-nomination-form.pdf), [Biblioteca Ayacucho’s collected selection](https://biblioteca.clacso.edu.ar/clacso/se/20191120054114/America_espera.pdf)

### Revolutionary defenses and political manifestos

- **1793:** *Correspondance du général Miranda avec le général Dumouriez, les ministres de la Guerre Pache et Beurnonville*. A printed documentary defense constructed from Miranda’s military correspondence concerning the French campaign; because it republishes exchanges with other officials, it is an authored compilation rather than an exclusively authored text. [Robertson bibliography](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/People/Francisco_de_Miranda/ROBMIR/Bibliography%2A.html)

- **1793:** *Miranda à ses concitoyens: discours que je me proposais de prononcer à la Convention nationale, le 29 mars dernier*. A speech prepared for, but apparently not delivered to, the National Convention and then made operative as a printed public defense. The publication is also catalogued by its opening description, *Discours que le général Miranda se proposait de prononcer…*. [Robertson bibliography](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/People/Francisco_de_Miranda/ROBMIR/Bibliography%2A.html)

- **1793:** *Lettre de Miranda au président de la Convention nationale, Paris, le 4 avril 1793*. A separately printed political and juridical intervention arising from Miranda’s prosecution in France. It is a letter made into a public act, and thus closer to a manifesto than to private correspondence. [Robertson bibliography](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/People/Francisco_de_Miranda/ROBMIR/Bibliography%2A.html)

- **1795:** *Le général Miranda à la Représentation nationale*. A pamphlet assembling Miranda’s addresses of January 1795 and a letter to the National Convention in defense of his liberty and political standing. Bibliographies sometimes treat it as a version or continuation of his *Opinion*, so the boundary between the two pamphlets is bibliographically unstable. [Bibliothèque nationale-derived publication list](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Miranda), [digitized 1795 pamphlet](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3AFrancisco_de_Miranda_-_Le_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9ral_Miranda_%C3%A0_la_Repr%C3%A9sentation_nationale%2C_1795.djvu/3)

- **1795:** *Opinion du général Miranda sur la situation actuelle de la France, et sur les remèdes convenables à ses maux*. A twenty-three-page diagnosis and constitutional intervention in post-Terror France: an “opinion” in title, but functionally a political manifesto proposing remedies. [Contextual documentary edition](https://www.franciscodemiranda.info/es/documentos/opinionrevolucion.htm), [Springer’s analytical anthology](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-96938-6)

### Continental and constitutional projects

- **1790:** *Projet de Constitution pour les colonies hispano-américaines*. Miranda’s first known governmental design for an emancipated Spanish America, presented in connection with his approaches to William Pitt. The surviving status is problematic: scholarship reports the text through a later copy and notes a closely related or identically titled 1798 project, so the two should not be silently collapsed. [Academy of Political and Social Sciences study](https://www.acienpol.org.ve/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/BOLETIN-161-2020.pdf), [CLACSO anthology](https://biblioteca.clacso.edu.ar/clacso/se/20190905093410/Pensamiento_politico_de_la_emacipacion_1.pdf)

- **1797:** *Acta de París*. A jointly signed revolutionary compact by Miranda, José del Pozo y Sucre and Manuel José de Salas, with Duperon as secretary, establishing principles and diplomatic authority for Spanish-American emancipation. It resists classification as Miranda’s sole work: it is a collective protocol and political project to which he was a principal maker and signatory. [Biblioteca Ayacucho, *América espera*](https://biblioteca.clacso.edu.ar/clacso/se/20191120054114/America_espera.pdf), [Springer’s analytical anthology](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-96938-6)

- **1798:** *Projet de Constitution pour les colonies hispano-américaines*. A continental constitutional project combining a federal legislature with an executive headed by two Incas and provincial Curacas. Its relationship to the reported 1790 text is disputed in the scholarship, while the date 1798 is securely used for the developed surviving version. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html), [Academy of Political and Social Sciences study](https://www.acienpol.org.ve/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/BOLETIN-161-2020.pdf)

- **1801:** *Esquisse de gouvernement provisoire*. A protocol for transitional government during liberation, designed to operate only through the war and until a federal constitution could be established. Later Spanish editions call it *Bosquejo* or *Proyecto de Gobierno Provisional*, and some have confused the 1801 text with Miranda’s revisions of 1808. [Study of the textual problem](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/yuridicheskie-istochniki-konstitutsionnyh-proektov-fransisko-de-mirandy), [CLACSO anthology](https://biblioteca.clacso.edu.ar/clacso/se/20190905093410/Pensamiento_politico_de_la_emacipacion_1.pdf)

- **1801:** *Esquisse de gouvernement fédéral*. A projected permanent constitution for the emancipated continent, revising the institutional design of 1798. It is commonly translated as *Bosquejo* or *Proyecto de Gobierno Federal*; the surviving versions and later 1808 redrafting have sometimes been misdated or conflated. [Study of the textual problem](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/yuridicheskie-istochniki-konstitutsionnyh-proektov-fransisko-de-mirandy), [Springer’s analytical anthology](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-96938-6)

- **1808:** *Revised sketches of provisional and federal government*. Miranda revised the 1801 constitutional pair in response to the Spanish imperial crisis, notably allowing an emergency dictatorship for a limited period. These revisions have often circulated under the titles and date of the 1801 texts, making them distinct projects without securely distinct conventional titles. [Study of the textual problem](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/yuridicheskie-istochniki-konstitutsionnyh-proektov-fransisko-de-mirandy), [constitutional-history study](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/libro/745467.pdf)

- **1783–1811:** *Colombia, or the “Colombian Continent”*. Miranda’s long-developed project for treating Spanish America as one emancipated continental polity. This is not a single text or completed state but an intention manifested across constitutional drafts, negotiations, nomenclature, propaganda, flags and an expedition—an obra precisely because it exceeds any one medium. [Springer’s analytical anthology](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-96938-6), [study of Miranda’s London projects](https://www.scielo.sa.cr/scielo.php?lng=es&nrm=is&pid=S1409-469X2013000200004&script=sci_arttext)

### Printed apparatus of the 1806 expedition

- **10 January–2 August 1806:** *Proclamación de Don Francisco de Miranda, Comandante-General del Ejército Colombiano, a los pueblos habitantes del continente Américo-Colombiano*. A manifesto written in New York, printed aboard the expedition through Jacmel, and activated at Coro by manuscript additions of place and date. Earlier bibliographers suspected two proclamations, but comparison of manuscript and surviving print indicates one adaptable object used in both invasion attempts. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study and census](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html), [Cervantes foundational-document portal](https://cervantesvirtual.com/portales/portal_nacional_venezuela/documentos_fundacionales/)

- **1806:** *Esqueletos de despachos*. Printed blank commissions or appointment forms for officers of the Colombian Army, completed for particular recipients and signed by Miranda and his secretary. These are protocols that produced offices rather than texts meant principally to be read; surviving examples confirm their printing, although no single fixed title belongs to the series. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

- **24 March 1806:** *Fórmula de juramento*. A protocol binding members of the expedition to its political and military purpose. Its text is known through testimony and an English translation, but whether Miranda’s shipboard press actually printed it remains uncertain. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

- **1806:** *Proclama de recompensa*. A manifesto promising rewards in connection with the expedition. The historical evidence makes its printing probable rather than certain, and its descriptive modern title should not be mistaken for a securely established original title. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

- **August 1806:** *Amigos y compatriotas*. An address associated with Miranda’s second landing attempt and apparently produced by the expeditionary press. It functions as spoken appeal, handbill and manifesto at once. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

- **7 August 1806:** *Deseoso aún de dar pruebas de moderación y afecto…*. A proclamation from Coro announcing Miranda’s withdrawal and addressing the population that had fled the city. The manuscript survives; its contemporary printing is considered highly probable, not conclusively demonstrated. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

- **19 August 1806:** *Habitantes de Aruba*. A proclamation addressed to Aruba after the Coro operation, extending Miranda’s expeditionary politics beyond a single Venezuelan landing. It belongs to the group attributed to the press aboard the *Leander*. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

### Publishing and editorial projects

- **1801:** *Carta dirigida a los españoles americanos*. Miranda did not write Juan Pablo Viscardo y Guzmán’s letter, so it resists inclusion as an authored work; what Miranda made was its Spanish-language publication and political circulation, issued in London with a fictitious Philadelphia imprint and later distributed with his 1806 proclamation. It is therefore an editorial manifestation of his independence project, not his text. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes study](https://www.cervantesvirtual.com/obra-visor/escritos-selectos--0/html/ff713986-82b1-11df-acc7-002185ce6064_18.html)

- **15 March–May 1810:** *El Colombiano*. A five-number London periodical conceived, financed and directed by Miranda for circulation in Spanish America, but principally edited by José María Antepara with other collaborators. It is a collective publishing project rather than a wholly Miranda-authored work; contemporary Spanish officials likewise described the authorship as collaborative. [Scholarly study of Miranda’s London activity](https://www.scielo.sa.cr/scielo.php?lng=es&nrm=is&pid=S1409-469X2013000200004&script=sci_arttext), [Spanish archival catalogue](https://pares.cultura.gob.es/BicentenarioIndependencias/catalogo/description/6171977)

### Flags and political images

- **1806:** *Colombian tricolour flown by the Leander expedition*. A yellow-blue-red political emblem first raised during Miranda’s expedition and subsequently foundational for the flags of Venezuela, Colombia and Ecuador. Miranda’s responsibility for the tricolour is well established, but descriptions of the precise order of its colors in 1806 conflict, and later anecdotes about Goethe as its inspiration are not secure evidence. [Academy of History discussion of the conflicting evidence](https://es.scribd.com/document/47794836/BOLETIN-179academia-nacional-de-historia-ecuador), [Robertson’s history of the 1811 adoption](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/People/Francisco_de_Miranda/ROBMIR/19%2A.html)

- **1806:** *Bandera de Miranda para su proyectado Ejército con el nombre de Colombiano*. A black-red-yellow military flag design associating its bands with the racialized categories “negros,” “pardos” and “indios.” The surviving drawing is preserved through Spanish records of the expedition; whether Miranda personally drew the archival sheet, as distinct from devising the emblem it records, is not established. [Description of the Archivo General de Indias drawing](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandera_de_Venezuela)

- **1811:** *Flag and cockade of independent Venezuela*. Miranda served with two other deputies on the congressional committee that selected these national emblems, which adapted his earlier tricolour and added an allegorical Indigenous figure. This is a collective design and state manifestation, not a solely authored Miranda work. [Robertson’s account](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/People/Francisco_de_Miranda/ROBMIR/19%2A.html)

### Oratory, correspondence and official writing

- **1771–1812:** *Correspondence*. Thousands of letters, memorials, petitions, diplomatic notes and military orders survive across *Colombeia*; many are individually datable but were not conceived as separately titled works. Treating each archival item as a named obra would falsely convert an open documentary practice—including incoming letters and secretarial copies—into a bibliography of books. [UNESCO’s archival description](https://media.unesco.org/sites/default/files/webform/mtd001/colombeia-generalissimo-francisco-de-miranda-s-archives-nomination-form.pdf), [Springer’s analytical selection of Miranda’s letters](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-96938-6)

- **1811:** *Interventions in the Constituent Congress of Venezuela*. Speeches and motions delivered as a deputy, especially in the debates over independence and constitutional government. They survive through congressional records rather than as one authorially titled work, and the record may preserve summaries rather than Miranda’s exact spoken wording. [Springer’s analytical anthology](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-031-96938-6)

- **1812:** *Capitulación de San Mateo*. A military-political agreement negotiated with Domingo de Monteverde to terminate resistance by the First Venezuelan Republic. It is a collectively constituted legal act, not solely Miranda’s composition, and its guarantees, interpretation and alleged violation became matters of immediate dispute. [Francisco de Miranda documentary collection](https://www.franciscodemiranda.info/es/documentos/index.htm)

- **1813:** *Memorial dirigido a la Audiencia de Caracas*. Miranda’s prison defense of his conduct as generalissimo and of the 1812 capitulation, addressed to Spanish justice after months of confinement. It is at once a legal petition, political apologia and unfinished attempt to act from captivity. [Biblioteca Virtual Miguel de Cervantes, *Escritos*](https://cervantesvirtual.com/portales/francisco_de_miranda/obra-visor/escritos--1/html/), [Francisco de Miranda documentary collection](https://www.franciscodemiranda.info/es/documentos/index.htm)
