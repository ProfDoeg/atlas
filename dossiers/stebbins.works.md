## Works by Geneviève Stebbins

### Systems and pedagogical projects

- **c. 1880s–1907:** *Harmonic Gymnastics*. An evolving movement-and-breathing system combining American Delsartism, Swedish gymnastics, aesthetic dance, voice culture, and Stebbins’s own exercises and choreography. It is a pedagogical project and embodied practice rather than a single text; its changing printed manifestations do not exhaust it. [Mullan 2017](https://doi.org/10.1386/jdsp.9.2.159_1); [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1893–1907:** *New York School of Expression*. A school and institutional Gesamtkunstwerk founded by Stebbins at Carnegie Music Hall to unite movement, voice, oratory, dramatic expression, statue posing, and aesthetic dance; later administrators and teachers participated in its making, so it is not solely an authored “work.” [Lynch 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/24692921.2022.2144176); [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

### Books, manuals, and protocols

- **1885–1902:** *Delsarte System of Expression*; initially *Delsarte System of Dramatic Expression*. A philosophical treatise and practical protocol of lessons in gesture, bearing, pantomime, voice, and aesthetic gymnastics, repeatedly enlarged across editions dated 1885, 1887, 1889, 1891, 1894, and 1902. Its attribution is layered: Stebbins systematized material associated with François Delsarte and Steele MacKaye while adding translations, commentary, exercises, and original applications. [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327); [Acting Archives](https://www.actingarchives.it/catalogo/consultazione/scheda/delsarte-system-of-dramatic-expression-by-genevieve-stebbins-original-illustrations.html)

- **1888–1891:** *Society Gymnastics and Voice Culture: Adapted from the Delsarte System*. A practical exercise and voice-culture manual issued in successively revised editions; “adapted” accurately signals that it is Stebbins’s arrangement and development of inherited Delsartean material, not an entirely autonomous invention. [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1892–1893:** *Dynamic Breathing and Harmonic Gymnastics: A Complete System of Psychical, Aesthetic and Physical Culture*. A protocol joining breath training, bodily exercise, mental discipline, and expressive movement; sources give 1892 for the first publication and 1893 for a subsequent manifestation. [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327); [Kraler 2022](https://rw-ktf.univie.ac.at/fileadmin/user_upload/i_religionswiss/Yoga_Breath._The_Reinvention_of_Prana_and_Pranayama_in_Early_Modern_Yoga__Magdalena_Kraler_2022_.pdf)

- **1893:** *The New York School of Expression*. A thirty-one-page prospectus for Stebbins’s school: simultaneously institutional manifesto, curriculum, advertisement, and documentary manifestation of the larger school project. [Library of Congress-derived record](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1499368W/The_New_York_School_of_Expression); [Lynch 2022](https://doi.org/10.1080/24692921.2022.2144176)

- **1895:** *Genevieve Stebbins’s Drills*. A collection of choreographed exercise protocols, including theatrical movement sequences that hover between gymnastics, dance, pantomime, and ritual enactment. [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1895:** *Appendix to Society Gymnastics*. A separately issued supplement containing the *Eastern Temple Drill*, *Energizing Dramatic Drill*, *Minuet Fan-Drill*, and *Æsthetic Drill*; it is a protocol collection rather than a conventional discursive book. [Stebbins bibliography summarized in the biographical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genevieve_Stebbins#Selected_works)

- **1898–1913:** *The Genevieve Stebbins System of Physical Training*. An enlarged synthesis of basic exercises, progressive series, breathing practices, and elaborate drills, first published in 1898 and enlarged in 1913. It is both a book and the textual manifestation of a bodily curriculum. [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327); [1913 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1913:** *Delsartian Aesthetic Drills*. A separately titled instructional publication of movement sequences; available bibliographies do not establish whether its contents are wholly new or repackaged from Stebbins’s earlier drill collections, so its independence as an obra remains uncertain. [Stebbins bibliography summarized in the biographical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genevieve_Stebbins#Selected_works)

### Individual movement drills

- **1895:** *Eastern Temple Drill*. A choreographed protocol modeled, through a nineteenth-century Orientalist lens, on religious ceremonials attributed to several Islamic and Middle Eastern communities; it resists separation into “exercise” and “dance.” [Foxen’s account of the 1898 system](https://de.scribd.com/document/917443984/Inhaling-Spirit-Harmonialism-And-the-Western-Roots-of-Modern-Yoga-Anya-P-Foxen-Z-Library); [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *English Drill—The May*. A music-accompanied movement sequence drawing on an imagined English seasonal or folk idiom; it is at once exercise protocol and theatrical dance. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *Energizing Dramatic Drill*. A protocol designed to cultivate forceful dramatic expression through coordinated movement. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *Spanish Drill—The Carmen*. A choreographed drill using a stylized Spanish persona and movement vocabulary; its category lies uneasily between character dance, physical culture, and theatrical Orientalism. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *Roman Drill—The Amazon*. An athletic dance-drill derived principally from poses of Amazon sculptures in the Vatican; it translates sculpture into a moving bodily protocol. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf); [Ruyter 1996 preview](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781134808335_A24933924/preview-9781134808335_A24933924.pdf)

- **1895:** *Minuet Fan-Drill*. A choreographed protocol combining minuet figures, fan work, and physical training. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *Athenian Drill—The Victory*. A movement composition based on classical statuary and Athenian imagery; it occupies the boundary between statue posing and dance. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *Greek Drill—The Nymphs*. A group or solo-capable classical movement sequence centered on nymph imagery and a dance to Apollo. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1895:** *Æsthetic Drill*. A choreographed sequence intended to cultivate movement beautiful in itself rather than narrate a fixed story; its resistance to being either mere exercise or representational dance is part of its design. [1913 manual](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___genevieve_stebbins_system_of_physical_training.pdf)

- **1898:** *Special Drill for Home Use*. A domestic exercise protocol included within Stebbins’s physical-training system; unlike the elaborate theatrical drills, it was designed for individual practice. [Foxen’s account of the 1898 system](https://de.scribd.com/document/917443984/Inhaling-Spirit-Harmonialism-And-the-Western-Roots-of-Modern-Yoga-Anya-P-Foxen-Z-Library)

### Dance-pantomimes and embodied compositions

- **By 1892:** *Miriam, the Prophetess* (also reported simply as *Miriam*). A five-scene solo “musical, dramatic and pantomimic monologue” retelling the Exodus through Miriam rather than Moses and combining gesture, dance, music, recitation, and cymbals. Its multimedia form resists classification as either dance or spoken drama. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance); [Mullan 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **By 1892:** *Jephtha’s Daughter*. A biblical solo dance-pantomime centered on a female figure; surviving scholarship names the composition but does not supply a secure premiere date or complete scenario. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance)

- **By 1892:** *The Dance of Day* (also reported as *Dance of the Day*). A solo movement composition tracing a cycle from morning awakening through afternoon and sunset back to sleep. The variant title and the absence of a surviving score or choreographic text make it an unstable work whose clearest manifestation is Stebbins’s performance. [Centre national de la danse](https://mediatheque.cnd.fr/spip.php?id_article=&id_article=46&page=la_danse_libre-article); [Mullan 2017](https://doi.org/10.1386/jdsp.9.2.159_1)

- **1892:** *The Myth of Isis*. A three-part Egyptian dance-pantomime employing movement, costume, instrumental music, and lighting to embody Stebbins’s interpretation of Isis symbolism. Contemporary evidence establishes the performance, but not a fixed choreographic text. [Mullan 2017](https://doi.org/10.1386/jdsp.9.2.159_1)

- **By 1894:** *Esther at Shushan*. A biblical dance-pantomime centered on Esther; scholarship records its title but not a recoverable full choreography or certain first-performance date. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance)

- **By 1894:** *Ariadne*. A mythological solo combining statue-derived poses and transitional movement; its surviving identity is chiefly a title in accounts of Stebbins’s repertoire. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance)

- **By 1894:** *Psyche*. A mythological solo dance-pantomime; no complete choreographic document is known from the consulted scholarship, so it survives more securely as a reported performance than as a stable text. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance)

- **By 1894:** *Ceres*. A classical solo composition based on the goddess Ceres, joining statue posing and expressive movement. Its precise date and structure remain unestablished in the consulted sources. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance)

- **By 1894:** *The Descent of Ishtar*. A mythological dance-pantomime centered on a strong female figure; it survives in repertoire lists without a complete scenario or secure first-performance date. [Preston 2011](https://www.scribd.com/document/401667603/Modernism-s-Mythic-Pose-Gender-Genre-Solo-Performance)

- **1903:** *The Rubáiyát: A Pantomime*. A multimedia protocol for movement performed alongside a reading of Edward FitzGerald’s version of Omar Khayyám, published with music and nine illustrative photographs of Marie G. Macdonald in poses taught by Stebbins. The poem is not Stebbins’s creation, and accounts disagree over whether the photographs show poses created by Macdonald or a pantomime devised and taught by Stebbins; her secure contribution is the embodied arrangement and instruction. [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Omar+Khayyam); [Toepfer](https://karltoepfer.com/2019/06/29/pantomime-and-modernism-american-women-exert-influence-on-pantomime/)

### Dramatic and periodical texts

- **1885:** *Delsarte System of Dramatic Expression*. A short contribution printed in the June issue of *Werner’s Magazine* and associated with the book-length system appearing that year; whether it should be treated as an independent work or an anticipatory manifestation of the book is unclear. [Mullan 2020 bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1894:** *Practical Delsartism II: Physical Culture*. An instructional periodical essay explaining the physical-culture component of Stebbins’s practice. [Mullan 2020 bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1894:** *Practical Delsartism VI: Artistic Statue Posing*. An essay-manifesto and protocol defending statue posing as an artistic and educational embodied practice. [Mullan 2020 bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1894:** *Practical Delsartism VII: Pantomime*. An instructional essay defining and advocating Stebbins’s form of expressive pantomime. [Mullan 2020 bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1895:** *The Eve of Marriage: Monologue for a Lady*. A dramatic monologue written for female performance, not a physical-training manual; the consulted scholarly bibliography does not clarify its performance history. [Stebbins bibliography summarized in the biographical record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genevieve_Stebbins#Selected_works)

- **Undated; preserved in an 1889–1891 scrapbook:** *Physical Training: Gymnastics for Women—Successful Woman in This Profession—The Royal Beauty of the Statue Reveals to Her an Excellent Field of Work*. An essay preserved as a clipping in the Seidl Society records; its unwieldy reported heading and uncertain date should not be normalized into a confidently fixed title. [Mullan 2020 bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

### Translation and editorial creation

- **1901:** *An Egyptian Initiation by Iamblichus, a Neo-Platonist of the Fourth Century*; later issued as *Egyptian Mysteries: An Account of an Initiation*. Stebbins translated from P. Christian’s 1870 French text rather than directly from Greek; the later title and 1965 edition are manifestations of the translation, not evidence of a new 1965 work by her. The attribution to the ancient philosopher Iamblichus is part of the source tradition and should not be treated as securely authenticated authorship. [Mullan 2020 bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0149767720000327)

- **1913:** *The Quest of the Spirit, by a Pilgrim of the Way*. An abridged editorial arrangement of a much larger manuscript: Stebbins states that the printed booklet contains less than a quarter of it and names herself as editor, not author. Later research identifies the pseudonymous “Pilgrim” as her husband Norman Astley and proposes collaboration, but authorship remains contested; it therefore resists inclusion as an uncomplicated Stebbins work. [1913 scan](https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1913__stebbins___quest_of_the_spirit.pdf); [Johnson](https://adepts.light.org/category/genevieve-stebbins/)
