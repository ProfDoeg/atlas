## Works by Claude Elwood Shannon

### Theses, papers, reports, memoranda, lectures, and other texts

- **1938:** *A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits*. Published paper derived from Shannon’s MIT master’s thesis; it is a mathematical engineering text connecting Boolean algebra with relay-circuit design.
- **1939:** *Letter to Vannevar Bush*. A private research letter, subsequently published; its epistolary status makes it evidence of a developing project rather than a formal paper.
- **1940:** *An Algebra for Theoretical Genetics*. MIT doctoral dissertation applying algebraic methods to population genetics.
- **1940:** *A Theorem on Color Coding*. Bell Laboratories memorandum superseded by *A Theorem on Coloring the Lines of a Network*; it survives as an earlier state of that project.
- **1940:** *The Use of the Lakatos-Hickman Relay in a Subscriber Sender*. Bell Laboratories engineering memorandum; only an abstract entered the collected papers.
- **1941:** *Mathematical Theory of the Differential Analyzer*. Published mathematical paper on analog computation.
- **circa 1941:** *A Study of the Deflection Mechanism and Some Results on Rate Finders*. National Defense Research Committee report; only its abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1941:** *Backlash in Overdamped Systems*. National Defense Research Committee report.
- **1941:** *A Height Data Smoothing Mechanism*. National Defense Research Committee report not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1941:** *The Theory of Linear Differential and Smoothing Operators*. National Defense Research Committee report not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1941:** *Some Experimental Results on the Deflection Mechanism*. National Defense Research Committee report; only an abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1941:** *Criteria for Consistency and Uniqueness in Relay Circuits*. Unpublished typescript on switching theory.
- **1942:** *The Theory and Design of Linear Differential Equation Machines*. Bell Laboratories report to the military services: simultaneously a mathematical text and a protocol for designing computing machinery.
- **1942:** *The Number of Two-Terminal Series-Parallel Networks*. Published combinatorial paper made with John Riordan.
- **1943:** *Analogue of the Vernam System for Continuous Time Series*. Bell Laboratories memorandum proposing a continuous-signal analogue of a cryptographic system.
- **1943:** *On the Integration of the Ballistic Equations on the Aberdeen Analyzer*. Applied Mathematics Panel report made with William Feller.
- **1943:** *Pulse Code Modulation*. Bell Laboratories memorandum not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1944:** *Feedback Systems with Periodic Loop Closure*. Bell Laboratories memorandum not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1944:** *Two New Circuits for Alternate Pulse Counting*. Illustrated Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1944:** *Counting Up or Down With Pulse Counters*. Short illustrated Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1944:** *Circuits for a P.C.M. Transmitter and Receiver*. Engineering memorandum made with Bernard M. Oliver; only an abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1944:** *The Best Detection of Pulses*. Bell Laboratories memorandum on signal detection.
- **1944:** *Pulse Shape to Minimize Bandwidth With Nonoverlapping Pulses*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1945:** *A Mathematical Theory of Cryptography*. Formerly classified Bell Laboratories report; it was superseded, but not erased as an obra, by *Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems*.
- **1945:** *Mixed Statistical Determinate Systems*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1946:** *Data Smoothing and Prediction in Fire-Control Systems*. Wartime technical report made with Ralph B. Blackman and Hendrik W. Bode; the bibliography says it was superseded by later work.
- **1946:** *The Transient Behavior of a Large Number of Four-Terminal Unilateral Linear Networks Connected in Tandem*. Bell Laboratories memorandum made with C. L. Dolph; only an abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1946:** *Electronic Methods in Telephone Switching*. Unpublished illustrated Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1948:** *Some Generalizations of the Sampling Theorem*. Unpublished illustrated typescript.
- **1948:** *The Normal Ergodic Ensembles of Functions*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1948:** *Systems Which Approach the Ideal as P/N → Infinity*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1948:** *Theorems on Statistical Sequences*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1948:** *A Mathematical Theory of Communication*. Two-part journal paper constituting Shannon’s principal formulation of information theory.
- **1948:** *The Philosophy of PCM*. Published paper made with Bernard M. Oliver and John R. Pierce.
- **1948:** *Samples of Statistical English*. Bell Laboratories typescript consisting of experimental language samples; it resists a clean division between scientific text and generated linguistic artifact.
- **1948:** *Network Rings*. Illustrated Bell Laboratories typescript on network theory.
- **1948:** *Significance and Application [of Communication Research]*. Contribution to a Department of Defense symposium.
- **1948:** *Note on Certain Transcendental Numbers*. One-page Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1948:** *A Case of Efficient Coding for a Very Noisy Channel*. Short unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1948:** *Note on Reversing a Discrete Markhoff Process*. Illustrated Bell Laboratories typescript; the bibliography preserves “Markhoff” in the title.
- **1949:** *Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems*. Published cryptographic paper derived from the declassified 1945 report.
- **1949:** *The Mathematical Theory of Communication*. Book made with Warren Weaver; Shannon’s portion is essentially the 1948 paper, so the volume is a collaborative manifestation and reframing rather than an entirely new Shannon text.
- **1949:** *Communication in the Presence of Noise*. Published paper on signal transmission and sampling; its bibliography gives an evidently erroneous receipt year, “1940 [1948?],” and explicitly flags the uncertainty.
- **1949:** *A Theorem on Coloring the Lines of a Network*. Published graph-theory paper superseding the 1940 memorandum.
- **1949:** *Information Theory*. Five-page abstract of a talk for the American Statistical Association.
- **1949:** *The Synthesis of Two-Terminal Switching Circuits*. Published switching-theory paper.
- **1949:** *Review of Transformations on Lattices and Structures of Logic by Stephen A. Kiss*. Published book review.
- **1949:** *Review of Cybernetics, or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine by Norbert Wiener*. Published book review.
- **1950:** *A Simplified Derivation of Linear Least Squares Smoothing and Prediction Theory*. Published paper made with Hendrik W. Bode.
- **1950:** *Programming a Computer for Playing Chess*. Published paper specifying strategies for machine chess; as much project and protocol as descriptive article.
- **1950:** *A Chess-Playing Machine*. Expository article on the machine-chess project.
- **1950:** *Memory Requirements in a Telephone Exchange*. Published engineering paper.
- **1950:** *A Symmetrical Notation for Numbers*. Published mathematical note.
- **circa 1950:** *Proof of an Integration Formula*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **circa 1950:** *A Digital Method of Transmitting Information*. Undated Bell Laboratories typescript; the standard bibliography supplies only the approximate date.
- **1950/1953:** *Communication Theory—Exposition of Fundamentals*. Symposium paper delivered in 1950 and published in the 1953 proceedings.
- **1950/1953:** *General Treatment of the Problem of Coding*. Symposium paper delivered in 1950 and published in the 1953 proceedings.
- **1950/1953:** *The Lattice Theory of Information*. Symposium paper delivered in 1950 and published in the 1953 proceedings.
- **1950/1953:** *Discussion of Preceding Three Papers*. Recorded symposium discussion made with E. C. Cherry, S. H. Moss, W. Uttley, I. J. Good, W. Lawrence, and W. P. Anderson; it resists treatment as a solely authored work.
- **1950:** *Review of Description of a Relay Computer, by the Staff of the Harvard Computation Laboratory*. Published book review.
- **1950:** *Recent Developments in Communication Theory*. Expository magazine article.
- **1950:** *A Method of Power or Signal Transmission to a Moving Vehicle*. Illustrated Bell Laboratories memorandum for record; a textual specification for an engineering project.
- **1950/1952:** *Some Topics in Information Theory*. Congress contribution delivered in 1950 and published in 1952.
- **1951:** *Prediction and Entropy of Printed English*. Published paper reporting experimental estimates of linguistic entropy.
- **1951/1952:** *Presentation of a Maze Solving Machine*. Published record of Shannon’s 1951 presentation of the learning maze apparatus later called Theseus.
- **1952:** *Creative Thinking*. Unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1953:** *A Mind-Reading (?) Machine*. Bell Laboratories typescript describing a predictive game-playing apparatus; the question mark is part of Shannon’s title.
- **1953:** *The Relay Circuit Analyzer*. Bell Laboratories memorandum made with Edward F. Moore; only an abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1953:** *The Potentialities of Computers*. Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1953:** *Throbac I*. Bell Laboratories typescript describing the Roman-numeral calculating machine.
- **1953:** *Throbac—Circuit Operation*. Companion Bell Laboratories typescript not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1953:** *Tower of Hanoi*. Bell Laboratories typescript, apparently associated with a machine or circuit treatment of the puzzle.
- **1953:** *Machine Aid for Switching Circuit Design*. Published paper made with Edward F. Moore.
- **1953:** *Mathmanship or How to Give an Explicit Solution Without Actually Solving the Problem*. Short, unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1953:** *Computers and Automata*. Published survey and argument concerning computing machinery.
- **1953:** *Realization of All 16 Switching Functions of Two Variables Requires 18 Contacts*. Illustrated Bell Laboratories memorandum.
- **1953:** *The Relay Circuit Synthesizer*. Bell Laboratories memorandum made with Edward F. Moore; only an abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1954:** *A Relay Laboratory Outfit for Colleges*. Bell Laboratories memorandum made with David W. Hagelbarger; both a description and an instructional apparatus project.
- **1954:** *Efficient Coding of a Binary Source With One Very Infrequent Symbol*. Bell Laboratories memorandum.
- **1954:** *Bounds on the Derivatives and Rise Time of a Band and Amplitude Limited Signal*. Illustrated, unpublished Bell Laboratories typescript.
- **1954:** *Reliable Circuits Using Crummy Relays*. Bell Laboratories memorandum made with Edward F. Moore and subsequently divided into two published papers.
- **1954/1956:** *A Universal Turing Machine With Two Internal States*. Bell Laboratories memorandum subsequently published in *Automata Studies*.
- **1954/1956:** *Computability by Probabilistic Machines*. Bell Laboratories memorandum and later book chapter made with Karel de Leeuw, Edward F. Moore, and Norman Shapiro.
- **1955:** *Concavity of Transmission Rate as a Function of Input Probabilities*. Bell Laboratories memorandum; only an abstract was reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1955:** *Some Results on Ideal Rectifier Circuits*. Bell Laboratories memorandum.
- **1955:** *The Simultaneous Synthesis of s Switching Functions of n Variables*. Bell Laboratories memorandum.
- **1955:** *Game Playing Machines*. Published lecture on mechanical and computational games.
- **circa 1955:** *Information Theory*. Encyclopedia article written around 1955 and appearing in later printings of the fourteenth edition of *Encyclopaedia Britannica*.
- **circa 1955:** *Cybernetics*. Encyclopedia article written around 1955; it was not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **1955:** *The Rate of Approach to Ideal Coding*. Published abstract rather than a full paper.
- **1956:** *Reliable Circuits Using Less Reliable Relays I*. First published paper made with Edward F. Moore from their 1954 memorandum.
- **1956:** *Reliable Circuits Using Less Reliable Relays II*. Second published paper made with Edward F. Moore from their 1954 memorandum.
- **1956:** *Automata Studies*. Research anthology jointly edited with John McCarthy; Shannon also contributed two papers, listed separately here.
- **1956:** *Concavity of Resistance Functions*. Published paper made with David W. Hagelbarger.
- **1956:** *The Bandwagon*. Editorial warning against indiscriminate uses of information theory; as an intervention intended to redirect a field, it approaches a manifesto.
- **1956 and succeeding years:** *Information Theory*. A changing body of MIT seminar notes rather than one fixed publication. Its numerous titled sections concern inequalities, probability tails, coding, feedback, Gaussian channels, channel capacity, side information, and error bounds; its open-ended accretion makes it a pedagogical project as much as a single text.
- **1956:** *Reliable Machines from Unreliable Components*. Notes for five MIT lectures.
- **1956:** *The Portfolio Problem, and How to Pay the Forecaster*. Notes taken by W. W. Peterson from a Shannon lecture; their textual authorship is therefore mediated rather than straightforwardly Shannon’s.
- **1956:** *Notes on Relation of Error Probability to Delay in a Noisy Channel*. Notes of an MIT lecture.
- **1956:** *Notes on the Kelly Betting Theory of Noisy Information*. Notes of an MIT lecture.
- **1956:** *The Zero Error Capacity of a Noisy Channel*. Published information-theory paper.
- **1956:** *A Note on the Maximum Flow Through a Network*. Published paper made with Peter Elias and Amiel Feinstein.
- **1956/1957:** *Some Geometrical Results in Channel Capacity*. English conference paper published across the 1956–1957 proceedings record; a German-language manifestation also appeared, but is not counted as another work.
- **1957:** *Certain Results in Coding Theory for Noisy Channels*. Published information-theory paper.
- **1958:** *Von Neumann’s Contribution to Automata Theory*. Published historical and analytical paper.
- **1958:** *A Note on a Partial Ordering for Communication Channels*. Published information-theory paper.
- **1958:** *Channels With Side Information at the Transmitter*. Published paper developing what became known as the Shannon strategy.
- **1959:** *Probability of Error for Optimal Codes in a Gaussian Channel*. Published information-theory paper.
- **1959:** *Coding Theorems for a Discrete Source With a Fidelity Criterion*. Published formulation of rate-distortion theory; a revised reprinting is a later manifestation, not a separate work.
- **1960/1961:** *Two-Way Communication Channels*. Symposium paper presented in 1960 and published in 1961.
- **1963:** *Computers and Automation—Progress and Promise in the Twentieth Century*. Rice University semicentennial lecture published in the university’s lecture volume.
- **1967:** *Lower Bounds to Error Probability for Coding on Discrete Memoryless Channels I*. Published paper made with Robert G. Gallager and Elwyn R. Berlekamp.
- **1967:** *Lower Bounds to Error Probability for Coding on Discrete Memoryless Channels II*. Second published paper in the series made with Gallager and Berlekamp.
- **1978:** *The Fourth-Dimensional Twist, or a Modest Proposal in Aid of the American Driver in England*. Illustrated All Souls College typescript; a playful geometrical project not reproduced in the collected papers.
- **circa 1980:** *Scientific Aspects of Juggling*. Unpublished typescript developing a mathematical theory of juggling.
- **1982:** *Claude Shannon’s No-Drop Juggling Diorama*. Published article describing Shannon’s mechanical juggling display.
- **circa 1982:** *A Rubric on Rubik Cubics*. Unpublished typescript on Rubik’s Cube; the date is approximate and the standard bibliography does not claim publication.

### Patented and abandoned invention projects

- **1946–1957:** *Communication System Employing Pulse Code Modulation*. U.S. Patent 2,801,281, made with Bernard M. Oliver; filed in 1946 and granted in 1957.
- **circa 1946–1948:** *A Sender Circuit for Panel or Crossbar Telephone Systems*. Patent project made with B. D. Holbrook; the application was abandoned in 1948, so the project exists without a granted patent.
- **1948–1951:** *Cathode-Ray Device*. U.S. Patent 2,576,040, made with John R. Pierce and John W. Tukey; filed in 1948 and granted in 1951.
- **1951–1954:** *Control Apparatus*. Patent project filed in 1951 and abandoned in 1954; the standard bibliography does not supply a patent number or fuller fixed title.
- **1953–1957:** *Electrical Circuit Analyzer*. U.S. Patent 2,776,405, made with Edward F. Moore; filed in 1953 and granted in 1957.

### Machines, toys, and experimental apparatus

- **Youth, date not fixed:** *Backyard telegraph line*. A telegraph system Shannon constructed using barbed-wire fencing to communicate with a friend; surviving accounts describe the construction, but not a formal title or secure date.
- **1950:** *Theseus*. An electromechanical mouse-and-maze system whose hidden relay circuitry learned a route and then repeated it. The mouse, maze, and controller form one cybernetic apparatus rather than a literary “work.”
- **early 1950s:** *Endgame*. A special-purpose chess-playing machine able to demonstrate a restricted chess ending. Accounts connect it with Shannon’s computer-chess project, but published sources do not consistently provide a single construction date.
- **1952:** *Mind-reading machine*. A relay-based game apparatus that predicted whether its human opponent would choose heads or tails by exploiting patterns in the opponent’s play.
- **1953:** *Throbac*. A mechanical or electromechanical calculator operating with Roman numerals; its name abbreviates “Thrifty Roman-Numeral Backward-Looking Computer.”
- **1950s, date not fixed:** *Ultimate Machine*. A box whose sole action was to switch itself off after a user switched it on. Versions and replicas complicate identification of a single definitive object, so it is better understood as a recurring machine-concept with physical manifestations.
- **date not fixed:** *Relay circuit analyzer and synthesizer apparatus*. Experimental machinery made in connection with Shannon and Edward F. Moore’s switching-circuit research; the textual record clearly establishes the project, while surviving descriptions do not always separate analyzer, synthesizer, and patented implementations.
- **date not fixed:** *Juggling machine*. An apparatus constructed to model or perform juggling. Sources use the phrase broadly, and it should not automatically be identified with the separate no-drop diorama.
- **before 1982:** *No-Drop Juggling Diorama / Little Juggling Clowns*. A black-lit mechanical diorama in which three fluorescent figures—Ignatov, Rastelli, and Virgoaga—appear to juggle rings, balls, and clubs. It is a kinetic tableau, not simply an illustration of Shannon’s juggling paper.
- **date not fixed:** *Rubik’s Cube-solving device*. A mechanism made to solve or assist in solving the puzzle; institutional accounts attest to it but do not provide a secure individual title or date.
- **date not fixed:** *Wearable roulette computer*. A concealed analog computing and signaling system developed with Edward O. Thorp to predict roulette outcomes. Its collaborative authorship, multiple components, and secret operational use make it a system-project rather than a single unambiguous object.
- **date not fixed:** *Motorized pogo stick*. A recreational machine reported among Shannon’s experimental constructions; no stable individual title or construction date is supplied by the institutional source.
- **date not fixed:** *Rocket-powered flying discs*. Experimental propelled discs, often described retrospectively as “rocket-powered Frisbees”; the plural reports a family of trials rather than a securely catalogued single object.
- **date not fixed:** *Flame-throwing trumpet*. A hybrid musical, mechanical, and pyrotechnic contraption. It is close to a miniature Gesamtkunstwerk because instrument, performance, flame, and joke cannot be cleanly separated.
- **date not fixed:** *Water-walking shoes*. Buoyant footwear made for crossing the surface of Shannon’s lake; surviving popular accounts describe the experiment but provide neither a formal title nor a firm date.

### Manifestations not counted as new works

- **1950–1981:** *Translations and reprints of Shannon’s papers and books*. German, Italian, French, Spanish, and Russian manifestations are documented in the standard bibliography, but they are not separately counted above because the bibliography attributes their translation or editorial making to others and identifies them as versions of existing works.
- **1993:** *Claude Elwood Shannon: Collected Papers*. This collected-works edition is the principal catalogue used here, but it was edited by Neil J. A. Sloane and Aaron D. Wyner. Shannon made the constituent texts, not this post-career bibliographical arrangement.
