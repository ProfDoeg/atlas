## Works by Donald Ervin Knuth

### Lifelong projects and evolving obras

- **1962–present:** *The Art of Computer Programming*. Knuth’s defining obra: an unfinished, continually revised project intended to give a comprehensive account of fundamental algorithms. Its incompleteness is constitutive rather than accidental; Volumes 4C–7 remain projects, while published volumes and fascicles are manifestations of the larger design. [Knuth’s official project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **1977–present:** *TeX and METAFONT*. A Gesamtkunstwerk of software, programming literature, mathematical typography, type design, and publishing practice—not merely two programs. Knuth froze TeX and METAFONT except for error correction, so their slowly converging version numbers function as declarations of stability. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1981–present:** *Literate programming*. A programming method, manifesto, and continuing body of practice in which programs are written primarily for human readers and secondarily for machines; WEB, CWEB, Knuth’s program books, and many experimental programs are its manifestations. [*Literate Programming*](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/lp.html)

### The Art of Computer Programming: completed volumes and provisional manifestations

- **1968:** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 1: Fundamental Algorithms*. The first volume of the larger project; substantially remade for its 1973 second and 1997 third editions, which are revisions rather than separate obras. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1969:** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 2: Seminumerical Algorithms*. A monograph on numerical and probabilistic algorithms; later editions extensively revise the same work. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1973:** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 3: Sorting and Searching*. A monograph whose 1998 second edition is a revised manifestation, not a different work. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2005:** *Volume 1, Fascicle 1: MMIX—A RISC Computer for the New Millennium*. A provisional replacement for Volume 1’s original MIX-machine sections and a textual manifestation of the MMIX architecture. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2005:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 2: Generating All Tuples and Permutations*. A beta-test publication later absorbed into Volume 4A; it therefore resists classification as either an independent book or merely an edition fragment. [TAOCP fascicles](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2005:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 3: Generating All Combinations and Partitions*. A provisional fascicle subsequently incorporated into Volume 4A. [TAOCP fascicles](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2006:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 4: Generating All Trees; History of Combinatorial Generation*. A provisional fascicle subsequently incorporated into Volume 4A. [TAOCP fascicles](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2008:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 0: Introduction to Combinatorial Algorithms and Boolean Functions*. A provisional paperback manifestation later incorporated into Volume 4A. [TAOCP fascicles](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2009:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 1: Bitwise Tricks & Techniques; Binary Decision Diagrams*. A provisional fascicle later incorporated into Volume 4A. [TAOCP fascicles](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2011:** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 4A: Combinatorial Algorithms, Part 1*. The first consolidated Volume 4 hardcover, incorporating and revising Fascicles 0–4. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2015:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 6: Satisfiability*. A provisional component of what became Volume 4B. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2019:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 5: Mathematical Preliminaries Redux; Introduction to Backtracking; Dancing Links*. A provisional component subsequently incorporated into Volume 4B. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2022–2023:** *Collected Indexes for The Art of Computer Programming*. An electronic reference object assembled from the indexes of the published volumes; its 2023 manifestation adds Volume 4B to the 2022 compilation. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2023:** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 4B: Combinatorial Algorithms, Part 2*. The second consolidated Volume 4 hardcover, incorporating revised forms of Fascicles 5 and 6. [TAOCP project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2025:** *Volume 4, Fascicle 7: Constraint Satisfaction*. The first published manifestation of the projected Volume 4C; Knuth explicitly describes 4C as only beginning, so the fascicle is both a book and part of an unfinished project. [TAOCP project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **2025–present:** *Volume 4C pre-fascicles*. Alpha-test drafts, including *Hamiltonian Paths and Cycles* and *A Potpourri of Puzzles*; these deliberately resist finished-work status and solicit expert debugging. [TAOCP project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

- **Projected:** *The Art of Computer Programming, Volumes 4C–7*. The intended continuation of the obra. These are projects, not extant completed books, and neither dates nor final contents should be inferred for them. [TAOCP project page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/taocp.html)

### Other authored and coauthored books

- **1971:** *MIX*. A compact manual and protocol for programming the imaginary MIX computer used in the early editions of *The Art of Computer Programming*. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1974:** *Surreal Numbers*. A mathematical novella: a fictional dialogue that simultaneously constructs Conway’s number system and dramatizes mathematical discovery. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1976:** *Mariages stables et leurs relations avec d’autres problèmes combinatoires*. A French lecture monograph on stable matching; the 1997 English translation, *Stable Marriage and Its Relation to Other Combinatorial Problems*, is a later manifestation rather than a separate obra. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1979:** *TeX and METAFONT: New Directions in Typesetting*. A multipart presentation of the first TeX/METAFONT system, combining manuals, sample output, and programs; superseded technically but historically distinct from the later five-volume exposition. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1981:** *Mathematics for the Analysis of Algorithms*. A coauthored textbook with Daniel H. Greene; its later editions revise the same work. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1984:** *The TeXbook*. A user manual, reference work, exercise book, and playful textual demonstration of TeX; it also became Volume A of *Computers & Typesetting*. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1986:** *TeX: The Program*. A book-length literate program constituting both executable source and critical exposition; Volume B of *Computers & Typesetting*. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1986:** *The METAFONTbook*. The user and reference manual for METAFONT and Volume C of *Computers & Typesetting*. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1986:** *METAFONT: The Program*. METAFONT’s complete literate source and Volume D of *Computers & Typesetting*: simultaneously a book, specification, and executable artifact. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1986:** *Computer Modern Typefaces*. The literate sources and design account of the Computer Modern family, published as Volume E of *Computers & Typesetting*. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1986:** *Computers & Typesetting, Volumes A–E*. The five-volume Gesamtkunstwerk uniting manuals, source programs, algorithms, and typefaces; the component volumes remain separately identifiable works. Millennium and Jubilee issues are later manifestations. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1989:** *Concrete Mathematics: A Foundation for Computer Science*. A textbook coauthored with Ronald L. Graham and Oren Patashnik; the 1994 second edition enlarges the same work. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1989:** *Mathematical Writing*. A coauthored course book with Tracy Larrabee and Paul Roberts, assembled from Knuth’s Stanford seminar; it is both pedagogy and a protocol for mathematical prose. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1990:** *3:16 Bible Texts Illuminated*. A work of “stratified sampling” that examines chapter 3, verse 16 in every biblical book, joining calligraphy, theology, textual scholarship, and visual design. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1991:** *Kunuusu Sensei no Program-Ron*. A Japanese anthology edited by Makoto Arisawa from Knuth’s writings; because the selection and editorial form are Arisawa’s, this is a mediated manifestation of Knuth’s work rather than an independently composed Knuth book. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1992:** *Literate Programming*. A curated and revised collection of papers that also acts as the manifesto of literate programming. [Official book page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/lp.html)

- **1992:** *Axioms and Hulls*. A research monograph on computational geometry, axiomatic closure systems, and finite configurations. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1993:** *The CWEB System of Structured Documentation*. A manual and specification coauthored with Silvio Levy; the 2001 hypertext version is a revised manifestation. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1994:** *The Stanford GraphBase: A Platform for Combinatorial Computing*. A book, software library, benchmark collection, and set of literate “programmatic essays”—a workbench rather than merely a text. [Official GraphBase page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/sgb.html)

- **1996:** *Selected Papers on Computer Science*. The first topically titled volume in Knuth’s archival papers series, revising earlier publications into a deliberately consistent book form. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1999:** *Digital Typography*. An archival collection that reworks Knuth’s papers on TeX, METAFONT, type design, and mathematical typography. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1999:** *MMIXware*. A collection of literate programs that specifies, assembles, simulates, and analyzes the MMIX computer; it is at once a book and an operational realization of an imaginary machine. [Official MMIXware page](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/mmixware.html)

- **2000:** *Selected Papers on Analysis of Algorithms*. An archival book that revises and supplements earlier papers rather than merely reprinting them. [Official book page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/aa.html)

- **2001:** *Things a Computer Scientist Rarely Talks About*. A book made from Knuth’s MIT “God and Computers” lectures, combining autobiography, theology, aesthetics, and computer-scientific habits of thought. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2003:** *Selected Papers on Discrete Mathematics*. An archival volume containing what Knuth describes as almost everything he had written on mathematical topics during the preceding four decades. [Official book page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/dm.html)

- **2003:** *Selected Papers on Computer Languages*. An archival and revised collection of Knuth’s work on programming-language description, parsing, semantics, and literate programming. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2010:** *Selected Papers on Design of Algorithms*. An archival collection of algorithmic constructions, frequently including new addenda to the original articles. [Official book page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/da.html)

- **2011:** *Selected Papers on Fun and Games*. An archival collection of recreational mathematics, puzzles, fiction, and music; its range makes it resist the category “technical papers.” [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2011:** *Companion to the Papers of Donald Knuth*. An annotated autobiography, bibliography, and cumulative index to the eight-volume selected-papers series; it is both finding aid and authored reflection. [Official book page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/cp.html)

- **2016:** *Puzzle Box, Volume 1*. A collaboratively authored puzzle anthology; Knuth is one contributor among Andrea Gilbert, Bram Cohen, Ed Pegg Jr., and others, so the whole book should not be attributed to him alone. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2017:** *Puzzle Box, Volume 2*. A multi-author puzzle anthology containing Knuth’s contribution but not solely made by him. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2017:** *Puzzle Box, Volume 3*. A multi-author puzzle anthology containing Knuth’s contribution but not solely made by him. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2018:** *Fantasia Apocalyptica Illustrated*. A book co-created with Duane R. Bibby that aligns Biblical Greek, English translation, musical notation, and 128 illustrations; it is a visual-textual manifestation of the musical work, not simply its score. [Knuth’s official page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/fant.html)

### Software, languages, machines, and typefaces

- **1959:** *RUNCIBLE*. An algebraic compiler for a limited computer, documented in Knuth’s paper “RUNCIBLE—Algebraic Translation on a Limited Computer”; the surviving historical identity combines program and paper. [Official bibliography, P2](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1961:** *SMALGOL-61*. A small ALGOL implementation created collaboratively with G. A. Bachelor, J. R. H. Dempster, and J. Speroni. [Official bibliography, P7](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1964:** *SOL*. A symbolic language for general-purpose systems simulation, co-created with J. L. McNeley and accompanied by a formal definition. [Official bibliography, P16–P17](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1960s:** *MIX*. An imaginary binary-decimal computer devised as the common machine model for early *Art of Computer Programming* algorithms. It is a specification and pedagogical fiction, not a manufactured computer. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1977–1984:** *METAFONT*. A programming language and interpreter for constructing typefaces parametrically; its 1984 redesign superseded the earlier implementation while preserving the project’s identity. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1977–1982:** *TeX*. A digital typesetting system made in two major historical forms, TeX78 and the substantially redesigned TeX82. It is software, a typographic protocol, and the generator of innumerable later textual manifestations. [Knuth’s archived early sources](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1978–1985:** *Computer Modern*. A parametrically defined family of text and mathematical typefaces made with METAFONT. Knuth revised the glyphs in 1992 and declared the corrected forms final; the family is a design system rather than one font file. [Knuth’s font notice](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/cm.html)

- **1981:** *WEB*. The original literate-programming system, combining a source language for human-readable exposition with processors that “tangle” compilable Pascal and “weave” typeset documentation. [*Literate Programming*](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/lp.html)

- **1986:** *plain TeX*. The foundational TeX macro format: a working protocol that instructs TeX how to interpret higher-level typographic commands. [*Computers & Typesetting*](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/knuth/abcde.html)

- **1987–present:** *CWEB*. A C/C++ literate-programming system developed with Silvio Levy. Its authorship is explicitly collaborative, and its evolving versions are manifestations of one continuing system. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1980s:** *AMS Euler*. A mathematical typeface co-created with Hermann Zapf: Zapf supplied the calligraphic designs and Knuth developed their digital mathematical realization, so sole attribution to either man would be misleading. [Official bibliography, P125](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1993–1994:** *The Stanford GraphBase*. A CWEB library, collection of standard graph datasets, and combinatorial-computing workbench inseparable from its book-length documentation. [Official GraphBase page](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/sgb.html)

- **1999:** *MMIX*. A 64-bit RISC architecture invented as MIX’s successor. It is a rigorously specified imaginary computer made operational through assembler, simulators, test programs, and the book *MMIXware*. [Official MMIXware page](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/mmixware.html)

- **1999:** *MMIXAL*. The assembly language of MMIX and its assembler; it is a protocol for expressing programs on the imaginary architecture. [Official MMIXware page](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/mmixware.html)

- **1999:** *MMIX simulators and MMMIX*. Literate software implementing MMIX, including a basic simulator and a configurable meta-simulator for pipelines, caches, branch prediction, and interrupts. [Official MMIXware page](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/mmixware.html)

- **1991–present:** *CWEB experimental-program corpus*. Hundreds of small research, puzzle, enumeration, graphics, and verification programs written during Knuth’s work. Knuth publishes only a subset and calls them programs written largely for his own edification, so this corpus resists any claim of complete public cataloguing. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1992:** *HWTIME*. A brief “Hello, world” and clock program constructed as a demonstration of CWEB. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1992:** *SHAM*. A CWEB program enumerating symmetrical Hamiltonian cycles. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1991:** *REFLECT*. A program enumerating equivalence classes of reflection networks, also called CC systems. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1992:** *HULL, HULLS, HULLT,* and *HULLTR*. A related family of experimental programs used to produce and test examples for *Axioms and Hulls*; treating them as one suite better reflects their functional unity. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1994:** *DECAGON*. A program, with several change files, for packing golden triangles into decagons, stars, pentagons, and related configurations. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1994:** *TCALC*. An interactive calculator for extremely large numbers. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1994:** *SGBFIG*. MetaPost source for the illustrations in *The Stanford GraphBase*. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **1996:** *OBDD*. A program enumerating perfect matchings of bipartite graphs. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2000–2025:** *color-mode.el*. An experimental line-oriented color mode for Emacs, revised in 2025. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2000–2025:** *Knuth’s FVWM2 configuration and icon set*. A personal but published interface artifact used to arrange book-writing desktops; it resists the dignity of “work” only because configuration files are conventionally treated as incidental. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2002–2023:** *RANARRAY*. Portable C and FORTRAN manifestations of Knuth’s recommended subtractive random-number generator, corrected over time to agree across languages. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2005:** *DonKeys* and *DonKeysExtended*. Custom Macintosh keyboard layouts, with associated icons, that exchange several punctuation-key positions; these are designed tools rather than texts in the ordinary sense. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2010:** *The TPK algorithm in INTERCAL*. A deliberately unusual implementation of the standard Trabb Pardo–Knuth test algorithm in the esoteric INTERCAL language. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2021:** *matulatree.m*. Mathematica code for generating Matula trees in rectree format. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **2021:** *randomfreetree.m*. Mathematica code for generating random free trees in rectree format. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

- **undated:** *my6x13* and *my6x13B*. TeX-oriented bitmap fonts made for Knuth’s Emacs editing environment; the official archive supplies no fixed creation date. [Official program archive](https://cs.stanford.edu/content/contacting-donald-knuth/programs.html)

### Research papers, occasional writings, and unpublished notes

- **1957–present:** *Refereed papers P1–P160*. Knuth’s official bibliography treats these numbered papers as a distinct corpus beginning with “The potrzebie system of weights and measures” and extending through collaborative and single-author research in algorithms, languages, typography, combinatorics, and history. Reprints in the *Selected Papers* books are revised manifestations, not new works; starred entries in the bibliography indicate papers written by a coauthor, so authorship is not uniform across the corpus. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1959:** *RUNCIBLE—Algebraic Translation on a Limited Computer*. A paper and surviving account of the RUNCIBLE compiler; its dual status as documentation and principal historical manifestation of the program makes paper/program separation difficult. [Official bibliography, P2](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1960:** *An Imaginary Number System*. A research paper introducing arithmetic with base \(2i\). [Official bibliography, P3](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1961:** *Minimizing Drum Latency Time*. An algorithms paper on arranging instructions and data for rotating-drum memory. [Official bibliography, P5](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1962:** *A History of Writing Compilers*. An early historical study of compiler construction. [Official bibliography, P10](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1965:** *Finite Semifields and Projective Planes*. Knuth’s doctoral research published as a mathematical paper. [Official bibliography, P21](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1965:** *On the Translation of Languages from Left to Right*. The paper that introduced LR parsing; it is theory but also a protocol for constructing parsers. [Official bibliography, P23](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1968:** *Semantics of Context-Free Languages*. The paper introducing attributed grammars; its 1971 errata belong to the same work. [Official bibliography, P32](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1974:** *Computer Programming as an Art*. Knuth’s Turing Award lecture; an argument and disciplinary manifesto asserting programming’s artistic dimension. [*Literate Programming* contents](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/lp.html)

- **1984:** *Literate Programming*. The foundational paper naming and demonstrating the method; it functions as manifesto, protocol, and worked example. [*Literate Programming*](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/lp.html)

- **1989:** *The Errors of TeX*. An empirical and autobiographical study of TeX’s defects and their discovery, later expanded in *Literate Programming*. [Official bibliography, P124](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1989:** *AMS Euler—A New Typeface for Mathematics*. A paper coauthored with Hermann Zapf documenting the collaboratively made AMS Euler typeface. [Official bibliography, P125](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2000:** *Dancing Links*. A research paper presenting the reversible linked-list technique associated with Algorithm X; the algorithm, implementation, and exposition are tightly coupled. [Official preprint list](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/preprints.html)

- **various dates:** *Other publications Q1–Q284*. Reviews, forewords, obituaries, interviews, reminiscences, lectures, problem solutions, letters, and occasional writings form a separately numbered corpus in Knuth’s own bibliography. It resists being called one uniform body of “works”: some entries are authored texts, some collaborations, and some records of appearances. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **various dates:** *Reports R1–R47*. Technical reports and documents of limited circulation form the third numbered textual corpus in Knuth’s bibliography. Their restricted circulation does not make them nonexistent, but some are institutional or editorial objects rather than solely authored works. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1991–2026:** *“Unpublications.”* Informal mathematical and computational notes that Knuth explicitly does not intend to polish for publication, including *Bipermutations*, *Diamonds and Dragons*, *Amazing Grace*, *Xqueens and Xqueenons*, *Claude’s Cycles*, and *Fillomino Densities*. These resist work-status by Knuth’s own description: they are notes to himself and colleagues that might become nuclei of later work. [Official preprint and unpublication register](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/preprints.html)

### Patented inventions

- **1969:** *Digital Computers Having an Indirect Field Length Operation*. A patented computing mechanism co-invented with Roger E. Packard; U.S. Patent 3,422,405. [Official CV](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1969:** *Computer Edit System*. A patented text-editing system co-invented with Donald P. Hynes; U.S. Patent 3,454,929. [Official CV](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1970:** *Random Number Generator*. A patented apparatus credited to Knuth; U.S. Patent 3,548,174. [Official CV](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1971:** *Scaling and Number Base Converting Method and Apparatus*. A patented method co-invented with LeRoy R. Guck and Lawrence G. Hanson; U.S. Patent 3,626,167. [Official CV](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1994–1996:** *Methods of Controlling Dot Size in Digital Halftoning with Multi-Cell Threshold Arrays*. A digital-halftoning invention co-created with Stephen N. Schiller, patented in the United States and Europe. [Official CV](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

### Music

- **1950s:** *Milton and the Rhinoceros*. A substantial symphonic-band spoof based on a Roger Price story. The manuscript was lost by Knuth’s band director and no trace is known to survive; the work therefore exists only by testimony and resists manifestation as a recoverable score. [Knuth’s account](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/fant.html)

- **1950s–2000s:** *Short compositions and hymn arrangements*. A small corpus written in high school, college, and adulthood, with selected pieces gathered in Chapters 19–21 of *Selected Papers on Fun and Games* and several hymn arrangements printed there and in *Things a Computer Scientist Rarely Talks About*. The official sources do not claim that these selections constitute a complete catalogue. [Knuth’s account](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/fant.html)

- **2011–2017:** *Fantasia Apocalyptica*. A large multimedia composition translating all 404 verses of Revelation into musical motifs, principally for organ. Its score comprises 23 PDF parts and 64 pages; its relation among Greek text, projected images, musical notation, algorithms, and performance makes it a Gesamtkunstwerk rather than simply “a book.” [Official score and commentary](https://cs.stanford.edu/~knuth/fant.html)

### Recorded lectures and audiovisual manifestations

- **1985:** *Problem Solving with Donald Knuth*. A sequence of twenty 75-minute Stanford instructional videotapes; a performed course and audiovisual work rather than a printed text. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1993:** *The Literate Mathematician*. A thirty-minute BBC radio talk first broadcast in *Maths Miscellany*. The broadcast is a manifestation jointly shaped by Knuth and its BBC producers. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1993:** *Computer Musings: The Associative Law, or The Anatomy of Rotations in Binary Trees*. A recorded lecture treating mathematical exposition and algorithmic demonstration as performance. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1996:** *Graph Drawing from a User’s Perspective*. A recorded opening lecture at the MSRI Graph Drawing conference. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1997:** *Some Research Problems for Combinatorialists*. A recorded MSRI lecture proposing problems rather than reporting a completed project. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1999:** *God and Computers*. Seven public MIT lectures; they are the performed precursor of *Things a Computer Scientist Rarely Talks About*, not merely an edition of that later book. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **1999:** *MMIX: A RISC Computer for the New Millennium*. A recorded public lecture presenting the MMIX architecture. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2002:** *All Questions Answered*. A public lecture structured around audience questions; its event-character resists reduction to a fixed written text. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2003:** *Bottom-Up Education; or, The Search for Intelligent Life in the University*. A recorded keynote address at ITiCSE 2003. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **2007:** *Donald Knuth: My Life Story*. A 97-segment, 442-minute oral-history performance produced by Peoples Archive. Knuth is its speaking subject and principal narrator, but the audiovisual object itself is collaborative and institutionally produced. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)

- **various dates:** *Computer Musings*. An open-ended series of Stanford lectures distributed online. Knuth’s bibliography stopped attempting to enumerate internet videos around 2004, so no honest claim of audiovisual completeness is possible. [Official bibliography](https://www-cs-faculty.stanford.edu/~knuth/vita.pdf)
