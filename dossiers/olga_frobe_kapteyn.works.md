## Works by Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn

### Gesamtkunstwerk, institution, and intellectual projects

- **1920–1962:** *Casa Gabriella / Casa Eranos*. Fröbe-Kapteyn transformed her home and grounds at Moscia, Ascona, into a combined dwelling, meeting place, image laboratory, and spiritual-intellectual environment. It is less a single work than the physical matrix from which her later projects emerged.

- **1927–1932:** *International Centre for Spiritual Research*. An unrealized or short-lived institutional project conceived for encounters between Eastern and Western spiritual traditions; its lecture hall displayed her Meditation Plates. It preceded and materially prepared Eranos, so its unfinished status is part of the obra.

- **1933–1961:** *Eranos conferences*. A continuing curatorial and hospitable project founded and directed by Fröbe-Kapteyn: annual interdisciplinary gatherings at Casa Eranos rather than a text or conventional institution. Because it joined landscape, architecture, ritualized hospitality, lectures, pictures, publications, and personal relationships, *Gesamtkunstwerk* is the most accurate category, although the name *Eranos* was suggested by Rudolf Otto. ([Eranos Foundation](https://eranosfoundation.org/PRE/history_b.htm); [Religiographies](https://www.cini.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Religiographies-v.4-n.1.pdf))

- **c. 1935–mid-1950s:** *Eranos Archive for Research in Symbolism* (*Archiv für Symbolforschung*, subsequently associated with the Archive for Research in Archetypal Symbolism/ARAS). A research instrument assembled from approximately 6,000–6,500 photographic reproductions, organized by archetypal themes and used in exhibitions and lectures. It resists attribution as an artwork because most constituent images were made by others; Fröbe-Kapteyn’s obra lies in selecting, photographing or commissioning photographs, classifying, mounting, juxtaposing, and activating them as a visual argument. The original collection was later transferred to the Warburg Institute. ([Kunsthalle Mainz guide](https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/39535/docfile/7abfbb97ea126f2c873327c728853276.pdf); [Eranos Foundation documentary collection](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/page.php?page=20&pagename=documentary+collection); [AWARE](https://awarewomenartists.com/artiste/olga-frobe-kapteyn/))

- **1938:** *The Great Mother*. A thematic constellation of photographs and reproductions assembled from the Eranos image archive for the conference and yearbook *Gestalt und Kult der “Grossen Mutter”*. Neither wholly an exhibition nor wholly a text, it is an iconographic atlas whose meaning arises from arrangement; Erich Neumann later used the collection in his own research. ([Kunsthalle Mainz guide](https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/39535/docfile/7abfbb97ea126f2c873327c728853276.pdf); [Religiographies](https://www.cini.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Religiographies-v.4-n.1.pdf))

### Visual obra: paintings, drawings, albums, and prints

- **c. 1926–1934:** *Meditation Plates* (*Meditation Drawings*). A corpus of geometrical paintings and drawings in tempera, ink, and gold leaf, conceived as instruments of meditation, research, and psychological or spiritual effect rather than autonomous pictures. Its completeness is disputed: the Eranos Foundation has counted approximately 127 known plates, while the Kunsthalle Mainz guide describes about 200, including repetitions and variants; at least ten originals sent to Turin were reportedly lost during wartime bombing. ([Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn: artista-ricercatrice](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/page.php?page=28&pagename=catalogues); [Kunsthalle Mainz guide](https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/39535/docfile/7abfbb97ea126f2c873327c728853276.pdf); [Gerrish Fine Art](https://gerrishfineart.com/exhibition/the-meditation-plates-olga-frobe-kapteyn/))

- **c. 1926–1938:** *Blue Book*. The modern name for Fröbe-Kapteyn’s privately preserved anthology of Meditation Plates and Visions. It is simultaneously album, visual diary, archive, and retrospective ordering of her artistic corpus—not a conventional published book—and its boundaries should not be confused with those of either constituent series. ([Eranos Foundation](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/project.php?proj=140))

- **1934–1938:** *Visions*. Approximately 300 mixed-media drawings, most dated and organized by Fröbe-Kapteyn into numbered albums or series, made through Jungian “active imagination.” They function as a psychological diary in pictures and therefore resist separation into fully independent titled works; many remain untitled, and the surviving correspondence and notes concerning them have not been exhaustively studied. ([Kunsthalle Mainz guide](https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/39535/docfile/7abfbb97ea126f2c873327c728853276.pdf); [Eranos Foundation](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/project.php?proj=140))

- **c. 1930:** *Complete Set of Theosophical Prints* / *Meditation Drawing Screenprints*. A portfolio of fourteen color screenprints commissioned from Vincenzo Bona in Turin after Meditation Plate originals. The edition size is undocumented—often estimated at about one hundred sets—and some source paintings were subsequently lost. ([Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/900081); [Kunsthalle Mainz guide](https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/39535/docfile/7abfbb97ea126f2c873327c728853276.pdf))

### Writing and private circulation

- **1932:** *Meditation Plates* introductory text. A short explanatory text associated with the plates and Fröbe-Kapteyn’s lectures; later scholarship cites it, but the surviving publication history and exact bibliographical form remain insufficiently established to treat it confidently as a conventional book. ([Religiographies](https://www.cini.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Religiographies-v.4-n.1.pdf))

- **1933:** *Symbolische Geschichten*. An unpublished German typescript of symbolic short narratives, privately circulated in only a few copies and accompanied in at least one copy by photographs of Meditation Plates. It is a made textual object but not a formally published book; its rarity and private distribution make completeness difficult to establish. ([Bernard Quaritch catalogue](https://www.quaritch.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Quaritch-California-2026.pdf))

- **1933–1961:** Prefaces, introductory notes, correspondence, and working notes connected with Eranos. These are a dispersed textual practice rather than one fixed work; portions survive in the Eranos documentary archive, but Fröbe-Kapteyn’s letters and notes have not been exhaustively examined or published, so no complete item-level list is presently supportable. ([Kunsthalle Mainz guide](https://cdn.contemporaryartlibrary.org/store/doc/39535/docfile/7abfbb97ea126f2c873327c728853276.pdf); [Eranos Foundation documentary collection](https://www.eranosfoundation.org/page.php?page=20&pagename=documentary+collection))

### Edited manifestations: Eranos yearbooks

- **1933; published 1934:** *Yoga und Meditation im Osten und im Westen*. Eranos-Jahrbuch I, edited by Fröbe-Kapteyn from the first Eranos conference papers; an editorial and curatorial obra rather than a sole-authored book.

- **1934; published 1935:** *Ostwestliche Symbolik und Seelenführung*. Eranos-Jahrbuch II, edited conference proceedings.

- **1935; published 1936:** *Westöstliche Seelenführung*. Eranos-Jahrbuch III, edited conference proceedings.

- **1936; published 1937:** *Gestaltung der Erlösungsidee in Ost und West (1)*. Eranos-Jahrbuch IV, edited conference proceedings; a later 1986 supplementary volume was edited by Rudolf Ritsema and is not Fröbe-Kapteyn’s work.

- **1937; published 1938:** *Gestaltung der Erlösungsidee in Ost und West (2)*. Eranos-Jahrbuch V, edited conference proceedings.

- **1938; published 1939:** *Gestalt und Kult der “Grossen Mutter”*. Eranos-Jahrbuch VI, edited conference proceedings integrated with Fröbe-Kapteyn’s Great Mother image research.

- **1939; published 1940:** *Vorträge über die Symbolik der Wiedergeburt in der religiösen Vorstellung der Zeiten und Völker*. Eranos-Jahrbuch VII, edited conference proceedings.

- **1940–1941; published 1942:** *Trinität, christliche Symbolik und Gnosis*. Eranos-Jahrbuch VIII, combining papers from two wartime conferences.

- **1942; published 1943:** *Das hermetische Prinzip in Mythologie, Gnosis und Alchemie*. Eranos-Jahrbuch IX, edited conference proceedings.

- **1943; published 1944:** *Alte Sonnenkulte und die Lichtsymbolik in der Gnosis und im frühen Christentum*. Eranos-Jahrbuch X, edited conference proceedings.

- **1944; published 1945:** *Die Mysterien*. Eranos-Jahrbuch XI, edited conference proceedings.

- **1945:** *Studien zum Probleme des Archetypischen*. Eranos-Jahrbuch XII, conceived as a seventieth-birthday Festschrift for C. G. Jung rather than the record of the regular annual conference.

- **1945; published 1946:** *Der Geist*. Eranos-Jahrbuch XIII, edited conference proceedings.

- **1946; published 1947:** *Geist und Natur*. Eranos-Jahrbuch XIV, edited conference proceedings.

- **1947; published 1948:** *Der Mensch (Erste Folge)*. Eranos-Jahrbuch XV, edited conference proceedings.

### Applied art and objects of uncertain survival

- **Date unknown:** Embroidered binding for a book by André Germain. A work of textile and book art reported in Caterina Beretta’s memoir; no secure title, date, present location, or complete object record has been established, so it cannot responsibly be treated as a catalogued book.

- **Date unknown:** Wax Nativity scene. An applied-art object described by Caterina Beretta as a remarkable wax crib; its date, title, dimensions, and survival are unknown. These two objects resist inclusion in the securely catalogued oeuvre because they are known from testimony rather than the current scholarly inventory. ([Biographical notice citing Caterina Beretta](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Fr%C3%B6be-Kapteyn))
