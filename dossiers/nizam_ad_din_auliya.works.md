## Works by Nizam ad-Din Auliya

### Recorded oral discourse

- **1308–1322:** *Fawāʾid al-fuʾād* (“Benefits of the Heart”). A Persian *malfūẓāt*—188 dated assemblies containing Nizam ad-Din’s conversation, teaching, anecdotes, and responses—written down and shaped into five parts by his disciple Amir Hasan Sijzi; it is therefore a collaboratively transmitted manifestation of his speech, not a book he personally authored. Scholars treat it as the principal and most reliable record of his teaching, while the compilation itself belongs to Hasan Sijzi; tradition also says Nizam ad-Din reviewed and corrected portions of the record. ([Khaliq Ahmad Nizami](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1370500M/The_Life_and_times_of_Shaikh_Nizam-u%27d-din_Auliya); [bibliographical study](https://isamveri.org/pdfdkm/14/DKM140522.pdf); [Fons Vitae](https://fonsvitae.com/product/a-mine-of-meaning-madin-ul-maani-discourses-of-sheikh-sharafuddin-maneri-as-recorded-by-zain-badr-arabi/))

- **Date unknown, before 1325?:** *Durar-i Niẓāmī* (“Nizami Pearls”). An undated collection of Nizam ad-Din’s conversations compiled by his disciple ʿAli Jandar; it survives obscurely and lacks the dated, sequential documentation of *Fawāʾid al-fuʾād*. It should be classed as attributed transmitted speech rather than Nizam ad-Din’s authored text, and its completeness and precise date cannot presently be established. ([Encyclopedia of Religion bibliography](https://www.encyclopedia.com/environment/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/nizam-al-din-awliya); [study of *malfūẓāt* transmission](https://blog.sufinama.org/malfuzat-an-analysis-of-the-criticism-on-anees-ul-arwah/))

### Disputed manifestations of his speech

- **Date unknown; first printed 1887:** *Afżal al-fawāʾid* (“The Best of Benefits”). Ostensibly a record by Amir Khusrau of conversations with Nizam ad-Din, it would be another collaborative oral-textual object rather than an autograph work; modern scholarship, however, regards its attribution to Khusrau as inauthentic or forged, so it cannot securely document something Nizam ad-Din made. ([Bruce B. Lawrence, “Afzal ul-Fawa’id—A Reassessment”](https://duke.academia.edu/Departments/Religion/Documents); [discussion and bibliography](https://www.scribd.com/document/573716333/The-Intersection-between-Sufism-and-Powe))

- **Date unknown:** *Rāḥat al-muḥibbīn* (“Comfort of the Lovers”). This title is attributed to a disciple’s record of Nizam ad-Din’s assemblies, but its compiler, date, textual history, and authenticity are uncertain; scholars have grouped it with later or doubtful *malfūẓāt*. It resists inclusion as his work because even the underlying speech cannot presently be separated securely from later compilation. ([Khaliq Ahmad Nizami](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1370500M/The_Life_and_times_of_Shaikh_Nizam-u%27d-din_Auliya); [survey of the attributed corpus](https://www.kahkashan-e-marefat.com/en/articles/khwaja-nizamuddin-auliya-may-allah-have-mercy-on-him-sultan-ul-mashayikh-and-the-beloved-of-allah-en))

### Social and architectural projects

- **Late thirteenth century–1325:** *The Ghiyaspur khānqāh*. A hospice, teaching circle, communal kitchen, devotional assembly, and network of disciples established and directed by Nizam ad-Din: an institutional obra—or Gesamtkunstwerk of dwelling, food distribution, teaching, ritual, and social relations—rather than a single text or securely reconstructable building. The original complex should not be confused with his posthumous tomb or with the repeatedly altered present dargah. ([Khaliq Ahmad Nizami](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL1370500M/The_Life_and_times_of_Shaikh_Nizam-u%27d-din_Auliya); [National Monuments Authority](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=2&level=2&lid=67&ls_id=76&nma_type=0); [Columbia University study](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/cujsas/Volume%20I/Issue%20II/Michael%20Snyder%20-%20Where%20Delhi.pdf))

- **Traditionally 1320–1322; attribution disputed:** *The baoli at Ghiyaspur*. A spring-fed stepwell traditionally said to have been constructed under Nizam ad-Din during his conflict with Sultan Ghiyas al-Din Tughluq, making it a communal-water project rather than a text. Conservation authorities date it to his lifetime, but architectural research notes that the earliest near-contemporary accounts do not mention it at his gravesite and consequently argues that the surviving structure may be posthumous; patronage, date, and the extent of original fabric therefore remain unsettled. ([Nizamuddin Urban Renewal Initiative](https://www.nizamuddinrenewal.org/conservation/nizamuddin-baoli/); [National Monuments Authority](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=2&level=2&lid=67&ls_id=76&nma_type=0); [Keshani, architectural-historical study](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/bitstreams/f059e346-8efd-45b6-99cd-c8a5d55c7e7a/download))
