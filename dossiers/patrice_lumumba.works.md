## Works by Patrice Émery Lumumba

### Journalism and editorial projects

- **1948–1956:** *Contributions to La Croix du Congo*. A corpus of approximately sixty-two or sixty-three signed reports, notices, opinion pieces, and association communications, beginning in 1948 or 1950 according to differing counts and corpus boundaries; Jean-Marie Mutamba Makombo’s scholarly inventory treats these individual journalistic objects as Lumumba’s earliest sustained body of writing, but not every contribution possessed a stable title. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf); [Mutamba Makombo](https://www.editions-harmattan.fr/catalogue/livre/patrice-lumumba-correspondant-de-presse-1948-1956/56115)

- **August 1948–December 1958:** *Contributions to La Voix du Congolais*. Thirteen contributions are identified by Omasombo and Verhaegen, whereas accounts based on a narrower period sometimes give twelve; many are reports or communiqués concerning the associations AES, ADAPES, APIC, and APIPO, and some duplicate material printed in *La Croix du Congo*. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1950:** *Obituary of a former Marist pupil and postal clerk*. A short journalistic memorial published in *La Croix du Congo* on 3 September; its descriptive title is editorial because the scholarship does not supply a fixed original title. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1950:** *Questions addressed to the editors of La Croix du Congo*. A reader’s intervention published on 24 December, structured as questions about clerical customs and racial segregation; the wording functions less as a conventional article than as a public interrogation of colonial and ecclesiastical arrangements. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1951–1956:** *L’Écho postal*. An editorial and associational project: Lumumba was editor-in-chief and the most prolific contributor to this quarterly organ of the Amicale des postiers indigènes de la Province orientale. It resists reduction to a single authored work because it was simultaneously a periodical, an institutional voice, and a collective production. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1951:** *Advice to the members of the Association des évolués de Stanleyville*. A moral and educational address published in *La Voix du Congolais* in August; the title here describes the untitled or variably titled periodical contribution. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1955:** *Les races humaines*. A serialized popular essay in *L’Écho postal*, based in part on UNESCO material, rejecting ideas of racial purity and innate intellectual inequality; its dependence on an institutional source makes it an adaptation and pedagogical synthesis rather than wholly original research. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1955:** *L’accès du Congolais dans les établissements publics pour Européens*. A lecture delivered at Stanleyville on 18 August and subsequently printed in two September issues of *La Croix du Congo* and the October *Voix du Congolais*; it is one address manifested in several periodical objects, not several separate works. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1955:** *Bilan des activités de l’APIPO en 1955*. An editorial in *L’Écho postal* reviewing the association’s year; the descriptive title is supplied because the scholarly account does not establish a fixed printed title. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1955–1956:** *Report and address for the APIPO Saint-Nicolas celebration*. An association report incorporating Lumumba’s ceremonial address of 3 December 1955, printed in *L’Écho postal*, *L’Afrique et le Monde*, and *La Croix du Congo* on 1 January 1956; the surviving manifestations blur report, speech, and reprint. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **from 1955:** *Contributions to Le Stanleyvillois, L’Écho de Stan, and L’Afrique et le Monde*. A dispersed journalistic corpus identified by the scholarly biography in addition to Lumumba’s better-catalogued newspaper work; its completeness and individual titles remain insufficiently established, so it resists honest item-by-item enumeration. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

### Book-length political obra

- **1956–1957; published posthumously 1961:** *Le Congo, terre d’avenir, est-il menacé?* A book-length political manuscript, also issued in translation as *Congo, My Country*, composed before Lumumba’s later demand for immediate independence and therefore preserving an earlier, more gradualist project. Its posthumous publication history and editorial mediation should not be confused with a book issued by Lumumba himself during his lifetime. [Omasombo and Verhaegen bibliography](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Construire%20notre%20r%C3%A9publique%20-%20introduction%20%C3%A0%20la%20pens%C3%A9e%20politique%20congolaise%20-%20J.%20Kasa-Vubu%2C%20P.%20Lumumba%2C%20J.%20Mobutu%2C%20L.-D.%20Kabila.pdf); [contemporary review of *Congo, My Country*](https://www.marxists.org/history/etol/newspape/isr/vol24/no02/v24n02-w163-spring-1963-int-soc-rev.pdf)

### Poem

- **September 1959:** *Pleure, ô Noir, frère bien-aimé*. A poem published in the MNC newspaper *Indépendance* and subsequently incorporated into Jean Van Lierde’s collected edition; it is a literary work rather than a speech, even though later political anthologies often place it among Lumumba’s public texts. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

### Institutions and collective political projects

- **1955:** *Association du personnel indigène de la colonie (APIC)*. An organization Lumumba helped create: an institutional project rather than a text, included because founding an association is among the things he made politically. Its precise authorship was collective. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **1951–1956:** *Amicale des postiers indigènes de la Province orientale (APIPO)*. An associational project led and publicly articulated by Lumumba, with statutes of twenty-four articles approved in 1951; the organization and its statutes were collective creations and should not be assigned to him as sole author. [Omasombo and Verhaegen](https://haulet.be/livres/congo/Patrice%20Lumumba%20-%20Jeunesse%20et%20apprentissage%20politique%2C%201925-1956%20-%20B.%20Verhaegen%20%26%20J.%20Omasombo%20Tshonda.pdf)

- **October 1958:** *Mouvement national congolais (MNC)*. A nationwide political organization co-founded by Lumumba and others; as a project it sought to make a unified, non-ethnic Congolese political constituency, but it cannot properly be called Lumumba’s sole work. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **1959–1960:** *Indépendance*. The MNC newspaper in which Lumumba published and through which the party acted publicly; it is a collective editorial manifestation rather than an individually authored book. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **1959–1960:** *Uhuru*. A party newspaper associated with Lumumba’s political organization and direction; surviving descriptions credit him with overseeing it, but not with sole authorship of its whole contents. [Progress Publishers biographical account](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/ussr/fighters-nat-lib.pdf)

### Manifestos, resolutions, and programmatic texts

- **6 September 1959:** *Resolution of the General Assembly of the Mouvement national congolais*. A collectively adopted resolution, presided over by Lumumba, demanding a Congolese government and opposing colonial elections; it acts as a manifesto, but its corporate adoption prevents secure attribution to Lumumba alone. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **September 1959:** *Open letter announcing the MNC’s refusal to cooperate with Belgium*. A manifesto-like public letter that turns non-cooperation into political action; references confirm its existence, although its precise printed title varies or is not supplied. [Workers Liberty historical account](https://www.marxists.org/history/etol/newspape/wl/308.pdf)

- **31 March 1960:** *Message de Patrice Lumumba à la population du Congo*. A mass political message delivered during the transition to independence; it is preserved as a discrete archival text by the Royal Museum for Central Africa. [AfricaMuseum Archives](https://archives.africamuseum.be/repositories/3/archival_objects/81486)

- **10 September 1960:** *Solemn Appeal to the President and Members of the Security Council and to All Member States of the United Nations*. A manifesto in diplomatic form, intended to compel international recognition and action after Lumumba’s dismissal. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

### Speeches, lectures, and broadcast manifestations

- **11 December 1958:** *Speech at the All-African Peoples’ Conference, Accra*. A pan-African conference speech presenting Congolese independence as part of continental liberation; Van Lierde begins his principal collected corpus with this period. [Africa Commons](https://africacommons.net/artifacts/3159054/1958-patrice-lumumba-speech-at-accra/); [Van Lierde](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **28 December 1958:** *Allocution de Patrice Lumumba à Léopoldville*. A public address following the Accra conference, preserved as a separate archival document; the archive supplies a descriptive rather than literary title. [AfricaMuseum Archives](https://archives.africamuseum.be/repositories/3/archival_objects/81486)

- **22 March 1959:** *Speech at the closing session of the International Seminar at Ibadan*. A lecture or conference speech on African unity and the common struggle against colonial rule, often retitled from its concluding appeal; its circulated titles are editorial. [Speakola source record](https://speakola.com/political/category/1940-59)

- **April 1959:** *Speech in Brussels on the Congo’s political future*. A public question-and-answer address setting out a timetable for Congolese government and continued negotiated relations with Belgium; the collected edition prints it as “Speech, April 1959,” without a securely fixed authorial title. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **9 October 1959:** *Address to the Executive Committee of the Pan-African Conference, Accra*. A political report and appeal concerning the Congolese independence struggle; it is a conference intervention rather than an independently issued essay. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **27 January 1960:** *Declaration at the Belgo-Congolese Round Table Conference*. A negotiating statement delivered by Lumumba as national president of the MNC and preserved in the AfricaMuseum documentary collection. [AfricaMuseum Archives](https://archives.africamuseum.be/repositories/3/archival_objects/81486)

- **20 February 1960:** *Statement at the Closing Session of the Belgo-Congolese Round Table Conference*. A formal conference statement marking the negotiated transition to independence. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **28 June 1960:** *Radio address by the Prime Minister of the Republic of the Congo*. A national broadcast delivered two days before independence and preserved as a distinct archival object. [AfricaMuseum Archives](https://archives.africamuseum.be/repositories/3/archival_objects/81486)

- **30 June 1960:** *Discours à la cérémonie de la proclamation de l’indépendance du Congo*. A ceremonial speech that also acts as an anticolonial manifesto; Lumumba was not originally scheduled to speak, and early editions and later scholarship use several variant titles, so “Independence Speech” is conventional rather than certainly authorial. [Marxists Internet Archive](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/1960/06/independence1.htm); [AfricaMuseum recording and study guide](https://lumumbacenter.org/study)

- **22 July 1960:** *Interview with a France-Soir correspondent*. An interview in which Lumumba answers accusations that he was a Communist; it is a jointly produced journalistic object because the interviewer’s questions determine its form. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **28 July 1960:** *Interview with TASS*. A political interview addressing the Congo crisis and foreign intervention; as an interview, it resists classification as a solely authored text. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **16 August 1960:** *Statement at a press conference in Léopoldville*. An oral political statement made during the Congo crisis and preserved through press transcription. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **17 August 1960:** *Statement at a press conference in Léopoldville*. A second crisis statement whose textual form depends upon journalistic recording rather than an authorial publication. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **19 August 1960:** *Statement at a press conference in Léopoldville*. A third separately documented press-conference statement. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **August 1960:** *Address to Congolese Youth*. A public exhortation conventionally dated only to August in the documentary corpus; no more precise date should be manufactured. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **25 August 1960:** *Opening Speech at the All-African Conference in Léopoldville*. A pan-African diplomatic address opening the conference and proposing continental solidarity amid the Congo crisis. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **31 August 1960:** *Concluding Speech at the All-African Conference in Léopoldville*. The conference’s closing address, calling for African unity to become political action. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **5 September 1960:** *Radio Broadcast Message*. A broadcast response to President Joseph Kasa-Vubu’s attempt to dismiss Lumumba; as spoken political action it is both a text and a manifestation in sound. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

### Diplomatic correspondence and prison letters

- **July–August 1960:** *Correspondence with United Nations Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld*. A sequence of letters and telegrams concerning Belgian intervention, Katanga, and the UN operation; it is a correspondence project rather than one stable text, and the collected selections do not necessarily represent every exchanged document. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **1 August 1960:** *Letter to the President of the United Nations Security Council*. A diplomatic demand concerning the Congo crisis; some editions reproduce only an extract, so the commonly circulated text is not necessarily the complete letter. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **1 August 1960:** *Telegram to the President of the United Nations Security Council*. An urgent diplomatic communication distinct from the letter of the same date; editions again often print only an extract. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **11 November 1960:** *Letter to the President of the United Nations General Assembly*. A diplomatic letter written while Lumumba was under restriction; the documentary edition circulates an excerpt, and its completeness should not be assumed. [Marxists Internet Archive document index](https://www.marxists.org/subject/africa/lumumba/)

- **late November or December 1960:** *Last letter to Pauline Lumumba*. A prison letter addressed to his wife and transformed posthumously into a political testament; neither its exact date nor the label “last letter” is entirely secure, and translations differ, so it should be understood as a private communication whose later publication made it a manifesto-like public object. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf); [BnF/Takamtikou description](https://takamtikou.bnf.fr/bibliographies/notices/afrique/patrice-lumumba)

### Posthumous editorial creations—not works made by Lumumba as books

- **1963:** *La pensée politique de Patrice Lumumba*. A posthumous collected-works edition assembled and presented by Jean Van Lierde, with a preface by Jean-Paul Sartre; Lumumba made the constituent speeches and documents, but he did not make this anthology, its title, selection, or arrangement. Van Lierde describes it as containing almost all the important speeches and lectures from December 1958 to January 1961, not every surviving utterance or paper. [CCFr/Bibliothèque nationale de France record](https://ccfr.bnf.fr/portailccfr/ark%3A/16871/00110365223); [Van Lierde’s preface](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)

- **1972:** *Lumumba Speaks: The Speeches and Writings of Patrice Lumumba, 1958–1961*. Helen R. Lane’s English translation of Van Lierde’s collection; this is a later manifestation of the collected corpus, not a new book authored and organized by Lumumba. [Van Lierde, *Lumumba Speaks*](https://www.rahs-open-lid.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Lumumba-Speaks_-The-Speeches-and-Writings-of-Patrice-Lumumba-1958-1961-PDFDrive-.pdf)
