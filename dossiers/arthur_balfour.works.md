## Works by Arthur James Balfour

### Philosophical and theological books

- **1879:** *A Defence of Philosophic Doubt: Being an Essay on the Foundations of Belief*. A philosophical monograph attacking claims that scientific or empirical belief rests on uniquely rational foundations; it is Balfour’s first major book, not merely a later edition or extract. ([Gifford Lectures archive](https://giffordarchives.org/lecturers/arthur-james-balfour); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1895:** *The Foundations of Belief: Being Notes Introductory to the Study of Theology*. A systematic philosophical-theological book that developed rather than simply reprinted the argument of *A Defence of Philosophic Doubt*. Some bibliographies give 1896 because of later or overseas manifestations, but the first London edition is dated 1895. ([Gifford Lectures archive](https://giffordarchives.org/lecturers/arthur-james-balfour); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1914–1915:** *Theism and Humanism*. A project first manifested as Balfour’s 1914 Gifford Lectures at Glasgow and then as a book in 1915; the lecture-series and book are related manifestations of the same philosophical obra, not two independent works. ([Gifford Lectures archive](https://giffordarchives.org/lecturers/arthur-james-balfour); [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/57773/pg57773-images.html))
- **1922–1923:** *Theism and Thought: A Study in Familiar Beliefs*. Balfour’s second Glasgow Gifford course, delivered in 1922–23 and published in 1923; it is both a lecture project and a philosophical book. ([Gifford Lectures archive](https://giffordarchives.org/lecturers/arthur-james-balfour))
- **1925–1926:** *Familiar Beliefs and Transcendent Reason*. The Hertz Lecture delivered to the British Academy in 1925 and issued as an eighteen-page pamphlet in 1926; it is better classified as a separately manifested lecture than as a full-length book. ([British Academy memoir](https://www.thebritishacademy.ac.uk/documents/4562/16p410.pdf); [Cambridge review record](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031819100015540))

### Literary, philosophical, and public addresses

- **1887–1888:** *The Pleasures of Reading*. A rectorial address delivered at St Andrews University on 10 December 1887 and published separately in 1888; later collections sometimes call it “The Benefits of Reading,” but the original pamphlet title is *The Pleasures of Reading*. ([Antiquarian Booksellers’ Association catalogue](https://aba.org.uk/assets/catalogues/Nineteenth-Century.pdf); [Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1888:** *The Religion of Humanity*. An address delivered to the Church Congress at Manchester and published as a pamphlet; its first life as an occasion-specific speech remains part of what the text is. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1904:** *Reflections Suggested by the New Theory of Matter*. The presidential address delivered to the British Association for the Advancement of Science at Cambridge on 17 August 1904, subsequently issued as a pamphlet. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1908:** *Decadence*. The Henry Sidgwick Memorial Lecture delivered at Newnham College on 25 January 1908 and printed that year; it is an interrogative historical essay whose lecture and pamphlet forms are manifestations of one work. ([Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/68219/68219-h/68219-h.htm))
- **1909:** *Questionings on Criticism and Beauty*. The initial printed manifestation associated with Balfour’s Romanes Lecture; its relationship to the substantially rewritten text of 1910 means it resists treatment as either wholly independent or merely another edition. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1909–1910:** *Criticism and Beauty: A Lecture Rewritten*. A rewritten obra derived from the 1909 Romanes Lecture and published in 1910; Balfour’s subtitle explicitly marks the revision as more than a transcript of the event. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1925:** *Introduction* to Joseph Needham’s *Science, Religion and Reality*. A contributed introductory essay, not a Balfour-authored book: the volume itself is a multi-author construction edited by Needham. ([Smithsonian Libraries record](https://www.si.edu/object/science-religion-and-reality-arthur-james-earl-balfour-et-al-edited-joseph-needham%3Asiris_sil_806109))

### Psychical-research papers and addresses

- **1888:** *Some Remarks on Professor Richet’s Experiments on the Possibility of Clairvoyant Perception of Drawings*. A journal paper reporting and assessing psychical-research experiments; it is a research communication rather than a book. ([Psi Encyclopedia](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/articles/arthur-balfour/))
- **1894:** *Reflections on the Scientific Investigation of Hypnotic Mental States*. A short paper in the *Journal of the Society for Psychical Research*. ([Psi Encyclopedia](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/articles/arthur-balfour/))
- **1894:** *Presidential Address*. Balfour’s address to the Society for Psychical Research, published in its *Proceedings*; its generic title belongs to an institutional occasion and should not be silently converted into a more distinctive invented title. ([Psi Encyclopedia](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/articles/arthur-balfour/))

### Political arguments, speeches, and pamphlets

- **1903:** *Economic Notes on Insular Free Trade*. A fiscal-policy pamphlet advancing an argument rather than neutrally reporting one; later it was incorporated into *Fiscal Reform*. ([CTHS, “Balfour Arthur James”](https://cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=116978); [1913 publisher’s list](https://www.scribd.com/document/1032924718/1913-Nationality-and-Home-Rule))
- **1905:** *Imperial Defence*. A corrected publication of Balfour’s House of Commons speech of 11 May 1905; the made thing lies between parliamentary act and authorized pamphlet. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1906:** *Fiscal Reform*. A constructed collection of Balfour’s speeches from 1880–1905, *Economic Notes on Insular Free Trade*, correspondence with Joseph Chamberlain, and a new preface. Balfour made its component arguments, but the volume is also an editorial assemblage and should not be mistaken for a single newly written treatise. ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1913:** *Nationality and Home Rule*. A political pamphlet reworking, “with slight alterations,” an article first published in the Montreal *University Magazine*; it is a polemical intervention against Irish Home Rule rather than a protocol. ([digitized 1913 text](https://www.scribd.com/document/1032924718/1913-Nationality-and-Home-Rule); [CTHS](https://cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=116978))
- **12 December 1914:** *Speech at the Great Mass Meeting, Colston Hall, Bristol*. A wartime recruiting speech supporting the War Office appeal for additional soldiers; the speech survives through later publication and is an oratorical act rather than a freestanding book. ([Wikisource author record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Arthur_James_Balfour))
- **5 May 1917:** *The Oldest Free Assemblies*. An address delivered before the United States House of Representatives; its title names a speech subsequently preserved as a text. ([Wikisource author record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Arthur_James_Balfour))
- **27 February 1918:** *Response to Woodrow Wilson’s Speech of 11 February 1918*. A diplomatic-parliamentary response whose descriptive title is supplied by the documentary context rather than by a conventional title page. ([Wikisource author record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Arthur_James_Balfour))
- **3 June 1918:** *Letter to Edvard Beneš*. A diplomatic letter, not a “work” in the usual literary sense; it nevertheless resists exclusion from an atlas of made texts because it was a purposeful political communication. ([Wikisource author record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Arthur_James_Balfour))

### Manifestos, declarations, and collective state texts

- **2 November 1917:** *The Balfour Declaration*. A manifesto-like diplomatic letter: it acts by publicly announcing British government policy rather than merely describing it. Balfour signed and issued it to Walter Rothschild as foreign secretary, but the operative declaration was submitted to and approved by the Cabinet and emerged from several Zionist and governmental drafts; sole authorship should therefore not be assigned to Balfour. ([TIME account of the drafts](https://time.com/4702147/balfour-drafts-1917/); [Wikisource author record](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Arthur_James_Balfour))
- **1923:** *Recommendations of the National and Imperial Defence Committee … upon the Relations of the Navy and the Air Force, and the Co-ordination of the Defence Forces*. A governmental protocol or policy report associated with Balfour and the Committee of Imperial Defence, not an individually authored literary obra; its corporate authorship resists inclusion among “Balfour’s books.” ([Online Books Page](https://onlinebooks.library.upenn.edu/webbin/book/lookupname?key=Balfour%2C+Arthur+James%2C+1848-1930))
- **1926:** *Inter-Imperial Relations Committee Report* (“Balfour Declaration of 1926”). A collectively produced constitutional formula defining Britain and the Dominions as autonomous and equal in status. Balfour chaired the committee, but the report is a negotiated state text rather than his sole composition; the familiar title is retrospective shorthand and can be confused with the unrelated 1917 declaration. ([Oxford Public International Law overview](https://opil.ouplaw.com/display/10.1093/law:epil/9780199231690/law-9780199231690-e1084); [Encyclopaedia Britannica, “Balfour Report”](https://www.britannica.com/event/Balfour-Report))

### Collections made from earlier texts

- **1893:** *Essays and Addresses*. An authorial collection joining seven previously made essays and speeches: “The Pleasures of Reading,” “Bishop Berkeley’s Life and Letters,” “Handel,” “Cobden and the Manchester School,” “Politics and Political Economy,” “A Fragment on Progress,” and “The Religion of Humanity.” The collection is itself an obra of selection and arrangement, while its components retain their earlier identities. ([Google Books contents](https://books.google.co.ls/books?id=w7nM1pj5iA8C); [PhilPapers record](https://philpapers.org/rec/BALEAA-4))
- **1883:** *Bishop Berkeley’s Life and Letters*. A critical essay first published in the *National Review* and later collected in *Essays and Addresses*. ([contemporary-text study and source table](https://www.iast.fr/sites/default/files/IAST/IAST_V1/Conferences/elect/papers/spirling.pdf))
- **1887:** *Handel*. An essay first published in the *Edinburgh Review* and later collected in *Essays and Addresses*. ([contemporary-text study and source table](https://www.iast.fr/sites/default/files/IAST/IAST_V1/Conferences/elect/papers/spirling.pdf))
- **1882:** *Cobden and the Manchester School*. A political-historical essay first published in the *Nineteenth Century* and later collected in *Essays and Addresses*. ([contemporary-text study and source table](https://www.iast.fr/sites/default/files/IAST/IAST_V1/Conferences/elect/papers/spirling.pdf))
- **1885:** *Politics and Political Economy*. An essay first published in the *National Review* and later collected in *Essays and Addresses*. ([contemporary-text study and source table](https://www.iast.fr/sites/default/files/IAST/IAST_V1/Conferences/elect/papers/spirling.pdf))
- **1891:** *A Fragment on Progress*. An address delivered at Glasgow University and later collected in *Essays and Addresses*; “Fragment” is Balfour’s title and status-marker, not evidence that the surviving text is bibliographically defective. ([contemporary-text study and source table](https://www.iast.fr/sites/default/files/IAST/IAST_V1/Conferences/elect/papers/spirling.pdf))
- **1912:** *Arthur James Balfour as Philosopher and Thinker*. A selection of passages from Balfour’s non-political writings, speeches, and addresses, arranged by Wilfrid M. Short. It is not a newly composed Balfour book: Balfour made the source texts, while Short made the anthology. ([Gifford Lectures archive](https://giffordarchives.org/lecturers/arthur-james-balfour); [Rasor bibliography description](https://www.bloomsbury.com/ca/arthur-james-balfour-18481930-9780313288777/))
- **1920:** *Essays, Speculative and Political*. An authorial collection whose original London edition is dated 1920; a New York manifestation is dated 1921. Its contents combine previously delivered or published speculative and political essays rather than constituting a single continuous treatise. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf); [CTHS](https://cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=116978))
- *Beauty and the Criticism of Beauty*. A speculative essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political* and related to Balfour’s Romanes-lecture project; because the collected form may embody revision, it should not automatically be equated word-for-word with either *Questionings on Criticism and Beauty* or *Criticism and Beauty*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *Bergson’s “Creative Evolution”*. A philosophical critical essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *Francis Bacon*. A historical-philosophical essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *Psychical Research*. An essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*; it should not be confused with Balfour’s separately published 1888 and 1894 psychical-research papers. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *Anglo-German Relations*. A political essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *Treitschke’s View of German World-Policy*. A critical political essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *The Freedom of the Seas*. A political essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *The Foundations of a Durable Peace*. A political essay incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf))
- *A Brief Note on Zionism*. Balfour’s introduction to Nahum Sokolow’s *History of Zionism*, subsequently incorporated into *Essays, Speculative and Political*; it is a contributed paratext, not a separately authored history of Zionism. ([Wikimedia scan and contents](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Essays,_speculative_and_political_(IA_cu31924031042736).pdf); [Palestine Return Centre bibliography](https://prc.org.uk/upload/library/files/Balfour1CCNewSS.pdf))
- **1927:** *Opinions and Arguments from Speeches and Addresses of the Earl of Balfour, 1910–1927*. A retrospective collection of public discourse, not one newly composed argument; its status depends on selection and arrangement as well as Balfour’s original speeches. ([Palestine Return Centre bibliography](https://prc.org.uk/upload/library/files/Balfour1CCNewSS.pdf); [CTHS](https://cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=116978))
- **1928:** *Speeches on Zionism*. An Israel Cohen-edited collection containing the 1917 declaration and addresses titled “Great Britain and Palestine,” “Zionism in America,” “A Defence of the Mandate,” “The Ratification of the Mandate,” “The Hebrew University,” “Aspects of the Jewish National Home,” “Ten Years After,” and “Retrospect.” Balfour made or delivered the component texts, but Cohen made the volume as a collection. ([Heywood Hill catalogue](https://www.heywoodhill.com/pdf/autumn21_complete.pdf); [CTHS](https://cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=116978))

### Unfinished autobiographical project

- **1928–1930:** *Chapters of Autobiography* / *Retrospect: An Unfinished Autobiography, 1848–1886*. Balfour began this autobiographical project in 1928, but illness and death left it unfinished and reaching only 1886; Blanche E. C. Dugdale edited it posthumously in 1930. The British and American titles are alternative manifestations of the same incomplete obra, and the editorially finished books must not be mistaken for a completed autobiography by Balfour. ([Google Books record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Retrospect.html?id=CI3w0QEACAAJ); [Gifford Lectures archive](https://giffordarchives.org/lecturers/arthur-james-balfour); [publication history](https://thetelos.org/retrospect-an-unfinished-autobiography-1848-1886-arthur-balfour/))

### Correspondence as a dispersed textual corpus

- **1869–1892:** *Letters exchanged with the third Marquess of Salisbury*. These are individually made communications rather than a single Balfour obra; the surviving corpus was only constituted as *Salisbury–Balfour Correspondence* by later editors. ([Rasor bibliography description](https://www.bloomsbury.com/ca/arthur-james-balfour-18481930-9780313288777/); [bibliographical notice](https://www.cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=116978))
- **1885–1917:** *Letters to Lady Elcho*. Again, the letters are Balfour’s texts but the bounded work called *The Letters of Arthur Balfour and Lady Elcho* is a posthumous editorial construction by Jane Ridley and Clayre Percy; treating it as a book “written” by Balfour would erase that distinction. ([Rasor bibliography description](https://www.bloomsbury.com/ca/arthur-james-balfour-18481930-9780313288777/); [Psi Encyclopedia](https://open-data.spr.ac.uk/articles/arthur-balfour/))
