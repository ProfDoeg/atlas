## Works by Jacinto Molina Álvarez

### Screenplays and screen projects

- **1968:** *La marca del hombre lobo*. Feature-film screenplay, credited to Molina under his professional name Paul Naschy; it introduces Waldemar Daninsky, the tragic werewolf who became his recurring creation. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1968:** *Las noches del hombre lobo*. Screenplay for a proposed second Daninsky film. Naschy maintained that René Govar shot it, but no print, release record, or independently verified production evidence survives; it may be a lost film, an unfinished project, or a film that was never made. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1970:** *Los monstruos del terror*. Screenplay—also circulated through such titles as *Dracula contra Frankenstein* and *Assignment Terror*—bringing Daninsky into an ensemble of classic monsters. The production underwent changes of director and title, so the released film is not simply the unaltered manifestation of Molina’s script. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1971:** *La noche de Walpurgis*. Screenplay for León Klimovsky’s Daninsky film, internationally retitled *The Werewolf versus the Vampire Woman* and by numerous other names. The obra is the screenplay and its filmed manifestation, not the proliferating release titles. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1971:** *La furia del hombre lobo*. Daninsky screenplay filmed by José María Zabalza. Conflicting edits and production difficulties make the surviving feature an unstable manifestation of Molina’s text. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1972:** *Dr. Jekyll y el hombre lobo*. Screenplay combining Stevenson’s Jekyll-and-Hyde structure with Molina’s continuing Daninsky cycle. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1972:** *El espanto surge de la tumba*. Horror screenplay creating the medieval sorcerer Alaric de Marnac, whom Molina later reused in other media and films. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1972:** *Los crímenes de Petiot*. Crime screenplay inspired by the historical murderer Marcel Petiot; the released film was directed by José Luis Madrid. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1972–1973:** *La rebelión de las muertas*. Supernatural-horror screenplay directed by León Klimovsky; release sources variously date it by production or premiere. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1972–1973:** *El jorobado de la Morgue*. Gothic-horror screenplay, filmed by Javier Aguirre, built around Molina’s role as the hunchback Gotho. Its year varies between production-based and release-based filmographies. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1973:** *La venganza de la momia*. Screenplay for Carlos Aured’s mummy film, in which Molina also devised and performed two linked roles. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1973:** *El retorno de Walpurgis*. Daninsky screenplay filmed by Carlos Aured and also distributed as *Curse of the Devil*; alternative titles designate releases, not separate works. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1973:** *Los ojos azules de la muñeca rota*. Giallo-inflected crime-and-horror screenplay directed by Carlos Aured. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1973:** *El gran amor del conde Drácula*. Vampire screenplay associated with Molina and director Javier Aguirre; credit references are not wholly consistent about the division of writing. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1974:** *El mariscal del infierno*. Medieval occult screenplay, also known internationally as *The Devil’s Possessed*, directed by León Klimovsky. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1974:** *Una libélula para cada muerto*. Crime-thriller screenplay filmed by León Klimovsky. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1974–1975:** *La cruz del diablo*. Original screenplay drawing on Gustavo Adolfo Bécquer, subsequently rewritten and directed by John Gilling. Molina disowned the result and wanted his name removed, so the released film resists identification as his work without qualification. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1974–1975:** *Exorcismo*. Possession-horror screenplay directed by Juan Bosch. Molina said he wrote it before the release of *The Exorcist*, although the film appeared afterward; that chronology is his claim, not independently settled fact. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1975:** *La maldición de la bestia*. Daninsky screenplay, filmed by Miguel Iglesias and widely retitled *The Werewolf and the Yeti* or *Night of the Howling Beast*. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1975:** *Los pasajeros*. Thriller screenplay made into a feature by José Antonio Barrero. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1975:** *Todos los gritos del silencio*. Crime screenplay filmed by Ramón Barco. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1975:** *Muerte de un quinqui*. Crime screenplay for León Klimovsky’s film, with Molina also playing the principal criminal figure. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1976:** *Secuestro*. Crime-thriller screenplay filmed by León Klimovsky; some credit databases divide the writing among Molina and collaborators. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1976:** *Inquisición*. Screenplay and Molina’s completed feature-directing debut, signed as Jacinto Molina; he also performed the inquisitor Bernard de Fossey. It is therefore a composite cinematic obra rather than merely a written text. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1977:** *El francotirador*. Political-action screenplay co-written by Molina and directed by Carlos Puerto. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1977:** *El transexual*. Social-drama screenplay co-written by Molina and filmed by José Jara. The surviving circulation copy is reportedly poor, making the work’s accessible manifestation incomplete or degraded. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1977–1978:** *El huerto del francés*. Feature co-written with Antonio Fos and directed by Molina, based on the historical Peñaflor murders. Sources use both its production year and its 1978 release year. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1978:** *Madrid al desnudo*. Urban drama directed by Molina; writing credits vary among catalogues, so it is secure as his directed work but less securely a sole-authored screenplay. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1978–1980:** *El caminante*. Fantasy screenplay co-written with Eduarda Targioni and directed by Molina, who embodied the Devil wandering through Golden Age Spain. It was shot in 1978 and released in 1980. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1980–1981:** *Los cántabros*. Historical feature written and directed by Molina after he replaced Amando de Ossorio; he substantially rewrote Joaquín Gómez’s prior story or script, whose contribution complicates any claim of sole creation. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1980:** *El carnaval de las bestias*. Spanish-Japanese crime-and-horror feature written and directed by Molina, also circulated as *Bestias humanas*, *Human Beasts*, and *The Pig*. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1980–1981:** *El retorno del hombre lobo*. Daninsky feature written and directed by Molina and conceived partly as a new treatment of *La noche de Walpurgis*. It was filmed in 1980 and released in Spain in 1981. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1982:** *La espada del samurái*. Television work directed by Molina; documentation is sparse, and some filmographies treat it as a stand-alone television film while others connect it to his Japanese productions. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy)]
- **1983:** *Latidos de pánico*. Feature written, directed, produced, and performed by Molina, reviving Alaric de Marnac; it is one of the clearest examples of his cinema as a multi-role obra. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1983:** *La bestia y la espada mágica*. Spanish-Japanese Daninsky feature written, directed, and co-produced by Molina. Its fusion of werewolf film, period fantasy, and Japanese swordplay resists a single genre category. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1983–1984:** *El último kamikaze*. International action feature written and directed by Molina for Aconito Films; shot in 1983 and released in 1984. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1983–1984:** *Mi amigo el vagabundo*. Social melodrama written and directed by Molina, starring his son Sergio; produced in 1983 and released in 1984. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1984–1985:** *Operación Mantis (El exterminio del macho)*. Spy parody directed by Molina from a screenplay principally attributed to Joaquín Oristrell. It belongs to Molina’s directed corpus, not securely to his writing corpus, and its commercial failure ended Aconito Films. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1987–1988:** *El aullido del diablo*. Feature written, directed, produced, and performed by Molina; made in 1987 and first shown on Spanish television in 1988. Its parade of classic-monster personae makes it a deliberately self-reflexive, mixed manifestation of his screen career. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1988–1990?:** *Horror en el museo de cera*. Video feature written and directed by Molina but never completed; it is a project and surviving production trace rather than an established finished film. Sources disagree on its year, commonly assigning 1988 or approximately 1990. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1988:** *El último guateque II*. Drama for which screenwriting credit is attributed to Molina in film-credit databases, directed by Juan José Porto. [[Paul Naschy filmography](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)]
- **1993:** *La noche del ejecutor*. Low-budget revenge feature written and directed by Molina and released directly to video; some sources date it 1992 from production and others 1993 from release. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1996–1997:** *Licántropo: el asesino de la luna llena*. Return to Waldemar Daninsky, written by Molina and directed by Francisco Rodríguez Gordillo. Production and release years account for the competing dates. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1999:** *Rondadores nocturnos 2*. Short film co-written and performed by Molina; its limited circulation makes the precise allocation of authorship difficult to verify beyond the surviving credit records. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **2007–2010:** *Empusa*. Digital vampire feature begun as *Gaviotas*, co-written and initially co-directed with Carlos Aured. Aured withdrew; Molina finished the shoot but died before post-production, which producer Ángel Mora completed, so the released film is a posthumous collaborative manifestation rather than a wholly finished authorial object. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **2009–2011:** *Crotón el Grande*. Screenplay by Molina for a projected starring vehicle; he died before performing it, and Luis Colombo completed the film with Damián Varea. The script is Molina’s work, while the finished film is another maker’s posthumous realization. [[Naschy filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]

### Recurring fictional creation

- **1968–2004:** *Waldemar Daninsky*. A continuing fictional persona created by Molina for *La marca del hombre lobo* and repeatedly rewritten and embodied across mutually inconsistent films. It is better understood as a serial creation or body of variations than as one continuous narrative work. [[Waldemar Daninsky](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldemar_Daninsky)] [[Fancine](https://ibdigital.uib.es/greenstone/sites/localsite/collect/fancine/index/assoc/Fancine_/2010_001.dir/Fancine_2010_001.pdf)]

### Prose books

- **1997:** *Memorias de un hombre lobo*. Autobiographical memoir and retrospective filmography, published by Alberto Santos; it is also one of the principal first-person sources for defining Molina’s corpus. [[Bibliographic record](https://www.uniliber.com/ficha/paul-naschy-memorias-de-un-hombre-lobo_115831570/)]
- **2000:** *Las tres caras del terror: un siglo de cine fantaterrorífico español*. Collaborative book edited by Molina/Naschy with Narciso Ibáñez Serrador and Jesús Franco; it is a critical and historical compilation rather than a sole-authored monograph. [[Bibliographic notice](https://www.encyclopedia.com/arts/educational-magazines/molina-jacinto-1934)]
- **2008:** *Cuando las luces se apagan*. Second memoir, published by Fundación AISGE and T&B Editores; it revisits rather than merely reprints the earlier autobiography. [[Bibliographic discussion](https://cerebrin.wordpress.com/2009/01/09/biografias-de-manuel-de-blas-y-paul-naschy/)]
- **2009:** *Alaric de Marnac*. Horror novel transforming Molina’s recurring screen sorcerer into a prose protagonist; published shortly before his death. [[Bibliographic listing](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Bibliograf%C3%ADa)]

### Pocket westerns and an obstructed manuscript

- **1960–1961:** *Yo sé que ganarás*. Pocket western published by Ediciones Rollán under the publisher-suggested pseudonym “Jack Mills” or, on at least one surviving cover, “Jack Mill.” [[Proyecto Naschy](https://proyectonaschy.com/2011/11/)]
- **1960–1961:** *Three further western novels published as Jack Mills/Jack Mill, titles not securely recovered*. Molina stated that four westerns were published, but the consulted documentation identifies only *Yo sé que ganarás* by title. These anonymous-to-the-present entries resist individual naming and should not be supplied with conjectural titles. [[Proyecto Naschy](https://proyectonaschy.com/2011/11/)]
- **Early 1960s:** *Untitled western novel rejected by the censor*. Molina described this as his best western manuscript and said it was refused for excessive violence and sex. It is an unrealized publication project rather than a recoverable book; no secure title or surviving text is identified. [[Proyecto Naschy](https://proyectonaschy.com/2011/11/)]

### Graphic-design corpus

- **1950s–1960s:** *Record sleeves for Decca, London, and Alhambra*. A body of commercial graphic design attributed to Molina, including sleeves connected with performers such as Elvis Presley, Bill Haley, and Frankie Laine. The available account does not provide a catalogue raisonné or reliably match individual designs to titles, so this corpus cannot honestly be decomposed into named works. [[Proyecto Naschy](https://proyectonaschy.com/2011/11/)]

### Performances as manifestations rather than authored works

- **1960–2009:** *Screen-acting corpus of Paul Naschy*. More than one hundred film, television, short-film, and voice performances are documented, including multiple embodiments of Daninsky, Alaric de Marnac, Dracula, the mummy Amenhotep, the Devil, and other figures. These are creative manifestations made through performance, but they are not thereby films authored by Molina; the standard filmography records them separately from his 52 reported writing credits and 23 directing credits. [[IMDb credits](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0596701/)] [[Complete chronological filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1968:** *Las noches del hombre lobo*. Molina’s claimed performance as Daninsky cannot be treated as a securely extant manifestation because the film’s completion and even its material existence remain disputed. [[Complete chronological filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1988–1990?:** *Horror en el museo de cera*. Molina reportedly performed in his own unfinished video production, but the project was never completed and is described as lost; the performance survives, if at all, only as an uncertain fragment. [[Complete chronological filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1995–2019:** *Los resucitados*. Molina’s performance was filmed for Arturo de Bobadilla’s unfinished feature in 1995, but post-production was not completed until decades later. The eventual release is a delayed reconstruction, not a manifestation finalized during Molina’s lifetime. [[Complete chronological filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **1997:** *El ojo de la Medusa*. Performance in a crime feature apparently never completed or publicly released; consequently it resists classification as a finished film appearance. [[Complete chronological filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
- **2009–2012:** *O Apóstolo*. Voice performance recorded shortly before Molina’s death for Fernando Cortizo’s stop-motion feature and released posthumously. The vocal performance is his manifestation; the animated film as a whole is not his authored work. [[Complete chronological filmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Naschy#Filmography)]
