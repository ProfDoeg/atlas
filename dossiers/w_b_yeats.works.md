## Works by William Butler Yeats

### Poetry: authored books, cycles, and materially distinct collections

- **1885:** *“Song of the Fairies” and “Voices.”* Yeats’s first poems in print, published together in the *Dublin University Review*; they survive as early manifestations rather than a book. [CW I; Wade]
- **1889:** *The Wanderings of Oisin and Other Poems*. Yeats’s first substantial poetry book, combining the long narrative poem with lyrics, ballads, and earlier dramatic verse. [CW I; Wade]
- **1889:** *Crossways*. A retrospectively ordered poetic cycle, not a separately published 1889 book: Yeats first used the section title in *Poems* (1895), while his collected edition assigned it the date 1889. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1892–1893:** *The Rose*. Another retrospectively constituted cycle rather than a book of that title; most poems first appeared in *The Countess Kathleen and Various Legends and Lyrics* (1892), while Yeats later dated the group 1893. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1899:** *The Wind Among the Reeds*. A poetry book and deliberately ordered symbolic cycle, accompanied in its first edition by Yeats’s explanatory notes. [CW I; Wade; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1903:** *In the Seven Woods*. A collection of fourteen poems and two plays in its original book-form; it was the first book issued by the Yeats sisters’ Dun Emer Press. [CW I; CW II; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1906:** *Poems, 1899–1905*. A designed gathering of verse and plays whose revisions make it more than a neutral reprint, though it is not a new poetic cycle. [Wade]
- **1910:** *The Green Helmet and Other Poems*. A mixed verse volume containing lyrics and dramatic work; the poetry cycle and the play *The Green Helmet* are distinct obras within its covers. [CW I; CW II; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1912:** *A Coat*. A privately printed single-poem pamphlet and therefore a publication-object as well as a poem. [Wade–Alspach]
- **1913:** *Poems Written in Discouragement*. A small Cuala Press sequence responding to the Hugh Lane controversy; it was later absorbed into *Responsibilities*. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1914:** *Responsibilities*. A Cuala Press poetry book; the enlarged 1916 *Responsibilities and Other Poems* is a revised manifestation rather than an entirely separate work. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1916:** *Easter, 1916*. A privately printed single-poem publication whose very restricted initial circulation made it both poem and political intervention. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1918:** *In Memory of Major Robert Gregory*. A memorial poem issued as a separate Cuala Press booklet. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1918:** *The Leaders of the Crowd*. A separate Cuala Press pamphlet containing three poems. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1917–1919:** *The Wild Swans at Coole*. The 1917 Cuala book comprised poems and a verse play; the substantially enlarged 1919 edition added many poems and constitutes a distinct manifestation of the project. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1921:** *Michael Robartes and the Dancer*. A book of fifteen poems, issued in February 1921 although its title page says 1920. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1922:** *Seven Poems and a Fragment*. A Cuala Press gathering whose “fragment” is deliberately presented as such rather than silently completed. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1922:** *Later Poems*. A retrospective collection and revision-project, not a wholly new composition. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1924:** *The Cat and the Moon and Certain Poems*. A mixed Cuala Press object joining a play to poems. [CW I; CW II; Wade–Alspach]
- **1927:** *October Blast*. A short Cuala Press poetry book later incorporated into *The Tower*. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1928:** *The Tower*. A major poetry book of thirty poems and sequences; its architecture and ordering make the volume itself an obra, not merely a container. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1929:** *The Winding Stair*. A signed, limited Fountain Press poetry book; it is the earlier and materially distinct precursor of the larger 1933 collection. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1932:** *Words for Music Perhaps and Other Poems*. A Cuala Press collection, including song-like poems conceived for voice while resisting conventional musical setting. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1933:** *The Winding Stair and Other Poems*. A greatly enlarged poetry book of sixty-four poems and sequences, distinct from the 1929 limited volume. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1933:** *The Collected Poems of W. B. Yeats*. Yeats’s canon-making revision and arrangement of his poetry; because he repeatedly altered titles, wording, and groupings, it is an editorial obra as well as a collection. [CW I; Wade–Alspach]
- **1934:** *The King of the Great Clock Tower*. A Cuala Press mixed booklet containing the play and associated poems and songs. [CW I; CW II; Wade–Alspach]
- **1935:** *A Full Moon in March*. A mixed poetry-and-drama book; its title names both the volume and one of its plays. [CW I; CW II; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1938:** *New Poems*. Yeats’s final poetry book published during his lifetime, comprising thirty-five poems. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1939:** *Last Poems and Two Plays*. A posthumously issued but author-prepared Cuala Press volume containing twenty-two late poems, *The Death of Cuchulain*, and *Purgatory*. Its posthumous appearance should not be confused with posthumous composition. [CW I; CW II; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **1884–1939; posthumously assembled:** *Early and Uncollected Poems*. This is not one Yeats-made book but a scholarly recovery category for abandoned, fugitive, or excluded poems; its resistance to being called a single work is bibliographically significant. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **c. 1884–1934; posthumously assembled:** *Narrative and Dramatic Poems*. A collected-edition category for seven long or dramatic poems, not a lifetime book bearing that title. [CW I; Yeats.ie Poems]
- **c. 1882–1891; first collected 1995:** *Under the Moon: The Unpublished Early Poetry*. A posthumous editorial reconstruction of early manuscripts; it documents Yeats’s poetic projects but is not itself an author-designed Yeats volume. [CW I; Chicago]

### Plays, dance-dramas, and performance scores

- **1886:** *Mosada: A Dramatic Poem*. A verse play first printed in a periodical and then as Yeats’s first separate publication, in an edition of one hundred copies paid for by his father. [CW II; Wade]
- **c. 1884–1885; incomplete:** *The Island of Statues*. An early Arcadian verse-play project published serially in incomplete form; a recovered fuller manuscript appeared only posthumously, so no single lifetime text is definitive. [CW I; Wade]
- **1892:** *The Countess Cathleen*. A verse play first published as *The Countess Kathleen* and repeatedly rewritten through several substantially different versions; title, spelling, structure, and date therefore resist reduction to one fixed text. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1894:** *The Land of Heart’s Desire*. A one-act verse play first performed in March and published in April 1894, then repeatedly revised. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1896–1902; unfinished:** *The Shadowy Waters*. Initially a dramatic-poem project, published in 1900 and later transformed into a stage play; the poetic and acting versions are related but materially different obras. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1901; first published 1951:** *Diarmuid and Grania*. A play written collaboratively with George Moore and incidental music by Edward Elgar; the division of authorship was contentious, and it was not published in Yeats’s lifetime. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1902:** *Cathleen ni Houlihan*. A one-act nationalist play created with Lady Gregory, although lifetime publications credited Yeats alone; Gregory’s degree of authorship must not be erased. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1902:** *Where There Is Nothing*. A five-act play later dismantled and reworked with Lady Gregory as *The Unicorn from the Stars*; it survives as an independent abandoned version, not merely a draft. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1903:** *On Baile’s Strand*. A Cuchulain verse play first published in *In the Seven Woods* and repeatedly revised for performance. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1903/1907:** *The Hour-Glass*. A morality play first written in prose and later remade in verse; the collected works preserve the two forms as distinct versions rather than treating one as a simple correction. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1903–1904:** *The Pot of Broth*. A one-act comedy written with Lady Gregory but long credited only to Yeats; the collaboration makes sole authorship untenable. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1904/1922:** *The King’s Threshold*. A verse play for which Yeats supplied a radically new ending in 1922; both the early and revised dramatic structures belong to the work’s history. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1907:** *Deirdre*. A verse tragedy designed for the Abbey Theatre’s stylized performance practice. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1908:** *The Unicorn from the Stars*. A prose play by Lady Gregory and Yeats, reworking *Where There Is Nothing*; it is collaborative and should not be folded silently into Yeats’s sole oeuvre. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1910:** *The Green Helmet*. A heroic farce in verse, first performed under the title *The Golden Helmet* before publication and revision. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1916–1917:** *At the Hawk’s Well*. The first of the “plays for dancers,” combining spoken verse, music, mask, costume, and dance; it is a small Gesamtkunstwerk rather than merely a dramatic text. Michio Itō, Edmund Dulac, and musicians participated in its first production. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1919:** *The Only Jealousy of Emer*. A dance-play in the Cuchulain cycle; Yeats later transformed it into the ballet-play *Fighting the Waves*. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1919:** *The Dreaming of the Bones*. A dance-play joining the Easter Rising to the legend of Diarmuid and Dervorgilla. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1921:** *Calvary*. A dance-play published with the other three works in *Four Plays for Dancers*. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1919:** *Two Plays for Dancers*. A publication-object containing *The Dreaming of the Bones* and *The Only Jealousy of Emer*; it became part of the enlarged *Four Plays for Dancers*. [CW II; Wade–Alspach]
- **1921:** *Four Plays for Dancers*. A designed cycle comprising *At the Hawk’s Well*, *The Only Jealousy of Emer*, *The Dreaming of the Bones*, and *Calvary*. Its unity lies in a performance protocol of masks, dance, music, and restricted staging. [CW II; Wade–Alspach]
- **1922:** *The Player Queen*. A prose comedy begun much earlier and repeatedly revised before publication. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1924:** *The Cat and the Moon*. A dance-play whose music, movement, mask, and dialogue make the printed words only one layer of the obra. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1927:** *The Resurrection*. A short play concerning Christ’s resurrection, written for stylized performance and first staged only in 1934. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1928:** *Sophocles’ King Oedipus*. A stage version rather than a direct translation from Greek: Yeats worked through existing translations and reshaped the tragedy for modern performance. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1930:** *Fighting the Waves*. A ballet-play made by transforming *The Only Jealousy of Emer*, with music by George Antheil; it is neither merely a revision nor wholly independent of its precursor. [CW II]
- **1934:** *The Words upon the Window-Pane*. A prose play in which a séance produces the voice of Jonathan Swift; the drama turns mediumship itself into theatrical form. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1934:** *Sophocles’ Oedipus at Colonus*. A stage version assembled without Yeats knowing Greek; music and chorus are constitutive, so “translation” alone is an inadequate category. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1934–1935:** *The King of the Great Clock Tower*. A verse play first published in 1934 and subsequently revised; its songs also circulate as poems. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1935:** *A Full Moon in March*. A verse play reworking the same dramatic materials as *The King of the Great Clock Tower*, but not merely another title for it. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1938:** *The Herne’s Egg*. A late prose comedy and political fantasy. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1938–1939:** *Purgatory*. A one-act verse play first performed in 1938 and published posthumously in 1939. [CW II; Chicago]
- **1939:** *The Death of Cuchulain*. Yeats’s final Cuchulain play, completed shortly before his death and issued posthumously; its written script specifies music, dance, and a stylized severed head. [CW II; Chicago]

### Fiction, folklore constructions, and mythic prose

- **1888:** *Fairy and Folk Tales of the Irish Peasantry*. An edited anthology with Yeats’s introduction, arrangement, and notes; most tales are by earlier collectors, so it is an editorial construction rather than wholly authored fiction. [CW VI; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1890:** *Irish Fairies*. An illustrated periodical essay or folklore compilation, not a separately conceived book. [Wade–Alspach]
- **1891:** *Representative Irish Tales*. An anthology selected and introduced by Yeats; authorship lies chiefly in selection, framing, and cultural argument. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]
- **1891:** *John Sherman*. A novella first published with *Dhoya* under the pseudonymous framing name “Ganconagh.” [CW VII; Wade]
- **1891:** *Dhoya*. A short mythic tale paired with *John Sherman* but generically and narratively independent of it. [CW VII; Wade]
- **1892:** *Irish Fairy Tales*. An anthology selected and introduced by Yeats, substantially overlapping his folklore project but materially distinct from the 1888 collection. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]
- **1893/1902:** *The Celtic Twilight*. A hybrid of collected folklore, memoir, visionary report, and literary tale; the enlarged 1902 edition added chapters and is the form Yeats retained. Its uncertain border between testimony and fiction is part of the obra. [CW VIII; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1896:** *Rosa Alchemica*. An occult tale first published in *The Savoy* and then incorporated into *The Secret Rose*. [CW VII; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1896–1897:** *The Tables of the Law*. An occult story first printed in *The Savoy* and privately issued with *The Adoration of the Magi*. [CW VII; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1897:** *The Adoration of the Magi*. A companion occult story privately printed with *The Tables of the Law*. [CW VII; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1897:** *The Secret Rose*. A collection joining occult tales, legendary narratives, and the original Red Hanrahan stories; later editions removed or rearranged material, so the title covers changing configurations. [CW VII; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1896–1902; unfinished; first published 1976:** *The Speckled Bird*. An abandoned autobiographical novel surviving in several manuscript states. The scholarly edition makes the project legible but cannot turn it into a finished Yeats-authorized novel. [Chicago]
- **1905:** *Stories of Red Hanrahan*. Six interconnected tales rewritten from *The Secret Rose* with substantial linguistic assistance from Lady Gregory; the collaboration complicates sole authorship. The book says 1904 but was issued in 1905. [CW VII; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1927:** *Stories of Red Hanrahan and The Secret Rose*. A late recombination and revision of Yeats’s mythic fiction, accompanied by poetry; it is a canon-forming manifestation rather than a new set of stories. [CW VII; Wade–Alspach]

### Essays, autobiography, visionary systems, and public argument

- **1903:** *Ideas of Good and Evil*. A collection of essays on poetry, symbolism, magic, Blake, Shelley, Irish literature, and theatre. [CW IV; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1907:** *Discoveries*. A sequence of compact reflections or “discoveries”; it lies between notebook, aphoristic essay, and aesthetic manifesto. [CW IV; Chicago]
- **1911:** *Synge and the Ireland of His Time*. A memorial-critical essay that also intervenes in disputes over Irish theatre and cultural nationalism. [CW IV; Wade–Alspach]
- **1912/1919:** *The Cutting of an Agate*. A changing collection of essays on theatre, poetry, art, and nationality; Yeats revised and enlarged it in 1919. [CW IV]
- **1916:** *Reveries over Childhood and Youth*. An autobiographical work printed by the Cuala Press with a 1915 imprint but issued in March 1916. [CW III; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1918:** *Per Amica Silentia Lunae*. A hybrid philosophical and occult book comprising a frame, the essays “Anima Hominis” and “Anima Mundi,” and a poem; it is closer to a visionary ars poetica than ordinary nonfiction. [CW V; Yeats.ie Prose]
- **1921:** *Four Years: 1887–1891*. An autobiographical memoir of Yeats’s early literary and political milieu. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1922:** *The Trembling of the Veil*. A multi-part autobiography extending his life narrative through the 1890s and early twentieth century. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1923:** *Plays and Controversies*. A compound book of drama, theatrical essays, and polemical documents; it functions partly as a manifesto for Yeats’s theatre. [CW II; CW IV]
- **1924:** *Essays*. A selected and revised gathering of earlier prose rather than a newly composed unit. [CW IV; Wade–Alspach]
- **1925:** *The Bounty of Sweden*. Yeats’s prose account of receiving the Nobel Prize and of Irish cultural history, developed from his Nobel lecture and related addresses. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1925:** *A Vision: An Explanation of Life Founded upon the Writings of Giraldus and upon Certain Doctrines Attributed to Kusta Ben Luka*. The first book-form system derived from the Yeatses’ automatic-writing experiments; its fictional apparatus and occult claims make “nonfiction” inadequate. [CW XIII; Chicago]
- **1926:** *Estrangement*. A privately printed selection from Yeats’s diary, occupying the border between notebook, memoir, and aphoristic essay. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1926:** *Autobiographies of William Butler Yeats*. Yeats’s first large-scale assembly of his memoirs; it is a deliberately revised life-work rather than a transparent documentary record. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1928:** *The Death of Synge and Other Passages from an Old Diary*. A Cuala Press memoir assembled from diary material. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1929:** *A Packet for Ezra Pound*. A prose-and-poetry explanation of the system behind *A Vision*, addressed to Pound; portions were transformed for the 1937 *Vision*. [CW XIV; Wade–Alspach]
- **1935:** *Dramatis Personae, 1896–1902*. An autobiographical continuation centred on Yeats’s collaborators and the creation of Irish theatre. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1937:** *A Vision*. A wholesale rewriting of the 1925 book, with a different architecture and framing fiction; scholars therefore designate the two independent states “A” and “B,” not successive printings of one stable text. [CW XIV; Chicago]
- **1937:** *Essays 1931 to 1936*. A Cuala Press gathering of late essays on politics, culture, poetry, and social order. [CW V; Wade–Alspach]
- **1938:** *The Autobiography of William Butler Yeats*. A revised omnibus combining earlier autobiographical books with *The Death of Synge*, *Dramatis Personae*, and related pieces. [CW III; Wade–Alspach]
- **1938–1939:** *On the Boiler*. A two-part late pamphlet combining political and social polemic, poems, and the dramatic dialogue *The Old Stone Cross*. Its authoritarian and eugenic arguments are part of its status as an intervention, not incidental context. The second part appeared posthumously. [CW V; Wade–Alspach]
- **1922–1928; first collected 1960:** *Senate Speeches*. Speeches Yeats delivered as a senator of the Irish Free State, including interventions on divorce, censorship, education, and cultural policy; they were oral political acts before becoming texts. [Chicago]
- **1908–1939; posthumously collected:** *Prefaces and Introductions*. Thirty-two framing texts for Yeats’s own books, anthologies, and books by others. The collection is posthumous, but each preface is a separate act of literary mediation. [CW VI]
- **1886–1939; posthumously collected:** *Uncollected Prose*. Hundreds of reviews, newspaper articles, manifestos, letters to editors, theatre statements, and occasional pieces gathered in scholarly editions. This is an archive-category, not a Yeats-made single obra, and completeness remains dependent on continuing bibliographical discovery. [UP I–II; Wade–Alspach]
- **1870s–1939; posthumously edited:** *Letters*. Yeats made thousands of letters, but no single “Collected Letters” is an authored work: surviving correspondence is incomplete, editorially selected, and still being published. [CL]
- **c. 1916–1937; first published 1972:** *Memoirs*. Frank autobiographical manuscripts that Yeats withheld from lifetime publication. The posthumous title denotes an editor-shaped manuscript group rather than a finished book authorized by Yeats. [Chicago]

### Editorial, collaborative, and translational obras

- **1886:** *Poems and Ballads of Young Ireland*. An anthology edited by Yeats; its made form is selection and arrangement rather than sole composition. [Wade]
- **1893:** *The Works of William Blake: Poetic, Symbolic, and Critical*. A three-volume edition, interpretation, and reproduction project made with Edwin John Ellis. Its editorial attributions and reconstructed Blakean system have not all been accepted by later scholarship. [Wade–Alspach; CW VI]
- **1895:** *A Book of Irish Verse*. An anthology selected and introduced by Yeats; later editions revised its contents and argument about an Irish poetic tradition. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]
- **1900:** *The Shadowy Waters* stage and image project. Yeats’s revisions, scenic thinking, music, and performance experiments made the play an evolving multimedia project rather than a text fixed by its 1900 printing. [CW II; Yeats.ie Plays]
- **1912:** *Selections from the Writings of Lord Dunsany*. A selection and framing project edited by Yeats. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]
- **1912:** *Gitanjali (Song Offerings)*. Rabindranath Tagore’s English poems, for which Yeats selected material and wrote the influential introduction; it is Tagore’s obra, with a significant Yeats-made editorial frame, not a Yeats book. [CW VI]
- **1914:** *The Hour-Glass, The Tables of the Law, and The Adoration of the Magi*. A Shakespeare Head Press recombination of drama and occult fiction, materially distinct as a designed limited book but not three new compositions. [Wade–Alspach]
- **1935–1937:** *Broadsides: New Irish and English Songs*. A sequence of large illustrated song-sheets edited by Yeats and Dorothy Wellesley, with music and visual design by other contributors. It is collaborative printed performance culture—a modest Gesamtkunstwerk—not solely Yeats’s writing. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]
- **1936:** *The Oxford Book of Modern Verse, 1892–1935*. An anthology selected and introduced by Yeats. Its canon-making exclusions and idiosyncratic definitions of “modern” are part of the editorial work. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]
- **1938:** *The Ten Principal Upanishads*. English versions made with Shri Purohit Swami; because Yeats did not know Sanskrit, these are collaborative poetic renderings mediated through Purohit Swami, not independent scholarly translations by Yeats. [CW VI; Wade–Alspach]

### Projects, institutions, ritual documents, and multimedia manifestations

- **1885–1886:** *Dublin Hermetic Society*. An occult-study organization co-founded by Yeats. It is a collective institutional project rather than a text, and responsibility for its precise programme cannot be assigned to Yeats alone. [CL; Wade–Alspach]
- **1887–1891:** *The Esoteric Section and Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn work*. Yeats participated in, copied, revised, and performed ritual and instructional material. These documents are protocols and collective traditions; many are anonymous, pseudonymous, or collaboratively transmitted, so they cannot responsibly be catalogued as sole-authored Yeats works. [CW XIII–XIV]
- **1891:** *The Rhymers’ Club*. A collective literary and social project co-founded by Yeats and Ernest Rhys; its meetings and anthologies were manifestations of a group rather than Yeats’s individual obra. [CL]
- **1892–1899:** *The Celtic Mystical Order*. An intermittently developed project for an Irish mystery religion, represented by plans, invocations, symbols, and correspondence but never completed as an operating order. Its unfinished intention is the work. [CL; CW XIII]
- **1897:** *The Irish Literary Theatre manifesto*. A performative founding statement issued by Yeats, Lady Gregory, and Edward Martyn: it acts by announcing a theatre rather than merely describing one. Authorship is collective. [Abbey Studies]
- **1899–1904:** *Irish Literary Theatre*. A collective theatre project initiated by Yeats, Lady Gregory, Edward Martyn, and collaborators to produce new Irish drama. It survives through productions and institutions, not as a single text. [Abbey Studies]
- **1902–1904:** *Irish National Theatre Society*. A company-building project involving the Fay brothers, Yeats, Lady Gregory, George Russell, actors, and others; any account presenting Yeats as its sole maker would erase disputed and distributed labour. [Abbey Studies]
- **1904 onward:** *Abbey Theatre*. A national-theatre project made collectively by writers, actors, managers, designers, workers, and Annie Horniman’s financing. Yeats was a founding director and major artistic shaper, but the institution emphatically resists classification as his individual work. [Abbey Studies]
- **1904–1938:** *Yeatsian stagecraft*. Masks, curtains, patterned movement, spoken chorus, dance, lighting, and anti-naturalistic scenery formed an evolving performance system across productions. No single fixed title or date contains it; it is a body of collaborative practical work. [CW II; Stagecraft]
- **1908–1915:** *Beltaine, Samhain,* and *The Arrow*. These theatre periodicals and pamphlet series carried Yeats’s editorial statements, production records, polemics, and manifestos. They are composite institutional publications rather than solely authored books. [CW IV; UP I]
- **1917–1920:** *The automatic-script experiment*. George Yeats produced replies while W. B. Yeats posed questions and organized the resulting system: roughly 3,600 pages from about 450 sittings, together with notebooks, workbooks, and hundreds of cards. The material is a collaborative process and claimed manifestation, not straightforward sole authorship; even its status as “made” rather than “received” is the central dispute. [CW XIII–XIV; Automatic Script]
- **1917–1937:** *The system of A Vision*. Charts, cones, gyres, lunar phases, historical cycles, fictional instructors, automatic script, dreams, card files, and two incompatible book versions form a long intellectual-artistic project. It is more accurately a changing system or Gesamtkunstwerk of diagram, dialogue, fiction, and doctrine than one stable text. [CW XIII–XIV]
- **1923:** *Nobel lecture: “The Irish Dramatic Movement.”* An address delivered on receiving the Nobel Prize; it is simultaneously speech, cultural history, and public manifesto for the theatre movement. [CW V]
- **1923–1928:** *Irish Free State cultural legislation and advocacy*. Yeats’s Senate speeches and committee work were political acts concerning censorship, education, museums, coinage, language, and minority rights. The resulting laws and objects were collective state products, not works solely “by Yeats.” [Chicago]
- **1926–1928:** *Irish Free State coinage project*. As chair of the coinage design committee, Yeats helped establish the programme, imagery, and competition ultimately realized in Percy Metcalfe’s animal designs. The coins are Metcalfe’s designs and a state production; Yeats made the curatorial protocol, not the pictured objects by himself. [CW VI]
- **1931–1937:** *Radio readings and poetry broadcasts*. Yeats made performances for the BBC in Belfast and London, including talks, solo readings, directed ensemble speaking, and sung or chanted verse. These are sonic manifestations of poems rather than new written works; surviving recordings and broadcast documentation are incomplete. [BBC; PennSound]
- **1937:** *BBC programme of poems for several voices*. Yeats selected, rehearsed, revised for speech, and directed poems performed with Margot Ruddock and others. Authorship is distributed among writer, voices, musicians, broadcaster, and recording medium, making “text” an insufficient category. [BBC; Smithsonian]
