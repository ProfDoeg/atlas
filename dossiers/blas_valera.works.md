## Works by Blas Valera

### Histories and ethnographic works

- **By February 1580:** *Relación del suceso de la predicación evangélica en esos reinos*. A history of Christian evangelization among the Andean peoples, documented by a letter acknowledging the work in progress; its manuscript is lost, its final title is uncertain, and scholars disagree over whether it was independent or an early form of the *Historia Occidentalis*. ([Hyland, 2003](https://press.umich.edu/Books/T/The-Jesuit-and-the-Incas); [Borges Morán, 1981, p. 88](https://www.fondazioneintorcetta.info/pdf/biblioteca-virtuale/documenti_1/DeAcosta.pdf))

- **1579–1594:** *Historia Occidentalis*. A large Latin history of the Incas and colonial Peru, reportedly arranged in several books; most of the manuscript was lost or damaged around the English sack of Cádiz in 1596, and only fragments incorporated or translated by Inca Garcilaso and notices in later writers survive. Its relationship to the earlier evangelization history is unresolved, so treating them as two wholly separate obras remains provisional. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 98–99](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0); [Rojas, 2018](https://upao.edu.pe/pdf/Pueblo%20continente%2029-1.pdf))

- **Before 1631; unfinished:** *Vocabulario antiguo* or *Vocabulario histórico*. An alphabetically arranged Quechua lexicon combined with historical and cultural notes—thus closer to an unfinished linguistic encyclopedia than to a simple dictionary. Giovanni Anello Oliva reported consulting Valera’s manuscript at the Jesuit college in La Paz and said that it ended at the letter H; the original is now lost, and no secure date of composition survives. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 98–99](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0); [Rojas, 2018](https://upao.edu.pe/pdf/Pueblo%20continente%2029-1.pdf))

- **1594–1595, attributed:** *Relación de las costumbres antiguas de los naturales del Pirú*. An anonymous treatise on Inca religion, government, and customs, preserved substantially complete and first printed by Marcos Jiménez de la Espada in 1879. Sabine Hyland argues for Valera’s authorship, while other scholars assign it to another Jesuit—especially Luis López—or regard the attribution as unproved; it therefore resists entry as an uncontested Valeran obra. ([Hyland, *Gods of the Andes*, 2011](https://www.psupress.org/books/titles/978-0-271-04880-4.html); [Jesuit Online Bibliography](https://jesuitonlinebibliography.bc.edu/catalog/9373); [Rojas, 2018](https://upao.edu.pe/pdf/Pueblo%20continente%2029-1.pdf))

### Linguistic and pastoral works

- **1583–1584, collaborative:** *Doctrina christiana y catecismo para instrucción de los indios*. A trilingual pastoral protocol in Spanish, Quechua, and Aymara produced collectively under the Third Council of Lima: Valera reviewed the Quechua translation and played a leading role in producing the Aymara version. It is not properly an individually authored book, but his documented linguistic labor helped make the printed object. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 86–88](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0); [Biblioteca Nacional de Chile](https://www.memoriachilena.gob.cl/602/w3-article-8648.html))

- **1585, collaborative:** *Confessionario para los curas de indios*. A trilingual protocol for priests hearing confessions, created by the Third Council’s collective of theologians, translators, and revisers. Valera’s participation belongs to the larger conciliar translation-and-correction project, but the surviving volume does not assign him individual authorship. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 89–90](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0))

- **1585, collaborative:** *Tercero cathecismo y exposición de la doctrina christiana por sermones*. A trilingual collection of model sermons—a pastoral protocol intended to standardize Indigenous-language preaching—made by the same conciliar workshop. Valera contributed to the linguistic program, particularly its Aymara work, but calling the whole volume “his book” would erase its corporate production. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 86–90](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0))

- **1582–1583; attested in 1595:** *Arte de la lengua índica*. A manuscript grammar commissioned from Valera by the Third Council of Lima and seen at Seville in 1595; it is now lost except for a short paraphrased passage. Research indicates that it was more extensive than, and textually distinct from, the anonymous printed grammar of 1586, although the two were closely related. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 83–96](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0))

- **1586, directed or substantially shaped by Valera:** *Arte y vocabulario en la lengua general del Perú llamada Quichua, y en la lengua Española*. An anonymous printed Quechua grammar-and-lexicon produced from the Third Council’s collective linguistic materials. José Alejandro Cárdenas Bunsen identifies Valera as the project’s director and likely prologue writer, but the text itself claims no individual author and other candidates, including Alonso de Barzana, have been proposed; it is best understood as a corporate obra under Valera’s probable direction. ([Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014, pp. 89–96](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0); [Bendezú-Araujo, 2020](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/22383/21629))

### Posthumous and rejected attributions

- **1615:** *El primer nueva corónica y buen gobierno*. The Miccinelli documents claim that Valera conceived or principally composed this illustrated manuscript while Felipe Guaman Poma served as a public authorial “mask.” Because the claim requires Valera to have survived his documented death in 1597 and rests on fiercely disputed documents, mainstream scholarship retains Guaman Poma’s authorship; this is not securely a Valeran obra. ([Adorno and Boserup, Royal Danish Library](https://poma.kb.dk/per_ms.htm); [Cárdenas Bunsen, 2014](https://revistas.pucp.edu.pe/index.php/lexis/article/download/9850/10262/0); [Barnes, “The Virtual and the Real”](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230186114_The_Virtual_and_the_Real_The_Case_of_the_Mysterious_Documents_from_Naples))

- **1618, purported:** *Exsul immeritus Blas Valera populo suo*. A multilingual, multimedia manuscript incorporating Latin and Quechua writing, drawings, numerical devices, and accounts of khipu—a would-be Gesamtkunstwerk rather than an ordinary book. It presents itself as Valera’s posthumous manifesto to his people, but its date is twenty-one years after his documented death and the authenticity and truthfulness of the Miccinelli collection remain deeply disputed; it cannot be accepted unconditionally as something he made. ([Laurencich Minelli, ed., 2007](https://jesuitonlinebibliography.bc.edu/catalog/1367); [Barnes, “The Virtual and the Real”](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230186114_The_Virtual_and_the_Real_The_Case_of_the_Mysterious_Documents_from_Naples); [Rojas, 2018](https://upao.edu.pe/pdf/Pueblo%20continente%2029-1.pdf))

- **Seventeenth century, purported contribution:** *Historia et rudimenta linguae Piruanorum*. A composite Miccinelli manuscript principally attributed within the document to Italian Jesuits rather than to Valera, although three illustrated leaves and associated khipu material have been claimed as his work. Its composite authorship, private provenance, post-1597 dating, and contested authenticity make even those leaves resistant to classification as Valeran works. ([Laurencich Minelli, ed., 2007](https://jesuitonlinebibliography.bc.edu/catalog/1367); [Barnes, “The Virtual and the Real”](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230186114_The_Virtual_and_the_Real_The_Case_of_the_Mysterious_Documents_from_Naples))
