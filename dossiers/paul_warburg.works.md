## Works by Paul Moritz Warburg

### Banking-reform essays, plans, and legislative protocols

- **1907:** *[Defects and Needs of Our Banking System](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=35)*. An analytical and programmatic essay on American banking, first published in the *New York Times Annual Financial Review*; it inaugurates Warburg’s public campaign for structural reform.
- **1907:** *[A Plan for a Modified Central Bank](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=55)*. A reform plan—part diagnosis, part institutional design—proposing a politically adapted American central bank.
- **1908:** *[American and European Banking Methods and Bank Legislation Compared](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=65)*. A comparative essay originally delivered in Columbia University’s lecture series on the currency problem.
- **1908:** *[A Modified Central Bank of Issue: A Suggestion of a Bill](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=97)*. A proposed statute: properly a legislative protocol rather than merely an essay. Warburg’s 1930 foreword says it was accidentally omitted from the 1914 collected volume.
- **1908:** *[A Central Bank System and the United States of America](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=121)*. An essay adapting European central-banking principles to American political and geographic conditions.
- **1910:** *[A United Reserve Bank of the United States](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=143)*. A detailed institutional project for a national reserve organization, framed to avoid the politically charged name “central bank.”
- **1910:** *[Principles That Must Underlie Monetary Reform in the United States](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=191)*. A programmatic essay or manifesto setting out non-negotiable principles for currency and reserve reform.
- **1910:** *[The Discount System in Europe](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=209)*. A technical comparative study prepared for the National Monetary Commission.
- **1911:** *[Circulating Credits and Bank Acceptances](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=245)*. A technical essay advocating an American acceptance market and a more liquid form of commercial credit.
- **1913:** *[The Owen-Glass Bill as Submitted to the Democratic Caucus: Some Criticisms and Suggestions](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=263)*. A legislative critique and revision memorandum addressed to an evolving bill rather than a freestanding theoretical work.
- **1913:** *[The Owen-Glass Bill: Should There Be Four or Eight Federal Reserve Banks?](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=299)*. An institutional-design memorandum concerning the number, hierarchy, and territorial organization of reserve banks.
- **1913:** *[The Owen-Glass Bill: Gold or Lawful Money, Note Issue, Government Bonds, and Similar Questions](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=319)*. A technical legislative memorandum proposing changes to monetary, note-issue, and bond provisions.

### Addresses and public interventions while on the Federal Reserve Board

- **1915–1918:** *[Address before the Members of the Twin City Bankers’ Club, St. Paul, Minnesota](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=333)*. A public address on the young Federal Reserve System; Warburg’s collection does not give it a more distinctive title.
- **1915–1918:** *[“Whence and Whither?”—The Future Financial Course of American Nations](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=345)*. A Pan-American financial address combining diagnosis with a proposal for international monetary cooperation.
- **1915–1918:** *[The Federal Reserve System and the Business Man](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=361)*. An explanatory and persuasive address translating central-bank policy for a commercial audience.
- **1915–1918:** *[Some Economic Problems of the Day](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=381)*. A policy address on contemporary economic and financial conditions.
- **1915–1918:** *[Progress in Banking Relations Between American Countries: International Gold Clearance Fund](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=403)*. An address containing an institutional project for international gold settlement; the second phrase functions as a subtitle, not securely as a separate work.
- **1915–1918:** *[New Opportunities for American Commerce and Industry](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=425)*. A public address linking the new banking machinery to foreign trade and industrial expansion.
- **1916:** *[The Federal Reserve System and the Banks](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/statements-speeches-paul-m-warburg-454/federal-reserve-system-banks-5325)*. An address to the New York State Bankers’ Association advocating broader participation in the System.
- **1916–1918:** *[The Reserve Problem and the Future of the Federal Reserve System](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=467)*. A policy address on reserve concentration and the System’s future development.
- **1915–1918:** *[Government and Business](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=507)*. An address on the proper wartime relationship between public administration and private enterprise.
- **1915–1918:** *[Trade Acceptances](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=533)*. A technical and promotional address intended to alter American commercial-credit practice.
- **1918:** *[Capital Issues for State and Municipal Debts and Their Relation to War Financing](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext#page=551)*. A wartime policy address accompanied by administrative data from the Federal Reserve Board’s Capital Issues Committee.

### Post–Federal Reserve Board addresses, essays, appeals, and resolutions

- **1918:** *[Some Phases of Financial Reconstruction](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/statements-speeches-paul-m-warburg-454/phases-financial-reconstruction-475621)*. An address to the War Emergency and Reconstruction Congress concerning postwar domestic and international reconstruction.
- **1918–1929:** *[The Organization of the American Acceptance Council](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. An institutional statement explaining and advocating the council Warburg helped create.
- **1918–1929:** *[Investment of American Capital Abroad](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A policy address on foreign investment and American responsibility in postwar reconstruction.
- **1918–1929:** *[Government Loans and Taxation](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A fiscal-policy essay or address.
- **1918–1929:** *[Some Problems of the Investment Banker](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A professional address on investment banking’s public and economic functions.
- **1918–1929:** *[Acceptances in Our Domestic and International Commerce](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A technical address continuing Warburg’s campaign for acceptance financing.
- **1919:** *[Amsterdam Economic Conference: International Appeal](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. An international public appeal: closer to a manifesto intended to produce coordinated action than to a descriptive essay.
- **1918–1929:** *[Address at the First Annual Meeting of the American Acceptance Council](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A presidential institutional address; its generic title resists treatment as an independently titled literary work.
- **1918–1929:** *[Fiscal and Currency Standards as the Future Measure of the Credit of Nations](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. An essay or address proposing standards by which national credit should be judged.
- **1918–1929:** *[Inflation as a World Problem and Our Relation Thereto](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A policy address treating inflation as an international rather than exclusively domestic problem.
- **1918–1929:** *[Europe at the Crossroads](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A programmatic intervention on European reconstruction.
- **1918–1929:** *[President’s Annual Address before the American Acceptance Council](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. An official annual address whose status lies between authored text and an act of institutional governance.
- **1918–1929:** *[Barking Up the Wrong Tree](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A polemical essay or address opposing a mistaken diagnosis of contemporary financial problems.
- **1918–1929:** *[Political Pressure and the Future of the Federal Reserve System](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A public warning about political interference with central-bank policy.
- **1918–1929:** *[Dollar Acceptances and Europe’s Financial Rehabilitation](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A technical proposal linking the dollar-acceptance market to European reconstruction.
- **1918–1929:** *[The Rehabilitation of Europe](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A policy essay or address on postwar European recovery.
- **1918–1929:** *[Uncle Sam’s Gold Policy](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A polemical intervention in American gold policy.
- **1918–1929:** *[Hammer or Anvil?](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A rhetorically framed policy address about whether the United States would shape or merely endure international financial change.
- **1918–1929:** *[Politics a Menace to the Federal Reserve System](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A defensive manifesto for central-bank independence.
- **1918–1929:** *[Theory and Practice—Price Fluctuations and the Discount Policies of Central Note-Issuing Banks](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A technical essay comparing monetary theory with central-bank practice.
- **1928:** *[Benjamin Strong—Preamble and Resolution Presented at the Tenth Annual Dinner of the American Acceptance Council](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A memorial resolution: an institutional speech-act that commemorates and formally records, rather than an essay that merely describes.

### Books and constructed textual corpora

- **1914:** *[Essays on Banking Reform in the United States](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440/fulltext)*. A deliberately assembled corpus in the *Proceedings of the Academy of Political Science*, collecting eleven pre–Federal Reserve essays; it is a new manifestation of those works, not eleven additional works. The 1930 edition notes the omission of *A Modified Central Bank of Issue* from this original collection.
- **1930:** *[The Federal Reserve System: Its Origin and Growth—Reflections and Recollections](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192)*. A two-volume historical argument and documentary construction: volume I is Warburg’s retrospective narrative, while volume II incorporates the earlier texts listed above. It is therefore both an obra in its own right and a curated manifestation of prior work.
- **1930:** *[The Federal Reserve System: Its Origin and Growth, Volume I](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-1-5441)*. The narrative volume of Warburg’s retrospective, written partly to contest Carter Glass’s claims about authorship of Federal Reserve reform.
- **1930:** *[The Federal Reserve System: Its Origin and Growth, Volume II—Addresses and Essays](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/federal-reserve-system-origin-growth-192/volume-2-addresses-essays-1907-1924-5440)*. A selected documentary anthology of forty-four texts. Its own foreword expressly says that the post-1914 material is selective and that articles unrelated to banking were excluded, so it cannot be treated as a complete collected-works edition.

### Collaborative institutional projects

- **20–30 November 1910:** *[The Jekyll Island plan for a Reserve Association of America](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/jekyll-island-conference)*. A collaborative institutional design produced by Warburg, Nelson Aldrich, A. Piatt Andrew, Henry Davison, Arthur Shelton, and Frank Vanderlip. It became the basis of the Aldrich Plan; sole authorship cannot be assigned to Warburg.
- **1910–1912:** *[The Aldrich Plan / National Reserve Association proposal](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/jekyll-island-conference)*. A legislative and institutional project developed from the Jekyll Island design and revised through the National Monetary Commission. Warburg was a major contributor, but the plan was collective and formally issued under Aldrich and the commission.
- **1913:** *[The Federal Reserve System as an institutional design](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/jekyll-island-conference)*. Warburg’s writings and consultations materially shaped its technical architecture, but the enacted system was a congressional and administrative creation incorporating competing proposals. Contemporary and later writers disputed the allocation of credit among Warburg, Carter Glass, Robert Owen, Woodrow Wilson, and others; calling it simply “Warburg’s work” would erase that dispute.
- **1919:** *[American Acceptance Council](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/people/paul-m-warburg)*. An organization founded by Warburg to develop the American acceptance market; he served as the first chairman of its executive committee. As a collective institution, it is better classified as an organized project than as his sole obra.
- **1921:** *[International Acceptance Bank](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/people/paul-m-warburg)*. A banking institution organized by Warburg to facilitate international finance and European reconstruction. It was a corporate, collaborative creation, not an individually authored object.
- **1907–1913:** *The campaign for an American central-bank system*. A distributed body of plans, memoranda, private consultations, speeches, and public persuasion rather than one fixed work; Federal Reserve historians describe Warburg’s sustained campaign but also show that it passed through many hands and institutional forms. It resists being counted as a single text or credited as a solitary creation. [Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/1989/paul-warburgs-crusade-to-establish-a-central-bank-in-the-united-states)

### Archival remainder and limits of the corpus

- **1904–1932:** *[Paul Moritz Warburg Papers](https://archives.yale.edu/repositories/12/resources/4722)*. An archival accumulation of correspondence, drafts, documents, and materials relating to numerous writings, not a single work made or titled by Warburg. Its survival demonstrates why the forty-four-text 1930 selection cannot establish a literally complete oeuvre; unpublished drafts and untitled administrative texts resist work-by-work enumeration without manuscript-level research.
- **1915–1923:** *[Statements and Speeches of Paul M. Warburg](https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/statements-speeches-paul-m-warburg-454)*. A later archival aggregation of public transcripts, not a collection conceived by Warburg as one work. It overlaps the 1930 anthology and should not be counted as an additional authored book.
