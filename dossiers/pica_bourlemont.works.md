## Works by Pica Bourlémont

### Embodied creations

- **1181 or 1182–1226:** Francis of Assisi, baptized Giovanni. A human body and life jointly made by his mother and Pietro di Bernardone—not an authored *obra* in the ordinary sense, and therefore resistant to classification as a “work.” The birth year is disputed; early sources identify his parents but do not securely establish that his mother was French, noble, or a Bourlémont ([Thomas of Celano and other early sources](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/stfran-lives.asp); [Maria Pia Alberzoni](https://www.sanfrancescopatronoditalia.it/notizie/attualita/alberzoni-san-francesco-e-la-madre-48568); [MeTU historical survey](https://www.umbriametu.it/museums/museo-civico-palazzo-dei-consoli-gubbio/saint-francis-and-the-wolf-of-gubbio/the-figure-of-monna-pica-mother-of-francis-facts-and-controversies/?lang=en)).
- Angelo, called in Assisi records “Angelo, son of Pica.” He is a second embodied creation rather than a titled work; his birth date is not securely known, and even his name comes from archival and later testimony rather than the earliest biography of Francis ([Franciscan archival summary](https://ofsaustralia.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/LESSON-1-FRANCIS-OF-ASSISI-BIRTH-NAME-AND-FAMILY.pdf); [Alberzoni](https://www.sanfrancescopatronoditalia.it/notizie/attualita/alberzoni-san-francesco-e-la-madre-48568)).

### No securely attributable works

- No surviving text, building, image, manufactured object, manifesto, protocol, artistic project, or other conventional *obra* is attributed to Pica in the standard corpus of early Franciscan sources. Statements and actions assigned to Francis’s unnamed mother by Thomas of Celano and Bonaventure are hagiographical representations of her, not surviving things made by her; they should not be converted into a fictitious oeuvre ([*Francis of Assisi: Early Documents* project description](https://franciscanstudies.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/sl-133-1.pdf); [MeTU historical survey](https://www.umbriametu.it/museums/museo-civico-palazzo-dei-consoli-gubbio/saint-francis-and-the-wolf-of-gubbio/the-figure-of-monna-pica-mother-of-francis-facts-and-controversies/?lang=en)).
- The identity “Pica Bourlémont (c. 1160–1226)” itself is not securely established. Medieval evidence supports a woman called Pica—probably Francis’s mother—but “Giovanna,” noble Provençal origin, membership in the Bourlémont family, and the proposed life dates belong to uncertain or much later traditions; consequently no works can responsibly be catalogued under that full identity without qualification ([Treccani, “Pietro Bernardone”](https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/pietro-bernardone_%28Enciclopedia-Dantesca%29/); [MeTU historical survey](https://www.umbriametu.it/museums/museo-civico-palazzo-dei-consoli-gubbio/saint-francis-and-the-wolf-of-gubbio/the-figure-of-monna-pica-mother-of-francis-facts-and-controversies/?lang=en)).
