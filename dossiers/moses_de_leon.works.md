## Works by Moses de Leon

### Hebrew kabbalistic works issued under his name

- **1286:** *Shushan Edut* (“Lily of Testimony”). De Leon identified this kabbalistic treatise as his first work; only about half survives, principally in Cambridge MS Add. 505. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **1287:** *Sefer ha-Rimmon* (“Book of the Pomegranate”). A systematic exposition of the mystical reasons for the commandments, preserved in several manuscripts; its close verbal and conceptual relations with Zoharic homilies are central to scholarship on de Leon’s authorship. ([Wolfson, critical edition](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.2307/j.ctvzgb92b); [Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **1290:** *Ha-Nefesh ha-Hakhamah* (“The Wise Soul”), also called *Sefer ha-Mishkal* (“Book of the Balance”). A kabbalistic treatise written for a pupil named Jacob, concerning the soul, death, transmigration, resurrection, and reward; the Basel 1608 printing is corrupt and incorporates additions by another contemporary kabbalist. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **1292:** *Shekel ha-Kodesh* (“The Holy Shekel”). A kabbalistic treatise on the divine world, the soul, judgment, punishment, and atonement; an important manuscript witness is Oxford, Bodleian MS Opp. 563. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **c. 1293:** *Mishkan ha-Edut* (“Tabernacle of Testimony”), also called *Sefer ha-Sodot* (“Book of Secrets”). An eschatological kabbalistic work on the soul’s fate after death, judgment, heaven, and punishment; it incorporates material from de Leon’s projected re-creation of a Book of Enoch. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **after 1293:** *Maskiyyot Kesef* (“Settings of Silver”). A kabbalistic commentary on Jewish prayer and a sequel to the lost *Tappuhei Zahav*, surviving in manuscript. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated:** *Commentary on the Vision of Ezekiel*. A merkavah or divine-chariot commentary transmitted in numerous manuscripts as an independent book, although it also belongs to the textual complex of *Mishkan ha-Edut* and therefore resists classification as wholly separate. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated:** *Untitled commentary on the Ten Sefirot and penances*. A lengthy, partly preserved kabbalistic treatise, much of it in Munich MS 47; lacking an authorial title, it should not be converted into a securely named “book.” ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated:** *Sod Eser Sefirot Belimah* (“Secret of the Ten Sefirot of Nothingness”). A distinct commentary on the ten sefirot preserved in El Escorial MS G-III-14. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated:** *Responsa on points of Kabbalah*. A collection of answers to mystical questions rather than a continuously composed book; Isaiah Tishby edited the surviving material in *Kovez al Yad* 5 (1951). ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated:** *Untitled treatise on various mystical themes*. A manuscript work surviving in Schocken Library MS Kabbalah 14 and Vatican MS 428; its absence of a stable title is part of its material status. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))

### Anonymous and pseudepigraphic creations

- **before 1286, according to Scholem; stratification disputed:** *Midrash ha-Neʿelam* (“Hidden Midrash”). A pseudepigraphic mystical midrash written chiefly in Aramaic, with related Hebrew ethical and eschatological pieces, and later incorporated into the Zoharic corpus. Scholem assigned it to de Leon, while later source-critical scholarship treats the formation and boundaries of both this work and the Zohar as composite and contested. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon); [Bar-Asher, “Sefer ha-Neʿelam”](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/_file_1434612299.pdf))
- **early period; exact date uncertain:** *Sefer Or Zaruʿa* (“Light Is Sown”). An anonymous creation treatise traditionally dated 1288/89 but reassigned by Avishai Bar-Asher to de Leon’s earlier, pre-theosophical phase; its reconstruction is complicated because one manuscript version was expanded by another kabbalist into a commentary covering Genesis 1–6. ([Bar-Asher, “Sefer ha-Neʿelam”](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/_file_1434612299.pdf); [Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **early period; undated:** *Sod Darkhe ha-Otiyyot* (“Secret of the Ways of the Letters”). An anonymous, strongly pseudepigraphic discourse on Hebrew letters, transmitted more widely than the other works in de Leon’s early corpus; its attribution rests on modern comparative scholarship rather than an authorial signature. ([Bar-Asher, “Sefer ha-Neʿelam”](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/_file_1434612299.pdf))
- **early period; fragmentary:** *Sefer ha-Neʿelam* (“The Hidden Book”). A pseudepigraphic work long known principally through references, from which Bar-Asher identified a substantial surviving fragment and argued for de Leon’s authorship. It remains a reconstructed manuscript work rather than a securely bounded medieval book. ([Bar-Asher, “Sefer ha-Neʿelam”](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/_file_1434612299.pdf))
- **undated:** *Testament of Rabbi Eliezer the Great*, also called *Orhot Hayyim* (“Ways of Life”). A Hebrew ethical pseudepigraphon placed in the voice of an ancient rabbi; its shifting attribution between Eliezer ben Hyrcanus and Simeon bar Yohai exposes de Leon’s construction of fictional authority. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **late literary period; undated:** *Sod Aseret ha-Dibberot* (“Secret of the Ten Commandments”). An unsigned theosophical commentary identified by Bar-Asher as de Leon’s; it recasts Zoharic homilies in Hebrew and attributes innovative teachings to purported ancient authorities. ([Bar-Asher, English abstract](https://www.magnespress.co.il/api/magnes/book/5749/preview))
- **late literary period; undated:** *Untitled commentary related to Sefer Etz ha-Hayyim*. A fragmentary, unsigned commentary whose text corresponds to the otherwise lost work cited in *Sod Aseret ha-Dibberot*; its precise independence and original boundaries remain uncertain. ([Bar-Asher, English abstract](https://www.magnespress.co.il/api/magnes/book/5749/preview))
- **late 1270s–early 1300s; authorship and extent disputed:** *Sefer ha-Zohar* (“Book of Splendor”), or the Zoharic corpus. This is not straightforwardly one book: it is an evolving pseudepigraphic body of Aramaic and Hebrew midrash, homily, narrative, and mystical exposition presented through Simeon bar Yohai and his circle. Scholem attributed its bulk—but not every later stratum—to de Leon; subsequent scholarship continues to debate single authorship, collaborative production, redaction, and the boundaries of his contribution, so “creation,” “project,” or “corpus” fits better than an uncontested authored work. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon); [Bar-Asher, “Sefer ha-Neʿelam”](https://openscholar.huji.ac.il/sites/default/files/jstudies/files/_file_1434612299.pdf))

### Lost works and projects known by title or reference

- **undated; lost:** *Tappuhei Zahav* (“Apples of Gold”). A lost kabbalistic work known because de Leon describes *Maskiyyot Kesef* as its sequel. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost or unidentified:** *Sefer Etz ha-Hayyim* (“Book of the Tree of Life”). A work cited as his own by the author of *Sod Aseret ha-Dibberot*; no complete copy is known, though a brief quotation appears to match an otherwise untitled fragment. ([Bar-Asher, English abstract](https://www.magnespress.co.il/api/magnes/book/5749/preview))
- **undated; incomplete project:** *A new Book of Enoch*. De Leon intended to construct a pseudepigraphic Enochic book, but only material embedded in *Mishkan ha-Edut* is known; the intention itself is therefore the recoverable project, not a demonstrably completed book. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost:** *Sefer Pardes* (“Book of the Orchard/Paradise”). A work attributed to de Leon in the standard scholarly bibliography but no longer extant. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost:** *Shaʿarei Tzedek* (“Gates of Righteousness”). A work on Ecclesiastes known from bibliographical testimony rather than a surviving complete text. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost:** *Mashal ha-Kadmoni* (“The Ancient Parable”). A lost work whose title echoes that of de Leon’s associate Isaac ibn Sahula; its contents and form cannot now be securely characterized. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost:** *Responsa concerning Elijah*. A set of answers to questions about Elijah, known only through references; whether it constituted a deliberately unified book is uncertain. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost:** *Commentary on the Song of Songs*. An exegetical work reported in the standard bibliography but not securely extant. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
- **undated; lost:** *Polemic against the Sadducees or Karaites*. A controversial text mentioned by Abner of Burgos; even the identity of its opponents is uncertain, and the work survives only as testimony to a polemical intervention. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))

### Rejected or unstable attribution

- **mid-fourteenth century; not by Moses de Leon:** *Sefer ha-Shem* (“Book of the Name”). From the fifteenth century onward this treatise on the names and epithets of the sefirot was attributed to de Leon, but Scholem assigns it to a different kabbalist named Moses; it belongs here only as a rejected attribution, not as one of de Leon’s obras. ([Scholem, *Encyclopaedia Judaica*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/moses-ben-shem-tov-de-leon))
