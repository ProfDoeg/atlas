## Works by Diego Armando Maradona

### Football performances

- **1976–1980:** *Argentinos Juniors performances*. A body of improvised, collaborative football: Maradona’s first-team play, goals and assists cannot be separated from teammates, opponents, rules and spectators, so it is better understood as performance practice than as a collection of self-contained works. [S1]
- **1977–1994:** *Argentina senior national-team performances*. Ninety-one recognized internationals and thirty-four goals form a discontinuous collective obra spanning four World Cups; the statistical catalogue treats matches and goals as events, not solely authored objects. [S2]
- **1979:** *Argentina at the FIFA World Youth Championship*. A seven-match, six-goal tournament performance culminating in the championship; Maradona was its captain and principal player, but the made thing was irreducibly collective. [S3]
- **1981–1982; 1995–1997:** *Boca Juniors performances*. Two widely separated playing periods—an early championship campaign and the final stage of his professional career—constitute one recurring club project rather than a single finished work. [S1]
- **1982–1984:** *FC Barcelona performances*. A body of club play interrupted by illness, serious injury and the 1984 Copa del Rey-final brawl; its incompleteness and violence resist treatment as a polished oeuvre. [S1]
- **1984–1991:** *SSC Napoli performances*. A seven-season collaborative obra through which Maradona’s play and public persona became entangled with the city of Naples; it includes two Serie A championships, the Coppa Italia, the UEFA Cup and the Supercoppa Italiana. [S1]
- **22 June 1986:** *The Hand of God*. A goal-manifestation produced with the hand against England and incorrectly allowed by the referee. Its status is intrinsically disputed: it is simultaneously an official goal, an admitted infringement and a deliberately named public myth. [S4]
- **22 June 1986:** *The Goal of the Century*. Maradona’s second goal against England, a roughly sixty-metre solo movement past five outfield opponents and the goalkeeper; FIFA later adopted the popular superlative as its conventional title. [S4]
- **2–29 June 1986:** *Mexico 1986*. A seven-match tournament performance—five goals, decisive creative play and captaincy of the winning Argentina side—that functions almost as a football Gesamtkunstwerk: bodily improvisation, collective tactics, television, commentary and national mythology are inseparable. It remains a team achievement, not Maradona’s sole authorship. [S3] [S4]
- **1992–1993:** *Sevilla FC performances*. A short post-suspension return to European club football; a partially realized sporting project rather than a completed, autonomous work. [S1]
- **1993–1994:** *Newell’s Old Boys performances*. Five official appearances comprise an unusually fragmentary club project, terminated before a sustained body of play could emerge. [S1]
- **1994:** *Argentina at the 1994 FIFA World Cup*. A two-match return ending after a positive drug test and expulsion from the tournament. This is best classified as an interrupted project whose incompleteness is part of its meaning. [S2]
- **10 November 2001:** *Maradona’s farewell match*. A staged testimonial at La Bombonera combining football, autobiography, celebrity spectacle and ritual leave-taking; because the occasion deliberately fused sport, television, family and public address, it approaches a Gesamtkunstwerk. [S5]

### Coaching projects

- **1994:** *Textil Mandiyú*. Maradona’s first managerial project, brief and unsuccessful; the “work” is a collective process of selection, training and match direction rather than a durable object. [S6]
- **1995:** *Racing Club*. A second short managerial project whose limited duration resists description as a completed coaching system. [S6]
- **2008–2010:** *Argentina national team*. A twenty-four-match managerial project culminating in the 2010 World Cup quarter-final; tactical authorship was shared with staff and players and remains contested in assessments of Maradona’s work. [S6]
- **2011–2012:** *Al-Wasl*. A club-management project in the United Arab Emirates, terminated after one season. [S6]
- **2017–2018:** *Fujairah*. A second Emirati coaching project, ending after the club failed to secure automatic promotion; its intended result was not achieved. [S6]
- **2018–2019:** *Dorados de Sinaloa*. A two-tournament managerial project that twice reached, but lost, the Mexican second-division promotion final. [S6]
- **2019–2020:** *Gimnasia y Esgrima La Plata*. Maradona’s final coaching project, interrupted first by the COVID-19 suspension and then by his death; it therefore has no authored conclusion. [S6]

### Books and oral autobiographies

- **2000:** *Yo soy el Diego (de la gente)*. An autobiography made from Maradona’s first-person oral testimony with Daniel Arcucci and Ernesto Cherquis Bialo; editions sometimes shorten the title to *Yo soy el Diego*. The collaborative shaping means it is Maradona’s text without being a conventionally solitary act of writing. [S7] [S8]
- **2016:** *México 86: Mi mundial, mi verdad: Así ganamos la Copa*. A second oral-autobiographical book, prepared with Daniel Arcucci and confined to the 1986 World Cup. Catalogues and editions rearrange or abbreviate its subtitle, so no single shortened form should be treated as uniquely authoritative. [S9]

### Recorded music

- **1986–1987:** *Querida amiga*. A Spanish-language song recorded with Pimpinela and addressed to Maradona’s mother and mothers generally; it is a collaborative vocal recording, not a Maradona solo composition. Sources date the recording and album appearance to 1986, while the separately issued single is also catalogued as 1987. [S10] [S11]
- **1987:** *Mia dolce amica*. The Italian-language manifestation of *Querida amiga*, again performed with Pimpinela. It is an alternate recording/version rather than an independent song-cycle or album. [S10] [S11]

### Television works

- **15 August–7 November 2005:** *La noche del 10*. A thirteen-episode live entertainment and interview series presented by Maradona and produced by Artear; his role included hosting, interviewing, performance and the staging of his own mediated persona, but not sole authorship of the programme. [S12] [S13]
- **2014:** *De Zurda*. A World Cup television series co-presented with Víctor Hugo Morales for teleSUR and Argentina’s TV Pública. Part analysis programme, part political platform and part celebrity performance, it resists separation into journalism, spectacle and manifesto. [S14]
- **2014:** *De Zurda: la película*. A teleSUR feature assembled from the programme’s first season and previously unseen material. Maradona is its central performer and co-presenting intelligence, but the film’s production and editorial authorship belong collectively to teleSUR’s makers. [S15]

### Screen performances

- **1980:** *¡Qué linda es mi familia!*. A cameo as himself in Palito Ortega’s musical comedy; Maradona contributed a screen performance, although another performer reportedly dubbed his voice. It is not a film authored by Maradona, and that weak form of “making” should remain explicit. [S16]
- **1981:** *Te rompo el rating*. A special appearance as himself in Hugo Sofovich’s comedy. This is a performed contribution rather than a Maradona obra in the strong authorial sense. [S17]
- **1996:** *El día que Maradona conoció a Gardel*. A dramatic feature in which Maradona performs a fictionalized version of himself. Direction and screenplay belong to Rodolfo Pagliere and collaborators, so Maradona made a role within the film, not the film as a whole. [S18]
- **2019:** *Diego Maradona*. Maradona supplied his voice and testimony to Asif Kapadia’s documentary, alongside extensive archival manifestations of his earlier performances. He is participant and raw material rather than the documentary’s author; calling the entire film his work would erase that distinction. [S19]

### Public speech and theatre

- **10 November 2001:** *“La pelota no se mancha” farewell address*. A brief spoken manifesto delivered after the farewell match: it acts by separating the value of football from the speaker’s admitted errors and by sealing his public departure. The title is conventional, derived from its best-known phrase rather than from a formally published text. [S5] [S20]
- **16 January 2017:** *Tre volte dieci*. A one-night theatrical narration at Naples’s Teatro di San Carlo, created and performed with Alessandro Siani to mark thirty years since Napoli’s first league championship. It combined reminiscence, comic dialogue, civic ritual and celebrity presence rather than operating as a conventional scripted play. [S21]
