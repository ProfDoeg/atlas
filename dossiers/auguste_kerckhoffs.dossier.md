# Auguste Kerckhoffs: Research Dossier

**Full-life chronological dossier**  
**Prepared:** 30 August 2026  
**Subject:** Jean Guillaume Auguste Victor Alexandre François Hubert Kerckhoffs, generally known as Auguste Kerckhoffs  
**Coverage:** 1835–1903, with posthumous reception through 2026

## Research Note and Evidence Standards

This dossier is organized as a documentary reconstruction rather than a continuous essay. The most important biographical source is Jean-Claude Caraco, Rémi Géraud-Stewart, and David Naccache's archive-based study, [“Kerckhoffs' Legacy”](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/556) (2020). Its authors used municipal and national archives in the Netherlands, France, Germany, Portugal, and Switzerland; reproduced civil-status records, Kerckhoffs's Latin curriculum vitae, his doctorate, French naturalization papers, school evaluations, correspondence, a Swiss death certificate, and the Montparnasse burial record; and distinguished archival findings from conjecture. Claims attributed below to “the 2020 archival study” refer to that work.

The dossier also uses Kerckhoffs's own [*La cryptographie militaire*](https://www.petitcolas.net/kerckhoffs/la_cryptographie_militaire_i.htm) (1883), contemporary catalogues and periodicals transcribed or reproduced by the archival study, David Kahn's *The Codebreakers*, institutional records, and modern scholarship on cryptography and Volapük. Where an original record is not independently accessible online, the record is identified through the archival study rather than represented as directly inspected. “Documented” means supported by a record or contemporaneous publication; “reported” identifies a later account; “inference,” “conjecture,” “unverified,” and “disputed” are used deliberately.

The surviving record is uneven. Kerckhoffs's public years in education and Volapük are relatively well documented; his private life, 1895–1900 activities, widow's later life, estate, and the fate of his papers remain poorly known. No complete personal archive is known to survive.

### The name “Nieuwenhof”

Nieuwenhof, locally *Nuinhof*, was the Nuth lease farm at which Kerckhoffs was born; the farm had medieval origins and the birth house was demolished in 1934 during construction of the A76. Kerckhoffs used *de*, *van*, and *von Nieuwenhof* in different settings. The style should not be taken, by itself, as proof of a hereditary noble title. A nineteenth-century genealogy used to support the family's noble pretensions was partly fabricated; the matter is treated below. The shorter “Auguste Kerckhoffs” became his normal professional name.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents

- **Father:** Jean Guillaume Kerckhoffs (1798–1883), clerk-registrar and justice of the peace at Oirsbeek, mayor of Nuth during the Belgian-administration period, approximately 1830–1836. A later report also records his candidacy for the Dutch House of Representatives in 1850. Descriptions of him merely as a “well-to-do landlord” compress a more specific legal and municipal career.
- **Mother:** Jeannette/Jeanette Elisabeth Lintjens (1814–1887). The spelling with two *n*s appears in Nuth documentation; other genealogies use one. No detailed evidence of her education, property, or public activity was found.

The household was prosperous and socially prominent by local standards. French given names and a family tradition of service under Napoleonic administration reflect the Francophone orientation of some upper- and middle-class Limburg families. A contemporary 1888 newspaper sketch called the family traditionally Francophile. That characterization is consistent with Auguste's later naturalization and marriage but is not a substitute for an independently documented political program.

### Siblings

The 2020 archival study identifies four siblings, though dates remain incomplete:

| Victor Kerckhoffs (1838–1908) | Married Elisa Voncken; children recorded as Maria-José (1884–1976), Pauline (1885–1978), and Alphons (born 1888) |

| Jean-Louis Kerckhoffs | Married Maria-Anna Kerckhoffs; no securely identified children in the consulted study |

These data rebut an overly broad claim that the family had no descendants, although no surviving descendant of Auguste himself has been identified.

### Extended family, claimed nobility, and the Bokkenrijders

Auguste's great-uncle **Joseph Romain Louis Kerckhoffs** (1789–1867) was a physician in Napoleon's army, later chief physician of Dutch military hospitals, and a Belgian knight. Seeking to support his social ascent and the name *van den Kerckhove van de Varent*, he commissioned an 1839 genealogy, *Mémoire généalogique sur la branche de la très-ancienne noble famille de Van den Kerckhove…*, associated with P. E. de Borcht. The 2020 investigators, drawing on Dutch genealogical research and a corrective sheet in the CBG family-history collection, conclude that the pedigree is reliable only back to a limited point and then attaches the family to a nonexistent line with invented titles and heraldry. The extinct noble *van den Kerckhove* branch was not Auguste's demonstrated line.

The reputational motive may have included distancing the family from **Joseph and Balthasar Kerckhoffs**, eighteenth-century leaders associated with the Bokkenrijders or “Buckriders,” criminal bands surrounded by demonic folklore. The archival reconstruction places those men in an extended collateral relationship, not as proof that Auguste descended directly from criminal leaders. The Bokkenrijder story later became regional folklore, television material, and a theme-park attraction. It is both a documented episode in extended-family reputation management and a mythologized tradition; it supplies no evidence of criminality by Auguste.

### Marriage and child

Kerckhoffs married **Marie Émilie Thévenin** (born 13 February 1835 at Coulommiers) in the 10th arrondissement of Paris on **5 July 1864**. Her parents were bailiff Nicolas Jean Baptiste Thévenin and Émilie Delphine Santerre. Through her mother, Marie Émilie was a granddaughter of Revolutionary general **Antoine Joseph Santerre** (1752–1809). Before marriage she was recorded at 118 rue du Faubourg-Saint-Denis, Paris. The archival investigators raise, but cannot prove, the possibility that she and her mother operated or inherited a guesthouse there.

Their only known child was **Wilhelmine Émilie Pauline Kerckhoffs**, usually Pauline, born at 1 a.m. on **13 May 1865** at the family home, 23 quai d'Almont, Melun. Her grave later gave 11 May, a conflict resolved in favor of the civil birth register. Pauline was certified as a Volapük teacher during her father's movement years. She married **Alfred Armand Mowat** on 10 April 1890; HEC director Édouard Jourdan witnessed the marriage. Alfred's father, the English-born numismatist and archaeologist Robert Knight Mowat, had received the Legion of Honour.

Pauline died in Paris on **9 January 1893**, age 27, while living at 10 rue des Feuillantines. Her cause of death is not stated in the evidence consulted. The 2020 study mentions cholera during the 1892–1893 epidemic only as a possibility that might explain burial-record problems; it is not an established diagnosis. No child of Pauline and Alfred has been securely identified. A succession file exists, but the beneficiary has not been identified.

Marie Émilie acquired the perpetual family burial concession in 1903 and, under French law, would ordinarily have controlled her husband's literary rights. Her subsequent life, death, estate administration, and disposal of his papers remain unknown in the consulted research.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1835–1852

Kerckhoffs was born into a Catholic, locally prominent household at Nieuwenhof farm in Nuth. No diary, childhood correspondence, or direct evidence about his home education survives in the accessible record.

From **October 1847 to August 1852** he attended the boarding school at Rolduc, Kerkrade, near Aachen. Surviving school results show consistently strong but not uniformly first-place work:

- 1847–1848: second prize; second-best student in sixth grade.
- 1848–1849: third prize; fourth in fifth grade.
- 1849–1850: second prizes in mathematics, history, and geography.
- 1850–1851: second prize; second in third grade.
- 1851–1852: poetry class.

In one examination he ranked fourth of fourteen. A conduct penalty moved him from fourth to seventh place because his behavior was “not entirely satisfactory.” The record is too slight to justify later psychological interpretation, but it is useful counterevidence to uniformly decorous retrospective portraits.

David Kahn reports that Kerckhoffs then spent about eighteen months in Britain to improve his English. Kahn also reports that he later accompanied the American Clarence Prentice, son of *Louisville Journal* founder George D. Prentice, through western Europe. These episodes are plausible and repeatedly cited, but the 2020 archival biography did not document them from a primary record. They should therefore be treated as reported rather than fully corroborated.

## Education and Formation

### Maastricht, Liège, Leuven, Bonn, and Tübingen

Kerckhoffs's own 1876 Latin curriculum vitae gives the following sequence:

- 1852–1853: gymnasium study at Maastricht.
- 1853–1855: two years of philosophy at the University of Liège.
- 1855–1857: two years of natural sciences at the Catholic University of Leuven.
- 1873–1876: German philology and early German language/literature at Bonn and Tübingen.

Older biographies often say he took degrees in letters and science at Liège, or even a doctorate there. The stronger reconstruction is more cautious: his Latin résumé styled him a fine-arts graduate and master of natural sciences, but his securely documented doctorate was the later German degree. At Tübingen he received the degree **Doctor of Philosophy and Master of Liberal Arts**, *bene comprobatam*, in 1876. His dissertation appeared at Paderborn in 1877 as *Daniel Casper von Lohenstein's Trauerspiele mit besonderer Berücksichtigung der Cleopatra: Beitrag zur Geschichte des Dramas im XVII. Jahrhundert*.

His supervisor was the Romanist and Germanist **Adelbert von Keller** (1812–1883). The thesis was dedicated to **Alexander Reifferscheid** (1847–1909), probably an important Bonn influence or informal mentor. It treated Lohenstein's *Cleopatra* and the technical development of seventeenth-century German tragedy.

### Intellectual formation

The surviving works show an unusually broad formation rather than a single disciplinary trajectory:

- Comparative command of French, Dutch/Flemish, English, German, and Latin; working engagement with constructed languages.
- Philology and pedagogy, especially practical language instruction.
- Archaeology and the then-new physical anthropology of European prehistory.
- Cryptographic history, cipher mechanisms, linguistic frequency analysis, and practical cryptanalysis.
- Catholic identity alongside secular educational, feminist, anti-antisemitic, and Dreyfusard public positions.

No evidence was found of Freemasonry, an occult society, or esoteric initiation. His work on religion and monumental art and his Catholic self-identification do not establish occult affiliation.

## Early Career, 1857–1873

### Eindhoven, Meaux, and Melun

After passing public examinations *cum laude*, according to his own curriculum vitae, Kerckhoffs taught at Eindhoven from **1857 to 1860**. He then moved permanently into the French educational world:

- **1860–1863:** teacher at a private institution in Meaux.
- **19 October 1863–5 March 1873:** English and German teacher at the collège in Melun.

He could substitute in Latin, Greek, history, and mathematics. Administrative reports praised his learning and pedagogy while later evaluations noted classroom-discipline problems. In 1867 the departmental council authorized him to offer a free winter English course in Melun. He also helped found the **Société d'encouragement à l'instruction**, serving as secretary-librarian without pay; this service later contributed to an academic honor.

At Melun, Kerckhoffs married, became a father, and built a mixed income from salary, board, and private instruction. His house at 23 quai d'Almont no longer survives.

### Franco-Prussian War and National Guard

During the Franco-Prussian War, Kerckhoffs registered in the **National Guard** and served consistently in the prison service, according to the prefect's naturalization inquiry. Rumors circulated that his planned German studies reflected relations with the enemy. The prefect investigated and declared the allegation unfounded. There is no known arrest, charge, trial, or adverse finding.

The specific Guard unit and duties are unknown. Later speculation that this service placed him in military intelligence is not documentary fact.

### Naturalization and finances

On **16 March 1870**, Kerckhoffs petitioned the Minister of Justice for authorization to establish legal domicile under article 13 of the Napoleonic Code. The prefect described him as intelligent, learned, diligent, and morally blameless. Authorization was granted on **23 May 1870**. After the statutory interval he applied for citizenship; the Council of State approved him on **19 November 1873**, and the decree was published on **11 December 1873**.

The 1873 inquiry provides the best surviving financial snapshot:

- Estimated estate: **25,000 francs**.
- Annual public salary: about **2,000 francs**.
- Estimated annual income from lodging and teaching foreign pupils: **3,000–4,000 francs**.

The archival study notes that the median French daily wage around 1880 was approximately 3.34 francs. It also gives the franc's gold content at roughly 0.29 grams; the estate therefore represented about 7.25 kilograms of gold at the legal-metal measure, though that is not a full purchasing-power conversion. The figures describe comfort rather than extreme wealth. No company ownership, securities portfolio, major property acquisition, debt proceeding, bankruptcy, or inheritance dispute has been located.

## Chronological Life History

### 1873–1880: German study, Portuguese patronage, and attempted military-school appointment

Kerckhoffs took leave in March 1873 and went to Bonn, later Tübingen, to deepen his German philology and qualify for higher-level teaching. Evidence of lodging at the Bellevue Hotel at Rhöndorf suggests comfortable means, but it does not prove ownership or continuous luxury.

He supported himself partly as tutor to **Rodrigo/José Ferreira Pereira Felício de São Mamede**, later second Count of São Mamede and secretary to the Portuguese king. After returning to France in 1876, Kerckhoffs tutored two younger São Mamede children. Portuguese archival registration confirms honors arising from this service:

- Knight, later **Commander of the Portuguese Order of Christ**, with the commander's registration dated 11 August 1879.
- **Officer of the Portuguese Military Order of Saint James of the Sword**, an order for scientific, literary, and artistic merit despite its historical “military” designation.

The family returned to France in 1876 and lived at **11 boulevard du Château, Neuilly-sur-Seine**.

Kerckhoffs sought a German-language chair at a prestigious French military institution. Kahn dates an application to the École Militaire Supérieure to 1878; the archival study documents a failed **1880 application to the École Polytechnique**, rejected after an administrative error about his nationality. His sustained interest in military education helps contextualize *La cryptographie militaire*, but does not prove employment by the army.

A contemporary press item and Kerckhoffs's own later writing indicate that he knew and corresponded with **Victor Hugo** around 1860. The surviving record does not establish a close friendship, frequent meetings, or political collaboration.

### 1881–1883: HEC, École Arago, anthropology, and cryptography

On **1 November 1881**, Kerckhoffs took the German chair at the École des hautes études commerciales de Paris, then at 108 boulevard Malesherbes. He also taught German at the municipal **École Arago**. In 1883 he lived at **62 rue Claude-Bernard**, Paris, and unsuccessfully sought a chair at one of the normal schools for teachers.

In 1883 the Ministry of Public Education named him **Officier d'Académie** for educational service, the historical distinction later incorporated into the Palmes académiques. He left École Arago that year. On **19 July 1883** he joined the **Société d'anthropologie de Paris** under the full style Kerckhoffs von Nieuwenhof; the [CTHS institutional notice](https://cths.fr/an/savant.php?id=104063) also records his membership in the Société d'archéologie, sciences, lettres et arts de Seine-et-Marne.

### 1883: *La cryptographie militaire*

Kerckhoffs published the work in two installments in the *Journal des sciences militaires*, new series, volume IX: pages 5–38 in January and 161–191 in February 1883. An offprint followed from Librairie militaire L. Baudoin: vi + 64 pages, with illustrations and tables. The [HathiTrust catalogue](https://catalog.hathitrust.org/Record/100321556) confirms the offprint's physical and publication data.

The work was not merely a six-point manifesto. It contained:

1. A history of cryptography and military secret writing.
2. A critique of current French practice and of reliance on secret codebooks.
3. Operational requirements created by the electric telegraph and large military organizations.
4. Classification of transposition, monoalphabetic, polyalphabetic, and code systems.
5. Frequency-analysis instruction.
6. Cryptanalytic treatment of double-key or variable-base systems.
7. Discussions of grilles, cipher disks, the Saint-Cyr slide, dictionaries, and cipher machines.
8. A worked solution of an authentic encrypted Havas telegram concerning British operations in Egypt.

#### The six desiderata

Kerckhoffs distinguished private, temporary correspondence from a military system intended for many commanders over an indefinite period. His six conditions were:

1. The system must be materially/practically, if not mathematically, indecipherable.
2. **“Il faut qu'il n'exige pas le secret”**: the system must not require secrecy and may fall into enemy hands without inconvenience.
3. The key must be communicable and memorable without written notes and changeable at the correspondents' will.
4. It must work with telegraphic correspondence.
5. It must be portable and operable by one person.
6. It must be easy to use, without mental strain or a long body of rules.

The famous “Kerckhoffs principle” is therefore the second member of a larger operational doctrine. In the article, “secret” refers to the material system—tables, dictionaries, or mechanisms—not the proper key. He did not write the now-standard English sentence “everything about the system except the key is public knowledge.” That is a later, faithful compression. Nor did he require publication as a political or licensing rule; he required security **under the assumption of system exposure**. Fabien Petitcolas emphasizes this distinction in his [electronic edition and commentary](https://www.petitcolas.net/kerckhoffs/).

#### Technical contributions beyond the principle

Kahn identifies two important cryptanalytic contributions:

- **Superimposition:** aligning multiple messages encrypted under the same key so corresponding positions form monoalphabetic columns, then joining columns with similar frequencies. This generalized attacks on polyalphabetic systems beyond the narrower Kasiski method.
- **“Symmetry of position”:** exploiting preserved relative distances among cipher letters across mixed alphabets to reconstruct parts of a tableau.

Kerckhoffs also clarified the structural identity among a polyalphabetic tableau, cipher disk, and Saint-Cyr slide. His underlying methodological claim was that a cipher's value must be tested by actual cryptanalysis, not by an inventor's combinatorial assurances. Kahn reports that the French Ministry of War bought 300 copies and treats the book as a stimulus to the late-nineteenth-century French cryptologic revival. The purchase figure is reported by Kahn; no independently accessible procurement record was found in this research.

#### Limits and errors

Kerckhoffs was not a modern mathematical cryptographer. He believed mathematically absolute security impossible under his conditions and aimed at practical resistance. His historical discussion mistakenly projected field ciphers too far back before the telegraph. Some operational desiderata—human memorability, manual portability, one-person operation—must be translated rather than copied literally into computerized settings. Modern cryptographic security also depends on implementations, protocols, random-number generation, side-channel resistance, and key management, areas outside his model.

### 1884–1892: Volapük's rise, institutional conflict, and schism

Johann Martin **Schleyer**, a German Catholic priest, had introduced Volapük in 1879–1880. Kerckhoffs joined the movement around **1884**, after the first Friedrichshafen congress. In 1885 he founded or organized the **Association française pour la propagation du Volapük** and became its energetic secretary-general. Contemporary accounts credit his publications and lectures with bringing the language into France and then helping its spread in Belgium, Spain, Portugal, Scandinavia, and the commercial world.

He presented Volapük as an international commercial instrument. The language's success was rapid: HEC lectures, press coverage, clubs, teacher diplomas, and courses at the Grands Magasins du Printemps. A contemporary report claimed that Printemps director Jules Jaluzot learned it with 121 employees. Numbers in movement propaganda vary and should not be treated as audited membership statistics.

His publications included the 144-page *Cours complet de Volapük* (1886, eight editions), the short *Premiers éléments* and *Grammaire abrégée*, and the 319-page *Dictionnaire volapük-français et français-volapük* (1887). He also edited or directed *Le Volapük* and prepared yearbooks and Academy circulars.

At the second congress in Munich, **6–9 July 1887**, delegates established the International Volapük Academy. Schleyer remained *cifal* or grand master; Kerckhoffs became *dilekel*, usually translated director, and effectively presided over academic work. Descriptions of him as “president” are functionally understandable but can obscure the dual hierarchy.

#### Kerckhoffs's program

Kerckhoffs wanted a precise, learnable system for commerce and science. He removed or proposed removing grammatical cases and tenses, favored prepositions over inflection, demanded regular word formation, and compared the language to a code whose user possessed a key. Roberto Garvía's [*Esperanto and Its Rivals*](https://satesperanto.org/IMG/pdf/roberto_garvia_-_esperanto_and_its_rivals__the_struggle_for_an_international_language-university_of_pennsylvania_press_2015_.pdf) reconstructs the difference: Schleyer sought expressive nuance and claimed creator's control; Kerckhoffs sought utility and Academy governance.

Kerckhoffs published *Examen critique de quelques simplifications qu'il y a lieu d'introduire dans le Volapük* before the 1887 congress. Later summaries call his reform “Nuvo-Volapük,” though no single stable, fully published replacement language should be inferred. A surviving draft described by Johann Schmidt changed pronouns, infinitives, volitives, and plurals; Kerckhoffs did not publish that draft as a completed language.

#### Conflict with Schleyer

The core issues were simultaneously linguistic, constitutional, personal, and proprietary:

- Did Schleyer possess a veto as inventor and owner?
- Could the Academy simplify grammar and vocabulary?
- Was Volapük a literary universal language, a commercial tool, or a peace/science project?
- Would authority remain centralized in a quasi-ecclesiastical hierarchy or move to an international body?

Kerckhoffs convened the third congress in Paris, **19–21 August 1889**, despite Schleyer's opposition. Volapük itself served as the working language. Charles E. Sprague helped moderate. The congress did not simply enact all radical reforms, but it strengthened Academy authority. Schleyer rejected the result and established a rival loyalist Academy at Constance in 1890. He removed Kerckhoffs from his list of Volapükists in March 1891.

Under Kerckhoffs, the Academy issued *Glamat nomik* in 1890. Kerckhoffs ultimately left the Academy and Volapük in **1892**; Woldemar Rosenberger replaced him on 14 December. Kahn gives 1891, probably conflating Schleyer's expulsion and Kerckhoffs's HEC departure with his formal Academy exit. The Academy's reforming line eventually produced Idiom Neutral under Rosenberger, not a completed Kerckhoffs language.

Volapük's collapse cannot be assigned to one act. The schism, competing reform projects, creator-versus-community governance, organizational exhaustion, uneven learning difficulty, and competition from Esperanto all mattered. Contemporary and later hostile narratives often cast either Schleyer as autocrat or Kerckhoffs as destroyer; both are polemical simplifications.

### 1891–1895: HEC dismissal, provincial teaching, and “Ravachol's cauldron”

Kerckhoffs taught at HEC from 1 November 1881 to **14 July 1891**. HEC administrators had already questioned his Volapük lectures. In April 1889 he assured the director that he had followed instructions, had held five Sunday lessons for about twelve students, and would withdraw from Volapük activity if the administration wished.

His departure in 1891 followed an end-of-year examination. Commissioned to examine German, he objected to a grade or the manner in which a ministry jury member handled a student. The remark was interpreted as criticism of the ministry representative; the administrative council ended his collaboration. His own letter asked HEC to certify ten years of good teaching while accurately recording that cause. This was a dismissal/nonrenewal over institutional criticism, not a criminal or academic-fraud proceeding.

He then lived at **17 rue Vauquelin, Paris**, while seeking a provincial lycée post:

- 1891–1892: German teacher, lycée of Mont-de-Marsan.
- From 16 September 1892: German teacher, lycée of Lorient.

Lorient reports called him distinguished, conscientious, clear, and methodical but said that, at his age, he had trouble imposing authority in crowded classrooms. In 1895 Kahn says declining health and grief led him to take renewable leave. No diagnosis or medical file was found.

#### The 1894 prank

In March 1894 Kerckhoffs anonymously sent colleague **Mr. Dejean** a small novelty explosive called a “Ravachol's cauldron,” packaged with mock-anarchist clippings and a coarse constipation joke. Dejean's wife opened it and was frightened when the tiny lead pot exploded. Police were notified, then the complaint was withdrawn once Kerckhoffs was identified.

The Interior Minister asked Education to consider a reprimand. Kerckhoffs admitted sole responsibility, explained that colleagues had planned it as dinner entertainment, and apologized to Mrs. Dejean. The rector concluded that the prank was trivial, recommended no punishment, and criticized the police commissioner's overreaction. This was a documented lapse in judgment and official inquiry, but not a bombing attempt, anarchist affiliation, indictment, or conviction.

### 1895–1903: poorly documented final years

The decade after Lorient is fragmentary. Kahn says Kerckhoffs renewed leave annually. The 2020 study records his return to Paris by 1900. Commercial-language bulletins show him giving **free German courses in 1900–1901** through the Société commerciale pour l'étude des langues étrangères. Newspapers in 1899 and 1900 advertised public cryptography lectures, apparently weekly Monday sessions associated with the École des Chartes/Sorbonne; the École's fragmentary archive does not preserve a parallel institutional file.

His last known Paris address was **6 rue Rataud**, fifth arrondissement, within meters of what later became major French cryptography research offices. A 1903 Société d'anthropologie membership list called him a former university professor. He was probably retired by 1902–1903, but no pension decision or retirement decree was located.

### Technologies and projects

Kerckhoffs did not patent a cipher machine in the evidence found. His cryptographic work analyzed existing technologies—grilles, disks, slides, telegraphy, codebooks, Wheatstone-type devices—and proposed cryptanalytic procedures rather than a commercial apparatus. The offprint subtitle advertised “a new method of decipherment applicable to double-key systems.”

His constructed-language activity produced textbooks, dictionaries, periodicals, an international teaching network, diploma systems, congress organization, and governance proposals. It was an intellectual and publishing enterprise, but no incorporated company, equity structure, or personal publishing fortune has been identified.

### Property and finance

Beyond the 1873 wealth assessment, addresses, and a perpetual family grave purchased by his widow, no reliable evidence was found of landholdings, investments, patents, business partnerships, bankruptcy, tax litigation, or suspicious financial activity. A Bellevue Hotel stay is evidence of expenditure, not proof of ownership or patronage.

## Political, Religious, and Intellectual Development

### Religion

Kerckhoffs explicitly identified as Catholic in 1876. His marriage connected him to a Revolutionary military family rather than to a clerical dynasty. His scholarly work on monumental art and religion and his cooperation and conflict with the Catholic priest Schleyer do not reveal a systematic theology. No record of clerical office, religious discipline, or anticlerical activism was found.

### French patriotism and naturalization

He moved from Dutch birth and a Francophile Limburg household to French legal residence and citizenship. During the 1870 invasion he served the National Guard. In *La cryptographie militaire*, he criticized French weaknesses as a public service and argued that open teaching of cryptography would strengthen the army. His “patriot” self-description coexisted with cosmopolitan language projects.

### Women's rights

In a July 1886 interview with *La Citoyenne*, Kerckhoffs supported women's civil and political rights, praised feminist **Hubertine Auclert**, and predicted women's suffrage in France. This is a documented public position, not evidence of organizational membership in Auclert's movement.

### Dreyfus Affair

In 1898 he signed a published petition protesting the prosecution and persecution of Colonel **Georges Picquart**, whose work supported revision of Alfred Dreyfus's conviction. The signature places Kerckhoffs among public supporters of legal revision; it does not establish a personal relationship with Dreyfus, Picquart, or Émile Zola.

### Antisemitism

In the Volapük controversy, Kerckhoffs objected to a derogatory verb meaning “to act as a Jew” and wrote that the movement should have nothing to do with antisemitism. He and Leopold Einstein accused Schleyer's camp of antisemitism. This position is documented in movement polemics; the precise responsibility for every hostile word in the broader controversy varies by author.

### Cryptographic philosophy

Kerckhoffs's central intellectual move was operational: institutions cannot safely distribute a large secret system to many users in war, because capture, corruption, error, or turnover will expose it. A short, replaceable key is a more manageable secret. Public or adversarial scrutiny is therefore not merely philosophical openness; it is a realistic test condition.

Claude Shannon recast the idea mathematically in 1949: [“the enemy knows the system being used”](https://www.4el.ch/crypto/bibliotheque/PDF/shannon1949.pdf). NIST's historical account describes Kerckhoffs as formalizing the basis for encryption standards: algorithm security should not depend on algorithm secrecy. This later lineage is genuine, but Kerckhoffs did not formulate computational complexity, semantic security, public-key cryptography, or modern protocol definitions.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, and Unresolved Questions

### 1. Suspicion of German collaboration, 1870–1873

- **Claim:** His German contacts and proposed study trip indicated relations with the Prussian enemy.
- **Status:** Contemporaneous allegation investigated during naturalization.
- **Finding:** The prefect called the allegation unfounded and cited consistent National Guard service.
- **No evidence of:** arrest, charge, conviction, dismissal, or espionage finding.

### 2. Secret military or intelligence connection

The 2020 study identifies genuine puzzles:

- Kerckhoffs knew details about Henri Berthaut's cryptanalytic ability that were not readily public.
- He used an authentic encrypted Havas telegram about current Egyptian operations.
- He knew then-current army and foreign-ministry cipher practices.
- He served in the National Guard and sought military-school teaching.
- The prefect's rapid clearance and his profile as a naturalized traveler resemble what later French intelligence called an *honorable correspondant*.

These facts support an unresolved question about access to informed military contacts. They do **not** prove that he was an intelligence officer, agent, paid source, or covert cryptanalyst. The authors themselves label the connections as possibilities and list military archives for future research.

### 3. Vera Zasulich story

A reputable 1887 newspaper claimed Kerckhoffs had played a forensic cryptographic role in the prosecution of Russian revolutionary **Vera Zasulich**. The archival investigators found the account implausible: Zasulich acted alone, no such encrypted correspondence or expert appeared in trial documentation, Kerckhoffs himself credited Russians with breaking nihilist codes, and no corroborating source was located. This is best classified as a **false or unverified press story**, not established state service.

### 4. HEC departure

His contract ended in 1891 after he criticized a ministry examiner's grading. The underlying letter is documentary evidence. There was no claim of plagiarism, financial misconduct, false credentials, or abuse. Kahn's psychological gloss—that Volapük's collapse left him crushed and embittered—is an interpretation, not a medical finding.

### 5. Volapük schism

Both camps accused the other of damaging the movement. Kerckhoffs pressed reforms before a settled constitutional procedure existed; Schleyer asserted creator's veto and later purged or replaced the Academy. The result was institutional fracture. Statements that either man “destroyed Volapük” are causal interpretations, not singular facts.

### 6. Ravachol prank

Documented administrative investigation, admitted conduct, no punishment. It must be distinguished from real anarchist violence and from any allegation that Kerckhoffs was an anarchist.

### 7. Criminal, financial, and abuse record

No evidence was found of a lawsuit against Kerckhoffs, indictment, conviction, imprisonment, fraud, corruption, money laundering, abuse allegation, patent dispute, bankruptcy, addiction, or sustained psychiatric crisis. Absence from the consulted record is not proof that no minor civil matter ever existed, but no significant episode surfaced in multilingual archival and bibliographic searches.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### Noble descent and coat of arms

1. **Story:** The Kerckhoffs were an ancient noble family descending from a Franconian castle line, with hereditary titles and arms.
2. **Appearance/circulation:** Formalized in the 1839 genealogy commissioned in the orbit of Joseph Romain Louis Kerckhoffs; repeated in nineteenth-century biographical sketches and later full-name styles.
3. **Promoters:** A socially ambitious collateral relative and genealogist P. E. de Borcht; later biographers.
4. **Evidence:** The pedigree is credible only to a limited generation. Dutch archival corrections mark the connection to the noble *van den Kerckhove van de Varent* line and its titles as fiction. Auguste's birth at Nieuwenhof explains the territorial style without proving nobility.
5. **Effect:** It enlarged his name, encouraged aristocratic descriptions, and obscured the family's more prosaic legal, medical, and municipal standing.

### Bokkenrijder bloodline

1. **Story:** Kerckhoffs descended from demonic goat-riding robbers or criminal leaders.
2. **Appearance/circulation:** Rooted in eighteenth-century Limburg criminal prosecutions and folklore; revived in later genealogical and popular culture.
3. **Promoters:** Hostile folklore and, later, descendants' heritage associations and entertainment media.
4. **Evidence:** Joseph and Balthasar Kerckhoffs were associated with Bokkenrijder bands and lie in an extended collateral network. Direct descent by Auguste is not demonstrated, and demonic goat-riding is folklore.
5. **Effect:** It likely contributed to family genealogical reputation management; today it adds colorful regional mythology rather than stigma.

### Birth in Maastricht and the 31 January date

1. **Story:** He was born in Maastricht, sometimes on 31 January 1835.
2. **Appearance:** Contemporary shorthand profiles called him Maastricht-born; his own Latin résumé gives 31 January.
3. **Evidence:** The civil birth material fixes Nuth, 19 January. Maastricht was a later school location and recognizable regional city.
4. **Effect:** Both errors propagated into biographical summaries.

### Death in Paris, death on 11 August, and being “cut in two”

1. **Story:** Kerckhoffs died in Paris; alternatively, he died at Därligen on 11 August after being cut in two by a train.
2. **Appearance:** Paris entered encyclopedic biographies, probably because it was his residence and burial place. Schleyer's diary supplied the 11 August/cut-in-two version.
3. **Evidence:** Därligen's death certificate gives 9 August 1903. The *Oberländisches Volksblatt* of 11 August reported that he was fatally struck while greeting acquaintances arriving by train; his heavily mutilated body went to Interlaken and then Paris. It does not establish the diary's exact “cut in two” wording.
4. **Effect:** Paris remained common in reference works; the grisly version became a memorable biographical tag.

### “Father of computer security”

1. **Story:** Kerckhoffs founded computer security.
2. **Appearance:** Late-twentieth- and twenty-first-century institutional and popular histories, including material associated with HEC and cybersecurity education.
3. **Evidence:** He died before electronic computers. His second desideratum is foundational to modern cryptographic design and standards, but computer security encompasses far more than cryptography.
4. **Effect:** The title expanded his modern visibility while anachronistically narrowing his actual identity as language teacher, philologist, cryptanalyst, and Volapük organizer.

### “The algorithm must be public”

1. **Story:** Kerckhoffs required all cryptographic algorithms or source code to be openly published.
2. **Appearance:** Modern “open source versus security through obscurity” summaries.
3. **Evidence:** He required that a military system remain safe if its material design fell into enemy hands and advocated open teaching in military schools. This justifies designing under full adversarial knowledge, but it is not identical to a universal legal duty to publish every implementation.
4. **Effect:** The stronger slogan has promoted auditability and standards, but sometimes converts an adversary model into a political claim he did not literally state.

### “Kerckhoffs destroyed Volapük”

1. **Story:** His reforms and ambition alone killed the first successful constructed language.
2. **Appearance:** Schleyer-aligned accounts and simplified cautionary tales about language communities.
3. **Evidence:** Kerckhoffs's challenge materially contributed to the schism. Schleyer's veto claims, competing purposes, multiple reform projects, organizational weakness, and Esperanto also contributed.
4. **Effect:** It made Kerckhoffs either a pragmatic democrat or a destructive usurper depending on the narrator.

### Secret cryptanalyst or intelligence operative

1. **Story:** His knowledge proves covert work for the French state.
2. **Appearance:** A hypothesis developed from anomalies in the 2020 archival biography; the Zasulich newspaper tale supplies a separate legend.
3. **Evidence:** Unusual knowledge and contacts are real; employment, payment, tasking, or agency membership is not documented.
4. **Effect:** It offers an explanation for how a language teacher wrote such an informed military study, but remains an unresolved research question.

## Death, Burial, and Estate

Kerckhoffs arrived at the **Pension Du Lac in Därligen** on 24 July 1903 as a vacation or health-resort guest. On Sunday, **9 August 1903**, at about 7:30 p.m., he approached the station to greet acquaintances arriving by train and was struck and fatally injured. The body, described by the local paper as heavily mutilated, was taken to the Interlaken morgue and then transported to Paris. The record supports accidental death; no evidence of suicide, intoxication, foul play, or mechanical sabotage was found.

His widow acquired a perpetual concession on 15 August. Burial followed on 16 August at Montparnasse. The modest family stone records Auguste and Pauline Mowat, née Kerckhoffs. The stone's Pauline birth date is two days earlier than the civil register. Researchers rediscovered the grave through correspondence in the 1980s and photographed and flowered it in 2020. Because French law can permit recovery of abandoned concessions, the investigators notified authorities of its historical importance.

No probate inventory, final asset valuation, will, medical report, or definitive catalogue of his library was found. Under the French law of 14 July 1866, his widow would normally have received literary rights, subject to any marriage contract. Autographs surfacing in the late 1990s suggest possible dispersal of papers, but provenance is unresolved.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### 1903–mid-twentieth century

Obituaries appeared in French and Swiss papers, but his personal reputation receded quickly. Volapük declined, and his last decade was obscure. Cryptographers, however, continued to cite *La cryptographie militaire*. French writers including Josse, de Viaris, Valerio, Delastelle, and Bazeries worked in the intellectual environment the book helped shape.

Shannon's 1949 adversary assumption made the core idea native to mathematical secrecy theory, although Shannon did not simply quote or rename Kerckhoffs. Later writers called it Kerckhoffs's law, doctrine, axiom, assumption, desideratum, or principle; “Shannon's maxim” became a closely related formulation.

### Modern computer-security reception

The principle supports open standards and public cryptanalysis: AES and other standard algorithms are designed to remain secure when their design is known. [NIST's computer-security history](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=914537) explicitly credits Kerckhoffs with the basic idea that ciphertext security rests on the key rather than algorithm secrecy.

The principle does not imply that obscurity can never add tactical delay, that implementation details are harmless, or that keys are the only sensitive assets in a real system. It says obscurity must not be the **foundation** of cryptographic security. Modern systems may also protect code, hardware layouts, metadata, authentication secrets, seeds, and operational details while still satisfying the adversary-knowledge assumption at the design level.

### Institutional commemoration

The **Kerckhoffs Institute for Computer Security**, founded in 2005 as a collaboration of the University of Twente, Eindhoven University of Technology, and Radboud University Nijmegen, offered a two-year master's track; its [archived website](https://web.archive.org/web/20130514064110/http://www.kerckhoffs-institute.org/) documents the commemoration. His name remains standard vocabulary in cryptography curricula, standards discussions, hardware security, and software-security commentary.

### Recovery of the person behind the principle

Modern scholarship has reversed two distortions:

1. It shows that his lifetime celebrity came principally from Volapük, not cryptography.
2. It reconstructs him not as an abstract “father of cybersecurity” but as a Dutch-born, French-naturalized Catholic language teacher, public educator, reformist interlinguist, amateur anthropologist, technically serious cryptanalyst, and occasionally difficult institutional actor.

The 2020 biography also corrected his death place, recovered financial and political information, documented his wife and daughter, exposed the fabricated noble pedigree, and preserved the grave. Important gaps remain: his route into military cryptographic knowledge, final retirement, widow and estate, and missing papers.

## Research Gaps and Confidence Summary

### High confidence

Civil identity; parentage; birth place/date; Rolduc education; broad university sequence; marriage and daughter; French naturalization; 1873 financial snapshot; doctorate; HEC service; principal publications; Volapük offices; HEC departure mechanism; Lorient prank; political statements; Swiss death and Paris burial.

### Moderate confidence

Exact scope of British travel; Clarence Prentice journey; closeness to Victor Hugo; full list and print runs of Volapük adaptations; chronology of leave after 1895; scale of cryptography lectures; Ministry of War purchase of 300 copies.

### Unresolved/speculative

Formal military or intelligence connection; source of the Havas telegram; personal relationship with Henri Berthaut; Vera Zasulich forensic role; complete noble genealogy beyond verified generations; Pauline's cause of death; widow's later life; final estate; location and integrity of personal papers; authorship of the 1898 Valkenburg play.
