### Names, labels, and recurrent public characterizations

Harari has not adopted a literary alter ego. Publishers and speaking organizations have marketed him as a “global thinker,” “historian of humankind,” or prophet of the future. Critics have variously called him a popularizer, a “populist scientist,” a technological determinist, a transhumanist, or an ideological voice of the global elite. These are interpretive descriptions, not alternative identities. “Dataism,” a term he popularized for a possible data-centered worldview, is frequently treated online as his creed; in his books it is principally an object of description and warning, although his presentation sometimes blurs prediction, diagnosis, and rhetorical speculation.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and siblings

- **Father:** Shlomo Harari, born Shlomo Blajberg, an engineer employed in Israel's state armaments sector. His work was classified. Family accounts describe him as an enthusiastic reader of history who had been born on a kibbutz but became skeptical of socialist organization. He died in 2010. Public sources do not establish the precise employer, job title, place of death, or cause of death.
- **Mother:** Pnina Harari, née Luttinger. She worked as an office administrator and later helped answer correspondence generated by her son's publishing success. In Parker's family reconstruction she remembered Yuval learning to read at three or four; Harari places it at four or five.
- **Siblings:** Harari is the youngest of three children and has two older sisters. The consulted high-quality profiles do not consistently publish their names or occupations; this dossier does not infer them from unsourced genealogy pages.

The household is described as secular, Jewish, bookish, and materially comfortable rather than affluent. The family lived in Kiryat Ata in the Haifa district. Harari has recalled feeling intellectually different from peers while remaining embedded in a conventional Israeli middle-class family.

### Grandparents and ancestry

The best documented branch is maternal. Harari has publicly commemorated his maternal grandmother **Fanny Luttinger**, née Kosowski, born 1 December 1921 in Halberstadt, Germany, into a Polish Jewish family. In his centenary tribute he described her as a living witness to twentieth-century displacement. Family memory includes a story that Nazi thugs chased Fanny and a sister; available public material does not supply an archival police or immigration record for that episode. The family's flight from Germany and resettlement in Mandatory Palestine form part of Harari's own family narrative.

His maternal grandfather, generally identified as **Abraham (Bruno) Luttinger**, was born in Chernivtsi, then in Bukovina, and later served in the British Army as a Jewish Palestinian during the Second World War. Harari has also posted about the grave of **Avigdor Perets Kosowski**, his grandmother's grandfather. These social-media posts are primary testimony to family memory, not a substitute for a full civil-record genealogy.

Published summaries often say that Harari has Eastern European and Lebanese roots. The Eastern European/German/Bukovinian maternal line is reasonably traceable in public accounts. The exact Lebanese branch, migration dates, and relevant ancestors were not established in the consulted primary or close-family sources. Later UK filings identify Harari as German as well as Israeli; it is plausible that the status relates to European family ancestry, but that remains an inference.

### Spouse, household, and children

Harari met **Itzik Yahav**, who had also grown up in Kiryat Ata, through an online dating service around 2001–2002. Detailed profiles favor 2002, while a later television clip gives 2001. The couple eventually married in a civil ceremony in Toronto because Israel did not provide a domestic civil same-sex marriage ceremony. The exact marriage date is not firmly stated in the strongest consulted records. Israel recognizes qualifying civil marriages performed abroad.

Yahav had worked in nonprofit theatre production. He became Harari's manager, original literary agent, business partner, and later co-founder of Sapienship. Harari has described Yahav as his practical interface with the world; Yahav negotiated or coordinated publishing, travel, media, and organizational work while Harari concentrated on research, writing, and speaking. The relationship is therefore both marital and economically consequential.

No children are reported. A 2020 profile described the couple living with a dog. Harari has discussed the decision not to have children in interviews, but he has not made a medical infertility claim.

## Childhood and Early Life, 1976–1993

Harari was born in Kiryat Ata in an Israeli society shaped by the aftermath of the Yom Kippur War and by continuing Arab–Israeli conflict. He taught himself to read unusually early. At about eight he began attending a school for gifted children that required two bus journeys. His childhood interests were historical and strategic: a large map of Europe supported elaborate war games, and he reconstructed campaigns in his imagination. Parker's profile also records the family anecdote that he once dug an exceptionally deep hole—an image later used by friends and journalists as a metaphor for his tendency to pursue questions past ordinary boundaries.

The First Intifada began when Harari was eleven. He later described his adolescent politics as right-wing and nationalist. This was not a record of party membership or political organizing. It was a youthful identification with the Israeli national story and its security assumptions. The position weakened during university as he studied longer historical timescales and encountered evidence that nations and identities are historically made rather than timeless.

He also made an adolescent attempt to become religious or to compel himself to believe. He has said the effort failed. The experience contributed to his later interest in the difference between consciously professed doctrine, embodied practice, subjective experience, and collectively maintained institutions.

No juvenile arrest, imprisonment, expulsion, serious accident, or documented addiction was found. Nor is there evidence that he was raised in an Orthodox household, despite later online claims that treat his Jewish family background as proof of religious authority.

## Education and Formation

### Hebrew University of Jerusalem, 1993–1998

Harari entered the Hebrew University of Jerusalem at approximately seventeen. He studied history in the university's accelerated academic-reserve environment. His early interests were military and medieval history rather than the universal history with which he later became identified.

A formative undergraduate project became “The Military Role of the Frankish Turcopoles: A Reassessment.” Published in 1997 in *Mediterranean Historical Review*, it argued from crusading sources about the tactical functions of locally recruited mounted troops. Harari later remembered the paper as the moment he discovered that he could pose an original historical question and answer it with sources.

**Benjamin Z. Kedar**, the distinguished historian of the Crusades, became an important teacher and mentor. Kedar encouraged rigorous source work and later helped Harari secure a teaching place at Hebrew University. When the young scholar considered writing a modern history of gay life in Israel, Kedar reportedly advised postponing so politically and personally exposed a project until after tenure. Harari followed the advice and never completed that proposed history.

Harari's academic-reserve path ordinarily pointed toward military service after study. He did not complete service in the Israel Defense Forces because of a health condition that he and his family have not publicly specified. Parker reports that the condition was not catastrophic; responsible reconstruction goes no further. Assertions that ideological opposition, deception, or privileged intervention caused the exemption are unsupported by the consulted evidence.

### Oxford, 1998–2002

Harari went to **Jesus College, University of Oxford**, for doctoral work under **Steven Gunn**, a historian of early modern England. His dissertation was titled *History and I: War and the Relations Between History and Personal Identity in Renaissance Military Memoirs, c. 1450–1600*. Oxford awarded the DPhil in 2002. The thesis examined how military memoirists connected war experience to identity and to the writing of history; it became the basis for *Renaissance Military Memoirs*.

Oxford was intellectually productive and personally difficult. Harari has described culture and climate shock, loneliness, and a broader impasse over identity and happiness. He went to London nightclubs, used MDMA/Ecstasy on several occasions, dated men, and came out as gay. No evidence indicates dependency, criminal prosecution, or a continuing drug problem. He later called this period a false or incomplete liberation: sexual and social openness did not by itself end his distress.

A friend, **Ron Merom**, a software engineer, introduced him to Vipassana meditation as taught in the tradition of **S. N. Goenka**. Harari attended a ten-day retreat in western England in 2000. The regimen—silence, observation of breath and sensation, and refusal to evade discomfort—became central to his work habits and philosophical formation. He later practiced about two hours daily, undertook annual retreats of thirty to sixty days, often in India, and served as an assistant teacher. He dedicated *Homo Deus* to Goenka. Harari treats meditation as an empirical method for observing mind and suffering, not as supernatural revelation.

### Formative intellectual influences

Harari's mature synthesis joins several traditions:

- the comparative world history associated with writers such as Jared Diamond;
- Kedar's and Gunn's source-based medieval and military history;
- evolutionary biology and cognitive science;
- Buddhist accounts of impermanence, craving, and the absence of a fixed self;
- social-scientific theories of constructed institutions;
- modern liberal, nationalist, communist, and religious ideologies treated comparatively as meaning-producing stories;
- information theory and Silicon Valley discourse about data, algorithms, biotechnology, and artificial intelligence.

He has often said that the central historical question is how large numbers of strangers cooperate. His answer is that humans can collectively believe in entities—money, corporations, states, gods, legal rights—that do not exist like rocks or trees but do exist intersubjectively through institutions, symbols, and behavior. “Fiction” in this scheme does not ordinarily mean a simple lie. Critics argue that his compressed vocabulary slides too readily between falsehood, convention, institution, belief, and narrative.

## Early Career, 1997–2010

### Specialist scholarship

Between 1997 and 2010 Harari built a conventional academic record in medieval and military history. His research moved from campaign logistics and cross-front cooperation to the testimony of combatants and the cultural meaning of battlefield experience.

His first three major articles treated crusader cavalry, Edward III's 1346 campaign, and the strategic logistics of late-medieval invasions. The doctoral work then produced *Renaissance Military Memoirs: War, History and Identity, 1450–1600* (2004). It distinguished retrospective military autobiography from chronicles and asked how combat shaped narrators' sense of self.

*Special Operations in the Age of Chivalry, 1100–1550* (2007) examined small, concealed, or irregular missions—assassination, abduction, sabotage, infiltration, and surprise—across medieval warfare. Reviewers valued its cases but disputed whether the modern category “special operations” was analytically stable when projected backward.

*The Ultimate Experience: Battlefield Revelations and the Making of Modern War Culture, 1450–2000* (2008) traced a change in Western war culture: combat came to be imagined not merely as an ordeal or source of glory but as privileged access to existential truth. Related articles developed the contrast between the eyewitness, who claims factual authority by seeing, and the “flesh-witness,” who claims experiential authority by suffering or acting bodily.

These works are important to understanding the popular books. Harari's later interest in myths is not simply a rejection of documentary history; it grew from specialist research on how narrators turn experience into identity and authority.

### Appointment and teaching

Harari returned to Jerusalem around 2001–2002 and joined the Hebrew University history faculty. In 2003 he was assigned a broad world-history survey. The course forced him to connect biological evolution, agrarian societies, empires, capitalism, industrialization, and science in a single narrative. He later offered courses including “The Laws of History,” “Technology and the Future of Humanity,” and “A Guide for Data Revolutionists.”

By the time of his international fame, Hebrew University had arranged an unusually light teaching burden: a 2020 profile described one semester a year and roughly one day a week on campus, with no doctoral students then under his supervision. This was an institutional accommodation to research, writing, and public obligations, not a reported dismissal from ordinary duties.

### Awards before global fame

Harari received the Hebrew University's Polonsky Prize for Creativity and Originality in 2009 and again in 2012. The Society for Military History awarded him a Moncado Prize in 2011 for outstanding article work. He was elected to the Young Israeli Academy and participated from 2012 to 2015. These honors preceded or overlapped with the Hebrew success of *Sapiens* and contradict the claim, sometimes made polemically, that he had no scholarly standing before becoming a celebrity author. They do not settle later criticism of his macrohistorical claims.

## Chronological Life History

### 2010–2013: From lecture course to Hebrew bestseller

Around 2008 Harari began turning approximately twenty scripted world-history lectures into a book. His father died in 2010 while the work was in progress. In June 2011 the Israeli publisher Dvir issued **קיצור תולדות האנושות** (*Qitsur Toldot ha-Enoshut*, literally “A Brief History of Humankind”). The book became an Israeli bestseller and widened his audience beyond academic history.

Harari and Yahav next produced a self-translated, print-on-demand English version, with illustrations supplied or arranged by Yahav. It sold fewer than two thousand copies. This unsuccessful edition is a significant stage in the book's history: the global phenomenon was not automatic, and the familiar English text emerged only after professional representation and editorial redevelopment.

In 2013 the Israeli literary agent **Deborah Harris** took on the book. An international rights process followed, including a London auction reported to have attracted twenty-two publishers. Harvill Secker acquired UK rights; Harper acquired US rights. Translators, editors, and publishers helped reshape the work for an Anglophone trade audience. Edition-level credits should be consulted before assigning any one person the entire translation: Harari was heavily involved in the English adaptation, while professional editorial assistance materially changed the commercial edition.

### 2014–2016: *Sapiens* becomes an international phenomenon

Harvill Secker published *Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind* in English in 2014. The book divided human history into the Cognitive, Agricultural, and Scientific Revolutions. Its best-known propositions were that flexible shared stories enabled large-scale cooperation; that agriculture could be read as a trap for individual humans even while expanding the species; that empire, money, and religion created large orders; and that capitalism and science formed a mutually reinforcing modern system.

The initial United States launch was more subdued than the British and European response. Recommendations from Barack Obama, Bill Gates, Mark Zuckerberg, and other highly visible readers accelerated sales. The endorsements are documented publicity events, not evidence that those figures shared all of Harari's politics or commissioned his work.

The book's success changed Harari's career. He moved from specialist conferences and university teaching into global publishing and elite policy forums. As sales expanded, Yahav increasingly managed the enterprise. By 2024 Penguin Random House reported more than twenty-five million copies of *Sapiens*, more than forty-five million copies of Harari's books overall, publication in sixty-five languages, and more than 220 weeks for *Sapiens* on the *New York Times* bestseller list. Current institutional biographies place aggregate sales above fifty million; exact totals are publisher claims and change with reporting dates.

The Hebrew **ההיסטוריה של המחר** (*Ha-Historia shel ha-Mahar*, “The History of Tomorrow”) appeared in 2015. Its English adaptation, *Homo Deus: A Brief History of Tomorrow*, followed in 2016. The book projected that powerful institutions would turn from overcoming famine, plague, and war toward engineering longevity, happiness, and godlike capacities. It introduced “Dataism” as a potential worldview in which data flow becomes the supreme value. Harari repeatedly presented these as scenarios, but the declarative prose invited readers to treat them as forecasts.

### 2015–2018: International platforms and political access

Harari began appearing at TED, the World Economic Forum, policy conferences, and meetings with political and technology leaders. In a 2015 Edge conversation with Daniel Kahneman, he speculated about a future “useless class”: people whose labor and military value automation had displaced. The phrase later became central to hostile internet mythology. In context it described a feared political and psychological problem, not a program to kill or discard people.

He met or spoke publicly with figures including Mark Zuckerberg and, at different events, Emmanuel Macron, Angela Merkel, Frank-Walter Steinmeier, Mauricio Macri, and Israeli leaders. He attended a 2015 dinner with Benjamin Netanyahu but later described no substantive exchange. These meetings indicate access, influence, and market demand for his analysis. They do not by themselves establish a formal advisory office.

At TED2018 Harari appeared live from Tel Aviv as a holographic/digital projection. His talk distinguished nationalism from fascism and warned that concentrated biometric and behavioral data could make dictatorship unusually efficient. The technological staging became part of his public image: a historian of very old systems using futuristic media to warn about new systems.

In 2018 he published *21 Lessons for the 21st Century*, organized around contemporary problems rather than a past-to-future arc. It addressed work, liberty, equality, community, nationalism, religion, immigration, terrorism, war, ignorance, education, meaning, and meditation. He dedicated it to his husband.

That year Harari declined to participate in an event sponsored by Israel's Los Angeles consulate. He cited opposition to the Nation-State Law and to discriminatory aspects of Israeli surrogacy law. This was a public boycott of a specific state-sponsored engagement, not a general refusal to speak with Israelis or an endorsement of an academic boycott of Israel.

### 2019–2021: Sapienship, censorship dispute, pandemic, and media consolidation

In 2019 Harari and Yahav founded **Sapienship**. It combined management of intellectual property and media with social-impact projects, research, educational content, and public advocacy. The original operation was described as a for-profit company with think-tank ambitions. It employed about twelve people by 2020. The enterprise reduced Harari's personal administrative burden and professionalized a brand already extending across books, speaking, video, curricula, and adaptations.

In September 2019 Harari attended the Yalta European Strategy meeting in Kyiv, a forum backed by Ukrainian businessman Victor Pinchuk. He met or shared the program with Olena Zelenska, then-president Petro Poroshenko, Steven Pinker, David Rubenstein, and other political and intellectual figures. The event illustrates his integration into transnational conference networks; it is not evidence of a Ukrainian governmental position or financial patronage beyond ordinary event participation.

#### Russian-edition dispute

The 2019 Russian edition of *21 Lessons* altered politically and socially sensitive passages. Harari acknowledged approving replacement of a discussion of Vladimir Putin's public falsehoods about the seizure of Crimea with an example about Donald Trump. He argued that the compromise allowed Russian readers access to the rest of the book. Reporting also found changes that he said he had not authorized, including language treating Crimea's “conquest” as “re-attachment” and changing a dedication from “husband” to “partner.” Critics called his consent cowardice or self-censorship; Harari replied that he was openly gay and critical of Putin and had not authorized all alterations. The documented fact is selective authorized accommodation plus additional unauthorized changes—not wholesale secret authorship by the Russian state.

Iranian authorities reportedly banned sales of Harari's books in 2019, with reporting connecting the ban to his discussion of human evolution. The consulted report does not reproduce a full Iranian administrative order, so the precise legal scope and duration remain uncertain.

#### Pandemic period

During the COVID-19 pandemic Harari wrote widely circulated essays arguing that emergency surveillance could become permanent and that global cooperation was essential. In April 2020 he and Yahav, through their organization, donated **US$1 million** to the World Health Organization after the Trump administration suspended US funding. The donation is documented; no condition or policy purchase was reported.

Pandemic interviews sometimes became raw material for conspiratorial edits. His claim that technology could enable “under-the-skin” surveillance was a warning about state and corporate capability. It was later recirculated as if he had admitted to implanting or designing such a system. No patent, biomedical company, device development program, or governmental surveillance office linked to him was found.

In 2020 the Free University of Brussels (VUB) awarded him an honorary doctorate. Sapienship and its publishing partners also began *Sapiens: A Graphic History*, co-written with David Vandermeulen and illustrated by Daniel Casanave. Volumes appeared in 2020 and 2021. The adaptation uses fictional guides, courtroom scenes, and popular visual genres; it is an authorized collaborative restaging, not a verbatim comic edition of the prose book.

A 2020 *New Yorker* profile gave the fullest public account of Harari's childhood, parents, Oxford years, marriage, household, and business. It also described a comfortable home in the moshav Mesilat Zion, about thirty minutes from Jerusalem, with a swimming pool, and a Porsche SUV. These observable lifestyle details establish prosperity but not net worth.

In 2021 the Foreign Press Association in Israel honored Harari for contributions to journalism/public discourse. CBS's *60 Minutes* profiled him, further enlarging the US audience. The total publishing operation now included adult nonfiction, graphic adaptation, children's books, video, and a growing staff.

### 2022: Ukraine, children’s history, and philanthropy

After Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Harari argued that Vladimir Putin had already lost the most important historical contest if Ukrainians emerged with a stronger national identity. He emphasized that nations are stories without implying that they are unreal or powerless: a collectively maintained identity can organize sacrifice and resistance.

He published *Unstoppable Us, Volume 1: How Humans Took Over the World*, illustrated by Ricard Zaplana Ruiz, translating the basic *Sapiens* account for younger readers. Sapienship also released **Once Upon AI**, an experimental short film composed from roughly 260 images made with thirteen generative-AI systems and shown in association with Tomorrowland. The project is evidence of artistic experimentation with AI, not a patented technical invention.

Israeli financial press reported that Harari and Yahav donated **NIS 250,000** to IGY, an Israeli LGBTQ youth organization. As with the WHO donation, no evidence indicates that the gift bought an institutional office or financial return.

### 2023: Israeli constitutional crisis, AI warnings, and 7 October

Harari became a visible opponent of the Netanyahu government's judicial overhaul. In speeches and articles he described the package as a coup against checks on executive power. He addressed mass demonstrations and defended reservists who said they would not serve a dictatorship. This was partisan political intervention by a private intellectual, not candidacy for office or membership in a reported party leadership.

In March 2023 he signed the Future of Life Institute's open letter calling for at least a six-month pause in training AI systems more powerful than GPT-4. The letter asked for safety protocols and public governance; it did not call for a permanent prohibition on artificial intelligence. Harari separately argued that bots should be legally forbidden to impersonate humans in public conversation.

After Hamas's 7 October attack, Harari criticized parts of the international left for minimizing or rationalizing atrocities against Israeli civilians. At the same time, he rejected destruction of Gaza as a political answer and insisted that any stable future required credible freedom and hope for Palestinians. His public position combined support for Israel's existence and security, opposition to Hamas, opposition to permanent occupation, and increasing hostility to Netanyahu's leadership.

### 2024: *Nexus* and war-era public argument

In 2024 Sapienship was reported to have about fifteen employees. Harari published *Nexus: A Brief History of Information Networks from the Stone Age to AI* on 10 September. It revised a common assumption in his earlier reception: more information does not automatically produce truth or wisdom. Networks prevail by balancing truth production against mythology, bureaucracy, loyalty, speed, and error correction. The book moved from ancient records and sacred texts through totalitarian files and mass media to algorithms capable of making decisions.

The third graphic volume, *The Masters of History*, and *Unstoppable Us, Volume 2: Why the World Isn't Fair* also appeared. Harari continued interviews about Israel–Palestine. In *The Atlantic* he said he wished to reclaim Zionism from ethnonationalist exclusivity while acknowledging despair over both political leaderships. He treated Israeli and Palestinian national stories as powerful constructed realities whose historical construction does not cancel people's attachment or rights.

### 2025–August 2026: Corporate formalization, AI governance, and current activity

On **23 May 2025**, UK **SAPIENSHIP LTD** (company number 16471499) recorded Harari and Yahav as directors. Companies House lists each as holding more than 25 percent but no more than 50 percent of shares and voting rights. Harari is recorded as Israeli/German and UK-resident; Yahav as Israeli/Portuguese and UK-resident. The record supports a present UK corporate and residence connection. It does not establish that the couple abandoned their Israeli home, the exact beneficial split within all related entities, or Harari's tax domicile.

Sapienship worked with Harvard's LabXchange on educational material about storytelling, cooperation, and misinformation. Harari continued to advocate Israeli–Palestinian peacebuilding and to write on information networks, AI agents, and “non-human companies.” He argued that legal systems should not grant autonomous AI systems ordinary corporate personhood merely because they can execute many managerial functions.

In early 2026 Argentine president **Javier Milei's** government publicly disputed Harari's warning that Argentina was considering legal pathways for AI-controlled companies. The Argentine government said the proposed framework concerned human accountability for AI-assisted entities, not rights for machines. The exchange concerned the interpretation and possible effects of a developing legal proposal; it was neither a lawsuit nor a formal finding that either side had fabricated documents.

In January 2026 **LawZero**, an AI-safety nonprofit founded by Yoshua Bengio, appointed Harari to its board of directors alongside technology, science, and business figures. The role is governance and public-interest oversight, not employment as an AI engineer.

*Unstoppable Us, Volume 3: How Enemies Become Friends* was published on 3 February 2026. A fourth graphic-history volume, *The Age of Revolutions*, was advertised for 2026; its precise release status should be checked against the relevant territory's publisher because official series pages can list forthcoming books before publication.

Harari remained active in 2026 interviews and conferences, including renewed appearances connected to the World Economic Forum. He argued that AI could act as a manipulative nonhuman agent, criticized Netanyahu's handling of the Gaza war, and described Hamas's 7 October operation as a startling initial military success that was strategically squandered through atrocities and subsequent consequences. That formulation generated political criticism; it was analytical language about military surprise, not praise for killing civilians.

## Political, Religious, Philosophical, and Intellectual Development

### Nation, empire, and “intersubjective fictions”

Harari's movement from adolescent nationalism to macrohistory did not lead him to say nations are simply fake. He separates three domains:

1. **Objective phenomena**, which persist independent of human awareness, such as radioactivity.
2. **Subjective phenomena**, which exist in an individual's experience, such as pain.
3. **Intersubjective phenomena**, which exist in communication and coordinated behavior among many people, such as money, corporations, citizenship, law, and religions.

National myths are therefore causally real even when their narratives are invented, selective, or anachronistic. A currency can lose value when shared confidence collapses; a corporation can be dissolved by legal action; a nation can mobilize armies because millions recognize symbols and obligations. This is the direct conceptual link between Harari's corpus and national mythology.

The same framework produces an ambiguity. “Fiction” can mean a formally enacted legal person, a sacred cosmology, a moral norm, a false empirical assertion, or a simplifying narrative. Anthropologists and historians have criticized the compression because each has a different relation to evidence and coercive power. Harari answers that his intended contrast is ontological and organizational, not a claim that all institutions are harmless fantasies.

He treats empires as both violent conquerors and mechanisms that integrate law, language, trade, and culture. He treats nationalism as a modern system capable of welfare and solidarity within a state, but inadequate for nuclear war, ecological breakdown, pandemics, and AI. He usually favors international cooperation rather than abolition of national government.

### Liberalism, democracy, and Israel

Harari broadly identifies with liberal democracy: individual liberties, electoral choice, independent courts, a free press, scientific autonomy, minority protections, and the capacity to correct error. His account is not libertarian in the narrow economic sense. He accepts collective institutions and global regulation where concentrated technology creates cross-border risks.

In Israel his positions developed from criticism of occupation and religious-nationalist power to direct participation in the 2023 protest movement. He opposes settlements and indefinite control of Palestinians, supports a political horizon for Palestinian self-determination, and also supports Israel's continued existence as a national home. After 7 October 2023 he criticized both Hamas's violence and voices abroad that erased Israeli victims. During the Gaza war he accused Netanyahu's government of sacrificing long-term security and international legitimacy. He has not endorsed Hamas, ethnic cleansing, or the erasure of either national community.

The constructed character of Zionism and Palestinian nationalism, in his view, does not make either attachment disposable. In later interviews he described both peoples as indigenous in the practical historical sense that they have deep ties and nowhere else to go. He argues that exclusive claims to indigeneity become obstacles when they are used to deny the other's presence.

### Religion, atheism, and meditation

Harari's books interpret religions as orders built around superhuman norms. He provocatively includes ideologies such as liberalism, communism, and capitalism in comparable meaning-making frameworks. He rejects the existence of a personal creator deity and is an atheist in ordinary public description.

Vipassana is nevertheless central to his daily life. The apparent tension is resolved by his distinction between claims about cosmic authority and practices that can be tested in subjective experience. Meditation taught him, he says, to observe sensations and cravings without constructing a permanent self around them. This contributes to his skepticism about free will and his repeated warning that an algorithm able to predict desires could manipulate a person who mistakes those desires for an autonomous essence.

The practice is disciplined: approximately an hour in the morning and an hour in the evening, with annual long silent retreats and periods assisting instruction. Public accounts differ over whether every retreat was thirty, forty-five, or sixty days; these lengths varied over time. Yahav once practiced with him but did not continue at the same intensity.

### Animals, veganism, and suffering

Harari regards industrial animal agriculture as one of history's largest systems of suffering. He has been described as vegan and, in some interviews, as “vegan-ish”: he avoids supporting the meat and dairy industries but has acknowledged occasional exceptions in family or social settings. The careful formulation is that he follows an animal-welfare-motivated, predominantly vegan practice rather than that every meal has been independently verified.

His concern for animals connects evolutionary continuity to Buddhist attention to suffering. It also informs his criticism of *Sapiens*' agricultural revolution: domesticated species may multiply genetically while individual animals live constrained, painful lives.

### Free will, consciousness, biotechnology, and AI

Harari argues that no uncaused, indivisible self has been found behind decisions. Human choices arise from biological, psychological, and cultural processes. He does not therefore say deliberation is pointless; he argues that self-knowledge becomes more important when outside systems can model and manipulate preference.

His technological warning combines:

- biometric sensing that can infer internal states;
- machine learning that can predict behavior;
- control of data by governments or corporations;
- AI systems that generate language and thereby participate in culture;
- biotechnology that can alter bodies and brains;
- unequal access that could convert economic inequality into biological inequality.

“Hackable animals” is his compact phrase for this vulnerability. It is an extrapolative warning, not a report that every person has already been completely decoded. Similarly, “data colonialism” describes a possible world in which data extracted from one society gives foreign platforms decisive power.

His three recurrent global threats are nuclear war, ecological collapse, and technological disruption. He argues that no state can solve them alone. Critics see a preference for technocratic global governance; supporters see a defense of democratic cooperation against private and authoritarian concentration. Harari himself warns that global cooperation without accountability could also produce digital dictatorship.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, Crimes, Fraud, Corruption, or Financial Questions

### Evidentiary finding on legal and criminal matters

No consulted court record, government filing, or reputable investigation identified Harari as arrested, indicted, criminally charged, convicted, sued for fraud, found liable for corruption, investigated for money laundering, or employed by an intelligence service. No substantiated plagiarism finding or university disciplinary case was found. This is a bounded research finding, not proof that no dispute or private claim has ever existed.

### Scholarly criticism: synthesis, sourcing, and error

**Documented fact:** *Sapiens* is a very large synthesis written for general readers. Harari has acknowledged that its opening material depended on synthesis rather than his own original prehistoric field research. The original trade editions did not provide the dense source apparatus expected of an academic monograph; Harari later published a chapter-by-chapter reference list online.

**Christopher Hallpike's critique:** The anthropologist Christopher Hallpike published a long critical review arguing that *Sapiens* mishandled anthropological evidence, projected modern concepts backward, oversimplified foraging societies, and converted debatable speculation into confident universal history. His criticism is methodological and factual, not an institutional finding of fraud.

**Darshana Narayanan's 2022 critique:** Writing in *Current Affairs*, cognitive scientist Darshana Narayanan consulted specialists and catalogued contested claims about a sudden cognitive revolution, gossip, animal communication, pre-state violence, and human biological change. She characterized Harari's method as “populist science.” The article is a critical synthesis rather than peer review or a formal misconduct investigation. Its underlying specialist objections deserve separate evaluation; they do not establish that every thesis in the book is false.

**Other reviews:** Archaeologists, anthropologists, and historians have objected to a linear march from foraging to agriculture to states, to selective treatment of violence, and to claims that agriculture was history's greatest fraud. More favorable reviewers credit Harari with integrating dispersed fields, emphasizing animal suffering, and making uncertainty discussable at mass scale. Academic reviewers of *Special Operations* likewise valued the cases while questioning the transhistorical category.

**Finding:** The controversy is real and substantive: Harari uses compression, analogy, and high-confidence prose beyond his specialist training. The evidence supports criticism of particular claims and method. It does not support a blanket finding of fabrication, credential fraud, or plagiarism.

### Russian censorship, 2019

**Documented:** Harari authorized at least one politically motivated substitution in the Russian *21 Lessons*: a Putin/Crimea example became a Trump example. Other changes, including “husband” to “partner” and “conquest” to “re-attachment,” were reported as unauthorized by him.

**His explanation:** Limited compromise would reach Russian readers with ideas otherwise unavailable; he still criticized Putin and objected to the unapproved changes.

**Criticism:** Writers and readers accused him of sacrificing principle and helping Russian propaganda. The criticism is supported insofar as he knowingly approved one alteration. It overstates the evidence when it attributes every Russian change to him.

**Status:** Publishing and reputational dispute; no reported legal charge or fraud finding.

### Political speech and the Gaza war

Harari's description of 7 October as an initial Hamas military success produced accusations that he glorified terrorism. The surrounding comments describe a successful surprise operation followed by extraordinary cruelty and long-term strategic failure. “Success” was analytical, not moral approval. Conversely, opponents of the Gaza war have accused him of giving insufficient weight to Palestinian deaths, while Israeli nationalists accuse him of undermining the state. These are political judgments. His documented positions include condemnation of Hamas atrocities, criticism of destruction in Gaza, support for Palestinian political prospects, and opposition to Netanyahu.

### AI, elitism, and conflicts of interest

Harari has extraordinary access to technology executives and policy forums while warning that those same networks may concentrate power. This creates a legitimate question about platform dependence and elite circulation. Public records document speaking, interviews, and nonprofit governance; they do not reveal all honoraria or travel sponsorship. No evidence found establishes that he owns a material interest in Meta, OpenAI, a surveillance platform, or a biotech firm.

The $1 million WHO donation and Sapienship's public-interest work are documented financial relationships. Claims that WHO, WEF, or a government secretly pays him to promote depopulation or vaccination were not supported by a contract, filing, communication, or transaction in the consulted sources.

### The Milei dispute, 2026

Harari warned in the *Financial Times* that legal recognition of companies run by nonhuman AI could allow autonomous agents to accumulate resources and evade human responsibility. Argentina's government responded that he had misrepresented a proposal intended to ensure that a human remained accountable. Reporting confirms a genuine policy proposal and genuine public disagreement. The precise legal effect depends on final statutory language and implementation; neither a judicial ruling nor a concluded enforcement action existed in the consulted record.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### 1. “Harari is Klaus Schwab's top adviser” or a WEF official

**Story:** Viral posts identify Harari as Klaus Schwab's “top adviser,” right-hand man, chief ideologue, or a formal World Economic Forum decision-maker.

**Appearance and circulation:** The language spread widely in anti-WEF and Great Reset media during the COVID-19 period, often attached to clips from his Davos speeches.

**Evidence:** WEF's own pages identify Harari as a speaker, contributor, author, and academic. They do not list an employment, board, or adviser position. He has repeatedly appeared at WEF events and thus has a real platform relationship.

**Assessment:** **Unsupported title built around a documented association.** Calling him a WEF participant is factual; calling him Schwab's official top adviser is not supported by the institution's records.

**Effect:** The label gives decentralized public speaking the appearance of a hidden hierarchy and makes Harari a personification of “Davos” in hostile political mythology.

### 2. “Useless people” as an extermination or depopulation plan

**Story:** Harari supposedly says elites no longer need most humans and therefore intend to eliminate, drug, or imprison them.

**Appearance and circulation:** The narrative draws mainly on a 2015 Edge conversation and later talks in which he asked what politics would do with a “useless class” after automation. Short clips proliferated during pandemic and AI debates.

**Evidence:** The underlying phrase is genuine. He speculated, bleakly, that drugs and immersive games might pacify people whose economic role disappeared. In full context he framed automation-driven redundancy as a danger and policy problem. No source consulted contains a Harari proposal to kill or depopulate that group.

**Assessment:** **Genuine provocative forecast transformed into an unsupported programmatic claim.** The rhetoric reasonably invites criticism for reducing human status to market usefulness; the extermination inference lacks evidence.

**Effect:** “Useless class” became the single most powerful hostile symbol attached to Harari and is routinely paired with Nazi terminology he did not use.

### 3. “Humans are hackable animals” means Harari is running the hack

**Story:** Harari has confessed that a global cabal can already read every mind and is helping governments or companies implant control technology.

**Appearance and circulation:** Edits of his 2020 Davos speech and pandemic interviews, especially passages about biometric surveillance “under the skin.”

**Evidence:** He did say that humans are becoming “hackable animals,” meaning sufficiently detailed biological and behavioral data could allow institutions to predict or manipulate choices. His speeches warn against centralized control. No operational surveillance post, relevant patent, laboratory, or implant project under his direction was found.

**Assessment:** **Authentic wording, inverted agency.** It is fair to debate whether his forecasts exaggerate technological capability; it is unsupported to treat the warning as an admission of implementation.

**Effect:** The phrase made Harari a visual and verbal icon in anti-vaccine, anti-biometric, and end-times content.

### 4. “Harari called for conspiracy theorists to be eliminated”

**Story:** A 2023 headline alleged that Harari demanded the elimination of conspiracy theorists.

**Appearance and circulation:** The claim moved through The People's Voice and derivative sites, including AzerNews, after Harari's interview on Lex Fridman's podcast.

**Evidence:** The full published transcript discusses why conspiracy theories attract people, how global-cabal explanations work, and why they can be dangerous. It does not contain the alleged call for physical or political elimination.

**Assessment:** **Fabricated quotation/headline.** Criticism of conspiracy narratives was converted into a demand against the people who believe them.

**Effect:** The story reinforced the image of Harari as an authoritarian who wishes to silence opponents and circulated independently of corrections.

### 5. “Jesus is fake news” and the abolition of God

**Story:** A graphic attributed to WEF or Schwab supposedly quoted Harari saying Jesus was fake news and that AI would replace God or write an officially mandated new Bible.

**Appearance and circulation:** Christian social media and anti-WEF networks circulated a fabricated WEF-branded tweet, often beside excerpts from Harari's talks on AI-generated religion.

**Evidence:** Harari is an atheist and argues that religious traditions are historically produced stories. He has said familiar portraits are not documentary images of the historical Jesus and has speculated that AI could generate religious texts or new cults. The specific WEF post and quotation were not authentic, and no proposal for a compulsory AI Bible was found.

**Assessment:** **Fabricated artifact assembled from real but different views.**

**Effect:** The image made him a theological antagonist in apocalyptic narratives and broadened opposition beyond policy criticism.

### 6. Harari as the “prophet” or architect of transhumanism

**Story:** Admiring publicity casts him as a prophet who foresaw AI and pandemic politics; hostile variants say he is the chief designer of a transhumanist program to merge people with machines.

**Appearance and circulation:** Publisher copy, technology conferences, profile headlines, and later counter-media. Harari sometimes invites the prophetic frame with large predictions and phrases such as *Homo Deus*.

**Evidence:** His work assembles existing developments in AI, genetics, surveillance, and economics. He does not hold an engineering post and has no identified patents. Forecasts in *Homo Deus* are conditional scenarios. He now serves on an AI-safety board and advocates restraint.

**Assessment:** **Self-reinforcing public mythology.** Harari is an influential narrator of technological futures, not their sole originator or engineer.

**Effect:** The prophet label enhances book sales and speaking authority while allowing critics to blame him for developments he describes.

### 7. “Harari says nations and money are unreal, so facts do not exist”

**Story:** Because he calls money, nations, rights, religions, and corporations fictions, Harari supposedly believes evidence is meaningless or that these institutions have no effects.

**Appearance and circulation:** Simplified summaries of *Sapiens*, sometimes used by admirers as much as critics.

**Evidence:** His explicit three-part scheme distinguishes objective, subjective, and intersubjective reality. A corporation is not a physical organism, but law and coordinated belief make it capable of owning assets and causing harm.

**Assessment:** **Misleading compression.** A substantial scholarly debate remains about whether “fiction” is too blunt a category.

**Effect:** The simplified formula made the thesis memorable and portable into arguments over nationalism, religion, finance, and corporate personhood.

### 8. Family and intelligence legends

**Story:** Harari's father's classified armaments work proves that Harari is connected to Israeli intelligence or was groomed for global influence.

**Appearance and circulation:** Conspiratorial biographies link the father's state engineering, Harari's military exemption, and later meetings with leaders.

**Evidence:** The father's classified engineering employment is reported. Harari did not complete military service because of an undisclosed health condition. No intelligence appointment, security clearance, operational role, payment, or documentary recruitment record for Harari was found.

**Assessment:** **Association presented as proof.** The family background is relevant but does not establish the alleged connection.

**Effect:** It allows his later elite access to be narrated as a lifelong state project rather than as a consequence of publishing success.

### Broader works that challenge his historical model

David Graeber and David Wengrow's *The Dawn of Everything* (2021) is not a book about Harari, but its account of diverse and reversible political forms challenges the sort of linear, stage-based story associated with popular big histories including *Sapiens*. Works in archaeology and anthropology on forager violence, agricultural transitions, state formation, and indigenous political experimentation supply the evidence needed to evaluate particular Harari claims. They should not be miscatalogued as Harari biographies.

## Reputation, Influence, and Prospective Legacy

Because Harari is living, this is a record of present reception rather than a posthumous verdict.

### Academic reputation

His early reputation rests on recognizable peer-reviewed work in military history, especially war memoir, witness authority, logistics, and battlefield experience. His later influence is much larger but less disciplinary. Specialists often distinguish the careful archival medievalist from the assertive big-history narrator. Others see continuity: both bodies of work study how stories transform bodily events into institutions and identity.

The main unresolved academic question is not whether every claim in *Sapiens* is correct—some are demonstrably contested or outdated—but whether its organizing synthesis produces understanding that outweighs compression. The book's global classroom role guarantees continued line-by-line reassessment.

### Public and political influence

Harari gave millions of readers a portable vocabulary: cognitive revolution, imagined order, intersubjective reality, agricultural trap, Dataism, useless class, hackable animals. The phrases travel independently of their chapters. They shape discussions of nationalism, corporations, money, religion, animal agriculture, surveillance, and AI.

His access to heads of government, CEOs, Davos, TED, and mass media made him a broker between historical scholarship and decision-making elites. It also turned him into a target for movements that treat global forums as an integrated conspiracy. His public image is polarized between wise warning voice and sinister technocrat. Both images simplify a more conventional institutional path: successful academic book, literary-rights operation, global speaking, nonprofit and corporate structure, and policy advocacy.

### Literary and publishing influence

*Sapiens* helped normalize “big history” as a mass-market genre and showed publishers that a translated Hebrew history survey could become a global franchise. Its structure—biological deep time, a few named revolutions, striking moral reversals, and future scenarios—has been imitated and attacked. Graphic and children's adaptations extend the model to readers far outside universities.

The Harari/Yahav partnership is part of that legacy. Rather than treat authorship as isolated individual labor, they built a staff organization able to manage translation, rights, fact research, audiovisual development, philanthropy, and political interventions. The institutional author-brand may become as historically significant as the individual books.

### Mythic and symbolic afterlife already in progress

Harari appears as a character in his own graphic histories, speaks as a hologram, lives publicly as an ascetic millionaire, and alternates between ancient history and high technology. Those juxtapositions generate hagiography and hostile myth. Admirers use him as a secular sage; opponents use him as the face of posthuman rule. His own theory predicts this process: a person becomes a node around which many strangers coordinate stories.

The durable legacy will therefore include not only what he wrote but what was attributed to him. Fabricated quotations, clipped speeches, publisher superlatives, political condemnations, and the visual mythology of Davos are primary evidence for twenty-first-century information networks—the subject of *Nexus* itself.

## Atlas Connections

### Adam Smith

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari discusses Smith’s *The Wealth of Nations* and presents his 1776 argument that reinvested profit can enlarge the economy as a foundational ethical as well as economic shift in modern capitalism. The treatment appears in Chapter 16 of *Sapiens* and in Harari’s later discussion of the book. [Harari’s official excerpt](https://www.ynharari.com/topic/money-and-politics/), [EconTalk transcript](https://www.econtalk.org/yuval-harari-on-sapiens/)

### Charles Darwin

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** In *Sapiens*, Harari writes about Darwin’s theory of evolution, the reaction to treating *Homo sapiens* as another animal species, and the *Beagle* expedition’s relationship to imperial science. An authorized excerpt from the book also invokes Darwin while arguing that modern scientific research depends on political, economic, or ideological sponsorship. [Authorized *Sapiens* excerpt](https://www.salon.com/2015/02/16/the_myth_of_pure_science_its_all_about_political_economic_religious_interests/)

### Christopher Columbus

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari treats Columbus’s 1492 voyage in *Sapiens* as an emblem of the emerging European willingness to acknowledge ignorance and explore unknown territory, while also placing the voyages within imperial conquest. Harari’s official chapter references cite scholarship specifically concerning Columbus and the conquest of the Americas. [Official *Sapiens* references](https://www.ynharari.com/sapiens-references/)

### George Orwell

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari invokes Orwell’s formulation “ignorance is strength” in *Nexus* and uses *1984* as an important literary model for understanding totalitarian information systems, manufactured history, and surveillance. The connection is textual, not personal. [Review documenting the Orwell discussion](https://elpais.com/babelia/2024-09-13/nexus-de-yuval-noah-harari-un-mundo-ahogado-en-informacion.html)

### Isaac Newton

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari discusses Newton and the *Principia* when comparing truth-producing scientific works with socially binding religious narratives; *Nexus* argues provocatively that a text can exercise greater historical power without being more empirically accurate. Newton also appears in the book’s supporting notes. [Review of *Nexus*](https://elpais.com/babelia/2024-09-13/nexus-de-yuval-noah-harari-un-mundo-ahogado-en-informacion.html), [official *Nexus* notes](https://www.ynharari.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Notes-NEXUS-Yuval-Noah-Harari-September-2024.pdf)

### Jesus of Nazareth

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari writes about Jesus as a historical and religious figure in *Sapiens*, including Christianity’s growth from a small Jewish movement into a universal religion and the role of Christian narratives in large-scale cooperation. An authorized excerpt groups Jesus with Muhammad, Buddha, and Confucius when contrasting premodern sacred authority with modern belief in progress. [Authorized *Sapiens* excerpt](https://www.salon.com/2015/02/16/the_myth_of_pure_science_its_all_about_political_economic_religious_interests/)

### Karl Marx

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari repeatedly engages Marx’s analysis of religion, class, capitalism, and historical change in *Sapiens*, *Homo Deus*, and later lectures. In *Homo Deus* he calls Marx’s mid-nineteenth-century economic insights brilliant while using Marx as an example of how knowledge can alter the system it describes. [Authorized *Homo Deus* extract](https://arkisto.sitra.fi/en/news/yuval-noah-harari-better-understand-happening-make-minds-makes-minds-us/)

### Muhammad

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari writes about Muhammad and the development of Islam in his broad histories of religion and empire. An authorized *Sapiens* excerpt expressly names Muhammad among the premodern religious authorities whose inability to abolish famine, disease, poverty, and war contrasts with modern confidence in scientific progress. [Authorized *Sapiens* excerpt](https://www.salon.com/2015/02/16/the_myth_of_pure_science-its-all-about-political-economic-religious-interests/)

### Noah

- **[T1-] Documented fact:** Harari uses the biblical Noah and the flood narrative in *Nexus* as an example of a story whose cultural and organizational power cannot be reduced to its empirical accuracy. Contemporary review coverage describes the book as moving directly between Noah’s Ark, mythology, and present-day artificial intelligence. [Review of *Nexus*](https://elpais.com/babelia/2024-09-13/nexus-de-yuval-noah-harari-un-mundo-ahogado-en-informacion.html)
