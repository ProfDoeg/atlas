## Works by Gilles de Montmorency-Laval

### Liturgical institutions and ensembles

- **Before 26 March 1435:** *Foundation of the collegiate chapel of the Holy Innocents at Machecoul*. An institutional obra comprising a chapel, chapter, clergy, revenues, possessions, vestments, music, and ceremonial offices rather than merely a building; Gilles confirmed the earlier foundation on 26 March 1435, but papal authorization for the collegiate body was reportedly refused. ([foundation document](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ABossard_-_Gilles_de_Rais_dit_Barbe-Bleue%2C_1886.djvu/84); [Bossard, pp. 60–63](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Fichiers_PdF/ABC/Bossard/Gilles_Rais.pdf))

- **Before 1435; lost:** *Rules and statutes of the collegiate chapel of the Holy Innocents*. A protocol governing the projected institution, reportedly written in Gilles’s own hand; its existence and authorship rest on testimony attributed to one of his associates, while the text itself is not known to survive. ([Bossard, p. 62 and note 2](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Page%3ABossard_-_Gilles_de_Rais_dit_Barbe-Bleue%2C_1886.djvu/86))

- **By the mid-1430s:** *Household chapel*. A mobile liturgical Gesamtkunstwerk assembled under Gilles’s patronage: approximately twenty-five to thirty clergy, chaplains, singers, and choirchildren, elaborate vestments and ornaments, and portable organs carried during travel. It resists treatment as a single work because it was simultaneously a household department, performing ensemble, ceremonial image, and collection of commissioned objects. ([Vallet de Viriville, *Histoire de Charles VII*, vol. II](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/TUV/Vallet_Viriville/Charles_VII/T2/C7_2603.htm); [Bossard, chapter III](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/ABC/Bossard/Rais/Rais_030.htm))

### Performances and theatrical patronage

- **Circa 1432–1440; individual dates and titles unrecorded:** *Mysteries, farces, moralities, morisques, and personnages*. A documented but unitemizable body of commissioned or sponsored performances: the heirs’ memorandum distinguishes these forms, yet preserves no title for any individual production. This therefore resists classification as a list of authored plays; what survives is evidence of a theatrical project and patronal practice, not identifiable texts. ([Bossard, chapter IV](https://www.mediterranee-antique.fr/Auteurs/Fichiers/ABC/Bossard/Rais/Rais_040.htm))

- **Allegedly 1435, or 1435–1439; attribution disputed:** *Le Mystère du siège d’Orléans*. Various scholars have described Gilles as commissioner, sponsor, partial composer, producer, or performer, but none of those roles is securely established. The sole surviving text is anonymous, probably took shape only after 1450, and may incorporate earlier material; Philippe Contamine finds no positive evidence connecting Gilles’s documented theatrical activity at Orléans with this particular mystery, while Vicki Hamblin also questions whether the surviving version was ever staged. ([Hamblin edition review in *Revue d’histoire de l’Église de France*](https://www.persee.fr/doc/rhef_0048-7988_2003_num_89_222_4043_t13_0158_0000_2); [review in *Le Moyen Âge*](https://shs.cairn.info/revue-le-moyen-age-2004-2-page-I?lang=fr); [IRHT/CNRS JONAS record](https://jonas.irht.cnrs.fr/oeuvre/6229))
