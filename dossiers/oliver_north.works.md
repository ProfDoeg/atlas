## Works by Oliver Laurence North

### Testimony as documentary manifestation

- **1987:** *Taking the Stand: The Testimony of Lieutenant Colonel Oliver L. North*. A published transcript of North’s Iran–Contra testimony, introduced by Daniel Schorr. This resists classification as an authored book: the underlying words are North’s, but the made object is a congressional record selected and packaged by others.

### Memoir, reportage, devotional writing, and political argument

- **1991:** *Under Fire: An American Story*. Memoir co-authored with William Novak.
- **1993:** *One More Mission: Oliver North Returns to Vietnam*. A memoir-reportage hybrid, co-authored with David Roth, structured around North’s return to Vietnam.
- **2004:** *True Freedom: The Liberating Power of Prayer*. A short devotional and argument about Christian prayer, co-authored with Brian Smith. Some bibliographies date it 2003, while the publisher record and contemporary promotion identify the 2004 publication.
- **2004:** *A Greater Freedom: Stories of Faith from Operation Iraqi Freedom*. An anthology of military and religious testimony for which North was executive editor; Sara Horn edited and assembled the stories. It is therefore an editorial project rather than a solely authored text.
- **2003:** *War Stories: Operation Iraqi Freedom*. Illustrated military reportage derived from North’s Fox News television coverage; the book and accompanying DVD make it a mixed-media manifestation of the television project.
- **2004:** *War Stories II: Heroism in the Pacific*. Illustrated military-history narrative, associated with the television series and co-authored with Joe Musser.
- **2005:** *War Stories III: The Heroes Who Defeated Hitler*. Illustrated military-history narrative, likewise co-authored with Joe Musser and developed from the television project.
- **2008:** *American Heroes: In the Fight Against Radical Islam*. Reportage and military-profile collection, the first volume of the *American Heroes* sequence.
- **2010:** *American Heroes: In Special Operations*. Reportage and oral-history-style profiles of special-operations personnel; Chuck Holton is credited editorially.
- **2013:** *American Heroes: On the Homefront*. Reportage and profiles concerning military personnel, veterans, and their families in the United States.
- **2020:** *Veterans’ Lament: Is This the America Our Heroes Fought For?* Political argument constructed partly from veterans’ interviews, co-authored with David L. Goetsch.
- **2021:** *We Didn’t Fight for Socialism: America’s Veterans Speak Up*. Political polemic and collection of veterans’ testimony, co-authored with David L. Goetsch.
- **2022:** *Tragic Consequences: The Price America Is Paying for Rejecting God and How to Reclaim Our Culture for Christ*. Christian political and cultural polemic, co-authored with David L. Goetsch.
- **2023:** *American Gulags: Marxist Tyranny in Higher Education and What to Do About It*. Political polemic and proposed program of action, co-authored with David L. Goetsch and Archie P. Jones.

### Novels

- **2002:** *Mission Compromised*. Political and military thriller co-authored with Joe Musser; the first Peter Newman novel.
- **2003:** *The Jericho Sanction*. Political and military thriller co-authored with Joe Musser; the second Peter Newman novel.
- **2005:** *The Assassins*. Political and military thriller co-authored with Joe Musser; the third Peter Newman novel.
- **2009:** *After Jihad*. Speculative political thriller and fourth Peter Newman novel. The first publication is reported as 2009, although later catalogue records sometimes supply 2012 or 2013 dates for subsequent editions.
- **2012:** *Heroes Proved*. Political thriller and fifth Peter Newman novel.
- **2014:** *Counterfeit Lies*. Stand-alone crime and counterterrorism thriller co-authored with former FBI agent Bob Hamer.
- **2019:** *The Rifleman*. Historical thriller centered on a Marine marksman in the Pacific War.
- **2022:** *The Giant Awakes: A Jake Kruse Novel*. Political and crime thriller co-authored with Bob Hamer; the first Jake Kruse novel.

### Broadcast projects and screen work

- **1995–2003:** *The Oliver North Radio Show*, also called *Common Sense Radio with Oliver North*. A nationally syndicated talk-radio project hosted by North. It is a body of broadcasts rather than a single fixed text, and no authoritative episode-level catalogue appears to have been published.
- **1999–2000:** *Equal Time*. A political television discussion program co-hosted by North during this period. His hosting contribution is a performance and editorial role, not authorship of the series as a whole.
- **2001–2016:** *War Stories with Oliver North*. A television military-history project hosted by North, comprising 101 reported episodes and later generating books and individual DVD releases. It resists treatment as North’s sole work: Pamela K. Browne was executive producer, while North was its presenter, interviewer, and organizing public voice.
- **2012:** *Call of Duty: Black Ops II*. A video game to which North contributed as military consultant and in which he voiced an on-screen version of himself. This is a credited contribution to a collaborative Gesamtkunstwerk, not a game authored by North.
- **2014:** *“Martial Eagle”*. An episode of *The Americans* for which North and Tracey Scott Wilson received joint “story by” credit; the teleplay credit belongs to Wilson. North’s obra here is therefore the underlying television story contribution, not the completed episode in isolation.

### Uncollected and institutionally produced texts

- **1980s:** *National Security Council memoranda and operational papers*. North produced a large body of official memoranda, plans, electronic messages, and briefing papers while serving on the National Security Council. This corpus has no single title or complete authorial edition; attribution is further complicated because investigators found that North rewrote or altered some documents after the Iran–Contra affair became public, so originals and later altered manifestations must not be silently conflated.
- **Before 1994; later resumed for approximately seventeen years:** *Syndicated political columns*. A dispersed body of newspaper commentary rather than a fixed book. No complete scholarly or publisher-issued checklist was located, so treating every web repost or newspaper appearance as a separate work would confuse texts with their editions.
- **1987:** *Iran–Contra opening statement and congressional testimony*. An oral-political text that acted as public defense, legal testimony, and political performance—closer to a manifesto-like intervention than a conventional descriptive essay. Its later books, transcripts, recordings, and broadcasts are manifestations of the testimony, not additional authored works.
