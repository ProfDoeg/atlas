## Works by Blaise de Vigenère

### Original treatises, protocols, and political texts

- **1576:** *La somptueuse et magnifique entrée du très-chrestien Roy Henry III en la cité de Mantoue*. An illustrated festival-book and eyewitness chorography: Vigenère describes Henri III’s ceremonial entry and Mantua’s antiquities, artworks, and monuments. It is both historical report and multimedia manifestation, since text and engraved “portraicts” jointly construct the event. ([EDITEF](https://editef.univ-tours.fr/it/personne/1121/); [Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/?lang=en_US&product_id=209&view=product))

- **1578:** *Traicté des comètes, ou estoilles chevelues, apparoissantes extraordinairement au ciel, avec leurs causes et effects*. A cosmological and astrological treatise prompted by contemporary comets, combining observation, natural philosophy, historical compilation, and prognostication rather than constituting an astronomical protocol in the modern sense. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf); [ILAB astronomy catalogue](https://ilab.org/assets/catalogues/Early-Astronomy-list-compressed.pdf))

- **1586:** *Traicté des chiffres, ou secrètes manières d’escrire*. An encyclopedic treatise on writing, signs, alphabets, numerology, and cryptography; within it Vigenère supplies operational cipher protocols, including his autokey system. The later cipher conventionally called the “Vigenère cipher” is not simply identical with his invention, so that name should not be projected uncritically onto the whole work. ([RenTexte edition and bibliographical notice](https://wulfric.artsci.utoronto.ca/rentexte/vigenere/); [Jean-François Maillard study cited here](https://alde.fr/fr/livres-anciens/sciences/traicte-des-chiffres-ou-secretes-manieres-d-escrire-f00c8))

- **1594:** *Discours sur l’histoire du roy Charles VII, jadis escripte par Maistre Alain Chartier son secrétaire*. An anonymous royalist and anti-Spanish political manifesto originally conceived as a long preface to the accompanying chronicle; Abel L’Angelier’s testimony supports the attribution to Vigenère, but the printed text itself does not name him. The chronicle was then attributed to Alain Chartier but is now assigned to Gilles Le Bouvier, making the volume’s advertised authorship doubly unstable. ([USTC 7029](https://www.ustc.ac.uk/editions/7029); [bibliographical discussion](https://www.librairiedechaud.online/LibrairieAncienneJeanMarcDechaudCatalogue46DownLoad.pdf))

- **Written before 1596; published posthumously, 1618:** *Traicté du feu et du sel*. An alchemical, cosmological, and theological treatise found among Vigenère’s papers after his death. Its posthumous editorial state prevents certainty that the printed object represents a final authorial work rather than an unfinished or reorganized project. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/?lang=en_US&product_id=209&view=product); [University of Chicago study](https://knowledge.uchicago.edu/nanna/record/1409/files/NiermeierDohoney_uchicago_0330D_14609.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0))

### Histories, editions, and adaptive compilations

- **1573:** *Les Chroniques et annales de Poloigne*. A historical compilation and free adaptation, principally following Jan Herburt’s Latin epitome of Marcin Kromer rather than merely translating it. Vigenère openly abridged, rearranged, omitted, and supplemented his source, so the thing resists classification as either wholly original history or straightforward translation. ([Rita Mazzei, “Blaise de Vigenère e la Description du Royaume de Poloigne”](https://oajournals.fupress.net/index.php/cromohs/article/download/16438/14895?inline=1); [Urus scholarly record](https://urus.uw.edu.pl/copy/8923))

- **1573:** *La Description du royaume de Poloigne, et pays adjacens, avec les statuts, constitutions, mœurs et façons de faire d’iceux*. A geographical, political, ethnographic, and legal companion to the *Chroniques*, assembled from prior writings and Vigenère’s own framing rather than conceived as a travel narrative. Scholarship treats it and the *Chroniques* as two distinct works issued together in some surviving volumes. ([Mazzei](https://oajournals.fupress.net/index.php/cromohs/article/download/16438/14895?inline=1); [study of early collected volumes](https://publications-prairial.fr/pratiques-et-formes-litteraires/index.php?id=315&lang=en))

- **1585:** *L’Histoire de Geoffroy de Villehardouyn, mareschal de Champagne et de Romenie, de la conqueste de Constantinople*. A philological edition and intralingual translation: Vigenère prints Villehardouin’s Old French beside a modernized, expanded French rendering, adding historical and linguistic mediation. Because its source text, modernization, and annotations coexist, it is more accurately an editorial-translation apparatus than a single authored narrative. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf); [bibliographical description](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d8/Catalogue_d%27une_collection_de_livres_rares_et_pr%C3%A9cieux._Ouvrages_sur_la_chasse%3B_ancien_po%C3%ABtes_fran%C3%A7ais%3B_romans%2C_contes_et_fac%C3%A9ties%3B_voyages_dans_la_Terre-Sainte_et_en_Am%C3%A9rique_%28IA_cataloguedunecollectionpotier%29.pdf))

- **1594:** *L’Histoire mémorable des grands troubles de ce royaume sous le roy Charles VII*. Vigenère prepared this edition of a fifteenth-century chronicle for the duke of Nevers; the text was printed as Alain Chartier’s but is now attributed to Gilles Le Bouvier. Vigenère’s suppressed preface became the separate *Discours sur l’histoire du roy Charles VII*, so the edition and manifesto are related but not the same made thing. ([bibliographical account](https://www.librairiedechaud.online/LibrairieAncienneJeanMarcDechaudCatalogue46DownLoad.pdf); [USTC 7029](https://www.ustc.ac.uk/editions/7029))

### Translations with annotations and substantial re-creation

- **1575:** *Le Traicté de Cicéron de la meilleure forme d’orateurs; le sixiesme livre des Commentaires de Caesar; et la Germanie de Cornelius Tacitus*. A composite translation-object containing three ancient prose works, with Vigenère’s learned mediation. Since the three translations share one designed publication but retain distinct source identities, the volume is a small Gesamtkunstwerk of philology rather than one homogeneous “book.” ([Biblissima/Bibliothèques françoises](https://portail.biblissima.fr/fr/ark%3A/43093/edata39515abc3d1548f3c4d61875b6f208d200ba5f08))

- **1576–1589:** *Les Commentaires de César, des guerres de la Gaule*; later expanded as *Les Commentaires de Jules César, des guerres de la Gaule, plus ceux des guerres civiles*. An evolving translation-and-commentary project whose successive manifestations—1576, 1582/1584, and the enlarged 1589 version—revise Vigenère’s language and eventually broaden the Caesarian corpus. Scholarship analyzes these as successive authorial translations, not independent works created by catalogue duplication. ([Marie-Clarté Lagrée](https://www.persee.fr/doc/hes_0752-5702_2006_num_25_4_2616); [Brill study documenting the 1576 and 1589 forms](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004426474/BP000005.pdf))

- **1577:** *L’Histoire de la décadence de l’Empire grec et establissement de celuy des Turcs*. A French translation of Laonikos Chalkokondyles accompanied by a preface and extensive annotations that make it an interpretive history of Greeks, Ottomans, and contemporary Europe. Later continuations by Artus Thomas and others are not works by Vigenère and are therefore excluded. ([RenTexte bibliographical notice](https://wulfric.artsci.utoronto.ca/rentexte/vigenere/vigenere_notice.htm); [Anthony Kaldellis bibliography](https://bosnett.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Laonikos-Chalkokondyles-Anthony-Kaldellis-Ed.pdf))

- **1578; substantially revised through the posthumous 1597 manifestation:** *Les Images ou tableaux de platte peinture de Philostrate Lemnien*. A translation, ekphrastic commentary, art-theoretical treatise, and mythographic encyclopedia built around Philostratus’s imaginary paintings. Vigenère’s arguments and annotations vastly exceed a neutral translation; the expanded 1597 edition appeared after his death, although its title says it had been revised and augmented by the translator. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf); [Henri Bardon bibliographical reference](https://www.musicologie.org/Biographies/v/vigenere_blaise_de.html))

- **1579:** *Trois dialogues de l’amitié: le Lysis de Platon, le Laelius de Cicéron, et le Toxaris de Lucian*. A deliberately assembled triptych of translations on friendship. The three source texts remain separate dialogues, but Vigenère made their juxtaposition into a comparative philosophical collection. ([Biblissima/Bibliothèques françoises](https://portail.biblissima.fr/fr/ark%3A/43093/edata4f81af1c05bb6677fdb7aa3c8e8ed460d694d358))

- **1583:** *Les Décades qui se trouvent de Tite-Live*. A translation of the surviving Livian decades enlarged by annotations, archaeological figures, geographical descriptions, and a general chronology. These additions turn it into an antiquarian research instrument as well as a translated history. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/?lang=en_US&product_id=209&view=product); [De Gruyter/Brill bibliography](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110629675-025/pdf?licenseType=open-access))

- **Embedded in the annotations to the 1583 *Décades*:** *Adaptation of Paracelsus’s Liber de nymphis*. A shortened and rewritten French adaptation concerning nymphs, sylphs, pygmies, salamanders, and other elemental beings. It resists treatment as an autonomous work because Vigenère did not publish it separately; modern editors have extracted and titled it as a text in its own right. ([CELLF/CNRS colloquium programme](https://cellf.cnrs.fr/colloque/blaise-de-vigenere-1523-1596-prolixe-autodidacte/); [Oxford study](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A6d0221be-0af8-49e4-a21e-ffc02e58665c/files/d6q182m13g))

- **1590:** *Le Pouvoir et Commission de Monseigneur l’illustrissime et révérendissime cardinal Caëtan, légat député par le Saint-Siège apostolique au royaume de France*. A short French translation of a Latin diplomatic-ecclesiastical instrument, signed only “De la version de B.D.V.” It is closer to an operative political protocol than to an authored literary work. ([Paul Chavy in *Blaise de Vigenère, poète et mythographe*](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **1595:** *La Hierusalem du sieur Torquato Tasso rendue françoise*. A prose translation and expansive adaptation of Tasso’s *Gerusalemme liberata*. Vigenère says that he varied, omitted, added, and digressed, so this is a deliberate prose re-creation rather than a line-for-line equivalent of Tasso’s poem. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf); [EDITEF](https://editef.univ-tours.fr/it/personne/1121/))

- **Completed by 1593; published posthumously, 1605:** *L’Art militaire d’Onosender, autheur grec, où il traicte de l’office et devoir d’un bon chef de guerre*. A translation of Onasander’s military manual enveloped in extensive historical, political, and technical annotations. As a text that instructs commanders it is a protocol, but Vigenère’s commentary also makes the printed volume an encyclopedic military compilation. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf); [scholarly bibliography of Onasander](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Onasander/Introduction%2A.html))

### Philostratean translation project

- **1578; expanded posthumously, 1597:** *La Suite de Philostrate le jeune, avec arguments et annotations*. A translation and commentary on the younger Philostratus’s second series of imaginary paintings. It is related to, but textually distinct from, the elder Philostratus’s *Images*; publishers often bound the two together, which can make the separate work disappear into an edition. ([bibliographical description](https://www.livre-rare-book.com/book/5472657/19124); [Musicologie bibliography](https://www.musicologie.org/Biographies/v/vigenere_blaise_de.html))

- **Prepared before 1596; published posthumously, 1599:** *Philostrate, De la vie d’Apollonius Thianéen*. The first complete French translation of Philostratus’s philosophical biography, published from Vigenère’s manuscript. Later editions were corrected or augmented by other hands, so those alterations cannot all be assigned to him. ([Éditions Lyonnaises de Romans](https://rhr16-elr.unicaen.fr/elr/ELR.xml/ELR_58.html); [Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **Prepared before 1596; published with the *Apollonius* project by 1599/1611:** *Les Vies des sophistes*. A French translation of Philostratus’s collective intellectual biography, transmitted as part of the posthumous *Apollonius* publication complex rather than as a securely author-supervised independent book. ([BnF description of the composite edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb31102211c))

- **Prepared before 1596; published with the *Apollonius* project by 1599/1611:** *Response d’Eusebe, evesque de Césarée, contre Hierocles*. A French translation of Eusebius’s polemic comparing Apollonius and Christ, incorporated into the posthumous Philostratean compilation. Its status is therefore that of a component text rather than a separately manifested work. ([BnF description of the composite edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb31102211c))

- **Prepared before 1596; published posthumously by 1599/1611:** *Démonstration de l’immortalité de l’âme, tirée d’un seul axiome d’Aristote*. Vigenère’s own philosophical counter-text appended to the *Life of Apollonius*, opposing an Aristotelian demonstration to the biography’s marvelous revelation. It is an original essay, but its material existence is inseparable from the posthumously assembled *Apollonius* volume. ([BnF description of the composite edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark%3A/12148/cb31102211c))

### Devotional works and scriptural transformations

- **1587:** *Traicté de la pénitence et de ses parties*. A comparative sacramental and devotional treatise ranging across Jewish, Christian, Islamic, and ancient rites; its first manifestation also contains twenty-five psalms turned into Vigenère’s “prose mesurée.” The treatise describes doctrine but also seeks to regulate religious practice, giving parts of it the force of a protocol. ([Bruno Petey-Girard](https://projetorphee.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/vigenere-desportes-et-la-divine-poesie-et-musique-des-psaumes-bruno-petey-girard.pdf); [bibliographical description](https://books.google.com/books/about/Traict%C3%A9_de_la_penitence_et_de_ses_parti.html?id=FfB0i2Rwr80C))

- **1588:** *Le Psaultier de David torné en prose mesurée, ou vers libres*. A complete French transformation of the Psalms into a deliberately intermediate form between prose and verse. It is simultaneously translation, poetic creation, and liturgical instrument; “prose mesurée” names a made verbal form rather than a neutral container. ([Petey-Girard](https://projetorphee.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/vigenere-desportes-et-la-divine-poesie-et-musique-des-psaumes-bruno-petey-girard.pdf); [Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **1593–1595:** *Des prières et oraisons qui se doibvent conformer toutes à l’Escriture saincte, selon que l’Église catholique les reigle et ordonne*. A portable prayer manual begun in 1593 and published in 1595, assembling scripturally grounded instruction with psalmic prayer in Vigenère’s “demi-poésie.” It is explicitly a protocol for praying as well as a devotional composition. ([François Roudaut in *Blaise de Vigenère, poète et mythographe*](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf); [Corpus EVE](https://journals.openedition.org/eve/pdf/1450))

- **Prepared before 1596; published posthumously, 1605/1606:** *L’Esguillon de l’amour divin de sainct Bonaventure*. A French devotional translation attributed in the print tradition to Vigenère. Because it appeared only after his death, its precise authorial completion and the extent of later editorial intervention are not securely visible from the manifestation. ([Musicologie bibliography](https://www.musicologie.org/Biographies/v/vigenere_blaise_de.html))

### Lost or unmanifested projects

- **Before 1596; now lost:** *French verse translation of the Book of Job*. Antoine Du Verdier reported that Vigenère had made this scriptural version with annotations drawn from Kabbalah, the Zohar, and the Talmud; no surviving manuscript or print is presently identified. It is therefore a reported project or lost obra, not an extant text that can be independently examined. ([Paul Chavy’s survey in *Blaise de Vigenère, poète et mythographe*](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **Before 1596; now lost:** *French verse translation of Proverbs*. Reported by Du Verdier as part of the same annotated scriptural undertaking; its title, completion, and physical form are unknown. ([Paul Chavy’s survey](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **Before 1596; now lost:** *French verse translation of Ecclesiastes*. This survives only as a bibliographer’s report, so calling it a finished work would exceed the evidence. ([Paul Chavy’s survey](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **Before 1596; now lost:** *French verse translation of the Song of Songs*. A reported, annotated scriptural translation whose manuscript and exact title are lost; it is best classified as an unattested manifestation of a documented project. ([Paul Chavy’s survey](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **Before 1596; now lost:** *French verse translation of Ecclesiasticus*. Du Verdier includes it in Vigenère’s Kabbalistically annotated biblical versions, but no surviving object establishes its final extent. ([Paul Chavy’s survey](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))

- **Before 1596; manuscript translations left at death, exact inventory uncertain:** *Other manuscript translations*. Contemporary bibliography says that Vigenère died leaving “several translations” in manuscript, but not every item can now be matched securely to a surviving text or posthumous edition. This residue resists enumeration as works: it records a body of unfinished or lost making, not a licence to invent titles. ([Cahiers V. L. Saulnier](https://presses.ens.psl.eu/media/upload/document/secure-Blaise%20de%20Vigene%CC%80re.pdf))
