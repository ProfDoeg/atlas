## Works by Simon bar Jonah

### Historically recoverable activity

- **No fixed date:** *Oral preaching and teaching*. Simon Peter evidently produced repeated oral performances rather than a recoverable authored text; their wording and extent are lost. The speeches placed in his mouth in Acts and later Petrine literature are literary manifestations of that activity, not transcripts securely attributable to him.

### Canonical letters attributed to Peter

- **c. 60–65 or c. 75–95 CE:** *First Epistle of Peter (1 Peter)*. A Greek circular pastoral letter naming Peter as sender and Silvanus as its intermediary; some scholars defend composition during Peter’s lifetime, but most critical scholars regard it as a posthumous pseudonymous obra, so it cannot securely be called something Simon himself made ([Horrell](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0142064X0202400402); [Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* 3.3](https://www.ecatholic2000.com/eusebius/untitled-52.shtml)).

- **Late first to second century CE:** *Second Epistle of Peter (2 Peter)*. A testamentary letter-manifesto speaking as “Simon Peter” and presenting itself as his second letter; its dependence on Jude, retrospective treatment of the apostolic generation, and reception of Paul’s letters as scripture lead most scholars to judge it pseudepigraphic, while Petrine authorship remains a minority position ([*Jewish Annotated New Testament*](https://citer.ucp.pt/sites/default/files/2024-03/citer-catedra-abravanel-de-gois-amy-jill-levine-and-marc-zvi-brettler-the-jewish-annotated-new-testament.pdf); [Eusebius](https://www.ecatholic2000.com/eusebius/untitled-52.shtml)).

### Early works ascribed to Peter

- **Second half of the second century CE:** *Gospel of Peter*. An anonymous passion-and-resurrection narrative whose surviving principal manuscript has neither title nor complete beginning or ending; ancient readers associated it with Peter, but scholarship treats it as pseudepigraphic and the surviving object as only a fragment of an uncertain larger whole ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, ECCA 670/CANT 13](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/gospel-of-peter/); [Oxford, *The Apocryphal New Testament*](https://academic.oup.com/book/34752/chapter-abstract/296715966)).

- **Early second century CE:** *Apocalypse of Peter* (Greek/Ethiopic). An apocalyptic vision or revelatory dialogue in which Peter receives a tour or disclosure of postmortem rewards and punishments; it survives through discrepant Greek fragments and fuller Ethiopic witnesses, making its original extent and form uncertain, and it is pseudonymous rather than Peter’s own obra ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, ECCA 723/CANT 317](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/apocalypse-of-peter/)).

- **Second or third century CE:** *Coptic Apocalypse of Peter*. A distinct pseudonymous apocalypse, not a version of the Greek/Ethiopic work, presenting Peter as recipient of esoteric visions about crucifixion, persecution, and competing Christian authorities; it survives in a fourth-century Coptic manuscript and cannot be attributed historically to Peter ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, ECCA 782/CANT 324](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/apocalypse-of-peter-coptic/)).

- **Second century CE, date disputed:** *Acts of Peter*. An apocryphal acts narrative about Peter’s contest with Simon Magus and his martyrdom, preserved chiefly in a Latin witness plus Greek martyrdom texts. Eusebius lists it among works ascribed to Peter, but it is fundamentally a story about him rather than a work plausibly made by him, and scholars dispute the relationship, date, and original extent of its surviving components ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, ECCA 933/CANT 190](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/acts-of-peter/); [Baldwin, *Whose Acts of Peter?*](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/whose-acts-of-peter-9783161570612/)).

- **Second century CE, approximate:** *Preaching of Peter (Kerygma Petrou)*. A lost instructional or missionary text surviving only in quotations and reports; it was anciently ascribed to Peter, but its fragmentary state prevents reconstruction of either its complete wording or precise genre, and it is not accepted as his historical composition ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, ECCA 684/CANT 208](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/preaching-of-peter/); [Eusebius](https://www.ecatholic2000.com/eusebius/untitled-52.shtml)).

- **Late second or early third century CE:** *Letter of Peter to Philip*. A short pseudonymous letter that becomes the framing device for a much larger post-resurrection revelatory dialogue; two Coptic witnesses preserve variant forms of a presumed Greek composition, so even calling the entire obra a “letter” is misleading ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, ECCA 612/CANT 26](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/epistle-of-peter-to-philip/); [Lanzillotta and Bremmer, *The Apocalypse of Peter in Context*](https://www.peeters-leuven.be/pdf/9789042952096.pdf)).

- **Probably third century CE in its prefatory form; transmitted in fourth-century compilations:** *Epistle of Peter to James*. A pseudonymous protocol directing James to restrict and supervise transmission of Peter’s teachings; it functions as a prefatory document within the Greek Pseudo-Clementine *Homilies*, not as independently verifiable correspondence by Simon Peter ([NASSCAL e-Clavis, *Pseudo-Clementines*, ECCA 638/CANT 209](https://www.nasscal.com/e-clavis-christian-apocrypha/pseudo-clementines/)).

### Mediated manifestation rather than Petrine obra

- **Late first or early second century CE, underlying tradition older:** *Gospel according to Mark*. Papias, as quoted by Eusebius, describes Mark as Peter’s interpreter who wrote remembered teachings, and later tradition consequently treated the Gospel as a manifestation of Peter’s preaching. The Gospel itself is anonymous and does not claim Peter as maker; the proposed connection is a disputed reception tradition, not Petrine authorship.
