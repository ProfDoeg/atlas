## Works by Nizami Ganjavi

### Composite poetic cycle

- **c. 1166–1197:** *Khamsa* (“Quintet”), also called *Panj Ganj* (“Five Treasures”). This is the collective obra formed by five long Persian *masnavis*, not an additional sixth poem; although manuscript tradition treats it as a unified cycle, its constituent poems were made separately over decades, and scholars dispute their chronology and even whether the two parts of *Eskandar-nama* should count as separate works. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamsa-of-nezami/); [Cambridge University Press](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/73642/excerpt/9780521873642_excerpt.pdf))

### Didactic masnavi

- **Probably 1166 or shortly afterward; traditionally 1174 or 1176:** *Makhzan al-Asrar* (“Treasury of Mysteries”). A didactic and ethical Persian *masnavi* organized around twenty discourses and illustrative narratives; François de Blois’s early dating differs by about a decade from dates traditionally inferred from the manuscripts. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamsa-of-nezami/); [TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/nizami-i-gencevi))

### Narrative and romantic masnavis

- **1175/76–1191 or shortly afterward:** *Khosrow o Shirin*. A Persian romantic epic about the Sasanian king Khosrow II and Shirin, constructed in stages rather than produced on one securely datable occasion: its writing, completion, dedication, and later epilogue have different proposed dates, and both its dedicatees and textual extent remain matters of discussion. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica: “Khosrow o Shirin”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kosrow-o-sirin); [Encyclopaedia Iranica: “Khamsa of Nezami”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamsa-of-nezami/))

- **1188, with possible additions through 1192:** *Leyli o Majnun*. A Persian romantic *masnavi* recasting the Arabic story of the separated lovers Layli and Majnun; the core poem is securely associated with 584 AH/1188, while some scholars recognize later additions that complicate any single completion date. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamsa-of-nezami/); [TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/nizami-i-gencevi))

- **1197:** *Haft Peykar* (“Seven Portraits”), also known as *Bahram-nama*. A romantic-didactic epic organized around the Sasanian king Bahram Gur, seven princesses, seven pavilions, and their embedded tales; de Blois’s reconstruction makes it the last-composed poem of the *Khamsa*, although most manuscripts place it before *Eskandar-nama*. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/haft-peykar/); [Encyclopaedia Iranica: “Khamsa of Nezami”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamsa-of-nezami/))

### Alexander epic

- **Probably 1194; other chronologies place its parts later:** *Eskandar-nama* (“Book of Alexander”). An epic-didactic obra made of two formally separate *masnavis*, *Sharaf-nama* and *Eqbal-nama* (also called *Kherad-nama*), which respectively emphasize Alexander as conqueror and as philosopher-prophet; it therefore resists classification as either one text or two. De Blois dates the whole to 590 AH/1194, whereas other traditions assign the parts substantially later, even beyond some proposed dates of Nizami’s death. ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/kamsa-of-nezami/); [Cambridge University Press](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/73642/excerpt/9780521873642_excerpt.pdf); [TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/nizami-i-gencevi))

### Disputed and fragmentarily surviving lyric corpus

- **No secure date:** *Divan*. Medieval reports describe a collection of approximately 20,000 verses comprising *qasidas*, *ghazals*, stanzaic poems, quatrains, and fragments, but no complete copy survives; three heavily truncated manuscripts and poems preserved in anthologies do not establish a stable corpus, and the transmitted *Divan* has been suspected of forgery. This is therefore less a securely bounded surviving obra than a reported collection whose extent and attribution remain unresolved. ([Cambridge University Press](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805218/73642/excerpt/9780521873642_excerpt.pdf); [TDV İslâm Ansiklopedisi](https://islamansiklopedisi.org.tr/nizami-i-gencevi); [Encyclopaedia Iranica: “Nozhat al-Majales”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/nozhat-al-majales/))
