## Works by Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi

### Autobiographical and political obras

- **1960–1961:** *Mission for My Country* (*Maʾmuriyat barā-ye vaṭanam*). An autobiography, national history, and political programme presented as the Shah’s own work; the Persian edition is dated 1339/1960–61, while English editions appeared in 1961. Its production involved unnamed or incompletely credited collaborators, so individual authorship should not be treated as certain. [1][2]

- **1980:** *Answer to History* (*Réponse à l’histoire*; *Pāsokh be tārikh*). A memoir and political apologia composed in exile during the Shah’s final illness; it answers accusations against his reign and interprets the revolution that overthrew him. French and English versions appeared in 1980, whereas the Persian publication cited by the standard bibliography is posthumous, so those publication dates are editions rather than separate obras. [1][3]

### Manifestos and programmes of rule

- **1966–1967:** *The White Revolution* (*Enqelāb-e sefid*; also *The White Revolution of the Shah and the People*). A manifesto and explanatory protocol for the reform programme begun in 1963: it does not merely describe policy but legitimates and directs a continuing royal project. Persian bibliographies date it 1345/1966–67, producing the one-year variation found in Western references. [1][4]

- **1977:** *Toward the Great Civilization* (*Be su-ye tamaddon-e bozorg*). A manifesto-project setting out the Shah’s intended next stage of economic, technological, social, and cultural transformation. The “Great Civilization” remained an unrealized political project after the monarchy’s collapse, so the project—not merely its printed exposition—is part of the obra. [1][5]

### Speeches and interview corpora

- **1961:** *Collection of Speeches* (*Majmuʿa-ye noṭqhā*). A published corpus of royal speeches rather than a continuously composed book. The Shah made the constituent public utterances, but their assembly, transcription, and editorial form belong partly to court institutions; the standard biographical bibliography records the Tehran collection without identifying those mediators. [1]

- **1961–1963:** *Speeches of Shahanshah Aryamehr from 1340 to 1342* (*Sokhanān-e Shāhanshāh Āryāmehr az sāl-e 1340 tā 1342*). A bounded compilation of speeches corresponding approximately to 1961–64; it is best classified as an archival manifestation of performed political texts, not as a single authored treatise. The surviving catalogue description does not establish whether it wholly or partly overlaps the broader speech collections. [6]

- **By 1976:** *Iran’s Domestic and Foreign Policy: Excerpts from Speeches and Interviews of His Imperial Majesty Mohammad Reza Pahlavi Aryamehr, Shahanshah of Iran*. An English-language documentary compilation drawn from otherwise dispersed speeches and interviews. It resists classification as a new work by the Shah because Manoutchehr Ardalan compiled it; the identifiable Shah-made things are the underlying oral statements, whose complete boundaries are not supplied by the volume. [7]

### Dynastic commemoration of uncertain date

- *Reza Shah the Great*. A short biographical and commemorative text about the Shah’s father, attributed to Mohammad Reza Pahlavi and translated into English by M. Varasteh. Accessible records give no defensible original date—the reported English item is explicitly undated, while another repository’s “1922” date is impossible for a work by its three-year-old attributed author—so no date is imposed here. [6][8][9]
