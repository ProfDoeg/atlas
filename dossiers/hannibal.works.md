## Works by Hannibal Barca

### Literary works

- **After 189–before 183 BC:** *To the Rhodians, on the Deeds of Gnaeus Manlius Vulso in Asia*. A lost Greek prose work—apparently a political-historical address concerning Vulso’s Asian campaign—known only from Cornelius Nepos; even its conventional English title is reconstructed from Nepos’ description. ([Nepos, *Hannibal* 13.2](https://www.attalus.org/translate/nepos23.html); [Dickinson commentary](https://dcc.dickinson.edu/it/nepos-hannibal/chapter-13))

- **Before 183 BC; individual dates and titles unknown:** *Other books in Greek*. Nepos says that “several” Greek books by Hannibal existed but identifies only the address to the Rhodians; their number, subjects, titles, extent, and relationship to one another are irrecoverable, so this corpus resists division into separate works. ([Nepos, *Hannibal* 13.2](https://www.attalus.org/translate/nepos23.html); [*Fragmenta Historicorum Graecorum*, vol. 3, p. 99](https://www.dfhg-project.org/DFHG/urn_retriever.php?URN=urn%3Alofts%3Afhg.3.hannibal_carthaginiensis%3A))

### Inscribed monument

- **205 BC:** *Altar and record of Hannibal’s achievements at the sanctuary of Juno Lacinia*. A lost bilingual Punic-and-Greek commemorative monument: Livy describes an altar bearing an extensive inscription, while Polybius describes a bronze tablet or stele containing Hannibal’s troop lists. They are commonly treated as the same monumental complex, but the differing accounts leave its precise material form uncertain; as altar, dedication, inscription, archive, and political self-representation together, it approaches a *Gesamtkunstwerk*. ([Polybius, *Histories* 3.33.18](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Plb.3.33); [Livy 28.46.16 and discussion](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780511741012_A24404745/preview-9780511741012_A24404745.pdf); [Getty Research Institute study](https://www.getty.edu/research/exhibitions_events/events/ancient_mediterranean/kuttner.pdf))

### Diplomatic and governmental instruments

- **215 BC:** *Oath-treaty between Hannibal and Philip V of Macedon*. A jointly made diplomatic protocol rather than Hannibal’s sole-authored work: Polybius preserves a Greek version of the covenant sworn by Hannibal, his associates, and Philip’s representatives. Its Near Eastern covenant form and language have been taken as evidence that the surviving Greek derives from an official Punic document, although its precise drafting process and individual authorship remain unknown. ([Polybius, *Histories* 7.9](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0234%3Abook%3D7%3Achapter%3D9); [Elias Bickerman, “An Oath of Hannibal”](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Magazine/T/Transactions%20of%20the%20American%20Philological%20Society/1944/Bickerman%20-%201944%20-%20An%20Oath%20of%20Hannibal.pdf))

- **196 BC:** *Reform of the Carthaginian tribunal of the Hundred and Four*. An enacted constitutional project, not a freestanding literary text: according to Livy, Hannibal carried a law through the popular assembly replacing the judges’ life tenure with annual terms and barring immediate reelection. The measure survives only through a later Roman narrative, not as the text of the law itself. ([Livy, *History of Rome* 33.46](https://bcs.fltr.ucl.ac.be/liv/XXXIII.html))

- **196 BC:** *Fiscal audit and indemnity-payment program*. An administrative project attributed by Livy to Hannibal: he investigated diverted public revenues and proposed paying Rome’s indemnity without a new levy on citizens. Because no accounts, decree, or implementing protocol survives, this is recoverable as a policy made effective rather than as a securely bounded textual work. ([Livy, *History of Rome* 33.47](https://bcs.fltr.ucl.ac.be/liv/XXXIII.html))

### Urban projects and attributed foundations

- **During Hannibal’s eastern exile; exact date unknown:** *Plan or foundation of Artaxata*. Strabo and Plutarch credit Hannibal with recognizing the Armenian site’s advantages, designing a city for Artaxias I, and supervising its construction; in this account the work is an urban project executed under a king rather than a city made by Hannibal alone. Modern scholarship often doubts the tradition, and *Encyclopaedia Iranica* calls the associated story difficult to believe, so the attribution must remain disputed. ([Strabo 11.14.6 and Plutarch, *Lucullus* 31, summarized by *Encyclopaedia Iranica*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/armenia-ii/); [*Encyclopaedia Iranica*, “Artaxata”](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/artaxata-gk/))

- **During Hannibal’s residence with Prusias I; exact date unknown:** *Prusa beneath Mount Olympus*. Pliny calls Hannibal the city’s founder, while later interpretation understands him more cautiously as its planner or adviser to King Prusias, whose name the city bore. The conflicting foundation traditions make this an attributed urban project rather than a securely documented personal work. ([Pliny, *Natural History* 5.43](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0137%3Abook%3D5%3Achapter%3D43); [*Dictionary of Greek and Roman Geography*, “Prusa”](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.04.0064%3Aentry%3Dprusa-geo); [Dexter Hoyos, *Hannibal: Rome’s Greatest Enemy*, “Hannibal in Exile”](https://academic.oup.com/liverpool-scholarship-online/book/43403/chapter-abstract/363223986))

### Defensive architecture

- **Before 183 BC:** *Seven concealed exits from Hannibal’s residence at Libyssa*. A private escape system—variously described as doors, underground passages, caves, or vaults—made or ordered by Hannibal in anticipation of betrayal. Livy and Plutarch agree on seven exits but differ in architectural detail, and no physical remains are securely identified; it is therefore an attested defensive project rather than a recoverable building. ([Livy, *History of Rome* 39.51](https://reader.humanitext.ai/en/read/urn%3Acts%3AlatinLit%3Aphi0914.phi001.humanitext-lat2%3A39.51.1-39.51.12); [Plutarch, *Flamininus* 20](https://oll-resources.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/oll3/store/titles/1806/0988-04_Bk.pdf))
