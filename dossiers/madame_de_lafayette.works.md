## Works by Marie Adrienne Françoise de Noailles

### Biographical obra

- **1795–1796; printed 1800:** *Notice sur la vie de A. L. H. d’Aguesseau, duchesse d’Ayen*. A biographical and memorial obra about her mother, composed during and after imprisonment: the surviving manuscript was written clandestinely in the margins of a Buffon volume, and the first private printing appeared at Dampierre in Year IX. Later editions often place it before Virginie de Lasteyrie’s life of Adrienne, creating a composite, multigenerational book that should not be mistaken for a single-authored work. ([BnF bibliographic record](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb307135644); [Library of Congress finding aid, folder 18b](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf); [1869 composite edition](https://catalogue.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cb37274925x))

### Correspondence as an obra

- **At least 1783–1807:** *Surviving correspondence*. This is an extensive but incompletely published epistolary corpus, not a book: it includes letters to family members, political intermediaries, diplomats, estate agents, clergy, and George Washington. The principal family archive preserves originals, drafts, later transcripts, and notebooks copied by her daughters, so neither its exact size nor the completeness and authority of every copy can presently be stated. ([Library of Congress finding aid, especially folders 1b–1d, 3a, 7–7b, 18, 26–33, 37a, 136 and 143a](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf); [Cornell Lafayette Collection description](https://rmc.library.cornell.edu/findingaids.html))

- **26 December 1783:** *Letter to George Washington*. An English-language diplomatic and personal letter congratulating Washington on peace and retirement; it is simultaneously correspondence and a small public-political act, asserting Adrienne’s identification as both French and American. The currently accessible scholarly transcript is explicitly marked “early access” and not yet a final edited text. ([George Washington Papers, Founders Early Access](https://rotunda.upress.virginia.edu/founders/default.xqy?keys=FOEA-print-01-02-02-6233&mode=deref))

- **1792–1797:** *Letters and petitions concerning Lafayette’s captivity*. A dispersed campaign of action-texts addressed to judges, diplomats, intermediaries, and supporters; as petitions and interventions they act rather than merely describe, and therefore approach the atlas’s category of manifesto without forming one fixed manifesto. The archive preserves originals, copies, and transcriptions, but no definitive complete edition establishes a closed corpus. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folders 3a, 4a–4a bis, 7–7b and 18](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **8 September 1795:** *Letter to Lafayette*. A letter written immediately before Adrienne and her daughters joined Lafayette at Olmütz; the archive identifies it individually but does not supply a formal authorial title. It resists treatment as an autonomous “work,” although its preservation as a separately described object gives it a distinct documentary existence. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folder 18](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **1796–1797:** *Letters from Olmütz to John Parish, François-Antoine Boissy d’Anglas Masson, the princesse d’Hénin, Thomas Pinckney, Justus Erich Bollmann, and George Washington Lafayette*. These prison letters form an identifiable epistolary cluster preserved partly as copies; they combine family communication with an international campaign for release. The finding aid does not enumerate every item, so the cluster must remain an open corpus rather than a falsely complete list of titled works. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folders 7–7b](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **1798–1807:** *Family, estate, charitable, and political correspondence after Olmütz*. This continuing corpus includes letters to Lafayette, Mme Beauchet, Mlle Marchais, Jean-François Rewbell, the tutor Grellet, Adrienne’s children, Mme de Montagu, her aunt at Chavaniac, and the bishop of Rennes. It is practical writing—often closer to governance, instruction, or protocol than literary composition—and survives in an uncertain mixture of originals, drafts, and family copies. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folders 26–33, 37a, 136 and 143a](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

### Protocols, accounts, and documentary writings

- **1787–1794:** *Testaments et instructions*. A group of wills and instructions written before and during the Revolution and in prison: texts intended to govern property, guardianship, conduct, or action under the possibility of death. They are protocols and legal-personal instruments rather than a single literary work; the archival description does not provide individual titles or secure dates for every component. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folder 67a](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **Probably 1800–1803:** *Relation de la maladie de Charles de Maubourg*. An untitled or archivally titled account by Adrienne of Charles de La Tour-Maubourg’s illness. Its precise date and intended audience are not supplied by the finding aid, so it should be treated as a documentary narrative rather than assigned an invented title or year. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folder 33](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **1799–1807:** *Financial drafts, accounts, receipts, and payment records*. A practical documentary corpus produced while Adrienne managed family estates and finances; some items are drafts in her hand, while others are signed jointly or survive among records compiled by agents. These resist classification as “works” in the literary sense, but they are made texts and evidence of sustained administrative authorship. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folders 45, 136 and 143–143a](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **1807:** *Dernières volontés*. Adrienne’s final wishes, preserved as a separately classified archival object. This is a performative legal and devotional text—a protocol meant to take effect—rather than a retrospectively titled literary composition. ([Library of Congress finding aid, folder 46a](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

### Collaborative social projects

- **1785/1786–1792:** *The Cayenne or La Belle Gabrielle gradual-emancipation experiment*. A collaborative and ultimately unfinished abolitionist project initiated by Lafayette and increasingly administered by Adrienne: it joined plantations at La Gabrielle, La Maripa, and Saint-Régis to an intended program of instruction, paid labor, and gradual emancipation. It cannot be credited to Adrienne alone, and it remained compromised by continued legal enslavement; revolutionary confiscation and sale terminated the experiment before emancipation was completed. ([Lafayette College, “The Cayenne Experiments”](https://sites.lafayette.edu/slavery/the-cayenne-experiments/); [Library of Congress finding aid, folder 43](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))

- **1802–1803:** *Société and cemetery of Picpus*. A collective memorial project made by families of those buried in Picpus’s revolutionary mass graves: Adrienne participated in the subscription and served on its eleven-member committee, while her sister, the marquise de Montagu, was the principal organizer and committee president. It is an institution, burial ground, and continuing commemorative practice—not Adrienne’s sole “work”—and the society has no securely documented single foundation date beyond subscriptions beginning in 1802 and acquisition of the full site in 1803. ([Diocese of Paris history of Picpus](https://dioceseparis.fr/le-cimetiere-de-picpus.html); [Library of Congress finding aid, folder 42](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/eadxmlmss/eadpdfmss/1997/ms997004.pdf))
