# Samuel Oppenheimer: Research Dossier

## Scope, Identification, and Evidentiary Method

This dossier concerns **Samuel ben Simeon Oppenheim**, commonly called **Samuel Oppenheimer**, the seventeenth-century Jewish merchant, banker, imperial court factor, military contractor, and creditor of Emperor Leopold I.

It does **not** concern:

- the American lawyer and historian Samuel Oppenheim (1857–1928);
- the Austrian astronomer Samuel Oppenheim (1857–1928);
- J. Robert Oppenheimer (1904–1967);
- the later banking firm Oppenheimer & Co.;
- Joseph Süß Oppenheimer (c. 1698/99–1738), a documented relative but a distinct person.

The surviving record is unusually rich in government financial and judicial material but poor in intimate evidence. No full modern documentary biography has superseded Max Grunwald’s archival study of 1913. Exact figures in the literature often conflict because the imperial government, Oppenheimer’s firm, his suppliers, and his heirs held overlapping claims, assignments, guarantees, and debts; many business books were destroyed in the riot of 1700.

Evidentiary labels used below:

- **Documented fact:** supported by contemporary records, archival descriptions, or convergent scholarly sources.
- **Reported fact:** stated in a credible secondary source but not independently verified here from the original document.
- **Allegation:** an accusation made at the time or later, not established as fact.
- **Disputed interpretation:** historians or accounts disagree about meaning, motive, or responsibility.
- **Rumor or myth:** a circulating story without adequate documentary support.
- **Unresolved:** available evidence is insufficient to determine the matter.

## Family and Ancestry

### Parents and earlier family

According to the genealogical reconstruction in the *Neue Deutsche Biographie*:

- Father: **Simeon/Simon Wolf**, 1580–1664, resident in Worms.
- Mother: **Edel**; some later genealogical sources identify her more fully with a Drach or Bacharach family, but the precise maternal lineage varies between family trees.
- Brother: **Moses**, a Jewish communal leader in Heidelberg and Fürth.
- Sister: **Serlen**, wife of **Isaac Brilin**, a rabbi in Mannheim.

The family belonged to an interconnected elite of Ashkenazi merchant, rabbinical, and court-agent families. It had been expelled from Frankfurt am Main in 1612. The Oppenheim/Oppenheimer network intermarried with the Wertheimer, Behrens, Gomperz, Guggenheim, Drach, Sinzheim, and related families.

The surname likely denoted an ancestral association with the town of Oppenheim. It should not by itself be treated as proof that every later Oppenheim or Oppenheimer family belonged to Samuel’s direct line.

### Marriage

Samuel married **Sandela**, who died in 1705. She was a daughter of **Manoach Carcassone**, from a family reported to have migrated from France into Bohemia.

No evidence located in this research indicates a second marriage, extramarital partnership, or publicly documented domestic scandal.

### Children

The NDB records **four sons and five daughters**, although not every daughter is named in the compact genealogy.

Known sons include:

- **Emanuel**, also called **Mendel Oppenheimer**, died 1721. He became deeply involved in the family firm, was arrested in 1688 to pressure the firm into accelerating military deliveries, was arrested with his father in 1697, and later conducted the long struggle to recover imperial debts after Samuel’s death.
- **Wolf**, died 1726. He married **Frade**, daughter of the Hanoverian court agent **Leffmann/Elieser Behrens**.
- **Abraham Oppenheimer**.
- **Nathan Oppenheimer**.

A **Simon Wolf Oppenheimer**, sometimes identified as a son who established a Hanover banking house, appears in genealogical accounts. The variation between “Wolf” and “Simon Wolf” may reflect inconsistent rendering of compound Jewish names.

Known daughters include:

- **Frumet**, died 1713, who married **Josef Guggenheim**.
- Daughters who married **Löb Deutz zur goldenen Arche** of Frankfurt, **Emanuel Drach**, **Jacob Mannheimer**, and **Löw Sinzheim**, according to the NDB’s list of sons-in-law.

These marriages were components of the commercial infrastructure of the firm. Kinship linked capital, market information, court access, guarantees, and credit across imperial territories.

### Nephews, descendants, and extended relations

- **Rabbi David ben Abraham Oppenheim/Oppenheimer** (1664–1736), chief rabbi of Moravia and later Prague and Bohemia, was Samuel’s nephew. David inherited or absorbed part of Samuel’s wealth and book collection.
- **Samson Wertheimer** (1658–1724) was described variously as Samuel’s nephew, distant relative, or nephew-like associate. Modern biographical writing preserves uncertainty about the exact kinship. Wertheimer worked with Oppenheimer in Vienna before becoming an independent imperial financier.
- **Ruben Elias Gomperz** (1655–1705), a military supplier and court agent in Wesel, was a relative and business associate.
- **Joseph Süß Oppenheimer** (c. 1698/99–1738), the later Württemberg court financier, is called a relative in the NDB. Sources differ on whether he was properly Samuel’s nephew, grandnephew, or a more distant relation.
- **Fromet Guggenheim** (1737–1812), wife of philosopher Moses Mendelssohn, was Samuel’s great-granddaughter according to the NDB.

Claims that Samuel was a direct ancestor of **J. Robert Oppenheimer** are frequently repeated in unsourced internet genealogy but were not established by the authoritative material consulted. Shared surname, German-Jewish background, or descent from an older Worms family is not enough to prove direct descent.

## Childhood and Early Life

Very little securely documented information survives about Samuel’s childhood.

He was born during the Thirty Years’ War into a Jewish family whose recent history included expulsion from Frankfurt and residence in Worms and the Palatinate. This setting exposed the family to:

- the fragmented jurisdictions of the Holy Roman Empire;
- legal residence dependent on individual or communal protection;
- recurrent warfare and military requisitioning;
- the importance of mobile kinship and credit networks;
- close interaction between Jewish merchants and princely administrations.

No reliable source located here identifies a childhood school, private tutor, yeshiva, apprenticeship master, or formal academic education. His later activities demonstrate advanced commercial numeracy, familiarity with bills, assignments, contracts, commodity procurement, and administrative practice. Whether he personally wrote in German, Yiddish, Hebrew, or other languages, or regularly employed scribes, cannot be reconstructed fully from the sources consulted.

No documented early journey outside the German-speaking imperial territories has been identified.

## Education and Formation

### Formal education

**Unresolved.** No university attendance or formal professional qualification is known.

His upbringing was probably shaped by Jewish religious education and family-based commercial training, but this is an inference from milieu rather than a documented curriculum.

### Formative influences

The most demonstrable formative institutions were:

- his extended merchant family;
- the Palatine court and fiscal administration;
- the Jewish communal leadership of Heidelberg and Worms;
- the system of princely safe-conducts and court privileges;
- military supply during the recurrent wars of Louis XIV’s reign.

His success depended less on owning a conventional deposit bank than on commanding personal confidence across a network of merchants, landlords, carriers, court factors, officials, and princely clients.

## Early Career in the Palatinate

### Around 1660: supplier and chamber agent

By about **1660**, Oppenheimer was operating in Heidelberg as an army supplier to **Elector Karl Ludwig of the Palatinate**. Other accounts describe him as a chamber agent and tax collector for Palatine Jewry.

His work likely combined:

- purchasing provisions and materials;
- advancing commodities before government payment;
- collecting or remitting revenues;
- arranging credit;
- coordinating subcontractors and transport.

This was the characteristic basis of court-factor finance: the ruler granted access and privilege, while the factor supplied goods and liquidity that ordinary fiscal institutions could not obtain quickly.

The precise amount of Oppenheimer’s early capital is unknown. Sources do not establish whether it was inherited, borrowed from relatives, accumulated through tax collection, or assembled through partnerships. His later firm relied heavily on other people’s money and merchandise.

### 1672: financing the Rhine campaign

From **1672**, he supplied substantial funds and especially commodity credit for the imperial campaign on the Rhine against France.

Commodity credit meant that Oppenheimer and his suppliers delivered goods before the treasury paid. He financed the interval with his own capital, bills, assignments on future revenues, and loans from commercial partners.

This expanded his activity from Palatine service to imperial military finance.

## Move to Vienna and Imperial Appointment

### 1670 expulsion and the exceptional return

Emperor Leopold I had expelled Vienna’s Jewish community in **1670**. Yet the loss of Jewish taxes, credit, trade, and customers created economic problems. Access was gradually reopened on an exceptional basis to protected merchants.

Oppenheimer moved to Vienna in **1674**, according to the NDB. Some older popular accounts date his arrival to approximately 1680. The 1674 date is better supported in the modern synthesis.

Leopold allowed him to reside in Vienna with his **Gesinde**, literally household or retinue. The term covered servants, clerks, commercial dependants, relatives, and protected Jewish families. Oppenheimer’s privilege thereby became a legal shelter under which a limited Jewish presence could re-emerge.

He eventually received permission to own or occupy a substantial house in central Vienna, an exceptional concession following the expulsion.

### 1679: imperial war factor

In **1679**, Oppenheimer was appointed **kaiserlicher Kriegsfaktor**, imperial war factor or official military supplier.

His responsibilities grew to encompass:

- procurement of flour and grain;
- bread supplies;
- oats, hay, and straw for horses;
- remounts;
- textiles, equipment, and other military necessities;
- transport to magazines and armies;
- cash advances and bills;
- court luxury goods and ordinary household provisions;
- timber for imperial construction;
- monetary transfers between territories.

His organization was not a corporation in the modern sense. It was a personally managed house held together by kinship, partnership, privilege, correspondence, bills, and reputation.

## Chronological Life History

### 1679–1683: consolidation in imperial service

Between his appointment and the Ottoman siege, Oppenheimer enlarged a Central European supplier network. Identified counterparties in the broader literature include Jewish court agents and purveyors such as:

- Aron Beer of Frankfurt;
- Löw Sinzheim;
- Moses Isaac of Bamberg;
- members of the Gomperz family;
- Leffmann Behrens;
- Samson Wertheimer.

He also bought grain surpluses from large estate owners, especially in Bohemia. He arranged transport by road and along the Danube.

The balance of risk was asymmetrical. The government demanded performance on military schedules but paid late, incompletely, or through assignments on future taxes. Oppenheimer remained liable to his own suppliers even when the emperor had not paid him.

### 1683: Ottoman siege of Vienna

During the Ottoman siege and relief of Vienna in **1683**, Oppenheimer helped supply the relief army and imperial forces.

His network furnished food, fodder, money, and other necessities for the army moving to relieve the capital. In the subsequent reconquest of Hungary, he supplied military magazines.

The NDB records a river fleet of **eight vessels with fifty-two men**, employed to carry grain and fodder on the Danube.

Later tradition says **Prince Eugene of Savoy** called him the “savior from Turkish distress.” This is widely repeated, but the exact original wording and documentary context were not established in the sources consulted. It should be treated as a **reported encomium**, not as a verified verbatim quotation.

The *Jewish Encyclopedia* also reports that Oppenheimer made it possible for Prince Eugene to provide medical attendance to the army. The detailed transaction remains unclear.

### 1683 arrest and established financial loss

Oppenheimer’s value to the state did not protect him from prosecution.

In **1683**, a business associate accused him of cheating or causing loss to the Court Chamber. He was arrested. According to the NDB:

- a loss of **21,000 gulden** to the treasury was established;
- Oppenheimer had to reimburse it;
- he also had to extend the treasury a loan on unfavorable terms.

This episode should be classified carefully:

- **Documented fact:** complaint, arrest, established treasury loss, repayment, and compelled or disadvantageous credit.
- **Not established:** that his entire career was fraudulent or that the 21,000-gulden finding proves a systematic criminal enterprise.
- **Context:** mixed public-private accounts, subcontracts, late payment, and opaque assignments made disputes common, but contextual complexity does not erase the recorded finding against him.

### 1688: arrest of Emanuel

In **1688**, Samuel’s son **Emanuel** was arrested to force the firm to accelerate deliveries to imperial forces at Philippsburg.

This was not a conventional adjudication of Emanuel’s personal crime. It was a coercive technique directed at the family enterprise. The event demonstrates the vulnerability of even highly privileged court agents: access to the ruler could coexist with detention without ordinary commercial due process.

### Special jurisdiction

Private creditors and litigants also pursued Oppenheimer. Leopold removed him from ordinary Vienna municipal jurisdiction and placed him under the **Obersthofmarschallamt**, the court marshal’s judicial authority.

This privilege served multiple purposes:

- shielding a strategically important supplier from disruptive local litigation;
- preserving direct court control over him;
- separating him from a municipal population often hostile to Jewish privilege;
- ensuring that disputes affecting imperial finance were handled as court business.

It was protection, but also a form of dependence.

### 1680s–1690s: imperial military-financial network

Oppenheimer’s greatest expansion coincided with Habsburg warfare against the Ottoman Empire and France.

His operations included:

- acquiring grain from Bohemian and other estate owners;
- maintaining magazines in Hungary;
- supplying bread grain and horse fodder;
- financing transport;
- issuing or accepting bills;
- borrowing from subcontractors;
- advancing funds to commanders;
- delivering goods to the imperial court;
- transferring subsidies to allied rulers.

He obtained capital and supplies from both Jewish and Christian houses. The phrase “court Jew” describes his legal and political position but should not obscure the multi-confessional composition of the markets in which he worked.

### Relationship with Margrave Louis William of Baden

Margrave **Louis William of Baden**, an imperial commander in Hungary, became an important patron and defender.

The *Jewish Encyclopedia* reports that Oppenheimer advanced the margrave **100,000 gulden** for war expenses and that Louis recommended him for appointment as Oberfaktor. During the later murder-conspiracy affair, Louis reportedly defended Oppenheimer’s character and called him an honest man.

### Relationship with Prince Eugene of Savoy

Oppenheimer financed and supplied campaigns associated with **Prince Eugene of Savoy**, especially in the Ottoman and Spanish-successor wars.

Prince Eugene reportedly obtained Hebrew manuscripts in Ottoman territories for Oppenheimer. These entered Oppenheimer’s collection and later his nephew David’s celebrated library. The surviving Oppenheim collection is now at the Bodleian Library, although it is inaccurate to describe every item in it as acquired by Samuel.

### Samson Wertheimer: associate and potential rival

**Samson Wertheimer** came to Vienna through Oppenheimer’s circle, probably in the late 1670s or early 1680s. Sources differ on whether the move occurred in 1677 or 1684.

Wertheimer became:

- a business associate or silent partner;
- a financier in his own right;
- a collaborator in imperial war supply;
- a rival for court access;
- after Samuel’s death, the leading imperial court financier.

The relationship therefore combined kinship, mentorship, partnership, competition, and succession.

### 1692 onward: conflict with Leopold Kollonitsch

**Leopold Karl von Kollonitsch**, cardinal, Archbishop of Esztergom, and former president of the Hungarian and Court Chambers, became an influential opponent.

Modern sources describe Kollonitsch as hostile to Jews and eager to reduce or destroy Oppenheimer’s position. The exact mixture of personal, fiscal, religious, and factional motives remains difficult to disaggregate.

It would be too simple to interpret every investigation of Oppenheimer as fabricated by Kollonitsch: the 1683 treasury loss, for example, was recorded as proven. In the 1697 conspiracy affair, however, Kollonitsch appears to have promoted an accusation whose evidence collapsed.

### 1696–1698: alleged plot to murder Samson Wertheimer

#### The accusation

In 1697 an adventurer or officer called **Peters**, **Nikolaus Peter von Edelack**, or Colonel Edelack claimed that:

- Samuel Oppenheimer;
- Samuel’s relative and associate **Ruben Elias Gomperz** of Wesel;

had hired him to kill **Samson Wertheimer**, allegedly because Wertheimer had become an inconvenient competitor.

In Vienna, the story expanded to include an alleged threat against **Emperor Leopold I** himself.

#### Arrests

- Gomperz was arrested on **11 August 1697** by order of the elector of Brandenburg in his capacity as ruler of Cleves.
- Samuel and Emanuel Oppenheimer were arrested shortly after the imperial victory at Zenta in September 1697.
- The arrests damaged confidence throughout their credit network.

The memoirs of **Glückel of Hameln**, whose son Nathan was financially exposed to Oppenheimer paper, describe the immediate disappearance of credit and fear of cascading insolvency.

#### Release

At the beginning of October, the imperial government agreed to release Samuel and Emanuel in return for a new credit of **500,000 gulden**.

Accounts differ over whether Oppenheimer was imprisoned for approximately one month or substantially longer; the NDB’s compressed entry says he was released after nine months, while Peter Rauscher’s detailed reconstruction places arrest and release within September–early October 1697. The discrepancy may reflect different definitions, repeated custody, or an error in one synthesis. The precise total duration is therefore **unresolved**.

Oppenheimer rapidly paid obligations and restored his credit. Glückel reported relief that the Oppenheimers honored their investments.

#### Outcome and evidentiary status

The accusation was not established in court. Contemporary and later historical accounts describe it as baseless or as an intrigue.

Classification:

- **Documented fact:** Edelack made the accusation; Gomperz and the Oppenheimers were arrested; the affair involved Berlin and Vienna; a 500,000-gulden credit accompanied the Oppenheimers’ release.
- **Allegation:** Oppenheimer ordered Wertheimer’s murder.
- **Unsubstantiated escalation:** a plot against Leopold I.
- **Probable historical interpretation:** Kollonitsch amplified the case to weaken Oppenheimer.
- **No conviction:** Samuel was not judicially found guilty of attempted murder or conspiracy.

Gomperz’s later arrest on accusations including coin fraud concerned Gomperz and does not prove Samuel’s guilt in the earlier affair.

### 1696–1698: cemetery and hospital foundations

In **1696**, Oppenheimer acquired or assumed responsibility for the Jewish cemetery in the Rossau/Seegasse. He repaired it and replaced a wooden enclosure damaged during the Ottoman siege with a stone wall.

In **1698**, he founded a Jewish hospital or charitable care house near the cemetery. The institution survived in altered form for centuries. Its later building carried a dedication to suffering humanity.

These undertakings had practical and political significance:

- they restored communal institutions after the 1670 expulsion;
- they gave protected Jewish residents burial and medical infrastructure;
- they converted individual court privilege into limited communal continuity.

### 1697: Polish royal election and Saxon subsidies

The NDB reports that Oppenheimer transferred nearly **half a million gulden** in subsidy money to the elector of Saxony, assisting his election as king of Poland.

This refers to **Frederick Augustus I of Saxony**, elected **Augustus II of Poland** in 1697. Oppenheimer’s role was financial and diplomatic rather than ideological. The transaction illustrates his involvement in European high politics through payment logistics.

The precise origin of the subsidy, its contractual form, and whether Oppenheimer independently decided any political element are not fully described in the accessible sources.

### 1699: Peace of Karlowitz and mounting strain

The costs surrounding the **Peace of Karlowitz** in 1699, continued Hungarian commitments, and preparation for a new western war strained Oppenheimer’s system.

His firm’s financial health cannot be reduced to a single balance sheet:

- the monarchy owed him large sums;
- he owed suppliers and lenders;
- he held assignments on future revenues;
- claims were sometimes disputed;
- state payment delays weakened his liquidity;
- his wealth included property, goods, claims, and books rather than cash alone.

### 1700: suppression of *Entdecktes Judenthum*

Johann Andreas Eisenmenger produced *Entdecktes Judenthum oder Gründlicher und Wahrhaffter Bericht…*, a vast anti-Jewish polemic assembled from Jewish texts and hostile commentary.

In May 1700, the Frankfurt Jewish community warned the imperial court factor **Samson Wertheimer** about the impending publication. Wertheimer and Oppenheimer intervened at Leopold’s court.

On **21 July 1700**, Leopold ordered that further printing or circulation be stopped pending expert examination. Frankfurt nevertheless permitted printing under the condition that approximately 2,000 copies be deposited and not released.

Older accounts say Oppenheimer spent large sums to win the court and Jesuits to the Jewish side. The expenditure is plausible but no verified total was found.

Classification:

- **Documented fact:** Oppenheimer and Wertheimer intervened; Leopold prohibited dissemination; the initial edition was impounded.
- **Reported motive:** fear that the book would provoke persecution or violence.
- **Hostile contemporary interpretation:** wealthy Jews purchased censorship.
- **Historical context:** Eisenmenger’s work advocated severe restrictions on Jews and later became an important reservoir for antisemitic propaganda.
- **Later outcome:** a Prussian-backed edition appeared in 1711, after Oppenheimer’s death; the Frankfurt copies were eventually released decades later.

Oppenheimer did not author a printed rebuttal known to this research.

### 22 July 1700: the Judenkrawall and plunder of his house

#### Immediate events

On **22 July 1700**, a confrontation outside Oppenheimer’s Vienna residence escalated into a major anti-Jewish riot.

A later chronicle says:

1. Two chimney sweeps played a game opposite the house.
2. A Jew from Oppenheimer’s household laughed at or quarreled with one of them.
3. A chimney sweep deliberately made a noise or provocation disliked by the household.
4. Oppenheimer’s security watch intervened and struck a resisting Christian.
5. A crowd gathered.
6. Eggs and then stones were thrown.
7. The house was forced open and plundered.

The account was written about four decades later and must be treated cautiously. Other reconstructions emphasize deliberate provocation by chimney sweeps and reinforcements from a nearby tavern.

#### Plunder

The crowd:

- broke into Oppenheimer’s house;
- smashed strongboxes;
- stole or scattered money, gold, and silver;
- destroyed furnishings;
- emptied wine barrels;
- tore up correspondence, account books, contracts, and commercial records;
- forced Jewish inhabitants to shelter in locked vaults or cellars.

A city guard nearby reportedly failed to intervene promptly. Imperial orders eventually brought armed force. Gunfire was used, and cannon loaded with grapeshot were positioned to deter renewed disorder.

#### Executions and repression

Two men—a chimney sweep and a metalworker or sword-polisher—were summarily arrested and hanged at the iron grilles of Oppenheimer’s house. Some accounts say one principal looter was hanged and others imprisoned; the detailed narrative reports two exemplary executions.

Church authorities instructed residents to return stolen material, and some property was recovered.

The damage was estimated at approximately or more than **100,000 gulden**.

#### Responsibility and theories

- **Documented fact:** the riot, plunder, documentary destruction, armed suppression, executions, and large losses.
- **Reported fact:** nearby guards initially watched without intervening.
- **Disputed interpretation:** whether anti-Jewish hostility, resentment of Oppenheimer’s privilege, anger among impoverished veterans, craft conflict, or court factionalism was the principal cause.
- **Allegation or theory:** factions at court allowed or fomented the riot to pressure Oppenheimer into reducing state claims or extending further credit.
- **Evidence against treating the theory as fact:** no directive from Leopold or another identified court official organizing the riot has been produced in the sources consulted; the emperor ultimately ordered suppression.
- **Historical consequence:** destruction of business records later made it harder for the heirs to prove claims against the state.

The coincidence that the anti-Eisenmenger decree was dated 21 July and the riot erupted on 22 July is notable. No source consulted proves a causal connection.

### 1701: Oberkriegsfaktor and the War of the Spanish Succession

In **1701**, Oppenheimer was appointed **Oberkriegsfaktor**, chief or senior imperial war factor.

With Austria’s treasury depleted at the beginning of the War of the Spanish Succession, Samuel, two sons, and major German commercial houses undertook the commissariat of imperial armies in:

- Lombardy;
- the Upper Rhine;
- Tyrol;
- Bavaria;
- Hungary.

The system required advances on a scale that increasingly exceeded the liquidity of any individual house. The Crown’s military survival and Oppenheimer’s private credit became mutually dependent.

### Rákóczi uprising

The NDB associates the mounting pressure on Oppenheimer with the costs of suppressing the **Rákóczi uprising** in Hungary. Because Francis II Rákóczi’s full-scale uprising began in 1703, at the end of Oppenheimer’s life, this should be understood as preparation and early financing rather than years of personal management by Samuel.

### 3 May 1703: death

Samuel Oppenheimer died in Vienna on **3 May 1703**, aged seventy-two if the 1630 birth date is correct.

No reliable source consulted gives:

- a diagnosed terminal illness;
- an accident;
- suicide;
- poisoning;
- homicide;
- addiction;
- a documented psychological crisis.

The cause of death is therefore **not established**.

He was buried in the Jewish cemetery in the Rossau, now Seegasse 9.

## Companies, Institutions, Technologies, Projects, and Financial Interests

### The Oppenheimer house

The “Oppenheimer firm” was a private merchant-finance organization rather than a modern incorporated bank.

Its principal components were:

- Samuel and his sons;
- relatives by marriage;
- clerks and household personnel;
- regional suppliers;
- other court factors;
- Christian and Jewish commercial houses;
- estate owners selling grain;
- boatmen and overland carriers;
- creditors who accepted Oppenheimer bills or assignments.

The enterprise’s main asset was confidence in Samuel’s personal capacity to secure imperial recognition and eventual payment.

### Military procurement

Known commodities and services included:

- grain and flour;
- army bread;
- oats, hay, and straw;
- horses and remounts;
- military equipment;
- river and overland transport;
- medical provisioning;
- advances to commanders;
- maintenance of military magazines.

### Danube transport

The recorded fleet of eight boats and fifty-two men carried supplies, especially grain and fodder, toward Hungarian theaters.

No evidence indicates that Oppenheimer invented a new vessel, weapon, financial instrument, or patented technology. His significance lay in organizational scale and the integration of credit with logistics.

### Court supply

Oppenheimer also supplied:

- luxury goods;
- ordinary court necessities;
- timber for Kaiserebersdorf palace;
- fodder to the archducal supply office.

### Banking and exchange

His financial services included:

- direct loans;
- merchandise credit;
- bills of exchange;
- subsidy transfers;
- advances secured on future tax receipts;
- intermediation between government and other lenders;
- guarantees and assignments.

### Real property

Oppenheimer received exceptional permission to acquire or possess Vienna property. A documented address associated with his operation is **Freisingerstraße 6**, old number 577, containing offices and commercial rooms.

He also controlled or owned the Seegasse cemetery property from 1696.

A claim that he possessed a vast portfolio of palaces and gardens may confuse Samuel’s property with the later holdings of Samson Wertheimer. Only specifically documented properties should be attributed to Samuel.

### Art and manuscript collection

Oppenheimer owned a significant Hebrew manuscript and art collection. Prince Eugene reportedly brought valuable manuscripts from Ottoman territory.

After Samuel’s death, material entered the library of his nephew **David Oppenheim**. David’s collection eventually comprised thousands of manuscripts and printed books. The Bodleian acquired it in the nineteenth century.

The surviving Oppenheim collection therefore has several layers:

1. manuscripts and books personally acquired by Samuel;
2. David’s own acquisitions;
3. subsequent family custody;
4. the Bodleian acquisition.

The frequently repeated phrase that Samuel’s manuscripts were the “nucleus” of David’s library is plausible but should not be understood as meaning that Samuel assembled the entire Bodleian collection.

### Charitable institutions

- Restoration and protection of the Rossau cemetery, 1696.
- Stone enclosure for the cemetery.
- Jewish hospital or care institution at Seegasse, founded 1698.
- Assistance with renewed Jewish residence in Vienna.
- Support for Jewish books, scholarship, and communal activity is reported, although the exact disbursements are incompletely reconstructed.

### Banco del Giro

After Oppenheimer’s death, the government founded the **Banco del Giro** in 1703 as Austria’s first giro bank and as a response to the breakdown of his private credit system.

It received a nominal annual endowment of approximately four million gulden but was rapidly burdened with Oppenheimer assignments and other claims. Contemporary criticism held that the new institution lacked adequate funding.

Oppenheimer did not found or manage the bank; it was a posthumous state response to the crisis his death exposed.

## Wealth, Debts, and the Posthumous Insolvency

### Conflicting estimates

Sources provide several incompatible totals:

- approximately **4.5 million gulden** as his fortune in one later reconstruction;
- approximately **800,000 gulden** in net or immediately identifiable estate property, plus claims;
- approximately **five million gulden** owed by the state;
- approximately **six million gulden** in state debt to him;
- approximately **5.6 million gulden** in Oppenheimer assignments burdening the Girobank;
- some later proceedings claimed that the state was ultimately a creditor rather than debtor.

These are not necessarily direct contradictions. “Fortune,” gross assets, state receivables, outstanding assignments, and net estate were different accounting categories.

### Structural condition at death

At Samuel’s death:

- the monarchy owed or was alleged to owe enormous sums;
- the firm owed foreign and domestic suppliers and lenders;
- many claims rested on assignments rather than immediate cash;
- creditors depended on Samuel’s personal reputation;
- records destroyed in 1700 impaired proof;
- war needs continued without interruption.

His death therefore triggered both an estate insolvency and a public-credit emergency.

### Bankruptcy proceedings

The government placed the estate in bankruptcy or formal insolvency administration.

Supportive accounts say this was a “masterstroke” by which the state avoided paying its greatest creditor. The Austrian National Library’s historical account similarly states that the imperial house disposed of the debt by imposing bankruptcy on the estate and that this caused a crisis at the Frankfurt exchange.

The modern NDB is more cautious:

- the estate went into bankruptcy because of high receivables and obligations;
- partial repayment to Emanuel continued for years;
- missing written evidence impeded the heirs.

A later account says that after twenty years of litigation officials concluded that Oppenheimer’s excessive interest or “usury” had made the state his creditor.

Classification:

- **Documented fact:** bankruptcy proceedings, disputed imperial accounts, creditor distress, partial repayments, and prolonged litigation.
- **Documented fact:** the state initially recognized or assigned millions in claims connected with Oppenheimer.
- **Disputed interpretation:** whether the government deliberately engineered bankruptcy to repudiate valid debts.
- **Contested financial finding:** later government accounting that the state was net creditor because of overcharging or excessive interest.
- **Unresolved:** a definitive modern reconstruction of the firm’s net balance at death.

The surviving evidence makes both pure hagiography and pure fraud narrative inadequate. Oppenheimer was simultaneously a major imperial creditor, a leveraged intermediary indebted to others, a participant in disputed accounting, and a person against whom at least one treasury loss had been proved.

## Political Activity

Oppenheimer held no representative political office and left no known political manifesto.

His politics operated through court service:

- financing Leopold I;
- supplying imperial armies;
- transferring subsidies to Saxony;
- conducting delicate diplomatic or financial missions;
- negotiating through military commanders and court councils;
- using access to protect Jewish residence and institutions;
- intervening against Eisenmenger’s publication.

His position was not based on equal citizenship. It rested on personal imperial privilege and therefore did not automatically extend to all Jews.

He functioned within Habsburg absolutism rather than advocating a documented constitutional or ideological program.

## Religious, Intellectual, and Cultural Development

### Religious affiliation

Oppenheimer remained publicly Jewish and maintained a Jewish household and network. No conversion or crypto-conversion is documented.

His religious significance is demonstrated more through patronage and communal infrastructure than through surviving theology.

### Communal role

His privilege allowed Jewish dependants to reside in Vienna. Twenty years after his death, one estimate held that more than one hundred people remained there under rights derived from the Oppenheimer privilege.

The title **Judenkaiser** expressed the breadth of this quasi-political position. It may have conveyed respect, dependence, resentment, or all three.

### Books and scholarship

Oppenheimer:

- collected Hebrew manuscripts;
- supported his learned nephew David;
- helped preserve or enlarge Jewish book culture;
- acted with Wertheimer to suppress an anti-Jewish polemic;
- is named in connection with sponsorship of rabbinical publications.

There is no evidence that Samuel himself was a rabbi, philosopher, theologian, or author of a systematic work.

### Sabbatianism and other movements

No reliable source consulted documents a personal relationship with **Sabbatai Zevi**, a commitment to Sabbatianism, or an explicit position in contemporary messianic disputes.

Some accounts associate Oppenheimer with assistance to **Judah he-Hasid’s** group traveling toward the Land of Israel around 1700. The exact financial role requires further archival verification and is best treated as a reported act of communal patronage.

## Important Relationships and Networks

### Emperor Leopold I

Nature: patron, debtor, employer, protector, and coercive sovereign.

Leopold granted Oppenheimer residence and jurisdictional privilege and relied on his credit. The same government arrested him, required disadvantageous loans, and later subjected his estate to bankruptcy proceedings.

### Margrave Louis William of Baden

Nature: military client, patron, and defender.

Oppenheimer reportedly advanced him 100,000 gulden. Louis recommended or supported Oppenheimer’s court status and defended him during the murder accusation.

### Prince Eugene of Savoy

Nature: military commander, beneficiary of supply and credit, cultural intermediary.

Oppenheimer supported campaigns associated with Eugene. Eugene reportedly obtained Hebrew manuscripts and helped arrange medical provisioning through Oppenheimer’s resources.

### Samson Wertheimer

Nature: relative or quasi-relative, protégé, partner, collaborator, competitor, and successor.

They jointly financed war requirements and intervened against *Entdecktes Judenthum*. Wertheimer’s growing independence may have supplied the alleged motive in the unproven murder plot. He succeeded Samuel as the leading imperial financier.

### Leopold Kollonitsch

Nature: court and fiscal opponent.

Kollonitsch promoted the murder-conspiracy affair and was generally hostile to Jewish influence. Claims that every action against Oppenheimer originated with him are not established.

### Emanuel Oppenheimer

Nature: son, operational partner, fellow prisoner, and heir.

Emanuel worked in the firm, was used as a hostage for military performance, was arrested with Samuel, and pursued imperial claims after 1703.

### Ruben Elias Gomperz

Nature: relative and business associate.

Gomperz was accused of serving as intermediary in the alleged Wertheimer murder plot. The accusation was never proved. His detention showed how the Oppenheimer network’s affairs crossed political jurisdictions.

### David Oppenheim

Nature: nephew, scholarly beneficiary, heir to part of the collection and wealth.

David’s library preserved Samuel’s cultural legacy and eventually entered the Bodleian.

### Glückel of Hameln and Nathan

Nature: indirect creditor-network connection.

Glückel’s memoir records how Samuel’s 1697 arrest threatened her son Nathan’s credit and how the family’s payment restored confidence. This is among the most vivid contemporary testimonies to the systemic reach of the Oppenheimer house.

### Jewish communities of Frankfurt and Vienna

Nature: communal patrons, clients, petitioners, and beneficiaries.

Frankfurt’s Jewish community sought the Vienna court factors’ help against Eisenmenger. Vienna’s emerging protected Jewish population depended on privileges and institutions associated with Oppenheimer.

## Controversies, Allegations, Investigations, Crimes, and Financial Questions

### Proven treasury loss, 1683

**Status: documented adverse finding.**

Oppenheimer had to repay 21,000 gulden after a complaint that he had disadvantaged the Court Chamber. This is the clearest recorded financial finding against him.

### Coercive credit following arrest

**Status: documented event; interpretation disputed.**

His release from arrest was repeatedly connected with new loans to the government. This can be read as negotiated settlement, coercive extraction, or both.

### Attempted-murder accusation, 1697

**Status: allegation; no conviction; generally regarded as unsubstantiated.**

The alleged targets were Samson Wertheimer and, in an expanded version, Leopold I. Edelack was the accuser; Kollonitsch promoted the case. Oppenheimer was released after extending a 500,000-gulden credit.

### Excessive charges or “usury”

**Status: recurrent accusation; partly reflected in later state accounting; ultimate balance unresolved.**

The government and hostile writers charged that Oppenheimer overcharged, extracted high interest, or exploited fiscal desperation. Later proceedings reportedly concluded that the state was a creditor. Other records show the state recognizing millions in obligations to him.

Without a modern account-level reconstruction, neither blanket exoneration nor blanket conviction is justified.

### 1700 riot as a court conspiracy

**Status: theory, not documented fact.**

The theory holds that court factions permitted or arranged the plunder to destroy records and reduce claims. Evidence supporting suspicion includes:

- guards’ initial inaction;
- court hostility;
- the government’s dependence on and indebtedness to Oppenheimer;
- the later benefit created by missing account books.

Evidence against treating it as established includes:

- no identified order organizing the riot;
- immediate imperial suppression;
- executions of alleged rioters;
- plausible spontaneous causes involving anti-Jewish hostility and street conflict.

### Estate bankruptcy as deliberate state fraud

**Status: disputed interpretation.**

The state clearly benefited from controlling the estate proceedings and resisted claims for years. Whether the bankruptcy was principally a legitimate response to an insolvent leveraged firm or a device to evade imperial debts remains contested.

### Criminal activity

No evidence located establishes that Samuel was convicted of:

- murder or attempted murder;
- treason;
- counterfeiting;
- money laundering, a modern legal category inapplicable without specific conduct;
- political violence;
- bribery;
- organized criminal conspiracy.

The 1683 treasury loss remains the principal substantiated adverse finding.

## Myths, Legends, Rumors, Propaganda, and Disputed Narratives

### “Savior from the Turks”

**Story:** Oppenheimer was the indispensable “savior from Turkish distress,” allegedly praised in those terms by Prince Eugene.

**Appearance and circulation:** biographical and Austrian heritage literature.

**Evidence:** his material role in supplying the relief of Vienna and the Hungarian campaigns is documented. The exact quotation has not been traced here to Eugene’s correspondence.

**Assessment:** factual core, rhetorically amplified title.

**Reputational effect:** made him a Jewish patriot-benefactor within Austrian memory.

### “Fugger of his age”

**Story:** Oppenheimer was the seventeenth-century equivalent of the Fugger dynasty.

**Evidence:** he controlled an extensive finance and supply network and became the monarchy’s greatest creditor.

**Against literal use:** his house was highly leveraged, dependent on one sovereign client, and collapsed after his death; it lacked the durable multigenerational institutional structure of the Fuggers.

**Effect:** conveys scale while obscuring fragility.

### “Judenkaiser”

**Story:** contemporaries called him the “Jewish emperor.”

**Evidence:** widely reported in historical literature.

**Meaning:** probably referred to his patronage, protected household, authority, and exceptional court access, not a formal Jewish office.

**Effect:** supported both hagiography and hostile portrayals of excessive Jewish power.

### Court-engineered riot

**Story:** the imperial court secretly instigated the 1700 riot to frighten Oppenheimer, destroy evidence, or force cancellation of debts.

**Where circulated:** popular histories and later summaries.

**Evidence for:** court indebtedness, factional hostility, guard inaction, destruction of records.

**Evidence against:** absence of a documentary command; imperial armed intervention; punishment of rioters.

**Status:** unresolved theory, not established fact.

### Complete confiscation of a 4.5-million-gulden fortune

**Story:** after Samuel’s death, Leopold simply confiscated his entire fortune.

**Evidence:** the government did impose insolvency proceedings and resisted or reduced claims.

**Against literal version:** David and other heirs received property or wealth; Emanuel obtained partial repayment; the estate also had large liabilities.

**Status:** exaggerated simplification of a real state-controlled bankruptcy conflict.

### Murder plot against Wertheimer

**Story:** Samuel tried to have his rival Samson Wertheimer killed.

**Circulators:** Edelack, court opponents, and officials who expanded the accusation.

**Evidence for:** accusation and arrests.

**Evidence against:** failure of proof; release; contemporary defenders; later scholarship’s description of the charge as baseless or politically manipulated.

**Status:** historical allegation, not established crime.

### Murder plot against Leopold I

**Story:** Oppenheimer’s conspiracy extended to assassination of the emperor.

**Evidence:** appears as an expansion of Edelack’s accusation in Vienna.

**Assessment:** still less supported than the Wertheimer allegation.

**Status:** unverified accusation or hostile mythology.

### Founder of modern Austrian banking

**Story:** Oppenheimer personally founded Austria’s first bank.

**Evidence against:** the Banco del Giro was founded by the government after his death.

**Factual core:** its creation responded directly to the crisis left by his firm’s collapse.

### Direct ancestor of J. Robert Oppenheimer

**Story:** Samuel was the direct ancestor of the Manhattan Project physicist.

**Circulation:** unsourced online genealogy and surname-based speculation.

**Evidence:** none established in the authoritative sources consulted.

**Status:** unverified genealogical claim.

### Confusion with Joseph Süß Oppenheimer

Samuel’s life is sometimes merged with the later “Jud Süß” story.

Differences:

- Samuel served Leopold I in Vienna and died in 1703.
- Joseph Süß served Duke Karl Alexander of Württemberg and was executed in 1738.
- Samuel was never executed.
- The novels and films titled *Jud Süß* concern Joseph, not Samuel.

The confusion magnifies hostile myths by transferring Joseph Süß’s trial, sexual accusations, execution, and twentieth-century propaganda to Samuel.

## Works by Samuel Oppenheimer

No book, treatise, manifesto, literary work, scientific paper, patent, composition, or artwork authored by Samuel Oppenheimer has been identified.

His documentary output consisted primarily of business and administrative material.

### Surviving or historically attested documentary corpus

1. **Contracts with the imperial Court Chamber**  
   Original-language administrative records in German, including contracts for army supplies and oats for Hungary. These are archival documents, not published literary works.

2. **Bills of exchange, assignments, receipts, and accounts**  
   Created throughout his business career. A large portion of the Vienna business archive was destroyed in the 1700 riot.

3. **Petitions and memorials to Leopold I and imperial councils**  
   Authorship may be institutional or scribal rather than personally autograph. One reported petition reminded the emperor how many provinces Oppenheimer had helped reconquer.

4. **Correspondence with suppliers, relatives, commanders, courts, and creditors**  
   Surviving examples are dispersed through Austrian and German archives. No complete critical edition was identified.

5. **Last will and testament**  
   Reported and partially quoted in older Vienna histories. In it he advised his children to continue serving the emperor and imperial councillors honestly, faithfully, and diligently. The text should be treated as a dictated or notarized legal document unless an autograph is established.

6. **Privileges issued to or concerning Oppenheimer**  
   These were not authored by him but are central documentary evidence for residence, protection, jurisdiction, property, and household rights.

7. **Book ownership and provenance marks**  
   Manuscripts once belonging to Samuel became part of David Oppenheim’s collection. Ownership does not constitute authorship.

### Misattribution warning

Works written by Rabbi **David Oppenheim**, including *Mo‘ed David* and numerous manuscripts, must not be attributed to Samuel. Likewise, twentieth-century scientific and historical works by other Samuel Oppenheims are unrelated.

## Death, Burial, and Fate of the Grave

Oppenheimer died on 3 May 1703 in Vienna. His wife Sandela survived him by approximately two years.

He was buried in the Jewish cemetery at Rossau/Seegasse. His tombstone carried an elaborate Hebrew inscription documented by Bernhard Wachstein before the Second World War.

During the Nazi period, members of Vienna’s Jewish community moved or buried many stones to protect them. About 250–280 stones were later recovered from the Central Cemetery and returned. Other fragments remained buried at Seegasse.

Excavations beginning in 2008 recovered substantial portions of Samuel Oppenheimer’s monument, which were restored to the cemetery. The survival and reconstruction of the stone transformed his grave into one of the principal physical memorials of early modern Jewish Vienna.

## Posthumous Reputation and Legacy

### Immediate financial legacy

His death revealed how completely Austrian war finance depended on private personal credit. The government created the Banco del Giro, imposed moratoria and special proceedings, and tried to prevent a chain of executions against interdependent creditors.

The crisis reached beyond Vienna into German commercial centers, including Frankfurt.

### Family legacy

His children continued parts of the business and pursued claims. Emanuel spent years negotiating with the state. Samson Wertheimer assumed the dominant position in imperial finance.

Through David Oppenheim, part of Samuel’s manuscript collection entered one of the most important surviving collections of Hebrew books and manuscripts.

Through Fromet Guggenheim, Samuel entered the genealogy of Moses Mendelssohn’s family.

### Vienna Jewish legacy

His protected household and dependants helped restore a Jewish presence after the 1670 expulsion. His cemetery work and hospital created institutional continuity before a fully recognized modern congregation re-emerged.

### Historiographical interpretations

Oppenheimer has appeared as:

- the indispensable financier of Habsburg victory;
- a prototype of the “court Jew”;
- an exploited creditor of an ungrateful monarchy;
- a highly profitable war entrepreneur;
- a communal benefactor;
- an emblem of supposedly excessive Jewish financial influence;
- a case study in the fragility of personal credit;
- a precursor, in some narratives, to Jewish emancipation and modern banking.

The first three roles often dominate Jewish and Austrian commemorative writing. Hostile economic and antisemitic literature emphasizes alleged usury, manipulation, and secret power. Modern scholarship focuses more closely on reciprocal dependency: the state possessed coercive and judicial power, while Oppenheimer possessed commercial information, contacts, and credit the state could not easily replace.

### Antisemitic afterlives

The “court Jew” became a recurring antisemitic archetype. Later propaganda merged distinct people and episodes, particularly Samuel and Joseph Süß Oppenheimer.

Nazi-era writing about court Jews drew on older stories of hidden financial control. Samuel’s genuine political access and large state claims supplied factual fragments that could be detached from the coercive fiscal system and reorganized into conspiracy mythology.

### Material legacy

The surviving Seegasse cemetery, restored tombstone, hospital records, portraits, Court Chamber contracts, and Bodleian manuscripts are the principal material traces of his life.
