## Works by Micaela Bastidas Puyucahua

### Dictated correspondence

- **23 November 1780–23 March 1781:** *Nineteen letters to José Gabriel Condorcanqui Túpac Amaru*. An epistolary sequence of military intelligence, logistical instructions, strategic argument and intimate address, preserved within the documentary corpus of the rebellion. Bastidas could sign but did not write the letters herself: she dictated them to unidentified amanuenses, so these are her texts without being autograph manuscripts; several survive without an exact day, and their familiar modern description as “cartas de amor y guerra” is editorial rather than an original collective title. ([Guardia, pp. 21–25](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf); [Nueva Colección Documental, pp. 156–162, 177–178, 314–315, 632–633, 729–730, 800–801](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))
- **2 December 1780:** *Letter to Juan de Zubizarreta*. An administrative command-letter concerning delivery of copper, transport animals, payment and the attachment of a corregidor’s property; although cast as correspondence, it operates as an executive protocol issued during her husband’s absence. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 178](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))
- **7 December 1780:** *Letter to Agustín and Lucas Núñez de la Torre and Matías Canal*. A mobilization order warning the governors to have their forces ready for the projected descent upon Cuzco; it is simultaneously a letter and a coercive manifesto because it announces consequences for disobedience. ([Guardia, pp. 19–20](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf); [Zasqua/CDIP document unit, pp. 329–333](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02210/))
- **15 December 1780:** *Letter to Baltasar Cárdenas, Tomás Enríquez and Mariano Flores*. An appeal and command to support the insurgency’s campaign against the corregidores; the document’s received text belongs to Bastidas’s governing correspondence rather than to private letters. ([Guardia, p. 20](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf); [Zasqua/CDIP document unit, pp. 355–359](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02220/))
- **22 January 1781:** *Letter to Francisco Molina*. A terse summons ordering Molina to come immediately to Tinta for his own safety; its brevity makes it function more as an operational command than as descriptive correspondence. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 314](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf); [Zasqua/CDIP document unit, pp. 458–460](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02254/))
- **7 March 1781:** *Letter accompanying ammunition and supplies to Túpac Amaru*. A logistical dispatch recording the shipment of musket and cannon cartridges, money and coca; it is both a letter and an inventory accompanying material action. ([Nueva Colección Documental, pp. 729–730](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))
- **24 March 1781:** *Letter to Pedro Santisteban*. A protective summons advising Santisteban and another cleric to come to Tinta to escape possible violence; this falls just outside Guardia’s stated date-span for the nineteen letters to Túpac Amaru. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 801](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))

### Passes, commissions and protocols

- **27 November 1780:** *Pass for travellers between Cuzco and their home districts*. A signed salvoconduct or protocol commanding Spanish and Indigenous guards and spies to allow its bearers free passage in both directions. The surviving wording is mediated by a scribe, and no individual bearer is identified in the cited edition. ([Guardia, pp. 19–20](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf))
- **10 December 1780:** *Commission to Diego Berdejo to arrest Francisco Sucacagua*. An executive protocol authorizing Berdejo, local communities and their alcaldes to seize and guard a man accused of opposing Túpac Amaru’s orders. ([Nueva Colección Documental, pp. 202–203](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf); [Guardia, p. 20](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf))
- **23 January 1781:** *Order to the caciques and alcaldes of Marcapata*. A protocol requiring local authorities to provide the customary services and emoluments to the curate José Salazar, under threat of punishment. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 314](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf); [Zasqua/CDIP document unit, pp. 458–460](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02254/))
- **6 February 1781:** *Appointment of Antonio Chávez Mendoza to administer the parish of Sicuani*. An administrative protocol transferring sacramental and church-property responsibilities while Pedro de Landa was employed elsewhere; the text requires a report confirming execution of the order. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 633](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))
- **18 February 1781:** *Order to the alcaldes of Sanca concerning provisions*. A supply protocol directing assistance to Bernardo Carrasco in bringing potatoes, fruit and other food from embargoed estates to the cabildo. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 654](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))
- **18 February 1781:** *Order reserving embargoed livestock for the Inca*. A related but separately formulated protocol placing cattle and other seized livestock exclusively at Túpac Amaru’s disposal; the edition gives no separate date after the preceding order, so 18 February is contextual rather than independently repeated in the document. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 654](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))
- **22 March 1781:** *Commission to Bernardino Zegarra*. A punitive protocol instructing Zegarra to notify Quiquijana’s caciques and community to cease attacks on Spanish residents and their property, with death and confiscation threatened for continued violations. ([Nueva Colección Documental, p. 800](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf); [Zasqua/CDIP document unit, pp. 592–594](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02286/))

### Manifestos and public commands

- **13 December 1780:** *Bando concerning the Catholic faith and clergy*. A publicly operative manifesto commanding reverence for the Catholic faith and respect for priests. It is transmitted among a mixed group of letters and a bando, and its surviving attribution rests on Bastidas’s subscribed name rather than an autograph composition. ([Zasqua/CDIP document unit, pp. 349–353](https://zasqua.org/pe-bn-cdip-02218/); [Guardia, pp. 19–20](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf))
- **15 December 1780:** *Order for recruitment to Tungasuca*. A public command granting local officials broad power to send Indigenous and Spanish inhabitants to Tungasuca, threatening disobedient persons with death and prescribing a cross on the hat as a Christian insignia. It resists a single category: it is at once a recruitment protocol, coercive manifesto and instrument of delegated authority. ([Nueva Colección Documental, pp. 220–221](https://www.acuedi.org/ddata/11738.pdf))

### Insurgent administration as an obra

- **Late November 1780–April 1781:** *Direction of the Tungasuca–Tinta insurgent headquarters*. A political, military and logistical obra rather than a single text: Bastidas organized communications, recruitment, provisioning, money, weapons, intelligence, prisoners and regional expeditions while issuing letters, passes, edicts and orders. Its boundaries and individual acts cannot be reconstructed completely from the surviving papers, and treating every action as a separately authored “work” would manufacture a catalogue the archive does not support. ([Guardia, pp. 18–27](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf); [O’Phelan Godoy, “Micaela Bastidas a partir de los testimonios…”](https://repository.usergioarboleda.edu.co/bitstream/handle/11232/1977/Micaela%20Bastidas%20a%20partir%20de%20los%20testimonios%20vertidos%20en%20el%20juicio%20a%20la%20Gran%20Rebeli%C3%B3n%2C%201780-1781.pdf?sequence=1))

### A text that resists authorship

- **21 April 1781:** *Confession in the criminal proceedings against Micaela Bastidas*. This is a juridical manifestation of her answers, not straightforwardly a work she made: Judge Benito de la Mata Linares controlled the interrogation and a court scribe converted oral testimony into written Spanish. Scholarship accordingly calls it mediated testimony, and it should not be silently counted as an autograph or freely composed text. ([Guardia, pp. 12–13, 68–75](https://cemhal.org/Libro_Micaela_Bastidas.pdf); [O’Phelan Godoy](https://repository.usergioarboleda.edu.co/bitstream/handle/11232/1977/Micaela%20Bastidas%20a%20partir%20de%20los%20testimonios%20vertidos%20en%20el%20juicio%20a%20la%20Gran%20Rebeli%C3%B3n%2C%201780-1781.pdf?sequence=1))
