## Works by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart

### Dramatic works: operas, Singspiele, serenatas, and theatrical music

- **1767:** *Die Schuldigkeit des ersten Gebots*, K. 35. Sacred Singspiel; Mozart composed the first part of a collaborative work whose other parts, by Michael Haydn and Anton Cajetan Adlgasser, are lost.
- **1767:** *Apollo et Hyacinthus*, K. 38. Latin musical drama written for Salzburg University.
- **1768:** *Bastien und Bastienne*, K. 50. German Singspiel.
- **1768:** *La finta semplice*, K. 51. Italian opera buffa.
- **1770:** *Mitridate, re di Ponto*, K. 87. Opera seria composed for Milan.
- **1771:** *Ascanio in Alba*, K. 111. Theatrical serenata composed for a Habsburg wedding in Milan.
- **1771:** *La Betulia liberata*, K. 118. Azione sacra—an oratorio-like dramatic work—whose intended first performance is uncertain.
- **1772:** *Il sogno di Scipione*, K. 126. Dramatic serenata; its original ceremonial destination remains disputed.
- **1772:** *Lucio Silla*, K. 135. Opera seria composed for Milan.
- **1775:** *La finta giardiniera*, K. 196. Italian opera buffa.
- **1775:** *Il re pastore*, K. 208. Dramatic serenata.
- **1779–1780:** *Zaide*, K. 344. Unfinished German Singspiel; Mozart left no overture, final act, or authoritative title, and the name *Zaide* is posthumous.
- **1779:** *Thamos, König in Ägypten*, K. 345. Incidental music assembled and revised over several years for Tobias Philipp von Gebler’s play.
- **1780–1781:** *Idomeneo, re di Creta*, K. 366. Opera seria, with extensive surviving revisions, substitutions, and alternative numbers.
- **1781:** *Der Schauspieldirektor*, K. 486. One-act German comedy with music, written for an imperial entertainment.
- **1781–1782:** *Die Entführung aus dem Serail*, K. 384. German Singspiel.
- **1783–1784:** *L’oca del Cairo*, K. 422. Unfinished opera buffa; substantial portions survive, but the project was abandoned.
- **1783–1784:** *Lo sposo deluso*, K. 430. Unfinished opera buffa surviving as an overture and four vocal numbers.
- **1785–1786:** *Le nozze di Figaro*, K. 492. Opera buffa on a libretto by Lorenzo Da Ponte; Mozart later composed replacement arias for particular performers.
- **1786:** *Der Stein der Weisen*. Collaborative Singspiel to which Mozart contributed at least the duet “Nun liebes Weibchen,” K. 625/592a; the extent of his participation in other numbers remains disputed.
- **1787:** *Don Giovanni*, K. 527. Dramma giocoso; the Prague and Vienna versions constitute distinct authorized manifestations rather than a single invariant score.
- **1789–1790:** *Così fan tutte*, K. 588. Opera buffa on a libretto by Da Ponte.
- **1791:** *La clemenza di Tito*, K. 621. Opera seria composed for the coronation festivities in Prague; the authorship of the secco recitatives is traditionally assigned to Franz Xaver Süssmayr.
- **1791:** *Die Zauberflöte*, K. 620. German Singspiel conceived with Emanuel Schikaneder and incorporating spoken theatre, spectacle, ritual allegory, and music—effectively a theatrical Gesamtkunstwerk.

- **1779:** *Dixit Dominus and Magnificat*, K. 193. Vespers movements whose dating in the catalogues has changed.
- **1779:** *Vesperae solennes de confessore*, K. 339. Vespers setting containing the frequently separated *Laudate Dominum*.
- **1780:** *Kyrie in D minor*, K. 341. Sacred fragment or independent movement; its dating and intended destination remain uncertain.
- **1782–1783:** *Great Mass in C minor*, K. 427. Monumental unfinished Mass; the surviving sources do not authorize a single complete performing version.
- **1785:** *Davidde penitente*, K. 469. Cantata adapting much of the unfinished C-minor Mass with newly composed music and an Italian text.
- **1791:** *Ave verum corpus*, K. 618. Eucharistic motet.
- **1791:** *Requiem in D minor*, K. 626. Unfinished liturgical project left at Mozart’s death; the familiar performing score incorporates completion work principally by Süssmayr, with earlier assistance from Joseph Eybler, and therefore resists treatment as an entirely Mozart-made object.

- **1776:** *Divertimento in F major*, K. 247, “First Lodron Night Music.” Festive chamber work.
- **1777:** *Divertimento in B-flat major*, K. 287, “Second Lodron Night Music.” Festive chamber work.

- **1784:** *Divertimento in E-flat major*, K. 563. String trio; notwithstanding its title, it is an expansive six-movement chamber work.
- **1787:** *Eine kleine Nachtmusik*, K. 525. String serenade; Mozart’s catalogue implies an additional minuet and trio that are lost.

- **1791:** *German Dances*, K. 600, K. 602, K. 605, and K. 611. Separate orchestral dance sets written for Viennese court balls.

### Church sonatas

- **1772–1780:** *Seventeen authenticated Church Sonatas*, K. 67–69, K. 144–145, K. 212, K. 241, K. 244–245, K. 263, K. 274, K. 278, K. 328–329, and K. 336. Short instrumental epistle sonatas made for insertion into Salzburg liturgy; each is an independent liturgical object even though modern editions often collect them as a cycle.
- **Uncertain:** *Church Sonatas attributed as K. 124c, K. 124d, and K. 124e*. Lost or dubiously attributed works recorded by later catalogues; their status as Mozart’s creations cannot be established securely.

### Arrangements, recompositions, and works that resist singular authorship

- **1782–1783:** *Six Preludes and Fugues*, K. 404a. Chamber arrangements after Bach and other composers; portions of the introductory preludes may be Mozart’s, but the collection’s authorship and assembly are not uniform.
- **1782:** *Five Fugues for string quartet*, K. 405. Arrangements of fugues from Bach’s *Well-Tempered Clavier*.
- **1788:** *Adagio and Fugue in C minor*, K. 546. String recomposition of the two-keyboard Fugue K. 426 with a newly composed introduction.
- **1788:** *Handel’s Acis and Galatea*, K. 566. Mozart’s orchestration and adaptation of Handel’s masque, made for Baron van Swieten’s circle.
- **1789:** *Handel’s Messiah*, K. 572. German-language reorchestration; a transformative arrangement, not an original oratorio by Mozart.
- **1790:** *Handel’s Alexander’s Feast*, K. 591. Reorchestrated adaptation.
- **1790:** *Handel’s Ode for St Cecilia’s Day*, K. 592. Reorchestrated adaptation.
- **1791:** *G. F. Handel arrangements and contrapuntal studies*. Individual adaptations and study manuscripts survive whose status ranges from performance arrangement to compositional exercise; they resist identification as autonomous Mozart works.

### Projects, sketches, fragments, and lost works

- **1765–1791:** *Compositional sketches and drafts*. The Köchel catalogue and the *Neue Mozart-Ausgabe* identify numerous independent sketches, abandoned openings, contrapuntal studies, and drafts; they are evidence of projects rather than necessarily intended finished works.
- **1765:** *Third Sketchbook*, K. 32a. Lost collection known from documentary evidence.
- **1766:** *Solos for flute*, K. 33a. Lost works reported in an early catalogue.
- **1766:** *Solos for cello*, K. 33b. Lost works reported in an early catalogue.
- **1766:** *Stabat mater*, K. 33c. Lost and incompletely documented sacred project.
- **1766:** *Keyboard sonatas in G, B-flat, C, and F major*, K. 33d–33g. Lost works; the F-major sonata is additionally doubtful.
- **1766:** *Waldhornstück*, K. 33h. Lost horn piece.
- **1767:** *Six divertimenti*, K. 41a. Lost ensemble works.
- **1767:** *Wind pieces of various instrumentation*, K. 41b. Lost works without secure individual titles.
- **1767:** *Various marches and minuets*, K. 41c–41d. Lost groups rather than recoverable individual scores.
- **1767:** *Fugues*, K. 41e–41f. Lost contrapuntal works.
- **1767:** *Serenade for two violins, cello, and organ*, K. 41g. Lost work.
- **1768:** *Offertory “Benedictus sit Deus”*, K. 47b. Lost sacred work.
- **1768:** *Trumpet Concerto*, K. 47c. Lost concerto known through documentary references.
- **1769:** *Symphonies in D and B-flat major*, K. 66c–66e. Lost symphonies whose documentation and attribution are not equally secure.
- **1781–1791:** *Unfinished fugues and contrapuntal projects*, including K. 153, K. 154, K. 401, K. 443, and K.  Anh. 33. These survive as fragments and should not be silently converted into finished pieces by later completions.
- **1782:** *Suite in C major*, K. 399. Unfinished keyboard project in deliberately archaic Baroque forms.
- **1782:** *Fantasy and Fugue in C major*, K. 394. Contrapuntal keyboard work associated with Mozart’s study of Bach and Handel.
- **1783:** *Fugue in G minor*, K. 401. Unfinished keyboard fugue, later completed by another hand.
- **1784–1787:** *Horn-concerto fragments*, K. 370b, K. 371, K. 412, K. 494a, and related leaves. Surviving materials document several overlapping projects; older editions sometimes combined music that the sources do not prove belongs together.
- **1785:** *Piano Concerto movement in D major*, K. 537a. Fragmentary or rejected concerto material.
- **1789:** *Clarinet Quintet fragment in B-flat major*, K. 516c. Abandoned chamber project.
- **1791:** *Cello Concerto project*. Recorded by later testimony but lost; neither its completion nor precise form can be established.
- **1791:** *The Tempest opera project*. Reported intention to create an opera from Shakespeare; no securely identifiable music survives, so this is a project rather than a made dramatic work.
- **1791:** *Requiem continuation materials*, K. 626. Mozart’s autograph breaks off at several different stages of completion; later completions are manifestations produced by collaborators, editors, and performers rather than recoverable versions of a finished Mozart score.

### Written and documentary things

- **1769–1791:** *Letters*. Hundreds of personal, professional, and family letters survive in the critical edition *Mozart: Briefe und Aufzeichnungen*; they are made texts but not a single literary obra, and many family letters contain additions by more than one writer.
- **1784–1791:** *Verzeichnüß aller meiner Werke*. Mozart’s autograph thematic catalogue of his recent compositions, beginning with K. 449 and ending with K. 623; it is simultaneously a record, an index, and a self-authored representation of his oeuvre.
- **Various dates:** *Annotations, dedications, album leaves, receipts, contracts, petitions, and other documents*. The documentary edition treats these separately from compositions; many are functional inscriptions or jointly produced records and resist classification as autonomous works.
- **Various dates:** *Cadenzas and lead-ins for concertos*. Composed performance materials survive both for Mozart’s concertos and for works by other composers; some are independent manuscript objects, while others function only as components of a particular performance protocol.

### Attributions rejected or unresolved by modern scholarship

- **Various dates:** *Symphonies formerly numbered 2, 3, 11, 23, and 37*. K. 17 is attributed to Leopold Mozart; K. 18 is by Carl Friedrich Abel; K. 84 and other early symphonies remain disputed; K. 444 is principally Michael Haydn’s symphony with a Mozart introduction.
- **Various dates:** *Violin Concertos K. 268 and K. 271i*. Their Mozart attribution is doubtful or rejected; they remain in the catalogue because attribution history is itself part of the record.
- **Various dates:** *Adelaide Concerto*, K. Anh. 294a. A twentieth-century fabrication by Marius Casadesus, not a Mozart work.
- **Various dates:** *Six violin sonatas, K. Anh. 291a*. Spurious works formerly published under Mozart’s name.
- **Various dates:** *Masses and sacred pieces in the Köchel supplement*. Numerous pieces once attributed to Mozart are now assigned to Leopold Mozart, Michael Haydn, Johann Ernst Eberlin, or unidentified composers; the supplement documents reception and misattribution, not things securely made by Wolfgang.
- **Various dates:** *Doubtful keyboard miniatures and teaching pieces*. Several pieces transmitted only in late copies or early printed anthologies remain unresolved; absence of an autograph does not by itself disprove authorship, but their status cannot responsibly be stated as certain.
