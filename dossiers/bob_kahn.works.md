## Works by Robert Elliot Kahn

### Protocols, architectures, and network systems

- **1968–1970:** *ARPANET system design and Interface Message Processor*. A collaborative technological obra: Kahn was responsible for ARPANET’s system design and participated in the BBN team that developed its packet-switching IMP. This is not exclusively his work and should not be reduced to a single authored object. [CHM]
- **1972:** *ARPANET public demonstration*. A large, collaborative technical performance organized principally by Kahn for the International Conference on Computer Communication: forty machines demonstrated a functioning network rather than merely describing one. [NIHF; CHM]
- **Early 1970s:** *Packet Radio Network (PRNET)*. An experimental mobile packet network for which Kahn was a principal architect at DARPA; hardware, protocols, research program, and demonstrations make it closer to a technological project than a single work. [CNRI-Bio; IEEE]
- **Early 1970s:** *Packet Satellite Network (SATNET)*. A DARPA-sponsored packet network developed under Kahn’s programmatic direction to connect American and European research sites. Its distributed authorship resists attribution to Kahn alone. [IEEE; DARPA]
- **1973–1983:** *Internetting project / DARPA Internet Program*. A research project originated by Kahn to interconnect heterogeneous, independently administered packet networks through an open architecture; the project’s eventual manifestation was the Internet, not a bounded object made by one person. [CNRI-Bio; CHM; DARPA]
- **1973–1978:** *Transmission Control Program, subsequently TCP/IP*. A protocol architecture co-invented with Vinton G. Cerf; its original unified transmission-control design was later divided into the Transmission Control Protocol and Internet Protocol. It is both a made technical system and a protocol in the strict sense—a text implemented to make machines act. [DARPA; NIHF]
- **Late 1970s–1980s:** *Packet voice research*. A DARPA research project pursued under Kahn’s direction, contributing to the technological lineage of voice over packet networks; sources do not support treating modern VoIP as his individual invention. [NIHF; IEEE]
- **1983–1985:** *Strategic Computing Program*. A large federal research project initiated by Kahn while directing DARPA’s Information Processing Techniques Office. Its obra was a coordinated program of funded research, not a single machine or publication. [CNRI-Bio]
- **1986–:** *Corporation for National Research Initiatives (CNRI)*. A nonprofit research institution founded by Kahn to develop network-based information infrastructure. As an enduring institution produced with many participants, it is better classified as an organizational obra than as a personally authored work. [CNRI-Bio; NIHF]
- **1988–:** *Knowbot programs and Knowbot Operating Environment*. A conception of mobile software agents co-invented by Kahn and Cerf and subsequently implemented by CNRI collaborators. It is a continuing software-and-infrastructure project whose precise boundary and authorship are collective. [CNRI-Bio; CNRI-Archive]
- **1990s–:** *Digital Object Architecture*. A middleware architecture developed by Kahn for identifying, resolving, accessing, and managing persistent digital objects. Its manifestations include papers, the Handle System, repository software, standards work, and patents; consequently it resembles a technological Gesamtkunstwerk more than one text. [CNRI-Bio; CNRI-Publications]
- **1990s–:** *Handle System*. A persistent-identifier and resolution infrastructure developed at CNRI as part of the Digital Object Architecture. Kahn supplied the originating architecture, but the deployed system is a collaborative CNRI obra and should not be attributed to him alone. [CNRI-Bio; CNRI-Publications]

### Patented inventions

- **Filed 1996; granted 1998:** *Unconstrained Pointing Interface for Natural Human Interaction with a Display-Based Computer System*. A patented human–computer interface co-invented with John H. Cafarella (U.S. Patent 5,793,361). A continuation produced U.S. Patent 6,404,416 in 2002; these are legal manifestations of one invention rather than two independent obras. [Patent-Bibliography]
- **Filed 1997; granted 2000:** *System for Uniquely and Persistently Identifying, Managing, and Tracking Digital Objects*. A patented network method co-invented with David K. Ely (U.S. Patent 6,135,646), and one legal manifestation of the broader Digital Object Architecture. [Patent-Bibliography]
- **Filed 1997; granted 2003:** *System for Distributed Task Execution*. A patented Knowbot system co-invented with Vinton G. Cerf and David K. Ely (U.S. Patent 6,574,628); a later application publication is another manifestation of the same invention. [Patent-Bibliography]
- **Filed 2001–2003; published 2002–2004:** *Identifying, Managing, Accessing, and Tracking Digital Objects and Associated Rights and Payments*. A family of patent applications co-invented with David K. Ely. The multiple publication numbers record related filings and continuations, not securely distinguishable independent works. [Patent-Bibliography]
- **Filed 2003; published 2003:** *Authenticating and Using Digital Objects*. A patent application co-invented with Patrice A. Lyons, describing authentication through information associated with a persistent identifier. [Patent-Bibliography]

### Dissertation and technical papers

- **1964:** *Some Problems in the Sampling and Modulation of Signals*. Kahn’s Princeton doctoral dissertation, a scholarly monograph rather than a commercially published book. [CNRI-Bio; DBLP]
- **1965:** *Sampling Representations and the Optimum Reconstruction of Signals*. A co-authored research article with Bede Liu on signal reconstruction. [DBLP]
- **1965:** *Some Bandwidth Properties of Simultaneous Amplitude and Angle Modulation*. A co-authored research article with John B. Thomas. [DBLP]
- **1969:** *The Following Comments Are in Response to John Kreznar’s Questions Which Were Raised in RFC 17*. An early Network Working Group note, retrospectively catalogued as RFC 17a; its title-like opening is descriptive and its status as an independently intended “work” is doubtful. [DBLP]
- **1970:** *Response to RFC 28*. A one-page Network Working Group communication catalogued as RFC 29. It is an intervention in a working exchange rather than a conventional paper. [DBLP]
- **1970:** *The Interface Message Processor for the ARPA Computer Network*. A collaborative conference paper with Frank E. Heart, Severo M. Ornstein, William R. Crowther, and David C. Walden describing the IMP. [DBLP]
- **1971:** *Host Accounting and Administrative Procedures*. RFC 136, an operational protocol-and-policy note: a text meant to govern conduct on the network. [DBLP]
- **1971–1972:** *Flow Control in a Resource-Sharing Computer Network*. A paper co-authored with William R. Crowther, first presented in conference proceedings and then published in revised journal form in 1972; these are two manifestations of one research work. [DBLP]
- **1972:** *Computer Communication Network Design: Experience with Theory and Practice*. A paper co-authored with Howard Frank and Leonard Kleinrock, appearing both in conference proceedings and in the journal *Networks*. The bibliography records both manifestations, not two separate works. [DBLP]
- **1972:** *Demonstration at International Computer Communications Conference*. RFC 371, a short planning and coordination text for the ARPANET demonstration; it acts as an invitation and operational notice rather than as a descriptive article. [DBLP]
- **1974:** *A Protocol for Packet Network Intercommunication*. A co-authored paper with Vinton G. Cerf that specifies the original Transmission Control Program and articulates open-architecture internetworking. The 2005 *Computer Communication Review* appearance is a reprint, not a new work. [DBLP; CNRI-Archive]
- **1975–1977:** *The Organization of Computer Resources into a Packet Radio Network*. Kahn’s account and design statement for packet radio, published in conference proceedings in 1975 and in revised journal form in 1977. The two publications are manifestations of one work. [DBLP]
- **1985:** *Information Processing Technology for Emergency Management*. A research and policy article on applying information-processing systems to emergencies. [DBLP]
- **1988:** *An Open Architecture for a Digital Library System and a Plan for Its Development*. A draft project document co-authored with Cerf for *The Digital Library Project, Volume I: The World of Knowbots*. Its explicit draft status matters: it sets out an intended system rather than documenting a completed one. [CNRI-Archive]
- **1992:** *Introduction*. An editorial introduction to the first issue of the *Journal of High Speed Networks*; it is a paratext rather than a freestanding research paper. [DBLP]
- **1994:** *The Role of Government in the Evolution of the Internet*. A historical and policy essay. [DBLP]
- **1995; revised publication 2006:** *A Framework for Distributed Digital Object Services*. A technical framework co-authored with Robert Wilensky, first issued by the authors in 1995 and later published in revised journal form. The dates identify manifestations of one evolving specification, not separate works. [CNRI-Archive; DBLP]
- **1997–2009:** *The Past and Future History of the Internet / A Brief History of the Internet*. A collaboratively authored historical account by Barry M. Leiner, Cerf, David D. Clark, Kahn, Leonard Kleinrock, Daniel C. Lynch, Jon Postel, Lawrence G. Roberts, and Stephen Wolff. Its 1997 article, 1999 preprint, and 2009 revised title are successive manifestations of an evolving collective text; the bibliography does not warrant assigning every revision specifically to Kahn. [DBLP]
- **1999:** *What Is the Internet (And What Makes It Work)*. A jointly authored explanatory and architectural paper with Cerf, prepared at the request of the Internet Policy Institute. [CNRI-Archive]
- **2001; revised publication 2006:** *Representing Value as Digital Objects: A Discussion of Transferability and Anonymity*. A paper co-authored with Patrice A. Lyons, first published in *D-Lib Magazine* and later revised for a law journal. These are two manifestations of one work. [CNRI-Archive; DBLP]
- **2003:** *A Brief Overview of the Digital Object Architecture*. A technical overview co-authored with Jay Allen Sears. CNRI points readers to a later updated overview, so the 2003 text should be treated as an earlier manifestation rather than a definitive specification. [CNRI-Archive]
- **2003:** *Introduction*. A short co-authored framing text with Cerf for the collection *Managing IP Networks*; it is paratext, not a separate technical system. [DBLP]
- **2010:** *The Long Quest for Universal Information Access*. A co-authored essay with Peter J. Denning on information infrastructure and access. [CNRI-Publications; DBLP]
- **2010:** *The Architectural Evolution of the Internet*. A technical-historical essay issued by CNRI. [CNRI-Publications]
- **2011:** *The Role of Architecture in Internet Defense*. A policy chapter connecting network architecture with cybersecurity. [CNRI-Publications]
- **2012:** *Information, Data, Security in a Networked Future*. A Turing-centenary conference contribution credited jointly to Cerf, John E. Hopcroft, Kahn, Ronald L. Rivest, and Adi Shamir. The bibliographic record is too slight to determine whether it was a jointly written paper, panel, or presentation artifact, so its status as a discrete authored work remains uncertain. [DBLP]
- **2012:** *Overview of the Digital Object Architecture*. An institutional CNRI overview rather than an individually signed Kahn text; it manifests the architecture he originated but resists inclusion as his personal work. [CNRI-Publications]
- **2018:** *Blocks as Digital Entities: A Standards Perspective*. A co-authored article with Patrice A. Lyons treating standardized information blocks as digital entities. [CNRI-Publications; DBLP]
