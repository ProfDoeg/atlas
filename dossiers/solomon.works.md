## Works by King Solomon

### Buildings and architectural complexes

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The First Temple (the House of YHWH) in Jerusalem*. A temple complex commissioned by Solomon and constructed by conscripted laborers, Phoenician specialists, and the bronze-worker Hiram/Huram; Kings dates its beginning to Solomon’s fourth regnal year and completion seven years later. Its historical form and even the date of its construction remain disputed because no securely identified contemporary remains or extrabiblical testimony survive, and the biblical description may incorporate later redaction. [1 Kings 5–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%205-8&version=NRSVUE); [Dubovský, *The Building of the First Temple*](https://www.mohrsiebeck.com/en/book/the-building-of-the-first-temple-9783161538384/); [Monson, “The Temple of Solomon in Iron Age Context”](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/10/3/198)

- **Traditionally c. 959–946 BCE:** *Solomon’s palace complex*. A thirteen-year royal building project adjoining the Temple; “Solomon made” here means that he commissioned and controlled a collaborative, coerced-labor obra rather than personally constructing it. [1 Kings 7:1–12](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A1-12&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 959–946 BCE:** *The House of the Forest of Lebanon*. A cedar-columned armory or ceremonial hall within the palace complex, not a house situated in Lebanon. [1 Kings 7:2–5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A2-5&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 959–946 BCE:** *The Hall of Pillars*. A columned porch or audience structure forming part of the palace complex. [1 Kings 7:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A6&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 959–946 BCE:** *The Hall of the Throne (Hall of Judgment)*. A cedar-lined judicial audience hall: architecture made to stage and perform royal judgment. [1 Kings 7:7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A7&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 959–946 BCE:** *Solomon’s residential house*. The king’s private residence within the palace enclosure. [1 Kings 7:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A8&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 959–946 BCE:** *The house for Pharaoh’s daughter*. A separate palace residence made for Solomon’s Egyptian wife; Chronicles repeats the attribution while giving it a ritual explanation. [1 Kings 7:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A8&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A11&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *The Millo*. A fortified earthwork, terrace, or structural filling in Jerusalem that Solomon is said to have built or repaired; its precise physical form and relationship to earlier construction remain uncertain. [1 Kings 9:15, 24; 11:27](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A15%2C24%3B11%3A27&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *The wall of Jerusalem*. A fortification project attributed to Solomon, probably an enlargement or strengthening rather than the creation of the city’s first wall. [1 Kings 9:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A15&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Hazor’s fortifications*. Kings assigns the fortified city to Solomon’s labor program, but the attribution of particular gates and strata to the tenth century remains archaeologically disputed. [1 Kings 9:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A15&version=NRSVUE); [“Hazor,” *Encyclopedia of the Bible*](https://www.biblegateway.com/resources/encyclopedia-of-the-bible/Hazor)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Megiddo’s fortifications*. A royal urban and military project in Kings; proposed “Solomonic” gates, palaces, and strata have repeatedly been redated or contested, so no excavated component can be assigned securely to Solomon. [1 Kings 9:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A15&version=NRSVUE); [Cline, “Searching for Solomon”](https://www.biblicalarchaeology.org/daily/ancient-cultures/ancient-israel/searching-for-solomon/)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Gezer’s reconstruction and fortifications*. Kings says Pharaoh destroyed Gezer, gave it to his daughter, and Solomon rebuilt it; the association of excavated monumental architecture with Solomon is disputed. [1 Kings 9:15–17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A15-17&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Lower Beth-horon*. A fortified town attributed to Solomon in Kings; Chronicles adds walls, gates, and bars and pairs it with Upper Beth-horon. [1 Kings 9:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A17&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A5&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Upper Beth-horon*. A fortified-town project found in Chronicles but not in Kings’ corresponding list. [2 Chronicles 8:5](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A5&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Baalath*. A fortified or administratively developed town attributed to Solomon; its location is uncertain. [1 Kings 9:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A18&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A6&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Tamar/Tadmor in the wilderness*. Kings’ textual tradition reads either Tamar or Tadmor, while Chronicles identifies Tadmor, later Palmyra; the place-name and therefore the project’s location are disputed. [1 Kings 9:18](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A18&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A4&version=NRSVUE); [USCCB note to 2 Chronicles 8](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/2chronicles/8)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *The cities returned by Huram*. Chronicles says Solomon rebuilt unspecified cities given to him by Huram and settled Israelites in them; Kings instead says Solomon gave twenty Galilean cities to Hiram, so the direction of the transfer is irreconcilably different in the two accounts. [1 Kings 9:10–14](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A10-14&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:1–2](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A1-2&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Storage towns in Hamath*. An unspecified network of warehouses or administrative settlements attributed only by Chronicles. Because neither number nor individual names are preserved, this resists division into separate works. [2 Chronicles 8:3–4](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A3-4&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Storage cities throughout Solomon’s dominion*. Kings and Chronicles describe an unnamed system rather than a recoverable catalogue of buildings. It is better classified as an infrastructural project than as a single obra. [1 Kings 9:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A19&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A6&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Chariot cities*. An unnamed network of military settlements made to house Solomon’s chariot establishment; neither their number nor a reliable list of locations survives. [1 Kings 9:19; 10:26](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A19%3B10%3A26&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Cavalry cities*. An unnamed network for horses and cavalry, paired with the chariot cities in the biblical building catalogue. [1 Kings 9:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A19&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A6&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Unspecified works in Jerusalem, Lebanon, and the land of his dominion*. Kings and Chronicles close their catalogues with an open-ended claim that Solomon built whatever he desired. This is evidence for a remembered building project, but it resists treatment as a list of identifiable works because no titles, quantities, or forms are supplied. [1 Kings 9:19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A19&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 8:6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%208%3A6&version=NRSVUE)

### Temple furnishings and ritual objects

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The two bronze pillars, Jachin and Boaz*. Monumental freestanding pillars made by the Tyrian bronze-worker Hiram for Solomon’s Temple; Solomon is patron, not fabricator. [1 Kings 7:13–22, 40–47](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A13-22%2C40-47&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The Sea of cast bronze*. A large basin resting on twelve bronze oxen, made by Hiram for Solomon; Chronicles assigns it a purificatory function. [1 Kings 7:23–26, 40–47](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A23-26%2C40-47&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:2–6](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A2-6&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *Ten bronze stands and ten basins*. A coordinated series of mobile washing vessels, richly decorated and cast by Hiram for Solomon. [1 Kings 7:27–39](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A27-39&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The bronze pots, shovels, and basins*. Sets of sacrificial-service utensils made by Hiram; their individual number and dimensions are not preserved. [1 Kings 7:40–47](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A40-47&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The bronze altar*. Chronicles attributes this large sacrificial altar to Solomon’s Temple project, although Kings does not include it in the same form in its furnishing inventory. [2 Chronicles 4:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A1&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The golden incense altar*. A ritual furnishing that Kings explicitly places among the vessels “Solomon made”; this formula denotes royal commission and workshop production. [1 Kings 7:48](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A48&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The golden table for the bread of the Presence*. Kings speaks of one table, whereas Chronicles speaks of tables in the plural, leaving the number textually uncertain. [1 Kings 7:48](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A48&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:8, 19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A8%2C19&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *Ten golden lampstands*. A designed series of five lampstands on either side of the inner sanctuary. [1 Kings 7:49](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A49&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 4:7](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A7&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The golden floral work, lamps, and tongs*. Associated components or utensils of the Temple lampstands, listed collectively rather than as individually recoverable works. [1 Kings 7:49](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A49&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The golden cups, snuffers, basins, incense dishes, and firepans*. A workshop-produced service assemblage whose individual quantities are not given. [1 Kings 7:50](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A50&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The golden door sockets*. Architectural metalwork for the doors of the inner sanctuary and the Temple nave. [1 Kings 7:50](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%207%3A50&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The Temple courts and their doors*. Chronicles credits Solomon’s project with a court for the priests, a great court, and bronze-overlaid doors; Kings’ account describes courts but does not inventory them in the same way. [2 Chronicles 4:9](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%204%3A9&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 966–959 BCE:** *The bronze platform*. A raised platform from which Solomon addresses the assembly at the Temple dedication, found only in Chronicles. [2 Chronicles 6:12–13](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%206%3A12-13&version=NRSVUE)

### Royal objects and transport

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Two hundred large gold shields*. Ceremonial royal objects made from beaten gold and displayed in the House of the Forest of Lebanon; they were later carried away by Pharaoh Shishak according to Kings. [1 Kings 10:16–17; 14:25–28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2010%3A16-17%3B14%3A25-28&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *Three hundred gold bucklers*. A second, smaller series of parade or armory shields, likewise displayed in the House of the Forest of Lebanon. [1 Kings 10:17](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2010%3A17&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *The great ivory throne overlaid with gold*. A six-stepped royal throne with armrests, lions, and a footstool; a political Gesamtkunstwerk combining sculpture, furniture, precious material, and ceremonial performance. [1 Kings 10:18–20](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2010%3A18-20&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 9:17–19](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%209%3A17-19&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *A fleet at Ezion-geber*. Ships constructed on the Red Sea coast near Eloth with assistance from Hiram’s experienced sailors; this was simultaneously a manufactured fleet and a long-distance commercial project. [1 Kings 9:26–28](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%209%3A26-28&version=NRSVUE)

- **During the traditional reign, c. 970–931 BCE:** *A “Tarshish” trading fleet*. Kings describes a joint fleet with Hiram returning every three years with luxury cargo; whether “ships of Tarshish” names a destination, a vessel type, or a conventional long-distance fleet is disputed. [1 Kings 10:22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2010%3A22&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 9:21](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%209%3A21&version=NRSVUE)

### Sanctuaries for other gods

- **Late in the traditional reign, before c. 931 BCE:** *The high place for Chemosh*. A sanctuary east of Jerusalem commissioned for the Moabite god; Kings treats it as evidence of Solomon’s apostasy rather than as a neutral architectural achievement. [1 Kings 11:1–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2011%3A1-8&version=NRSVUE)

- **Late in the traditional reign, before c. 931 BCE:** *The high place for Molech*. An Ammonite sanctuary paired with the high place for Chemosh; the textual and religious relationship among Molech, Milcom, and the “abomination of the Ammonites” is complicated by variant names elsewhere in the chapter. [1 Kings 11:5–8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2011%3A5-8&version=NRSVUE)

- **Late in the traditional reign, before c. 931 BCE:** *High places for Solomon’s foreign wives*. An unspecified series of cult installations where his wives offered incense and sacrifices. The account supplies neither their number nor their deities, so the series resists conversion into individually named works. [1 Kings 11:8](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%2011%3A8&version=NRSVUE)

### Attributed speech, songs, and wisdom

- **Traditionally c. 970–931 BCE; not independently preserved:** *Three thousand proverbs*. Kings attributes this body of spoken wisdom to Solomon, but no ancient catalogue identifies which sayings belonged to it; its relationship to the much later, composite Book of Proverbs cannot be established item by item. [1 Kings 4:29–34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%204%3A29-34&version=NRSVUE); [Society for Old Testament Study, “Proverbs”](https://www.sots.ac.uk/wiki/proverbs)

- **Traditionally c. 970–931 BCE; not independently preserved:** *One thousand and five songs*. A lost or unidentifiable song corpus attributed by Kings to Solomon. It is a reported body of work rather than a recoverable collection, and no individual title survives. [1 Kings 4:32](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%204%3A32&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 970–931 BCE; not independently preserved:** *Discourses on trees, animals, birds, reptiles, and fish*. Kings presents Solomon as speaking systematically about the natural world, but neither titles nor words survive; these may have been learned catalogues, comparisons, or wisdom teaching rather than “texts” in a modern bibliographic sense. [1 Kings 4:33](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%204%3A33&version=NRSVUE)

- **Traditionally c. 960 BCE within the narrative:** *The Temple-dedication address, prayer, and blessing*. Liturgical-political speeches placed in Solomon’s mouth by Kings and partly reworked in Chronicles; they act as dedication ritual as well as theological text. Their present literary form is generally assigned to later historians or editors, so they cannot securely be treated as verbatim Solomonic compositions. [1 Kings 8:12–61](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Kings%208%3A12-61&version=NRSVUE); [2 Chronicles 6:1–42](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=2%20Chronicles%206%3A1-42&version=NRSVUE)

### Canonical works bearing Solomonic attribution

- **Collected and edited over an uncertain span, substantially later than the tenth century BCE:** *Proverbs*. A composite wisdom anthology containing sections headed “Proverbs of Solomon,” other anonymous collections, sayings of “the wise,” and works attributed to Agur and Lemuel; Proverbs 25 expressly says that Hezekiah’s men transmitted a Solomonic collection. Some individual sayings may be old, but neither Solomon’s authorship of the whole book nor a secure list of sayings personally made by him can be maintained. [Society for Old Testament Study, “Proverbs”](https://www.sots.ac.uk/wiki/proverbs); [Proverbs 1; 10; 22–25; 30–31](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Proverbs%201%3A1%3B10%3A1%3B22%3A17%3B24%3A23%3B25%3A1%3B30%3A1%3B31%3A1&version=NRSVUE)

- **Date uncertain; commonly assigned in its received form to the postexilic period:** *Song of Songs (Song of Solomon)*. A collection or unified sequence of erotic love poetry whose heading can mean “by,” “for,” “belonging to,” or “concerning” Solomon; modern scholarship generally rejects direct Solomonic authorship, and Solomon also appears within the poem as a represented figure. [Oxford Academic, “The Eros Strand in Wisdom”](https://academic.oup.com/book/50250/chapter-abstract/422753621); [“Song of Songs,” *Encyclopedia.com*](https://www.encyclopedia.com/philosophy-and-religion/bible/old-testament/song-songs)

- **Probably Persian or Hellenistic period, not the tenth century BCE:** *Ecclesiastes (Qohelet)*. A philosophical wisdom text voiced by a “son of David, king in Jerusalem,” creating a Solomonic persona without actually naming Solomon; linguistic and historical evidence has led modern scholarship to reject Solomon as its author. [Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion, “The Book of Ecclesiastes”](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62249/chapter-abstract/551380615); [Society for Old Testament Study, “Ecclesiastes”](https://www.sots.ac.uk/wiki/ecclesiastes)

- **Date uncertain:** *Psalm 72*. A royal prayer headed *lišlōmō*, which can mean “of,” “for,” or “concerning Solomon”; its closing reference to the prayers of David and its composite transmission prevent confident attribution to Solomon. [Psalm 72](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%2072&version=NRSVUE); [Oxford Academic discussion of Solomonic headings](https://academic.oup.com/book/50250/chapter-abstract/422753621)

- **Date uncertain:** *Psalm 127*. A short pilgrimage psalm headed *lišlōmō* and concerned with building, security, labor, and descendants. The heading associates it with Solomon but does not establish that he composed it, and some manuscripts or ancient versions differ over the superscription. [Psalm 127](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Psalm%20127&version=NRSVUE); [Oxford Academic discussion of Solomonic headings](https://academic.oup.com/book/50250/chapter-abstract/422753621)

### Literary self-portrait rather than recoverable royal works

- **Probably composed centuries after Solomon:** *Qohelet’s houses, vineyards, gardens, parks, pools, estates, herds, treasury, singers, and pleasures*. Ecclesiastes 2 makes these things within a deliberately royal first-person experiment. They belong to a literary Solomon-like persona and cannot safely be added to the historical king’s architectural catalogue; their resistance to being called Solomon’s works is precisely part of what the text is doing. [Ecclesiastes 2:1–11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ecclesiastes%202%3A1-11&version=NRSVUE); [Oxford Research Encyclopedia of Religion, “The Book of Ecclesiastes”](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/62249/chapter-abstract/551380615)

### Later pseudonymous manifestations

- **After the second century BCE:** *Wisdom of Solomon*. A Greek Jewish wisdom book that adopts the voice and authority of the paradigmatically wise king without naming him in the text; scholarship usually locates its author in Alexandria and rejects actual Solomonic authorship. It is a manifestation of “Solomon” as an authorial persona, not something made by the tenth-century king. [Cambridge, *The Wisdom of Solomon*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/wisdom-of-solomon/FC3CD8AF0FB551AB15B8FC3104C09D30)

- **First century BCE:** *Psalms of Solomon*. A collection of eighteen Jewish psalms associated with Solomon in their transmitted title or superscriptions but reflecting the Roman conquest of Jerusalem in 63 BCE. Nothing in them supports composition by the historical Solomon. [Cambridge, “The Psalms of Solomon”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/outside-the-old-testament/psalms-of-solomon/D936BF7C4E9B4212AE25AA0FC14AC662)

- **Early centuries CE, date disputed:** *Odes of Solomon*. A collection of early Christian hymns transmitted under Solomon’s name but distinct from the *Psalms of Solomon*. The title is a pseudonymous association, not evidence that King Solomon made the hymns. [Cambridge discussion of the distinct Psalms and Odes](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/outside-the-old-testament/psalms-of-solomon/D936BF7C4E9B4212AE25AA0FC14AC662)
