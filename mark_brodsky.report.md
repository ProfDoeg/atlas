# Mark Brodsky (Aurelius Capital Management): research report
*2026-08-15. Compiled for the atlas journey file `mark_brodsky.journey.json`. Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, inference, or tradition. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

## Method and the shape of the problem

This subject is unusual in the corpus for an inverse reason: the litigation is exhaustively documented and the man is not. There is **no standalone English Wikipedia biography of Mark Brodsky [A: direct URL lookup returns 404]**; everything reachable about him is folded into the institutional article on **Aurelius Capital Management**. The court record names Aurelius entities as parties in dozens of proceedings; it almost never names Brodsky personally, because a fund principal is not a party. The result is a journey whose stops are mostly courtrooms he is documented to have litigated in through counsel, and whose biographical segment is four sentences long.

The comparison case in this corpus is `paul_singer`, whose file was written first and whose report supplied several of the shared-campaign coordinates used here. **The two subjects are not symmetrical and should not be written as if they were** (see Contradictions, §7).

Everything below is drawn from a verified research pool assembled and adversarially corrected in this session. Where the corrector overrode the researcher, the correction is what is recorded, and the override is noted.

## 1. Origins (1974 and a hole where the rest should be)

- **Bachelor's degree in political science, University of Pennsylvania, 1974 [A: Wikipedia, "Aurelius Capital Management"]**. This is the *only* formal biographical anchor found.
- **Gap, stated as a gap:** birth year, birthplace, parents, secondary schooling, law school, and bar admission are **not documented in any source reachable this session**. He is described as a lawyer; no source names where he took a law degree.
- **Employment at Elliott Associates (later Elliott Management / Elliott Investment Management), Paul Singer's fund, before founding Aurelius [A]**.
  - **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved:** Wikipedia "Aurelius Capital Management" calls him "a lawyer formerly employed by Elliott Associates." Wikipedia "Bracebridge Capital," quoting the *New York Times* verbatim, calls him "a former trader at Mr. Singer's Elliott Management." Both agree on the Elliott/Singer connection and that it preceded Aurelius. **Lawyer or trader is genuinely open.**
  - **Gap:** no start year, no leaving year. The journey file's Elliott stop is dated 1990 as a bare placeholder between the 1974 degree and the 2005 founding, and the `date_confidence` field says so in those words.
- **Coordinate correction applied:** the research pool originally pinned Elliott at 40.7128, -74.0060 (Lower Manhattan / City Hall). Elliott was always a Midtown firm, at 40 West 57th Street until its 2020 move to West Palm Beach. The journey file inherits the existing corpus pin from `paul_singer.journey.json`: **40.7648, -73.9776**.

**The Peru precedent [A as to Elliott; R as to Brodsky].** In 1996 Elliott bought defaulted Peruvian debt for $11.4 million, won a $58 million judgment, and was paid in full under the pari passu rule [A: Wikipedia, "Paul Singer (businessman)"]. *Elliott Associates v. Peru* is the case that built the method Aurelius later ran on its own book. **That Brodsky personally worked on or helped develop the theory is [R]**: it is the researcher's inference from his documented employment, not a sourced claim, and the journey file's `date_confidence` on that stop says so.

## 3. Argentina: the paper (2001–2010)

- **Default, December 2001 [A]**. **Contradiction, flagged:** Wikipedia "Republic of Argentina v. NML Capital, Ltd." says "$103 billion of debt"; Wikipedia "Argentine debt restructuring" says "US$93 billion of its external debt; of around $81.8 billion in bonds." The journey file states the range rather than picking one.
- **2005 exchange offer [A]**. **Contradiction, flagged:** the NML article gives par bonds due 2038 or 70%-discount bonds due 2033; "Argentine debt restructuring" gives discount bonds at a nominal 25–35% of original and reports the 2005 exchange taking in 76% of the defaulted bonds (US$62.5 bn).
- **Anachronism corrected.** The researcher's original entry said Aurelius "refused to participate" in the 2005 exchange. **Aurelius was founded in 2005 and no source establishes that it held Argentine paper on the date of the offer.** It bought into the defaulted paper on the secondary market. The journey file says so explicitly: *a holdout by purchase, not by refusal.*
- **2010 second exchange [A]**; combined acceptance across both exchanges passes 90%. Aurelius stays out, as do NML Capital (Elliott's Cayman vehicle), Davidson Kempner and Bracebridge.
- **Earliest documented Aurelius Argentina litigation: *Aurelius Capital Partners, LP v. Republic of Argentina*, 584 F.3d 120 (2d Cir. 2009) [A: footnote citation in Wikipedia, "Republic of Argentina v. NML Capital, Ltd."]**. Full case details not retrieved. This matters for dating: it puts Aurelius in its own Argentine suits several years before the consolidated pari passu campaign became famous, and implies an SDNY filing a year or two earlier.

## 4. Argentina: the injunction (2012–2014)

- **Griesa's pari passu holding, SDNY [A]**, dated **23 February 2012** in `paul_singer.report.md` §5 **[R as to the exact date in this file's sourcing; the Wikipedia article relied on here gives only 2012]**. Argentina may not pay exchange bondholders without ratably paying the holdouts.
- **Second Circuit affirms, 26 October 2012 [A]**.
- **Amounts fixed, November 2012 [A/R].** **Correction applied and important:** Wikipedia "Aurelius Capital Management" reads "October 2013 attempt to force payment of $1.3 billion following the 2010 default." Two errors in that sentence: (a) **Argentina did not default in 2010**; 2010 was the second exchange offer; (b) **$1.3 bn is not an Aurelius-only figure.** ~$1.33 bn is the aggregate owed to the whole holdout plaintiff group under Griesa's November 2012 order, and the NML article separately cites $2.4 bn ordered to NML. **The journey file refuses to present $1.3 bn as Aurelius's claim** and states the misattribution as part of the record.
- **Second Circuit affirms again, 23 August 2013 [A]**, with the "free transferability" reasoning. **Date correction applied:** the research pool originally dated the famous commercial-center passage to 2012 and attributed it to Griesa. It is from *NML Capital, Ltd. v. Republic of Argentina*, 727 F.3d 230 (2d Cir. 2013), decided **23 August 2013**, and it is the **Second Circuit's**, not Griesa's. The atlas quote also elided "one widely shared in the financial community"; the journey file restores the full sentence verbatim.
- **Supreme Court, 16 June 2014 [A]**: 7–1, Scalia writing, upholding the discovery order permitting NML to subpoena banks worldwide; Ginsburg dissenting, Sotomayor not participating. **Flagged:** the widely-reported *same-day denial of certiorari* in the pari passu case is **not** confirmed by the cited article. The journey file's `date_confidence` carries that hedge.
- **Coordinate correction applied:** the pool's 38.8897, -77.0089 is the US Capitol. The Supreme Court building is **38.8906, -77.0044**. Note this diverges from `paul_singer.journey.json`, which uses a generic Washington pin (38.9072, -77.0369) for the same-day ruling. **Divergence recorded, not reconciled.**
- **Selective default, 30 July 2014 [A]**: Argentina wires a coupon to its New York trustee, the injunction forbids onward payment, the money sits.
- **ICJ filing, August 2014 [A: Wikipedia, "Argentine debt restructuring"]**. **Gap closed, not left open:** the researcher recorded the disposition as unknown. The United States has never accepted the Court's compulsory jurisdiction, so the application was **never entered on the General List and no proceedings followed**. Coordinate corrected from central Den Haag to the Peace Palace, **52.0866, 4.2956**.
- **RUFO clause expires, 31 December 2014 [A]**. Rights Upon Future Offers had made any better deal for the holdouts a potential liability to every exchange holder. **[R]** on the causal link to the 2016 settlement: widely understood, not spelled out in the source.

## 5. Argentina: the settlement (2015–2016)

- **Macri elected, 22 November 2015 [A]**, having campaigned partly on ending the standoff.
- **$6.5 bn offer, 5 February 2016 [A: Wikipedia, "Republic of Argentina v. NML Capital, Ltd."; Wikipedia, "Mauricio Macri"]**.
- **Griesa lifts the injunction, 19 February 2016 [A]**, conditional on repeal of the Argentine lock laws and payment of settling holdouts by **29 February 2016** [A: via Wikipedia, "Paul Singer (businessman)"].
- **Pollack announces the Singer/Elliott settlement, February 2016 [A]**. **Gap:** *no dollar figure specific to Aurelius was found in any source reachable this session.* `paul_singer.report.md` §6 gives FT figures of **$4.653 bn to the four lead holdouts as a group, of which Elliott took roughly $2.4 bn**, which implies but does not state Aurelius's share **[R]**.
- **The clearest documented instance of Brodsky acting in concert with Singer [A: Wikipedia, "Bracebridge Capital," quoting reporting on the February 2016 settlement]:** Aurelius, Davidson Kempner and Bracebridge agree **not to try to block Argentina from raising the new money** needed to fund the settlements.
- **Argentine Congress repeals the lock laws, March 2016 [A]**.
- **New York court confirms, 13 April 2016 [A]**, Second Circuit affirming from the bench; Argentina cleared to pay 2005 holders, 2010 holders and holdouts alike.
- **Duplicate entry corrected.** The research pool contained a separate "14 April 2016" court-confirmation entry. There was no such second confirmation: **14 April 2016 was the date the settlement offer's stated validity expired.** Argentina actually paid on **22 April 2016**. The journey file records both dates and does **not** explain the eight-day gap, because no reachable source does.
- **November 2016: a further $475 million settled with smaller remaining holdouts [A]**.

**Spanish-language figure, flagged as inconsistent with the English sources and not reconciled:** Wikipedia (es), "Fondo buitre," citation [14]: *"En 2016, tras la asunción de Mauricio Macri, el gobierno decidió pagarles 9.300 millones USD a los fondos buitre."* US$9.3 bn against the FT's $4.653 bn to the four leads suggests the Spanish figure covers a wider class of creditors, but nothing reachable says so. **Recorded as an open discrepancy.**

## 6. Puerto Rico (2017–2020)

- **Debt figures [A: Wikipedia, "Puerto Rican government-debt crisis"]:** the Oversight Board filed under **PROMESA Title III on 3 May 2017**, against "$123 billion liabilities from debt ($74 billion) and unfunded pension obligations ($49 billion)." **Correction applied:** the pool's headline "$72 billion" is superseded by $74 bn in bond debt / ~$123 bn total liabilities.
- **Aurelius position: ~$468 million of the island's debt [A: Wikipedia, "Aurelius Capital Management"]**.
- **Appointments Clause suit. Date corrected by a full year.** The pool said 2018. Wikipedia "Puerto Rican government-debt crisis": *"On August 7, 2017, Aurelius Capital Management had Theodore Olson file another lawsuit, which now alleged that the selection of the Oversight Board violated the Appointments Clause."* 2018 is when the District Court for Puerto Rico denied the motion to dismiss. **Counsel: Theodore Olson, former Solicitor General [A]**.
- **First Circuit, February 2019 [A]:** appointments held unconstitutional, with a 90-day reappointment window and no unwinding of the board's prior acts (*Aurelius Inv., LLC v. Puerto Rico*, 915 F.3d 838 (1st Cir. 2019)). Exact day of the combined ruling not established here.
- **Certiorari granted 20 June 2019; argued 15 October 2019 [A]**.
- **Mediated restructuring of $35 bn in general obligation bonds, February 2020 [A]**, including resolution of disputes over the validity of post-2012 debt.
- **Supreme Court, 1 June 2020, Breyer writing [A]**. **Two corrections applied:**
  1. **"Unanimous" is true only as to the judgment.** Thomas concurred in the judgment while rejecting the majority's characterization of the board's duties as primarily local; Sotomayor concurred in the judgment while criticizing "this freewheeling exercise of control over a population."
  2. **The holding was misstated in the pool** as the board being "backed by Puerto Rican rather than federal law." The board was indisputably created by federal law (PROMESA). The holding is that officers exercising **primary local power** are not "Officers of the United States" for Appointments Clause purposes, and this board's powers are primarily local **even though federally created**.

## 7. Contradictions and cautions (not adjudicated)

1. **Lawyer or trader at Elliott.** Two sources, two words, both traceable. Unresolved.
2. **$1.3 bn.** Reported as Aurelius's claim; it is the plaintiff group's aggregate. Do not repeat the error.
3. **"The 2010 default."** Wikipedia's own sentence. There was no 2010 Argentine default.
4. **The word "vultures."** Attested, but the Wikipedia sentence applying it to Aurelius *over Argentine debt* footnotes Rao & Spink, Reuters, 18 September 2015, *"Exclusive: Hedge fund Aurelius holds Ukraine debt, asking better terms - source"*, an article about **Ukrainian** debt that never applies the word to Argentina and does not offer it as a Reuters characterization. The epithet is real and ubiquitous in the Argentine press as *buitre*; **that particular citation does not support that particular sentence.** The source sentence is also ungrammatical ("a 'vultures'") and must not be presented as polished quotation.
5. **$9.3 bn vs $4.653 bn** for the 2016 payout (see §5).
6. **Supreme Court coordinates** diverge between this file (38.8906, -77.0044) and `paul_singer.journey.json` (38.9072, -77.0369).
7. **Asymmetry with Singer, stated plainly.** The pool's claim that Aurelius ran "roughly the same decade-long span" as Singer, dated "2003–2016," is **impossible as written**: Aurelius was founded in 2005. Singer's Argentina campaign genuinely runs back to the 2001–02 default; **Aurelius joins several years late**, with c. 2007–2016 as the defensible span and 2009–2016 as the strictly citable one. **Adjudicated post-write (2026-08-15):** the journey file's title had inherited "Fifteen Years of Refusing the Offer," counting from the 2001 default despite this section's own caution against writing Brodsky's span as symmetrical with Singer's. Corrected to "Nine Years," matching the c. 2007–2016 defensible span, in both language editions.
8. **The ARA Libertad seizure at Tema, Ghana (2 October 2012)** is documented for **NML/Elliott/Singer specifically**. Whether Aurelius participated in or benefited from it is **not established**. Per the standing rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, **it is not staged in Brodsky's file**; it is `paul_singer`'s stop and remains there.
9. **Elliott's Peru (Brussels, 2000) and Congo (Kensington International) campaigns** likewise predate or lie outside Aurelius. The Peru precedent appears in Brodsky's file only as a scene *inside the firm where he worked*, tagged [R] as to his presence.

## 8. Interlocks

- **`paul_singer`: direct, two-directional, and the strongest in the file.** Brodsky was employed at Singer's firm before founding his own; the two then appear as co-plaintiffs on the same caption for roughly a decade; and in February 2016 Aurelius, Davidson Kempner and Bracebridge give the undertaking that makes Elliott's headline settlement executable. Named in campas.
- **`cristina_fernandez_de_kirchner`: direct and adversarial.** Her government is the defendant in the litigation Brodsky's firm ran; her administration's refusal to settle is the reason the case lasted; and she publicly named the holdout funds *fondos buitre* and, at the UN in 2014, "economic terrorists." Her own file's Beijing stop (4 Feb 2015) describes Argentina as "shut out of international credit markets by the New York courts and the holdout creditors her government refuses to pay." Brodsky is one of those creditors. Named in campas.
- **`javier_milei`: deliberately NOT named.** Rejected in `paul_singer.report.md` line 172 on the correct ground that inheriting a post-holdout debt landscape and passing through the same city are not relations between two people. The same reasoning applies here and is applied.
- **Mauricio Macri** is named as a historical actor. He has **no journey file** in the corpus; a second subject now needs him.
