# Thomas Alva Edison (1847-1931): research report

*2026-08-10. Compiled for the Atlas of Journeys from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses). Slug: `thomas_edison`. Journey: `thomas_edison.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or an attribution whose chain does not reach a primary document. Contradictions are printed, not adjudicated. Corrections the verification pass made to the lenses are gathered in section 11 rather than repeated in place.

---

## 1. Milan and Port Huron, 1847-1859

Born 11 February 1847 at Milan, Ohio, seventh and last child of Samuel and Nancy Edison **[A: Wikipedia; NPS; Israel 1998]**. The brick birth house above the Huron River survives as a museum **[A: Edison Birthplace Museum]**; its 1947 opening rests on the museum's own history alone **[R]**. To Port Huron, Michigan, 1854, after the Milan grain trade collapsed when the railroad bypassed the town **[A: Wikipedia; Josephson 1959]**. Hearing loss from about age twelve, attributed variously to scarlet fever and to recurrent middle-ear infection; almost no formal schooling, largely self-taught by reading **[A: Wikipedia]**. The competing aetiologies are not resolved in any source reached and are left unresolved.

## 2. The Grand Trunk line, 1859-1863

News butcher between Port Huron and Detroit from about thirteen; prints and sells the *Grand Trunk Herald* **[A: Wikipedia; Baldwin 1995]**. Chemistry bench and press in a baggage car, ended by a fire, boy and equipment put off the train **[A: Baldwin 1995]**. Reads at the Detroit Young Men's Society library, "later claiming to have worked through entire shelves in order" **[R: Josephson 1959, citing Edison; no other source]**.

The Mount Clemens rescue, pulling agent J. U. MacKenzie's toddler clear of a boxcar and being paid in telegraphy lessons: **[R]**. *Lens contradiction.* Geography tagged it **[A]** at 1862 while its own source line conceded "Edison's own account, exact date disputed across biographies"; chronology tagged it **[R]** at 1863. The verification pass ruled geography's tag wrong. The journey carries **[R]** at 1862 and states the year disagreement in `date_confidence`.

## 3. The tramp operator, 1863-1868

Night operator at Stratford, Ontario: required to wire the signal "6" hourly, rigs an automatic sender, sleeps, near head-on collision, leaves under pressure **[R]**. *Corrected:* geography tagged this **[A]** on Israel 1998 and Josephson 1959; the verification pass demoted it, since the hourly 6, the rigged sender and the near-collision all descend from Edison's own later retelling with no contemporary documentation.

Itinerant across Ontario, Michigan, Kentucky, Ohio, Massachusetts. Cincinnati 1863 and Memphis 1864 **[R: Israel 1998]**; Indianapolis 1864, an automatic repeater to slow incoming press traffic to a hand he can follow **[A: Israel 1998]**; Louisville Western Union 1866-1867, dismissed after sulfuric acid burns through the floor onto his manager's desk **[A: Josephson 1959; Israel 1998]**. Those three carry no stops of their own; they are named inside the Louisville campa, a compression made to hold the stop count.

## 4. Boston and the first patent, 1868-1869

Western Union operator in Boston; first patent application filed there; early stock ticker **[A: Wikipedia]**. Electrographic vote recorder built at Charles Williams Jr.'s shop, 109 Court Street, where he also demonstrates a duplex telegraph **[A: Israel 1998]**. U.S. Patent 90,646 granted 1 June 1869, commercially dead **[A: Wikipedia; the patent]**. The standard explanation for the failure, that legislative minorities depend on a slow roll call to filibuster, is in every biography but not in the pool: carried in the campa, flagged **[R]**.

## 5. New York and Newark, 1869-1875

Arrives broke; present when the Law Gold Indicator Company's transmitter fails at 42 Broad Street, repairs it, is hired **[A: Josephson 1959, printing an account that descends from Edison; the campa says "by the account he gave afterward"]**. Firm with Franklin Leonard Pope, October 1869 **[A: Wikipedia]**. About $40,000 for Universal Stock Printer improvements, 1871 **[A: NPS]**. Ward Street shops, Newark, 1870-1875 **[A: Israel 1998]**. Marries Mary Stilwell 25 December 1871 **[A: Israel 1998]**. Sells the quadruplex for about $30,000 in 1874, funding the next laboratory **[A: Wikipedia]**. London 1873: demonstrates the automatic telegraph at the Central Telegraph Office and fails to win the contract **[A: Israel 1998]**; the reason given in the campa, induction smearing signals on long British circuits, is standard but not in the pool **[R]**.

## 6. Menlo Park, 1876-1881

First industrial research laboratory established 1876, funded largely by the quadruplex sale **[A: Wikipedia; NPS; Israel 1998]**. Carbon transmitter 1877; tin-foil phonograph announced November 1877 **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**. Demonstration to the editors of *Scientific American*, 7 December 1877 **[A: the journal, 22 December 1877]**. To the National Academy of Sciences and to President Hayes at the White House late on 18 April 1878, Hayes waking his wife **[A: Israel 1998; White House Historical Association]**. Drexel, Morgan & Co. with the Vanderbilts capitalize the Edison Electric Light Company, 1878 **[A: Jonnes 2003; Israel 1998]**. Carbonized filament burning over thirteen hours 21 October 1879; application 4 November 1879, granted 27 January 1880 as Patent 223,898 **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**.

*Place corrected.* Geography put the *Scientific American* demonstration at Menlo Park; the journal's own text puts it in its New York office, so the stop moves to New York. The "Mary Had a Little Lamb" recitation is **[R]**, resting on Edison's 1927 re-enactment, with no 1877 cylinder preserved.

*Date hedge.* The 21 October 1879 date is carried, but geography tags the whole breakthrough **[R]**: Israel 1998 treats the clean single date as a commemorative construction popularized for the 1929 Jubilee, the notebooks showing incremental tests through the autumn. The hedge lives in `date_confidence`.

## 7. The system built, 1880-1882

Edison Illuminating Company chartered 17 December 1880 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Office and lighting headquarters to 65 Fifth Avenue, 1881; Edison Machine Works on Goerck Street, 1881-1886 **[A: Israel 1998]**. Holborn Viaduct station, London, switched on January 1882, 93 kW, among the earliest public supplies **[A: Wikipedia]**. Morgan's mansion at 219 Madison Avenue wired 1882 **[A: Jonnes 2003]**. Pearl Street Station opens 4 September 1882, 110 V DC, first commercial central plant in the United States **[A: Wikipedia; Israel 1998; Con Edison]**.

*A flat error corrected.* Chronology gave Pearl Street "946 customers" at opening, matching no documented figure. Startup was **400 lamps for 82 customers**, reaching **508 customers and 10,164 lamps by 1884**. Geography independently said "roughly 85," confirming chronology as the erring lens.

## 8. The War of the Currents, 1884-1893

June 1884: Tesla lands at Castle Garden with Batchelor's letter and is sent the same night to fix a steamer's dynamos; about six months at Goerck Street redesigning DC dynamos; resigns December 1884 over an unpaid bonus Edison allegedly called "American humor" **[A for employment and resignation: `nikola_tesla.journey.json`; the phrasing is Tesla's own account, *My Inventions*, Ch. IV]**. Tesla had already worked for Continental Edison in Paris (1882) and repaired the Edison plant at Strasbourg station (1883) **[A: same]**.

1886: Westinghouse begins competing AC systems, Edison campaigning publicly that AC is dangerous **[A: Wikipedia]**; Machine Works moved to Schenectady **[A: Israel 1998]**. 1888: Harold P. Brown's campaign, Post letter 5 June, Columbia College dog 30 July, a second 3 August, horse and calf at Edison's West Orange laboratory 5 December **[A: Wikipedia, War of the currents]**; Westinghouse licenses Tesla's polyphase patents in July for roughly $60,000 plus royalty **[A: `nikola_tesla.journey.json`]**. 1892: Morgan merges Edison General Electric with Thomson-Houston, Edison's name dropped, GE holding roughly three quarters of the U.S. electrical business **[A: Wikipedia]**. 1893: Westinghouse underbids GE and lights the Columbian Exposition with polyphase AC **[A: Jonnes 2003; `nikola_tesla.journey.json`]**. Carried in the report only, not independently checked: chronology's claim that by end of 1887 Westinghouse had 68 stations against Edison's 121 and was overtaking him in growth **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 9. Glenmont and West Orange, 1884-1896

Mary Stilwell Edison dies 9 August 1884, aged 29 **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**; chronology gave the place as "Menlo Park or New York" and attached Newark's coordinate, matching neither, and the campa says outright that the sources do not agree. Buys land on the Caloosahatchee at Fort Myers, house completed 1886 **[A: Edison and Ford Winter Estates]**; geography dated the purchase 1886, the estate's own history 1885. Marries Mina Miller at Oak Place, Akron, 24 February 1886 **[A: Baldwin 1995]**; the Morse-code courtship is in Baldwin **[A]**, the coded proposal tapped into her palm in a crowded carriage is **[R]** and stays out of the campa. Buys Glenmont, 29 rooms, Llewellyn Park, 1886 **[A: NPS]**. West Orange laboratory opens November 1887, ten times Menlo Park, under Charles Batchelor **[A: Wikipedia; NPS; Israel 1998]**. Ogdensburg ore-separation venture 1889-1900, a large fortune lost as western ore undercut it **[A: Israel 1998]**. Exposition Universelle, Champ de Mars, August 1889, with Eiffel atop the tower **[A: Israel 1998; Baldwin 1995]**. Kinetoscope with W. K. L. Dickson from 1891 **[A: NPS]**; Black Maria 1893, tar paper on a track rotating to follow the sun, first purpose-built film studio, $637.67 **[A: NPS]**; first Kinetoscope parlour, 1155 Broadway, 14 April 1894 **[A: Musser 1990]**, named in the Black Maria campa rather than given a stop. X-ray work from 1896, unpatented calcium tungstate fluoroscope, abandoned c. 1903 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

*Dates disentangled.* Chronology conflated the abandonment (c. 1903) with Clarence Dally's death. Dally's arms were amputated and he died **2 October 1904**; Edison's own near-loss of eyesight also drove the abandonment. Both are stated separately in the journey.

## 10. The late enterprises and the end, 1899-1938

Edison Portland Cement Company 1899; nickel-iron battery research from 1899, about 10,000 electrode and electrolyte combinations, patents and the Edison Storage Battery Company 1901 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Thomas A. Edison Incorporated consolidated 1911 **[A: NPS]**. West Orange fire December 1914: thirteen buildings, one employee killed, about $1.5 million, manufacture resumed within weeks **[A: Wikipedia]**; the pool gives the month only, so the commonly cited 9 December is not asserted. Naval Consulting Board from 1915 and Congressional Gold Medal 1928 **[A: NPS]**; the campa's claims that the Navy adopted almost none of his proposals and that the board's lasting product was the Naval Research Laboratory are standard but **not in the pool**, flagged **[R]**. Edison Botanic Research laboratory, Fort Myers, 1928, with Ford and Firestone, goldenrod among hundreds of species **[A: Edison and Ford Winter Estates]**; chronology dates the rubber project 1927 and geography 1928, both carried, discrepancy in `date_confidence`.

Light's Golden Jubilee, Greenfield Village, 21 October 1929, dedicated by Hoover, Edison re-enacting the invention in the Menlo Park laboratory Ford had shipped from New Jersey **[A: NPS; Baldwin 1995]**. Dies at Glenmont 18 October 1931, aged 84, after months of decline from diabetes complications **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**; NPS gives the hour as 3:21 a.m., the afterlife lens 3:24 a.m., both printed in the campa, neither preferred. The Ford breath-in-a-test-tube story **[R]**: a sealed tube is displayed at the Henry Ford Museum, but whether a breath was captured or the tube merely stood in the room is disputed and no contemporary medical documentation confirms it. Buried at Rosedale Cemetery, Orange; remains and Mina's exhumed and reburied behind Glenmont 1963 **[A: NPS]**. Lights dimmed across many American cities on the funeral night **[R]**: coordinated national blackout in some accounts, patchy voluntary tribute in others, left unresolved. Edison Memorial Tower, 131 feet, 13 ft 8 in Pyrex bulb, dedicated at the Menlo Park site 11 February 1938 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

Not carried as stops: the Rutgers Edison Papers Project from 1978 **[A]**; West Orange reopened 10 October 2009 **[A: Wikipedia]**, the afterlife lens's unsupported "$13 million" dropped; Ohio's 2016 replacement of its William Allen statue in the National Statuary Hall Collection **[A: Architect of the Capitol]**, the lens's "dedicated that May" unsupported and dropped; *The Current War*, Toronto 9 September 2017 but U.S. theatres only 25 October 2019 after the Weinstein collapse **[A: Wikipedia]**, so the lens's "2017 mainstream release" is not carried; a 2013 webcomic recasting Edison as villain and Tesla as martyr **[R]**, which has no site.

## 12. Quotations

Nine quotations are carried, all **[A]**, each with its source line in the stop's `quote_source`: the "kill a customer" letter to Johnson (1886); the Southwick letter naming Westinghouse's machines for the chair (1887); the *Scientific American* notice (1877); Tesla's diary line at the break with Edison (1884); inspiration and perspiration; "Everything comes to him who hustles while he waits" (Jones 1908); the Muscle Shoals currency remark (1921); "We don't know a millionth of one percent about anything" (1931); Tesla's "purely empirical method" assessment (1931).

*Two source lines corrected.* The inspiration/perspiration line was cited to a "Harper's Monthly (1932 interview)," impossible since Edison died in October 1931; *Harper's* printed it posthumously in September 1932, and the documented 1927 self-attribution is reproduced in the *Argus Leader*, 21 February 1932, p. 18. The 1921 currency remark had been silently modernized and truncated; the original wording is restored with its second sentence, which is the point of it.

**Not carried, all [R], no primary-era source, kept out of the quote fields:** "I have not failed. I've just found 10,000 ways that won't work" (earliest citation *73 Magazine*, March 1967, p. 52); "Opportunity is missed by most people because it is dressed in overalls and looks like work" (Mason, 1990, p. 55); "I owe my success to the fact that I never had a clock in my workroom" (via a 2007 parenting book, chain reaching no Edison document); "Everyone steals in commerce and industry. I've stolen a lot, myself. But I know how to steal!" (via *Tesla: The Modern Sorcerer*, 1999). The battery stop, where the 10,000 line would sit most naturally, carries the inspiration/perspiration line instead; the ten-thousand figure in that campa is the documented electrode-and-electrolyte count, not the quotation.

## 14. Honest gaps

1. The *Scientific American* office address for December 1877 has no coordinate in the pool; the stop uses a placeholder pin and says so.
2. Edison's presence at the 1893 Chicago fair is not established. Written around.
3. The Milan birthplace coordinate disagrees between lenses and nothing identifies the house.
4. Morgan's Madison Avenue coordinate disagrees between lenses by about 300 m.
5. The founding month of the Edison Electric Light Company (1878) is not in the pool; November is conventional and carried as reconstruction.
6. The day of the 1914 fire is not in the pool; only the month is asserted.
7. The Mount Clemens year, 1862 against 1863, is left contradicted.
8. The cause of the deafness is unresolved in every source reached.
9. **No primary Edison document was read.** Everything is at one remove: Wikipedia, NPS, the standard biographies, museum histories, a handful of contemporary press items.
