# Agha Hasan Abedi (1922-1995), research report

*Compiled 2026-08-10 for the Atlas of Journeys. Pakistani banker, founder of United Bank Limited (1959) and of the Bank of Credit and Commerce International (1972), closed by regulators in seven countries on 5 July 1991 in the largest banking fraud then on record.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the same line. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a claim a source carries without documenting. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Gaps are recorded as gaps.

**Standing caution.** The spine rests on two encyclopaedia articles plus Wikipedia on the bank. The only primary-bearing document reached is the US Senate report *The BCCI Affair* (Kerry and Brown, December 1992), read in the FAS mirror. Every quotation traces to it. No Pakistani press, no BCCI corporate record, no court filing, and no Bingham text was reachable.

## 1. Lucknow, and the border that moves (1922-1947)

- Born **14 May 1922 in Lucknow**, to an Urdu-speaking middle-class family. **[A: Wikipedia, "Agha Hasan Abedi"]**
- The family is often said to have served the Nawabs of Awadh. **[R]** Nothing consulted documents it; profile journalism, recorded as tradition.
- Held an **MA in English literature and an LLB from Lucknow University**. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Flagged.** The gathering pass entered "1940" as attested while conceding in its own note that Wikipedia gives no year; geography entered "c. 1940-1943," also unsourced. **Both are inventions of the compiler.** The journey carries the date as reconstruction counted back from 1946. **[R]**
- **Joins Habib Bank Limited in 1946**, aged twenty-four. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Anachronism corrected.** The geography pass put him at Habib Bank *in Karachi* from 1946. The bank was **founded in Bombay in 1941** and moved its head office to Karachi on **7 August 1947** at Jinnah's request. **[A: Wikipedia, "Habib Bank Limited"]** His 1946 desk was in Bombay; head office or branch is **undocumented**. **[R]**
- **Migrates to Pakistan after Partition, 1947.** **[A: Wikipedia]** No route, crossing date, or port given anywhere; the arrival stop uses a reconstructed August date, flagged. **[R]**

**Gap.** Nothing on his marriage, children, or household appears in any source reached. The private life of a man who ran a global bank for nineteen years is here entirely blank.

## 2. United Bank, and the Gulf (1959-1971)

- **Founds United Bank Limited in 1959**, first president; first branch on I.I. Chundrigar Road, Karachi, **November 1959**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Agha Hasan Abedi"; Wikipedia, "United Bank Limited"]**
- UBL becomes **the second-largest bank in Pakistan** under him. **[A: Wikipedia]** No source dates that moment; the journey uses a reconstructed 1969, flagged. **[R]**
- Develops the relationship with **Sheikh Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan** that becomes the financial spine of everything after. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- **Factual error corrected.** Both passes dated this to 1965 or "mid-1960s" while calling Zayed "ruler of Abu Dhabi." **Zayed became ruler on 6 August 1966**, replacing his brother Shakhbut (ruler since 1928), and became UAE president 2 December 1971. **[A: Wikipedia, "Zayed bin Sultan Al Nahyan"]** The journey uses 6 August 1966; any pre-1966 contact is **undocumented**. **[R]**
- The account of the relationship as beginning with **Pakistani labour remittances** and growing into a private treasury for the Al Nahyan family is **[R]**: plausible, not attested.

## 3. The bank with no country (1972-1981)

- **BCCI incorporated in Luxembourg, 1972**, roughly **US$2.5 million** starting capital; shareholding **Bank of America 25 percent, Sheikh Zayed 75 percent**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Bank of Credit and Commerce International"]**
- **Operational head office in London** from the same year, splitting the institution across two jurisdictions from birth. **[A: same]** The Leadenhall Street address is **[R]** as to the street.
- **Structure and auditing corrected.** The pool wrote that "BCCI Holdings" was registered in the Caymans; the source says the reverse, the group dividing into **BCCI Holdings**, under which sat **BCCI SA (Luxembourg)** and **BCCI Overseas (Grand Cayman)**. **Price Waterhouse audited BCCI Overseas; Ernst and Young audited BCCI and BCCI Holdings**, consolidated only in **1987**. **[A: same]**
- **By 1976: from 19 branches in five countries to 108 branches; assets from about $200 million to $1.6 billion**; **acquires Banque de Commerce et Placements, Geneva, 1976**; **into African markets, 1979** (no country list); **into the Shenzhen Special Economic Zone in the early 1980s**, among the first foreign banks in. **[A: same]** Shenzhen has no year in the source; the journey uses 1981, flagged. **[R]**
- **Peak footprint: two incompatible figures, both left standing.** The Abedi article gives **"over 72 countries," about 1.3 million depositors, about 16,000 employees**; the bank article gives **"more than four hundred branches in seventy-eight countries"**; Britannica gives **around one million depositors** affected at closure. **Flagged, not resolved.**

## 4. The invisible (1978-1990)

The only phase where Abedi speaks in recorded words; the Senate report is the sole carrier.

- **Financial Times, 17 May 1978**, "The man who adds a touch of mysticism to banking": *"Western Banks concentrate on the visible, whereas we stress the invisible."* **[A: *The BCCI Affair*, Ch. 3, n. 47, citing FT 17 May 1978, S. Hrg. 102-350 Pt. 3 p. 303]** **Correction:** the pool attributed this to *Euromoney*, summer 1978; footnote 47 says *Financial Times*. Wording **verified verbatim** by direct fetch.
- **Euromoney, July 1978**, on the refused licence: *"The Bank of England probably hasn't given permission because of the atmosphere surrounding the BCCI and the propaganda that has been spread about us. . . It is not only the Bank of England that is against us, but the Club."* **[A: same, Ch. 3, n. 70, Pt. 3 p. 308]** **Correction:** the sentences are **non-contiguous**; the pool joined them silently. Ellipsis restored.
- Internal management address, undated: *"IS BCC A DESIRE, OR IS BCC A BANK?"* **[A: same, Ch. 3, n. 62]**
Seen only through summarized fetch of the same chapter, therefore **[R]**: on the Bank of America stake, *"Bank of America agreed to become a shareholder, but we made it a condition that we would establish the management style"*; an address to forty-five managers in 1985 ("do not nip the flower in the bud"); a late-1972 Abu Dhabi remark thanking God for the livelihood of UBL's employees. The last two carry internal ellipses indicating condensation and are not used in the journey.

About him rather than by him, same status **[R]**: **Massihur Rahman** (CFO, 7 August 1991), *"I remember looking into his eyes and seeing God and the Devil balanced equally in them"*; **Abdur Sakhia** (7 October 1991), *"I said, Mr. Abedi you are making things very difficult ... He started crying"*; **Akbar Bilgrami** (13 July 1992), reporting a saying that a banker who cannot make money for himself cannot make money for the bank.

### First American Bankshares

- **1982: BCCI-linked clients buy Financial General Bankshares, renamed First American; Clark Clifford is installed as chairman to satisfy regulators while BCCI controls it behind him.** **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI and "Clark Clifford"]**
- The hidden holding was **approximately 60 percent or more**, through Arab nominees and pledged stock, exposed by a **1990 Price Waterhouse audit**. **Date range corrected:** the geography pass gave "1978-1991"; Clifford chaired First American **1982 to 1991**, and 1978 belongs to the earlier takeover battle. **[A: Wikipedia, "Clark Clifford"]**
- **Bert Lance**, testifying on Clifford's due diligence for him: *"Every instance o[r] report that I either got or from what Mr. Clifford told me came back that Mr. Abedi was a man of integrity and character."* **[A as text: *The BCCI Affair*, Ch. 13, n. 6]** **Correction:** the pool quoted the tail clause as Lance's own free-standing verdict.
- **Clark Clifford**, on first meeting Abedi, December 1977: *"I found him to be pleasant and a man of importance."* **[R]** Summarized fetch only.

### The client list

- Accounts tied to the **Abu Nidal Organization** ran through the **Sloane Street** branch, via the private client and "special duties" departments. **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI]** Ties to **Colombian cartels** and dealings involving the **CIA** were reported in July 1991. **[R: Britannica]**
- **Manuel Noriega** held BCCI accounts **[A: QUEUE.md 238]**; the Panama branch-level handling is **[R]**, no document reached. Iraqi weapons-procurement financing and Libyan state funds through BCCI are documented **outside this corpus** and are **[R]** here.
- **Abedi's personal role in vetting any specific account is not established by anything reached.** The institution's willingness to hold them is. The journey file preserves that distinction.

## 5. Infaq: the money turned back toward Pakistan (1980-1993)

- **1980: founds BCCI FAST with a Rs. 100 million donation**; it survives the bank as the **National University of Computer and Emerging Sciences**, six campuses. **[A: Wikipedia, "Agha Hasan Abedi"]**
- **Location corrected.** Both passes sited this in **Karachi** with two different sets of invented coordinates. Wikipedia puts the headquarters in **Islamabad, sector G-9, from the outset**, gives the original name as the **FAST Institute of Computer Science**, and dates the **National University's inauguration to July 2000**. **[A: Wikipedia, NUCES]** The journey re-sites to Islamabad, sector-level pin.
- **The Infaq Foundation** runs from a **single Karachi office** with **capital and reserves above Rs. 2.50 billion**; **Ghulam Ishaq Khan chaired it 1983-1995** and was **President of Pakistan from 1988**. **[A: Wikipedia, Abedi]** No meeting between the two men is documented; the political relationship is inferred from the chairmanship alone. **[R]**
- **GIK Institute, Topi, Swabi, founded 1993**, Infaq donation **Rs. 750 million**, plus **Rs. 50 million in December 1985**; the **Agha Hasan Abedi Auditorium seats 535**. **[A: Wikipedia, GIKI]**
- **Coordinate contradiction, resolved to the source.** Geography gave 34.0755 / 72.32; afterlife gave 34.0687 / 72.6449; Wikipedia gives 34°04′07″N 72°38′42″E, i.e. **34.0687 / 72.6449**. The geography figure is about 30 km wrong and is discarded.
- **SIUT** is listed among Infaq beneficiaries in the Abedi article **[A]**, but **SIUT's own article credits Adeebul Hasan Rizvi, dates the department to 1970 and autonomy to 1991, and never mentions Infaq.** **Flagged, not resolved.** It is "near Civil hospital Karachi," adjacent to and not inside the complex, contrary to the geography pass. **[A: Wikipedia, SIUT]**
- **NICVD** and **Lady Dufferin Hospital** are likewise listed beneficiaries; Lady Dufferin was **established 1898**, at approximately **24.8583 / 67.0078**, some 3 km from the pool's figure. **[A: Wikipedia, Abedi; Wikipedia, Lady Dufferin Hospital]** Neither is a separate stop; both are named inside the Infaq stop, nothing tying Abedi personally to either site.

## 6. C-Chase, and the failing heart (1986-1990)

- **1986: US Customs opens Operation C-Chase in Tampa**, led by Special Agent **Robert Mazur**, documenting solicitation of drug-trafficker deposits. **1988: the two-year operation ends with a fake wedding**; officers indicted. **1990: after a six-month trial BCCI pleads guilty on respondeat superior only**, paying **$10 million** and forfeiting **$550 million in American assets**. **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI]**
- **Correction.** The chronology pass compressed the sting's end and the plea into one 1988 event. They are two years apart and are split into two stops.
- **1988: heart transplant; leaves active management on health grounds.** **[A: Wikipedia, Abedi]**
- **The transplant location is unattested.** No hospital, no city. The geography pass supplied London coordinates twice, once at the city centre and once at Northwood, tagging one attested; both are conjecture. The journey keeps a London pin flagged as unsourced. **[R]**
- **1990: a heart attack; formally severs his connection with BCCI** **[A: Wikipedia, Abedi]**; and **after a Price Waterhouse audit reveals losses in the hundreds of millions, Zayed makes the loss good for an increased shareholding of 78 percent** **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI]**, where the geography pass wrote "roughly seventy-seven percent."
- **Further correction.** The geography pass wrote that Abedi was "incapacitated by his 1988 heart attack." 1988 is the transplant and departure; the heart attack is 1990. Both, not one.

## 7. Five July (1991-1992)

- **March 1991: the Bank of England requests a Price Waterhouse inquiry.** **24 June 1991: the "Sandstorm report" reaches the Bank of England**, finding widespread fraud and manipulation; losses estimated **$10-17 billion**. **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI]**
- **5 July 1991: regulators in seven countries seize BCCI simultaneously; a Luxembourg court orders liquidation; roughly one million depositors locked out.** Offices entered at **1pm London, 8am New York**. **7 July: Hong Kong closes its branches; liquidation 17 July.** **[A: same; Britannica]**
- **29 July 1991: a Manhattan grand jury indictment against Abedi and chief executive Swaleh Naqvi on twelve counts.** **[A: same]** The attribution to DA **Robert Morgenthau** personally is **[R]**: widely reported, not sourced in this pass.
- **The Kerry and Brown report was published December 1992.** **[A: *The BCCI Affair*]** Hearings attested; **Abedi never testified**, and no source records an attempt to compel him.
- **The Bingham Inquiry** into the Bank of England's supervision **reported October 1992**. **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI]** **Venue correction:** the geography pass placed it at the **Royal Courts of Justice** on coordinates inferred from Bingham's judicial title. It was a **government inquiry, not a proceeding of that court**, and no source names its sitting address; the journey pin is generic London, flagged unlocated. **[R]**
- **Arithmetic flagged, not corrected.** The geography pass wrote of "nineteen years" of failed supervision. A bank founded 1972 and closed July 1991 reaches nineteen only counted inclusively, and the Bank of England had refused it a licence in 1978. No source reached states the figure either way.

## 8. The defendant who cannot be fetched (1994-2023)

- **A UAE court convicts Abedi and sentences him to eight years.** **[R: Britannica]** No precise date; the pool offered 1994 and "1994-1995," and the journey uses 1994 with the hedge in `date_confidence`. Whether the trial was formally in absentia is **not stated anywhere reached**.
- **At his death he remained under indictment in the US and UK; Pakistan declined to extradite, calling the prosecutions politically motivated.** **[R: Wikipedia summary]** **No trial or conviction of Abedi ever occurred in either country.** **[A]**
- **Dies 5 August 1995 at Aga Khan University Hospital, Karachi, aged 73.** **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]** **Cause-of-death contradiction inside a single source, flagged:** the Wikipedia article says **"heart attack" in one passage and "heart failure" in another**, so the afterlife pass's claim that heart failure is given "consistently across sources" fails even within the one article cited. Not resolved.
- **Coordinate contradiction, resolved to the source.** The pool holds three pins for one hospital: 24.8909 / 67.0797, 24.8934 / 67.0821, 24.8933 / 67.0744. Wikipedia gives 24°53′36″N 67°04′28″E, i.e. **24.8933 / 67.0744**, which the journey carries.
- **Gap: the burial site is unknown.** No cemetery, grave, or marker is named anywhere reached; obituaries stop at the death, and a Find a Grave lookup returned **HTTP 403**. The journey stop states the gap rather than filling it. **Gap: no folk tradition or shrine practice is attached to Abedi in anything reached**, an absence in the sources checked rather than a finding that none exists.
- **2015: posthumously awarded the Hilal-i-Imtiaz.** **[A: Wikipedia, Abedi]** **Rank correction:** the afterlife pass called it the highest civilian honour. It is the **second grade in the Order of Imtiaz**; the highest is the **Nishan-i-Imtiaz**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Hilal-i-Imtiaz"]**
- **Clark Clifford** was indicted over First American; charges **dropped 1993** on health grounds; in **1998 he and Robert Altman settled with the Federal Reserve for $5 million**; he **died 10 October 1998, aged 91, buried at Arlington**. **[A: Wikipedia, "Clark Clifford"]** His remark *"I have a choice of either seeming stupid or venal"* is **[R]**: reported without a dated citation.
- **March 2023: Acting Comptroller of the Currency Michael J. Hsu states there are strong parallels between FTX and BCCI.** **[A: Wikipedia, BCCI]** The pool's anonymous "regulators and commentators" is replaced by the named speaker.
