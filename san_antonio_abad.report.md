# San Antonio Abad (Antony the Great): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug `san_antonio_abad`. Queue item 8: "Anthony the Great, Egyptian desert hermit, father of monasticism."*

Antony of Egypt, c. 251 to 356. Gave away an inherited estate at about twenty, learned the discipline from local ascetics for some fifteen years, shut himself in a derelict Roman fort for twenty more, came out in 305 to find he had become a movement, withdrew to a mountain near the Red Sea, and died at a hundred and five having arranged his burial so that nobody could find the grave.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or hagiography whose genre does not claim documentary status. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Every date hedge in the journey file lives in `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

**The one-source problem, up front.** The Vita Antonii is the sole substantial narrative witness, and Athanasius was not disinterested: Antony's denunciation of the Arians is the hinge of his own career. Where a claim rests only on the Vita it is marked [A] as *attested in Athanasius*, which is not the same as corroborated. There is no second contemporary life, no papyrus dossier, no archaeology tied to his person.

## 1. Coma: birth, inheritance, the Gospel (c. 251 to c. 271)

- Born c. 251 to wealthy Christian landowners **[A, Vita Antonii 1: "by descent an Egyptian," of good family and considerable wealth]**. The year is not in the Vita; the "12 January 251" of the reference works is a back-formation from the feast day.
- The village name **Coma** is **[R]**, not [A]. A full-text search of the NPNF translation at newadvent.org/fathers/2811.htm returns **zero hits for "Coma."** The name comes from Sozomen and later tradition. The research pool cited Athanasius for it; that citation is wrong.
- **Location corrected.** The pool pinned Coma at 29.0856, 30.9344, which is Herakleopolis Magna itself, matching en.wikipedia to five decimals. Qiman al-Arus, the usual modern identification, is at **29.3000, 31.1694**, about 33 km north-east. The journey uses the corrected pin for all four village stops.
- The pool's "close to the Thebaid" is **wrong** and is dropped: Qiman al-Arus is in Beni Suef Governorate, Middle Egypt, and the Thebaid begins some 250 km south. Wikipedia separately places Koma in *Lower* Egypt. Neither label is adopted; the journey gives a distance instead.
- Parents die when he is "about eighteen or twenty" **[A, Vita 1-2]**. 269 is arithmetic from the reconstructed birth.
- The Gospel of the rich young man read in church, the land signed over to the villagers **[A, Vita 2]**; a second reading, the movables sold, the sister placed with faithful virgins **[A, Vita 3]**. Athanasius separates the two by no interval, so the journey merges them.
- Apprenticeship to the old ascetic "in the next village," and the prudent-bee simile **[A, Vita 3, verbatim]**. The fifteen-year duration is **[R]**, reconstructed from narrative sequence by Britannica and Wikipedia.
- The tombs and the beast-shaped demons **[A, Vita 8-10]**: lions, bears, leopards, bulls, serpents, asps, scorpions, wolves, then the carrying to the village church. Undated.

## 2. Pispir: the fort (c. 285 to 305)

- Crossing the Nile east to a derelict Roman fort, c. 285-286, at about thirty-five **[A for the crossing, Vita 11-12; R for the year]**.
- **Coordinate confirmed.** 29.2276, 31.2185 matches Wikidata Q12212334 (Dayr al-Maymun) to four decimals, east bank as required. The only pool coordinate needing no correction.
- **The soldier friend is invented.** The pool had Antony ask a soldier to seal him in; Vita 12 says the opposite: "he built up the entrance completely, having stored up loaves for six months," and thereafter received loaves "let down from above, twice in the year."
- "Nearly twenty years he continued training himself in solitude" **[A, Vita 12]**; the voices heard through the wall **[A as narrated, Vita 13]**.
- 305: the door wrenched off by **acquaintances and would-be imitators**, not by villagers as the pool had it **[A, Vita 14, quoted verbatim in the journey]**. Christian History Institute gives 306. **Contradiction flagged.**
- The colony of cells, the healings and exorcisms credited to prayer and the name of Christ, no written rule **[A, Vita 14-15]**.

## 3. Alexandria under Maximinus (311)

- Antony serves the confessors in prisons and mines, attends the tribunal, hopes to be taken **[A, Vita 46]**. Athanasius names the persecution under Maximinus; 311 is the standard historical date **[R for the year]**.
- The washed cloak, and standing in plain sight after the governor bans monks from the tribunal **[A, Vita 46]**.
- Return to the outer mountain **[A, Vita 46-47]**, undated.

## 4. The inner mountain (c. 313 to 356)

- Withdrawal east with Saracen traders to a mountain with a spring and wild palms **[A, Vita 49-50]**. 313 comes from Christian History Institute and Wikipedia, not from Athanasius.
- **Coordinate confirmed**: 28.9239, 32.35. "Roughly forty kilometres inland" checks at about 37 km from the Gulf of Suez.
- **Two pool errors corrected.** (a) "What Athanasius counts as the last forty-five years" is not Athanasius; 45 is Wikipedia's figure and does not reconcile with the entry's own c.313 arrival (356 less 313 is 43). The journey says forty-three and owns the arithmetic. (b) "A natural cave partway up the slope" is not in Vita 49-50, which says only that he "abode in the mountain alone." The cave is later tradition.
- The garden, the wheat, the wild donkeys rebuked in words **[A as narrated, Vita 50]**; the thirteenth-century pilgrim's three springs and palm grove **[A, Wikipedia]**.
- Frequent crossings back to Pispir, disciples crossing out on a three-day walk **[A, Vita 15, 49]**. The mid-route pin 29.075, 31.785 is an **[R]** convenience marking the corridor, not a site.
- Letters to and from Constantine, Constantius and Constans **[A, Vita 81]**; 337 is approximate placement.

## 5. Alexandria against the Arians (338), and the philosophers

- Second visit, public denunciation of Arianism, crowds and conversions **[A, Vita 69-71]**. Christian History Institute gives 338, other chronologies 337. **Contradiction flagged.**
- **Three pool embellishments fail against the text and are dropped.** (a) "At Bishop Athanasius's urging": Vita 69 has a collective summons, "the bishops and all the brethren." (b) "More people came than for any spectacle the city had staged" is absent; ch. 70 has "as many became Christians in those few days as one would have seen made in a year." (c) "He leaves before nightfall each day" appears nowhere in the Vita.
- **The Nitria visit is [R], not [A]**, correcting the pool's tag. It rests on the Christian History Institute timeline alone, which the pool's own note admits was never checked against the primary text. Nitria appears once in the Vita, ch. 60, as Amoun's home "thirteen days' journey" away, learned of by vision and not visited. The journey keeps the stop and says so in the campa.
- **"Thirteen years in the Nitrian Desert" is almost certainly a corruption of "thirteen days' journey,"** not a rival tradition as first framed. That is an **[R]** judgement about transmission, presented as such.
- **The philosophers' debate is at the OUTER mountain.** Vita 72 heading: "How he reasoned with divers Greeks and philosophers at the 'outer' mountain." The pool pinned it at the inner; the journey moves it to 29.2276, 31.2185. Also: Athanasius does not say the visitors came from Alexandria, and in ch. 80 the demoniacs were already present, not brought as a test.

## 6. Paul of Thebes (c. 341, Jerome only)

Everything here is **[R]**, and the journey says so in its own prose.

- The dream, the road, the centaur and satyr, the raven with the whole loaf, the two lions digging the grave, the palm-leaf tunic at Easter and Pentecost: **[R, Jerome, Vita Pauli 7-16]**, none of it in Athanasius. Jerome's book was written partly to move the title of first monk from Antony to Paul, which is a motive, not a disqualification.
- **The Catholic Encyclopedia's article on Antony excludes the episode entirely**, which is itself evidence about reception.
- Paul's death given as "around 341" in one summary and 342 in another. **Unresolved.**
- **"Forty kilometres apart" corrected.** Monastery of Saint Anthony (28.9239, 32.35) to Monastery of Saint Paul (28.8467, 32.5517) is about 21 km great-circle, roughly 80 km by road. Forty matches neither. The journey says twenty in a straight line, eighty by road.

## 7. Death and burial (356)

- Farewell at the outer mountain, "I am near a hundred and five years old" **[A, Vita 89]**. The pool had him warn "his two attending disciples in advance of the day"; the Vita has him warn the *outer* mountain monks, then fall sick "after a few months" at the inner one.
- **Bequests corrected.** Vita 91 verbatim: "To Athanasius the bishop give one sheepskin and the garment whereon I am laid, which he himself gave me new, but which with me has grown old. To Serapion the bishop give the other sheepskin, and keep the hair garment yourselves." The hair shirt stays with the two disciples; the pool sent it to the outer-mountain monks. Athanasius gets sheepskin and mantle together.
- Explicit rejection of the Egyptian custom of keeping and wrapping bodies **[A, Vita 90-91]**.
- Age 105 **[A, Vita 89]**. 17 January is the liturgical feast day, not a date in Athanasius. "About eighty-five years in ascetic withdrawal" is **[R] arithmetic**, 105 minus roughly 20; the journey says "by simple subtraction."
- **The two disciples are not named by Athanasius.** Vita 91: "they were two in number who had remained in the mountain fifteen years." Macarius and Amathas come from **Jerome, Vita Pauli, prologue**, a different author with a polemical interest. The pool attributed the names twice to "Athanasius, Life of Antony 89-91," which that text cannot carry. **The journey does not name them.**
- Secret burial **[A, Vita 92: "no one knows to this day where it was buried, save those two only"]**. The pool's "no source, ancient or modern, claims to know the location" is contradicted by the pool itself, which carries both a relic-discovery tradition and a local tradition putting the tomb under the monastery church. The journey calls its pin a guess at a location engineered to have none.
- Athanasius writes the Life c. 356-360; 357 here after Christian History Institute. **Composition date debated, not resolved.**

## 8. Afterlife: text, bones, fire

- Two early Latin translations: the anonymous literal one in a single complete manuscript, and Evagrius of Antioch's freer version finished by 373-374 in roughly 170 manuscripts **[R, Corpus Christianorum 2019; Oxford Cult of Saints E00930]**. Earliest surviving manuscript of either is ninth-century, a gap of about 450 years.
- Augustine hears the Life recounted at Milan in 386 **[A, Confessions VIII]**; the fig tree in the garden is his own scene, ch. VIII.12, and the baptism at Easter 387 is **[A, Confessions IX]**. The causal weight he gives the Antony story is his own framing and the journey does not extend it.
- **Relic itineraries: two mutually exclusive chains, both preserved.** (i) Grave revealed and body translated to Alexandria, dated 361 in one telling and 561 in another, then Constantinople, then France. (ii) Coptic tradition: the body never left the eastern desert. **Neither verified; the journey resolves neither.** The Catholic Encyclopedia is silent on any translation.
- "635" for the Constantinople transfer is the **source's** figure (johnsanidopoulos.com) and is wrong: the Arab conquest of Egypt began December 639 and ended 641-642, and 635 is the fall of Damascus. Flagged in `date_confidence`, the source's year retained rather than silently corrected.
- Jocelin de Chateauneuf brings relics to La Motte-Saint-Didier **[R, foundation legend]**. fr.wikipedia dates his departure "vers 1074," not 1070; johnsanidopoulos instead has relics given to a Jocelin in 980 under Basil II. **Contradiction flagged.** The undocumented gap is 635 to 980, or 640 to 1074, not seven unbroken centuries. **Coordinate corrected**: Saint-Antoine-l'Abbaye is at **45.1758, 5.2172**, not the 45.2331, 5.3322 the pool repeated at three indices, which sits 11.5 km north-east near Saint-Marcellin.
- Saint Anthony's fire, later identified as ergotism; Gaston of Valloire founds the Hospital Brothers c. 1095, confirmed by Urban II that year; Honorius III sanctions 1218; Rule of Augustine 1248; canons regular 1297; about 370 hospitals at their height **[A, Wikipedia, confirmed date by date]**.
- **1297 is institutional, not architectural.** The Gothic church began "vers 1280" and was built in phases into the mid-fifteenth century over a Romanesque predecessor of c. 1088. On 10 June 1297 Boniface VIII raised the priory to abbey, made it mother house, and installed Aymon de Montagne as first abbot.
- **Arles: wrong year and wrong cause in the pool.** The relics were allegedly deposited at Saint-Julien on **9 January 1490**, not 1491, by the monks of Montmajour in a custody dispute. The Wars of Religion cannot be the motive; they began in 1562. At the Revolution the treasures went sealed to Grenoble and were largely preserved and forgotten, not lost.
- The pig is **[R]**: the standard explanation is the Antonine privilege of letting bell-collared pigs forage in town streets to feed the sick poor. No originating document located; absent from Athanasius.
- Isenheim Altarpiece 1512-1516, ergotism symptoms painted for patients being treated for the disease, now at the Unterlinden **[A]**. Flaubert's *La Tentation de saint Antoine* (1874) is in the pool and **not** in the journey: no site attaches to it, and the atlas needs a pin.
- **Monastery of Saint Anthony: two pool over-reaches pulled back.** (a) The cited article gives no year 1454, only "in the late 15th century." (b) "Oldest continuously occupied monastery in the world" is contested and is not made by the cited source, which awards that to Saint Catherine's. The $14.5m eight-year restoration begun 2002 is confirmed. The 1232-1233 mural date is **not** in Wikipedia, which says only 13th century, so the journey says thirteenth century.
- **The monastery's foundation date is a live contradiction.** One account dates the settlement to 298-300, which would precede Antony's arrival and make it not his community at all; Athanasius implies consolidation only after his death. **Unresolved**, flagged in the last stop.

## Contradictions carried into the journey unresolved

Fort door broken 305 or 306. Second Alexandria visit 337 or 338. Paul of Thebes dead 341 or 342. Relics discovered 361, or 561, or never (Coptic tradition; Catholic Encyclopedia silent). Constantinople transfer: the source says 635, history says 639-642. Jocelin c. 1074 or 980. Monastery of Saint Anthony founded 298-300 or after 356. Justinian's Sinai foundation 548-565 generally, 555 in `catherine_alexandria.journey.json`, 556 here, an atlas-internal inconsistency left visible. Coma in Middle Egypt or Lower Egypt; neither label adopted.

## Honest gaps

- **No independent contemporary witness to anything before 311.** The fort years in particular have exactly one source, who was not there.
- **The seven Letters of Antony**, surviving in Coptic, Georgian, Latin and Arabic and the only text plausibly by his own hand, were not examined. Largest hole in the dossier. **Palladius and the systematic Apophthegmata** were likewise not worked through; the quotes here all come from Ward's alphabetical collection.
- **No archaeology** is tied to Antony personally. Pispir, the cave at Mount Galala and the burial site are all traditional identifications, and only Pispir has a formalized coordinate.
- **Nitria's pin (30.9333, 30.35)** is this journey's own approximation for el-Barnugi, not inherited. It is the only invented pin in the file besides the two deliberately vague ones, the desert corridor and the grave.
- **ARCE's conservation reporting on the murals returned HTTP 403.** Hence "thirteenth century" rather than 1232-1233, and no naming of the painter Theodore.
