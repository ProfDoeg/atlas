# Cecil John Rhodes (1853-1902): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11. Slug `cecil_rhodes`. Journey file: `cecil_rhodes.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops, quotations at 10.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in the bracket. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a compiled account with no primary behind it. Contradictions are flagged in place and **not adjudicated**, except where the correction is itself documented, in which case the rejected reading is named so the error is not silently inherited.

Rhodes is over-documented and badly documented at once. The reference tier a fast pass reaches first carries a stable set of errors, all present in the pool this journey was written from and all corrected below: a departure year one year early, an age at election two years high, a concession seven months early, a protectorate a year early, a will dated by its probate, and coordinates drifting between 0.7 and 110 km at six sites.

## 1. Hertfordshire and the voyage out (1853-1870)

- Born 5 July 1853 at Bishop's Stortford, fifth son of the Reverend Francis William Rhodes and Louisa Peacock Rhodes **[A Wikipedia, "Cecil Rhodes"; Rotberg, *The Founder* (1988)]**. Birthplace the vicarage attached to St Michael's, Church Street **[A Rotberg]**.
- Educated at the local grammar school rather than a major public school; recurrent lung trouble marks him out among his brothers **[R Flint, *Cecil Rhodes* (1976)]**. The schooling is not in dispute; the "delicate son" framing is biographers' shaping. Sent to Natal on medical advice about heart and lungs.

**Coordinate corrected.** The vicarage was pinned at 51.8722, 0.1673, some 700 m east of the church, in open ground. St Michael's is at **51.8708, 0.1575** (OpenStreetMap).

**Contradiction resolved, correction documented.** The chronology pass gave the departure as **1869**, aged 16; the geography pass gave **June 1870**, aged 17. He sailed in June 1870, was 16 at departure, turned 17 at sea on 5 July 1870, and landed 1 September 1870 **[A Wikipedia; SAHO, "Cecil John Rhodes"]**. The 1869 reading is rejected as inconsistent with the attested arrival. The journey says June 1870 and sixteen.

**Open contradiction, unresolved: the port.** Rotberg gives Southampton, other biographies Plymouth; the geography pass pinned Plymouth while citing Rotberg, who does not say it. The journey pins **Southampton (50.9097, -1.4044)** and states the divergence. No passenger list was reached. **Gap.**

## 2. Natal and the diamond fields (1870-1873)

- Lands at Durban 1 September 1870; stays initially with Surveyor-General Peter Cormac Sutherland at Pietermaritzburg **[A Wikipedia; SAHO]**.
- Cotton venture with his brother Herbert in the Umkomaas valley; the crop fails **[A Wikipedia for the venture; R Flint for the reasons]**.
- Diamonds found on the Vooruitzigt farm, May 1871; joins Herbert at the diggings October 1871, aged 18; brother Frank returns to the camps January 1872 **[A Wikipedia; SAHO]**.
- First heart attack July 1872; cannot work for nearly eight months; then treks north through Bechuanaland and the Transvaal **[A Wikipedia; SAHO]**. **Gap:** the route is undocumented in anything reached. The stop pins the Bechuanaland leg generically at -24.6282, 25.9231 and says so.

**Coordinate corrected.** The Big Hole was given as -28.7411, 24.7656; the Kimberley Mine is at **-28.7389, 24.7589** (Wikipedia, "Big Hole"). **Claim narrowed:** the pool said Rhodes consolidated "this single pit", where De Beers Consolidated merged the De Beers and Kimberley mines with the Dutoitspan and Bultfontein interests **[A Turrell, *Capital and Labour on the Kimberley Diamond Fields 1871-1890*]**. The campa states the four-mine amalgamation.

## 3. Oxford, in fragments (1873-1881)

- Admitted to Oriel; attends one term before his mother's death and his own health send him back, reaching Kimberley by January 1874 **[A Wikipedia]**. Consolidates claims through the 1874 slump **[R SAHO]**. Returns for a second term in 1876 **[A Wikipedia; SAHO]**. Keeps terms intermittently to 1881 **[R interlock pass, consistent with Rotberg]**.
- Writes the "Confession of Faith", 1877, proposing British expansion and a secret society modelled on the Jesuits **[A SAHO; full text in Stead, *The Last Will and Testament of Cecil Rhodes* (1902), Part II ch. I, via Wikisource]**. Initiated a Freemason at the Apollo University Lodge, June 1877 **[A Wikipedia]**.

**Coordinate divergence, unresolved.** Geography pins Oriel at 51.7505, -1.2529, afterlife at 51.7519, -1.2528; both fall in the college block and nothing arbitrates. The journey uses the first for the 1870s stops and the second for the 2016 statue stop, recording the divergence at both ends. **Gap:** the Apollo Lodge's 1877 meeting place was not established; that stop is pinned to central Oxford and labelled as such.

## 4. The Cape parliament and the road north (1880-1889)

- De Beers Mining Company floated with Charles Rudd, April 1880 **[R Rotberg]**. Elected for Barkly West in 1880, seated 1881, holds it until death **[A Wikipedia; SAHO; Flint]**. **Error corrected:** SAHO says "age 29"; born July 1853, he was **27**, as Wikipedia gives.
- Co-founds the Kimberley Club, 1881 **[R Turrell]**. No stop: no dated event.
- Lobbies London over Bechuanaland, 1884 **[A SAHO]**. **Error corrected:** SAHO puts the protectorate in 1884. It was declared **31 March 1885** at the close of the Warren Expedition; British Bechuanaland became a separate Crown colony in **September 1885** **[A Wikipedia, "Bechuanaland Protectorate"]**. The journey separates lobbying from act.
- Rothschild financing negotiated at New Court for the amalgamation and then the charter **[R Rotberg]**.
- De Beers Consolidated Mines founded with Rudd, financed by N. M. Rothschild and Sons **[A Wikipedia; SAHO]**. **Open contradiction, unresolved:** Wikipedia gives **13 March 1888**, SAHO **April 1888**. The journey uses 13 March for ordering and states both.
- The Rudd Concession. **Error corrected, false hedge removed.** The pool presented "SAHO March 1888" against "Wikipedia 1888-1895" as a source divergence. It is an error, not a divergence: the concession was signed **30 October 1888 at Bulawayo**, by Lobengula to Rudd, James Rochfort Maguire and Francis Thompson for Rhodes **[A Wikipedia, "Rudd Concession"]**. Hedging would have manufactured uncertainty where none exists.
- Royal Charter of the British South Africa Company granted **29 October 1889** **[A SAHO for the day; Wikipedia gives the year; Flint for the Colonial Office lobbying]**.

## 5. Premier and chartered company (1890-1895)

- Prime Minister of the Cape from July 1890 (Rotberg: sworn in 17 July) to 12 January 1896 **[A Wikipedia; SAHO; Rotberg]**.
- **Error corrected.** The chronology pass dated the *dispatch* of the Pioneer Column to 13 September 1890. It left Macloutsie **28 June 1890**, crossed the Tuli **11 July**, halted at the Fort Salisbury site **12 September**, and the flag went up **13 September** under Lieutenant Edward Tyndale-Biscoe **[A Wikipedia, "Pioneer Column"]**. Two stops, muster and flag. **Coordinate corrected:** Macloutsie was pinned at -22.5333, 29.1167, south of the Limpopo inside present-day South Africa; the camp stood on the Macloutsie (Motloutse) river in the Protectorate, near **-22.138, 28.836** (OpenStreetMap).
- Rhodes was **not present** at the flag-raising **[A Rotberg]**. Kept in the campa; it is the most telling single fact about how the country was taken.
- Franchise and Ballot Act 1892: qualification raised from £25 to £75, disproportionately disenfranchising Black voters **[A Wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no month reached.
- Glen Grey Act: speech 27 July 1894, law in August; individual tenure to break communal holding, plus a labour tax **[A SAHO; Wikipedia]**.
- First Matabele War: BSAC forces defeat Lobengula, November 1893; the royal town found burned, the king fled **[A Flint]**. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool pinned "GuBulawayo" at -19.15, 28.7833, about 110 km too far north. Old Bulawayo is at **-20.3023, 28.6264** (Wikipedia, "Old Bulawayo"); the town burned in 1893 was Lobengula's later Bulawayo, on the site of the modern city, near **-20.15, 28.58**, which the journey uses while naming the alternative.
- Colonial Bulawayo laid out 1894; Zambesia renamed Rhodesia May 1895 **[A Rotberg; Wikipedia]**.
- **Groote Schuur: within-pool conflict resolved by the better source.** Geography said bought **1891** at -33.9631, 18.4614; afterlife said **1893** for £60,000 at -33.9638, 18.4640, with the 1896 fire and Baker's rebuild **[A Wikipedia, "Groote Schuur"]**. The afterlife values are used.
- Great Zimbabwe: Rhodes funds early excavation and promotes the ancient-outsider theory **[A Wikipedia; R Garlake, *Great Zimbabwe* (1973), for the excavation history]**. **Gap:** his personal visit is asserted only in later company-era accounts and is not documented to any date; the stop carries that hedge.

## 6. The Raid, the fall, the indabas (1895-1897)

- Jameson Raid launched from Pitsani Potlugo 29 December 1895; the intended Uitlander rising does not happen **[A Wikipedia; SAHO; Rotberg]**.
- **Open contradiction, flagged.** The chronology pass and Wikipedia's list of Cape premiers give **12 January 1896** for the end of the premiership, Sprigg taking office 13 January; the geography pass gives **6 January 1896** for the resignation. Six January may be the day he tendered it. The journey uses 12 January and records the other reading.
- Second Matabele War 1896: Rhodes goes to Matabeleland, appoints himself a colonel, takes an active part in suppression **[R Wikipedia]**. Negotiates peace with Ndebele chiefs in the Matobo indabas, August 1896 **[A Wikipedia]**. That he walked in **unarmed** is **[R Flint]**, the load-bearing element of the imperial canon's version of the scene; kept, marked as reconstruction.
- Chooses Malindidzimu as his grave during the same talks **[A Rotberg; and the will clause]**. **Coordinate corrected twice:** geography gave -20.5486, 28.4986 and afterlife -20.5423, 28.4936, both 5 to 6 km south-southwest of the hill. Malindidzimu is at **-20.4974, 28.5202** (OpenStreetMap); applied at all three World's View stops.
- **Claim softened.** The afterlife pass called Malindidzimu a Mwari shrine. The name means "place of benevolent spirits" and the hills do hold Mwari shrines, but the principal one is **Njelele**, a different hill.
- Testifies before the Select Committee on the raid, 1897, protecting Chamberlain and evading disclosure **[R Rotberg]**. **Gap:** "Committee Room 15" and February were not confirmed against the Committee's report.
- Buys and develops the Inyanga estate 1896-97 **[R Rotberg]**. No stop: cut for the 45-stop ceiling.

## 7. Cape to Cairo, the siege, the will, the death (1897-1902)

- Cape to Cairo railway and telegraph scheme; the Victoria Falls bridge, built to his instruction, completed **1905, after his death** **[R Rotberg]**.
- **Two gaps, kept as stops.** Whether Rhodes ever stood at Victoria Falls is tradition with **no dated primary account**, and **no biography places him in Egypt at all**. Both stops carry nominal dates and say in `date_confidence` that the pin marks the scheme, not a journey. A Cape-to-Cairo journey that silently omitted Cairo would be tidier and less honest.
- In Kimberley through the siege, 1899-1900; De Beers supplies water, fortifications and armoured equipment **[A Wikipedia; Rotberg]**.
- **The will: tag, date and place all corrected.** The chronology pass carried the scholarships as **[R]**, "not independently verified", dated **1902**. They are thoroughly documented, so **[A]**, and the main will is dated **1 July 1899** with codicils to March 1902; 1902 is the probate year **[A Wikipedia, "Rhodes Scholarship"; text in Stead, Part I]**. Founded for the British Empire and the United States, with **Germany added by later codicil**. The geography pass pinned the will stop at **Oriel College** while its own claim text described drafting **at Groote Schuur**; the journey pins Groote Schuur.
- Dies of heart failure at Muizenberg 26 March 1902, aged 48 **[A Wikipedia; SAHO; Rotberg]**. **Coordinate corrected twice:** geography gave -34.1058, 18.4703 and afterlife -34.1017, 18.4703; Rhodes Cottage, Main Road, Muizenberg is at **-34.1144, 18.4642** (OpenStreetMap).

## 8. The funeral, the grave, the statues (1902-2021)

- **Locations separated.** The geography pass had the body lying in state **in St George's Cathedral**. It lay in state in the **Houses of Parliament**; the cathedral hosted the **funeral service on 3 April 1902**, after which the procession went down Adderley Street **[A]**. Two stops. **Gap:** the exact days of the lying in state. The funeral train ran north, and at Kimberley practically the whole population is reported to have marched past the car **[A Wikipedia]**; folded into the burial campa for the stop ceiling.
- Buried at Malindidzimu **10 April 1902**; the indunas give the royal salute, Bayete, said to be the first offered to a white man **[A Wikipedia]**. The grave, cut into granite, lies beside Jameson and the 34 of the Shangani Patrol **[A Wikipedia]**.
- Rhodes Memorial dedicated 1912 on Devil's Peak: Herbert Baker, 49 steps for 49 years, G. F. Watts's *Physical Energy*, eight bronze lions by John Macallan Swan, and the inscribed dedication **[A Wikipedia, "Rhodes Memorial"]**. **Gap:** month of dedication not reached; the date defaults to January. Groote Schuur, burned 1896 and rebuilt by Baker in slate, is the official Cape Town residence of prime ministers 1910-1984 and now a museum **[A Wikipedia, "Groote Schuur"]**.
- December 2010, Bulawayo's provincial governor calls the grave an insult to African ancestors; February 2012, ZANU-PF activists demand exhumation; Chief Masuku, the archaeologist Godfrey Mahachi and Robert Mugabe oppose removal, and the grave stays **[A Wikipedia]**.
- 9 April 2015, UCT removes its statue a day after the Council vote **[A Wikipedia, "Rhodes Must Fall"]**; the precipitating March 2015 protest is widely reported but carried as **[R]**. 18 September 2015, the nose is cut from the bust at Rhodes Memorial **[A same]**.
- 19 January 2016, the Oxford Union votes 245-212 for removing the Oriel statue; 29 January the college says it stays, after donors threaten gifts and bequests worth over £100 million. 17 June 2020, Oriel votes toward removal and appoints a commission; 20 May 2021 it decides against on cost and planning despite the commission majority, and roughly 150 academics pledge to withhold teaching **[A Wikipedia, "Statue of Cecil Rhodes, Oxford"]**. One stop.

## 9. Quotations: verified and repaired

All Rhodes quotations come from W. T. Stead, *The Last Will and Testament of Cecil Rhodes* (1902), via Wikisource, which prints the 1877 Confession and the 1899 will in full. Twain comes from Gutenberg etext 2895. Three repairs, applied: (1) **the Matoppos clause is from the main will, not a codicil**, wording verbatim but attribution wrong, cited here as Last Will and Testament, 1 July 1899, Stead Part I; (2) **"You cannot rule South Africa without the Dutch" is not a Rhodes quotation**, Stead reports it in indirect speech in his own tenses, and Rhodes's direct words in the same chapter are **"you cannot govern South Africa by trampling on the Dutch"**, which the journey uses; (3) **the "parcelled out" passage was misquoted and spliced**, Stead reads "what there **is left** of it" and the planets remark is a separate utterance, so the journey quotes them separately at two stops. Noted, not changed: **"So much to do, so little done!"** is usually reproduced inverted; Stead gives this order.

## 10. Interlocks with the existing corpus

Genuine crossings, named in campa:

- **mahatma_gandhi** [A]. Gandhi lands at Durban in 1893, three years into the premiership, at the harbour Rhodes landed at in 1870, and stays 21 years. Named at the Durban landing and at the Glen Grey stop. They never meet, and the journey says so.
- **mark_twain** [A]. Twain's 1895-96 tour passed through South Africa during the premiership and the raid's aftermath; his journey has him at sea from South Africa in August 1896. Two Twain quotations carry the resignation and burial stops.
- **robert_finley** [A]. Finley took the Sierra Leone Company at Freetown as his working model for Liberia: the same chartered-company device Rhodes turned on southern Africa seventy years later, aimed at settlement rather than extraction. Named at the 1889 charter stop.
- **margaret_murray** [A]. Murray sails to South Africa in 1929 for the British Association, into the landscape the memorial now commands. Named at the Rhodes Memorial stop.

Recorded and deliberately unused in campa: **marcus_garvey**, **haile_selassie**, **nelson_mandela** and **joshua_haldeman** are queued, none researched, and the QUEUE's framing of Rhodes as Garvey's opposite pole is an editorial intention rather than a crossing. **muammar_gaddafi**, **mohammad_reza_shah_pahlavi**, **elsa_gindler** and **bethsabee_de_rothschild** each have a South Africa thread: same country, no crossing.

**New canonical pins.** No existing journey file pins Kimberley, Cape Town, Bulawayo or the Matobo Hills; this journey introduces them. Oxford is already canonical (Adam Smith, Locke, Roger Bacon, Morris, Tolkien) and the Oriel stops join that cluster. **Namesake collisions, flagged:** "Rhodes" elsewhere in the corpus means the Greek island (Cicero, Flaubert, Andronicus, Hannibal, Paracelsus, Dido, Savitri Devi) or the historian Richard Rhodes (Fermi, von Neumann).

## 11. Honest gaps

Port of embarkation, June 1870 (Southampton or Plymouth). Route of the 1872-73 convalescent trek, undocumented, so the pin is generic. The Apollo University Lodge's 1877 meeting place. Month of the Franchise and Ballot Act 1892. Whether Rhodes ever stood at Victoria Falls (tradition only). Whether he ever went to Egypt (no source places him there). Whether he personally visited Great Zimbabwe (later company-era assertion only). Exact days of the lying in state, April 1902. Room and month of the 1897 Select Committee testimony. Month of the Rhodes Memorial dedication, 1912.

One further gap is interpretive: historians dispute whether the will's "without regard to race" clause was meant to include non-white candidates **[A Wikipedia notes the dispute]**. The journey sets the 1899 clause beside the 1877 racial claims and does not adjudicate.
