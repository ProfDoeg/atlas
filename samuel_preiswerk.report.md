# Samuel Preiswerk (1799-1871): research report

*Basel Hebraist, editor of* Das Morgenland*, Antistes, maternal grandfather of C. G. Jung.*
*Compiled 2026-08-15. **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.*

**The single most important finding of this pass is a source correction.** The starting point for this man is not German Wikipedia, which is thin, uncited on several key dates, and organised around his church posts. It is the **Allgemeine Deutsche Biographie**, vol. 26 (1888), p. 552, freely readable at `de.wikisource.org/wiki/ADB:Preiswerk,_Samuel`: Preiswerk-centred rather than Jung-centred, denser on nearly every point, and **contradicting de.wikipedia on five separate dates or facts**. Where the two disagree, this file records both and says which it prefers and why.

---

## 1. Origins and education, 1799-1822

- **Born 19 September 1799 in Rümlingen, Basel-Landschaft. [A, ADB 26, p. 552; de.wikipedia]**
- **He is the son of the Reformed pastor of Rümlingen, Alexander Preiswerk. [A, ADB]** This corrects the usual description of him as coming from "a rural family with an old Basel patrician surname". He was a parsonage child.
  > "Preiswerk: Samuel P., Dichter geistlicher Lieder, wurde geboren am 19. September 1799 als Sohn des reformirten Pfarrers Alexander P. zu Rümlingen in Baselland." (ADB 26, p. 552)
- **Coordinate correction. [A]** Rümlingen is at **47.42452 N, 7.84858 E** (de.wikipedia infobox). The pool carried *two different wrong pins* for the one birthplace, 47.419 / 7.785 and 47.4147 / 7.7986, each 4-5 km west of the village. The journey file uses **47.4245, 7.8486**.
- **He studied at three universities, not two: Basel, then Tübingen, then Erlangen. [A, ADB]**
  > "Durch seinen Vater dazu vorbereitet, bezog er die Universität Basel und studirte hier und hernach auch in Tübingen und Erlangen Theologie."
  **Tübingen was absent from every lens of the research pool** and is absent from de.wikipedia. It is restored as a stop.
- **No matriculation year is given by any source read. [gap]** The widely circulated 1818 appears in neither ADB nor de.wikipedia and has no visible origin; it is treated as unsourced and the Basel stop carries `date_confidence: inferred`.
- **Winter semester 1821/22: joins the Erlangen student fraternity. [A, de.wikipedia, citing Ernst Höhne,** *Die Bubenreuther* **II (Erlangen 1936), p. 70]**
  **Name correction. [A, de.wikipedia, 'Burschenschaft der Bubenreuther']** In 1821/22 the body was the *Allgemeine Erlanger Burschenschaft*, founded 1 December 1817. The name *Bubenreuther* arises only around 1833, after the dissolution of 9 May 1833, from the village where the Arminen had met. "Joined the Bubenreuther" in 1821 is an anachronism; the membership itself is solid.
- **Unconsulted. [gap]** Helge Dvorak, *Biographisches Lexikon der Deutschen Burschenschaft* II (2018), pp. 547-548, cited by de.wikipedia: the likeliest place to find his student years dated properly.

## 2. The country curacies, 1822-1829

- **1822: vicariate at Biel-Benken. [A, de.wikipedia]** Coordinate correction: the municipality is at 47.5062, 7.5254, the Benken half at roughly **47.5032, 7.5191**. The pool's 47.489 / 7.51 fell 2 km south, toward the French border.
- **1824: pastor of the Basel orphanage (Waisenhaus). [A, de.wikipedia]** **The 1820s premises could not be identified [gap];** the stop is pinned on the old city centre and says so.
- **1824: marries Charlotte Hopf. 1825: she dies. 1826: marries Augusta Faber. [A, Personenlexikon Baselland]** A genuine addition of this pass. Most retellings say only "his first wife" and "his second wife", which is the vagueness that lets the household anecdotes float free of any record.
- **1828 or 1829: teacher at the Basel Missionshaus. [A, date disputed]**
  - de.wikipedia: **1828**, "teacher at a mission house", subject unstated.
  - ADB: **1829**, "Lehrer der hebr. Sprache am Missionshause daselbst", teacher **of Hebrew**.
  *Not adjudicated.* ADB is preferred in the journey file because the subject it names is what makes this post continuous with the rest of his life; the disagreement sits in `date_confidence`.
  **Coordinate caution. [R]** The mission headquarters is at Missionsstrasse 21 (**c. 47.5636, 7.5794**); the pool's 47.5602 / 7.582 is 450 m off. But the street is named *after* the mission, and **no source read confirms the seminary was at that address in 1828-29**. The stop carries the caution in its own text.
- **1829: writes "Die Sach ist dein, Herr Jesu Christ". [A, de.wikipedia, Werke]** Later at RG 801, EG Württemberg 593, MG 427.

## 3. Muttenz and the Baselland revolution, 1830-1834

This is where the pool's chronology was most wrong.

- **1830: pastor of Muttenz. [A, de.wikipedia]**
- **1832: driven from the parish after refusing to speak prayers favourable to the rising. [A, ADB]**
  > "Aus dieser Stellung wurde er im J. 1832 bei der in Baselland ausgebrochenen Revolution verjagt; er ernährte sich und die Seinen kümmerlich durch Privatunterricht, bis er im J. 1834 als außerordentlicher Professor der alttestamentlichen Exegese und der orientalischen Sprachen an die École de Théologie der evangelischen Gesellschaft in Genf gerufen ward."
  - **The "(July) Revolution" gloss in the pool is an outright error.** ADB says *in Baselland ausgebrochenen Revolution*: the cantonal rising that split Basel-Landschaft from Basel-Stadt, not the French July Revolution of 1830. The mistaken version converts a fight about who governs a valley into a gesture against Paris.
  - de.wikipedia says only "bald darauf" and gives no year, and en.wikipedia's sentence is undated; **ADB's 1832 is the only dated statement** and is followed here.
- **1832-1834: destitute, living on private tutoring. [A, ADB]** Missing from every summary consulted, all three Wikipedias included. The only documented period of poverty in his life. **Where the family lodged is unrecorded [gap];** the stop is pinned on Basel by inference and says so.

## 4. Geneva and the Irvingites, 1834-1837

- **1834: called to the École de Théologie of the Société évangélique de Genève** as *außerordentlicher Professor der alttestamentlichen Exegese und der orientalischen Sprachen*. **[A, ADB; de.wikipedia gives only "teacher".]**
- **Publishes the** *Grammaire hébraïque* **in French; four editions. [A, de/fr.wikipedia]** **No edition consulted, no publication year in any source read. [gap]**
- **1837: loses the post rather than break contact with representatives of Edward Irving. [A, de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**
  > "Seeking to maintain his connection with representatives of Edward Irving his job was lost in 1837." (en.wikipedia, 'Samuel Preiswerk', raw wikitext; the sentence carries no page-level citation there.)
- **The Irving-to-Zionism causal chain is NOT documented and has been dropped. [R, rejected]** The pool's strongest interpretive move was to route his restorationism through Irvingite eschatology. **No source located makes that link.** de.wikipedia confirms the contact and the dismissal and never mentions Palestine; the sources on his Zionism never mention Irving. The journey file leaves the connection unmade.
- **The Society's minutes for 1837, and any account Preiswerk wrote of the break, were not obtained. [gap]**

## 5. *Das Morgenland*, 1838-1843

- **Founded June 1838, ceases 1843, six annual volumes. [A, ADB]**
  > "In der stellungslosen Zeit gab P. eine Monatsschrift heraus, deren Inhalt sich aus dem Titel ergibt: 'Das Morgenland, Altes und Neues für Freunde der heiligen Schrift'. Es erschienen sechs Jahrgänge vom Juni 1838 bis zum Jahre 1843."
  The "five-year run" repeated in the pool and in de.wikipedia **undercounts**: six *Jahrgänge* across roughly five and a half years. Note ADB's framing, *in der stellungslosen Zeit*: he started it while out of work.
- **In the journal he supported the settlement of Palestine by the Jews. [A, Personenlexikon Baselland]** Verbatim: he *"unterstützte darin die Idee der Besiedlung Palästinas durch die Juden"*. **This upgrades the pool's central Zionism claim from [R] to [A]** and identifies the vehicle, which the pool could only guess at. An edoc.unibas.ch record independently calls him *"ein Basler Vorläufer des Zionismus"*.
- **No sentence of his own on Palestine is quoted anywhere in this file. [gap, and the important one.]** Nobody in this pass opened a volume of *Das Morgenland*. The evidentiary base is "two present-day reference works say the journal said it": enough to state as fact, not enough to characterise his argument or his audience.
- **The "decades before Herzl" framing checks out arithmetically. [A]** *Das Morgenland* 1838-43; *Der Judenstaat* 1896; First Zionist Congress, Basel, 1897.
- **1844: two friends publish sixteen of his hymns anonymously in the** *Evangelischer Liederkranz aus älterer und neuerer Zeit* **(Basel). [A, ADB, corroborated by Hymnary.org.]** Albert Knapp took nine into his *Liederschatz*, 2nd ed. 1850 **[A, both agree]**.
- **The "second hymn" doubt in the pool is retired. [A]** "Hark, The Church Proclaims Her Honour" is genuine: original *"Dies ist der Gemeine Stärke"*, translated by Catherine Winkworth, *Lyra Germanica*, second series (1858), thence *Psalms & Hymns* (Bedford, 1859) and the *Ohio Lutheran Hymnal* (1880), per Hymnary.org. The stray 1844 date is the Liederkranz; de.wikipedia's Werke list is headed *Auswahl*, so its silence proved nothing.

## 6. St Leonhard, the lectureship, the household, 1839/1843-1858

**Two dates are in open contradiction and are left so in the journey file.**

| | de.wikipedia | ADB |
|---|---|---|
| Lectureship | **1839**, Dozent für hebräische Sprache und Literatur | **1843**, Licentiat der Theologie und Privatdocent für alttestamentliche Exegese |
| St Leonhard | **1840** | **1843** |

**ADB is preferred on three grounds, but the disagreement is recorded rather than erased:** (1) its own arithmetic check, *"Nachdem er 16 Jahre zu St. Leonhard gestanden, wurde er im Jahre 1859 ... an den Münster gewählt"*, where 1843 + 16 = 1859 matches the undisputed Münster date and 1840 + 16 = 1856 matches nothing; (2) it ties licentiate, Privatdozentur and parish into one 1843 event; (3) the Burckhardt pamphlet is dated **1845**. The de.wikipedia dates are uncited in the article itself.

- **Coordinate corrections. [A]** Leonhardskirche **47.5550, 7.5878**, not the pool's 47.5591 / 7.5865 (470 m north). Basler Münster **47.55641, 7.59224**, not 47.5585 / 7.5927. Alte Universität, Rheinsprung 9, roughly **47.5596, 7.5893**.
- **1845: "Die Einsegnung des Herrn Samuel Preiswerk", containing an Einsegnungsrede by Jacob Burckhardt. [A, de.wikipedia, Literatur]**
  **Attribution correction. [A]** Almost certainly **Jakob Burckhardt the elder (1785-1858)**, head of the Münster congregation from 1838 and Antistes of the Basel clergy: exactly the officer who gives an Einsegnungsrede, and the man Preiswerk succeeded in 1859. **Not** the art historian Jacob Burckhardt (1818-1897), who left theology after four semesters and in 1845 became ausserordentlicher Professor of history while editing the *Basler Nachrichten*. The pool's gloss "then still active in church affairs before his turn to art history" is struck. **The pamphlet was not read. [gap]** The venue was likely the Leonhardskirche he was entering.
- **1848: Emilie Preiswerk born, his youngest child. [A, en.wikipedia]**
- **The occult household. [R throughout, marked as such in every stop.]** Family tradition, transmitted through Jung's biographers and not through any contemporary document: he kept a chair in his study for the spirit of his dead first wife and gave one day a week to conversing with her, to his second wife's dismay; he had his daughter Emilie sit behind him while he wrote sermons so that spirits could not disturb him from behind; he had learned Hebrew believing it the language spoken in heaven.
  **Traceability, honestly stated:** the first research pass could not locate this and flagged it as possibly invented. It is not invented. Closest published wording, eternalisedofficial.com: "would devote one day every week to conversing with the spirit of his deceased first wife, keeping a chair for her in his study, much to the dismay of his second wife Augusta Preiswerk". A peer-reviewed article, "Jungian Gothic" (*Psychological Perspectives*, doi 10.1080/00332925.2025.2567215), anchors the family material to **Deirdre Bair, *Jung: A Biography* (2003), p. 72**, which is the page to cite. **Bair p. 72 was not read directly** (archive.org `jungbiography0000bair` access-restricted), the sole reason the tag stays **[R]**.
- **He taught Paul Achilles Jung as his professor of Hebrew. [A, en.wikipedia, 'Carl Jung']** **No year for the coursework is given by any source read. [gap]** Paul was the son of **Carl Gustav Jung the elder**, the Basel professor, and married Preiswerk's daughter Emilie.

## 7. Antistes, 1859-1871

- **1859: elected to the Münster and to the office of Antistes, head of the Basel Reformed clergy and of the city's church-and-school administration. [A, ADB and de/fr.wikipedia agreeing]**
  > "Nachdem er 16 Jahre zu St. Leonhard gestanden, wurde er im Jahre 1859 als Antistes der Baseler Kirche an den Münster gewählt. Er starb am 13. Januar 1871."
- **Where the Antistes's administrative office physically stood was not established. [gap]** The stop is pinned on the Rathaus and says so in its own text.
- **1860: honorary doctorate in theology, University of Basel. [A, de.wikipedia]**
- **Deutsche Biographie's occupation list, in full. [A, deutsche-biographie.de/sfz38549.html]**
  > "1799 – 1871, Antistes der Basler Kirche; geistlicher Dichter; Pfarrer; Gymnasiallehrer; Theologe; Schriftsteller"
  The pool quoted only the first two occupations as though the string were complete. The **Gymnasiallehrer** post appears in no other source read here.
- **Dies 13 January 1871 in Basel, aged 71. [A, ADB; de.wikipedia; en.wikipedia]**
- **No burial site, funeral location, gravestone or plaque was located. [gap, stated as a gap.]** A failure of the research, not a claim that no grave exists; Basel church burial records were not searchable in this pass.

## 8. Afterlife and the family line

- **1875-07-26: grandson Carl Gustav Jung born at Kesswil to Emilie Preiswerk and Johann Paul Achilles Jung. [A]** Pin inherited byte-identical from `jung.journey.json`: **47.5966, 9.3172**.
- **1894/1895-1899: Jung attends the trance séances of his cousin Helly (Hélène) Preiswerk. [A, de.wikipedia, 'Carl Gustav Jung':** *"Jung besuchte von 1894 bis 1899 Seancen seiner Cousine Helly Preiswerk"***]** `jung.journey.json` records that the voice speaking through Helly was identified as **"dead grandfather Samuel Preiswerk, the Hebraist who kept a chair for the spirits"**, and pins the sittings at **47.554, 7.589**, inherited here.
  **Two corrections. [A]** (a) The séances ran five years, not the "four" often given. (b) **Helly's exact descent from Samuel could not be verified.** No Wikipedia article on her exists in either language; en.wikipedia calls her only "his cousin". Since Emilie (b. 1848) is documented as Samuel's *youngest* child and Helly was born decades later, calling her his granddaughter needs a genealogical source nobody produced.
- **The dissertation: 1902 or 1903. [A, both, for different acts.]** de.wikipedia dates the thesis 1902; en.wikipedia says it *"was published in 1903"*. The journey file uses "defended 1902, printed 1903" and notes the routine conflation.
  > "Influenced by an earlier study by Freud's contemporary Théodore Flournoy, Jung wrote his doctoral thesis on spiritualism, focusing on a young medium, his cousin Hélène Preiswerk, whose séances and table turnings he had attended." (en.wikipedia, 'Carl Jung', raw wikitext, section 1, verified verbatim.)
- **1897: First Zionist Congress at the Basel Stadtcasino. [A]** Pin inherited from `martin_buber.journey.json`: **47.5559, 7.5883**. **Ellenberger's remark that the choice of city was no coincidence is [R]**, reported as his opinion with the counter-fact beside it: Herzl came to Basel because Munich's Jewish community refused him a venue, and no delegate is recorded as having heard of Preiswerk. **No connection between Preiswerk and Herzl or any Zionist congress is claimed anywhere in the journey file.**
- **1923-01: Emilie Preiswerk-Jung dies. [A]** Pin inherited from `jung.journey.json`: **47.314, 8.581**.

## Interlocks with existing atlas files

Named in campa text, on the strength of a real relation and not of shared geography:

- **`jung`**, grandson. Named at Kesswil, at the Basel séance table (the voice was given his grandfather's name), at Zurich (the dissertation built on those sittings), and at Küsnacht (Emilie's death). `jung.journey.json` already documents every downstream link and calls Samuel "the Hebraist who kept a chair for the spirits"; no atlas file previously stated the grandfather's own side of it.
- **`carl_gustav_jung_the_elder`**, the other grandfather. Named once, where Preiswerk teaches Hebrew to Paul Achilles Jung, who was Jung the elder's son and became Preiswerk's son-in-law.

Deliberately **not** named: **`martin_buber`** and **`william_holman_hunt`**, who connect only through a shared restorationist current and, for Buber, a shared city. No contact in either direction, and shared geography is not a relation. **`carl_jung_of_mainz`** names Preiswerk in its own genealogy stop, but Samuel had no contact with him.
