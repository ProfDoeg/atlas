# Paolo Soleri (1919-2013): research report

*For the Atlas of Journeys, August 2026. Slug `paolo_soleri`. Companion: `paolo_soleri.journey.json`, 8 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or inference · *GAP* something the sources do not supply and which is not filled in.

The corpus is thin. This life is documented almost entirely through a dozen English Wikipedia articles (listed under Sources), Britannica, one Whitney Museum exhibition-archive page, Open Library catalogue records, and one primary document, Daniela Soleri's 2017 Medium essay. The verification pass corrected many pool coordinates and several substantive facts. Those are itemized below rather than folded in silently: a reader of the journey file needs to know which pins moved and why.

---

## 1. Turin, 1919-1946

**Born in Turin, 21 June 1919 [A: Wikipedia; Britannica].** The industrial capital of Piedmont, seat of Fiat, Lancia and Alfa Romeo. **Coordinate corrected**: the pool had 45.0708, 7.6858; Wikipedia's Turin is 45.07917, 7.67611, about 1.5 km west. File uses **45.0792, 7.6761**.

**Laurea at the Politecnico di Torino, 1946 [A: Wikipedia].** *Contradiction inside the pool, flagged.* The chronology lens called this a laurea (master's); the geography lens called it a doctorate. Not the same claim. The Italian laurea of that era conferred the title *dottore in architettura*, which is how English sources come to write "doctorate," but it is not a research doctorate. The file says laurea and says so in the hedge. Politecnico coordinates were essentially right (about 90 m); file uses **45.0631, 7.6611**.

**First crossing to the United States, December 1946 [A as to date; port is [R]].** No source reached names ship or port. New York is the conventional guess, pinned as such. *GAP: ship, port, and the day are all unknown.*

## 2. Taliesin, 1947-1949

**About eighteen months in fellowship with Frank Lloyd Wright, at Taliesin in Spring Green and at Taliesin West [A: Wikipedia, which names both sites].** Which months he spent where is **[R]**; the file follows the Fellowship's documented seasonal migration.

**Canonical pins inherited byte-identical from `frank_lloyd_wright.journey.json`:** Taliesin, Spring Green **43.1411, -90.0706** (Wright file lines 61-62; the pool's 43.1462, -90.073 is about 600 m off); Taliesin West **33.6064, -111.8453** (Wright file lines 434-435).

QUEUE.md pairs the rows explicitly, Wright's reading "direct interlock with paolo_soleri just queued, who apprenticed under him at Taliesin West" [A: QUEUE.md lines 280-281]. Wright's file records the 1985 removal of his remains to a memorial garden at Taliesin West, making that coordinate both the apprenticeship ground and Wright's terminal pin [A: `frank_lloyd_wright.journey.json` lines 539-548].

**The Beast Bridge and MoMA [R].** Two incompatible versions in the pool. Chronology lens: a bridge design "gains international notice and is shown at the Museum of Modern Art," dated 1949, no firm year in the retrieved passages. Geography lens: the "Beast Bridge" designed for Elizabeth Mock's MoMA book *The Architecture of Bridges*, dated 1948. **A book and an exhibition are different events**, and the lenses may be describing one thing badly or two things; neither was checked against a MoMA record. The file takes the publication reading, pins 40.7614, -73.9776, dates it 1948, and states the contradiction. *Not resolved.*

**Camelback Mountain [R].** Soleri and fellow apprentice Mark Mills camp there after leaving the Fellowship, in some accounts near 240 days. Three problems, flagged rather than smoothed. **Place**: Camelback is in **Phoenix**, between Arcadia and the town of Paradise Valley, not Scottsdale [A: Wikipedia, `Camelback Mountain`]. **Coordinate**: Wikipedia gives 33.51472, -111.96160 against the pool's 33.5199, -111.9648, about 700 m off; file uses **33.5147, -111.9616**. **Date and circumstance**: sources give 1947 or 1948 and disagree on dismissal versus resignation; `Mark Mills (architect)` says 1948, used here. The "240 days" has no support in any article reached and is carried as an unsourced round number. *GAP: nothing reached settles dismissal versus resignation.*

**The Dome House, Cave Creek [A as to address; contested as to client and dates].** 7199 E. Grapevine Road; NRHP reference #100002208, listed 15 March 2018 [A: Wikipedia, Cave Creek properties list, which states "The Dome was built in 1949"]. The pool's "1948-1950" is too wide at both ends; the file uses 1949.

*Client-name contradiction, flagged.* "Lenora Woods" (pool) against "Nora Wood" (Wikipedia, `Mark Mills (architect)`), with "Leonora Woods" also circulating. The daughter Soleri met on that site and married is Carolyn "Colly" Woods, which pulls toward *Woods*. **Not resolved**; the file names both and leaves it.

## 4. Cosanti, 1956-1969

**1956, land in Paradise Valley [A: `Cosanti`; `Arcosanti`].** The chronology lens said "settles in Scottsdale." Paradise Valley is a separately incorporated town adjacent to Scottsdale and the five-acre site is in it.

**Coordinate correction applied at four stops.** The geography lens used 33.5688, -111.9508 for Paradise Valley, Cosanti, the 1969 coinage and the 2013 death. Wikipedia's `Cosanti` gives **33.56683, -111.94191**, which the afterlife lens already carried correctly. Geography was the wrong lens; the file uses 33.56683, -111.94191 throughout.

**1959, official activities begin at Cosanti [A: `Arcosanti`].** The chronology lens claimed no discrete founding date exists and tagged the matter [R]. Two do exist, 1956 for the land and 1959 for the activities. **Retagged [A].**

**The elder daughter's name.** The pool gives "Kristine," a name absent from the retrieved Wikipedia text. *GAP: unverified, therefore unused.* Daniela Soleri is attested by name, with no birth date anywhere reached.

**Mesa City drawings to MoMA, 1960 [R].** From a secondary summary citing SMoCA materials, not an accession record. The study is dated 1958-1967 in the same summary.

**Lecturing at Arizona State [A as to fact: Wikipedia; undated].** No start year, end year or rank in anything reached. The file pins ASU Tempe and dates it 1962-01-01: *a placement, not a fact.* Coordinate corrected from 33.4242, -111.9281 to **33.4209, -111.9340**, about 600 m.

**1963 AIA Gold Medal for Craftsmanship; 1964 Guggenheim Fellowship [A: Wikipedia].** Merged into one stop at Cosanti; neither award has an attested ceremony location.

**1966, the Santa Fe amphitheater [A as to date; venue corrected].** The pool placed it at the Santa Fe Indian School. Wikipedia's `Paolo Soleri Amphitheater` puts it on the campus of the **Institute of American Indian Arts**, now occupied by the Indian School, which makes the pool's phrasing anachronistic for 1966. Opened 1970, closed 2010, marked for demolition 2011, still standing as of 2022. 1501 Cerrillos Road, **35.6724, -105.9678**, a site the geography lens omitted entirely.

**1969, `Arcology: The City in the Image of Man`, MIT Press [A].** Open Library confirms first publication 1969 with MIT Press among the publishers; Wikipedia's `Arcology` independently dates the coinage to 1969. *Contradiction, flagged and not resolved:* a Britannica summary consulted in the research pass associated Soleri with this title in **2006**, apparently conflating book with Cooper Hewitt award year. Two reachable records say 1969; the file says 1969 and names the discrepancy. The afterlife lens had tagged the book [R] because year and publisher were recalled from background knowledge; both are right and both are now sourced. **Retagged [A].**

## 5. Arcosanti and the 1970 retrospective

**Exhibition title, a conflation corrected.** The chronology lens called the Corcoran-organized show "City in the Image of Man." That is the **book**. The exhibition was **"The Architectural Vision of Paolo Soleri"**, as the Whitney's archive records for the traveling leg. The geography lens had it right.

**Corcoran dates, provisional.** 20 February to 5 April 1970, from a secondary catalogue summary, not independently confirmed; consistent with the verified Whitney leg but treated as provisional. Coordinate corrected from 38.8977, -77.0408 to **38.8958, -77.0399** (500 17th Street NW), about 230 m.

**Whitney dates, a major inversion corrected.** The pool dated the Whitney opening 20 September 1970. The Whitney's archive records the run as **17 July to 20 September 1970**, curated by James Monte: **20 September is the closing date**, and a chronology anchored on a September opening would be wrong by two months. File opens the leg 1970-07-17, pin 40.7735, -73.9631, the Breuer building at 945 Madison Avenue.

**1970, construction begins at Arcosanti near Cordes Junction [A: Wikipedia, `Arcosanti`; Britannica].** Pin **34.3425, -112.1006** for every Arcosanti stop.

**1973, `The Bridge Between Matter & Spirit is Matter Becoming Spirit` [A: Britannica; `Arcology` citation; Internet Archive].** Year right in the pool, **place wrong**: Anchor Books, Garden City, New York, not Arcosanti or Cosanti. Pinned 40.7268, -73.6343, approximate and flagged. **1975-1977, redesign for alternative energy [A: `Arcosanti`]**, dated 1975-01-01 with the span in the hedge.

**1976, "Two Suns Arcology" [A; place corrected].** The chronology lens said only "United States." Wikipedia puts it at the **Xerox Square Center, Rochester, New York**, about 43.1566, -77.6088. `Arcosanti` ties the two-suns concept to the 1975-1977 redesign, so the entries are one argument in two places and the file keeps the link.

**1976, UN Habitat, Vancouver [A: Wikipedia].** Canonical pin 49.2827, -123.1207. Dated 1976-06-01; the conference ran late May into June, which is general knowledge rather than pool-attested, so the month is placement and the day defaulted.

**1976, Newsweek [A: Wikipedia]**, with "the most important urban experiment undertaken in our time." Seen verbatim at footnote 9; the original article was **not reached**, and that mediation is stated in `quote_source`. **1980, "Critical Mass" [A: `Arcosanti`]**: the source gives no numeric threshold, so the file asserts no population figure.

## 7. 2006-2025: the bridge, the accusation, the decline

- **2006, Cooper Hewitt lifetime achievement award [A: Wikipedia; Britannica].** Pin 40.7847, -73.9587.
- **October 2010, Daniela Soleri resigns from the Cosanti Foundation board citing abuse by her father [A: Wikipedia, summarizing her public statements].** Day not given.
- **10 December 2010, Soleri Bridge and Plaza completed, Scottsdale [A].** The only full ISO date in the late chronology. Pin 33.4946, -111.9256, approximate to the canal crossing near Marshall Way.
- **2011, retirement as President and CEO at ninety-two [A: `Arcosanti`].**
- **9 April 2013, death at home of natural causes, aged 93, Paradise Valley [A].** Buried at Arcosanti beside Colly [A]. *GAP: no plot and no burial date in anything reached; the burial is pinned on the site coordinate and dated to the death date.*

**13 or 14 November 2017, Daniela Soleri's Medium essay [A, primary document reached].** "Sexual abuse: It's you, him, and his work," Medium, dateline 14 November 2017, cited as footnote 20 on Wikipedia's `Paolo Soleri`. The pool gives 13 November; both are carried, neither preferred.

**Quotation discipline, an important correction.** The pool carried this inside quotation marks:

> In my early adolescence, my father, an architect and craftsman, began sexually molesting me, eventually attempting rape when I was 17.

That is **not verbatim**. It is Wikipedia's silent recasting of the opening clause, presented as a quotation. The original reads:

> It was a clumsily literal dream that started in my early adolescence, when my father, an architect and craftsman, began sexually molesting me, eventually attempting rape when I was 17.

Traced to the Medium essay and corroborated as to wording by Daily Mail article-5207957. **The journey file uses the verbatim original and cites Daniela Soleri directly, not Wikipedia.** The essay also states she told some of Soleri's colleagues roughly 24 years earlier, about 1993, and that a wider circle knew from about 2004.

**2017, the Cosanti Foundation board's response [A, mediated].** Two quotations circulate. From footnote 22: "We are saddened by Daniela Soleri's trauma. Her decision to speak out about her father's behavior towards her helps us confront Paolo Soleri's flaws, and compels us to reconsider his legacy." From the chronology lens: "Paolo Soleri's narcissism prevented him from understanding the need for discipline and limits on abusive behavior." The Foundation's own page at cosantifoundation.org **could not be reached; connection refused.** Both are therefore mediated through Wikipedia, and `quote_source` says so.

**A quotation with no confirmed speaker.** "That work will have to stand on its own, and not be seen as an inseparable part of Soleri as a person, including his best and worst behaviors." The pool places it in the same 2017 context at footnote 20 but cannot say whether the speaker is Daniela Soleri or a commentator. Used in the file with the attribution gap stated rather than assigned to someone for tidiness. *Not resolved.*

**2013-2019, the retrospective wave.** `Prophet in the Desert` and `Beyond Form`, 2013 [A]; `Citizen of the Planet`, 2016 [A]; exhibitions at SMoCA and at the NAU Museum, Flagstaff, 2013 [R]; a further SMoCA retrospective, October 2017 [R]; Carter, `Repositioning Paolo Soleri`, 2019 [R]. The two SMoCA entries may be one event doubly reported and the pool does not distinguish them. **Not resolved.** None carries its own pin; the documentaries fold into the death stop.

**2025, institutional collapse [A: `Arcosanti`].** Layoffs over 80 percent of staff; workshops ceased 2024; volunteering ended by autumn 2025; leadership posts vacant; minimal maintenance and guest-service capacity.

## 8. Interlocks with the existing atlas

- **`frank_lloyd_wright`**: attested and direct. Fellowship 1947-1948 at both Taliesins; both coordinates inherited byte-identical. Named in campa at three stops, with the Taliesin West site named at a fourth.
- **`buckminster_fuller`**: **[R], conceptual only.** Geodesic dome at Black Mountain College 1948-49, patented 1954, developed in the same postwar decades as arcology. **No source reached attests a meeting.** Named once, at the 1969 MIT Press stop.
- **`le_corbusier`**: **[R], lineage only.** The same modernist critique of the sprawling city; the Radiant City is upstream of arcology as family resemblance, not documented influence. **No attested contact.** Cut from the campa at the Paris stop under house style (thematic echo only, no citation of contact either way); the stop now covers his 1984 medal and his other honors instead.
- **`tommaso_campanella`**: **[R], thematic only.** *La città del Sole* as ideal-city ancestor, named once at the Arcosanti groundbreaking: a framing device, not a factual claim.
- **Negative findings, stated rather than forced.** The Kaaba, Temple Mount and canonical Buenos Aires pins have no link to Soleri in any file searched.
