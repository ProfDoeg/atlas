# Cornelius Vanderbilt (1794-1877) research report
*2026-08-22. Written for `cornelius_vanderbilt.journey.json`. Staten Island boatman, steamboat operator, Nicaragua transit contractor, railroad president, founder of Vanderbilt University.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim I could not run to ground. Contradictions are recorded and left open. Where the verified pool corrected the dossier, the correction is stated and is what went into the journey file.

---

## 1. Staten Island, 1794-1817

**Born 27 May 1794, north shore of Staten Island. [A: Wikipedia, cross-checked with Britannica.]** The date is secure; the house is not, and no primary deed fixes it. **[R for the house.]** Best available fix: NYPL researcher Andrew Wilson overlaid an 1884 map on a modern one and located the Harrison homestead of the birth at present-day 203-209 Port Richmond Avenue, approximately **40.6350, -74.1349**. The dossier's -74.1418 fell some 600 m west toward Mariners Harbor. Pin corrected.

**Baptized 16 December 1794 by the Rev. James Burkley** of the Moravian congregation at New Dorp, at the house of John Harrison, sponsors John Housemen, John Garretson and Phebe van der Bilt. **[A: NYPL, "Finding Cornelius," 2016.]** The dossier tagged this [R]; the NYPL record gives officiant, house and sponsors by name, so it stands, and the dossier contradicted itself anyway by tagging the same event [A] in its geography lens. **Retagged [A].**

**Left school at about eleven; running his own periauger to Manhattan at sixteen, 1810. [A: Wikipedia.] The $100 loan from his mother is [R] and probably false:** *Scientific American*, 18 June 1853, published while he was alive and prosperous, says the boat "was owned by his father" and the boy worked it for a share. I did not read the 1853 issue directly. Legend and sketch are both carried in the campa, unresolved, because the dispute is itself the interesting fact.

**War of 1812 harbor carrying. [A for the trade. R for the place.]** No reachable source names a fort, a date, or a contract. Fort Wadsworth is a representative Narrows pin and `date_confidence` says so. **Married Sophia Johnson, first cousin, 19 December 1813 [A]**, place given only as "the New York area," so the family pin is used.

## 2. Gibbons, the Bellona, the monopoly, 1817-1829

**Captain for Thomas Gibbons from 24 November 1817. [A: Wikipedia.] Gibbons v. Ogden, 1824. [A.]** Vanderbilt was not a party and was not at the Court. Under the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there, **the file places no Washington stop**; the decision is narrated from the New York harbor pin where he was working, with the status in `date_confidence`.

**Bellona Hall. [A: Hidden New Jersey; Vanderbilt family genealogy blog.]** Sophia ran the tavern-hostelry at the New Brunswick landing, and **John Quincy Adams stayed overnight there in 1826 [A]**: a real one-night relation to the household, not shared geography, so it is stated as a fact of the house. **Gibbons died 1826; Vanderbilt stayed with the son William until 1829 [A]**, compressed into the same stop.

## 3. Fare wars, ferries, Manhattan, 1829-1848

- **1831, took his brother Jacob's Peekskill line [A]; 1834, paid by the Hudson combination to withdraw [A].** Merged into one Albany stop; the pin stands for the route's northern end, not a dated visit. **1847, president of the New York, Providence and Boston (Stonington) Railroad [A]**.
- **1838, control of the Richmond Turnpike Company. [A: official Staten Island Ferry history.]** *Correction:* the dossier put this at Port Richmond, duplicating the birthplace coordinate. The company's ferry, chartered by Daniel Tompkins in 1817 and running the *Nautilus*, operated from **Tompkinsville**. Pin corrected to **40.6367, -74.0763**.
- **1846, 10 Washington Place. [A: Daytonian in Manhattan.]** Lot bought 1845. *Correction:* the dossier's coordinate pointed into Washington Square Park; the house stood between Broadway and Mercer, approximately **40.7295, -73.9946**.

**Contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** Daytonian in Manhattan states as settled fact that he had Sophia committed to an asylum for three months over the move to Manhattan. Stiles calls the punitive version false or seriously distorted. **[R on both sides:** the institutional record was not reached, and Stiles's text was not read directly.**]** The two are irreconcilable as stated. The campa says the question is argued over; `date_confidence` on the 1846 house stop and on the 1868 death stop names the two sources against each other.

## 4. Nicaragua, 1849-1857

**Concession, 26 or 27 August 1849. [A for substance; contradiction on the day.]** The dossier gives 26 August, Encyclopedia.com 27 August; neither was checked against the Nicaraguan instrument. The file uses 1849-08-26 and states the split in `date_confidence`. **Two companies, kept distinct:** the concession went to the **American Atlantic and Pacific Ship Canal Company** (negotiated by Ephraim George Squier), while the **Accessory Transit Company** was the 1851 operating spin-off. The dossier blurred them.

**Transit operating from 1851; fares driven from about $300 to about $80 [A: Smithsonian National Postal Museum]. Route geography [A]; personal presence [R, negative]:** no source consulted documents Vanderbilt himself at Greytown, on the lake, or at San Juan del Sur. These remain stops on his own line, with `date_confidence` saying presence is undocumented. *Correction:* the dossier's "Virgin Bay" coordinate (11.4342, -85.828) is the inland town of **Rivas**. **La Virgen**, the lake port from which the stage road ran twelve miles to San Juan del Sur, is near **11.3667, -85.7667** (geonames 3618056). Pin corrected.

**1856-57, Walker. [A: Nicaragua country studies; Western Cover Society.]** Vanderbilt backed Costa Rica; allied forces seized the steamers and cut Walker's supply line. He directed it from New York, and the stop says so.

## 5. The North Star and the transit coup, 1853

**Departure pinned. [A.]** Due to sail 19 May 1853, actually sailed 21 May with a grounding at Corlaers Hook, returned **24 September 1853**. The dossier had only "1853-05." **Itinerary [A:** Stiles via erenow.org; Choules, *The Cruise of the Steam Yacht North Star*, 1854**]:** Southampton, Copenhagen, Kronstadt and St Petersburg, Le Havre, Gibraltar, Malaga, Naples, Valletta, Constantinople, Funchal. The file carries six as stops and folds Malta into the Constantinople campa, for length.

**Book title, contradiction resolved in the pool's favour.** The Library of Congress record (03011730) omits Italy; the Michigan Making of America printing (ADL7886.0001.001) and the Forgotten Books reprint name it, so Naples is covered by the title in most editions rather than swept into an "etc.," and the dossier's reasoning there was wrong.

**The Morgan-Garrison coup, 1853. [A for the event: Stiles.]** **The letter is [R], and worse than merely doubted.** Wikiquote files "Gentlemen: You have undertaken to cheat me. I won't sue you, for law is too slow. I'll ruin you" under **Misattributed**; it first appears in the *New York Times* obituary of 5 January 1877, no original is locatable, and every modern printing descends from that obituary. The dossier's wording was also corrupt, dropping "you" after "sue" and inserting "the" before "law." The file uses the canonical wording, labels it misattributed in `quote_source`, and says in the campa that it cannot be found in any archive.

## 6. War, rails, the last decade, 1855-1877

- **European steamship line, 1855-1860. [A: TheShipsList; GG Archives.]** *Corrections:* the line ceased in **1860**, not "around the start of the Civil War"; the flagships are **North Star and Admiral**, and **Ariel does not appear** among its ships, though the dossier listed her.
- **Steamship *Vanderbilt* transferred to the Navy 24 March 1862. [A: Wikipedia.]** The transfer *location* is **[R]**: no source names the yard, and Brooklyn stands in as the standard New York naval receiving point.
- **Lincoln's message to Congress, 17 July 1862. [A with a caveat.]** The Michigan *Collected Works* page returned HTTP 403; the text reached the pool as a search snippet.
- **Congressional Gold Medal, 28 January 1864, 13 Stat. 401. [A: CRS Report RL30076.]** Passed in Washington while he was in New York, so the stop is in New York.
- **Harlem corners. [A; dating split.]** Wikipedia dates them 1863; older railroad histories describe corners in both 1863 and 1864. Unresolved; the stop is dated 1863.
- **George Washington Vanderbilt died at Nice, 31 December 1863. [A.]** *Correction:* the cause is **not malaria**. Find a Grave 130329385 and genealogieonline record a lung disease contracted in Civil War service. A different George Washington Vanderbilt (1832-1836) died in infancy; this son was born 10 April 1839. **The stop is in New York, not Nice**, because Cornelius was not there.
- **1864, Hudson River Railroad bought, last ships sold [A]. January 1867, East Albany embargo and the New York Central [A]**; the testimony of 18 January 1867 is quoted from Wikiquote, the committee transcript not having been located.
- **Jefferson Davis bail, 13 May 1867. [A.]** $100,000, roughly twenty sureties including Greeley, Gerrit Smith and Augustus Schell (FAU Digital Library; Encyclopedia Virginia). *Correction, and it moves the stop:* Vanderbilt **did not go to Richmond**. He sent his son-in-law **Horace F. Clark** while Greeley and Smith appeared in person. The stop is in New York and the campa states the proxy.
- **Sophia died 17 August 1868 [A]; Erie War 1868 [A:** Appletons' Cyclopaedia; standard Gilded Age business history**]**.
- **Married Frank Armstrong Crawford, 21 August 1869, Tecumseh House, London, Ontario [A]**, officiant **Rev. Dr. William Briggs [A]**, bride 45 years his junior and a cousin from Mobile. **The Braxton Bragg and Thomas Bragg attendance is [R] and was downgraded**: Wikipedia, WikiTree Crawford-4851 and the Yancey genealogy give venue and officiant and no Bragg witnesses. The campa reports it as unconfirmed rumour.
- **Consolidation into the New York Central and Hudson River Railroad: 1 November 1869 per company annual reports, 1870 per Wikipedia.** Contradiction left standing. **Grand Central Depot, John B. Snook, built September 1869 to October 1871 [A]**, not the present terminal, which replaced it in 1913.
- **Woodhull, Claflin & Co., 44 Broad Street, 5 February 1870. [A for the financing. R for his presence in the office.] Church of the Strangers, opened 2 October 1870** in the former Mercer Street Presbyterian church **[A]**. *Price contradiction, unresolved:* vanderbilt.edu says $50,000, Daytonian says $100,000. The campa gives no figure; both are in `date_confidence`.
- **Vanderbilt University: gift March 1873, about $1 million [A: vanderbilt.edu, "The Founders"]**, made as **two gifts of $500,000** to Bishop Holland McTyeire, whose wife's kinship to Frank Crawford Vanderbilt put him in the household. The university credits **Cornelius and Frank jointly**. **He never visited Nashville [A]**, so the stop is in New York.
- **The "strengthening the ties" quote, upgraded [R] to [A], with a correction.** The words are Vanderbilt's own, from a letter to McTyeire months after the university opened, dated 2 December 1875 by HMdb marker 214550. The short form at vanderbilt.edu and on Wikipedia **drops "geographical" and "even in the smallest degree"** and presents it as McTyeire's framing, which is what created the ambiguity the dossier could not settle. The file quotes the **full 1875 sentence**, attributed to the letter, not to the 1873 gift.
- **Will, 9 January 1875 [A:** American Heritage, April 1966**]. Funeral 7 January 1877** at the Church of the Strangers, then the Battery, the ferry, and the old Moravian ground **[A]**.
- **Died 4 January 1877 at 10 Washington Place, age 82. [A.]** Cause **contested**: the press said exhaustion from chronic disorders; American Heritage, from the autopsy and will-contest testimony, identifies **peritonitis** as the immediate condition; **Renehan [R]**, using Dr. Jared Linsly's diary, argued for syphilis with neurological effects, which **Stiles rejects** in its strong form. Three positions, left open.

## 7. Afterlife

- **Mausoleum [A]:** Hunt commissioned 1884, built **1885-86** (William H. Vanderbilt died December 1885 before completion), modeled on the twelfth-century abbey church of **Saint-Gilles-du-Gard near Arles**, not a church in Arles. Grounds by **Frederick Law Olmsted** with stepson John C. Olmsted. *Coordinate corrected:* the dossier's 40.5738, -74.1013 lands about 1.5 km away in residential New Dorp; Wikipedia gives **40.5800, -74.11667**. Applied to all three afterlife stops that repeated the bad pin.
- **Reinterment 1888 [A:** Vanderbilt Cup Races blog, citing period press**]**. The two-stage burial (1877 ground, 1888 mausoleum) is absent from the dossier. Folded into the mausoleum campa, as is the **12 April 2016 landmark designation, LP-1208 [A]**.
- **Plassmann statue [A]:** cast 1869 for the St. John's Park freight depot, long misattributed to Albert De Groot until David M. Kahn corrected the record in *The Connoisseur*, June 1980. **Relocation resolved: 1929**, not 1913 (Wikipedia); the dossier left it open.
- **Gold medal donated to Vanderbilt University, autumn 2022. [A: Vanderbilt Libraries.]**
- **The potato chip legend [R, disproven].** The complaining-diner detail was manufactured by a potato chip trade group in the 1970s (Snopes; Wikipedia); Crum's sister claimed the invention herself in 1899; and the alibi is exact, since he was at sea or abroad from 21 May to 24 September 1853. *Coordinate corrected:* Moon's Lake House sat on the bluff at **43.0559, -73.7295**. Kept as a stop inside the North Star segment, because the alibi is the voyage.
- **Stiles's *The First Tycoon* (2009):** National Book Award 2009, Pulitzer 2010 **[A]**. A **Spanish edition is [R], unconfirmed**: "El primer magnate" appears only on a summary service, with no publisher, ISBN or catalogue record.
- **"The public be damned" [A as a misattribution]:** spoken by **William Henry** Vanderbilt to Clarence Dresser, 8 October 1882. Kept out of the journey file so it cannot bleed onto the Commodore.

## 8. Interlocks: used and refused

**Used, and named in a campa: `frederick_law_olmsted`.** Olmsted, with stepson John C. Olmsted, landscaped the grounds of the Vanderbilt mausoleum at New Dorp: a real one-directional relation to Cornelius himself, another traveler working the ground that holds his body.

**Refused.** Each appears in the pool's interlock lens; each fails the test that Vanderbilt must have a relation to the *person*, not the place.

- **`alexander_hamilton`:** the chain runs Hamilton to Federalist 78 to judicial review to Marshall to Gibbons v. Ogden. Marshall is the hinge, not Vanderbilt, and the Hamilton file never mentions him.
- **`charles_fourier`:** the pool reaches Vanderbilt through Greeley's *Tribune*, but the Vanderbilt-Greeley relation (co-sureties on the Davis bond) has nothing to do with Fourier. **Greeley is named in the campa as a person**, correctly; that is not an interlock.
- **`thomas_edison`:** Gould is a common adversary, but the Gould rivalry is Vanderbilt's own and needs no third file. Drexel, Morgan's backing of Edison Electric is his heirs' money, in 1878. See §9.
- **`jules_felix_coutan`:** the "Glory of Commerce" (1914) sits on the same Grand Central facade as Vanderbilt's statue, but a different building, four decades later, by a man with no relation to him. **Placed in `suggested_refs` on the statue stop** and kept out of the campa.
- **`luc_olivier_merson`, `jose_limon`, `merce_cunningham`:** a grandson's commission, and a namesake hall of fame five generations down. **`john_foster_dulles`:** same isthmus, sixty years later, no documentary link.

**Canonical pins.** **Paris (48.8566, 2.3522)** was flagged by the lens as unconfirmed for Vanderbilt personally and is not used: the North Star called at French ports and no source puts him in Paris. The **Kaaba** and **Temple Mount** were reported as having no connection, and none was invented. **23 Wall Street (40.7069, -74.0113)** and **Grand Central (40.7527, -73.9772)** are inherited byte-identical.

## 9. Apparatus relations for the operator

Vanderbilt is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so nothing below went into the journey file. Per the adversary's duty these are recorded rather than deleted, with the direction named. **All are forward, which the exceptions file does not license without the author's separate say-so.**

1. **Forward, Edison Electric, 1878.** Drexel, Morgan & Co. capitalized the Edison Electric Light Company with "the Vanderbilts" among the backers (`thomas_edison.journey.json`, line 212). That money is the residue Cornelius concentrated in William Henry by the 1875 will, deployed the year after his death. If the fortune is to be traced past the grave, this is the hook, and it needs a grant.
2. **Forward, Biltmore, 1888-95.** George Washington Vanderbilt II (grandson, not the son who died at Nice) hired Olmsted for Biltmore (`frederick_law_olmsted.journey.json`, lines 532-537). Olmsted is already named here for the mausoleum, which is backward-safe; Biltmore is not, and was left out.
3. **Forward, 660 Fifth Avenue.** William K. Vanderbilt commissioned Merson's stained glass for the Petit Château (`luc_olivier_merson.journey.json`, lines 325-329).
4. **Forward, Grand Central Terminal, 1913-14.** Coutan's group on the rebuilt terminal (`jules_felix_coutan.journey.json`, lines 356-364). Demoted to a `suggested_ref` rather than dropped.
5. **Forward, the Whitney Hall of Fame.** Limón (1997) and Cunningham, honoured in a hall named for Cornelius Vanderbilt Whitney, a great-great-grandson.

None is a backward application, so none is the relation the Austin and Searle exception was written for. My reading is that the file is complete without them; the decision is the operator's.

## 10. Honest gaps

- No primary record was read for the birth house; the location rests on one researcher's map overlay.
- The *Scientific American* article of 18 June 1853, the Assembly testimony of 18 January 1867, the will, and Lincoln's July 1862 message all reach this file through secondary reproductions, not originals. The Lincoln page returned HTTP 403. Stiles was consulted through an erenow.org excerpt, not the book.
- Nothing establishes Vanderbilt's personal presence anywhere in Nicaragua at any point. If he ever crossed his own transit, this research did not find it.
- Whether Confederate generals stood at his second wedding, whether Sophia was ever confined, whether the ruin-you letter was ever posted, and what killed him: four questions the record leaves open, and the journey file leaves open too.
