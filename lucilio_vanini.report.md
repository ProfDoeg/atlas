# Lucilio Vanini (Giulio Cesare Vanini), 1585-1619: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-16 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: the Carmelite friar from Taurisano who abjured twice, published two Latin books, taught in Toulouse under a false name, and was burned there on 9 February 1619 after his tongue was torn out.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-source detail, or inference. Contradictions are printed side by side and left open.

**Method and its limits.** The reachable record is three encyclopedia articles (English, French, Italian Wikipedia), two quotation collections (it.wikiquote, fr.wikisource), and a handful of adjacent Wikipedia articles used to check specifics. The three encyclopedias disagree on the year he moved to Padua, on whether he went to Rome in 1614, on whether he returned to Lecce, on the month of his arrest at Toulouse, and on the length of his English imprisonment. None of the modern scholarly editions (Papuli/Raimondi 1990; Namer; De Paola) was reachable. Anything resting on one encyclopedia alone is tagged **[R]** even where that encyclopedia states it flatly. Lucilio and Giulio Cesare are the same man: baptized Lucilio, he printed under Giulio Cesare.

---

## 1. Terra d'Otranto and the Studium of Naples (1585-1607)

- Born at **Taurisano**, Terra d'Otranto, Kingdom of Naples, in **1585** **[A: Wikipedia EN and IT]**. Italian Wikipedia gives the day as **19 or 20 January** **[R: it.wikipedia only, and it hedges between two days]**.
- Father **Giovan Battista Vanini**, a Tuscan businessman from Tresana; mother of the Spanish **Lopez de Noguera** family **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- Enrolls at the **University of Naples** in **1599** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- French Wikipedia alone places early study at **Rome** before Naples, uncorroborated by EN or IT **[R: recorded, not adopted]**.
- Enters the **Carmelite order** in **1603**, taking the religious name **Fra Gabriele** **[A: EN and IT, on both year and name]**.
- The house in which he took the habit is named by no source consulted. **Santa Maria del Carmine Maggiore** at Naples is a traditional identification only, tagged **[R]** in the journey file; presenting it as attested would be false precision.
- **Doctorate in utroque iure** (civil and canon law) at Naples, **June 1606** **[A: EN and IT]**.
- His father dies around **1606** **[R: approximate year only, no month and no place]**.

## 2. Padua (1608-1612)

- Moves to **Padua** for theology in **1608** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **A supposed contradiction, dissolved:** it.wikipedia has been read as dating the move to 1603, but it gives *no* year at all, saying only that on entering the order in 1603 he was later "trasferito a Padova per studi di teologia." There is no 1603-versus-1608 conflict; the 1603 Padua date is an inference, not a reading, and is dropped.
- The Carmelite house at Padua is the convent attached to **Santa Maria del Carmine**, held by the order from 1300 until 1806 **[A: it.wikipedia, Basilica del Carmine (Padova)]**, at **45.4132, 11.8741**. The figure 45.4085, 11.8776 in the working notes is about 600 m off to the south-east and is corrected in the journey file.
- Studies theology at the **University of Padua** and is drawn toward the anti-papal circle around **Paolo Sarpi** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- Preaches **Lenten sermons in 1611**, attracting the suspicion of religious authorities **[A: EN and IT]**.
- **January 1612:** removed from the Padua convent over anti-papal attitudes ("nel gennaio 1612 allontanato da Padova") **[A: it.wikipedia]**. His superior orders him back to Naples; he refuses.

## 3. The flight north (1612)

- Takes refuge with the **English ambassador at Venice** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The embassy residence at that date is identified by no reachable source; a general Venice coordinate is used **[R for the pin]**.
- Flees toward England with the fellow friar **Bonaventura Genocchi**, by way of **Bologna, Milan, the Swiss Grisons, the Rhine, and German and Dutch lands** **[A: it.wikipedia, for the route as a list]**.
- **Contradiction inside a single source, flagged:** it.wikipedia has him removed from Padua in January 1612 and then says "l'anno dopo fugge in Inghilterra," the following year, which cannot be squared with the same article's **July 1612** abjuration. Both the January 1612 expulsion and the 1612 flight are reported here, with the inconsistency left visible.
- No town on the Rhine is named in any source. **Cologne is a representative waypoint, not a confirmed stop [R]**, labelled as such in the journey file.
- Reaches **London**, lodged at **Lambeth Palace** under **Archbishop George Abbot** **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **July 1612:** publicly renounces Catholicism and embraces Anglicanism at the **Italian Church in London** **[A: EN and IT]**. Italian Wikipedia adds that **Francis Bacon** was present as a witness **[R: it.wikipedia only, but specific and named]**. The congregation's meeting-place in the City at that date is unconfirmed; the pin is approximate **[R]**.

## 4. England, the second turn, and the Gatehouse (1613-1614)

- **1613:** quarrels with his English hosts and appeals to the Pope for readmission as a **secular priest**; the request is granted **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Visits **Cambridge** and **Oxford** **[R: mentioned by EN and IT with no date, circumstance, or purpose]**.
- **January 1614:** arrested in London by order of Archbishop Abbot **[A: en.wikipedia]**; it.wikipedia adds that the arrest followed a religious service and that he was held in the **Gatehouse prison** at Westminster Abbey **[R: single source]**.
- **Length of imprisonment, contradiction:** fr.wikipedia says **49 days**, for attacks on the Anglican Church. Neither EN nor IT gives a figure, and 49 days does not fit a January arrest and a March escape without a precise January date no source supplies. **[R: unverified specific, recorded but not used]**.
- **Escape:** it.wikipedia dates it "tra il 10 e il 16 marzo 1614" and credits the **Spanish ambassador**; en.wikipedia gives March without the range **[A for escape and month; R for day-range and ambassador]**.

## 5. Reconciliation, Genoa, and the road to Lyon (1614-1615)

- **April 1614:** arrives at **Brussels**, received by the papal nuncio **Guido Bentivoglio** **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **May 1614:** at **Paris**, presented to the papal nuncio **Roberto Ubaldini**; writes a *Commentari in difesa del Concilio di Trento*, subscribing to Tridentine principles **[A: EN and IT]**.
- **Rome, 1614, contradiction left open:** en.wikipedia has him travel to Rome to answer for the English apostasy before the Inquisition. Neither the Italian nor the French account contains the journey; both move him from Brussels and Paris toward Genoa. **[R]**.
- **Genoa, several months, summer 1614 into January 1615 [A: EN and IT both]:** it.wikipedia, "Dimora per qualche mesi a Genova, dove ritrova l'amico Genocchi e si guadagna da vivere insegnando filosofia ai figli di Scipione Doria"; en.wikipedia likewise records months in Genoa teaching philosophy. Genoa is **not** a French-Wikipedia-only claim and should not be tagged as one.
- **Lecce, contradiction left open:** French Wikipedia alone has him return to Lecce and reconcile with the Church, and adds a brief stay at **Geneva**. Both are uncorroborated by EN and IT, which have him in Genoa across the same months. **[R]**.
- **January 1615:** Genocchi is arrested at Genoa as the Inquisition moves on friars returned from England; Vanini flees to **Lyon** **[A: EN and IT]**.

## 6. The two books (1615-1617)

- **June 1615, Lyon:** publishes ***Amphitheatrum Aeternae Providentiae Divino-Magicum***, ostensibly a defense of divine providence **[A: EN and IT]**. Manuscript copies were later preserved at Stuttgart and Hamburg **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Paris, 1615-1616: the Bassompierre problem.** The circulating claim that Vanini served as **chaplain to Marshal François de Bassompierre** fails twice. Bassompierre was not yet a Marshal of France: he received the baton for the Huguenot rising of 1621-22, and in 1615-16 was colonel-general of the Swiss and Grisons. And it.wikipedia never calls Vanini his chaplain; it describes him only as "uomo potente alla corte di Maria de' Medici" and as the **dedicatee** of *De Admirandis*. **The attested relation is patronage and dedication, tagged [R]; the chaplaincy is unsourced and is not asserted in the journey file.**
- **September 1616, Paris:** ***De Admirandis Naturae Reginae Deaeque Mortalium Arcanis*** is printed by the Protestant printer **Adrien Perier**, dedicated to Bassompierre, after approval by **two Sorbonne theologians** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- The Sorbonne theologians **withdraw their approval shortly after publication**, citing unauthorized textual changes; the work is condemned to be burned **[A: it.wikipedia]**. No month is given; "shortly after" is all the source offers.
- **April 1617:** after the assassination of **Concino Concini** throws Paris into upheaval, Vanini's position collapses and he leaves the capital **[A: EN and IT]**.

## 7. Brittany, the south, and the assumed name (1617-1618)

- Takes refuge in Breton abbeys; English Wikipedia specifies **Redon Abbey** (Saint-Sauveur) **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **1617-1618:** moves through **Guyenne and Languedoc** toward Toulouse under increasing surveillance **[A: it.wikipedia]**; takes a tutoring post with a **Monsieur de Berthier** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. No town is named for that household; a regional coordinate stands in **[R for the pin]**.
- **Adrien de Monluc-Montesquiou, comte de Carmain (Cramail, Caraman)**, 1571-1646, is his protector: "Il fut, pendant un temps, protecteur de Giulio Cesare Vanini, même si son rôle dans l'arrestation et la mort de celui-ci suscite quelques questions" **[A for the protection; the same sentence records that his role in the arrest is itself disputed]**. **Correction:** the title came through his wife Jeanne de Foix-Caraman, and **no source places Vanini at the town of Caraman**. The shelter is attested in Monluc's Toulouse circle; the coordinate 43.5333, 1.7833 in the working notes is an invention and is dropped.
- **1618, Toulouse:** teaches under the assumed name **Pomponio Uciglio** (also rendered Pompeo, and Lucilio, Uciglio) **[A: FR and IT]**.

## 8. Trial and execution (1618-1619)

- **Arrest date, three-way contradiction, left open:** it.wikipedia gives **2 August 1618** after a month-long search; en.wikipedia gives **August 1618** without the day; fr.wikipedia gives **November 1618**. None is adopted as settled. He is interrogated on his identity, his religious views, and his moral conduct **[A]**.
- **Guillaume de Catel** (1560 - 5 October 1626) is named by fr.wikipedia as having "played an important role in the condemnation and execution" of Vanini **[A]**. **Correction:** he was a **conseiller au parlement de Toulouse**, not procureur général, and the article gives **no account of a prosecutor struggling to prove the charges**. That circulating framing is not in the source.
- **February 1619:** convicted. English Wikipedia summarizes the charges as **atheism and blasphemy**; French Wikipedia gives the fuller list, **blasphemy, impiety, atheism, sorcery, and corruption of morals** **[A, both]**.
- **9 February 1619:** sentenced to have his tongue cut out, then to be strangled, then his body burned **[A: EN, FR, IT]**. The execution is at the **Place du Salin** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, at **43.5947, 1.4444**. The figure 43.5998, 1.4436 in the working notes falls about 570 m north, near the Place Esquirol, and is wrong.
- The sentencing court is the **Palais du Parlement**, **43.5936, 1.4447**, not the Château Narbonnais, which was demolished 1549-1556 **[A: fr.wikipedia, Château Narbonnais]**.
- **The refusal at the amende honorable.** Ordered to beg pardon of God, the King, and justice, he answers: "Quanto a Dio non credo affatto che esista; quanto al Re non l'ho mai offeso; e quanto alla giustizia, che i diavoli, se ce ne sono, la mandino in rovina." **[R]**. The words are verbatim on it.wikipedia, but the framing matters: the article gives them as his answer at the amende honorable, that is **at the execution on 9 February 1619, not as an interrogation reply in 1618**, and footnotes them only to a 2021 popular web article, **not trial documentation**. Treated here as a traditionally attributed last utterance.
- **The cry.** Gabriel Barthélemy de Gramond, Toulouse parlementaire and contemporary chronicler, in *Historiarum Galliae ab excessu Henrici IV* (1643): "jamais on n'entendit un cri plus horrible ; on auroit cru entendre le mugissement d'un bœuf qu'on tue." **[A for quotation and author; R for the fr.wikipedia detail that he refused to put out his tongue and pincers were needed]**.
- **The cleared square, misplaced wedding.** Italian Wikipedia alone reports the execution site cleared in haste because a **ducal wedding** was to be celebrated the following day **[R: single source]**. The wedding it names, **Victor Amadeus of Savoy to the thirteen-year-old Christine of France**, took place **at the Louvre in Paris on 10 February 1619**, not at a church in Toulouse. The Toulouse-church version of the anecdote is an error and is not repeated.
- **The ashes: an honest gap.** No source consulted records what became of them, and no legend of a frog or animal leaping from the severed tongue appears in any of the three encyclopedias. If such details circulate in popular tradition, they are not attested in anything reachable here.

## 9. Afterlife (not staged as stops)

These belong to other people, and appear in the journey file only as sources and suggested references on Vanini's own stops:

- **May and June 1619:** two anonymous publications are the first to name Vanini publicly as the author of the condemned dialogues **[A: it.wikipedia]**.
- **1623:** the Jesuit **François Garasse** judges his writings "l'opera più perniciosa che in fatto di ateismo fosse mai uscita negli ultimi cento anni" **[A for the wording as it.wikipedia prints it]**. Two cautions: the page gives this as an **indirect report**, not a direct quotation from Garasse's text, and does **not** tie it to *La Doctrine curieuse des beaux esprits de ce temps* (1623). That circulating work-and-year attribution is unsupported.
- **Leibniz**, letter to Christian Kortholt: "Je n'ai pas encore vu l'apologie de Vanini, je ne pense pas qu'elle mérite fort d'être lue. Les écrits de ce personnage sont bien peu de chose." **[A: as quoted on it.wikipedia; undated there]**. **1839: Schopenhauer** cites him as a precursor on determinism **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **9 June 1889:** the Giordano Bruno monument at Campo de' Fiori, Rome, by **Ettore Ferrari**, carries eight medallion portraits, Vanini among them with Sarpi, Campanella, Petrus Ramus, Paleario, Servetus, Wycliffe, and Hus **[A]**. A portrait of **Martin Luther** is hidden *inside the Vanini medallion itself*, "seminascosto anche un piccolo ritratto di Martin Lutero," undetected until **Lars Berggren** found it in **1991** **[A: it.wikipedia, Monumento a Giordano Bruno]**. Describing the Luther portrait as a separate effigy beneath the medallion is wrong. Vanini was never at Campo de' Fiori: this is Ferrari's stop, cited as a reference on Vanini's execution stop, not staged as a scene.
- **2007:** a plaque at the Place du Salin, inscribed to "penseurs précurseurs des Lumières, victimes de l'obscurantisme," names Vanini alongside **Étienne Dolet** and **Michel Servet**; an **Espace Giulio-Cesare-Vanini** was designated at the square's western corner by municipal decision of **26 November 2010** **[A: fr.wikipedia, Place du Salin]**.
- **1980 and 1990:** studies by **Émile Namer** and **Francesco De Paola**, and the critical edition by **Giovanni Papuli** and **Francesco Paolo Raimondi**, reframe Vanini as an original naturalist thinker **[A: EN and FR bibliographies]**. **2011:** an English translation of eight dialogues from *De Admirandis* appears in *The Philosophical Forum* **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## Contradictions carried forward, not adjudicated

1. **Rome 1614.** Present in en.wikipedia, absent from IT and FR, which route him Brussels-Paris-Genoa.
2. **Lecce and Geneva 1614-1615.** fr.wikipedia only; contradicted by the Genoa months in EN and IT.
3. **Arrest at Toulouse:** 2 August 1618 (IT), August 1618 (EN), November 1618 (FR).
4. **Length of the English imprisonment:** 49 days (FR only), against a January arrest and a 10-16 March escape (IT).
5. **Charges:** atheism and blasphemy (EN) against blasphemy, impiety, atheism, sorcery, corruption of morals (FR).
6. **Internal to it.wikipedia:** expelled from Padua January 1612, then "l'anno dopo fugge in Inghilterra," against the same article's July 1612 abjuration.

## Gaps stated as gaps

- **Late 1614 in France.** Between the May 1614 presentation to Ubaldini at Paris and the January 1615 flight to Lyon, nothing is accounted for beyond the Tridentine note and the loosely dated Genoa months.
- **Unnamed places:** the Naples house where he took the habit; the meeting-place of the Italian Church in London; the English embassy residence at Venice; the town of the Berthier household; any town on the Rhine.
- **The fate of the ashes** is unrecorded, and the **Cambridge and Oxford visits** are mentioned by EN and IT with no date, purpose, or circumstance attached.
