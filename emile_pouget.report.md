# Émile Pouget (1860-1931): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `emile_pouget.journey.json`. Slug: `emile_pouget`.*

Anarchist journalist, inventor of the Père Peinard, the man who put *sabotage* into the
vocabulary of the French labour movement, co-drafter of the Charte d'Amiens, editor of the
CGT's *La Voix du Peuple*. Aveyron by birth, radicalized behind a department-store counter,
imprisoned at Melun, exiled to London, condemned in absence at the Trial of the Thirty,
arrested with the whole CGT executive in 1908, dead in a suburb after eleven years of
compiling art catalogues.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Where
sources disagree, both readings are printed; nothing below silently picks a winner.

**Source base in one line.** The spine is the French and English Wikipedia articles on
Pouget, which contradict each other on several points of substance; the chronology of the
events around him comes from the French articles on those events, better sourced than
either; the quotations come from *Sabotage* and *Direct Action* in English, on
theanarchistlibrary.org. No archive, no monograph, and no run of any of his newspapers was
read directly, which is the principal weakness of everything below.

## 1. Aveyron, 1860-1875

- Born **12 October 1860 at Pont-de-Salars, Aveyron**, son of a notary **[A: fr. and
  en.wikipedia]**. Coordinate from the commune infobox, 44.2819 / 2.7283.
- **Father dies 1863** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Mother remarries; the boy is raised at
  **Salles-la-Source**, stepfather an employee of the public works administration
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. Coordinate corrected to the commune point, 44.4372 / 2.5150.
- At the **lycée of Rodez** he founds **Le Lycéen républicain**; his stepfather dies; he
  leaves for Paris. All **1875** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Contradiction flagged: the Narbonne Communards' trial.** An English gloss has Pouget
witnessing the trial of the Narbonne Communards at Rodez **in 1875, at the lycée**. That is
chronologically impossible. The trial opened at Rodez **13 November 1871** and closed on the
**18th**, all forty-four civilians acquitted **[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Commune de Narbonne']**;
Pouget was **eleven**. fr.wikipedia says only that he was *marqué à jamais* by it, with no
year and **no claim of attendance**. The file keeps the stop, dated 1871-11-13, with the
hedge in `date_confidence`. **Gap:** the address of the Palais de justice de Rodez was not
established, so the pin is the lycée coordinate 44.3522 / 2.5655.

## 2. Paris, the counter and the black flag, 1877-1883

- **1877**: shop assistant at **Le Bon Marché** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **1879**: helps found a union. **Contradiction flagged:** en.wikipedia calls it the
  **first union of shop assistants in Paris**; fr.wikipedia dates his part in founding a
  **Syndicat des employés du textile** to the same year. Neither names a hall, an address, or
  another member.
- **1881**: joins French anarchists at the **International Social Revolutionary Congress**,
  London, **14-20 July** **[A: fr.wikipedia only]**. Three cautions, all in
  `date_confidence`: en.wikipedia's Pouget article **never mentions the congress** (an earlier
  note crediting it to en.wikipedia was wrong); the article on the congress **does not list
  him** among named delegates; **no source names the venue**, so the earlier placed guess was
  dropped for the standing London pin **51.5072 / -0.1276**, declared a pin and not a hall.
- **1882**: begins the relationship with **Stéphanie Boisteux**, lasting to her death in
  1904 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no day, place, origin, or occupation.
- **9 March 1883**: leads the **march of the unemployed from the Esplanade des Invalides**
  with **Louise Michel**; three bakeries looted; the **black flag flown, by tradition for the
  first time** **[A: both wikis for the march; R for the primacy of the flag, the movement's
  own memory, not independently documented]**. Arrested at the **Place Maubert** standing
  between the police and Michel; **eight years for pillage** **[A: en.wikipedia for the
  scene, fr.wikipedia for the sentence]**. The route down the Boulevard Saint-Germain between
  the two attested endpoints is inference and is tagged **[R]** in the file.
- **Melun, 1883 to c. 1886.** **Contradiction flagged:** fr.wikipedia gives 1883-1886;
  en.wikipedia says three years "following amnesty pressure" and names no year. Both are
  printed. Rochefort's press campaign for his release **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  - **Coordinate trap, for the operator.** The historic *maison centrale* on the Île
    Saint-Étienne is **48.5350 / 2.6622** (OSM); a working 48.5386 / 2.6597 was the town
    centre. The modern **Centre pénitentiaire at 48.6009 / 2.5933 is at Réau and is NOT the
    1883 prison**; any geocoder offers it and it must be refused.

## 3. Le Père Peinard and the exile, 1888-1895

- **Ça ira**, edited **27 May 1888 to 13 January 1889** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; office address
  not given anywhere consulted. **Le Père Peinard founded 24 February 1889**, in Paris
  workshop argot through the persona of a cobbler **[A: both wikis]**. The comparison with
  Hébert's *Père Duchesne* is standard in the literature and **[R]** as to Pouget's own
  intent: no statement of his naming the model was located.
- **Series structure, corrected.** A bibliography line of "1889-1900" understates the run.
  fr.wikipedia, 'Le Père peinard': **Paris 1889-1894; London 1894-1895, eight issues;
  relaunched as La Sociale, then restored to the original title 1896-1899; third series
  January-April 1900; one final issue in 1902.** Other formulations close the paper in 1900;
  both are stated in the file.
- **21 February 1894**: last Paris issue, then flight **via Algiers to London**
  **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Single-sourced:** fr.wikipedia says only *contraint d'émigrer en
  Angleterre*. Coordinate corrected to the city and old port, **36.7729 / 3.0588**; a working
  figure lay offshore in the bay.
- Lodges at **Giovanni Defendi's Italian delicatessen**, London **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
  **Gap:** the street is not confirmed anywhere, so the stop sits on the standing London pin
  and says so.

**Major contradiction flagged: the Trial of the Thirty.** en.wikipedia's Pouget article says
he was charged in absentia and **ultimately acquitted**. Contradicted by the trial articles
on both wikis, and printed rather than resolved:

- fr.wikipedia, 'Procès des Trente', lists him as **"Émile Pouget (Condamné par
  contumace)"**, one of five accused who had gone underground (with Paul Reclus, Constant
  Martin, Louis Duprat, Alexandre Cohen); French contumacy law convicted the absent
  automatically. The acquittals covered the **twenty-six who appeared in person**, all
  acquitted except Ortiz, Chiericotti and Bertani **[A]**.
- Circumstantial support: he **stayed in London until the 1895 amnesty**. An acquitted man
  had no reason to. Trial span **6 August to 31 October 1894**, Cour d'assises de la Seine,
  Palais de Justice (48.8551 / 2.3450) **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Trial of the Thirty']**.

**Stop placement, per the house rule.** Pouget was never in that courtroom, so the stop sits
**in London, where he was**, with the Palais de Justice in `suggested_refs` as the place the
verdict was read.

- **London series of Le Père Peinard**, eight issues, **September 1894 to March 1895**,
  smuggled into France as pamphlets **[A: both wikis]**. **Amnestied 1895**
  **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; **La Sociale** from **11 May 1895** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 4. The confederation, 1896-1906

- **Fourth Congress of the Second International, London, 26-31 July 1896**, Pouget a
  delegate **[A: en.wikipedia; dates from 'Second International']**. The congress made the
  exclusion of the anarchists definitive; Malatesta and Landauer, refused credentials,
  heckled from the public galleries **[A]**.
  - **Venue flagged as unsupported.** Neither the 'Second International' nor the 'Queen's
    Hall' article places the 1896 congress there. The coordinate 51.5181 / -0.1425 is the
    Queen's Hall, held **[R]** and declared provisional in `date_confidence`.
- **October 1896**: Le Père Peinard resumes, syndicalist rather than individualist in
  content **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **September 1897, CGT congress of Toulouse**: sabotage
  adopted as a tactic after Pouget's advocacy **[A: both wikis]**.

**Contradiction flagged: the date and title of *Le Sabotage*.** An English gloss puts a
pamphlet called *Le Sabotage* in **1898**, uncorroborated outside en.wikipedia. Against it
**[A: fr.wikipedia, 'Sabotage']**: the word got its *baptême syndical* at **Toulouse in
1897**, where the brochure was **"Boycottage et sabottage"** (two t's); the book-length *Le
Sabotage* is **1911**, its opening sentence dating the word's currency to *"il y a encore une
quinzaine d'années"*; and the series is **Marcel Rivière's Bibliothèque du mouvement
prolétarien**, **not** "the CGT's own imprint". Both readings appear in the stop's
`date_confidence`; the stop is dated 1911.

- **1 December 1900**: first issue of **La Voix du Peuple**, CGT organ, under his editorship,
  out of the **Bourse du Travail, 3 rue du Château d'Eau** **[A: both wikis]**. Coordinate
  corrected to 48.8689 / 2.3620 (OSM).
- **Contradiction flagged:** fr.wikipedia makes him **assistant secretary of the federations
  section from 1901**; en.wikipedia makes him **vice-secretary heading it from the 1902
  merger** with the Fédération des Bourses du Travail. Title and year both differ; the merger
  is not in dispute. The file dates the stop 1901 to order the segment and prints both.
- **1904**: **Stéphanie Boisteux dies** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** no day, month, place,
  or cause. The stop uses the standing Paris pin and states that the pin is a placeholder,
  not a place of death.
- **13 October 1906, Congress of Amiens**: the motion **drafted by Victor Griffuelhes and
  Émile Pouget** is adopted, **830 of 839**, as the **Charte d'Amiens** **[A: fr.wikipedia,
  'Charte d'Amiens', quoting the commemorative plaque and naming both drafters verbatim]**.
  - **Venue caveat:** fr.wikipedia attests the **plaque** at the École Albert Roze, rue
    Rigollot, **not that the congress sat there**. Coordinate set to rue Rigollot,
    **49.8870 / 2.3100**.

## 5. Draveil-Villeneuve-Saint-Georges and the cells, 1908-1909

Where the received English chronology is worst. All corrections from fr.wikipedia, 'Grèves
de Draveil-Villeneuve-Saint-Georges' **[A]**:

| received | corrected |
|---|---|
| strikes "end" May 1908 | strikes **begin 2 May 1908** at Draveil, run to 8 August |
| police kill two workers, May 1908 | the shooting is **2 June 1908 at Vigneux**: Pierre Le Foll (48) and Émile Giobellina (17) killed, about ten wounded |
| (absent) | **30 July 1908, Vigneux and Villeneuve-Saint-Georges**: four killed, over two hundred wounded, the bloodiest day |
| Pouget arrested "1908" | arrests **30-31 July 1908**, 31 CGT leaders charged: Griffuelhes, Pouget, Yvetot, Dret, Monatte (who fled to Switzerland) |
| released February 1909 | **non-lieu for all 31, 30 October 1908**. February 1909 is the **Chamber's amnesty vote of the 11th, 451 to 5**, three months after he was out |

**Two events conflated, flagged:** the release and the launch of **La Révolution** are
separate. Freed late October 1908; *La Révolution* launched **February 1909** and folded
**March 1909** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Gaps:** no source names **which Paris prison** held
him (the stop carries an approximate 48.8339 / 2.3367, declared as marking no identified
building), and none says **where he was arrested**. **1909**: columnist for **Gustave Hervé's
La Guerre sociale** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; Hervé's later drift to the far right is standard
biography for Hervé, used as context.

## 6. The books

- **Comment nous ferons la révolution**, with **Émile Pataud**, **1909**; **L'Action
  directe** and **La Confédération générale du travail**, both **1910** (Bibliothèque du
  mouvement prolétarien); **Le Sabotage**, **1911** (see the contradiction above)
  **[A: en.wikipedia, fr.wikipedia]**. The English *Direct Action* consulted gives
  **neither translator nor edition**, a weakness carried on every quotation from it.
- **Sabotage**, trans. **Arturo Giovannitti**, Charles H. Kerr, Chicago, **1913**, the
  edition that carried the vocabulary into the IWW **[A: standard bibliographic record]**.

**Quotation correction, recorded.** A circulating form of one *Direct Action* quotation drops
the opening "Because" and capitalizes "Action", making a freestanding aphorism out of a
subordinate clause. The file uses the printed wording, "Because action is the salt of life
... Or, to put it more plainly and simply, it is life itself! To live is to act .. To act is
to be alive!", whose two-dot ellipsis is **genuine to the source**.

## 7. War, marriage, withdrawal, death, 1913-1931

- **1913**: stories in **Jaurès's L'Humanité**, offices at **142 rue Montmartre,
  48.8689 / 2.3434** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **August 1914**: takes the **national defence** position against Germany, called
  "unexpected" by en.wikipedia **[A]**. **Gap, the largest in the file:** **no text of
  Pouget's own explaining the turn is cited anywhere consulted, and none was located.** The
  file says so instead of inventing a motive.
- **17 April 1915**: marries **Augustine Caroline Jamaux**, **in the 18th arrondissement**
  (Mairie du 18e, 48.8922 / 2.3441) **[A: fr.wikipedia, verbatim]**. **Source misattribution
  corrected:** en.wikipedia **does not mention the marriage at all**, referring only to "his
  wife".
- **1915**: daily serial **Vieille Alsace** in *L'Humanité* **[A: en.wikipedia]**. A gloss
  placing a patriotic serial "in the summer of 1914" misdates it by a year: 1914 is the
  position, 1915 is the serial.
- **By 1920**: withdrawn from activism, at **Lozère, Palaiseau**, compiling catalogues of
  artists' works **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Coordinate corrected:** Lozère is
  **48.7046 / 2.2066**; workings of 48.7053 / 2.2394 and 48.7167 / 2.2455 sit 2.4 to 2.9 km
  east, **not in Lozère**.
- **21 July 1931**: dies at **Lozère, Palaiseau**, then Seine-et-Oise, aged 70 **[A: both
  wikis]**. **Buried in the cemetery of Palaiseau**, with **Pierre Monatte, Maurice
  Chambelland, Daniel Guérin** at the grave **[A: fr.wikipedia]**; the commune's only
  cemetery per OSM (way 25413308) is **48.7103 / 2.2360**.
- **[R]** **No source describes the present state of the grave**, whether it stands, is
  tended, or has been reclaimed. A gap in the record, not a resolved fact.
- **[R]** **No monument, statue, or plaque** to Pouget is recorded in Palaiseau or Paris in
  any source consulted. The afterlife runs through reprints instead: Langlais (1976), Mille
  et une nuits (2004), Les Nuits rouges (2006), Delaplace's Classiques Garnier edition of the
  first twenty-three issues of *Le Père Peinard* (2015), the *Almanach* volumes on Gallica,
  and the Charte d'Amiens commemorations. **[A for the editions; R for the claim that the
  commemorations return him to print]** None of them, nor the 1913 Chicago translation, is
  written as a stop: Pouget was not present at any of them, so per the house rule they are
  cited on his own stops for the texts he wrote and never staged as scenes.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**Nothing to escalate.** Pouget is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no relation in the
pool required his framework to be applied backward or forward to material he never treated.
Every stop in the file is an event of his own life on his own geography.

Two relations were kept as **citations rather than scenes**, noted so the calls are visible:

1. **The Hague, 1872.** The pool offers the Marx-Bakunin split as the ideological origin of
   the CGT's line. Pouget was **twelve** and in the Aveyron, so it is **not a stop**; it
   appears inside the campa of the **Amiens 1906** stop as the quarrel the Charter settles,
   with `karl_marx.journey.json` and `mikhail_bakunin.journey.json` in that stop's `sources`.
   These are the file's only two **interlocks named in campa** (Bakunin also in the London
   1881 stop, as head of the current Pouget joins there). Both are influence relations, not
   co-location: **[R]** as to the lineage, **[A]** as to the Hague events in both files.
2. **Chicago, c. 1912.** William Z. Foster returns from France "full of syndicalism" and
   founds the Syndicalist League of North America in Lucy Parsons's front room. The relation
   runs one way, toward Pouget's doctrine, and is real, but the travelling was Foster's; it
   is cited in `sources` on the **Le Sabotage (1911)** stop (`lucy_parsons.journey.json`).

**Relations dropped for insufficiency, listed rather than deleted:** Bakunin's 1861 Nadar
sitting and his writings on the Commune; Lucy Parsons's 1888 English tour; Lucía Sánchez
Saornil's CNT membership and her 1939 relief work near Paris; Landauer's Bromley years;
Mühsam's undated Paris stay; Eberhardt's 1899-1900 Paris; Debord's Left Bank; Marx's rue
Vanneau; Buber's *Paths in Utopia*. Each is shared geography or shared tendency **without a
documented tie to Pouget himself**, and the map already shows a viewer who stood where.

**Queue context, operator only:** QUEUE.md clusters Pouget with **Rudolf Rocker**, **Georges
Sorel** and **Buenaventura Durruti**, none of which has a journey file yet; when they do, the
Amiens and *Le Sabotage* stops are the hooks from their side.
