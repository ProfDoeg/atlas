# Henry Clay Frick (1849-1919): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-23 for `henry_clay_frick.journey.json`. 45 stops in 9 segments. Register: national mythology.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition, no document behind it · **[C]** contradiction between sources, flagged and left standing.

Method note. The pool arrived with `CORRECTED` annotations from an adversarial verify stage: thirteen coordinate errors between 220 m and 60 km, five factual corrections, two upgraded quote provenances. All are applied and each is restated in the relevant `date_confidence`, because a silent correction is indistinguishable from an error.

---

## 1. West Overton and the coke ovens, 1849-1880

Born 19 December 1849 in the springhouse on the Overholt property at West Overton, East Huntingdon Township, Westmoreland County, second of six children of John W. Frick and Elizabeth Overholt Frick; his maternal grandfather Abraham Overholt ran the milling and distilling complex there **[A: NPS, "Henry Clay Frick"]**. The father's relative poverty inside a wealthy in-law family is the standard framing in NPS and the family biographies, carried as background **[R]**.

Mount Pleasant Institute 1864, then Otterbein at Westerville, Ohio, roughly ten weeks in 1866, his whole college attendance; total formal education reckoned at about thirty months **[A: NPS]**. The Institute building could not be located, so the pin marks the town **(gap)**. Then store clerk for relatives, Pittsburgh salesman, and bookkeeper at A. Overholt and Company at Broad Ford, c.1868 **[A: NPS, which itself notes that sources differ on the order of these jobs and his age at each]** **[C]**. Later co-ownership of Old Overholt with Andrew W. Mellon, and their part in founding Fidelity Title and Trust, is **[A]** on NPS-derived material, uncheckable from Mellon's side since he has no file. In 1871, aged 21, he enters coke with Abraham O. Tintsman and Joseph Rist as Frick and Company, about 300 acres and roughly 50 beehive ovens; in 1873 he borrows through the Panic to buy out failing competitors, funded partly by roughly $50,000 earned brokering a railroad's sale to the Baltimore and Ohio **[A: NPS]**.

**[C] Sole proprietorship.** The pool dated the buyout to 1879. Verify found this unsupported: NPS says "by 1880," Wikipedia gives 1880, and both agree on a first million at about age thirty. The file gives "by 1880."

## 2. Clayton and the Carnegie alliance, 1881-1889

15 December 1881, marries Adelaide Howard Childs at the Childs home at Forbes and Halket, Oakland, the site later occupied by Magee-Womens Hospital **[A: The Frick Pittsburgh]**. Same year, first documented art purchase, George Hetzel's *Landscape with River* **[A: Frick Collection art files]**. August 1882, buys the eleven-room Italianate house at 7227 Reynolds Street for about $25,000 on about 1.4 acres, moves in early in 1883, and it takes the name Clayton **[A: The Frick Pittsburgh; Wikipedia]**. 1882, reorganizes as the H. C. Frick Coke Company, about $2 million in 40,000 shares, Carnegie and associates investing, and by autumn 1883 they hold the majority **[A: NPS]**. Children: Childs 12 March 1883, Martha Howard 9 August 1885, Helen Clay 2 September 1888 **[A: Wikipedia]**. 1888, Clayton wired for electricity at Westinghouse's urging **[A: The Frick Pittsburgh]**; that it had current before the White House is the museum's own claim, repeated as such **[R]**. 1889, chairman of Carnegie Brothers, consolidating the firms that become Carnegie Steel **[A: NPS]**. Excluded for want of a source: the honeymoon call on Carnegie at the Windsor Hotel.

## 3. The coke country: water, gas, and deputies, 1889-1891

**South Fork, 31 May 1889 [A].** The dam fails after heavy rain and destroys much of Johnstown; official list 2,209 dead, estimates above 3,000 **[A: NPS]**. Frick was a founding and prominent member of the club. `andrew_carnegie.journey.json` already stages this at lat 40.3481, lng -78.7757, dated 1889-05-31, naming the club as including "Frick, Mellon, Knox and Phipps," with club lawyers Knox and Reed defeating every liability suit. **That pin and date are inherited byte-identically.** Two caveats: **[C]** Carnegie's file gives 2,208; **[R]** whether Frick co-owned or engineered the dam is disputed, so the file claims membership and promotion only.

**Mammoth No. 1 mine, 27 January 1891 [A].** Gas explosion at an H. C. Frick Coke Company mine. **[C]** Three tolls circulate: 109 on the current Pennsylvania marker, 118 in a reference cited by Wikipedia, 134 in a federal HAER history. Seventy-nine buried in a mass grave at Scottdale, and a $25,000 relief fund, both **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the pool's "near Frick Park / Mammoth" conflated this Westmoreland mine with Frick Park some 60 km off; placed at Mount Pleasant Township, 40.2005 / -79.4672.

**Morewood, March or April 1891 [C, three ways].** The pool gave 2 April, seven killed, and National Guardsmen sent by Governor Pattison. Verify found **30 March**, **nine** dead, and **Captain Loar with twelve county deputies**, later acquitted, Pattison and the militia appearing nowhere in the article. The older readings still circulate, so the file uses 30 March, nine, and the deputies while stating the contradiction, and corrects the coordinates ~2.7 km. The Pattison error matters doubly: Pattison *did* order out the Guard at Homestead in July 1892, so the pool had transposed a real 1892 fact onto 1891. That Frick used Pinkertons to escort strikebreakers in 1884 and 1891 is **[R]**, from PBS research reached by search, and serves as background only.

**Martha, August 1891 [A].** Dies a week before her sixth birthday. Frick bought the Homewood lot the day after; Section 14 monument by Daniel Burnham, graves marked with plain uniform stones **[A: "Memory and Mourning"]**. Clayton remodelled to twenty-three rooms 1891-92 **[A: frick.org]**. His own disabling rheumatoid arthritis dates from this run of family illness **[A: The Frick Pittsburgh]**.

## 4 and 5. Homestead, 1892-1893

The best-documented stretch of his life, and the only one where the pool holds substantial primary text: the printed House Judiciary Committee investigation into the Pinkertons, read from Internet Archive OCR, and the Carnegie-Frick telegrams verified from raw PBS page text. Everything in the file's sequence is **[A]** except as set out below, plus two items: the exact day of the 25 June refusal to treat with the union is **[R]**, and the day of Frick's appearance before the committee in the winter of 1892-93 is a **(gap)**.

**[C] Militia strength.** The pool claimed roughly 8,500 troops. Verify found 4,000-plus arriving 12 July under Snowden with about 2,000 more camped above the town, so about 6,000. `andrew_carnegie.journey.json` also carries 8,500. The file uses the lower figure and names the discrepancy. **This is a live inconsistency between two corpus files; the operator may want it reconciled at the Carnegie end.**

**[C] Homestead Works coordinates.** The pool gave 40.4051 / -79.9153, the borough riverfront and Pinkerton landing marker area, about 1.5 km from the mill. Carnegie's file pins the works at 40.4111 / -79.8972, **inherited byte-identically** at all three Homestead stops.

**[R] Three claims held at arm's length.** Frick Jr.'s birthplace: the pool places it at Clayton but could not confirm the house, so the file uses Clayton's pin and says so. That Frick finished the day's work before letting a surgeon in: stated as *fact* in Carnegie's file, no medical record located, so the campa says "he is said to have." That he refused anesthesia: later tradition, no surgical chart, **not asserted anywhere in the file**. **[C]** Berkman's sentence is 22 years in contemporary sources, roughly 14 served in later ones.

**Two quote provenances upgraded, both to [A].** "I will fight this thing to the bitter end. I will never recognize the Union, never, never!" is normally cited to PBS, which gives no archival source. Verify traced it to John E. Milholland's written report to Whitelaw Reid of an interview at Frick's own house on **30 July 1892**, Frick in bed with "his face and head swathed in bandages," forwarded to President Harrison and printed in Harvey p. 151; the stop is dated and placed accordingly. Second, "The mills have never been able to turn out the product they should, owing to being held back by the Amalgamated men," tagged R as Wikipedia's rendering of an unfetched letter, is cited in Wikipedia's own footnote to Harvey p. 177: the letter is dated **31 October 1892**, so it *postdates* the battle and cannot explain Frick's pre-lockout intentions, as the surrounding sentence implies it does.

**Excluded stop: Canton, Ohio, 1896.** The pool has roughly 2,500 Homestead workers travelling to hear McKinley, newspapers disputing whether attendance was coerced **[R]**. **The travellers were the workers, not Frick**, so no stop was written.

## 6. The break with Carnegie, 1897-1901

1897, James H. Reed mediates a temporary settlement **[A: The Frick Pittsburgh]**. 5 December 1899, resigns **[A: Pitt, Frick Business Records finding aid]**. 14 February 1900, files suit in the Allegheny County Court of Common Pleas after Carnegie invokes the "iron-clad" agreement; settled out of court at Atlantic City later that year, his interest recognized near real value and his shares worth above $30 million within two **[A: Duane Morris, "Frick v. Carnegie"]**. **(gap)** The source names the city but no building and no date; the file's month and day are an ordering placeholder with no evidentiary weight. 1901, Morgan organizes United States Steel and Frick becomes a director **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**. 1903, Roosevelt tries to recruit him to the Isthmian Canal Commission and he declines **[R: no letter or date located]**, carried as a clause inside the U.S. Steel stop, since no Washington visit is documented.

## 7 and 8. New York and the pictures, 1904-1919

- **Eagle Rock, Prides Crossing [C].** The pool gave 1906; verify found NPS, used elsewhere in the same chronology, dating it to **1904**. The file uses 1904 for ordering and names the discrepancy. Coordinates corrected ~1.5 km to the village pin; the site is a **(gap)**, the source placing it only "between Hale Street and the Atlantic."
- 1905, leases 640 Fifth Avenue, the mansion built for William Henry Vanderbilt **[A]**. Titian's Pietro Aretino through Knoedler the same year, his first Italian Renaissance painting, and the Van Dycks, are **[R]**, unverified from a primary art file and folded into that stop rather than given one of their own.
- **1905, Equitable Life Assurance Society [C, claim softened].** The pool had Frick *chairing a committee investigating* the Hyde affair with E. H. Harriman and **James J. Hill**. Verify found Harriman, Frick, Morgan, and president James Waddell Alexander named among the board figures moving against Hyde, but **no Frick-chaired committee and no Hill at all**. The file drops both.
- 1912, the Titanic passage cancelled after Adelaide's ankle injury **[A: Smithsonian]**. The source says only "Italy," so Rome is an **explicit placeholder pin (gap)**. No evidence connects the cancellation to foreknowledge.
- 1912-1914, 1 East 70th Street built by Carrere and Hastings on the Lenox Library site for near $5 million **[A: Wikipedia]**. **[C] Move-in:** the pool's chronology gave 17 November 1914, its own geography entry 16 November, Wikipedia **16 November**; the file uses the 16th and names the inconsistency.
- 24 June 1915, final will **[A for substance, R for the date]**: frick.org confirms the roughly $15 million endowment and the will's role in creating The Frick Collection, but the date rests on an unverified finding-aid citation. Also **[R]**: the 1915 Fragonard installation, the 1915-1918 Morgan estate purchases through Duveen (720 Fifth Avenue corrected ~500 m), and the 1919 Vermeer as last purchase (Knoedler confirmed at 556 Fifth Avenue 1911-1925, pin corrected ~250 m).
- 2 December 1919, dies of a heart attack at 1 East 70th Street, aged 69 **[A: Wikipedia; NPS]**. The 1923 reappraisal at about $92,953,552 is **[R]**, from a probate record cited in the dossier and not fetched.

## 9. After

Burial at Homewood Cemetery, December 1919, in the lot bought the day after Martha's death **[A]**; the pool's coordinates fell ~1.7 km south, inside Frick Park, corrected to 40.441 / -79.909. The concrete-covered vault, said to have been requested for fear of what might be done to his remains, is **[R]**: one outlet, search-indexed only, the cemetery's own history silent, and the campa says so.

Frick Park, 151 acres and a $2 million trust, opened 25 June 1927, now 644 acres **[A: Wikipedia]**; the pool gave 150 acres and "nearly 600 acres by 1942," unsupported by its own cited source. Princeton, roughly $6 million, with the Frick Chemistry Laboratory of 1929 and a second building of 2010 **[A for the bequest, R for the dates]**. The Frick Collection: Adelaide dies 1931, John Russell Pope converts the house, soft opening for 700 invited visitors 11 December 1935, public opening 16 December; closed 2020, Frick Madison March 2021 to March 2024, reopened 17 April 2025 **[A]**. Clayton opens as a house museum in 1990 after Helen Clay Frick's death in 1984, is renovated 2013-2015, and is listed on the National Register in May 2025 **[A]**.

By 2020-2023 the Frick Pittsburgh is publicly reexamining Frick's "fraught legacy," naming the strike and the Pinkerton deployment, PA SHARP funded, prompted in part by 2020 and by its position beside the historically Black Homewood neighborhood; the 2022 exhibition "Slay" paired an Artemisia Gentileschi with a Kehinde Wiley **[A: PA Humanities, 2023]**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Frick is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed on his file. Per the adversary's duty, relations in the material **not rooted in Frick's own life** are named here rather than deleted, with the direction each points.

1. **Forward: the anarchist cluster.** Emma Goldman, Berkman's partner and, on her own account, a planner of the 1892 attempt, recurs across the corpus: `buenaventura_durruti.journey.json` (1936), `lucy_parsons.journey.json` (1897), `rudolf_rocker.journey.json` (1933). **None mentions Frick, and the Durruti and Rocker episodes postdate his death.** Goldman has no file of her own, and the relation runs one way, from her outward. She is named once in Frick's file, at the sentencing stop, for the one attested fact that she helped plan the attempt and was not charged. The other three are **not** named. That thread belongs on a Goldman file.
2. **Forward: the commemorative apparatus.** The Bost Building (NHL 1999), the markers for the strike (1992) and its victims (1993), and the 1989 designation of the Pinkerton landing site are later engagements with places Frick's actions made. They sit in `suggested_refs` and `sources` on his Homestead stops, never staged as scenes.
3. **Forward: the deathbed legend.** "I'll see him in Hell, where we both are going," said to be his reply to a reconciliation message from the dying Carnegie, and the title of Les Standiford's 2005 *Meet You in Hell* **[R]**. No contemporaneous source was located; the pool judges it a legend crystallized by the book's title. **It is nowhere in the file**, flagged because it is the most-quoted Frick line and its absence would look like an oversight.
4. **Not a relation at all: the Vanderbilt lead.** 640 Fifth Avenue was built for William Henry Vanderbilt, not the Commodore, and `cornelius_vanderbilt.journey.json` neither reaches that campaign nor mentions Frick. Naming the builder is a fact about the house and claims **no** interlock.

**Backward: none found.** Frick left no theoretical framework to apply to anything.

## Interlocks used

- **`andrew_carnegie.journey.json`**: partner, employer, adversary, litigant, correspondent, fellow club member, named at six stops. Two canonical pins inherited byte-identically, **Homestead Steel Works 40.4111 / -79.8972** (three stops) and **South Fork Dam 40.3481 / -78.7757** (one), with the 1892-06-28 and 1889-05-31 dates matching his file. Frick's stops sit there as the executive who was in Pittsburgh while Carnegie was in Scotland.
- **`john_d_rockefeller_sr.journey.json`**: named once, at the 1901 U.S. Steel stop, for the sale of his Mesabi ore and lake fleet from 26 Broadway into the same Morgan combination on whose board Frick took a seat. A corporate relation between parties to one negotiation, not shared geography: Frick's stop is on Wall Street, Rockefeller's at 26 Broadway, neither pin borrowed.

**Corpus gaps blocking obvious interlocks.** No file exists for Mellon, Morgan, Roosevelt, McKinley, Taft, Knox, Schwab, Goldman, or Berkman, all documented Frick interlocutors. Their names appear in campas where the underlying fact is attested, but none is an interlock, there being nothing on the other side to interlock with.
