# Policarpa Salavarrieta, "La Pola" (c. 1795 - 14 November 1817)
*Research report, 2026-08-11. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys, slug `policarpa_salavarrieta`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim not corroborated by a second reachable source. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and left standing; nothing below is silently harmonized.

**Method and its limits.** Every claim rests on tertiary encyclopedic material: Spanish Wikipedia, English Wikipedia, biografiasyvidas.com, literato.es, and the Banco de la República cultural encyclopedia (`enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org`). **No primary document was read in this pass.** The trial record of the Consejo de Guerra Permanente, the royalist gazettes of Santafé for November 1817, and any correspondence of the network sit in Colombia's Archivo General de la Nación and were not accessed. Where the encyclopedias disagree there is no deeper court of appeal available here, and the disagreements are recorded rather than settled.

---

## 1. Birth and origin, c. 1795

**Birth date.** Most sources give **26 January 1795** [A: es.wikipedia]. Other encyclopedic accounts give 1796; Banrepcultural widens the range to **1793-1795** [A: enciclopedia.banrepcultural.org]. The date is not settled. The canon has fixed on 26 January 1795, and the atlas follows the canon while flagging the range here.

**Birthplace, three claims, not adjudicated.**
- **Guaduas**, San Miguel de Guaduas, Cundinamarca, a market town on the royal road between Santafé and the Magdalena port of Honda [R]. The Colombian Academy of History affirmed Guaduas on **10 September 1991** [R: es.wikipedia; the ruling is reported, not read]. An academy voted on the birthplace of a national heroine. The vote is not a document.
- **Barrio Santa Bárbara, Santafé de Bogotá** [R: biografiasyvidas.com], a rival tradition surviving in older accounts.
- **Mariquita, Tolima** [R: biografiasyvidas.com], a minority claim listed with no documentation cited.

**Parents.** Joaquín Salavarrieta and Mariana Ríos [A: es.wikipedia].

## 2. Bogotá and the smallpox, 1797-1802

The family relocates from Guaduas to Santafé de Bogotá, settling in the parish of **Santa Bárbara** [A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]. **Year contradiction, unresolved:** es.wikipedia gives 1797, biografiasyvidas.com gives 1798.

*Coordinate:* the colonial Santa Bárbara parish sits at Carrera 7 with Calle 6a, **4.59222, -74.07861** [A: es.wikipedia, "Iglesia de Santa Bárbara"], its limits between calles 6 and 7 and carreras 4 and 10.

In **1802** a smallpox epidemic sweeps the capital and kills both parents and two of the children [A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia; en.wikipedia dates the deaths to August and September]. **Name contradiction, unresolved:** es.wikipedia names the dead siblings **Joaquín and María Ignacia**; en.wikipedia names **Eduardo and María Ignacia**. Both agree on María Ignacia; the brother's name is in dispute.

## 3. The Guaduas adolescence, c. 1804-1810

Her older sister Catalina takes Policarpa and their brother Bibiano back to Guaduas, into the household of her godmother **Margarita Beltrán** and an aunt named Manuela [A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]. The year is given as circa 1804 by the source itself, not as a fixed date.

She works as a **seamstress** [A: en.wikipedia]. The claim that she also **taught in a public school** is flagged by Wikipedia itself as traditional rather than documented [R], as is the related tradition that she studied and later taught reading at the **Convento de la Soledad** in Guaduas; no primary document of attendance or of a teaching role was located [R: biografiasyvidas.com].

**Alejo Sabaraín.** By about 1809 she is said to have met the brothers Leandro and Alejo Sabaraín in Guaduas, beginning a relationship with Alejo [R: biografiasyvidas.com]. Not corroborated by any other source fetched. Day-level precision elsewhere on that page proved unreliable (see section 4), so the whole courtship chronology is carried as reconstruction.

## 4. The revolution reaches Guaduas, 1810-1813

News of the **20 July 1810** declaration in Santafé reaches Guaduas; biografiasyvidas.com dates her learning of it to **22 July 1810** [R]. The two-day lag is plausible for the royal road, but the source does not explain how it knows it.

**Santafé, January 1812.** biografiasyvidas.com places her in the capital from January 1812, employed "como niñera y costurera" in the household of **María Matea Martínez de Zaldúa** [R]. Single-source: a full-text scan confirms the name does not appear in the es.wikipedia article, which dates her active involvement from 1816. **Contradiction, unresolved:** either she is resident in Bogotá from 1812, or she arrives in January 1817 (section 6). Both framings come from the same source page, and the atlas records both.

**San Victorino, 9 January 1813.** The January 1813 fighting at Santafé was the **Battle of San Victorino**, a **patriot civil war** between Antonio Nariño's centralist Estado Libre de Cundinamarca and the federalist Provincias Unidas under Baraya. Both sides were patriots; the centralists won, federalist losses given as more than 600 dead and 1,235 captured [A: es.wikipedia, "Batalla de San Victorino"]. Any description of it as a defence of the city against an attacker implies a royalist enemy and is wrong. Her brother-in-law Domingo García is said to have died later in Nariño's campaigns [R: biografiasyvidas.com].

**Sabaraín's tabardillo.** biografiasyvidas.com places Sabaraín's arrival in Santafé as a subteniente and his falling ill with tabardillo, a typhus-like fever, in **February 1813**, contracted on arrival [R]. A working draft dated the illness to June 1813 and added "recovers by mid-1813"; neither the June date nor the recovery clause is in the source, and both are dropped.

Patriot forces including Sabaraín march out of Santafé on the **Campaña del Sur** on 23 September 1813 [R: biografiasyvidas.com]. Policarpa is said to return to Guaduas by the end of 1813 [R: same source].

## 5. The reconquest, 1815-1816

Pablo Morillo's expeditionary army besieges **Cartagena de Indias** through 1815, the city capitulating on **6 December 1815**; the march inland begins only in **February 1816**, by the Magdalena and Ocaña. Cachirí falls 22 February 1816, the news reaching Santafé on 5 March [A: es.wikipedia, "Reconquista (Colombia)"].

**Correction, chronology.** The royalist occupation of Guaduas is often dated **1815**. That is impossible on the timeline above, and a full-text scan of the es.wikipedia Policarpa article contains no such claim: Guaduas, on the Honda-Bogotá royal road, falls in **1816** [R].

Morillo enters Santafé de Bogotá in **May 1816** and inaugurates the **Régimen del Terror**, executing patriot leaders [A: es.wikipedia]. This is the political weather of everything that follows.

**Recruitment.** A patriot officer named only as **Coronel Rodríguez** is said to approach Policarpa in Guaduas in December 1816 and propose that she work as an intelligence courier [R: biografiasyvidas.com]. Single-source; the officer is not further identified.

## 6. The network in Bogotá, 1817

She reaches the capital in **January 1817**, per biografiasyvidas.com accompanied by her brother Bibiano and carrying forged safe-conduct papers, posing as a seamstress seeking work in royalist households [R: single-source, uncorroborated by either Wikipedia]. See the 1812/1817 contradiction in section 4.

From 1816 she is actively involved in patriot resistance work, collaborating with **Andrea Ricaurte Torres de Lozano** and **María del Carmen Rodríguez Obregón** [A: es.wikipedia]. She lodges in Andrea Ricaurte's house, the operational center of the network [A: en.wikipedia]. From there she gathers intelligence inside the royalist households she sews for, relays messages between cells, helps purchase arms and war material, and recruits young men for the independence forces, including the guerrillas of the Llanos [A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]. A clandestine refuge in the **Barrio Egipto**, at the corner of the calle del Calvario and the carrera de San Bruno, is named as passing messages and recruits [R: biografiasyvidas.com].

The plains base the recruits are sent toward is **Pore, Casanare** [R]. The routes and intermediaries are documented nowhere in what was read here: the atlas can name the destination and cannot trace the road.

**The exposure.** Two mechanisms are given, not mutually exclusive:
1. The brothers **Ambrosio and Vicente Almeida** are captured carrying documents that compromise her [A: es.wikipedia].
2. **Alejo Sabaraín**, setting out for the Llanos in early August 1817 and captured on **3 September 1817** along the eastern route [R: biografiasyvidas.com], is carrying a **list of names of royalists and patriots that Policarpa had given him** [A: es.wikipedia]. The capture site is fixed by no source read; any coordinate is an approximation along the road corridor and is labeled as one.

## 7. Arrest, trial, execution, 10-14 November 1817

**The arrest.** es.wikipedia states she is arrested after a brief attempt at escape, seized "tan solo unas cuadras al sur" of Andrea Ricaurte's house, so the arrest did **not** occur at the house itself [A: es.wikipedia]. Her brother Bibiano is taken with her [R: biografiasyvidas.com].

**The 10 November problem, unresolved.** biografiasyvidas.com says she was detained "en la noche del 10 de noviembre" [R]. es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia and Banrepcultural all assign **10 November 1817** to the **sentence** of the Consejo de Guerra, four days before the execution [A]. The same date cannot be both. The sentencing is the better-attested reading; the arrest date is left open.

**The prison.** She is held in a dungeon of the **Colegio Mayor de Nuestra Señora del Rosario**, its colonial university building converted to a makeshift prison, while the tribunal deliberates [A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia]. *Coordinate:* the Claustro is at Calle 12c #6-25, **4.600231, -74.073465** [A: es.wikipedia, "Universidad del Rosario"]; a working coordinate 390 m south of it is not used.

The **Iglesia del Nazareno** is named among the religious sites tied to her final imprisonment, possibly where the condemned received last rites; the source lists the church without procedural detail [R: biografiasyvidas.com].

**The sentence.** A royalist **Consejo de Guerra** condemns her to death along with Alejo Sabaraín and others on **10 November 1817** [A: es.wikipedia, en.wikipedia, Banrepcultural]. **Count:** es.wikipedia's name list gives **nine executed in all**, Policarpa, Alejo Sabaraín Ramos, and seven others: José María Arcos, Francisco Arellano, José Manuel Díaz, Manuel Salvador Díaz, Antonio Galeano, Esteban Marufu (Mariño), José María Suárez. Counts of six or eight companions, circulating in working drafts, are wrong.

**The execution.** By firing squad at **nine in the morning on Friday 14 November 1817**, in the Plaza Mayor, today the Plaza de Bolívar [A: es.wikipedia, "las nueve de la mañana del viernes 14 de noviembre de 1817", corroborated by en.wikipedia and Banrepcultural; the date was indeed a Friday]. She refused to kneel or be shot in the back, the position customarily used for traitors, and faced the squad [A: en.wikipedia].

**The last words, four competing texts.** All are widely reproduced; **none of the pages that carry them cites a primary document**, no trial record and no eyewitness diary. They are textually attested in secondary literature and not primary-source verified. The variants:

| Text | Source |
|---|---|
| "¡Pueblo indolente! ¡Cuán distinta sería hoy vuestra suerte si conocierais el precio de la libertad!" | es.wikipedia, "Juicio y muerte" |
| "¡Pueblo indolente! ¡Cuan diversa sería hoy vuestra suerte si conocieseis el precio de la libertad!" | literato.es |
| "Pero no es tarde: ved que, aunque mujer y joven, me sobra valor para sufrir la muerte y mil muertes más." | es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia (both set "aunque mujer y joven" between dashes) |
| "Pero no es tarde. Ved que, mujer y joven, me sobra valor para sufrir la muerte y mil muertes más." | biografiasyvidas.com (no "aunque"; period, not colon) |
| "Viles soldados, volved las armas a los enemigos de vuestra patria." | en.wikipedia, "Trial and death" |
| "Muero por defender los derechos de mi patria." | en.wikipedia only; the other renderings end differently |
| "Miserable pueblo, yo os compadezco. ¡Algún día tendréis más dignidad!" | en.wikipedia; literato.es swaps to "Pueblo miserable" |

A further clause, "No olvidéis este ejemplo," circulates in fuller Spanish renderings but **could not be re-located in any page fetched and read in full in this pass** [R, unverified rather than seen].

**The refused drink.** en.wikipedia gives "I would not accept even a glass of water from my enemies!" in English only, without exclamation marks. es.wikipedia supplies the Spanish verbatim, **"¡No aceptaría ni un vaso de agua de mis enemigos!"**, and specifies that what the guard offered was **wine**: the force of the line is that she would refuse even water, the humblest thing. No primary citation on either page.

**An epitaph by a contemporary, not by her.** "Yace por salvar la patria," attributed by biografiasyvidas.com to fellow prisoner **Joaquín Monsalve**, composed in prison to honor her [A for the attribution as that page makes it].

## 8. The body, and where it is not

**Contradiction, unresolved.** es.wikipedia states she is first interred in a **common grave at the Iglesia de la Veracruz**, and that her two priest brothers later move her remains to the **Iglesia de San Agustín**. en.wikipedia and Banrepcultural both state the brothers claimed the body **directly** for San Agustín, with no Veracruz stage; Banrepcultural adds that her body, unlike her companions', was **never exposed in the streets** ("sus hermanos sacerdotes lo reclamaron y lo guardaron en la iglesia de San Agustín"). The Veracruz stage is single-source and is carried as [R], not [A].

*Coordinates:* Veracruz sits at Carrera 7 with Calle 16, the former Calle del Arco, **4.60236, -74.07292**; San Agustín at Carrera 7 with Calle 7, behind the Casa de Nariño, **4.594262, -74.077759**.

**The gap that does not close.** The **present-day location of her remains is unknown**; es.wikipedia states explicitly that it is not established [A]. There is no marked tomb and no pilgrimage grave. A national heroine on the banknote has no findable body.

**No corpus of writings.** No preserved letters or writings are documented in any source consulted, beyond the **false passport issued to her in 1817**. Stated as a gap in the sources, not as evidence that she left nothing.

## 9. Afterlife

- **1870**, Piura, Peru: a **Liberty statue bearing her image**, inaugurated under José Balta in honour of the resident Colombian colony [A: es.wikipedia]. The title "Alegoría de la Libertad" appears nowhere in the cited source and is dropped.
- **27 November 1893**, Buenos Aires: a street named **"Pola"** by municipal ordinance; Argentine and Uruguayan playwrights and poets take her as a subject [A: es.wikipedia].
- **1910**: centennial stamps, including a 1-centavo issue bearing her; a statue by **Dionisio Cortés** at Carrera 2A with Calle 18, Las Aguas, Bogotá, 4.6033/-74.0669 [A: both wikis]. A sculpture also stands at **Chiquinquirá, Boyacá**, and a municipality in Nariño carries her name [A: es.wikipedia].
- **1911**: the Bavaria brewery launches a beer named **"La Pola"**; *pola* becomes generic Colombian slang for beer [A: en.wikipedia].
- **1967**: **Ley 44 of 9 November 1967**, signed by Carlos Lleras Restrepo, designates **14 November** as the **Día de la Mujer Colombiana** [A: es.wikipedia]. en.wikipedia gives the law itself as "November 14, 1967", conflating the law with the day it designates; the difference is flagged, the Spanish text followed.
- **Currency**: the **2-peso oro banknote** of 1 January 1972, purple, Balsa Muisca on the reverse [A: es.wikipedia, "Peso colombiano"]; the **5-peso coin**, 1980-1988 [A: es.wikipedia Policarpa article only, uncorroborated]; the **10,000-peso banknote** from 1 March 1995 to 2016, Plaza of Guaduas on the reverse, replaced by **Virginia Gutiérrez de Pineda** [A]; a bicentennial coin, 10 January 2023 [A].
- **13 September 2010 to 27 July 2011**: RCN Televisión broadcasts **"La Pola,"** 98 episodes, directed by Sergio Cabrera, Carolina Ramírez as the adult Policarpa and Ana María Estupiñán as the adolescent [A: es.wikipedia, "La Pola (telenovela)"]. The run is 2010-2011, not 2010 alone.
- A punk band takes the name **"Policarpa y sus Viciosas"**; a tarantula, **Pamphobeteus lapola**, is named for her in 2022 [A: en.wikipedia].
- **A legend, carried as legend:** María Francisca Villanova, wife of Viceroy Antonio José Amar y Borbón, is said to have visited her house in Guaduas and foretold her fate [R: en.wikipedia].
- The **Casa Museo Policarpa Salavarrieta** in Guaduas anchors national memory of the birthplace. Its designation as her childhood home rests on local tradition, not on a documented chain of title [R].

## 10. Contradictions, listed and not adjudicated

1. Birth year: 1795 vs 1796 vs a 1793-1795 range.
2. Birthplace: Guaduas vs Santa Bárbara, Bogotá vs Mariquita, Tolima.
3. Move to Bogotá: 1797 (es.wiki) vs 1798 (biografiasyvidas).
4. Sibling killed by smallpox: Joaquín (es.wiki) vs Eduardo (en.wiki).
5. Arrival in Bogotá as an adult: January 1812 vs January 1817, same source page carrying both.
6. 10 November 1817: arrest (biografiasyvidas) vs sentencing (the other three sources).
7. Number of the condemned: the es.wiki name list yields seven companions, nine in all.
8. Burial: Veracruz then San Agustín (es.wiki) vs direct to San Agustín (en.wiki, Banrepcultural).
9. Last words: four distinct texts and further variants, none primary-sourced.
10. Ley 44 of 1967: 9 November (es.wiki) vs 14 November (en.wiki).

## 11. Gaps, stated as gaps

- No primary archival material was read. The Archivo General de la Nación trial records were not accessed.
- The routes and intermediaries by which recruits and intelligence moved from Bogotá to Casanare are undocumented in what was read; the destination is named, the road is not.
- The site of Sabaraín's capture on 3 September 1817 is fixed by no source; the journey file marks its coordinate as an approximation along the corridor.
- The present location of her remains is unknown.
- No corpus of her own writing survives, or none is documented.
- The identity of "Coronel Rodríguez," the officer said to have recruited her, is not established.
