# Nicholas of Cusa (Nikolaus von Kues, Nicolaus Cusanus), 1401-1464
*Research report, second pass, 2026-08-16. Prepared for `nicholas_of_cusa.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are printed side by side and left standing. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** Four lenses (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock) were gathered, then re-verified adversarially against German and English Wikipedia, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP), MacTutor, the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia, and Harper's 2007 printing of the Latin *Compendium*. Fourteen first-pass items were overturned; the corrections are recorded in place, not absorbed silently.

---

## 1. Moselle and schools, 1401-1425

**Birth, 1401, Kues. [A]** Son of Johan Krebs (Cryfftz), boatman and shipowner on the Moselle, and Katherina Roemer. The birth house survives. *(en.wiki "Bernkastel-Kues"; MacTutor; Meuthen.)*
- **Coordinate.** First pass pinned Kues at 49.9143, 7.0785: about 560 m off, on the **Bernkastel bank across the river**. The St. Nikolaus-Hospital is **49.9160, 7.0712**; centroid 49.91611, 7.06944. The file keeps the corpus's inherited pin (49.9188, 7.0679) on the birth stop and the corrected hospital coordinate on the two Cusanusstift stops. Flagged, not harmonised.

**Deventer, c. 1413. [R]** Older lives put the boy in the Brethren of the Common Life school beside the Lebuinuskerk. **No document places him there.** Meuthen flags it unproven; SEP does not mention Deventer at all; en.wiki hedges to "may have been their pupil." **Left open.**

**Heidelberg, 1416. [A]** Faculty of Arts, entered as a cleric of the diocese of Trier. *(de.wiki; MacTutor.)* **Coordinate:** first pass gave 49.3988, 8.6724, a centroid 2.5 km west of the Altstadt; the medieval quarter is Universitätsplatz, **49.4111, 8.7061**. The Alte Universität *building* (1712-1728) postdates him.

**Padua, 1417-1423. [A]** Canon law; doctorate 1423. Associates: Toscanelli, Giuliano Cesarini (later dedicatee of *De docta ignorantia*), Domenico Capranica. *(SEP; en.wiki; Meuthen.)* Palazzo del Bo is not the studium's seat until 1493, so the pin is the quarter.

**Cologne, 1425. [A / contradiction]** Doctor of canon law, teaching and practising; Heimerich de Campo puts Ramon Llull, Albertus Magnus and Proclus into his hands. Manuscript-hunting begins here (recovered Plautus). **Wikipedia 1425 vs SEP 1426: not adjudicated.**

## 2. The canonist's trade, 1425-1431

**Trier, from early February 1425. [A]** Service to Archbishop Otto von Ziegenhain *(de.wiki)*. **Correction:** the first-pass chronology folded this appointment and the Rome delegation into one 1427 line. Two events, two years: **service early 1425**, **Rome 1427**. The geography lens had 1425 right independently.

**Rome, 1427. [A]** First sight of the curia as episcopal delegate. **The content of the errand is nowhere stated: a gap.**

**Koblenz, deanery of St. Florin, 1427-1445. [A]** *(de.wiki benefice list.)* An income, not a residence. An English source dating the appointment to 1430 is **impossible**: Otto, who conferred it, died that year.

**Paris, 1428. [A]** Travels at his own expense to copy Llull's *Ars*; the quires end up in his library at Kues. *(de.wiki; Meuthen.)* **Coordinate:** first pass gave 48.8534, 2.3488, about 680 m NNE of the Sorbonne on the Right Bank, outside the quarter (the Sorbonne building is 48.8485, 2.3432). **But** the file's existing Paris pin (48.8487, 2.3438) is a documented deliberate deviation from the corpus civic pin, explained in its own `date_confidence`, and is **inherited unchanged rather than "corrected."**

**Louvain, 1428 and 1435. [R]** Offered a chair twice, refused twice *(SEP)*. **Priestly ordination, 1430s. [R]** SEP dates it only approximately; **no year is fixed anywhere reached.** This matters because one English account calls the 26 April 1450 episcopal consecration an "ordination," which it is not. **Nuremberg diet, 1431. [A]** Advocate for Ulrich von Manderscheid in the disputed Trier succession *(de.wiki)*.

## 3. Basel, 1432-1437

**Arrival 1432. [A]** At the council in the Manderscheid interest *(en.wiki)*. **Donation of Constantine shown a forgery, 1433. [A]** Demonstrated philologically from the Latin, years before Valla's independent demonstration. *(en.wiki; SEP; *De concordantia catholica* III.)*

***De concordantia catholica*, 1433/1434. [A / contradiction]** SEP 1433; Wikipedia no year at all; Wikiquote's translated text 1434. **Left standing.** Authority rising from consent, councils above popes, and an imperial election in which each elector ranks every candidate (an early precursor of the Borda count).

**Crossing to Eugenius IV; departure 17 May 1437. [A]** *(de.wiki; SEP.)* **Motive is nowhere stated.** Contemporaries called it opportunism; the record does not settle it and neither does the journey file.

## 4. The Greek embassy, 1437-1438

**[A]** Leaves Basel 17 May 1437, ships from Venice, reaches Constantinople 24 September 1437; negotiates the attendance of John VIII Palaiologos, Patriarch Joseph II and the Greek bishops. *(de.wiki; en.wiki; SEP.)* **The site of any imperial audience is unattested.** Blachernae was the standing residence by 1437, but that is inference, so the stop was **cut rather than staged on a guess**.

**The illumination at sea. [A as Cusa's own claim]** Reported in the dedicatory letter to Cesarini prefacing *De docta ignorantia*: received from above, on shipboard, returning from Greece, with **no date and no position**. **Correction:** first pass dated this "autumn 1438," a year out. The fleet **sailed west in November 1437** and **landed at Venice 8 February 1438**; the Greeks reached Ferrara early April 1438 *(de.wiki; en.wiki "Council of Florence")*. The stop is dated December 1437; the Aegean coordinate (36.5, 25.5) is carried forward as a symbolic midpoint and labelled as one.

## 5. The papal cause, 1438-1449

***De docta ignorantia* completed 12 February 1440. [A on date, R on place]** The colophon gives the day. **The place appears in no source reached**, only that he was in Germany rather than at sea. The geography lens proposed Koblenz (from the St. Florin provostship); the inherited pin is Kues. **Both are reconstructions; the disagreement is printed in `date_confidence`.** **Book II [A]:** earth neither at rest nor exactly central; no fixed centre, no circumference; God, "who is everywhere and nowhere," is circumference and centre *(II.11-12, trans. Hopkins; SEP)*. The argument is **metaphysical, not observational**, and proposes nothing resembling Copernican heliocentrism.

***Coincidentia oppositorum*, Book I. [A as doctrine, R as phrase]** **Contradiction flagged:** Hopkins' Translator's Introduction states that Cusa "nowhere says, in so many words, *Deus est coincidentia oppositorum*." The Latin tag is later shorthand; the doctrine pervades Book I (I.4, I.22). ***De coniecturis*. [A / contradiction]** Wikipedia 1441-42 vs SEP 1442-43. **Not adjudicated.**

**Astronomical purchases, 1444. [A, deflated]** MacTutor: he "purchased sixteen books on astronomy, a wooden celestial globe, a copper celestial globe and various astronomical instruments including an astrolabe." First pass inflated this into a **commissioned astrolabe with his name engraved in the brass, bought at Nuremberg**: none of that is in any source reached, and de.wiki gives no place. The journey file states the purchase and states that the place is unrecorded.

**Frankfurt Reichstag, 1446. [A]** Legation work on the princes for Eugenius IV. Piccolomini's epithet **"Hercules of the Eugenians" is genuine** *(Catholic Encyclopedia 1913)* and belongs **here**.

**Aschaffenburg, January 1447. [A]** The Princes' Concordat ends fifteen years of imperial neutrality *(de.wiki: "Aschaffenburg 1447")*. **Correction:** first pass staged this at **Mainz 1447** and credited Cusa with the **Concordat of Vienna**. Both wrong: that concordat (17 February 1448) was signed for the papacy by **Cardinal Juan Carvajal** and **does not name Cusa** *(en.wiki)*. Cusa's documented Mainz diet is **1441**. Scene relocated; epithet moved to Frankfurt 1446.

**The cardinalate, three stages, previously muddled. [A]** (i) **1446:** Eugenius IV nominates or intends him, **never publishes**, dies 23 February 1447 (de.wiki: "Cardinal (in pectore): planned by Eugen IV before his death"). (ii) **20 December 1448:** created *in pectore* by **Nicholas V**, published January 1449 (en.wiki). (iii) **11 January 1450:** red hat at Rome with the title of San Pietro in Vincoli (de.wiki; the 1913 Catholic Encyclopedia dates the proclamation as cardinal-priest of St Peter ad Vincula to 1449). First pass treated 1446 as a creation and 1448/49 as the investiture. **Both corrected; the stages kept distinct.**

## 6. Brixen and the Great Legation, 1450-1452

**Bishop of Brixen: appointed 23 March 1450, consecrated 26 April 1450. [A]** Papal legate to the German lands from 24 December 1450. **The *Idiota* dialogues, 1450 [A]:** *De sapientia*, *De mente*, *De staticis experimentis*, set among the Roman jubilee crowds. *De mente* grounds mind in measure: "Mind [mens] takes its name from measuring [mensurare]." *(I.57, trans. Hopkins, 1996, p. 533.)*

**The Great Legation, 1451 to spring 1452. [A in outline, R in itinerary]** Nearly three thousand miles; jubilee indulgence, reform synods, monastic visitation. **This is the weakest evidence in the file.** de.wiki gives only the commission date and an early-spring-1452 end, **with no route**. Salzburg, Vienna, Magdeburg, Hildesheim, Würzburg, Liège and Utrecht rest on **Meuthen alone and were not confirmable leg by leg.** First pass also dated Würzburg 1451 while calling Liège and Utrecht the "final northern stretch" of 1452, **an ordering inconsistency it flagged itself and did not fix.** The journey file carries a reduced set of legation stops, each with the weakness in `date_confidence`. **Coordinates corrected:** Magdeburg **52.12472, 11.63444** (was 52.1259, 11.6389); Hildesheim **52.14889, 9.94722**; Würzburg **49.79333, 9.93222**.

**Taking up the diocese, 1452. [A]** Sigismund holds advocacy, castles and nobility; conflict immediate, including the Sonnenburg convent's refusal of visitation.

## 7. The feud, 1453-1460

**1453, two books. [A]** *De visione Dei* for the monks of Tegernsee (the all-seeing icon; the wall of paradise within which opposites coincide) and *De pace fidei*, in the wake of the May conquest of Constantinople. **Andraz (Buchenstein), July 1457 to September 1458. [A]** Fourteen months of refuge *(de.wiki)*. **Enneberg, 5 April 1458 [A]:** armed clash in the Gadertal, carried in the Andraz campa rather than given a stop of its own.

**Cusanusstift, founding deed 3 December 1458. [A]** *(de.wiki "St. Nikolaus-Hospital"; en.wiki "Cusanusstift".)*
- **Corrections.** (i) The afterlife lens dated the foundation **1451**; that is the building campaign (contradiction 5 below). (ii) Not "thirty-three poor men" but **six nobles, six priests, twenty-one commoners**, thirty-three for the years of Christ's life. (iii) First pass said he left heart-burial instructions "decades ahead of his death"; December 1458 to August 1464 is **under six years**.

**Vicar general of the Papal States, 11 January 1459. [A]** First pass dated both this appointment and his departure from the Tyrol to 1461. **Named legate and vicar general 11 January 1459** *(de.wiki; en.wiki)*; **left the Tyrol 27 April 1460**, never to return.

**Bruneck, Easter 1460; capitulation 17 April 1460. [A]** Sigismund besieges the residence with a heavy bombard; Cusa signs dictated terms. Pius II excommunicates the duke and interdicts the Tyrol, to little effect. *(Pius II, *Commentaries*; de.wiki; en.wiki.)* Coordinate **46.7982, 11.9367**.

## 8. Roman exile and last books, 1461-1464

***Cribratio Alchorani* and *De li non aliud*, 1461. [A]** *(SEP.)* The **Mecca pin (21.4225, 39.8262)** is a canonical corpus coordinate, **inherited byte-identical**, marking the subject of the text, not a journey; Cusa never went to Arabia. The **Temple Mount pin (31.778, 35.2354)** is inherited on the same terms for *De pace fidei*. ***De venatione sapientiae* 1462; *De ludo globi* 1462-63. [A]** The ball hollowed on one side, which will not run straight but curls to rest along a spiral.

**The *Compendium* and the mind as a walled city.**
- **Content [A].** Genuine and correctly located at ***Compendium*, cap. viii.** Latin verified via Harper's: "Est igitur animal perfectum, in quo sensus et intellectus, considerandum ut homo cosmographus habens civitatem quinque portarum quinque sensuum..." A shut gate leaves the description defective; the cosmographer keeps all gates open, reduces the whole to "a well-ordered and proportionally measured map," then dismisses the messengers, shuts the gates, and turns his inner sight to the Creator.
- **Date, unresolved.** See contradiction 4 below. The stop is dated 1464 with the disagreement in `date_confidence`.
- **Chapter number, unverified.** "Chapter 8" is consensus and matches Harper's "cap. viii," but Opera Omnia XI/3 (Meiner) was unreachable and **jasper-hopkins.info is now a dead Bluehost placeholder**, so Hopkins' section and page numbering could not be checked.
- **Citation correction, serious.** First pass credited the English to Harper's "Findings," November 2007. The item is **Scott Horton's "No Comment" blog, Harper's Magazine, 18 November 2007, "Nicholas of Kues: the Cosmographer's Tale."** Worse, the wording presented as a quotation **matches neither Horton nor Hopkins**, who renders *cosmographus* as **geographer**. **The journey file therefore quotes only Hopkins, named, and paraphrases everything else.** Horton's English also **stops one sentence short** of the closing line about beholding the Creator in the relation of map to world, though his Latin includes it.

**Death at Todi, 11 August 1464. [A]** On the road from Rome toward Ancona. Coordinates **42.7803, 12.4067**. MacTutor adds Toscanelli present at the death; no second source reached confirms it, so **[R]** here.
- **Corrections.** (i) First pass had Pius II dying "eleven days later." He died **14 August 1464, three days later** *(en.wiki)*; Doge Cristoforo Moro's fleet arrived two days before, and the dying pope could only view it from a window. (ii) First pass had Sigismund capitulating "within days" of the death; that is dropped (contradiction 7 below).

**Burial. [A]** Body to San Pietro in Vincoli; **the exact original grave spot is reported lost.** Bregno's relief "Cardinal Nicholas before St Peter" survives, as do the chains. Coordinate **41.89389, 12.49306** (was 41.8955, 12.4917, about 220 m NW). The heart is sealed in the Cusanusstift chapel altar. **Lunar crater [A, corrected]:** Cusanus, 60.9 km, **near side, north-eastern limb, 72.0 N 70.8 E**, foreshortened from Earth. First pass put it on the **far side: wrong** *(en.wiki "Cusanus (crater)")*.

## Interlocks

- **Ramon Llull [A, one-directional].** Paris 1428, copying the *Ars* at his own expense; Heimerich de Campo put Llull in his hands at Cologne. `ramon_llull.journey.json` carries no reciprocal reference. **Named in campa prose.**
- **Johannes Trithemius [A, reciprocal].** Born downstream at Trittenheim in 1462, he names Cusa in the *Liber octo quaestionum* for Emperor Maximilian as his countryman from the Moselle, quoting the infinite-sphere formula. Already two-directional. **Named in campa prose.**
- **Giordano Bruno [A as descent, name suppressed].** MacTutor states plainly that Cusa's cosmology influenced Bruno and Kepler; Koyré (1957) traces it. **The previous version of this file named Bruno in the campa of the *De docta ignorantia* stop. That has been cut**; the connection now travels only in `suggested_refs`.
- **Giulio Camillo [R, structural].** The Theatre gathers total knowledge at one central viewpoint through *porte*; Cusa's cosmographer gathers the world through five gates into one map. **Structural, not documentary: nothing reached shows Camillo read Cusa.** `suggested_ref` only, **not named in prose**: shared form is not a relation.
- **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz [R, weakly sourced].** The claim that Leibniz named the *coincidentia oppositorum* among his ancestors appears in the previous version of this file and **nowhere else reached**; the Leibniz file has no "Cusa" hit. **Demoted from prose to a `suggested_ref`.**
- **Peter of Ravenna, Simonides of Ceos [R].** Queued, no journey files. Placement note only. **Johannes Cusanus [A]:** author of the *Tractatulus artificiose memorie* (1510, 1514, 1519), **a different person**, confirmed distinct in QUEUE.md. **That work must never be cited as Nicholas of Cusa's.**

## Contradictions left standing

1. Cologne arrival: **1425** (Wikipedia) vs **1426** (SEP).
2. *De concordantia catholica*: **1433** (SEP) vs **1434** (Wikiquote) vs no year (Wikipedia).
3. *De coniecturis*: **1441-42** (Wikipedia) vs **1442-43** (SEP).
4. *Compendium*: **1463/64** (de.wiki) vs **1464** (SEP, MacTutor); and internally, this file's chronology lens (1464) against its geography and afterlife lenses (1463).
5. Cusanusstift: **charter 1458** vs **"built in 1451" / "erected 1451-56."** Treated as foundation vs building campaign, but the two lenses flatly disagreed.
6. Place of completion of *De docta ignorantia*: **Kues** (inherited pin) vs **Koblenz** (geography lens). Neither is attested.
7. Sigismund's submission: **June 1464 settlement before the death** (de.wiki) vs **surrender after the death** (en.wiki), no interval anywhere.
8. Priestly ordination: **no year fixed in any source reached.**
9. Cardinal *in pectore*: **Eugenius IV 1446, unpublished** vs **Nicholas V 20 December 1448, published January 1449.** True of different acts; the first pass conflated them.

## Gaps stated as gaps

**Deventer:** no document places him in the school. **The Great Legation itinerary:** no city-by-city route in any encyclopaedic source reached; the named stops rest on Meuthen alone. **The 1427 Roman errand:** content nowhere stated. **Motive for the 1437 crossing to Eugenius IV:** nowhere stated. **Place of the 1444 astronomical purchases:** nowhere stated. **Site of any audience with John VIII:** nowhere stated, so the Blachernae stop was cut rather than invented. **Position and date of the shipboard illumination:** Cusa himself supplies neither. **The original grave in San Pietro in Vincoli:** reported lost, with no source saying when or how. ***Compendium* chapter number and Hopkins pagination:** consensus, not verified against a critical edition.
