# Martin Buber (1878-1965): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-04 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `martin_buber`. Companion: `martin_buber.journey.json`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named in the same sentence · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

Method: this pass worked from an adversarially verified research pool, not a fresh gather. The pool's own corrections are folded in below and labelled, since several overturn the first reading. Where correction and original disagree, both are printed.

## 1. Vienna and Lemberg, 1878-1896

**Birth.** Martin (Mordechai) Buber is born in Vienna on 8 February 1878 **[A, Wikipedia, 'Martin Buber']**, to Carl Buber and Elise née Wurgast.

**Correction, birthplace.** The first pass put the birth house in the Josefstadt (8th district) at 48.21/16.3467. Wrong. The Wien Geschichte Wiki records a plaque at **Franz-Josefs-Kai 45, Innere Stadt (1st district)**, unveiled 12 June 1990: "Geburtshaus des Philosophen Univ. Prof. Dr. Martin Buber geb. 8.2.1878 gest. 13.6.1965 in Jerusalem" **[A, geschichtewiki.wien.gv.at]**. Corrected to approx. 48.2135/16.3760, some 2 km east of the discarded pin. The caveat that the address is unconfirmed is withdrawn: it is confirmed and marked in situ.

**Lemberg.** The parents separate and the boy goes to his paternal grandparents, Solomon and Adele Buber **[A]**. **Contradiction, not resolved:** Wikipedia gives his age as three, SEP as four; the file dates the move 1881 and carries the conflict in `date_confidence`. Solomon Buber is a midrash scholar and wealthy landowner, and his library is the formative environment **[A, Wikipedia; Friedman]**. Buber's recollection, from the *Autobiographical Fragments*: "Time was not a sentence hanging over me; it was mine, for it was 'ours.'" **[A, SEP, citing Schilpp and Friedman eds. (1972), pp. 11-13]**. He attends the Kaiser-Franz-Joseph-Gymnasium c. 1888-1896 **[A, German Wikipedia]**. *Gap:* neither the school building nor the grandparents' address was located, so the Lviv old-town coordinate stands for the whole Lemberg period.

**Correction, the Kant episode.** The first pass dated the *Prolegomena* to 1892 and hedged "age 14 or 15." SEP says plainly "Age 15: read Kant's *Prolegomena*," so 1893 **[A, plato.stanford.edu/entries/buber/]**. The Nietzsche episode (*Zarathustra*, about age 17, so 1895) stands **[A, SEP]**. The two are merged into one stop. He returns to his father's house in Lemberg in 1892 **[A, Wikipedia]**, which sits oddly beside a grandparents' household running to 1896; neither source explains the overlap and it is left open.

## 2. Student years and the Zionist quarrel, 1896-1904

**Vienna, 1896:** matriculates in philosophy, art history, German studies and philology **[A, Wikipedia]**.

**Correction, where and when he joined the Zionist movement.** The chronology pass gave "1898, Vienna"; the year is right, the place wrong. SEP puts him at **Leipzig 1897-98** and **Berlin 1898-99**, joining through the Leipzig association, while the geography pass wrote "Leipzig 1898-1899" and double-booked him with the Berlin semester. Both corrected to SEP's sequence **[A, SEP; German Wikipedia]**. The substance (Nathan Birnbaum's cultural Zionism against Herzl's state-first line) survives **[A, Wikipedia; Encyclopaedia Judaica]**. German Wikipedia dates his first personal contact with Herzl to **February 1900**.

**Berlin, 1898-99:** lectures of Dilthey and Simmel **[A, SEP]**. **Zurich, 1899:** meets Paula Winkler **[A, Wikipedia; SEP]**. **Basel:** the Third (1899) and Fifth (1901) Congresses in the Stadtcasino hall, with the Democratic Faction **[A, Encyclopaedia Judaica; Wikipedia, 'First Zionist Congress']**. The same hall held the First Congress in 1897; his attendance is documented only from the Third onward.

**Correction, *Die Welt*.** The chronology pass said 1902. German Wikipedia: "Redakteur der *Die Welt* ab September 1901." He took it over in **September 1901** and clashed with Herzl over its cultural direction within months **[A, German Wikipedia; Encyclopaedia Judaica]**. The pool's geography pass had 1901 right, so this was another internal contradiction.

**Jüdischer Verlag, Berlin, 1902**, co-founded with Berthold Feiwel, E. M. Lilien and Chaim Weizmann **[A, Encyclopaedia Judaica]**. **Doctorate, Vienna, 1904**, on individuation in Nicholas of Cusa and Jakob Boehme **[A, Wikipedia; SEP]**. *Contradiction flagged:* some sources date the dissertation 1903, others 1904; not resolved.

**Correction, the marriage.** The geography pass put it at Munich in **1901**, tagging the location [R]. The [R] is right; the date is flatly wrong. Buber married Paula in **1907**, after her conversion and after Solomon Buber's death in 1906 **[A, SEP; German Wikipedia]**, as the pool's own chronology said. Munich stays **[R]**: no consulted source names the ceremony's location, and Munich is inferred from Paula's origin alone. She left the Catholic Church in 1901 **[R, Wikipedia]**. Two children precede the marriage: Rafael 1900, Eva 1901 **[A, SEP]**.

## 3. The Hasidic decade, 1904-1916

**Editorial trade.** Reader and editor for Rütten & Loening in Berlin; editor of *Die Gesellschaft* 1906-1912, forty volumes of social-psychological monographs **[A, SEP; Wikipedia]**. "Die Soziologie ist die Wissenschaft von den Formen des Zwischenmenschlichen" is quoted in SEP from E. Wiehn (1991), p. 192 **[A at second hand; the volume was not seen]**.

**Publications:** *Die Geschichten des Rabbi Nachman* 1906, *Die Legende des Baalschem* 1908, *Ekstatische Konfessionen* 1909, *Daniel* 1913 **[A, Wikipedia; SEP]**. **Residence:** Berlin-Zehlendorf, Vopeliuspfad, 1907-1916 **[A, German Wikipedia]**; the coordinate is the district centre.

**Correction, Medzhybizh.** The Baal Shem Tov's Podolian town was pinned at 49.4531/27.3011, some 8-9 km west of it in open country; English Wikipedia gives 49°26'N 27°25'E = **49.4333/27.4167**. The substantive caveat matters more: **no consulted biography records Buber ever travelling there** **[R]**. He worked from printed collections and from what he had heard in Galicia. The pin sits in the file as a flagged thematic anchor, not a stop on his itinerary.

**Prague, 1909-1911.** Three *Reden über das Judentum* to the Bar Kochba association **[A, Encyclopaedia Judaica; Mendes-Flohr]**; Kafka and Brod's circle absorbed them. *Gap:* the hall is unidentified, so the coordinate is the old university quarter. **Heidelberg.** Weber's Sunday circle and the correspondence over *Die Gesellschaft*, c. 1908-1914 **[R, asserted from `max_weber.journey.json`, not confirmed against a correspondence edition]**.

## 4. Heppenheim, *Der Jude*, and the dialogical turn, 1916-1932

**1916:** the family moves to Heppenheim an der Bergstrasse and stays until 1938 **[A, German Wikipedia; SEP]**; Buber founds *Der Jude* with Salman Schocken and edits it to 1924 **[A, SEP; Encyclopaedia Judaica]**; and in the same year he helps found a Berlin committee for Eastern European Jewish refugees **[A, Jewish Virtual Library]**. **1921:** returns to Zionist politics at the Twelfth Congress in Carlsbad **[A, SEP]**; the friendship with Rosenzweig begins **[A, Wikipedia]**. **Darmstadt:** lectures at Keyserling's Schule der Weisheit, active 1920-1933 **[R, asserted from `keyserling.journey.json`; the 1922 visit claimed there was not independently confirmed]**.

**1923:** *Ich und Du*. **Contradiction, not resolved:** SEP has him professor at Frankfurt 1923-1933, Wikipedia dates the honorary professorship to 1930. Both readings are carried, as a 1923 teaching stop and a separate 1930 professorship stop, with the conflict in `date_confidence` on each.

**Correction, the Freies Jüdisches Lehrhaus.** The geography pass claimed he "takes over its direction after Rosenzweig's death in 1929," over a continuous 1921-1933. Both parts are wrong. English Wikipedia, 'Franz Rosenzweig': "The Lehrhaus stayed open until 1930 and was reopened by Martin Buber in 1933." His leading role came during Rosenzweig's illness in the early 1920s and again at the **1933 reopening**; the institution **closed around 1930**, so the span is not continuous.

**1925:** the *Verdeutschung der Schrift* begins with Rosenzweig **[A, Wikipedia; SEP]**, completed alone in Jerusalem in 1961. **Correction, Brit Shalom.** Founded 1925 at Arthur Ruppin's home **[A, SEP]**, and Buber joined; but the Jerusalem coordinate misleads as a point on his itinerary, since he was at Heppenheim and **did not set foot in Jerusalem until 1938**. The file keeps the coordinate as the organisation's seat and says so in campa and `date_confidence`.

**1926-1930:** co-edits *Die Kreatur* with Viktor von Weizsäcker and Joseph Wittig **[A, SEP]**. **December 1929:** Rosenzweig dies **[A, SEP]**. **1932:** *Königtum Gottes* **[A, SEP]**.

## 5. Under the Reich, 1933-1938

Resigns the Frankfurt chair in 1933 before dismissal and founds the Mittelstelle für jüdische Erwachsenenbildung **[A, Wikipedia; SEP]**. Forbidden to lecture publicly **4 October 1933**, struck from the Reich Chamber of Literature in **1935**, emigrates March 1938 **[A, Wikipedia]**; the Heppenheim house is ransacked at Kristallnacht, 9 November 1938, the family already gone **[A, SEP]**. *Gap:* the itinerary of his 1933-38 teaching tours inside Germany is described only in general terms and by no reachable document, so the journey file names no cities for it.

## 6. Jerusalem, 1938-1965

**Correction, Mount Scopus, and a coordinate decision declared openly.** The geography pass gave 31.7825/35.2431, some 1.2 km south of the campus; the Hebrew University's Mount Scopus campus is at roughly **31.7936/35.2447**. The journey file nonetheless uses **31.7818/35.2408**, the byte-identical canonical pin already carried by `einstein.journey.json`, since inherited pins must coincide exactly with the corpus. That pin is itself about 1.4 km south of the campus: a deliberate inaccuracy in favour of pin coincidence, recorded rather than hidden. If the Einstein pin is corrected, this stop moves with it.

Professorship 1938-1951 **[A, Wikipedia; German Wikipedia]**; Wikipedia calls the chair anthropology and sociology, SEP sociology and social philosophy (**contradiction, not resolved**). Abu Tor from 1938 to the 1947-48 fighting, then Talbiya to his death **[A, Wikipedia]**; house numbers in both are unconfirmed, and the Hovevei Zion Street address is secondary only. Ihud founded 1942 **[A, Wikipedia; SEP; Encyclopaedia Judaica]**; loyal opposition after May 1948 **[A, SEP]**.

**Corrections, two books.** *Paths in Utopia*: the pool collapsed three editions into one wrong date. Hebrew *Netivot be-utopia* 1946/47 (SEP dates the work 1947), English 1949, German *Pfade in Utopia* 1950 (German Wikipedia works list); pairing the English year with the German title was wrong on its face, and the journey file gives all three. *The Eclipse of God*: SEP's 1951 does not match the editions, English (Harper) 1952 and German *Gottesfinsternis* 1953; 1951 most likely names the American lectures the book came out of, so the file dates lectures 1951 and book 1952.

**Prizes** **[A, Wikipedia; German Wikipedia]**: Hanseatic Goethe Prize, Hamburg, 1951; Friedenspreis des Deutschen Buchhandels, Paulskirche, Frankfurt, 27 September 1953; Israel Prize for the humanities 1958; Bialik Prize 1961; Erasmus Prize, Amsterdam, 1963.

**Buber-Rogers dialogue**, University of Michigan, April 1957 **[A, the published transcript]**; the campus building is unidentified in consulted sources. The public dispute with Carl Jung over *Answer to Job* is dated 1952 by Wikipedia and 1951-52 by the interlock pass **[A as to the dispute, R as to the year]**. **Paula dies in Venice, 1958** **[A, Wikipedia]**; *gap:* no consulted source gives the month, and the journey file defaults to 01 and says so.

**Death** at home in Talbiya, 13 June 1965, aged 87 **[A, Wikipedia]**. **Burial at Har HaMenuchot** **[A, Hebrew Wikipedia, "נקבר בהר המנוחות", which also describes the procession with the President in attendance]**. **Correction:** the burial pin was 31.7825/35.1917, some 2.2 km southeast of the cemetery, and disagreed with the pool's own afterlife section; English Wikipedia gives 31°47'53"N 35°10'40"E = **31.7981/35.1778**. The earlier sourcing worry (Findagrave HTTP 403) is relaxed by the Hebrew Wikipedia statement.

## 7. Afterlife

Papers and archive at the National Library of Israel, Givat Ram **[A, Wikipedia]**; *gap:* no consulted source dates the transfer, so the journey stop uses 1966-01-01 and flags the date as unestablished. Buber-Rosenzweig Medal established 1968 by the German Coordinating Council of Societies for Christian-Jewish Cooperation, still awarded as of 2024 **[A, Wikipedia]**. Warhol portrait 1980; Israel Post stamp April 1999; Israeli Mint gold medal 2001; streets in eleven named cities **[A, Hebrew Wikipedia]**.

**Correction, the HaZore'a forest.** The fact is confirmed (Hebrew Wikipedia: "בשנת 1964 נטעה קרן קיימת לישראל יער לכבוד מרטין בובר בקרבת הזורע") but the coordinate 32.6167/35.1167 sits about 3 km south of the kibbutz. Kibbutz HaZore'a is at 32°38'41"N 35°07'15"E = **32.6447/35.1208**. Corrected.

## 8. Quotations: what survived verification

Confirmed verbatim against the Internet Archive scan of the 1937 Ronald Gregor Smith translation (archive.org, `in.ernet.dli.2015.61530`) **[A]**, all Part One: "To man the world is twofold, in accordance with his twofold attitude." (opening line); "All real living is meeting."; "The primary words are not isolated words, but combined words."; and, at p. 28, "Through the Thou a man becomes I. That which confronts him comes and disappears, relational events condense, then are scattered..." (full wording in the journey file).

**Rejected: a misattribution.** "There is, however, one Thou which never becomes an It, the eternal Thou." was carried in the pool as Buber, Part One, p. 7. It is **not Buber**. In the 1937 edition it stands in the Roman-paginated **TRANSLATOR'S INTRODUCTION**; the cited "p. 7" misreads the Roman "p. vii," and Part One begins later in the scan. The wording differs too: "...the 'eternal Thou', God." Correct citation: Smith, Translator's Introduction, pp. vi-vii. **Not used in the journey file.**

**Corrected page.** "Through the graciousness of its comings and the solemn sadness of its goings it leads you away to the Thou in which the parallel lines of relations meet." is genuine and verbatim in Part One. The pool's p. 26 is wrong: printed markers run 29, 30, 31 before the passage and 33 immediately after, so **p. 33**, certainly within pp. 32-33.

**Corrected section name.** The paratext line beginning "This work in its original, German form has already, since its publication fourteen years ago, exercised on the Continent an influence..." is Smith's, p. v of the **Translator's Introduction**, not a "Translator's Preface." Not Buber, and not used as a Buber quotation.

From SEP **[A at second hand]**: "I have no doctrine. I only point to something. I point to a reality." (MBW I, 1114); "Time was not a sentence hanging over me; it was mine, for it was 'ours.'" (*Autobiographical Fragments*); "The problem is discussed many times whether I am a philosopher or a theologian or something else." (*Replies to My Critics*, 1967, p. 689).

**Corrected: the Zusya tale.** The pool had "Before his death, Rabbi Zusya said, 'Why were you not Zusya?'", which truncates the story and inverts its sense. WIST Quotations (sourced to *Tales of the Hasidim: The Early Masters*, 'Zusya of Hanipol', trans. Olga Marx, 1947) and English Wikiquote (1991 ebook, p. 251) agree on the full form: "In the coming world they will not ask me, 'Why were you not Moses?' They will ask me, 'Why were you not Zusya?'" The Moses/Zusya contrast is the point. **[R]**: the book could not be opened, so the page is secondary, and WIST flags the saying as only traditionally the historical Zusya's.

**[R], unverified:** "Greatness by nature includes a power, but not a will to power," widely attributed to *Between Man and Man* (trans. Smith, 1965). No copy fetched. Used once, flagged in `quote_source`.

## 10. Open contradictions, not adjudicated

1. Age at the parents' separation: three (Wikipedia) or four (SEP).
2. Return to the father's house in Lemberg dated 1892, against a grandparents' household running to 1896.
3. Dissertation year: 1903 or 1904.
4. Frankfurt professorship: 1923-1933 (SEP) or honorary from 1930 (Wikipedia).
5. The chair's field: sociology and social philosophy (SEP) or anthropology and sociology (Wikipedia).
6. *Paths in Utopia*: Hebrew 1947, English 1949, German 1950; SEP dates the work 1947, Wikipedia 1949.
7. *Eclipse of God*: SEP 1951 (lectures), Harper 1952, German 1953.
8. Buber-Jung dispute: 1952 (Wikipedia) or 1951-52 (interlock pass).
9. Mount Scopus: corrected 31.7936/35.2447 versus inherited pin 31.7818/35.2408; the file uses the pin, see section 6.
