# José Antonio de la Sierra, ensayador of the Potosí mint

*Research report, 2026-08-14. Subject: the assayer whose initial J stands on every gold and silver coin struck at Potosí for the Provincias Unidas del Río de la Plata in 1813, the first national coinage of Argentina.*

**Legend: [A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference · gaps are stated as gaps and never filled by inference.

---

## 0. The premise, corrected before anything else

The brief that opened this research described De la Sierra as the man who "designed and engraved" the first Argentine coinage. **No source reached in this research supports that.** [A, by absence across the whole reachable literature]

What the sources do support:

- He was **ensayador** (assayer) of the Casa de Moneda de Potosí in 1813, the officer who certified the fineness of the metal before striking. **[A: billetesargentinos.com.ar, "Primeras Monedas de Potosí"; economatos.blogspot.com, "Monedas Patrias"; Centro Numismático El Patacón blog, April 2009; BCRA, "Bicentenario de la primera moneda patria"]**
- The initial **J** on the 1813 Potosí coinage is his assay mark. **[A: same sources; "La inicial J. corresponde al ensayador José Antonio de Sierra."]**
- Before 1813 he was **fundidor interino**, interim founder/smelter, a subordinate metallurgical post. **[A: El Patacón]**

Who designed and who engraved is **contested and is left contested here** (section 6).

---

## 1. Before 1813: a life with no beginning

- **Birth: unknown.** No birth record, birthplace, parentage, or training has been located in any source. **[R, stated as a gap]**
- A birth year of **c. 1746** is obtainable only by subtraction: El Patacón's account describes him at his 1813 appointment as "un fundidor interino de 67 años." **[R: inference from El Patacón, 2009; the age is the attested element, the year is not]**
- **Do not infer a birthplace.** Potosí, Spain, and Upper Peru are equally unattested.
- A **Wikidata entry (Q97014544)** names a "José Antonio Sierra, Peruvian politician, 18th-19th century" with no dates and no mint connection. Treated as a probable namesake and **not used**. **[R]**

## 2. The mint under the crown, to 1812

- Potosí's Casa de Moneda had been the great silver house of the Spanish empire since the sixteenth century, fed by the ore of the **Cerro Rico** above the town. Sierra's connection to the mountain is through the metal that arrives at his furnace, not through any attested presence on the slopes. **[R: general background, billetesargentinos.com.ar]**
- **1811:** after the patriot defeat at **Huaqui**, Argentine forces abandon Potosí and its mint, ending the first patriot occupation. **[A: Instituto Histórico Bicentenario, Poder Legislativo de Corrientes, "La Primera Moneda Argentina" (PDF)]**
- **Pre-1813:** Sierra holds the interim founder's post through these changes of government. **[A: El Patacón]**

## 3. The road that reopens Potosí, 1812-1813

- **24-25 September 1812, Campo de las Carreras, Tucumán:** Belgrano disobeys the order to withdraw and defeats Tristán. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Tucumán"; coordinates 26°50'15"S 65°13'01"W]**
- **20 February 1813, Campo de Castañares, Salta:** Belgrano defeats the royalist army. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Salta"; coordinates 24°43'53"S 65°23'46"W, in the northern zone of the city]**
  - **Correction applied.** The research pool's claim that Salta "restored Argentine control over Potosí" is false. Salta opened the road; it did not deliver the mint. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Segunda expedición auxiliadora al Alto Perú"]**
- **7 May 1813:** patriot troops enter Potosí under **Eustaquio Díaz Vélez**; **19 May 1813:** Belgrano arrives. Nearly three months after Salta. **[A: same]**
- **21 June 1813:** Belgrano's headquarters are in the **Casa Real de Moneda** itself. **[A: belgrano.journey.json, existing atlas file, stop "Potosí, el cuartel general en la Villa Imperial"; canonical pin -19.5892, -65.7533]**

## 4. The decree, April to July 1813

- **13 April 1813, Buenos Aires:** the Asamblea General Constituyente decrees that new dies be opened and engraved at Potosí, bearing the Assembly's seal minus its sun on one face and a radiant sun with EN UNION Y LIBERTAD on the other. **[A: Instituto Histórico Bicentenario PDF, quoting the decree; BCRA]**
  - **Correction applied.** The pool placed this in the **Cabildo**. The Assembly did not sit in the Cabildo: its inaugural session was held in the building of the **Consulado de Buenos Aires**. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Asamblea del Año XIII"]** The stop is moved to the Consulado. Its precise coordinate is **approximate**: no source consulted gives the Consulado building a modern geolocation, so a point in the historic civic block south of the Plaza de Mayo is used. **[R, flagged]**
- **28 July 1813, Fortaleza de Buenos Aires:** the Executive Power promulgates, ordering the coin into circulation at the same intrinsic value as the Spanish money it replaces. Signed by Antonio Álvarez de Jonte, José Julián Pérez, Nicolás Rodríguez Peña, secretary Manuel José García. **[A: Instituto Histórico Bicentenario PDF]**

## 5. The assayer's mark, 1813

- **The vacancy.** El Patacón, summarising the mint's own records: "Numerosos oficiales de la Casa de Moneda huyeron con Goyeneche y por esta razón se debió ascender a oficiales subalternos, muchos de los cuales no estaban suficientemente capacitados pare ejercer los nuevos cargos habiendo cometido numerosos errores. Por ejemplo, el oficial 2do Pedro Venavidez fue promovido a Talla Mayor y el oficio de ensayador se encomendó a un fundidor interino de 67 años, José Antonio de Sierra." **[A: El Patacón, April 2009, verbatim]**
- **The promotion** of Sierra from fundidor interino to ensayador is therefore attested together with its cause: a wartime shortage of qualified men. **[A]**
- **The workplace within the mint.** The assay and weighing room (sala de balanza). No document places him in that chamber; it is reconstructed from the standard division of labour in a colonial mint. **[R, explicitly]**
- **The coinage.** Dies are cut at Potosí and coin is in circulation within days of the promulgation, with the legends **exchanged** relative to the decree: shield with EN UNION Y LIBERTAD on one face, radiant sun with PROVINCIAS DEL RIO DE LA PLATA on the other. **[A: Instituto Histórico Bicentenario PDF]**
- **Denominations.** Silver ½, 1, 2, 4, 8 reales at .896 fine; gold 1, 2, 4, 8 escudos at .875 fine, the 8 escudos 27 g and 35.3 mm. **[A: Numista catalogue, 1813 Provincias del Río de la Plata, KM#6-9; Infobae for the 8 escudos measurements]**
  - **Correction applied.** The pool's chronology lens omitted the **4 escudos**; the pool's own geography lens included it. The Numista catalogue confirms 1, 2, 4 and 8 escudos. Corrected to the full list.
- **The mark.** PTS in monogram for the house, J for the assayer, on every denomination. **[A: billetesargentinos.com.ar; economatos.blogspot.com; rpnumis.com catalogue entry "8 Reales 1813 J (José Antonio de Sierra)"]**
- **The varieties.** At least twenty documented die varieties of the 1813 8 reales (sun's rays, letter placement, numeral position), with spelling errors and crowded legends throughout. **[R for the count: Instituto Histórico Bicentenario PDF]** **[A for the errors as a class: Google Arts & Culture, Museo Janson]**
- **"Only two known" of the 2 escudos.** Given by one museum-derived account and **not corroborated anywhere else reachable**; carried in the journey file with in-text attribution rather than as fact. **[R, flagged]**

## 6. The contradiction that is not resolved: who made the coin

Three incompatible attributions, all left standing:

| Claim | Source | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Sierra **designed** the coin ("La misma fue diseñadas por José Antonio de Sierra") | Infobae, 25 Sept 2021, "La increíble historia de 8 Escudos" | [A as to what the source says; [R] as to the fact |
| **Pedro Benavídez**, jefe de talla of the Potosí mint, engraved the dies | Instituto Histórico Bicentenario PDF: "El grabado y acuñación de estas monedas estuvo a cargo del jefe de talla de la ceca de Potosí, don Pedro Benavídez"; elarcondelahistoria.com | [A as to what the sources say; contested |
| **Juan de Dios Rivera**, Cusco-born engraver commissioned by deputy Agustín Donado, engraved the first Argentine coin | es.wikipedia, "Juan de Dios Rivera Túpac" | [A as to what the source says; contested |
| **Nobody knows who designed the shield**: authorship variously given to Donado, Rivera, Isidro Antonio de Castro, Monteagudo, with "no existen constancias concluyentes" | es.wikipedia, "Escudo de Argentina" | [A] |

**Correction applied.** The pool tagged the Benavídez attribution **[A]** as settled. It is not: the Rivera attribution contradicts it directly. Both are carried, neither adjudicated. The one claim surviving all three sources is negative: **Sierra's documented role is assayer, not designer and not engraver.**

Also corrected: the pool dated the **Sol de Mayo** as a national emblem to 25 May 1810. The emblem takes its *name* from the May Revolution but its first formal use was the official seal of the Asamblea del Año XIII, **1813**. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Sol de Mayo"]** No 1810 emblem stop is used in the journey file.

## 7. The collapse, October to November 1813

- **1 October 1813, Vilcapugio:** Belgrano defeated by Pezuela. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Vilcapugio"; coordinates 19°02'19"S 66°33'31"W]** The pool's coordinate was ~45 km off and has been replaced.
- **14 November 1813, Ayohuma:** the Army of the North broken. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Ayohuma"; coordinates 18°51'19"S 66°07'36"W]** The pool's coordinate was ~30 km off and has been replaced.
- **19 November 1813:** Belgrano and Díaz Vélez evacuate Potosí; Belgrano orders the Casa de Moneda mined and blown up; the order "no fue cumplido" and the building survives. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Segunda expedición auxiliadora al Alto Perú"; corroborated by belgrano.journey.json, stop "Potosí, la mina bajo la Casa de Moneda"]**
- **20 November 1813:** royalist forces enter Potosí. **[A: same]**
  - **Correction applied.** The pool gave "December 1813" in one lens and "1813/1814" in another for the same event. Both are wrong; 19-20 November 1813 is documented.

## 8. Arrest, prison, purification, 1813-1815

The single richest body of evidence about the man, and it comes through one blog summarising Potosí mint records.

- **Arrest.** "El fundidor José Antonio de Sierra, cuya inicial 'J' aparece en todas las monedas para las Provincias Unidas del Río de la Plata de 1813, fue detenido por colaboracionista y permaneció en prisión por mas de un año hasta el 25 de enero de 1815, cuando el Conde de la Casa de Real de Moneda dictamino su inocencia y lo repuso en su antiguo empleo." **[A: Centro Numismático El Patacón, April 2009, verbatim]**
- **The verdict, 25 January 1815**, quoted by the same source as trial-record text and treated here as the closest thing to a primary document about him:
  > "Autos y vistos... se declare que Don José Antonio Sierra ha purificado su conducta de los hechos de que ha sido acusado y que todos fueron efecto del temor por la fuerza de las armas del gobierno del Río de la Plata, sin que en aquellas circunstancias tuviese libertad de repulsar las ordenes de los mandarines intrusos y en su virtud, libre de delito de infidencia contra el rey, quedando en su buena reputación y fama como lo estuvo antes de entrar los insurgentes en esta Villa."
  **[A: El Patacón, quoting the fallo of the Conde de la Real Casa de Moneda de Potosí]**
- **Caveat on the evidential base.** A numismatic society blog post of 2009, not an archival edition, citing no shelfmark, and the *only* source found for the arrest, the imprisonment, the age of 67, and the verdict text. This whole section stands or falls with it: **a single-source dependency.**

## 9. The mint without him, 1815

- **1 May / 9 May 1815:** Rondeau's forces enter Potosí; the city is held until **16 December 1815**. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Tercera expedición auxiliadora al Alto Perú"]**
- **The refusal or the exclusion.** "pues no se pudo contar con Sierra, que había actuado en 1813." **[A: economatos.blogspot.com; corroborated by billetesargentinos.com.ar]** Whether he declined or was distrusted is **not stated in any source**; both readings are carried. **[R]**
- **Francisco José de Matos** appointed ensayador primero, mark F. **[A: elarcondelahistoria.com via WebSearch synthesis; Google Arts & Culture]**
  - **Contradiction not resolved:** the research pool gives **30 August 1815** in its chronology lens and **31 August 1815** in its geography lens for the same appointment. Neither could be verified. The journey file uses 30 August with the discrepancy recorded in `date_confidence`.
- **Leandro Ozio**, ensayador segundo. **[R: elarcondelahistoria]**
- **"Isidro Escarza"** as assayer before Matos: given by one museum-derived account, **could not be corroborated**. **[R, flagged]**
- **Reduced fineness** in the 1815 emission compared with the 1813 standard, per the mint's contemporary papers as summarised by later numismatists. **[R: El Patacón]**
- **29 November 1815, Viluma / Sipe-Sipe:** Rondeau destroyed. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Batalla de Viluma"; coordinates 17°27'22"S 66°20'52"W]** The pool's coordinate was ~29 km off and has been replaced.

## 10. The end of the record

- **No death date. No place of death. No burial. No parish entry. No later appointment.** Nothing after January 1815 and the 1815 sentence about not being able to count on him. **[R, stated as a gap across every source consulted]**
- **Do not infer** that he continued as a royalist mint official, retired, or died in Potosí. No source says so.
- **No monument, statue, plaque, or memorial** to him has been found anywhere. **[R: gap, cross-checked against the Infobae auction coverage and the Wikipedia mint article]**

## 11. Afterlife

- **9 December 1824, Pampa de la Quinua, Ayacucho:** the end of Spanish rule; by 1825 Potosí stops striking for Ferdinand VII. **[A: billetesargentinos.com.ar]**
- **26 May 2008:** an 1813 8 escudos, authenticated by Héctor Carlos Janson and Alberto Derman, sells for **US$161,000** in the **Colección Millenia** sale held by **Ira & Larry Goldberg** auctioneers, the highest price ever paid for an Argentine coin. **[R: infobae.com, 2021, single source]**
  - **Correction applied.** The pool placed this in **New York**, while conceding the source names no venue. Goldberg is a **Los Angeles** firm (11400 W. Olympic Blvd); the journey stop uses its offices and **flags the venue as unconfirmed**. The Manhattan coordinate is discarded as invented.
- **Museo Histórico y Numismático Héctor Carlos Janson**, Banco Central de la República Argentina, **San Martín 216, Buenos Aires**, holds specimens of the first patriotic emission. **[A: Google Arts & Culture]**
  - **Correction applied.** The pool listed this museum **twice** ("BCRA museum, Reconquista 266" and "Museo Janson" ~1 km west). One institution; merged into a single stop at San Martín 216.
- **The building.** The Casa Nacional de Moneda still stands; ownership split 60% Bolivian judiciary, 40% Potosí municipality; transfer to the Central Bank of Bolivia's cultural foundation under negotiation; recognised in 2025 by the Andean Parliament as regional historical and cultural heritage. **[A: es.wikipedia, "Casa de la Moneda de Bolivia"]**

## 12. Coordinates: what was corrected

The research pool carried **four different coordinates for the same mint building**. The canonical pin already used by belgrano.journey.json and juana_azurduy.journey.json, **-19.5892, -65.7533**, is inherited byte-identically by every mint stop here; stops in the town but outside the mint use -19.5836, -65.7531. **Cerro Rico** inherits the canonical **-19.619, -65.75** (juana_azurduy, valientes).

Replaced: Tucumán (3.5 km error), Salta Castañares (~10 km, wrong side of the city), Vilcapugio (~45 km), Ayohuma (~30 km), Viluma (~29 km), Cabildo for Consulado (wrong building), the New York auction (invented city), the duplicated Buenos Aires museum.

## 13. Interlocks with the existing corpus

- **belgrano.journey.json** is the only file with a *personal* relation to Sierra and is the only traveler named in the journey prose. Belgrano's army employed him, Belgrano's headquarters was the building he worked in, Belgrano's government is what he was imprisoned for serving, and Belgrano's evacuation order would have destroyed his workplace. That is a real relation, not shared geography.
- **juana_azurduy**, **valientes**, **tupac_amaru_ii**, **guaman_poma_de_ayala**, **san_martin** and **martin_miguel_de_guemes** share pins and themes with this file (the mint building, the Cerro Rico, the silver corridor) but have **no attested relation to Sierra**. They are deliberately **not named in any campa**: the map already shows co-location.
