# Christian Rosenkreuz - research report

*Compiled 2026-08-06. Legendary founder of the Fraternity of the Rosy Cross, dated by the second manifesto to 1378-1484. Slug `christian_rosenkreuz`; journey file 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or later gloss. **[X]** a widely repeated claim found wrong, with the correcting source. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved.

**The governing problem.** No independent record of this man exists. Two anonymous German pamphlets printed at Kassel in 1614 and 1615 are the whole documentary basis; everything else is their text, arithmetic on their two numbers, or what others later did with the name. **[A - Wikipedia, "Christian Rosenkreuz": no verifiable record confirms him as real, and no vault matching the Fama's description has been located.]** The register is `national mythology: the canon is true`, so the campa narrates the Fama as fact and hedging sits in `date_confidence`. The English throughout is Vaughan's 1652 *Fame and Confession*; most errors below are paraphrases of Vaughan later quoted as the manifesto.

## 1. Birth and cloister (1378 - c.1393)

**Birth year 1378 [A - Confessio, trans. Vaughan];** the *Fama* nowhere dates his birth. **Birthplace: none given [A - absence across both manifestos]**: no town, province, river, or family seat. Pinned at the German centroid (51.1657, 10.4515), declared a placeholder.

**Placed in a cloister "in the fifth year of age," where he learned "indifferently the Greek and Latin Tongues" [A - Fama].** Three corrections against one secondary summary: **[X]** he is not said to be orphaned, and the age is the fifth year, not four ("orphaned at four" is Wikipedia's gloss; the manifesto's cause is "his poverty (although descended of Noble Parents)"); **[X]** no duration is given for the cloister years, the "twelve years" figure being absent from the text and irreconcilable with its own markers; **[X]** the monks are not said to be too poor to feed or clothe him, which the pool invented.

**Departure with Brother P.A.L., "being yet in his growing years" [A - Fama].** No age, year, road, or port. **[X] "At sixteen he set out" is a misplacement:** sixteen is the age at ARRIVAL at Damcar. Dated 1393, pinned 49.0, 11.0, for ordering only.

## 2. Cyprus, Damascus, and the Jerusalem never reached (c.1394)

**P.A.L. dies in Cyprus and "never came to Jerusalem" [A - Fama].** No town, grave, or cause. **Famagusta [R - tradition reasoning from Lusignan pilgrim traffic]**, carried only in `suggested_refs` as convenient rather than attested.

**[X] "Did not return" does not mean he failed to carry the body home.** The pool read it that way; the Fama never mentions a body. It means he did not turn back homeward.

**He goes to Damascus, not straight on to Damcar [A - Fama: he "went to Damasco, minding from thence to go to Jerusalem; but by reason of the feebleness of his body he remained still there"].** **[X]** The pool had him continuing "toward Damcar" from Cyprus; he is detained at Damascus, practises physick among the Turks, and only then bargains to be carried on.

**Jerusalem is never reached [A - Fama],** a fact about the journey and not a lacuna in the manuscript. Given a stop at the Holy Sepulchre (31.7784, 35.2298) whose `date_confidence` reads "not a visit": the one pin on the route that stays empty.

## 3. Damcar: the sages, the Book M, three years (c.1394 - 1397)

**Received "not as a stranger, but as one whom they had long expected," and called by his name [A - Fama].** The parenthesis "as he himself witnesseth" claims a source the text does not produce.

**Sixteen on arrival, "of a strong Dutch constitution." Three years of study, in "Physick" and "Mathematicks" [A - Fama].** **[X] Not "physics":** physick is medicine. **[X] Kabbalah and magic belong to the Fez episode.**

**Arabic learned, "the Book M." translated into good Latin "the year following" his arrival [A - Fama].** **[X] The manifesto never writes "Liber M" and never glosses M as "Book of the World";** both are later commentary. **[X] The pool's 1397 date is wrong on its own arithmetic:** the year following a 1394-95 arrival is c.1395.

### Contradiction flagged, not resolved: Damasco vs Damear

The manifestos disagree with each other, in the same translator's English, about where the wise men live.

- *Fama*: "the Wise men of Damasco in Arabia," folding the sages into the Syrian city. **[A]**
- *Confessio*: "the City Damear in Arabia," a separate place. **[A]**
- Scholarship treats Damear/Damcar as intended, Damasco as a translator's or printer's slip. **[R - Yates (1972) via Wikipedia; Yates not opened this pass.]**
- Some identify Damcar with **Dhamar in Yemen** (14.5427, 44.4079), the coordinate used for the three Damcar stops. **[R]**

Never reconciled in the primary texts; Damascus and Damcar are kept as separate stops.

## 4. Egypt, Fez, Spain (c.1397 - 1400)

**Red Sea crossing, "over Sinus Arabicus into Egypt" [A - Fama].** No port on either shore. **[X]** The pool's 26.1039, 34.2792 sits on the Egyptian coast at Al-Qusayr, asserting the very harbour its own claim text called unsupported. Corrected to 26.1039, 35.4000, mid-water.

**Egypt: "he remained not long, but only take better notice there of the Plants and Creatures" [A - Fama].** No city; Cairo (30.0444, 31.2357) is a declared placeholder.

**Fez: two years, directed there by the Arabians, sailing "over the whole Mediterranean Sea"; the Elementary Inhabitants, "who revealed unto him many of their secrets" [A - Fama]. His objection is doctrinal, not a rating of skill [A - Fama: "their Magia was not altogether pure, and also that their Cabala was defiled with their Religion"].** **[X]** The pool said he judges Fez's wisdom smaller than Damcar's; the Fama says the opposite about competence, "in those are they of Fez most skilful."

**Departure "with many costly things into Spain" after two years [A - Fama].** No port named; Tangier (35.7595, -5.834) is a declared route inference.

**Spain: the reformation offered and laughed at [A - Fama],** since granting his corrections would mean publishing their own errors. **No Spanish city is named [A - absence];** Toledo (39.8628, -4.0273) carries the pin as a labelled later guess.

**Contradiction flagged: the alumbrados.** A modern gloss ties the rebuff to them **[R - Wikipedia, citing no primary source]**; they do not exist until the 1500s, a century after the legendary date. **He tries other nations and it goes no better [A - Fama],** with no further country named.

## 5. Germany, the House of the Holy Spirit, the eight (c.1400 - 1484)

**Return to Germany, which he "heartily loved" for "the alterations which were shortly to come" [A - Fama]**, a clause written in 1614 about a man dated to 1400 and pointing at the Reformation. **He "builded a fitting and neat habitation" [A - Fama];** town never named. **[X] The five years are AFTER the return, not the length of his travels [A - Fama: "After five years came again into his mind the wished for Reformation"].** The pool read them as five years abroad; his time abroad runs at minimum seven.

**[X] The order begins with four, not eight [A - Fama: "first, by four persons onely"]:** C.R. plus **G.V., I.A., I.O.**, called out of his first cloister, who build a magical language and a dictionary said to be still in use. The other four come once the building "called Sancti spiritus was now finished": the kinsman R.C., a painter called B., and G. and P.D. as secretary, eight in all. **Its location is never named by either manifesto [A - absence];** the Kassel proposal is proximity to the printing history, not attestation **[R]**.

**The six articles [A - Fama]:** cure the sick and that gratis; no distinctive habit; meet yearly at the House; each names his successor; the word R.C. for seal and character; the Fraternity secret one hundred years. **[X] The date 1407 is not in the Fama;** it is later arithmetic that the pool cited to "Fama Fraternitatis narrative tradition." Kept for ordering only.

**The dispersal [A - Fama: five depart, B. and D. stay a whole year, then only the kinsman C.O. remains].** **[X] The pool's 1410 is invented: the Fama gives no date, and not all eight scattered.**

**England: one of the eight "cured a young Earl of Norfolk of the Leprosie" [A - Fama].** Country and title, no town. **[X] The pool's heading "Norfolk / Framlingham region" conflates title with county: Framlingham is in Suffolk.** The reasoning survives, the castle being the seat of the Bigod earls and later the Howard dukes of Norfolk. Coordinates corrected 52.2266, 1.3428 to 52.2241, 1.3469 **[A - Wikipedia, "Framlingham Castle"]**.

**Death: 106 years, hence 1484 [A for the number - Confessio; R for the year, arithmetic].** **[X] The pool reversed the attribution:** the Confessio supplies BOTH numbers in one sentence, and the Fama gives neither, its Elogium saying only "centenario major." **Manner of death: not by sickness, which had never touched his body, but God's spirit calling the illuminated soul out of it [A - the Fama's Elogium].** **[X] The pool attributed this to the Confessio; it is the Fama.** **Burial concealed from later brethren [A - Fama]:** the next generation of the order does not know where he lies.

## 6. The vault (1604)

**Discovery 120 years after the death [A for the interval - the door's "Post 120 annos patebo"; R for 1604, arithmetic depending on where the interval starts, which the Fama words inexactly].**

**The nail and the memorial table [A - Fama: the nail drawn out "took with him an indifferent big stone out of the thin wall"]. The vault: "seven sides and corners, every side five foot broad, and the height of eight foot" [A - Fama].** Ceiling divided by the seven sides around a bright centre; floor "parted in the triangle," showing "the power and rule of the inferior Governors"; each wall a door for a chest of books, "looking-glasses of divers virtues," bells, burning lamps. **[X] The pool's gloss "painted images of the heavens and the earth" is loose.**

### Contradiction flagged: the inscriptions are three, not one

The pool, following Wikipedia, gave one altar inscription: "Jesus mihi omnia, nequaquam vacuum, libertas evangelii, dei gloria intacta, legis jugum." **[X] The Fama has three distinct positions, and the Wikipedia version reorders the last three:**

1. Altar plate: **A. C. R. C. Hoc universi compendium unius mihi sepulchrum feci.** **[A]**
2. "Round about the first Circle or Brim": **Jesus mihi omnia.** **[A]**
3. Circumscribing four figures in the middle, in order: **Nequaquam vacuum. Legis Jugum. Libertas Evangelij. Dei gloria intacta.** **[A]**

**The body: "a fair and worthy body, whole and unconsumed" [A - Fama]. The book in his hand: I or T, unresolved [A - Vaughan 1652: "a parchment book, Called I."; other editions read T].** The pool gave "the book called T" flatly; Vaughan's I is quoted and the T variant named, without choosing.

## 7. Print, authorship, and the furore (1586 - 1652)

**Manuscript circulation in the Tyrol by 1610; Adam Haselmayer publishes a reply in 1612 [A - Wikipedia].** **[X] There is no 1610 imprint,** so the pool's "c.1610 vs 1614" is not a live dispute: 1610 dates the manuscript Haselmayer read. Wikipedia's "published circa 1610 in Kassel" is the source of the confusion.

**Fama first printed at Kassel, 1614, by Wilhelm Wessel [A - Wikipedia]**, bound into the *Allgemeine und general Reformation*. **[X] The Boccalini relationship runs the other way from the pool's:** Boccalini's 77th ragguaglio (Venice 1612) is the Italian ORIGINAL, the *General Reformation* a translation OF it, the Fama appended to that translation.

**Confessio Fraternitatis, Kassel, 1615 [A - Wikipedia]**, appended to Philip a Gabella's *Secretioris Philosophiae Consideratio Brevis*. **[X] The Kassel / Frankfurt conflict resolves to Kassel;** the "Frankfurt" reading in Wikipedia's "Rosicrucianism" article is an error there, not a dispute. **Chymische Hochzeit, Strasbourg, Lazarus Zetzner, 1616 [A - Wikipedia]**, its narrative dated 1459 in the German title.

**The opening line: corrected and verified.** The pool gave "In the year of our Lord 1459, upon a certain evening before Easter day, I sat at me table," tagged R because the text could not be reached. It was reached. **[A - E. Foxcroft trans., *The Hermetick Romance*, London: A. Sowle, 1690; archive.org `hermetickromance00rose`. First Day: "It was an Evening before Easter-Day, I sate at a Table..."]** The year 1459 is **not in the sentence**, attaching through the German title page, and "I sat at me table" is garbled for "I sate at a Table."

**Andreae born at Herrenberg, Wurttemberg, 17 August 1586; Chymical Wedding composed c.1605 [A - Wikipedia, reporting Andreae in *Menippus*].** **[X] The pool's chronology lens gave 1607 and its geography lens 1605: they disagreed with each other.** Wikipedia brackets "around 1605-1607"; 1605 is the anchor.

**The ludibrium [A - *Menippus* (1617), again in *Turris Babel* (1619)].** **[X] The pool said Andreae "explicitly denied any hand in the Fama or Confessio."** Wikipedia says only that in later life he presented himself as an orthodox Lutheran "who had nothing at all to do with" them: a distancing, not a denial.

**Fludd and Maier corrected;** the pool put both in 1618 in "England/Germany." **[X] Fludd: *Apologia Compendiaria* 1616, *Tractatus Apologeticus* 1617, both at Leiden, not England. [X] Maier: *Silentium post clamores* 1617, *Themis Aurea* 1618; Frankfurt am Main from 1616. [X] "Reframing its alchemy in symbolic rather than operative terms" is a stretch for Maier**, who remained an operative alchemist. **[A - Wikipedia, "Robert Fludd"; "Michael Maier."]**

**Some 400 pamphlets and books between the manifestos and c.1620, none producing a member; Paris posters, 1622, announcing "Deputies of the Higher College of the Rose-Croix"; Vaughan's English translation, London 1652; Foxcroft's English Chymical Wedding, 1690 [all A - Wikipedia].** Foxcroft is carried in the Strasbourg stop's `quote_source`.

## 8. The orders that take his name (1710 - 1915)

**Sincerus Renatus, 1710 [A for the book year - Wikipedia].** **[X]** The author is named Sigmund Richter in Wikipedia's "Order of the Golden and Rosy Cross" (Samuel also circulates); **[X]** the ORDER dates to the 1750s, so the book anticipates rather than founds it; **[X]** "the first organization to claim direct institutional descent" is an unsupported superlative, dropped. **Geheime Figuren, 1785 [A - same].**

**Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia [A - Wikipedia]:** formation meeting 1 June 1867 at Aldermanbury, London, Robert Wentworth Little elected Master Magus. **[X] The pool's 1865 is the traditional date for Little's obtaining the rituals, not the founding.**

**Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, London 1887 [A - Wikipedia]**, by Westcott, Woodman and Mathers out of the Cipher Manuscripts decoded that year. **[X] The pool's 1888 is the founding of the Isis-Urania temple, a different event.** **Rosicrucian Fellowship, 8 August 1909, Seattle [A - Wikipedia].** **[X] The pool placed the founding at Mount Ecclesia, Oceanside;** it opened only 28 October 1911, its temple dedicated 1920.

**AMORC incorporated by H. Spencer Lewis, 1915, United States [A - Wikipedia]. No city is given in the reachable material.** Pinned at San Jose, California (37.3382, -121.8863) as a declared soft pin: California enters through `theodor_reuss`, and San Jose is the order's later seat, not an attested 1915 address.

**Steiner and Heindel on the reappearance as the Count of St. Germain [R - Wikipedia; doctrinal tradition, not historical record]. Quinta da Regaleira, Sintra, 1904-1910, Carvalho Monteiro with Luigi Manini [A for site and dates; R for the Rosicrucian reading, later and contested].**

**Gap not closed:** that twentieth-century Rosicrucian bodies present C.R. as a living initiate rather than a literary figure could not be confirmed against a primary AMORC or Anthroposophical text. Carried as **[R]**.

## 10. Honest gaps

1. **No birthplace**, and **no location for Sancti Spiritus**, where the order meets, the founder is buried, and the vault is found.
2. **No date for the dispersal of the brethren;** the cited 1410 is invented. **No further country after Spain;** the rebuff is called general and nowhere else named.
3. **Damcar / Damear / Damasco unresolved** between the manifestos; **Book I or Book T unresolved** between editions.
4. **Yates (1972) not opened directly;** the Damcar reading arrives through Wikipedia's summary. **No primary AMORC or Anthroposophical text consulted** for the twentieth-century claims about a living C.R.
5. **No verifiable historical record of the man at all.** The governing gap, not resolved either way. **[A - Wikipedia, "Christian Rosenkreuz."]**
