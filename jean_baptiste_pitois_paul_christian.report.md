# Jean-Baptiste Pitois, called Paul Christian (1811-1877)
*Research report, 2026-08-23. For `jean_baptiste_pitois_paul_christian.journey.json` (8 segments, 44 stops).*

A Vosges boy sent toward the priesthood who left a Trappist novitiate, became one of the most prolific compilers of the Paris book trade, served as secretary to the marshal who conquered Algeria, presided over a Jacobin club in 1848 and was transported for it, spent the Second Empire writing Catholic devotional fiction, and in 1863 and 1870 invented at a Paris desk the Egyptian initiation beneath the Sphinx that the occult tarot has believed in ever since. He published under at least five names, and the national library gets his death date wrong by five years.

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition · contradictions flagged, never adjudicated.

## 1. Origins and the cloister (1811-1829)

- Born **Jean Baptiste Pitois at Remiremont, Vosges, 15 May 1811** **[A: BnF authority record `ark:/12148/cb12327453r`, fetched directly; independently encyclopedia.com]**. No father is named in anything reachable. A bookseller-publisher, **Pitois-Levrault**, stands in the wider family, and several early von Schmid translations carry the imprint "Christian Pitois-Levrault" **[R: *La littérature française contemporaine*, not re-fetched]**.
- Church schooling at **Harcourt (Eure)**, then among the **Clercs de la Chapelle Royale** in Paris **[R: Ross Nichols only; no register, roll, or bill of board located, and neither stage confirmed independently]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the dossier's 49.1667, 0.8167 is ~2.2 km east of the commune; fr.wikipedia gives 49.1678, 0.7872.
- **Trappist novitiate c. 1828-1829, religious name Marie-Bernard**, about a year, no permanent vows **[R: secondary occult-tarot biographies; encyclopedia.com confirms only "raised in a monastic community by his family, who wanted him to enter the priesthood"]**. No source names the house; Notre-Dame de la Grande Trappe at **Soligny-la-Trappe** is an inference from the generic name, and the community resettled there in 1827 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. *Coordinate corrected:* the dossier's 48.6172, 0.5279 is ~4 km southwest of the abbey, at 48.6358, 0.5731. Internal corroboration: **Marie-Bernard recurs twice more**, as his son's given names and as the invented author "Dom Marie-Bernard" of 1855.
- **Strasbourg, c. 1829-1835**, an uncle said to be rector of the academy **[R: Ross Nichols; no register, thesis, or qualification located]**.

**Gap.** Nothing documentary survives between birth and the mid-1830s. Every stop in segment 1 except the birth is [R], all from one channel: *La Lumière* and Ross Nichols's use of it.

## 2. The Caribbean legend (early-to-mid 1830s)

- Family tradition: **Martinique**, anti-slavery agitation, then Spain and partisan fighting **[R: the posthumous serial in *La Lumière* (1893-1894), apparently furnished by Pitois's son; retold by every later occult biographer]**. A competing strand names **Dominica** instead **[R: Ross Nichols]**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Dominica was British, which sits badly with the French colonial framing and suggests transmission error rather than a distinct episode.
- **No contemporary record of any kind was located**: no ship's list, passport, consular note, police report, or newspaper notice. Both stops are in the file, on two pins, so the contradiction is visible on the map.

## 3. Paris of the periodicals and the Ministry (1836-1846)

- **Revue Germanique (1836-1838), L'Éclair (1838), Gazette des salons (1839-1840)** **[R: tarot historians' reconstruction, no masthead fetched. His general presence in Parisian literary journalism is [A] via encyclopedia.com and the BnF record; the three postings are not]**.
- **Collaboration with Charles Nodier at the Bibliothèque de l'Arsenal** and the historical résumé for **Paris historique, promenade dans les rues de Paris (1838-1839)**, published under Nodier's name with plates by Régnier and Champin **[A: BnF authority record; encyclopedia.com independently; UPenn carries the title]**. The BnF wording supports *collaboration*, not a staff post; later occult biography inflates it into an apprenticeship.
- **Post in the library of the Ministry of Public Instruction, 1839** **[A: BnF authority record and encyclopedia.com independently]**. The inventory work on duplicates and confiscated monastic libraries is [A] in outline; *discovering* forgotten astrological volumes there is **[R: his own self-presentation]**.
- **The translation run** **[A by catalogue, dossier corrected]**. BnF SRU: Montaigne, Ossian, Young's *Nuits* (all 1842), Helvétius with a prefatory essay, Irving's *Alhambra*, Hoffmann (all 1843), Boccaccio (1844), von Schmid 1839-1846. **Machiavelli, named in older accounts, appears nowhere among the ~100 BnF records and has been dropped.** Range corrected from "1838" to 1839-1846.

## 4. Algeria (1843-1847)

- **Private secretary to Marshal Bugeaud, governor-general, 1843-1844, noncombatant** **[A: BnF authority record; encyclopedia.com, "the French army (Algiers, 1843-44)"; Effros, *Incidental Archaeologists* (2018), independently]**. Three independent sources, the best-attested non-literary fact of his life. No site within Algeria is documented, so Algiers stands for the theater.
- **The Kabylia adventure**: quicksand, a blade at the neck, a pistol believed unloaded that fires, rescue, two bullet wounds **[R: posthumous family tradition said to draw on a diary; no military report, correspondence, or newspaper found]**. Documented service, undocumented afternoon.
- **Souvenir du maréchal Bugeaud (1845)** and **L'Algérie de la jeunesse (1847)** **[A: BnF/UPenn listings]**.
- **L'Afrique française, l'empire de Maroc et les déserts de Sahara (1846)**, transmitting testimony on the **Dahra cave asphyxiations of June 1845**, at points critical of French "pacification" **[A for the publication, UPenn; [R] for the critical content and the Dahra transmission, Effros, not re-fetched]**. **Pitois was not present**: the killings postdate his service and he printed second-hand testimony.

## 5. 1848: the Jacobin, the arrest, the transportation

- **29 March 1848: opening address as provisional president of the Club central des Jacobins**, styled "le citoyen Pitois, Christian" **[A: the BnF catalogue holds the printed pamphlet *Discours prononcé à la séance d'ouverture, le 29 mars 1848* under his authority]**. *Tag upgraded from [R],* which rested only on Maitron returning 403: the pamphlet is documentary evidence on its own.
- **Journal des Jacobins: sentinelle des droits de l'homme (1848)**, editor-in-chief **[A for the periodical and his catalogued association, BnF SRU; [R] for "one issue only" and the editorship title, Hatin via a rare-book description, not fetched]**. Office address unknown.
- **Arrest order 23 June 1848; arrest 31 July, papers seized at his home; "transporté"; pardoned; returned December 1849** **[R: Maitron]**. **Maitron could not be read**: maitron.fr and the fusilles-40-44 mirror both returned **403 Forbidden**, two attempts. No charge, judgment, or conviction is claimed even in the summarized account, and his Paris address is nowhere established.
- **The destination of the transportation is unknown.** Belle-Île-en-Mer and Algeria are documented for the 1848 *transportés* as a class, never for Pitois by name. The stop is pinned at the point of departure, with the gap stated in `date_confidence`; guessing a coordinate was refused.

## 6. The turn (1850-1860)

- **Dieu et France. Agonie de la Révolution (1850)** **[A: bibliographic record]**. Published months after his return from transportation, by the man who had presided over a Jacobin club twenty-two months earlier. He never explains the reversal.
- **Staff of the Moniteur du Soir, c. 1850-1852** **[R: tarot-history scholarship and Ross Nichols; no masthead located]**.
- **Marriage, 1853, to an Italian woman said to be related to Walter Scott** **[R: Maitron, inaccessible]**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** a Lyon civil-register index gives a marriage to **Louise Marie Tortillet on 26 June 1877**. Neither source produces a reconciling document. Both are carried in the file.
- **Histoire de la Terreur (1853)** **[A: UPenn/BnF]**.
- **"Carmen sybillinum. 3 avril 1854"**: a prophecy of victory in the Crimea and the birth of an imperial heir, **printed in 1856, after both events** **[A for the publication; [R] for the 1854 composition date and the claimed acknowledgment from the Empress's household, both the work's own self-presentation]**.
- **Les Héros du christianisme (from 1855)**, published under the invented authorship of the monk **"Dom Marie-Bernard"** with Christian named only as editor; he later disclosed publicly that he wrote the whole series **[A for the series; [R] for the disclosure, reported in Decker/Depaulis/Dummett, not verified against the volumes' front matter]**.
- **The seven sacrament novels** **[A by catalogue, the dossier's single 1859 date corrected to 1859-1860]**. BnF, each subtitled with its sacrament: *Les Coeurs brisés* (pénitence), *L'Écho des Catacombes* (confirmation), *La Prison mamertine* (baptême), all 1859; *L'Ange de Wehrda* (mariage), *La Crosse et le glaive* (l'ordre), *La Forêt vierge* (extrême-onction), *Une page de ma vie* (l'Eucharistie), *Les Fleurs du ciel*, all 1860.

## 7. The occult system (1863-1870)

- **L'Homme rouge des Tuileries (1863)**, self-published, title page giving **22 rue d'Angoulême, Champs-Élysées**, advertised with 22 kabbalistic figures; the 78 cards reorganized as "symbolic houses" and the trumps renamed (the Magus, Isis-Urania, the Sphinx, Typhon, the Crocodile at zero, the Crown of the Magi) **[A for the publication and the redesign, UPenn and Decker/Depaulis/Dummett; [R] for the address: the Scribd scan returned only upload metadata]**. The street has been renamed and the coordinate is approximate.
- **Histoires héroïques des Français, racontées à S. A. Napoléon-Eugène, prince impérial (1863)** **[A: BnF catalogue data]**. **Le Moniteur catholique, editor-in-chief or manager, from 1865** **[A: BnF authority record and periodical listing]**.
- **Fulla l'Égyptienne (1866), signed Charles Moreau** **[A: BnF SRU returns the 1866 title; the authority record carries "Moreau, Charles (1811-1872)" among his rejected names]**. *Correction:* the dossier gave the form as "Christian Moreau"; BnF says **Moreau, Charles**.
- **Horoscope de Napoléon Ier, signed "Lussides" (1868)** **[R: Chacornac's attribution, on reuse of language from L'Homme rouge des Tuileries; strongly probable in the literature; no signed copy checked]**.
- **Editor of the Messager du Centre (1869)** **[R: secondary synthesis, no masthead located]**. The paper's town is unknown; the stop is pinned at Paris and says so.
- **Histoire de la magie, du monde surnaturel et de la fatalité à travers les temps et les peuples (Paris: Furne, Jouvet et Cie, 1870)**, viii + 666 pp., frontispiece and plates, seven books **[A: Wellcome Collection record, fetched directly; archive.org confirms the seven-book structure]**.

### The two fabrications, both [A] as findings

1. **The Iamblichus citation.** Christian attributes to Iamblichus's treatise on the Egyptian mysteries an account of initiation trials beneath the Sphinx and the Great Pyramid, with 78 stages and 22 sacred images matching his own 1863 redesign. **The cited passage does not exist in Iamblichus** **[A as a finding: Decker/Depaulis/Dummett (1996), against the *De mysteriis*; not re-run here]**. Two verbatim French sentences from the 1870 original were read in the archive.org digitization and are quoted in the journey file.
2. **The Cagliostro scene.** Christian narrates a supposed 1785 memoir scene of **Court de Gébelin** presiding over a masonic interrogation of **Cagliostro** at the Philalethes convention in Paris. **Court de Gébelin died 12 May 1784** **[A: Decker/Depaulis/Dummett, against a widely documented death date]**. Cagliostro's own file independently places him in Paris in 1785 (Rue Saint-Claude, 30 January; arrested 23 August).

- **Terminology.** Christian popularized **lames** and **arcanes** in French occult usage; the paired terms **"Major Arcana"/"Minor Arcana" are not his**, appearing later in Ely Star and Papus, late 1880s **[A: Decker/Depaulis/Dummett]**.

## 8. Last years, death, and the record (1870-1877)

- **Histoire de la guerre avec la Prusse et des deux sièges de Paris, 1870-1871**, 2 vols. (1872), apparently his last major history **[A: UPenn; BnF-linked data]**. His presence in Paris through the sieges is plausible but not separately documented.
- **Letters, February to June, to a pupil named Jacques Charrot**: onomantic astrology, Éliphas Lévi and the cartomancer Edmond, declining health, plans for a **"Faculty of Occult Sciences"** that never functioned **[R: Ross Nichols and later tarot historians, as surviving or once accessible; no primary letters located since]**.
- **Death: 12 July 1877, at Lyon, aged 66** **[A: encyclopedia.com, "Died July 12, 1877, at Lyons, France"; en.wikipedia and the Lyon indexing agree]**.
- **Contradiction flagged, not resolved: the BnF authority record gives 7 December 1872**, cataloguing him as "Christian, P. (1811-1872)" **[A as a record, fetched directly]**. 1877 has two independent supports; 1872 appears in one catalogue record. The file carries a stop for the BnF record at its own claimed date, and the Lyon stops after it.
- **Marriage to Louise Marie Tortillet (1825-1877) in Lyon 2e, 26 June 1877**, sixteen days before death **[R: Geneanet citing "Mariages de Lyon 02"; 403, and the civil act was not retrieved]**. Nothing explains what brought him from Paris to Lyon.

### Honest gaps

- **No grave.** No tomb, cemetery record, stone, or monument in any source consulted, and the Lyon death act has never been retrieved. A gap, not proof no grave existed.
- **No biography.** No film, documentary, or standalone archival biography. The best modern treatment remains chapters inside Decker/Depaulis/Dummett (1996, 2002) and Effros's 2018 recovery of his colonial-journalist career.
- **No addresses**: no Paris residence beyond the 1863 title page, no office for any of the six periodicals he is said to have worked for. **His first twenty-five years** rest on one family channel.
- **Reception correction:** the Kirkup and Shaw translation, edited by Ross Nichols, was **first published by Forge Press, London, 1952**, and **reissued by Citadel Press, New York, 1969**. Wikipedia and encyclopedia.com cite only the American reissue, which is why some accounts read 1969 as the first edition; OpenLibrary carries both. The dossier's 1952 is not an error. The 1928 Knapp/Hall material is **illustrations only**.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Pitois is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops were written. Two relations in the research pool are **not rooted in Pitois's own life** and were **kept out of the campas rather than silently deleted**. Both are **forward**, which the exception never licenses in any case:

1. **A. E. Waite (`arthur_edward_waite.journey.json`), forward.** Waite's *Pictorial Key to the Tarot* (1910) rejects the Egyptian-tarot myth by name ("Court de Gebelin's Egypt is a fabrication"), the lineage Christian elaborated in 1870. Waite's file targets Court de Gébelin, not Christian, and postdates Christian's death by thirty-three years. **Direction caught: forward.** If wanted, it belongs on Waite's own file.
2. **H. P. Blavatsky (`blavatsky.journey.json`), forward and thin.** Her Theosophical Society (1875) and its Egyptian-mystery imagery sit in the current Christian's 1870 volume fed. No citation of Christian in her file, no contact documented. **Direction caught: forward, and only a resonance.**

## Interlocks actually written into campas

Named only for a direct or one-directional relation, never for shared geography:

- **`cagliostro`**: Christian wrote the scene staging Cagliostro's interrogation. Backward, one-directional (d. 1795).
- **`abdelkader`**: Pitois was Bugeaud's secretary in the war against Abdelkader and published four books on that campaign, including the volume transmitting the Dahra testimony. The Dahra cave is **Abdelkader's stop, not Pitois's**: here it is only a Paris publication stop, with the cave in `suggested_refs`.
- **`etteilla`**: Christian rebuilt the deck Etteilla had already re-engineered and sold as an Egyptian book. Backward, no contact possible (d. 1791).
- **`napoleon`**: *L'Homme rouge des Tuileries* (1863) is built on the specter legend attached to Napoleon Bonaparte, and the 1868 *Horoscope de Napoléon Ier* casts his nativity.

Court de Gébelin, Nodier, Éliphas Lévi, Bugeaud and Iamblichus are named in prose but have no atlas files.
