# Claude Elwood Shannon (1916-2001) research report
*2026-08-05. Prepared for `claude_shannon.journey.json`. Petoskey and Gaylord; Ann Arbor; Cambridge; Cold Spring Harbor; Princeton; 463 West Street, Manhattan; Murray Hill; Winchester and Medford.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in the tag. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim repeated by secondary literature without a primary reached here. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; inaccessible sources are named with the reason.

**The record is unusually solid and unusually thin at once.** The papers are dated to the page and the volume, the honours to the year; the private life, including two weddings, a burial, and the naming of the central term of his own theory, rests on inference, uncited encyclopedia sentences, or anecdotes with competing versions. Five corrections below are corrections to the *place* of a well-dated event, which is the axis a geographic atlas cares about.

## 2. Ann Arbor and Cambridge, 1932-1940

**University of Michigan, 1932-1936, dual bachelor's degrees in electrical engineering and mathematics [A, Wikipedia + MacTutor].** The symbolic-logic course credited with planting the Boolean seed is attested in both, without a course number. **MIT graduate study from 1936, research assistant on Bush's differential analyzer [A, Wikipedia + ETHW]. Bell Labs summer, 1937, at West Street [A, Wikipedia].**

**Master's thesis, *A Symbolic Analysis of Relay and Switching Circuits*, 1937 [A, Wikipedia + ETHW].** Published as a paper 1938; **Alfred Noble Prize 1939** [A]. The pool carried two chronology rows for the prize (1938 "wins the following year" and a separate 1939 award). These are one event; the journey folds them into the thesis stop.

**Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory with Barbara Burks, 1939 [A, Wikipedia] / [CORRECTED coordinate].** The pool's 40.8623, -73.4571 sits roughly 900 m ENE of the laboratory, across the inlet. The laboratory is at **1 Bungtown Road, Laurel Hollow**, Wikipedia's own coordinate **40.8583, -73.4667**, which the journey uses. The hamlet and the laboratory named for it are not the same place.

**PhD in mathematics, MIT, 1940, dissertation *An Algebra for Theoretical Genetics* [A, MacTutor + ETHW].**

**First marriage, to Norma Levor, January 1940 [A month/year, Wikipedia] / [CORRECTED place] / [gap].** The pool placed this in New York City at 40.7128, -74.006. **The coordinate is invented.** No source consulted (Wikipedia, MacTutor, Wikipedia "Norma Barzman") locates the ceremony anywhere; in January 1940 Shannon was a graduate student at MIT, and the couple's attested joint residence is Princeton. The journey moves the marker to the canonical MIT pin and says in `date_confidence` that the venue is unattested. Levor married Ben Barzman in 1942, until his death in 1989 [A].

## 3. Princeton and the war, 1940-1945

**Institute for Advanced Study, National Research Fellow, 1940-1941 [A, MacTutor + ETHW].** Fuld Hall; Weyl, Einstein, and von Neumann in residence; Oppenheimer directs from 1947. **Bell Telephone Laboratories, full time from 1941, 463 West Street, Manhattan [A, Wikipedia + ETHW],** under National Defense Research Committee contract, section D-2: anti-aircraft fire control and cryptography. **Signal-flow graphs, 1942; Data Smoothing and Prediction in Fire-Control Systems, 1945, co-authored; the classified memorandum *A Mathematical Theory of Cryptography*, September 1945, declassified and published 1949 [all A, Wikipedia].**

**Turing at Bell Labs, early 1943, roughly two months [A, Wikipedia].** Teatime in the cafeteria; Turing on secure speech; the 1936 universal-machine paper handed over. No atlas file documents this meeting; see section 9.

## 4. Murray Hill and the theory, 1948-1952

### 4a. The place correction governing the whole section

The pool's chronology gave **"New York, New York"** for the 1948 paper and the 1949-1951 events, contradicting its own geography array, which correctly puts the 1948 publication at **Murray Hill, New Jersey (40.6852, -74.396)**. By 1948 Bell Labs research, mathematics included, was at Murray Hill. **The journey uses Murray Hill from 1948 onward** and keeps West Street only for 1941-1945. The year Shannon's desk moved is fixed in no source consulted; a gap.

### 4b. The papers

**"A Mathematical Theory of Communication," *BSTJ* 27 (1948), pp. 379-423 (July) and 623-656 (October) [A, verified against the reprint PDF read directly].** Three passages on p. 379 read verbatim: the opening "fundamental problem of communication" sentence; the second paragraph on the finite-message-set measure; and the sentence on logarithmic base in which the word **bit** enters the language, credited by Shannon to **J. W. Tukey**. Two of the three are quoted in the journey.

**"Programming a Computer for Playing Chess" [A, citation verified exactly].** Presented 9 March 1949; published *Philosophical Magazine*, series 7, vol. 41, no. 314 (March 1950), pp. 256-275. **[CORRECTED quotation.]** The pool carried "Although perhaps of no practical importance, the question is of theoretical interest." closed with a full stop, manufacturing a sentence Shannon never wrote. The primary text (Collected Papers reprint, Introduction, sec. 1) runs on through "a wedge in attacking other problems of a similar nature and of greater significance." The journey quotes it whole.

Also [A, Wikipedia]: "Communication Theory of Secrecy Systems," 1949, the declassified memorandum proving the one-time pad unbreakable; the Shannon number, about 10^120, 1950; Theseus the maze-solving mouse, 1950, built with Betty Shannon; "Prediction and Entropy of Printed English," 1951; the Special Cryptologic Advisory Group, May 1951.

**The Weaver book, University of Illinois Press, 1949 [A] / travel [R, negative].** Nothing in the sources consulted has Shannon travelling to Urbana; the journey's Urbana pin is a publisher's address and says so. A 1963 paperback follows, ISBN 0-252-72548-4 [A].

### 4c. Second marriage: 27 March 1949 [A date] / [CORRECTED claim] / [gap on venue]

MacTutor states verbatim: "On 27 March 1949 Shannon married Mary Elizabeth Moore." The pool's row read "marries ... **at Bell Labs**," a misreading of Wikipedia, which says he **met** her there, where she was a numerical analyst. **No source consulted gives the venue.** The pool's geography row placed it in New York City at 40.7128, -74.006, compounding the error, since by 1949 Bell Labs research was at Murray Hill. The journey uses Murray Hill and says in `date_confidence` that the pin follows her workplace, not a document.

### 4d. The entropy anecdote [R, contested, misattributed in the pool]

The pool listed "You should call it entropy, for two reasons..." under *quotes* as a **Shannon** quotation. Three corrections, applied. (1) **The words are von Neumann's**, recounted by Shannon, and must not run as Shannon's own. (2) **The citation was reversed:** per Scientific American's own site (DOI 10.1038/scientificamerican0971-179) it is **Edward C. McIrvine and Myron Tribus**, "Energy and Information," *Scientific American* 225, no. 3 (September 1971), 179-188, McIrvine first. (3) **The truncation cut the punchline:** "In the second place, and more important, no one really knows what entropy really is, so in a debate you will always have the advantage." The journey quotes it whole.

The SciAm article is paywalled and was not opened; the wording rests on Wikiquote and Wikipedia "History of entropy," both citing it, and Wikipedia hedges with "According to one source" while printing a competing version. **The phrase is absent from all 954 pages of the *Collected Papers*.** Date and place are guesses, c.1940-1948, Princeton or Bell Labs, and the journey says so.

### 4e. The unicycle and the juggling equation

Riding a unicycle through the Bell Labs halls while juggling is corroborated by colleagues' accounts [A, ETHW]. The "unified field theory of juggling," B/H = (D+F)/(D+E), is attested in the Horgan profile [A].

## 5. The return to Cambridge, 1956-1961

**[CORRECTED: the pool's chronology contradicted its own geography.]** The pool said Shannon "becomes a professor at MIT, holding an endowed chair" in 1956. MacTutor, MIT News, and ETHW agree on a four-step sequence, which the pool's own geography array already had right: **1956** visiting professor of communication sciences and mathematics; **1957** permanent faculty; **1958** Donner Professor of Science, held until **1978**, retirement and the emeritus title [all A, MacTutor + ETHW].

**Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, June-August 1956 [A, Wikipedia],** co-organized with McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester; *Automata Studies*, co-edited with McCarthy, the same year [A]. **"The Bandwagon," *IRE Transactions on Information Theory* 2, no. 1 (March 1956) [A for the citation; the quoted sentence seen only via Wikiquote, the IRE text not opened]. Winchester, Massachusetts, house on Mystic Lake, from 1956, later nicknamed Entropy House [A, ETHW + IEEE Spectrum],** at 42.438, -71.142.

**Roulette computer with Edward O. Thorp [A] / [CORRECTED place and date].** The pool put this at Cambridge, 1961. Wikipedia's Edward O. Thorp article: "The wearable computer was co-developed with Claude Shannon between 1960 and 1961. The final operating version of the device was tested in Shannon's home lab at his basement in June 1961." Shannon's home from 1956 was Winchester (MIT News obituary), not Cambridge. **The journey places it at Winchester, 42.438, -71.142, dated 1961-06-01.** The Minivac 601, designed by Shannon and sold commercially the same year [A, Wikipedia], is folded into that stop; no location is attested for the design work.

## 7. The interviews, 1987 and 1992: a source correction

The pool attached a speculative 1987 *Omni* attribution to two lines from "an IEEE Spectrum profile." **That attribution is wrong.** The piece is **John Horgan, "Claude Shannon: Tinkerer, Prankster, and Father of Information Theory," *IEEE Spectrum*, April 1992** (online 27 April 2016), reporting **Horgan's own interview at Winchester, Massachusetts**, citing no Omni interview; a search of the *Collected Papers*, which reprint the Liversidge/Omni interview, does not turn up the phrases. **Cite Horgan 1992.**

**Textually unstable quotation, flagged not resolved.** Spectrum prints "without much regard for **final** value or value to the world"; the *Collected Papers* biography, p. xvii, prints "without much regard to **FINANCIAL** value," the semantically coherent reading, from the IEEE Press authorized volume. **The journey does not quote this line.** It quotes the neighbouring line, stable in both sources ("I've spent lots of time on totally useless things."), and states the variant in the campa without adopting either reading.

**Used from Omni (August 1987), Liversidge interviewing [A via a compilation page; the magazine not opened]:** "I am not depressed by it. I am rooting for the machines!"; "I can visualize a time in the future when we will be to robots as dogs are to humans."; "My motivation in science has always been curiosity about something: How is it put together?"

**The Spanish variant [CORRECTED tag].** Spanish Wikipedia carries "¡Naturalmente! ¡Usted y yo somos máquinas y vaya si pensamos!", attributed only to "un periodista" with no citation, and an uncited translation cannot support an [A]. The underlying remark is the Horgan line "You bet. I'm a machine and you're a machine, and we both think, don't we?" The journey uses the English and does not treat the Spanish as independent evidence.
