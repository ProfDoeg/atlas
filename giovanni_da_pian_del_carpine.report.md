# Giovanni da Pian del Carpine (John of Plano Carpini), c. 1185 - 1252
*Research report, 2026-08-31. Franciscan; minister provincial of Germany, Spain and Saxony; papal legate to the Mongol court 1245-1247; author of the* Historia Mongalorum; *archbishop of Antivari 1248-1252.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a research-pool claim not independently reachable this pass. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated** unless a reason is given.

Everything after April 1245 rests on Giovanni's own book, a first-person itinerary with dates pinned to feast days. In Rockhill's 1900 translation, transcribed at Silk Road Seattle, it is the densest [A] source here and corrects the research pool at six points, all marked below.

---

## 1. Origin and the silent forty years (c. 1185 - 1221)

Born at Pian del Carpine, an Umbrian locality near Lake Trasimeno west of Perugia, later absorbed into the territory of Magione. **[R]** (Wikipedia; Treccani DBI). **No birth record survives.** Estimates run c. 1180 to c. 1190; the conventional 1182 or 1185 is back-calculated from his reported age, and the file dates the stop 1185-01-01 as a placeholder. *Coordinate correction:* the pool gave 43.1339, 12.2115 here but 43.1381, 12.2094 in its own afterlife entries, and neither is Magione; the file uses **43.1433, 12.2408** for the single Umbrian pin. **[R]** on the exact hamlet.

**Everything before 1221 is a gap and is stated as one.** No entry into the order, no novitiate, no house, no ordination date, no recorded contact with Francis of Assisi. Giovanni was in the order during Francis's lifetime and never mentions him in anything that survives.

## 2. The German mission (1221-1228)

- **30 May 1221, general chapter at Assisi.** Caesarius of Speyer selects about twenty-six friars, traditionally counted, for a renewed mission into German lands; Giovanni is among them with Jordan of Giano and Thomas of Celano. **[A]** on Jordan's *Chronica*, via the pool's summary.
- *Correction.* The pool says Jordan wrote "after 1262." He **dictated** the chronicle *at* the Halberstadt chapter of German Franciscans in 1262. **[A]** And on weight: the only surviving manuscript **breaks off at 1238**, so Jordan cannot cover the later career at all. *Coordinate correction:* the pool's Assisi pin (43.0714, 12.5701) sits 1.8 km NNW of the Basilica di Santa Maria degli Angeli, in open fields; the file uses **43.0578, 12.5811** per it.wikipedia. **[A]**
- **1221, Trento.** Giovanni and Barnabas the German go ahead to prepare the route. **[R]**. The sweep through Wurzburg, Mainz, Worms, Speyer, Strasbourg, Cologne and into Saxony **[R]** is folded into that campa.
- **1223, Speyer.** Albert of Pisa appoints him **custos of Saxony**. **[A]**, confirmed by Treccani, upgraded from the pool's [R]. *Coordinate correction:* the cathedral is at **49.3175, 8.4428**; the pool's 8.4325 falls 750 m west.
- **1224, chapter of Wurzburg.** Relieved of the Saxon custody, transferred to Cologne. **[R]**. **No source gives a reason**, and the campa says so. The notice calling him *vir gravis et corpulentus*, a heavy man who used a donkey while preaching, is **[R]**, pool-only, and sits in the Speyer campa rather than taking a stop.

## 3. Provincial: Germany, Spain, Saxony (1228-1239)

- **c. 1228, minister provincial of Germany**, appointed by Giovanni Parenti. **[A]** on the sequence, **[R]** on the year: the anchor is Francis's canonization on 16 July 1228, not the appointment. No source names a seat for the German provincial, so the file pins the stop at Cologne and calls that placement editorial.
- **1230, minister provincial of Spain. [R]**, sequence via the pool only. **Nothing survives from the Spanish years**: no letter, no foundation, no dispute. The Burgos coordinate is an **editorial placement standing for a province** and the weakest pin in the file.
- **1232, back north over the new province of Saxony**, held to 1239. **[R]**; Elias of Cortona's accession in 1232 is the external anchor. **[A]** *Contradiction resolved against the pool:* its chronology lens dates the Metz convent to **1230**, contradicting both its own geography lens (**1232-1239**) and its own claim that he went to Spain in 1230. Treccani places the foundation in the Saxon provincialate; the file uses **1232**.
- **31 August 1234**, letter of Gregory IX to Giovanni and Thomas of Celano concerning Agnes of Bohemia and the women's community at Prague. **[R]**: the register entry was not fetched.

## 4. The undocumented years (1239-1245)

**No secure documentary evidence exists for Giovanni between 1239 and 1245.** **[A]** as an absence. Traditions putting him in Barbary or Tunis, or as provincial at Cologne, are **[R]**. The file gives this no stop; it sits in the Lyon 1245 `date_confidence`.

## 5. The commissioning at Lyon (March-April 1245)

- **13 March 1245**, *Cum non solum homines*, demanding an account of Mongol intentions, and **25 March 1245**, *Cum simus super*, to Eastern patriarchs and bishops. Both **[R]**, register texts via the pool.
- **16 April 1245, Easter day**, departure from Lyon with Stephen of Bohemia. **[A]**, Giovanni's own itinerary, corroborated by Wikipedia giving the same span to 18 November 1247.
- *Chronology correction, and it matters.* The pool claims the **First Council of Lyon prompted** the commissioning. That is backwards: the council **opened 28 June 1245** **[A]**, over two months after both letters and after Giovanni had left. Innocent IV did preach there on five wounds, the fourth being the cruelties of the Tatars in Hungary, but that names the crisis the missions were **already answering**. No atlas file stages Lyon or Innocent IV, so nothing in the corpus can confirm the two letter dates: an open dependency on the pool.

## 6. The outbound journey (April 1245 - July 1246)

All **[A]** from Giovanni's itinerary via Rockhill unless marked.

- **Bohemia**, Wenceslaus I: route advice, letters, provisions, safe conduct. **Wroclaw**: Benedict the Pole joins as Slavic interpreter, corroborated by his own *De itinere*. **Leczyca**: Conrad of Mazovia supplies pelts. **Volodymyr-Volynskyi**: Vasilko briefs them, **[A]** on the fact, **[R]** on the site. *Two coordinate corrections:* the pool's Prague point is the Old Town, not a royal seat, so the file uses **50.09, 14.40** (Prague Castle) and notes the source names a king, not a building; Wroclaw moves to **51.1147, 17.0464**, the pool's point lying 550 m southwest.
- **Danilov.** "Before we reached Kiew, when in Danilov, I was ill to the point of death." The place **cannot be securely located**; 50.5, 25.0 is a rough Volhynian placement. The line fixes the illness **before** Kyiv, outbound. At **Kyiv**, *date correction:* the text reads "on the second day after the feast of the Purification of Our Lady (**February 4, 1246**), we started out from Kiew," so the pool's 3 February, an inclusive-count variant, is rejected. Then **Kaniv**, first Mongol-ruled town.
- *Contradiction flagged, not resolved: where Stephen of Bohemia dropped out.* The pool puts his collapse at **Batu's Volga camp**; en.wikipedia has him "broke down at **Kaniv near Kiev**", Treccani has him stopping before the Mongol outposts, and the Benedict article says a Mongol camp in Coman territory. The file follows the earlier siting and states the disagreement.
- **Corenza's camp**: the Alan prefect Micheas extracts presents through false protocol claims, **[A]** on the episode, **[R]** on the location. **Don crossing [R]**.
- **Batu's camp, lower Volga.** Arrival "Wednesday in Holy Week (4th April)" 1246, sharper than the pool's "April 1246." Two-fires purification confirmed verbatim; letters translated into Ruthenian, Saracen and Tartar script; the friars sent east. The pin approximates a **seasonal ordu**.
- **Yaik crossing [R]**; **Cangitae desert**, 16 April to near Ascension; **Bisermins**; **Omyl and the Black Kitayans**; **Naiman country**, great snow on 29 June 1246. *Date correction, Bisermins leg:* the pool gives 17 May to 24 June, but Carpini's "eight days before the feast of Saint John the Baptist (24th June)" glosses the feast, not the end of the leg. Corrected to **17 May to c. 16 June 1246**.
- **22 July 1246**, feast of Mary Magdalene, arrival at the **Sira Orda**. The **3,000 miles in 106 days** figure belongs to **this eastward ride**, not the whole embassy; the pool attached it to the wrong leg. *Pin inheritance:* the Guyuk-court stops use the canonical Karakorum coordinate **47.2103, 102.8478**, byte-identical from `kublai_khan.journey.json`, with the pool's caution preserved that the camp was near, not inside, the walled city.

## 7. At the imperial court (July - November 1246)

- **Enthronement of Guyuk**, son of Ogedei: hail forces postponement of the intended 15 August, and it falls on **24 August 1246**, the feast of Saint Bartholomew. *Place correction, and the pool had it wrong:* Carpini explicitly leaves the Sira Orda, riding "to another place some three or four leagues distant" where a second tent, the **Golden Orda**, is set up and "here it was that Cuyuc was to have been placed on the throne." The file separates the two pins.
- **Cosmas (Kuzma)**, a Rus goldsmith who made the imperial throne and cut the seal, provisions the starving friars; the ration for four is scarcely enough for one. **Yaroslav II of Vladimir-Suzdal** dies after eating from the hand of Guyuk's mother, "so that everyone believed that he had been poisoned": **[A]** on the report, framed by Giovanni as common belief and not fact, which the file preserves.
- **11 November 1246**, Guyuk's reply translated word by word and the Latin checked; it rejects the pope's position and demands personal submission. **[A]** on the scene; the sealed Persian original's survival in the **Vatican Apostolic Archives** is **[R]**, pool-only.
- **13 November 1246**, feast of Saint Brice: leave to depart, the sealed letter, fox-fur gowns and purple from the emperor's mother, partly stolen on the road by their own escort. *Quotation correction:* the pool's version opened "They gave to each of us," misattributing the gift; the source names the giver, "they sent us to the Emperor's mother, **who** gave to each of us."

## 8. The return (November 1246 - November 1247)

- Winter crossing sleeping in scraped hollows under snow **[A]**, quoted verbatim; **Batu's camp, 9 May 1247**, the Ascension.
- *Mauci date correction.* Carpini reaches Mauci "on the Saturday after the octave of Pentecost (**2nd June**)". The pool's 9 June is **Kyiv's date shifted onto the wrong stop**.
- *Kyiv contradiction, left open.* Rockhill gives "fifteen days before the feast of Saint John the Baptist (**9th June**)"; en.wikipedia gives **10 June**, a defensible inclusive-count variant. The file uses 9 June and names the disagreement. **Daniel of Galicia and Vasilko** then hold the friars eight days "against our will" and send envoys west about church relations.
- *Contradiction on a quotation's date.* The "rich presents" counsel attributed to Duke Vassilko is dated **1245, outward** in the pool's quotes lens, but Rockhill carries it in **Book III ch. II**, immediately after the return to Kyiv. The file places it on the return stop and states the disagreement rather than silently choosing.
- **Rhine crossed at Cologne**; **Lyon, 18 November 1247**, Guyuk's letter and the report delivered to Innocent IV.

## 9. Last years (1248-1252)

- **23 January 1248**, a papal document names him **penitentiary** at the curia, an office held some three months. **[R]**
- **Salimbene de Adam** meets him in France and records that Giovanni carried his book, let it be read, and explained the passages that prompted disbelief. **[A]** via the pool's citation of Salimbene's *Cronica*: the witness to **author-supervised circulation**, which is why the two recensions are treated as authorial. The fuller one ends with a witness list warning copyists off alteration. **[R]**
- **1248, embassy to Louis IX. [A]**, Treccani attesting "un'ambasceria presso il re di Francia Luigi IX nel 1248," upgraded from the pool's [R]. **The instructions are not known** and every source says so. *Location correction:* nothing places the meeting in Paris, and Louis left for Aigues-Mortes that summer, so the file's Paris coordinate is declared an **editorial placement**.
- **1248, archbishop of Antivari** (modern Bar, Montenegro), a see later styled with the primacy of Serbia. *Contradiction closed, with reasons:* Treccani and it.wikipedia give **1248**, en.wikipedia's "1247 to 1252" being the outlier. *Coordinate correction:* the pool's 42.093, 19.103 is **modern coastal Bar**; the medieval seat with the cathedral of St George is **Stari Bar**, 3 km inland, at **42.0928, 19.1381**. Both pins are moved.
- Jurisdictional conflict with the **bishop of Ragusa**; Giovanni captured and briefly imprisoned, then released, **with no evidence of criminal conviction**, the dispute running to **July 1252** after the bishop's death that May. **[R]**, pool-only.
- **29 July 1252**, last surviving document naming him **[A]** (Treccani). Death in his diocese, **1 August 1252** per the Franciscan Martyrology, Treccani having "probabilmente ai primi di agosto": **[A]** on the tradition, **[R]** on the exact day, which no document fixes.

## 10. Afterlife, and what is not there

**There is no grave.** No burial record, no tomb, no marker, no relic tradition, no local cult at Bar. **[A]** as an attested absence, checked across Treccani, Wikipedia, L'Osservatore Romano and the Magione program; the 2020 *Osservatore Romano* retrospective says only that he died "probabilmente nella stessa Antivari." Treat "his tomb" as a hole in the record, not a lost monument.

**Print history**, carried as `suggested_refs` on his own stops and never staged as scenes: Hakluyt, *The Principal Navigations* (1598), from the incomplete BL Royal MS 13 A XIV **[A]**; Beazley, 1903 **[A]**; van den Wyngaert, *Sinica Franciscana* I (1929) **[R]** on contents; Dawson, *The Mongol Mission* (1955) **[A]**; Menesto and others, *Storia dei Mongoli* (1989) **[R]**.

**Waldseemuller's *Carta marina* (1516)** and its dog-headed figures in Tartaria: **downgraded to [R]**, the pool's citation being a danakrehnblog post that **404s** on a blog whose index carries no such title. Van Duzer's work on the map's legends is real and the point may hold; as cited it cannot stand as [A].

**Vinland Map and the *Hystoria Tartarorum* (Beinecke MS 350A): two corrections. [R]** The anatase findings were presented by Yale's Richard Hark at a **2018** symposium, the announcement following in **2021**. And the pool's clean split, map fake and Tartar text unimpugned, is **too strong**: per en.wikipedia, anatase was found on **the first page of the Tartar Relation itself**, over erased iron-gall ink. The text is still medieval; its opening page is not.

**C. de Bridia's *Hystoria Tartarorum*, colophon 30 July 1247 [A]**, draws on the mission's information but is **not** Giovanni's second work. The Magione monument and the June 2026 conference **[R]** are somebody else's travel. All three are `suggested_refs`, not scenes.

## Interlocks

**Named in a campa.**

- **`genghis_khan`**, in the final stop. A real one-directional relation: Giovanni **wrote substantively about Chinggis Khan**, whose rise and campaigns fill a large part of the *Historia Mongalorum*, and rode through their wreckage among the Bisermins. Not shared geography, not an encounter.
- **`ctesias`** and **`gaius_julius_solinus`**, in one clause of the Cosmas stop, standing for the shelf the dog-head passage lands on. **The relation is [R], and flagged [R] in that stop's `date_confidence`**: Giovanni cites no authority for the motif. The pool asserts he inherited rather than invented it; the file states that as a placement, not an attested citation. If the operator judges it too thin, deleting the clause costs nothing else.

**Deliberately not named**, the relation being topical or running only toward Giovanni:

- `odoric_of_pordenone`, `ibn_battuta`: independent cynocephalus reports on the Andaman littoral, later and from a different tradition. Comparanda.
- `ratramnus_of_corbie`, `rimbert`, `adam_of_bremen`, `san_cristobal`, `jacobus_de_voragine`, `saint_mercurius`, `strabo`, `megasthenes`, `claudius_aelianus_aelian`, `herodotus`, `paul_the_deacon`: the corpus's cynocephalus backbone. Giovanni engages none by name and none engages him. Rimbert and Adam of Bremen come closest, missionary-frontier reports on the same eastern informant chain, but same motif on the same frontier is not a relation between persons.
- `marco_polo`, `kublai_khan`: **forward of Giovanni's death in 1252**, excluded on principle; only the **Karakorum coordinate** is taken, and a pin is not a name. The Mecca, Jerusalem and Venice pins offered for inheritance went unused.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Giovanni is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed and none were written. Two relations not rooted in his own life were caught and are recorded rather than deleted. Both run **forward**, so neither is licensable under the block rule and neither is staged as a stop: (1) the cartographic afterlife of his dog-head passage, Waldseemuller's *Carta marina* of 1516 and the scholarship built on it, which survives as a hedged `suggested_ref` on his own Guyuk-court stop; (2) the Magione monument and the June 2026 conference, other people's travel to his birthplace, likewise a `suggested_ref` on his own birth stop.

Neither needs an exception to stand as written. Flagged so the decision is visible, not silent.

**Not reached, and why**

- **Jordan of Giano, *Chronica***, the primary source for 1221-1224. Not fetched; it reaches this file through the pool's summary alone, so all 1221-1224 claims are **[R]** except the two Treccani confirms.
- **Papal registers** for the two 1245 letters, the 1234 Agnes letter, and the 1248 and 1252 documents. Pool-only: the letter **dates** are therefore [R], the departure they precede [A].
- **Van den Wyngaert, *Sinica Franciscana* I (1929)**, not opened; its standing rests on consistent citation elsewhere.
- **Antivari and Ragusa church archives** (the imprisonment) and the **Vatican Apostolic Archives** (Guyuk's letter): both pool-only.
- **beinecke.library.yale.edu/article/map-forgeries** and the cited **danakrehnblog** Carta marina post: both **404**. Yale's announcement is secondhand here; the Van Duzer attribution is real, the citation is not, so both Waldseemuller claims are downgraded.

**Known gaps, stated as gaps**

No birth record and no birth year; nothing whatever before 1221, including any contact with Francis in either direction; no reason recorded for the 1224 reassignment; nothing from the two Spanish years and no Spanish residence named anywhere; no secure document at all for 1239-1245; no instructions recorded for the 1248 mission to Louis IX; and no burial place, tomb or cult, which is the largest and cleanest absence in the file.
