# Matsuo Bashō (1644-1694): research report

*Slug: `matsuo_basho`. Compiled from the verified research pool. Journey file: `matsuo_basho.journey.json`, 8 segments, 43 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or contested attribution. Where verification overturned a gathering-pass claim, the superseded value is kept visible.

---

## 1. Iga, 1644-1666

**[A: Wikipedia, "Matsuo Bashō"; Ueda Makoto, *Matsuo Bashō*; Keene, *The Narrow Road to Oku*, introduction]** Matsuo Kinsaku is born in 1644 near Ueno, Iga Province (present-day Iga, Mie), into a family of samurai-adjacent status, probably *musokunin*: landowning peasants holding samurai privileges without samurai income. The birth is not seriously disputed. Month and day are unrecorded; the file defaults to `1644-01-01` and says so in `date_confidence`.

**[R: popular tradition, flagged as such by Wikipedia itself]** That the Matsuo family had ties to Iga's ninjutsu lineages, and that the young Bashō received related training, has no documentary support. Carried in the campa as a thing biographers wonder about, never as fact.

**[A]** In his late teens he enters service to Tōdō Yoshitada, a young kinsman of the ruling house. **Contradiction not resolved:** whether he served as page or as kitchen servant. Both readings circulate; the campa says "as page or as kitchen servant; the accounts differ." He adopts the pen name Sōbō; his first extant poem is published in 1662 **[A]**; in 1665 he takes part in a *hyakuin* with Yoshitada and their circle **[A]**. Yoshitada dies suddenly in 1666 **[A]**, the household dissolves, and Bashō leaves home; Ueda and Keene report that he considered taking vows.

**[A as quotation, R as biography]** Two lines survive in which Bashō recalls this period: "at one time I coveted an official post with a tenure of land," and "there was a time when I was fascinated with the ways of homosexual love." Wikipedia quotes both verbatim, notes the passage is vague and gives *no indication whether the obsessions described are real or fictional*, and names neither the underlying work nor the translator. The campa states both lines and lets the ambiguity stand. A third line from the passage, "the alternatives battled in my mind and made my life restless," is the stop's quote.

**Gap:** the years 1666-1672 are thin in every reachable source. Poems appear in anthologies from 1669 **[A]**; where he lived and how he kept himself between Yoshitada's death and the Edo move is not established here.

## 2. Edo, 1672-1683: trade, hut, fire

**[A]** In 1672 he publishes *Kai Ōi* (The Seashell Game) and moves to Edo in spring; Keene and Ueda place him near Nihonbashi, working a time as a minor clerk on the waterworks. **[A]** 1674: inducted into the inner circle of the haikai profession, receiving secret teachings from Kitamura Kigin. 1675: adopts the pen name Tōsei around the time Nishiyama Sōin visits Edo. 1680: twenty disciples publish *The Best Poems of Tōsei's Twenty Disciples*; he moves across the Sumida to a hut in Fukagawa, and a disciple gives him the *bashō* (Japanese banana) plant from which he takes the *haigō*.

**Coordinate correction, and an internal contradiction in the pool.** The gathering pass gave the Fukagawa hut two different coordinates in two lenses: `35.6717, 139.8017` (geography) and `35.6748, 139.8007` (afterlife). Both are wrong, and their disagreement about one building is itself a defect. ja.wikipedia's 常盤 (Kōtō ward), which explicitly records 松尾芭蕉がこの地に庵を結んだ, gives **35.685239, 139.795831**; the file uses **35.6852, 139.7958** for all three Bashō-an stops. The superseded values sit 1.2 to 1.6 km southeast, on the wrong side of the Bashō-an historic site and memorial museum.

**[R]** Zen study under the priest Butchō in the early 1680s is the standard secondary account and is *not* confirmed by Wikipedia, which says only that Bashō practiced Zen meditation, which "seems not to have calmed his mind," and names no teacher. The identification is entrenched nonetheless, and Butchō is independently attested as chief priest of Unganji, which Bashō visits in 1689 **[A]**. Handled as tradition in the campa.

**[A]** 1682, winter: the hut burns in a great fire, one of the Tenna-era Edo conflagrations. 1683: his mother dies; he travels to Iga; disciples build a second hut in Edo that winter. **Gap:** where exactly he sheltered between the fire and the second hut is not established from the reachable sources; the campa says only that he was out of the city.

**Displacement flagged.** The white-hair verse (a lock of his dead mother's hair melting in his palm like autumn frost) belongs to the **1684** Nozarashi return to Ueno, tagged **[R]**, not to the 1683 mourning. The 1683 campa says so rather than letting the more affecting scene migrate to the earlier date.

## 3. The first journeys, 1684-1688

**[A]** 1684: Bashō leaves Edo on the first of four major travel journeys, toward Mount Fuji, Ueno, and Kyoto; published on his return as *Nozarashi Kikō* (Records of a Weather-Exposed Skeleton), 1685.

**Distance figure removed.** The gathering pass attached "roughly 600 ri (about 2,400 km)" to the **1684** journey. That figure belongs to **1689**: ja.wikipedia (奥の細道) gives 約600里（2400キロメートル） for Oku no Hosomichi, and the same artifact repeats 2,400 km for 1689 elsewhere. Two journeys cannot both be the 2,400 km one, and the 1684 route is materially shorter. No distance is stated for 1684 in the file; the figure appears only at Senju.

**Coordinate correction, Fuji river.** Pool gave `35.14, 138.4055`. ja.wikipedia (富士川) places the river mouth at the Fuji City / Shimizu ward boundary on Suruga Bay, ≈138.62°E; the Tōkaidō crossing is ≈**35.14, 138.63**. The pool longitude put the stop in the Abe river uplands, a different river system. **[A]** The abandoned child there is Bashō's own report in *Nozarashi Kikō*; no independent witness exists, and scholars debate how literally to read a scene so composed for effect.

**Two errors corrected at Ise, 1684.** The pool claimed Bashō visited "during the twenty-year rebuilding" and was barred from the inner precinct for shabby dress. Both fail. The *shikinen sengū* runs on a fixed twenty-year cycle and the relevant Edo-period rebuildings are **1669** and **1689**, so 1684 falls between cycles; the 1689 sengū is precisely what the closing lines of *Oku no Hosomichi* send him to Ise to witness. And *Nozarashi Kikō* gives the reason he could not approach the inner shrine as his **Buddhist monk's habit**: clergy were barred from Ise under the shrine's own exclusion rules. Coordinates 34.455, 136.7256 are correct for the Naikū and are used for 1684. **[R]** The 1684 visit to his father's grave at Ueno is tagged [R] in the pool; no separate stop was written, and the fact is folded into the 1683 campa with its status explicit. **[A]** Yoshino, 1684-1685: the search for traces of Saigyō's hermitage. **[A]** 1686: the frog haiku, and the Edo contest at which the hokku places first.

**Correction at Kashima, 1687.** The pool wrote that Bashō travelled "with his Zen teacher Butchō." This inverts the trip: Butchō was the **destination**, having retired to Konponji near Kashima, and the party was Bashō, Sora, and the monk Sōha. Coordinates 35.9675, 140.6317 are within 120 m of Kashima Jingū and are kept; the rain and the journal's candour about it are confirmed **[A]**.

**Two corrections at the 1688 journals.** *Kashima Kikō*, not *Sarashina Kikō*, is conventionally the shortest of the travel accounts. And the Zenkōji visit does not close *Sarashina Kikō*, as the pool claimed: the journal ends after that section with the Mount Asama verse 吹き飛ばす石は浅間の野分かな. No Zenkōji stop was written; the correct ending is carried in the Obasute campa, and Zenkōji at 36.6608, 138.1875 sits in `suggested_refs`.

**Unresolved coordinate, honestly flagged.** The pool's Obasute coordinate `36.5378, 138.0844` is suspect; the moon-viewing ground (Chōraku-ji, Chikuma City) sits nearer **36.51, 138.12**, some 5 km southeast. No precise figure could be re-fetched: ja.wikipedia 姨捨山 returned only "≈36.3, 138.1". The file uses 36.51, 138.12 and the `date_confidence` states the coordinate is unconfirmed. This is the weakest pin in the file.

## 4. Oku no Hosomichi, 1689

**[A: Wikipedia, "Matsuo Bashō" and "Oku no Hosomichi"]** Departure from Edo with Kawai Sora on the 27th day of the 3rd month, Genroku 2 = **16 May 1689**. Journey length roughly 156 days, approximately 2,400 km, largely on foot, ending at Ōgaki.

Confirmed and left alone: Senju, Sōka, Nikkō, Sendai, Taga, Matsushima, Hiraizumi, Naruko, Ryūshakuji, Ōbanazawa, Haguro, Kisakata, Kanazawa, Yamanaka, Ōgaki. **[A: Bashō, *Oku no Hosomichi*]** Text-level details carried in the campa without a separate entry above: Kaemon's drawings of the northern sights, given at Sendai alongside the iris-corded sandals; the Kasajima hokku, which asks where Kasajima is and answers with the mud of the fifth month; the Taga stele inscription, which Bashō reproduces in his own prose; and the hokku on fleas, lice, and the horse at his pillow, written at the barrier keeper's house below Shitomae. All four are in the received text of the journal itself, not in secondary reconstruction.

### Contradictions inside the 1689 record, flagged not resolved

**Sōka vs Kasukabe.** *Oku no Hosomichi* puts the first night's lodging at Sōka; Sora's diary puts it at **Kasukabe**, among the best-known divergences between the finished prose and the travel record. The pool asserted Sōka flatly. The campa states both, and the `date_confidence` says the discrepancy is not resolved.

**Kurobane duration.** ja.wikipedia's itinerary puts him there from 4月4日 to about 4月16日, confirming the pool's "roughly two weeks," but Sora's dates and Bashō's prose still do not align and the file says so.

**Matsushima and the verse he did not write.** *Oku no Hosomichi* records no hokku by Bashō there. The famous "Matsushima ya, aa Matsushima ya, Matsushima ya" is credited in its earliest known printing, the **Matsushima Zushi (1820)**, to a kyōka poet named **Tawara-bō**; a separate strand of the pool gives it to **Kawai Sora**. Both are stated in the campa; neither is adopted, and no `quote` field carries the line.

**"Araumi ya" at Izumozaki: downgraded from [A] to [R].** Sora's diary records rain and cloud on the Izumozaki nights (7月4日), and substantial scholarship holds the Sado / Milky Way verse was composed or finalised later, perhaps at Naoetsu. The attribution is traditional, not established. The `date_confidence` carries the hedge and the campa states it in prose.

**Ōbanazawa: a fabricated poetic gloss removed.** The pool described a verse "likening the merchant's own unpretentious warmth to a silkworm still spinning quietly in its cocoon." No such verse exists. The silkworm hokku there is 蚕飼する人は古代のすがた哉, on the archaic look of the sericulture households; the verse addressed to Seifū's hospitality is 涼しさを我宿にしてねまる也. Host, safflower trade, and the comfortable interlude are correct; the campa now describes both verses accurately.

**Tsuruga: the sand-hauler was not a shrine priest.** The pool made him "a past head priest" of Kehi Shrine. He was the **Yugyō Shōnin**, itinerant patriarch of the Ji sect of Pure Land Buddhism, traditionally the second Yugyō, Ta'a. The anecdote's point is a Buddhist ascetic labouring for a Shinto site; calling him a shrine priest erases it. Coordinate moved from 35.6503, 136.0669 to **35.6553, 136.0708** (Kehi Jingū).

**Ōgaki dates corrected.** The pool gave "6th day, 8th month." ja.wikipedia (奥の細道) gives **arrival 8月21日, departure for Ise 9月6日**; the pool appears to have taken the departure day and written the wrong month. The file uses arrival 21st day, 8th month (`1689-10-04`) and departure 6th day, 9th month (`1689-10-18`).

**Ise 1689: back-reference removed.** The pool wrote that Bashō was "drawn again by the shrine's periodic rebuilding rite he had witnessed unfinished five years earlier." Invented, since 1684 was not a sengū year (see §3). 1689 *was*, and *Oku no Hosomichi* does end with him leaving Ōgaki 伊勢の遷宮おがまんと. The campa keeps the 1689 sengū and the 1684 rebuff without connecting them causally. Two Ise coordinates are used deliberately: **Naikū 34.455, 136.7256** for 1684, **Gekū 34.4874, 136.7043** for 1689, matching the pool's own two values rather than collapsing them.

## 5. Last years, death, afterlife

**[A]** 1691: return to Edo after roughly five years of revising the prose and verse; settles into a third Bashō hut with his nephew Tōin and his companion Jutei. **[A]** Eighth month of 1693: shuts his gate for about a month, a withdrawal associated with the late principle of *karumi*, lightness. A letter of these years, whose recipient, date, and translator are not given on the fetched page, says "disturbed by others, I have no peace of mind"; neither report nor file says more about it than that. **[A]** Summer 1694: leaves Edo for the last time toward Ueno and Kyoto; dies in Osaka of a stomach illness, surrounded by disciples, the deathbed verse on the dream wandering a withered field accepted by them as his farewell poem.

**The worst error in the pool, corrected.** The geography lens wrote "**12 October 1694 (28th day, 10th month, Genroku 7)**," inverting the calendars twice: the lunar day (12) and the Gregorian day (28) were swapped, and the lunar 10th month was read as October. ja.wikipedia (松尾芭蕉) gives 元禄7年10月12日 = **28 November 1694**, and two other lenses in the same pool state 1694-11-28 correctly, so the artifact contradicted itself. Correct form: **28 November 1694 (12th day, 10th month, Genroku 7)**.

**Death and burial coordinates: two sites, four pool values, two chosen.** For the death, geography gave 34.669, 135.501 (Namba, ~1.3 km south) and afterlife 34.6937, 135.5023 (Kita, ~1.5 km north); ja.wikipedia names the site as 南久太郎町 6-chōme, present-day Chūō-ku, ≈ **34.680, 135.503**. For the grave, geography gave 35.0084, 135.8942 (~1.4 km NE) and afterlife 35.0028, 135.8672 (~1.2 km W); ja.wikipedia (義仲寺) gives **35.00292, 135.88019**. Everything else at Gichū-ji confirms **[A]**: he asked that his remains go to the Kiso mound and was buried beside Minamoto no Yoshinaka in the 10th month of 1694; Tomoe Gozen's hermitage beside the tomb is the recorded origin story (formal establishment came much later); the disciple's verse is Shimazaki Yūgen's 木曽殿と背中合せの寒さかな.

**Correction to the Ten Disciples.** The pool listed **Hattori Dohō** among the Bashō Jūtetsu. He is not one of them: ja.wikipedia (蕉門十哲) gives Takarai Kikaku, Hattori **Ransetsu**, Mukai Kyorai, Naitō Jōsō, Morikawa Kyoriku, Sugiyama Sanpū, Kagami Shikō, Tachibana Hokushi, Shida Yaba, Ochi Etsujin. The error looks like a collision between the two Hattoris. Dohō was a genuine Iga disciple and author of the *Sanzōshi*, so he may be named as a disciple who recorded the teachings, never as one of the Ten.

**Correction to the translation sequence.** The pool's dates are reprints, not first editions. Keene's *The Narrow Road to Oku* is **1996** (Kodansha), not 1997; Hamill's *Narrow Road to the Interior* is **1998** (Shambhala), not 2000. Yuasa 1966 (Penguin) and Barnhill 2005 (SUNY) are correct.

**[A]** 1702: *Oku no Hosomichi* first printed, posthumously, an immediate success. **[A]** 1793: deified by Shinto authorities. **[A]** 1942: the Haiseiden built in Iga on the 300th anniversary of his birth, its circular roof named the traveler's umbrella. **Gap:** the pool did not confirm a complete list of the *kuhi* verse stones along the route; the file mentions them without enumerating.

## 6. Atlas interlocks

Named in campa where the paths genuinely cross:

- **`roland_barthes`** and **`ian_fleming`** at the Edo arrival stop. Barthes taught at Ichigaya from May 1966 and made the haiku an object of *Empire of Signs*; Fleming toured Tokyo in 1959 and 1962 for *You Only Live Twice*. Same city, three centuries apart, both reading its surfaces.
- **`keyserling`** at Ise, 1684. His *Travel Diary of a Philosopher* takes him through Kyoto, Nara, and Ise in June 1912, concluding Japan "has no metaphysics because it needs none." A genre sibling to Bashō's journal, on the same ground.
- **`ibn_battuta`** at the 1684 departure. Genre precedent rather than geography: the *Rihla* opens with a vow never to travel the same road twice, where *Nozarashi Kikō* opens with a skeleton.

**Coordinate-inheritance convention observed.** `roland_barthes.journey.json` records that the Institut franco-japonais (35.6984, 139.7398) and the Maison franco-japonaise (35.6441, 139.7157) "must not be conflated." Following the same discipline, generic **Edo/Tokyo** stops here use the corpus's canonical Tokyo pin **35.6762, 139.6503** byte-identically (1672 arrival, 1684 departure, 1693 closed gate), while Fukagawa (35.6852, 139.7958) and Senju (35.7497, 139.8017) get their own pins as genuinely separate places. No fourth Tokyo value is introduced.

**Considered and excluded.** `kublai_khan` (1266 letter at Tōdai-ji, Nara): same national geography four centuries earlier, no route overlap, tagged [R]. `claude_levi_strauss` (Tokyo, late 1970s) is marked reconstruction in its own file. `mao_zedong`, `madame_chiang_kai_shek`, and `mahatma_gandhi` return Japan grep-hits concerning 20th-century politics and war.

**Negative results.** No journey.json currently pins Nikkō, Matsushima, Hiraizumi, or Sado; these four are established fresh here. `census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md` returns no Bashō or Japan-route entry. The canonical Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), Temple Mount, Paris, and Buenos Aires pins do not intersect this life and are not used.
