# Rollo of Normandy: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `rollo.journey.json`. The journey file's register is national mythology, the canon is true. This report is the opposite register: it is where the canon is taken apart.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named and reachable · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or later narrative · contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated.

## The evidentiary scale, first

**There is exactly one securely contemporary document naming Rollo. [A]** A royal diploma of Charles III "the Simple", issued at the palace of Compiègne on 14 March 918, surviving in the original as Archives nationales K 16, no. 9. It gives the abbey of La Croix-Saint-Ouen, on the Eure in the pagus of Madrie, to Saint-Germain-des-Prés for the monks' table, *praeter partem ipsius abbatiȩ quam annuimus Normannis Sequanensibus, videlicet Rolloni suisque comitibus, pro tutela regni*. Latin verified verbatim against the CARO transcription.

It proves: Rollo existed; he led a group recognized and identified with the Seine by 918; Charles III had already conceded them land; the concession had a stated defensive function. It does not prove a treaty in 911, the place Saint-Clair-sur-Epte, any boundary, marriage, baptism, or ducal title. **The diploma records a prior grant without dating it.** That undated grant is the hinge of the whole legend, and 911 rests on Dudo alone. **[A]**

Second tier: **Flodoard of Reims [A]**, canon of Reims, *Annals* 919-966, naming Rollo s.a. 925, 927, 928. Near-contemporary, with no reason to flatter Rouen.

Third tier, and the source of most of what anyone knows: **Dudo of Saint-Quentin [A** as to what he says, **R** as to whether it happened**]**, *Historia Normannorum*, c. 996-1015, three generations later, at and for Richard II's ducal court. Legitimating court literature; Helmerichs notes Dudo habitually transfers other Viking leaders' exploits onto Rollo. Fourth tier: **thirteenth-century Icelandic [R]**, Snorri's *Heimskringla* and *Orkneyinga saga*, identifying the founder of Normandy with Gongu-Hrolfr, son of Rognvaldr Eysteinsson, earl of Møre.

## Origins: two nations, two incompatible fathers

Nothing before 911 can be independently verified **[R**, ORB**]**: Scandinavian birth, exile, English raids, the siege of Paris, and the wanderings on Seine, Loire and in Burgundy all rest on Dudo.

- **Danish tradition [R].** Dudo: son of a great landholder in "Dacia", brother Gurim; the father dies, a king kills Gurim, Rollo goes into exile. Dudo reaches Denmark through a false etymology joining *Daci* to *Dani*; Dacia properly names the lower Danube. The sleight is itself evidence of how thin the Danish claim is.
- **Norwegian tradition [R].** Hrolfr, son of Rognvaldr of Møre and Hildr daughter of Hrolfr Nefja; outlawed by Harald Fairhair for a cattle raid in Viken against the ban on plundering inside Norway; his mother pleads in verse and fails; he sails west through the Hebrides to Valland. The epithet *Gangr*, the Walker, is explained by his being too large for any horse: explanatory folklore, not a description.

**Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** These are not variants of one story. A dispossessed Danish heir and an outlawed Norwegian earl's son are different men with different fathers, and each tradition belongs to a nation with a stake. The file carries both as separate stops in one segment. **Birth date [R]:** Wikipedia gives "c. 835/870", two traditions rather than a range, and the epitaph implies the 850s; the pool's "c. 835/860-870" misreports the article.

**Three geographic corrections applied to the pool.** (1) *Giske*: the cited "Wikipedia, 'Giske'" is a disambiguation page and the municipality article has no Rollo material; the link is the Fjord Norway page, now cited, and coordinates move from 62.4794, 6.0339 (offshore) to 62.5625, 6.1019. (2) *Harald's court*: the pool's 59.9, 6.05 is uninhabited highland; *Heimskringla* names no court, only a Thing, so the stop moves to Avaldsnes on Karmøy (59.3497, 5.2733), Harald's attested seat. (3) *Hebrides*: the waypoint is *Heimskringla* ch. 24, not the *Orkneyinga saga*, whose Gutenberg text has no such passage.

## Before Francia: Dudo and nothing else

All **[R]**, all Dudo, ORB edition, trans. Lifshitz.

- **King Alstem.** Rollo raids among the Angles, allies with a King "Alstem" (equated variously with Guthrum or Aethelstan of East Anglia, neither securely), restores his realm, receives the dream of the mountain, the spring, and the birds nesting together. *Correction:* the pool has Alstem interpreting the dream. He does not. The interpreter is an unnamed Christian captive whom Rollo then frees with gifts.
- **Walcheren.** Storm on the crossing, a fight with Frisians off the island.
- **Siege of Paris, 885-886 [A for the siege / R for Rollo].** Historical, at the two fortified bridgeheads of the Île de la Cité. Rollo's presence "is not secured by any contemporary source naming him" (ORB). The journey file narrates the siege in the canon's voice and puts the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **Bayeux and Poppa.** The town taken and burned, Poppa daughter of a count Berengar seized and joined to him; she bears William. Dudo's wording is conquest and captivity and establishes no consent; "marriage *more danico*" is a modern label, not his term. Parentage contested: Settipani makes her a daughter of Guy de Senlis, Keats-Rohan of Berengar II of Neustria. Dudo dates the capture 885 or 889.
- **Loire, dropped from the journey file.** Dudo folds Loire Viking activity into Rollo's career; Ragenold of the Loire is a separate leader who should not be merged with him. A Loire stop would stage a scene where he probably never was. **Évreux dropped** likewise: composite, unverifiable, adding nothing the Bayeux stop does not carry.

## 911: the battle, the bargain, the missing parchment

- **Chartres, 20 July 911 [A].** FranceArchives gives the date and names the coalition as **Robert, marquis of Neustria; Ebles Manzer, count of Poitiers; Richard, duke of Burgundy**. *Corrections:* the pool named Bishop Gantelmus, who is not in that source, and omitted Ebles Manzer, who is.
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** FranceArchives says Rollo led the defeated Scandinavians and suffered "une grave défaite". ORB says flatly that no contemporary evidence ties Rollo to Chartres and that Dudo credits him with it by his usual pattern. Both sit in `date_confidence` on the Chartres stop.
- **The "Treaty" of Saint-Clair-sur-Epte [R].** No text survives; the name, place, date and clauses are reconstructed from Dudo. FranceArchives places a negotiated settlement in the second half of 911, after Chartres. The only contemporary anchor is the 918 diploma, issued at a different place entirely.
- **The terms [R].** Land from the Epte to the sea; Christianity; service; Gisla in marriage; hands in the king's hands; the grant hereditary.
- **Gisla [R], with a standing chronological objection.** Charles III first married (Frederuna) in May 907, so a legitimate daughter could be at most about four in 911. Historians treat her as illegitimate, as a child betrothal, or as Dudo's invention.
- **The foot [R].** Told he must kneel and kiss the king's foot, Rollo refuses; a deputed follower lifts the foot to his mouth without kneeling and topples Charles backward. Alice Taylor argues Dudo exaggerated an ordinary homage. *Correction:* the pool hedged that it "could not trace the scene to Dudo's actual text". It is Dudo's, and all three spoken lines are verified verbatim.
- **"Duke" [R].** Flodoard calls him *princeps*, a flexible word for leader. "First Duke of Normandy" is retrospective, not an office attested in his lifetime.

## Christianization, and how incomplete it was

- **Baptism, 912 [R].** Dudo: Franco of Rouen baptizes, Robert of Neustria stands godfather and gives his name, Rollo becomes Robert; leaders and army baptized over seven days from Rouen and Bayeux, with gifts to churches. No contemporary witness confirms date, officiant, or sponsor.
- **The relapse, 914 [A].** Archbishop Hervé of Reims writes to Pope John X about baptized Northmen of the Seine who have gone back: killing Christians and priests, sacrificing to idols, eating sacrificial food. John X counsels against severity lest they return further to their old errors. *Correction, and it is the whole point:* the pool dated this vaguely "before 928". Hervé was archbishop 900-922 and John X pope from March 914, so the consultation belongs to **914**, two years after the traditional baptism. The vague dating destroyed the force of the evidence.
- **Scope [A].** The evidence concerns the community. Rollo is not named personally in it.
- **Placement.** The letter was written at Reims, but Rollo never went there and Hervé is not the subject. Per the standing rule the stop sits in the Seine valley, where the converts were, citing Hervé as source.

## The law: an exemplum, not a code

- **Dudo's peace [R].** Theft banned absolutely, tools left in the furrow overnight; a stolen ploughshare investigated by ordeal by fire at Longpaon (Lifshitz's note: "Today, Darnétal"); the wife who hid the tools beaten into confession, husband and wife both hanged.
- **The gold ring in the forest of Roumare [R].** Later folklore resembling the Scandinavian Peace of Frodi. Not in Dudo, whose core is the ordeal.
- **"Laws of Rollo" [R].** Gilduin Davy, *English Historical Review*: the memory of a Rollonian founding constitution was substantially constructed and rewritten by early-modern historians and lawyers, not transmitted from the tenth century. Dudo quotes no law text anywhere; his only concrete legislation is the theft ban and the prohibition on carrying plough tools home.

## The documented decades, 918 to 928

- **918 [A].** The Compiègne diploma, above.
- **924 [A].** With Raoul's consent, **Maine and the Bessin** conceded in a sworn peace (Flodoard). Whether the Bessin passed to Rollo personally or to a wider coalition is disputed. Maine did not last; the grant may have conveyed tribute rights rather than possession.
- **925 [A].** The Northmen of Rouen break the peace and devastate Beauvais, **Arras**, Amiens and toward Noyon. *Corrections:* the pool omitted Arras and understated the Frankish response, which was not only counter-raiding: per ORB the Franks besieged the Northmen's stronghold of **Eu and destroyed it**.
- **925 [A].** "Their *princeps* Rollo" sends 1,000 Northmen from Rouen to reinforce Eu. This is nearly the only place in the contemporary record where Rollo is shown giving an order.
- **926 [R].** A public levy in Francia and Burgundy paid to the Northmen under a renewed peace on mutual oaths. The entry does not name Rollo.
- **927 [A].** At Eu, Charles III and Herbert II of Vermandois meet the Northmen; William commends himself to Charles and affirms friendship with Herbert. *Correction, material:* Charles was **not free**. Herbert had taken him in summer 923 at Château-Thierry and held him at Péronne, where he died in captivity on 7 October 929. Herbert produced his royal prisoner at Eu, and William commended himself to a king in another magnate's custody, which is why Herbert's friendship had to be sworn in the same act. Without that frame the scene reads backwards.
- **928 [A].** Rollo holds Herbert's son Odo hostage, releasing him only when Herbert with bishops and counts commits to King Charles. **Last securely dated appearance alive.**

## Death: two endings, both late, incompatible

- **Dudo [R].** Worn by age and war, unable to ride, Rollo transfers all his land to William "Poppa's son", takes the followers' oaths, lives about one further year, and "migrated full of days to Christ".
- **Adémar of Chabannes [R].** Eleventh century, a century after: the aging Christian goes mad near death and has Christian captives beheaded as sacrifices to pagan gods while also giving gold to churches. fr.wikipedia dates the alleged sacrifices to 932-933 and calls the anecdote "douteuse". Modern scholarship does not dismiss it; it reads as a sign of real religious ambivalence among the Seine Northmen.
- **Contradiction, flagged, not resolved.** Both endings are separate stops, in that order, neither adjudicated.
- **Dropped for want of a source.** The **100 captives** of later retellings: the De Gruyter/Brill chapter cited (`10.1515/9781782042976-004`) returns HTTP 403/405 on every route. The number is not in the journey file.
- **Date of death [R].** Bracketed 928 (Flodoard's last mention) to 933 (Raoul's grant to William); usual figure 932/933. *Correction:* the 928-933 bracket is **ORB's** reconstruction, not Wikipedia's; the English article gives 933 outright, following the epitaph.
- **The 933 grant [A].** Raoul granted William "lordship over much of the lands of the Bretons including Avranches, the Cotentin Peninsula **and the Channel Islands**", outside effective Carolingian power. The Channel Islands belong in the list; the pool omitted them.

## Succession and family

- **William Longsword [A].** Rollo's son per Flodoard (927), "Poppa's son" per Dudo. Ambushed and killed **17 December 942** at **Picquigny**, on an island in the Somme, by followers of **Arnulf I of Flanders**. *Correction:* the pool hedged this as a "traditional site"; Wikipedia states it flatly with full date and principal. The murder is **not** a stop in Rollo's file: he was a decade dead and Picquigny is William's own ground.
- **The Planctus [A], re-sourced.** Composed **943**, describing William as born overseas of a pagan father and a Christian mother, so before Rollo's Christianization was secure. *Correction:* the pool sourced this to Flodoard s.a. 927, which says nothing of the kind; the source is van Houts, *Anglo-Norman Studies* 36 (2014). The "943" dates the poem, not the death.
- **Gerloc / Adela [R].** Daughter of Rollo, married around 936 to William of Poitou. *Corrections:* he was then **count of Poitou only** (duke of Aquitaine 962-963), so that title is anachronistic for 936; and Adémar makes her husband William's **father Ebles Manzer**. Her mother is not named by Dudo. She appears inside a Rouen campa; her marriage is her own travel and gets no stop here.
- **The line [R].** Rollo, William Longsword, Richard I, Richard II, Robert the Magnificent, William the Conqueror: Rollo is William's great-great-great-grandfather.

## Afterlife: tomb, bones, bronze

- **Burial [R].** Rouen Cathedral, church of his baptism; tradition makes him the first layman buried there. **1063 [A]:** Maurille transfers the remains near the south door of the Romanesque cathedral then standing.
- **The epitaph [A], upgraded from the pool's [R]**, which could not pull the Latin. fr.wikipedia gives it: `IN.SINU.TEMPLI.ROLLO.QUIESCIT / A.SE.VASTATAE.CONDITAE.NORMANNIAE.PATER.AC.PRIMUS.DUX / LABORE.QUI.FRACTUS.OCCUBUIT.OCTOGENARIO.MAIOR.AN.CM.XXXIII`: father and first duke of the Normandy he had himself laid waste, past eighty, 933. Commemorative and late, and the source of the flat "933" the encyclopedias repeat.
- **1944 [A], pool corrected.** The tomb stood in the chapel of the Petit Saint-Romain. The destroyed effigy was itself **medieval**, contemporary with William Longsword's gisant, head and legs remade in the seventeenth century. Its replacement is an actual **nineteenth-century** copy of the gisant of **Henry the Young King**, not a "nineteenth-century-style" imitation. A genuinely old monument was lost. **1956 [A]:** the remaining ashes went under a modern base in the south ambulatory.
- **1865 and 1911 [A], pool reversed.** Rouen's Rollo is **Arsène Letellier's 1865 statue** in Chauvigny stone, garden of the Hôtel de Ville, moved within that garden in 1956, repeatedly vandalized (finger amputated, sword broken). The **Ålesund and Fargo pieces are 1911 bronze replicas** by **Alphonse Guilloux** for the Normandy millennium. The pool had Rouen dated 1911 with the others derived from it, which is backwards; the Fargo cast, formerly "20th century", is therefore 1911. On the millennium: Chaline, *Études Normandes* 38-3 (1989), whose subtitle "Rouen 1911" the pool dropped.
- **2016, Fécamp [A].** Tombs attributed to Richard I and Richard II opened for Y-chromosome DNA; one yielded a lower jaw with eight teeth; radiocarbon showed both sets significantly predated the dukes (third century BCE and eighth century CE). Negative evidence about the Fécamp tombs, not about Rollo.
- **The modern frame [A], re-attributed.** That Normandy was politically the most successful Viking-founded polity yet culturally among the least distinctively Scandinavian is **Pierre Bauduin's** judgement, quoted in **John Howe's** review, *The Medieval Review* 07.03.09. The pool attributed it to the review and dated it "n.d.".
- **Vikings (History Channel) [R], not in the file.** Rollo as Ragnar Lothbrok's brother, Clive Standen, married to a princess Gisla. *Corrections:* the series ran **2013-2021**, and no source supports "**younger** brother". Excluded only because it has no place to stand on a map.

## Honest gaps

1. **No birthplace, no birth year, no parent two sources agree on.** The file opens on a generic central-Scandinavian pin and says so.
2. **No death date.** No annal records it. The 933 comes from a late commemorative epitaph.
3. **Nothing on his person.** No contemporary description of face, height, voice, or manner. "Too large for a horse" is thirteenth-century folklore. Every statue is invention.
4. **911 itself is unanchored.** The 918 diploma records a prior grant and does not date it.
5. **The 100 captives** of the Adémar story: unverifiable, De Gruyter/Brill 403/405.
6. **The Fargo statue's ceremony, donor, and site:** not confirmed, no Fargo-side source reached. The 1911 casting date is solid; the American end is not.
7. **The Fécamp 2016 press coverage:** not re-verified; carried on Wikipedia.
8. **Poppa:** contested parentage, no independent attestation, and a modern Bayeux statue to a woman whose existence rests on one court historian a century later.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Rollo is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no apparatus relation was found in the pool that would need him added. Nothing was silently deleted. Three categories, for the record:

1. **Reception material, kept, believed licit.** Nine stops in the final segment postdate his death: Dudo c. 1005, the 1063 translation, the Hôtel de Soubise in 1808, the 1865 statue, the two 1911 bronzes, the 1944 destruction and 1956 reinterment, the 2016 DNA test. All concern **Rollo's own body, tomb, and name**. No other person's framework is applied to him and no other traveler's journey is staged inside his: under the block rule, a new block embedding old hashes. Flagged only so the count is visible.
2. **Thematic links found by the interlock lens and dropped, per the shared-geography rule.** `charles_martel` (Neustria, same territory two centuries earlier), `leif_erikson` and `burnt_njal` (same Norse diaspora; the lens itself finds no shared place, person, or date and calls the link "not evidenced textually"), `adam_of_bremen` (checked directly, no mention of Rollo, the Normans, or 911). None is named in any campa.
3. **`roger_ii_of_sicily`, queued but unresearched, dropped.** The Hautevilles descend from Norman lords who descend from Rollo's colonists, not from Rollo. Institutional descent, not personal relation.

**The one interlock kept:** `william_the_conqueror`, named in the campa of the 1063 translation stop, Rollo's great-great-great-grandson by direct descent. His own file carries the hook back: the Falaise stop "the bronze horseman above Rollo", whose plinth names the six dukes before him "from Rollo the Norseman onward". That monument is **William's** stop, not re-narrated here, appearing only as a `suggested_ref`. `richard_lionheart.journey.json` is cited on a cathedral stop, since Richard's heart-tomb is in the same church, but Richard is named in no campa and his burial is not staged: that is his own file's business.
