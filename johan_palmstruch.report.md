# Johan Palmstruch (1611-1671): research report

*Riga to Amsterdam to Stockholm. Founder of Stockholms Banco, issuer of the first banknotes printed by a European bank, and the first European banker convicted for issuing them.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Where the pool asserted a contradiction that does not exist, that too is recorded: a fabricated conflict is worse than a real one.

The spine is the **Svenskt biografiskt lexikon** article (Riksarkivet, sok.riksarkivet.se/sbl/Artikel/8037), hereafter SBL, the only scholarly source reached. Everything else is Wikipedia in three languages, one museum page, and the 1911 Britannica. Where SBL and Wikipedia disagree, both readings are printed.

## 1. Riga, 1611 to the 1630s

**Birth [A, SBL].** Born 13 June 1611 in Riga, in Livonia, son of the merchant Reinholdt Witmacker (Wittmacher) and Anna Bjelska. Birth name Hans Witmacker. The city is under Polish-Lithuanian rule at his birth and passes to Sweden in 1621, when he is ten.

**The mother [A as to descriptor, gap as to place].** en.wikipedia records Anna Bielska as of Ruthenian descent, possibly of Jewish heritage. No town, region, or family is named anywhere consulted. A real gap, left as one and not plotted.

**The parish, the house, the guild [R].** The pool assigns the family to St. Peter's (the German Lutheran merchant church), the merchant quarter around Rātslaukums, and the Great Guild. None is documented for this family; all three are inferences from the standard topography of German merchant Riga, hedged in `date_confidence` and never in the prose. No source describes the route out either; departure down the Daugava is reconstructed from ordinary Baltic practice.

## 2. Amsterdam, c. 1634-1647

**Arrival and citizenship. Source conflict, flagged.** en.wikipedia gives the move as c. 1634 and citizenship 1635; SBL dates both together to 1635. Not adjudicated. Both readings appear in `date_confidence`.

**The Beurs [R, two corrections to the pool].** The pool called the Beurs van Hendrick de Keyser "the world's first purpose-built bourse". Antwerp's Handelsbeurs of 1531 holds that title; Amsterdam's building (1608-1611) is the first purpose-built *stock* exchange. The pool's coordinate (52.3706, 4.8952) is roughly 190 m southeast of the building, which stood over the Rokin just south of Dam Square at about 52.3717, 4.8933. Both corrected. His personal use of the exchange is documented nowhere and stays [R]; what is [A] is that Swedish sources describe Stockholms Banco as modeled on Amsterdam institutions.

**The business journey [A, SBL].** He sets out in August 1637 on an extended continental journey. No itinerary survives. Paris was checked as a canonical atlas pin (48.8566, 2.3522) and found unsupported. Not used.

**The imprisonment, February 1639. Cause contested, flagged.** SBL: imprisoned in February 1639 on his return, over a disputed commercial matter he maintained he was innocent of. sv.wikipedia agrees. de.wikipedia gives the same year but hedges the cause, "allegedly over unpaid debts". The pool stated flatly that "Wittmacher's Amsterdam ventures collapse under unpaid debts" and tagged it [A], which overstates every source. The year is [A]; the cause is written as contested.

**Which prison: gap.** No source names the building; the Rasphuis and the other correction houses are candidates and nothing more. The pin marks the Rokin business district, not a prison.

**Marriage, February 1644 [A, en.wikipedia].** To Margrieta van der Bosch, in Swedish sources Margareta von der Busch, born 30 December 1617 (SBL). A daughter is baptized in Amsterdam the same year at the **Oude Kerk** (en.wikipedia). The pool guessed the Nieuwe Kerk for the marriage; the baptism record makes Oude Kerk better supported, and the pool's Nieuwe Kerk coordinate was in any case about 90 m off, landing on the Royal Palace. Marriage and baptism are merged into one Oude Kerk stop, which states that the church of the marriage itself is undocumented.

**Release, 18 July 1646 [A, SBL].** The pool claimed "the sources give no account of how the debt was settled or resolved". False. SBL is explicit: "På grundval av drottning Kristinas promotorial 18 juli 1646 frigavs han ur fängelset och flyttade till Sverige." He was freed on the basis of a promotorial from Queen Christina of Sweden. The mechanism is documented, and a foreign queen's letter is the hinge of the whole biography.

**Arrival in Sweden, 1647 [A].** SBL's own header reads "flyttade till Sverige 47"; en.wikipedia also gives 1647. The pool asserted a 1646/1647 conflict between them. **No such conflict exists.** Recorded so it is not rediscovered.

**Kommerskollegium: a second fabricated conflict, dismissed.** The pool flagged a contradiction between English sources dating his Board of Trade post to 1647 and SBL dating it to 1654. en.wikipedia says only "He became a commissioner in the National Board of Trade after his arrival in Sweden in 1647", dating no appointment. SBL's "eo kommissarie i Kommerskoll 16 mars 54" stands unchallenged.

## 3. Sweden, 1647-1656: the plans

- **1651, 18 June [A, SBL and sv.wikipedia].** Ennobled with his brothers, for their late father's merits; takes the name Palmstruch in place of Witmacker. *Venue caution:* the pool put the ceremony at Riddarhuset and gave 59.3252, 18.0705, roughly 300 m off the actual 59.32583, 18.06528. Worse, the building was under construction from 1641 and not completed until 1660, so a ceremony inside it in 1651 is doubly speculative. Corrected coordinate used; the doubt sits in `date_confidence`.
- **1652 [A, SBL].** First banking plan presented to Queen Christina, the sovereign whose letter had freed him six years earlier.
- **1654, 16 March [A, SBL].** Extraordinary commissioner (extraordinarie kommissarie) at the Kommerskollegium.
- **1655 [A, SBL].** Proposes a municipal bank for Stockholm. Pinned at Stortorget, the city's rådhus, as [R].
- **1656 [A, SBL, sv.wikipedia].** Goes over the city's head to Charles X Gustav with a plan for an exchange bank and a loans bank on the Amsterdam model, with his brother-in-law Reinhold Rademacher.
- **1656, 30 November [A, SBL and en.wikipedia, same date].** The royal charter (oktroj) is confirmed. The pool closed this entry with "chartered a full year before its doors open"; the bank opened in July 1657, eight months later.
- **Tre Kronor coordinate.** The pool's 59.3258, 18.0717 for the royal castle falls on Storkyrkan. Tre Kronor stood at 59.32667, 18.07167. Corrected throughout.

**The copper problem [A].** Sweden's currency is plate money struck at Avesta; a ten-daler plate weighs close to twenty kilograms. No source places Palmstruch at Avesta, so it gets no stop; the plate money is carried at the Skeppsbron landing instead.

## 4. The bank, 1657-1661

- **July 1657 [A, SBL and en.wikipedia].** The exchange bank (växelbanken) opens, taking fee-based deposits at no interest, Palmstruch its director.
  - **Address [A, SBL].** "P skaffade sin bank ett centralt affärsläge i Sthlm i en fastighet vid Västerlånggatan." The bank was on **Västerlånggatan**. The pool put it at Järntorget four separate times, with a coordinate (59.3218, 18.0708) about 140 m south of Järntorget itself, out on the Kornhamnstorg water edge. That association is almost certainly a back-projection from Södra Bankohuset, the Riksbank's 1680s building: another institution, another decade. All four stops relocated to approximately 59.3235, 18.0700.
- **1657 [A, SBL].** Promoted to ordinary commissioner (ordinarie kommissarie). Folded into the July 1657 stop.
- **Spring 1659 [A, SBL and en.wikipedia].** The loans bank (lånebanken) opens, lending against property; the halves merge into Stockholms Banco under Palmstruch as general manager.
- **1660 [A, SBL].** Deposits pass 400,000 daler kopparmynt; gross lending reaches roughly 830,000.
- **1660 [A, en.wikipedia].** A reduction of about 17 percent in the copper content of the coinage sets off withdrawal demands and exposes the bank's liquidity.
- **July 1661 [A, SBL, en.wikipedia].** Kreditivsedlar issued in denominations of 5, 25, 100 and 1000 daler kopparmynt, signed by hand: the first banknotes issued by a European bank.

**The branch network: refuted [R at most].** sv.wikipedia says the business went so well that he opened offices in Gothenburg, Falun and Åbo. SBL rejects that sentence: "Det har också hävdats att P genom filialer i Gbg, Falun och Åbo var på väg att förverkliga idén om en bankrörelse över hela landet. Detta är dock knappast riktigt. I Gbg fanns endast ett växlingsombud." Gothenburg had an exchange agent only; Falun met distrust and its planned branch was never established; what happened in Åbo, SBL says, is unknown. The pool carried the sv.wikipedia version at [A]. All three towns keep a stop, and all three stops say what SBL says.

## 5. The collapse, 1663-1667

- **1663 [A, en.wikipedia].** Note issue outruns the metal and the notes lose value.
- **October 1663 [A, both sources].** The bank increasingly refuses to redeem notes presented to it. SBL: "Från oktober månad måste den i växande omfattning vägra att inlösa de kreditivsedlar som kom in." en.wikipedia places the same October in 1663. The pool asserted a conflict between October 1663 and October 1664. **There is none**; both say 1663.
- **1664 [A, SBL, sv.wikipedia].** Government and Riksdag prohibit the notes; operations cease; a riksråd commission sits.
- **1666: an open contradiction, not resolved.** en.wikipedia describes a second series of 10, 25, 50 and 100 silver daler, known as Palmstruchare. sv.wikipedia 'Kreditivsedel' says notes were issued "från 1661 till 1666". sv.wikipedia 'Stockholms Banco' says only "Åren 1661-1664" and mentions neither the series nor the term. SBL has operations ceasing with 1664, so a new series would fall two years after the ban. The journey file states the disagreement rather than picking a winner. SBL does record Palmstruch on the coinage commissions in September and October 1666, which may be the real event behind the date.
- **1667 [A, SBL].** Liquidation completes with the redemption of the last outstanding note. On 2 May the council resolves to put Palmstruch before the Svea hovrätt.

## 6. The verdict, 1668-1670

**22 July 1668 [A, SBL and en.wikipedia, in agreement].** The worst error in the research pool and the one most worth recording. The pool dated the verdict 22 July **1667** and flagged en.wikipedia's 1668 as a conflicting tradition. SBL reads: "rådet beslöt 2 maj 1667 att ställa P till ansvar inför Svea hovrätt. **Domen föll 22 juli följande år.**" The year following 2 May 1667 is 1668. SBL and en.wikipedia agree. The pool would have misdated the trial by a year and invented a source conflict on top of it.

**The sentence [A, SBL].** Loss of office, loss of the banking privilege, perpetual exile, convertible to death if the damages went unpaid: "P dömdes till avsättning och förlust av privilegiet på bankrörelse samt evig landsförvisning."

**Venue [R, the pool's guess rejected].** The pool placed the sentencing on Riddarholmen. Svea hovrätt did not sit at the Wrangelska palatset there until the eighteenth century; in 1668 it sat in the royal castle, which burned in 1697. Tre Kronor is used, marked reconstructed.

**The names of the co-accused: dropped.** The pool had Palmstruch blaming Chancellor Magnus Gabriel De la Gardie and State Treasurer Gustav Bonde. Neither name is in the cited sources; sv.wikipedia says only that he claimed other shareholders were "medansvariga".

**17 September 1668 [A, en.wikipedia].** The banking privilege passes to Riksens Ständers Bank, forerunner of Sveriges Riksbank.

**26 February 1669 [A, SBL].** The council lifts the death penalty, contingent on unpaid damages from the start rather than commuted at sentencing.

**Length of custody: the pool understated it.** sv.wikipedia 'Stockholms Banco' says that when judgment fell in 1668 he "satt redan i fängelse sedan flera år". SBL fixes only the end: "hölls i fängsligt förvar till 1670." Custody runs from the mid-1660s to 1670, not from 1668. The starting year is a gap; so is the prison, which no source names.

**1670 [A, SBL, sv.wikipedia].** Released, eleven months before his death.

## 7. Death, burial, afterlife

- **8 March 1671 [A, SBL, en.wikipedia].** Dies in Stockholms domkyrkoförsamling, the Storkyrkan parish, at about sixty.
- **Burial, Täby kyrka [A, en.wikipedia].** The date is given by no source consulted. The pool's two lenses gave two coordinates: geography 59.49083, 18.05667 (correct, matching sv.wikipedia 'Täby kyrka'), afterlife 59.4439, 18.0686, about 5.4 km too far south with the Stockholm longitude copied down the file. Reconciled to the former.
- **The tomb [A, corrected].** The pool called the survival of a grave marker a gap. It is not: en.wikipedia carries photographs captioned "Tombstone of Johan Palmstruch, Täby Church, 1671" and "Arms of Johan Palmstruch, Täby Church, 1671". Genuinely undocumented is the inscription text and its legibility, and that narrower claim is the one the journey file makes.
- **Gribbylund [A, sv.wikipedia].** "Johan Palmstruch gifte sig före 1644 med Margareta von der Busch och bosatte sig i Gribbylunds gård i Täby." sv.wikipedia 'Gribbylund' gives 59.46222, 18.08611, about 600 m from the pool's value. Palmstruchs väg is named after him; the estate lies about 3.5 km from the church.
- **20 March 1677 [A, SBL].** Margareta von der Busch dies.
- **1962 and 2 December 1964 [A, sv.wikipedia].** The Riksbank funds Stiftelsen Riksbankens Jubileumsfond against its tercentenary counted from 1668. The institution dates itself from the year it absorbed Palmstruch's bank, not the year he founded it.
- **Portrait: gap, narrowed.** The pool said Wikimedia Commons holds nothing. The category URL does 404, but Commons hosts the tombstone and arms images embedded in en.wikipedia. Correct statement: no portrait or likeness located, no dedicated category, but images relating to him exist.

## 8. Quotations

Two only, neither in Palmstruch's own voice, both verified in the fetched page rather than taken on trust.

1. 1911 Encyclopaedia Britannica, 'Sweden', via English Wikisource: "The first Swedish bank, called the Palmstruch bank after its founder, Johan Palmstruch, was incorporated in 1656. It began to issue notes..." An encyclopedia entry, not a primary document.
2. Ekonomiska museet, 'Europas första banksedlar': "bankdirektören Johan Palmstruch redan från början ville ersätta de stora plåtmynten med sedlar eftersom de var lättare att hantera." A museum paraphrase of his motive.

**No document in Palmstruch's own voice was reached.** His petitions, his bank plans of 1652 and 1655, and his defense before the Svea hovrätt exist in Swedish archives and none was reachable. That is the largest gap here. The journey file carries the museum line at the 1661 stop; its only other quote is Paterson's, inherited from william_paterson.journey.json.

## 9. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Verified against the journey files, not taken from the pool's summary.

- **william_paterson.** The strongest link. Paterson's Amsterdam stop has "the whole apparatus of paper credit he wants to build for somebody already stands here in brick", annotated as "the working model of public banking Paterson would import to London". Stockholms Banco (1657) is the middle term between that model (1609) and the Bank of England (1694), and the Paterson file does not name it. His prospectus sentence, quoted at Grocers' Hall, is the exact charge that ended Palmstruch. Named in campa; the Grocers' Hall pin (51.5134, -0.0897) inherited byte-identical for the closing stop.
- **john_locke.** Dutch exile 1683-1689 in the same Amsterdam, half a century after Palmstruch left it, then the Board of Trade during the recoinage argument. Named in campa.
- **newton.** The Great Recoinage of 1696 rebuilds the metal under England's paper. Named at the Grocers' Hall stop.
- **samuel_oppenheimer.** Vienna, 1670s-1680s: personal court credit advanced on a man's own body and fortune, against Palmstruch's impersonal chartered paper. Named in campa; no shared pin, since he never went to Vienna.
- **richard_wagner, edward_witten.** Both hold Riga stops. Named in campa at the birth stop.
- **hilma_af_klint, miranda.** Stockholm pin 59.3293, 18.0686, inherited byte-identical here for four stops. Not named in campa: sharing a city two centuries apart is not a crossing. **jules_verne**, Gamla Stan pin 59.3251, 18.0711, inherited for the two stops where prison and release cannot be placed more precisely than the old town. **mikhail_bakunin** stands at 59.3258, 18.0717, the coordinate the pool wrongly gave for Tre Kronor: noted, not inherited.

### Interlock claims from the pool that are wrong

1. **"The Amsterdam pin 52.3738, 4.891 is inherited byte-identical across william_paterson and john_locke."** False. Paterson uses 52.3738, 4.891. Locke uses **52.3676, 4.9041** and annotates it as "the canonical atlas Amsterdam pin, inherited byte-identical"; the pool's afterlife lens independently gave the same. Two of three witnesses agree, so that is the value used here, with the conflict stated in the journey file rather than papered over. **The atlas has two Amsterdam pins and needs a decision it has not made.**
2. **"No existing journey.json contains the word Stockholm."** False, and badly so: seventeen files do, hilma_af_klint being substantially a Stockholm life. The true and narrower claim: no file connects Stockholm to banking.
3. **"Riga is a canonical pin inherited across four travelers."** Half false. Wagner and Witten hold Riga stops at two different coordinates (56.9503, 24.1136 and 56.9403, 24.0925); Kant and Weber name Riga in prose with no pin. There is no canonical Riga pin, so this file uses site-specific coordinates and says so.
4. **Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), Temple Mount (31.778, 35.2354), Buenos Aires (-34.6037, -58.3816), Paris (48.8566, 2.3522).** Checked per instruction, all four irrelevant to a life between Riga, Amsterdam and Stockholm. Recorded so the check is not repeated.
5. **QUEUE.md.** Confirmed: Palmstruch and Paterson were queued as a matched pair, the Palmstruch line reading "the corpus's central-banking thread (william_paterson just queued) reaches back further still through him". René Descartes is queued and unresearched; his 1650 Stockholm death falls seven years before Stockholms Banco opened in the same city.
