# Ibn al-Durayhim al-Mawsili: research report

*Taj al-Din Ali ibn Muhammad ibn Abd al-Aziz ibn Futuh ibn Ibrahim ibn Abi Bakr, called Ibn al-Durayhim al-Mawsili al-Taghlabi al-Shafi'i. Born Mosul, Sha'ban 712 AH (c. December 1312); died 762 AH (November 1360 to October 1361), place unrecorded.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

Standing caution: the dictionaries that would settle most of the open questions here (al-Safadi's *al-Wafi bi'l-Wafayat* and *A'yan al-'Asr* in full, Ibn Hajar's *al-Durar al-Kamina*) were **not accessible in this pass**. Everything below runs through encyclopedia articles citing them, which is a thin base for a fourteenth-century life.

---

## 1. The tradition he inherits (context, not biography)

- **al-Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi (718-786) compiles *Kitab al-Mu'amma*, the first Arabic book on enciphered messages, at Basra.** **[A]** (English Wikipedia, 'History of cryptography') The book does not survive; the method does. *Corrections:* the pool's "Great Mosque of al-Nuri, Basra" (30.5085 / 47.7835) is fabricated, al-Nuri being the **Mosul** mosque, and eighth-century Basra stood ~15 km southwest of the modern city; the journey file uses the corpus pin. Whether Ibn al-Durayhim knew this book is **not attested anywhere**. **[R]**

- **al-Kindi (d. c. 873) writes *Risala fi Istikhraj al-Kutub al-Mu'ammah*, establishing frequency analysis.** **[A]** (English Wikipedia, 'Frequency analysis') Ibn al-Durayhim's work is described as employing "the statistical techniques pioneered by Al-Kindi and Ibn 'Adlan," the one **attested, direct** link between them. **[A]** (English Wikipedia, 'Ibn al-Durayhim') *Corrections:* he died c. 873, not c. 870; the common 850 for the *Risala* is unsourced, and the journey file uses 843 after `al_kindi.journey.json`; Ibn al-Nadim credits at least **260** works, not 250; "fewer than a third survive" is unsourced and dropped. **[A/R]**

- **Ali ibn Adlan al-Mawsili (1187-1268), born at Mosul, adds the sample-size rule.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'ابن عدلان'; English Wikipedia, 'Ibn Adlan') He studied with the Mukabbari grammarians, moved through Aleppo and Damascus, and settled and died in Cairo, teaching Arabic at the Salihiyya mosque. *Corrections:* al-Nuri and the al-Hadba minaret were built 1172-73, about fifteen years **before** his birth, so "already old by his childhood" is wrong. The twenty groups of rules and the **~90-character minimum sample** come from the corpus and secondary literature, not the manuscript. **[A/R]** The Aleppo and Damascus stops are bare: no patron, no duration, no work.

- **Mosul produced two of the four men in this chain, a century and a quarter apart**, and no source suggests Ibn al-Durayhim grew up hearing Ibn Adlan's name. **[R]**

## 2. Birth, name, and Mosul (1312 - c. 1332)

- **Born in Mosul in Sha'ban 712 AH.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'ابن الدريهم') English Wikipedia gives 1312 with no month. Sha'ban 712 falls at about December 1312, the date the journey file uses.
- **Lineage variant, unresolved:** the fourth name reads **ibn Fath** in one place and **ibn Futuh** (English Wikipedia: *Ibn Futūḥ*) in another. Both recorded; neither preferred. **[A, variant flagged]**
- **Epithets:** al-Mawsili, al-Taghlabi (of the Taghlib), al-Shafi'i, and the honorific **Taj al-Din**, Crown of the Religion, used by contemporaries including al-Safadi. **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'ابن الدريهم' and 'صلاح الدين الصفدي') "Born in Mosul" rests substantially on the nisba itself; no birth record exists.
- **The nisba al-Durayhim is the diminutive of *dirham*,** inherited from an ancestor rather than describing him. Which ancestor, and why, is not recorded. **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia)
- **Learns the Qur'an under Abu Bakr ibn al-Ilm al-Mawsuli.** **[A]** Undated in the source; the journey file's 1320 is an approximate childhood placement and says so.
- **Studies Shafi'i jurisprudence *and* mathematics under Zayn al-Din ibn Shaykh al-Uwayna.** **[A]** Undated. The name appears as **al-Uwayna** and **al-Uwayya** in different renderings of the same notice; not resolved. The pairing of law with arithmetic under one teacher is attested, not inferred.
- **No source dates or describes a departure from Mosul.** The journey file's "road south" stop is explicitly labelled reconstruction in `date_confidence`. **[R]**

## 3. Egypt (before July 1344 at the latest)

- **Travels to Egypt and studies hadith there.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia) No teacher, no year, no city named; Cairo is **inferred** from where his grammar teacher taught. **[R as to place]**
- **Receives a formal *ijaza* in Arabic grammar from Athir al-Din Abu Hayyan al-Gharnati.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'ابن الدريهم' and 'أبو حيان الأندلسي') Undated, capped only by Abu Hayyan's death.
- **Abu Hayyan dies blind (ضريرًا) at his house outside Bab al-Bahr on the outskirts of Cairo, after the afternoon prayer, Saturday 28 Safar 745 AH / 10 July 1344, aged 87-88; buried the following day in the cemetery of the Sufis outside Bab al-Nasr.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, verified verbatim) Pin corrected to **30.0575 / 31.264**: the cemetery lay north of Bab al-Nasr (~30.0561 / 31.2637), while the pool's 30.0555 / 31.2618 falls inside the walls.
- **Al-Azhar is not attested as his teaching venue.** The notice says only that he took knowledge from the scholars of Cairo; the al-Azhar stop is labelled reconstruction. **[R]**
- **Reputation as an on-sight cryptanalyst:** handed an enciphered letter, he converts it and reads it aloud in plain Arabic. **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'علم التعمية') Undated, unplaced, no witness named. The same passage credits him with **reading hieroglyphic inscriptions** **[A as a report; false as a claim about the world]**, which the journey file marks as an accretion.

## 4. The cryptological work (undated)

- **The treatise.** **[A that it exists; title unsettled]** Arabic Wikipedia's work list gives **مفتاح الكنوز في إيضاح المرموز**, *Miftah al-kunuz fi idah al-marmuz*, Key to the Treasures on Clarifying the Encoded; English Wikipedia gives **مقاصد الفصول المترجمة عن حل الترجمة**, *Maqasid al-fusul al-mutarjama an hall al-tarjama*, "Clear Chapters Goals and Solving Ciphers." *Correction:* the widely repeated **"Miftah al-kunuz fi idah al-lughz"** ("Clarification of the Riddle") is **wrong**; the Arabic reads *al-marmuz*, the encoded. The two attested titles are not translations of each other, and whether they name one work or two is **not settled by anything reachable**.
- **Content.** "Also traced to Ibn al-Durayhim is an exposition on and a worked example of cryptanalysis, including the use of tables of letter frequencies and sets of letters which cannot occur together in one word." **[A]** (English Wikipedia, 'History of cryptography', quoted verbatim)
- **The tableau.** English Wikipedia: he "gave detailed descriptions of eight cipher systems that discussed substitution ciphers, leading to the earliest suggestion of a 'tableau' of the kind that two centuries later became known as the 'Vigenère table'." **[A, quoted verbatim from the article]**
  - **Contradiction, left open.** One verification pass reported that **eight cipher systems is not in the cited source**; a quotation pass reproduced the sentence containing it verbatim from that same article. Either the article changed or one pass misread it. The journey file quotes the sentence as found and does not assert "eight" in its own prose. The two-century interval is sound: d. 1360/61 to Vigenère 1586.
- **A didactic *qasida* setting out methods of enciphering.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'علم التعمية') Undated; no line of it quoted in anything reachable.
- **The riddle correspondence with al-Safadi.** He writes his side up as *al-Muhawara al-Salahiyya fi al-Ahjahi al-Istilahiyya*, surviving uniquely as **Escorial manuscript 432**; al-Safadi's parallel *Najm al-Diyaji fi Nazm al-Ahjahi* does not survive. **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'صلاح الدين الصفدي') Damascus is inferred from al-Safadi's residence, not stated.
- **Corpus size.** Roughly **two dozen** treatises. **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, ~24) The pool used three totals in three places (25, 27, "roughly two dozen"); the journey file uses the lowest, which is what the cited source supports.
- **No composition date survives for any of the cryptological work, and no place of composition for any of it.** Stated as a gap.

## 5. The animal book (1355)

- **Compiles *Kitab Manafi' al-Hayawan*, the Book on the Usefulness of Animals, in 1355 CE, drawing on Ibn Bakhtishu' and Aristotle; the surviving copy contains 91 animal illustrations and is held at the Escorial Library, shelfmark Ar. 898.** **[A]** (English Wikipedia, 'Ibn al-Durayhim') This is one of the very few firm dates in the life.
- *Corrections:* "dedicated to a Mamluk sultan" appears in **no** source consulted, and "one of only two of his works to survive complete" is contradicted by Escorial 432, which makes at least three; both dropped, along with the pool's Citadel coordinate for a work whose place of composition it calls unknown.
- **How the manuscript reached Spain is unrecorded.** The journey file offers the 1612 capture of Sultan Zidan's library and its 1614 arrival at the Escorial as the likeliest occasion, marked **[R]**, unconnected by any source.

## 6. Death (762 AH)

- **Dies in 762 AH, aged 48-49.** **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia)
- **762 AH runs from 11 November 1360 to 30 October 1361**, so the Hijri year maps to **1360/1361**, not flatly to 1361; the pool's "762 AH = 1361" overstated the conversion. **[A, corrected]** English Wikipedia gives a range instead, **1359/62**. **Contradiction flagged, not resolved:** the English-language cryptology literature prints 1359, 1361 and 1362 for the same man.
- **No source consulted names a place of death or burial.** Genuine gap; the journey file pins the death stop at the canonical Cairo coordinate and says in `date_confidence` that this is a placeholder for the last attested milieu, not a claim. **No government or scribal post is documented for him** either: some secondary accounts call him a chancery cryptanalyst, and nothing reachable supports it.

## 7. Al-Safadi's notice, the only near-contemporary portrait

- **Salah al-Din Khalil ibn Aybak al-Safadi (696 AH / 1296 - 23 July 1363)**, katib al-sirr and keeper of the treasury at Damascus, wrote the entry on which nearly every later account depends. **[A]** (Arabic Wikipedia, 'صلاح الدين الصفدي') His birth year is **1296**, not 1297 as the pool had it in two places.
- **The sentence:** كان أعجوبة من أعاجيب الزمان في ذكائه: "he was a wonder among the wonders of the age in his intelligence." **[A]** (verified present in the raw wikitext of Arabic Wikipedia's ابن الدريهم, introduced by قال فيه الصفدي)
  - **Attribution corrected.** The footnote cites al-Safadi's ***A'yan al-'Asr wa-A'wan al-Nasr*, iii.523-24**, **not** *al-Wafi bi'l-Wafayat*, which most summaries assign it to. Both are his, so the author is right and the book was wrong; neither was checked against a printed edition. The circulating "a marvel of the age" is a paraphrase, not a translation, and is not quoted as such in the journey file.
- **What the notice actually lists:** cryptography, the properties of letters, magic squares and riddles, alongside jurisprudence, hadith and usul al-din. It does **not** list mathematics, Qur'anic studies or astronomy; those belong to the general account of his subjects and were folded into the quotation by two of the pool's lenses. **[A, corrected]**

## 8. Transmission: al-Qalqashandi, and the two contradictory afterlives

- **Ahmad ibn Ali al-Qalqashandi (b. Qalqashanda, Qalyubia, 1355 or 1356; d. Cairo 1418)** served in the Mamluk chancery as **katib al-darj**, Scribe of the Scroll, and completed the fourteen-volume *Subh al-a'sha fi Sina'at al-Insha* in **1412**. **[A]** (English Wikipedia, 'Al-Qalqashandi')
  - *Correction:* he was born roughly **six years before** Ibn al-Durayhim's death, not after it, so he was a small child in those last years. The two never met either way, but the pool's sentence was wrong.
- **"Al-Qalqashandi quoted the text relevant to cryptology from the work of Ibn al-Durayhim (1312–1361) that was once considered lost."** **[A, verbatim]** (English Wikipedia, 'Al-Qalqashandi') **Kahn's assessment** is fuller than the pool's rendering: the section holds "the first published discussion of the substitution and transposition of ciphers, **and the first description of a polyalphabetic cipher**." **[A]**
- **Unsupported specifics dropped.** "Book 13/14," "on secret correspondence," and "nearly verbatim" appear in **nothing reachable**; *Subh al-a'sha* and Kahn could not be retrieved. The journey file carries no locator.

### The contradiction at the centre of the afterlife, left standing

1. **Lost.** "This information was attributed to Ibn al-Durayhim who lived from AD 1312 to 1361, but whose writings on cryptography have been lost." **[A, verbatim]** (English Wikipedia, 'History of cryptography')
2. **Found.** "His book titled Clear Chapters Goals and Solving Ciphers was recently discovered, but has yet to be published." **[A, verbatim]** (English Wikipedia, 'Ibn al-Durayhim') No repository, no date, no finder is named in that sentence.
3. **Found *in Istanbul, beside al-Kindi's*.** The most widely circulated version, and a **conflation**: English Wikipedia attributes the Süleymaniye recovery to **al-Kindi's** works only, and **no source consulted places Ibn al-Durayhim's treatise there.** The journey file asserts the al-Kindi recovery, hedges his own discovery with no location, and names the merger as the reason the lost / found split looks sharper than the evidence warrants.

- **Ibrahim A. al-Kadi, "Origins of Cryptology: The Arab Contributions," *Cryptologia* 16, no. 2 (1992)** made the chain visible to modern cryptologists. **[A as citation; article not retrieved]** (ResearchGate 403) The year is **1992**, not 1990. The **Series on Arabic Origins of Cryptology** (Mrayati, Meer Alam, al-Tayyan; King Faisal Center, Riyadh) reportedly edits his cryptological material. **[R]** (publisher pages unreachable, DNS failure; check a library catalogue before treating as attested)
- **No tomb, shrine, marker, ziyara tradition, miracle story or popular legend** attaches to him in anything consulted. The afterlife is textual: al-Qalqashandi in the fifteenth century, al-Kadi and Kahn in the twentieth. **[R, argument from absence]**

- **`al_kindi`**: attested and direct, the treatise "includes the use of the statistical techniques pioneered by Al-Kindi and Ibn 'Adlan." **[A]** `al_kindi.journey.json` already carries stops for Ibn al-Durayhim (1350) and for al-Qalqashandi's manual drawn "straight out of Ibn al-Durayhim" (1412). Pins inherited byte-identically: **Cairo 30.0444 / 31.2357**, **Baghdad 33.3475 / 44.335**, **Istanbul 41.0164 / 28.9639**, **Damascus 33.5138 / 36.2765** (from `ibn_battuta.journey.json`), **Basra 30.3919 / 47.7011**.
- **`al_khalil_ibn_ahmad_al_farahidi`**: indirect, one-directional inheritance of a method, the *Kitab al-Mu'amma* and the enumerative alphabet standing behind the "letters and their properties" al-Safadi praises. **[R: no source shows him citing al-Farahidi.]**
- **`aristotle`**: direct and one-directional, *Manafi' al-Hayawan* "draws largely on the work of Ibn Bakhtishu' and Aristotle." **[A]**

Deliberately **not** named in any campa: **`ibn_battuta`** (Cairo 1326, Damascus 1326 and 1348, overlapping milieu, no attested contact); **`aaron_the_levite`** (shared sultan only); **`roger_bacon`, `claude_shannon`, `betty_shannon`** (endpoints of the al-Kindi transmission, no relation either way); **`maslama_al_qurtubi`** (cited only in *sources*, for the Escorial pin and the Zidan route; the link is between manuscripts and a library, not between people).

## 10. Open contradictions, listed together and not resolved

1. **Death year.** 762 AH = 1360/1361 (Arabic Wikipedia) versus 1359/62 (English Wikipedia); 1359, 1361 and 1362 all circulate in English cryptology reference text.
2. **Treatise title.** *Miftah al-kunuz fi idah al-marmuz* versus *Maqasid al-fusul al-mutarjama an hall al-tarjama*. One work or two, unknown.
3. **Lost or found.** Called lost in one Wikipedia article, recently discovered in another; the popular "found in Istanbul with al-Kindi's" is a conflation supported by neither.
4. **Eight cipher systems.** Quoted verbatim from English Wikipedia by one pass; reported absent from that same article by another.
5. **Smaller splits:** corpus size ~24 against 25 and 27; the al-Safadi sentence, *A'yan al-'Asr* against *al-Wafi bi'l-Wafayat*; **al-Uwayna** / **al-Uwayya**; ibn **Futuh** / ibn **Fath**.
