# Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646-1716): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for `gottfried_wilhelm_leibniz.journey.json`. Leipzig to Hanover by way of Jena, Altdorf, Nuremberg, Mainz, Paris, London, the Low Countries, the Harz, Vienna, Italy, Berlin, Bad Pyrmont; plus one place he never went, Beijing, from which the decisive letter came.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Coordinates below are those adopted after correction; the replaced values are given, several having been large enough to drop a stop into open country.

---

## 1. Leipzig, 1646-1666: the father's library and the refused doctorate

**[A: Wikipedia, *Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz*; SEP]** Born 1 July 1646 in Leipzig, son of Friedrich Leibniz, professor of moral philosophy. Baptized at the Nikolaikirche (51.3403, 12.3757). The father dies in 1652; the boy is raised by mother and uncle and given the run of the father's private library, teaching himself Latin before he is twelve. Every reachable biography keeps this detail.

**[A: Wikipedia]** Matriculates at Leipzig in 1661, aged fourteen, guided by Jakob Thomasius, who had studied under Friedrich Leibniz. Bachelor of philosophy 1662; master 1664; bachelor of law 1665.

**[A: MacTutor]** A term at Jena, summer 1663, under Erhard Weigel (Collegium Jenense, 50.9296, 11.588), who taught that philosophical demonstration should follow mathematical proof. That this is the seed of the *characteristica universalis* is the biographers' interpretation: the causal claim is **[R]**.

**[A: Wikipedia; SEP]** 1666: defends the habilitation thesis that becomes the first part of *De Arte Combinatoria*, written at nineteen. The same year the Leipzig law faculty declines his doctorate. Traditional explanation: his youth. Other accounts: a dean's grudge **[A as fact, [R] as to motive: Aiton (1985); Wikipedia]**. **Not adjudicated.**

### Contradiction 1: the date of the Altdorf doctorate

He leaves for Altdorf, takes the doctorate in law for *De Casibus Perplexis*, and declines the professorship offered him **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.

German Wikipedia and *Universität Altdorf*: **November 1666**. MacTutor: **February 1667**. SEP: **Doctorate of Law: 1667**. The stop is dated 1666-11-01 with the disagreement carried in `date_confidence`. The pool stated 1666 flatly in two lenses; corrected in both.

## 2. Nuremberg, Frankfurt, Mainz, 1667-1672

**[A: MacTutor]** 1667: first salaried post, secretary to a Nuremberg alchemical society, a subject he knew little about when he applied. Meeting house unrecorded, membership roll not surviving: an honest gap. The story that he wrote the application out of obscure terms copied from alchemical books is **[R]**, sourceless in every version reached. **[A: Antognazza (2009); MacTutor]** By November 1667, at Frankfurt (Römerberg, 50.1102, 8.6821), he meets **Johann Christian von Boineburg**, at that date a *dismissed* minister of Mainz, and enters his private service.

### Contradiction 2: when the Mainz service begins

The pool's chronology lens dated service to the Elector to **1667**; its own geography lens dated the appointment **1669-1672**. Reachable evidence supports the geography lens: Boineburg's private service from 1667, appointment as assessor to the Court of Appeal and work on the legal code from **1669** to 1672 **[A: SEP; Wikipedia]**; the *Catholic Demonstrations* begun c. 1668-1669 **[A: SEP]**. The file uses 1669 and folds both Mainz items into one stop. In these years he also drafts the memorandum urging Louis XIV toward Egypt **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 3. Paris and London, 1672-1676: the calculus

**[A: MacTutor]** Arrives Paris October 1672 on the Egypt mission. No audience is granted. Boineburg dies December 1672, the Elector February 1673, ending the errand; he stays four years. **Gap:** no Paris residence is recorded anywhere reachable, so the city pin (48.8566, 2.3522) serves all Paris stops but one.

### Contradiction 3: where the Royal Society met in early 1673

**[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia, *Royal Society*]** January to February 1673: sent to London as escort on a peace errand, he demonstrates the calculating machine (in development since 1670) and meets Henry Oldenburg and John Collins. Elected Fellow **9 April 1673**, confirmed independently by German Wikipedia. The pool placed this at **Gresham College** (51.5155, -0.0817), wrong for the date: the Society moved to **Arundel House** after the 1666 Fire and returned to Gresham only later in 1673. Corrected to roughly 51.5115, -0.1157, an error of about 2.4 km. The October 1676 stop keeps the Gresham pin, no longer anachronistic by then.

**[A: SEP; Wikipedia]** 1672-1676: Huygens sets him reading Pascal and Descartes. He develops the differential and integral calculus in this period, independently of Newton's earlier unpublished fluxions; earliest surviving notebook use, **1675**. **[A: SEP]** 29 October 1675: the dated manuscript page carrying the long S, with *d* following shortly after.

**[A: Wikipedia]** The friendship with **Tschirnhaus** belongs to **1675**, not 1672: he reached Paris only that year. The pool filed him under a 1672 heading; corrected.

### Contradiction 4: the Paris Academy, venue and admission year

**[A: MacTutor]** 1675: applies as a foreign honorary member, refused for lack of a vacancy.

- **Venue.** The pool's 48.8606, 2.3376 is the **Louvre**, occupied by the Academy only after the reorganization of 20 January 1699. In 1675 it sat in the King's Library, about 48.8680, 2.3380, used here; the Louvre pin is kept for 1717.
- **Admission year.** One strand of the pool says **1700**, MacTutor **1701**. **Not adjudicated**; the campa says "around 1700" and names both in `date_confidence`.

**[A: Wikipedia; SEP]** October 1676: second London visit; he calls on Collins and is shown Newton manuscripts on infinite series. How much he read and understood is the central and unanswerable question of the dispute. Stated as unanswerable, not resolved. November 1676: Delft, van Leeuwenhoek's microscopes (52.0116, 4.3571).

**[A: SEP, dating the visit 18-21 November; Wikipedia]** The Hague: several days with **Spinoza** over the finished but unpublished *Ethics*, at the Paviljoensgracht house, corrected to 52.0742, 4.3104 (the pool's value is about 300 m off). The pool's "Spinoza dies four months later" is wrong: he died **21 February 1677**, three months later.

## 4. Hanover, 1676-1690: court, mines, archives

**[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]** Arrives Hanover December 1676 as counsellor to the Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg; librarian from the following year; **Privy Counselor of Justice** from 1677, held to his death. Forty years, three rulers, one employment. Leineschloss coordinates corrected to 52.3706, 9.7336 (the pool's value had borrowed the longitude of the Neustädter Kirche).

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1680-1685: the Harz drainage scheme, windmill-driven pumps at Clausthal-Zellerfeld (51.8058, 10.3389), shut down as a costly failure by **Duke Ernst August in 1685**. That the same years produced his first work on the conservation of force and the fossils behind the *Protogaea* is **[A]** as chronology, **[R]** as causal link. Decades of correspondence with the **Electress Sophia** at Herrenhausen (52.3915, 9.6976), and later her daughter **Sophie Charlotte** in Berlin.

### Correction: he did not co-found the *Acta Eruditorum*

The pool has him founding the journal "together with Otto Mencke." Wikipedia (*Acta Eruditorum*) is explicit: founded 1682 in Leipzig by Mencke, its first editor, Leibniz supporting from Hanover and contributing 13 articles in four years. **Backer and principal contributor, not co-founder.**

**[A: Wikipedia, *Leibniz's notation*; SEP]** 1684: *Nova Methodus pro Maximis et Minimis*, the first printed calculus, introducing *d* in print; 1686, *De Geometria Recondita*, first print appearance of the integral sign, and the *Discourse on Metaphysics* with the **Arnauld** correspondence following it, the *Discourse* itself first printed 1846.

**[A: Wikipedia; Antognazza; MacTutor]** November 1687 to June 1690: the archival journey for the Brunswick history. Vienna (Hofburg, 48.2082, 16.3653); Rome, where he works among Jesuits of the Collegio Romano, several of them China-mission veterans, and is elected to the Accademia Fisico-Matematica **[A: Perkins (2004); Antognazza]**; Florence and the Medici collections; **Modena**, where he finds the Guelf-Este link the journey was for; Venice on the way home. He is considered for **Vatican Librarian** and does not take it, unwilling to convert **[A: Antognazza, second hand, flagged]**. **[R]** The Adriatic storm on the voyage home and his refusal to hold a rosary belong to the eulogy tradition, not to a document; hedged in the campa.

**[A: Wikipedia; German Wikipedia, *Herzog August Bibliothek*]** 1691: librarian of the **Herzog August Bibliothek**, Wolfenbüttel (corrected to 52.1642, 10.5303), held 1691-1716 *nebenberuflich*, without living in the town. His catalogue scheme is copied elsewhere in Europe.

## 5. 1695-1705: the public philosopher, the Academy, one and zero

### Correction: where the *New System* was published

The pool places the 1695 *Système nouveau* at **Hanover**, where he wrote it. It was **published in Paris**, in the *Journal des Sçavans*, June to July 1695, and that is what made it his public entry into European philosophy **[A]**. Pinned to Paris.

**[A: Wikipedia, *Prussian Academy of Sciences*]** 11 July 1700: the Brandenburg Society of Sciences founded in Berlin, Leibniz its first president, with Sophie Charlotte's backing. **Coordinates corrected:** the pool's 52.5192, 13.401 sits near Monbijou, about 1 km from the Society's early seat in the Marstall on Dorotheenstraße (52.5185, 13.3877). **Gap:** the pool's Berlin visit "on the same errand in 1698" is unverifiable here, the documented journeys clustering on 1697 and 1700. **Dropped**, not downgraded.

### Contradiction 5: the Bouvet letter, sent 1701, received 1703

**[A: Wikipedia, *Joachim Bouvet*; Perkins]** Bouvet, from Beijing, sends the diagram of the 64 I Ching hexagrams in the Fuxi/Shao Yong arrangement, dated **4 November 1701**. It does **not reach Hanover until April 1703**. The pool compressed those eighteen months and dated the recognition to 1701. Corrected: sent 1701, read 1703, and the delay is why the binary essay and the Chinese material converge when they do. **Also corrected:** the pool placed Bouvet at the **Nantang**, the Portuguese-padroado church, but Bouvet was a *French* Jesuit; Kangxi granted the French a site inside the Imperial City, the residence completed 1702 that became the **Beitang** (about 39.9236, 116.3766), used here. The substance stands **[A]**: he never went to China, and read the hexagrams as confirmation of a universal rational order.

**[A: Wikipedia; Perkins; SEP]** 1703: *Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire*, composed at Hanover, crediting Bouvet and presenting the hexagrams as an ancient Chinese anticipation of binary notation; binary arithmetic itself dates from about 1679. **Correction:** the Académie's *Mémoires* volume **for 1703** was printed only in **1705**, a standard lag. 1703 is composition and session; 1705 is circulation.

## 6. The priority dispute, 1699-1716

**[A: Wikipedia, *Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy*]** 1699: **Nicolas Fatio de Duillier** publicly accuses him of plagiarizing Newton.

### Contradiction 6: the anonymous review and *De Quadratura Curvarum*

The pool has Newton publishing *De Quadratura Curvarum* in 1704 "partly in response" to an anonymous review implying he took the fluxional calculus from Leibniz. Causality inverted: *De Quadratura* appeared **1704**, and the anonymous *Acta Eruditorum* review, widely attributed to Leibniz, was a review **of it**, in the January **1705** issue. Folded into the Crane Court stop's context rather than given a stop.

### Contradiction 7: Keill's accusation, date and nature

The pool dated **John Keill**'s charge to 1708 and to 1708/1710 in different lenses, and asserted Newton's tacit approval. MacTutor places the quoted phrase in **1711**, in Keill's *reply* to Leibniz's formal complaint, not in an earlier review, and does **not** say the accusation carried Newton's blessing. The stop is dated **1711** and the claim of approval dropped. The phrase itself is verbatim in MacTutor, internal ellipsis included.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1710: the *Essais de Théodicée*, Amsterdam, his only full-length philosophical book published in his lifetime. 1713: **Johann Bernoulli** enters on his side from Basel.

**[A: Wikipedia; SEP]** 1712: the Royal Society committee, effectively controlled by Newton, issues the **Commercium Epistolicum**. **Coordinates:** the pool gave 51.5099, -0.118, which is **Carlton House Terrace, the Society's home only since 1967**, an anachronism of 255 years. In 1712 the Society sat at **Crane Court**, Fleet Street, bought 26 October 1710. The file inherits the canonical London pin **51.5074, -0.1278** byte-identical from `newton.journey.json`.

## 7. Vienna, Hanover, and the end, 1712-1717

**[A: Wikipedia]** December 1712 to September 1714: Vienna, **Imperial Court Councillor**. 1714: composes the ninety-paragraph summary later titled the **Monadologie**, published only posthumously; that it was written for Prince Eugene of Savoy is **[R]**, widely repeated and not documented in anything reachable here. The same year Georg Ludwig becomes **King George I**, the court moves to London, and Leibniz is forbidden to follow until the Brunswick history is complete, which it never is. The Electress **Sophia** dies that June.

**[A: Wikipedia]** June 1716: **Bad Pyrmont**, the meeting with **Tsar Peter the Great**, who appointed him an advisor in 1711 and whose Russian projects he had advised on since about 1708. Coordinates corrected to 51.9836, 9.2484 (Schloss Pyrmont); the meeting is documented at the spa town, not specifically inside the castle.

**[A: Wikipedia; German Wikipedia, *Leibnizhaus (Hannover)*]** Dies 14 November 1716 in the house on the Schmiedestraße occupied since 29 September 1698, of gout and a self-prescribed remedy. Buried in the **Neustädter Hof- und Stadtkirche St. Johannis**. Neither George I nor any courtier apart from his secretary Eckhart attends; neither the Royal Society nor the Berlin Academy marks the death; the grave is unmarked for more than fifty years.

### Contradiction 8: three sets of coordinates for one church

The pool gave the Neustädter Kirche **three positions**: 52.3721, 9.7314 (geography lens) and 52.3739, 9.7367 (afterlife lens, twice). German Wikipedia gives **52.3711, 9.7286**, at the Neustädter Markt in the Calenberger Neustadt. The afterlife value lands across the Leine, about 650 m off, the worst single coordinate error in the pool. All three reconciled to 52.3711, 9.7286. **[R]** The wartime fate of the grave marker: bomb damage to the church in 1943 and postwar restoration are well attested, but what became of the OSSA LEIBNITII plaque was confirmed by no source reached, and the journey file does not assert it.

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1717: **Fontenelle**, secretary of the French Academy of Sciences, delivers the éloge no institution Leibniz served offered him. **Gap:** the session date was not fixed in reachable sources; the stop is dated 1717-01-01 and says so.

## 8. Quotations: what is verbatim and what was not

Every quote was checked against the wording its source prints. Four failed and were repaired.

1. **Monadology 33.** The pool's "those of reasoning are necessary; those of fact are contingent" is a **compression Leibniz did not write**. Wikiquote's full two-sentence wording is used.
2. **The unwritten knowledge.** The pool trimmed the opening clause and changed "anything we find in books" to "anything found in books." Restored to Wikiquote's text.
3. **The 1673 letter to Oldenburg.** The pool's version, correctly tagged **[R]** at gathering, proved a paraphrase with a clause dropped without ellipsis. Verbatim text used; tag moves to **[A]**.
4. **The *ex nihilo* binary passage** to Duke Rudolph Augustus is **verbatim** in Wikipedia's *Binary number*, but Wikipedia **gives no date**. The pool's 1697 is impossible on its own chronology, the hexagrams having arrived after 1701. Date **dropped**; quote_source reads "undated in source; after 1701."

Verified and used as given: the *characteristica universalis* passages; "this restitution of their meaning"; the Conti letter of 9 April 1716; Keill's "pretty plain indications..." redated to 1711; the funeral witness's "buried more like a robber." **Flagged:** Newton's "not a single previously unsolved problem was solved," in MacTutor with no citation to the *Commercium Epistolicum*, so attributed to MacTutor.

## 9. Interlocks with existing atlas files

Named in campas because Leibniz has a real relation to the person, not because a pin coincides:

- **`newton`**: the priority dispute, documented on both sides. `newton.journey.json` names Leibniz in its 1712 Commercium stop and supplies the canonical London pin **51.5074, -0.1278**, inherited byte-identical here.
- **`rene_descartes`**: read in Paris at Huygens's direction; Leibniz spent decades attacking the Cartesian measure of force.
- **`leonard_euler`**: took the mathematics directorship of the Berlin Academy Leibniz founded, working throughout in Leibniz's notation; `leonard_euler.journey.json` itself dates the Society to 1700 with Leibniz as first president.
- **`gilles_deleuze`**: *Le Pli: Leibniz et le baroque* (Minuit, 1988), a whole book on Leibniz.

**Deliberately not named:** `gauss`, `karl_marx`, `mary_wigman`, `savitri_devi` (shared Hanover pin only); `brothers_grimm` (role echo); `merce_cunningham`, `xul_solar` (shared I Ching material, no relation to Leibniz); `edmond_halley` (Royal Society proximity, no documented contact). `kant` was considered, his first manuscript being on the Cartesian-Leibnizian *vis viva* quarrel, and left out only because no stop falls where it would not intrude.

## 10. Honest gaps

- **No Paris address.** Four formative years, residence unrecorded. All Paris stops but one use the city pin.
- **The Nuremberg alchemical society:** no meeting house, no roll.
- **Antognazza, Aiton, Perkins** reached only through relay. Anything resting on them alone is flagged in place, including the Vatican Library offer.
- **The Academy éloge date** (1717) is not fixed. **The burial date:** the interval between death on 14 November 1716 and the funeral is not fixed in reachable sources; the stop is dated to the days following and says so.
- **Correspondent count:** estimates run from about 600 to over 1100. Not resolved; the file claims no precision.
