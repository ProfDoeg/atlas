# Leon Black: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-31 for `leon_black.journey.json`. Subject living (b. 31 July 1951): no death,
no tomb, no posthumous reception. What stands in for an afterlife is naming gifts, litigation
still running, and a biography not yet published.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or press folklore,
unverified against a primary record · **[CORRECTED]** the working dossier is wrong and the
correction is stated, not smoothed over · **[CONFLICT]** sources disagree and this file does not
pick.

Roughly half the corrections below come from reading the primary documents the dossier had only
summarised: the **Dechert LLP memorandum** (SEC EDGAR, Apollo Form 8-K of 25 January 2021,
accession 0001193125-21-016405, **Ex-99.1**) and **Black's own letters** (Ex-99.2 of the same 8-K;
the October 2020 investor letter). They disagree, repeatedly, with the secondary account of
themselves.

"as I near my 70th birthday this July"]**. **Birthplace is an honest gap:** the Wikipedia
`birth_place` field is *empty* (checked via `action=raw`) and no source reached names a city,
hospital, or neighbourhood. The file pins a general Manhattan point and says so. **[R]**

Father **Eli M. Black**, born **Elihu Menashe Blachowicz, Lublin, 9 April 1921**, ordained rabbi,
later chairman of **United Brands**, successor to United Fruit **[A: Wikipedia, 'Eli Black']**.

**Dartmouth, AB in philosophy and history, 1973** **[A: Wikipedia]**. **[CORRECTED]** The pool

**Peat Marwick and Boardroom Reports** **[A: Wikipedia, citing Melby and Perlberg, *Bloomberg

**The Lehman rejection** ("lacked the brains or personality for Wall Street") **[R: recurs in

## 2. Drexel (1977–1990)

**Joins Drexel Burnham Lambert 1977; managing director, head of M&A, co-head of corporate finance,
under Michael Milken's high-yield network** **[A: Wikipedia; background in Bruck and Stewart]**.

**[CORRECTED: the pool's worst single error here.]** It said Drexel "pleads no contest … and, in
1990, files for bankruptcy." Two events, one mis-described: **December 1988**, an **Alford plea**
(guilty in law, innocence maintained) to **six felonies**, three counts stock parking and three
stock manipulation, with a **$650m** fine **[A: Wikipedia, 'Drexel Burnham Lambert']**, which is
not *nolo contendere*; then **13 February 1990**, Chapter 11 **[A]**. Black was never charged in
either matter **[A: no source reached shows any charge]**.

**Geography note:** the file pins Drexel at 60 Broad Street **[A as headquarters]**, but Milken's
desk ran out of Beverly Hills and no source places Black there. Flagged, not resolved.

## 3. Apollo (1990–2011)

**Founded 1990 with Joshua Harris, Marc Rowan, and Antony Ressler; first fund ~$400m within about
six months** **[A: Wikipedia; SEC EDGAR]**. **[CORRECTED]** The pool named only Harris and Rowan;
**Ressler** is restored.

**[CORRECTED: coordinate collision.]** The pool gave the Apollo founding office as `40.7614,
-73.9776`, **character-for-character the MoMA pin** used three other times in the same pool: two
unrelated stops as one dot. The file uses **9 West 57th Street, the Solow Building, `40.7638,
-73.9750`**, confirmed on the letterhead of Black's own 2021 letter. The 1990 address itself is
undocumented and declared.

**Executive Life.** **[CORRECTED: substance, not just the pin.]** The pool had Apollo and Lion
Advisors *buying* the junk-bond portfolio. They did not. The purchaser of record, **November 1991,
$3.25 billion**, was **Altus Finance**, a Crédit Lyonnais unit later shown to be the real buyer
through secret agreements; **Apollo and Lion managed the portfolio and shared its gains**, and
California's expanded complaint later named Apollo parties **[A: Wikipedia, 'Executive Life
Insurance Company'; California AG, "Lockyer Expands Lawsuit…"]**. The pool's caveat that Black was
never criminally convicted here is correct and kept; the coordinate is a declared LA city-centre
placeholder.

**Melanoma Research Alliance, $25m, 2007, after Debra Black's diagnosis, under the Milken
Institute** **[A: curemelanoma.org]**. **Elysium Management, family office, 2008, one employee
growing to ~ten by 2014** **[A: Dechert]**; street address undocumented.

**Hexion / Huntsman, settled 14 December 2008** **[A: primary: Hexion Form 8-K filed 2008-12-15,
accession 0000898822-08-001236]**: $325m termination fee, $425m cash from the Apollo affiliates,
$250m of Huntsman convertible notes, **$1bn aggregate**; Huntsman terminated 13 December.

**[REJECTED: "Black personally agreed to contribute up to $60 million."]** Appears twice in the
pool and is false. The Settlement Agreement (Ex-10.1), which Black **signs as a party**, states at
¶3: *"The Apollo Parties (except for Leon Black and Joshua J. Harris) and the Hexion Parties
(except for Craig O. Morrison) are jointly and severally liable for the payment of all sums due to
Huntsman."* No $60m figure appears anywhere in it. The **$60m in the Black record is Epstein's fee
demand** on the step-up-basis transaction (Dechert Ex-99.1, pp.18–19): a cross-contamination of two
unrelated figures. The file drops the sentence and says why.

**[CONFLICT, not adjudicated]** Deal size: **$6.5bn** (the July 2007 merger agreement) vs
**$10.6bn** (enterprise value including assumed debt). Both circulate; the file names both and says
which measure each is.

**IPO.** **[CORRECTED]** Priced **29 March 2011 at $19.00**, 424B4 filed 30 March, raising ~$565m
**[A: Wikipedia; accession 0001193125-11-082478]**. The pool's **5 April** belongs to a reported
NYSE bell-ringing ceremony that could not be verified and is not used. **Epstein's Financial Trust
Company bought 263,257 shares in the offering, March 2011** **[A: Dechert Ex-99.1, p.22; the count
is exact in the primary document]**.

**Omitted with reason:** the **Harrah's / Caesars** buyout (2008, with TPG) is attested, but the
pool gives its place only as "United States." Rather than invent a pin, it is left out of the
journey and recorded here.

## 6. The fee (2012–2018)

**[CORRECTED: the retainer date.]** The pool dated the formal retainer to 2012. Dechert (Ex-99.1,
pp.5, 15) is different: work on the 2006 **grantor retained annuity trust** problem begins **late
2012**, but the **written services agreement, between Black and Southern Trust Company, Inc., was
drafted by Black's counsel and signed 13 February 2013**. First payment **$15m on 15 February 2013**;
second **$8.5m on 15 October 2013**.

**$10m to Gratitude America, October 2015** **[A: Dechert]**. **Donation to ASU's Origins Project
at Epstein's request** **[A: Dechert]**; no date and no amount in the pool, so the file's 2014 is a
declared ordering placeholder. This is the weakest dated stop in the file and says so.

**[CORRECTED: the 2017 loans.]** The pool: "January 2017, $30.5m, repaid $10m before his death."
Dechert (Ex-99.1, p.17): **two loans, $22.5m and $8m, "in early 2017"**, no month, so January is a
placeholder and not a fact, from **BV70 LLC** to **Plan D LLC**, in connection with an art
transaction. **Black demanded repayment in full in early 2018**; Epstein repaid **only $10m**,
leaving ~**$20.5m** outstanding. The repayment was **2018**, not vaguely "before his death."

**Last documented fee payment, April 2017.** **[CONFLICT, both figures kept]** Dechert: **$158m** in
fees 2012–April 2017. Bank **Suspicious Activity Reports** later reviewed by Senate Finance staff:
**~$170m** in transfers. **Direct communication ends October 2018** **[A: Dechert]**.

**[CORRECTED: the "60-cent dollars" quotation.]** The dossier attributed *"sixty-cent dollars"* to
the January 2021 Dechert report. Spelling and source both wrong. The attested form is **"60-cent
dollars"**, from **Black's own opening statement to the House Oversight Committee, 26 June 2026**,
where he added: *"What I believed to be $95 million of net fees paid to him over five years was
actually $158 million"* **[A: Quartz, 26 June 2026]**. Searches for "sixty-cent dollars" return
nothing; the only page carrying that spelling is registration-walled, so **the Dechert attribution
is dropped as unverified**.

**[CORRECTED: Senate Finance wording.]** The pool paraphrased. The release's own sentence: *"The
rates Black paid Epstein were 30 times higher than he paid the elite tax and estate advisors he
already employed."* **[A: finance.senate.gov, 23 March 2026]**. Quoted verbatim in the file.

**Not used:** *"avoid public disclosure … maximize deductions."* A fragment of an email reached only
through a secondhand summary of Wyden's letter, with neither the email nor the letter PDF obtained
**[R]**. Too thin to quote; recorded here so it is not lost.

## 7. Exposure and departure (2019–2021)

**Epstein arrested 6 July 2019; dead in custody 10 August 2019** **[A]**. Black's initial public
characterisation of his contact as **limited** **[A: dossier]** contradicts the $158m accounting
Dechert produced eighteen months later. **The contradiction is recorded, not resolved:** no source
explains how he arrived at "limited," and the file lets the two statements stand side by side.

**Investor letter, 12 October 2020** **[A: primary text at DocumentCloud doc. 7225038]**.
**[CORRECTED: punctuation.]** The pool rendered the best-known sentence with commas; the original
uses em dashes: *"With the benefit of hindsight—and knowing everything that has come to light about
Mr. Epstein's despicable conduct more than fifteen years ago—I deeply regret having had any
involvement with him."* The journey file quotes a different verbatim line from the same letter
(*"Apollo never did any business with Mr. Epstein, at any point in time"*), so no em dash enters the
file; the regret sentence is paraphrased in the campa and recorded verbatim here.

**Dechert memorandum delivered 22 January 2021, filed 25 January** **[A]**. Findings: $158m in fees
2012–April 2017; no evidence Black, Elysium, or Apollo personnel participated in Epstein's criminal
activity; no evidence Epstein introduced or offered to introduce Black to an underage woman.
**Recorded as what the review concluded**, by counsel the board retained and paid, not as an
adjudication. The pool's HTTP 403 flag on the exhibit no longer applies: it was read directly.

**Black's letter of 25 January 2021** **[A: Ex-99.2]**. **[CORRECTED: the pledge.]** The pool
described "$200 million toward initiatives for women, girls, and anti-trafficking efforts." The
letter says *"$200 million towards initiatives that seek to achieve **gender equality** and protect
and empower women, including supporting maternal health, women in finance and the arts … as well as
helping survivors of domestic violence, sexual assault and human trafficking."* **"Girls" does not
appear**, and anti-trafficking is the last item in a long list, not the headline.

**22 March 2021: departure accelerated to immediate; Black resigns as CEO and chairman; Marc Rowan
succeeds** **[A: Wikipedia]**. Apollo attributed the timing to health issues affecting Black and
his wife; the same month, Guzel Ganieva's allegations became public. The file records the stated
reason without adjudicating it, and the coincidence of timing as a coincidence of timing.

**MoMA chairmanship: elected May 2018, effective 1 July 2018; declined reelection, term ended 1 July
2021, amid MoMA Divest protests** **[A]**. Canonical MoMA pin `40.7614, -73.9776` inherited
byte-identical from `mark_lombardi.journey.json`.
