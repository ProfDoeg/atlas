# Nicolaus Copernicus (Mikolaj Kopernik), 1473-1543: research report
*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `nicolaus_copernicus.journey.json`. Calendar: **Julian** throughout the life (the Gregorian correction he was consulted about in 1513 arrives in 1582, thirty-nine years after his death); afterlife dates from 1582 onward are Gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or dossier assertion not independently confirmed. Contradictions between sources are flagged and left standing. Coordinate corrections carried out of the verification pass are recorded here and applied in the journey file.

---

## 1. Torun and the guardianship, 1473-1491

- Born **19 February 1473** in Torun (Thorn), Royal Prussia, within the Crown of the Kingdom of Poland, to the copper merchant Nicolaus Koppernigk and Barbara Watzenrode, a Torun patrician family. **[A, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy (SEP), 'Nicolaus Copernicus'; Wikipedia, 'Nicolaus Copernicus']**
- **The birthplace is disputed and the journey file tags it R.** English Wikipedia says only that the house at ul. Kopernika 15/17 "is considered by many historians to be the birthplace". Polish Wikipedia (`Dom Mikolaja Kopernika w Toruniu`) records that Krzysztof Mikulski argues for the family's more prestigious house at Rynek Staromiejski 36, demolished in the nineteenth century, and that before 1881 ul. Kopernika 40 was the accepted site. **Not resolved.**
- **Coordinate correction.** The dossier gave 53.0103/18.6043 for the birth house. That is essentially the Copernicus monument's coordinate on the Old Town Market Square, roughly 120 m north of the museum house, which is at **53.00922/18.60394**. Corrected in the file.
- Baptism at the font of St John's is **[R]**, tradition only; no baptismal register entry survives. MacTutor treats the early schooling and church attendance as proposals, not documented attendance.
- Father dies **1483**; guardianship passes to the maternal uncle **Lucas Watzenrode the Younger**, elevated **Prince-Bishop of Warmia in 1489**. **[A, Wikipedia]** Everything downstream (schooling, canonry, Italy) follows from this.
- **Wloclawek cathedral school, c. 1488: [R].** MacTutor asserts that "around 1488" his uncle sent him there for a year of preparatory study; no enrolment record names him. **Coordinate correction:** Wloclawek Cathedral is at **52.661/19.068**, not the dossier's 52.6493/19.0678, which lands ~1.3 km south, past the Vistula bank.

## 2. Krakow, 1491-1495

- Matriculated **winter 1491/92**, registered as *Nicolaus Nicolai de Thuronia*; studied arithmetic, geometry, optics, cosmography, and the astronomical tables during the university's strong mathematical period. **No recorded degree.** **[A, SEP; Wikipedia]** Coordinates for the Collegium Maius (50.0614/19.9328) verified as sound.
- Left for his uncle's court in Warmia in **1495**, still without a degree. **[A, Wikipedia]**
- The canonry in the Warmia cathedral chapter was secured **20 October 1497, by proxy, while he was already in Italy**. **[A, Wikipedia]** This is the financial engine of the whole life and it is worth stating flatly: he is paid, from age twenty-four to age seventy, by an institution he was largely absent from for the first decade.

## 3. Italy, 1496-1503

- **Bologna, enrolled 19 October 1496** in the German nation of law students, registered for canon law, lodging in the household of astronomy professor **Domenico Maria Novara**. **[A, MacTutor; SEP; Wikipedia]** The dossier gives the stay as 1496-1500; MacTutor and Wikipedia extend it into spring 1501. **Flagged, not resolved.**
- **Occultation of Aldebaran by the Moon, 9 March 1497**, observed with Novara: his earliest securely dated observation. **[A, Wikipedia]** The observing site within Bologna is not recorded.
- **Rome, Jubilee year 1500.** Rheticus's later report that he lectured on mathematics there is the only evidence; no venue, no title, no hearer's account. **[A for the visit; R for the lectures' content]** Lunar eclipse observed **5-6 November 1500**, MacTutor gives the night of 6 November, Wikipedia 5-6 November. **Minor discrepancy flagged.**
- **Return to Warmia, 1501**, to obtain further leave from the chapter for medical study. **[A, Wikipedia]**
- **Padua, 1501-1503**, medicine; Greek improved here; **no medical degree taken**. **[A, Wikipedia; Britannica]** **Coordinate correction:** Palazzo Bo is at **45.4093/11.8762**; the dossier's 45.4066 sits ~300 m south.
- **Ferrara, doctorate in canon law, 31 May 1503**, his only securely documented degree. **[A, MacTutor; Britannica]**
  - **Venue correction, and it matters.** The dossier pinned this on **Palazzo Paradiso**. That is anachronistic: per it.wikipedia `Palazzo Paradiso`, Cardinal Ippolito II d'Este leased the building to the magistrato dei Savi only in **1567** so the university faculties could move in, sixty-four years after the degree. The examination cannot be staged there. The file re-anchors on the cathedral quarter (**44.8358/11.6194**), where doctoral examinations were held in 1503, and says so in `date_confidence`.
- **Wroclaw sinecure**, collegiate church of the Holy Cross and St Bartholomew, Ostrow Tumski: granted by a document **dated at Padua, 10 January 1503**, held until **1538**. **[A, Wikipedia]** No travel to Wroclaw is documented; the benefice appears to be pure absentee income. **Coordinate correction:** Ostrow Tumski is at **51.115444/17.044272**; the dossier's 51.1105/17.0396 falls ~750-800 m southwest, off Cathedral Island entirely.

## 4. Lidzbark Warminski and the first printed book, 1503-1512

- Lived mainly at his uncle's episcopal castle as **physician, secretary, and diplomatic aide**, riding with Watzenrode to the Prussian diets. **[A, Britannica; MacTutor; Wikipedia]** MacTutor's judgement that the decisive work on the Earth's motion probably began in these years is an inference; **no triggering incident is documented**, and the journey file says so.
- **c. 1509:** Haller's press in Krakow prints his Latin prose translation from Greek of **Theophylact Simocatta's** moral, rustic, and amatory letters, dedicated to Watzenrode: his first printed book, and not astronomy. **[A, Wikipedia; Britannica]** The exact press location within Krakow is approximate.

## 5. Frombork, 1512-1516

- **Settlement at Frombork.** The dossier's flat "1510" is wrong as a single anchor. Wikipedia gives settlement as "1510 or 1512" and dates the grant of his **external curia to early June 1512**, after **Watzenrode's death on 29 March 1512**. The file uses **1512-06** and states the ambiguity. **[A with the range flagged]**
- **Systematic coordinate error, ~1.2 km, affecting every Frombork pin in the dossier.** Frombork Cathedral hill is at **54.357083/19.681667** (en.wikipedia, 'Frombork Cathedral'); the town centroid is 54.350/19.683. The dossier's 54.3477-54.3486 / 19.6702-19.6714 values lie outside the chapter precinct altogether. **All Frombork stops in the journey file use 54.3571/19.6817.**
- **Calendar consultation: redated 1514 → 1513.** Wikipedia dates the Fifth Lateran Council's calendar-reform consultation to **1513**. **The source the dossier cited does not support the claim it was attached to:** SEP says only that Copernicus had "limited involvement" in calendar reform, evidenced by "a no longer extant letter". It does not date an invitation, name a commission, or say he judged reform premature. The file carries the 1513 date, keeps the lost letter, and states in prose that the "premature" gloss has no surviving text behind it. **[A for the consultation; R for the content of his answer]**
- **Tower purchased within the chapter's defensive wall, 1514**, used for residence and for naked-eye observation (quadrant, triquetrum, armillary sphere) until 1543; more than half of his sixty-odd recorded observations were made at Frombork. **[A, Wikipedia]**
- **Commentariolus.** Fixed to **"by 1514"** by a library catalogue dated **1 May 1514**; seven postulates; circulated privately; never printed in his lifetime; the surviving manuscript copy found in Vienna and published **1878**. **[A, Wikipedia, 'Commentariolus'; SEP]**

## 6. The administrator and the economist, 1516-1526

- **Economic administrator of the chapter estates**, Olsztyn (Allenstein) and **Pieniezno (Mehlsack)**, 1516-1521, recording deserted and reassigned peasant holdings in the register *Locationes mansorum desertorum*, in his own hand. **[A, Wikipedia]**
  - **The dossier under-tagged Pieniezno.** Wikipedia states outright that he was "economic administrator of Warmia, including Olsztyn (Allenstein) and Pieniezno (Mehlsack)". The district is named in the record; only the individual journeys are uncited. **Coordinate correction:** Pieniezno is at **54.217/20.117**; the dossier's 54.2378 is ~2.3 km north of the town.
- **January 1520:** Teutonic Order raid devastates Frombork; instruments probably destroyed. **[A for the raid; R for the fate of the instruments]**
- **16 November 1520, letter to King Sigismund I** asking protection for Olsztyn: **[R]**, dossier assertion, no fetched source confirmed the date, and the text was not reachable. Kept, flagged in `date_confidence`.
- **January 1521, defence of Olsztyn Castle.** The dossier's hedge cut the wrong way. Wikipedia's `Olsztyn Castle` states that the administrator "was Nicolaus Copernicus, who organized and led the Polish defense". The file therefore attests **command of a fortified defence** and denies only **personal generalship in the open field**, which is the part later Polish memory adds. **[A]**
- **1522, Diet of Grudziadz:** presented currency-reform proposals; not adopted. **Upgraded from R to A** on MacTutor, which states this directly. Coordinates 53.4837/18.7536 verified correct.
- **1517:** early formulation of a quantity theory of money; **1526:** *Monetae cudendae ratio*, stating that debased coin drives out sound coin. **[A, Wikipedia; SEP notes the monetary writings]** The principle is now called Gresham's law; the file says only that it carries an Englishman's name, and asserts no priority dispute.
- **3 June 1524, Letter against Werner**, to Bernard Wapowski, on Johann Werner's handling of precession. **[A, Wikipedia; Britannica]**

## 7. The last decade, 1531-1543

- **Anna Schilling.** What the sources fetched this pass support is narrow: "from at least 1531 until 1539 his relations with Anna Schilling, a live-in housekeeper, were seen as scandalous by **two** bishops of Warmia who urged him over the years to break off relations" (Wikipedia). **The dossier's specifics do not survive verification:** a December 1538 date, Dantiscus acting through intermediaries, and a written promise to comply are supported by nothing fetched, and **SEP does not discuss Schilling or Dantiscus at all**. The file gives the span, notes two bishops, and states that no letter, testimony, or verdict survives. **[A for the span and the pressure; the sexual relationship is neither attested nor refuted, and is left open]**
- **1533, Rome:** **Johann Albrecht Widmanstetter** explains the heliocentric system to Clement VII and the cardinals, apparently to a favourable reception. **The dossier called him a Jesuit; he was not.** Per en.wikipedia, `Johann Albrecht Widmanstetter`, he was a humanist and orientalist who became secretary to Clement VII in 1533; he lived 1506-1557 and the Society of Jesus was not founded until 1540. Corrected accordingly, and he lectured to the pope **and** the cardinals. **[A as corrected]** This is his travel, not Copernicus's, so it gets no stop; it sits inside the Frombork campa for the Schoenberg letter.
- **Cardinal Nikolaus von Schoenberg's letter** urging publication and offering to pay for a fair copy, later printed at the front of *De revolutionibus*: **letter [A]; the date 1 November 1536 [R]**, the full Wikipedia article on *De revolutionibus* does not give the date, and nothing fetched this pass confirmed it.
- **1537:** one of four candidates for the see of Warmia; **Johannes Dantiscus**, already coadjutor, is elected. **[A]**, but **on Wikipedia, not SEP**: the SEP entry contains nothing on the candidacy, contrary to the dossier's citation.
- **1538:** relinquishes the Wroclaw sinecure held since 1503. **[A]**
- **May 1539: Rheticus arrives at Frombork**, a twenty-five-year-old Lutheran professor of mathematics appointed at Wittenberg by Melanchthon in 1536, and stays two years as the only known direct pupil. **[A, Wikipedia, 'Georg Joachim Rheticus', which gives the month]**
- **1540:** Rheticus's *Narratio prima* printed at **Gdansk**, the first printed exposition of the system. **1542:** the trigonometric section printed separately at **Wittenberg** as *De lateribus et angulis triangulorum*. **[A]** Copernicus went to neither city; both are handled inside a Frombork stop rather than staged at presses he never entered.
- **1541, Konigsberg:** summoned by Duke Albrecht of Prussia to treat his councillor Georg von Kunheim. **[R]**, dossier assertion, not independently confirmed this pass. Kept as a stop, with the R status stated in `date_confidence`.
- **1543, Nuremberg:** Petreius prints about **400 copies**; **Andreas Osiander**, a Lutheran preacher, inserts the unsigned *Ad lectorem* framing the hypotheses as calculational devices, ahead of Copernicus's own dedication to Paul III. **[A, Wikipedia, 'De revolutionibus orbium coelestium']** The Osiander quotation in the file is labelled in `quote_source` as **not Copernicus's words**.
- **Death at Frombork, 24 May 1543, aged 70.** The date is conventional and rests on early tradition; no death certificate survives. **[A as convention, with the qualification stated]**

### The deathbed copy: a contradiction left standing

- **SEP** states flatly that Copernicus "held a copy of the finished work on his deathbed".
- **Tiedemann Giese's near-contemporary report**, as summarized in the biographical literature, instead describes memory and mental vigour failing for **many days before death**.
- No delivery record and no eyewitness account of the dramatic same-day scene has been produced, and **Giese's letter itself could not be read in a primary text this pass**.

The file does not reconcile them: the campa says the copy reached Frombork "or does not", names the tradition as a tradition, and puts the contradiction in `date_confidence`. The 1542 stroke is likewise **[R]**, resting on Giese.

## 8. Afterlife

- **5 March 1616:** Congregation of the Index suspends *De revolutionibus* pending correction of **nine sentences** presenting heliocentrism as certain rather than hypothetical; corrections approved **1620**; removed from the Index by **Benedict XIV in 1758**. **[A, Wikipedia]**
- **Later editions:** Basel 1566 (Petrus), Amsterdam 1617 (Mulerius), a Warsaw Polish translation of 1854 restoring Copernicus's own preface in place of Osiander's, a Thorn German translation of 1873. **[A]** No stops; recorded here.
- **Autograph manuscript** of *De revolutionibus* survives at the **Jagiellonian Library, Krakow, BJ 10 000**. **[A]** **Coordinate correction:** the library is at al. Mickiewicza 22, **50.0615/19.9236**; the dossier's 19.9366 put the pin near the Main Market Square, about a kilometre east. **No stop of its own, because no sourced date for its arrival in Krakow was obtainable this pass**; it is cited in the campa and `suggested_refs` of the Matejko stop instead. **A stated gap.**
- **Warsaw monument**, Thorvaldsen, unveiled **1830**, before the Staszic Palace; inscriptions in Latin and Polish. Nazi plaque installed 1939, **torn down by Maciej Aleksy Dawidowski on 11 February 1942**; statue removed toward Nysa after the 1944 Uprising to be melted down, recovered, reunveiled **22 July 1949**. **[A, Wikipedia, 'Nicolaus Copernicus Monument, Warsaw']**
- **Torun monument**, Tieck, Fischer foundry Berlin, completed **1853**, committee formed 1839, largest donation from **Frederick William IV of Prussia**; Humboldt's Latin epigram *terrae motor, solis caelique stator*. **[A]** The Polish epigram "Wstrzymal Slonce, ruszyl Ziemie" is a later commemorative condensation, **not Copernicus's own words** **[R for the historiographic judgement; A for Humboldt's authorship of the Latin]**.
- **Matejko, *Astronomer Copernicus, or Conversations with God*, 1873**, bought by public subscription, hangs in the Collegium Novum. **[A]**
- **Grave.** Remains located by **Jerzy Gassowski's team, 2005**, near the altar of his canonry; **DNA identification announced November 2008**, matched to hairs in **Copernicus's own copy of Johannes Stoeffler's *Calendarium Romanum Magnum***, taken to Sweden as war booty in the mid-seventeenth century and held at the **Museum Gustavianum, Uppsala**; peer-reviewed publication **Bogdanowicz et al., PNAS 106 (2009): 12279-12282**. **[A]**
  - **The dossier's flagged "2008 vs 2009 discrepancy" is not one:** announcement 2008, publication 2009, two events.
  - **The dossier's "14 November 2010" reburial date is not supported by anything reachable and appears to be an error, not a source disagreement.** en.wikipedia `Frombork` gives reinterment on **Sunday 23 May 2010**; news coverage of the ceremony dates it **22 May 2010**. The ceremony ran across both days. **The real discrepancy is 22 vs 23 May, and it is left unresolved in the file's `date_confidence`.**
- **Copernicium**, element 112, proposed by the GSI team **14 July 2009** (originally symbol Cp, changed to **Cn**), confirmed by IUPAC **19 February 2010**, timed to the birth anniversary. **[A]**

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Copernicus is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stops are licensed on this file. Per the standing instruction, relations found but not rooted in his own life are reported here rather than silently deleted.

**Direction: forward (not licensable without the author's say-so; nothing of this was written into the file).**

- **`galileo_galilei`**, QUEUE.md's own row for Copernicus names the link: "the heliocentric model that galileo_galilei already queued was later condemned for defending", and Galileo's row cites Copernicus as "its direct predecessor". The 1633 condemnation postdates Copernicus's death by ninety years. Copernicus's file therefore carries the **1616 Index decree** (an event about his own book) but **names no later traveller** in any campa.
- **`johannes_kepler`**, Mastlin teaching "the system of Copernicus" privately at Tubingen c. 1591; *Mysterium Cosmographicum* (1596); *Epitome astronomiae Copernicanae* (1618-1621). All real, all forward, all Kepler's own stops.
- **`giordano_bruno`**, the 1583 Oxford disputation defending "the Copernican motion of the earth"; the London dialogues and the Ash Wednesday supper. Forward; Bruno's own stops.
- **`rene_descartes`**, the 1633 news of Galileo's abjuration over "a book teaching the motion of the earth", and the withdrawal of *Le Monde*. Forward, and two steps removed.
- **`christiaan_huygens`**, the Voorburg planetarium, "a mechanical model of the Copernican system in which the planets keep their true relative periods". Forward; Huygens's own stop.
- **`edmond_halley`**, indirect and thin: Halley's file names Kepler, not Copernicus. Not used.
- **`humboldt`**, caught late and handled. Alexander von Humboldt composed the Latin epigram *terrae motor, solis caelique stator* for the Torun monument's pedestal in 1853, which is a genuine one-directional relation (he wrote about Copernicus; the reverse is impossible) and it is attested. But `humboldt.journey.json` exists, so naming him in a campa on a 1543 file would be a forward pointer, new hash in an old block. **The epigram stays in the campa, which is a fact about Copernicus's own monument; Humboldt's authorship moves to `suggested_refs` on that stop.** The campa now credits "a naturalist in Berlin" without the name. If the author wants the name restored in prose, that is a forward grant and it is his to make.

If the author wants any of these carried, it needs a forward grant in `EXCEPTIONS.md` in his own words; an agent may not grant it.

**Direction: backward (licensed by the block rule itself, since the earlier artifact already existed; used once).**

- **`nicholas_of_cusa`**, *De docta ignorantia*, finished at Kues 12 February 1440, argues that the universe has no fixed centre or circumference and that the Earth is not at rest, arrived at from theology rather than astronomy. This is an old hash in a new block and is legal without any exception. **It is named once**, in the Commentariolus campa, and the campa states the limit of the evidence plainly: **Copernicus never cites Cusa, and no record puts the book in his hands.** The relation is a precursor claim in the scholarship, not an attested influence, and the file does not upgrade it.

**Rejected as mere co-location** (the map already shows co-location to a viewer): `john_dee` and `edward_kelley` at Krakow 1583-1584; `johann_faust` and the Krakow legend; `carl_menger` at the Collegium Maius in the 1860s; the Padua overlap of `nicholas_of_cusa` (1417-1423) and of `giordano_bruno` and Galileo (1591-1592). None is named in any campa.

**Canonical pins.** The interlock pass reported a negative result and it is repeated here as instructed: none of the canonical pins (Kaaba 21.4225/39.8262; Temple Mount 31.778/35.2354; Paris 48.8566/2.3522; Buenos Aires -34.6037/-58.3816) has any textual or biographical connection to Copernicus in the journey files, QUEUE.md, or the census. **None is inherited, and none was forced.** Copernicus never went to Paris, or anywhere south of Rome.

## Honest gaps

1. **No death certificate, no burial record, no baptismal record.** The two ends of the life are conventions built on early tradition.
2. **Giese's letter on the last months was not read in a primary text this pass.** Everything about the stroke and the deathbed rests on it, at one remove.
3. **The 1513 calendar letter is lost.** What he actually advised is unknown; the "premature" gloss is biographers' currency with no text behind it.
4. **The 1520 letter to Sigismund I** was not located; only the date circulating in biographies.
5. **The 1541 Konigsberg journey** is dossier-only.
6. **The Schoenberg letter's date (1 November 1536)** is dossier-only; the letter itself is solid.
7. **No sourced date for the autograph manuscript's arrival at the Jagiellonian Library**, so no stop was made for it.
8. **Nothing survives of the Anna Schilling household** beyond two bishops' displeasure. The file refuses both the romance and the exoneration.
9. **PNAS 106: 12279-82 was reachable only via PubMed Central**; the Wikipedia article 'Grave of Nicolaus Copernicus' 404'd on one WebFetch attempt and resolved on another. Both recorded as partial access.
10. **One quotation is not usable and was not used:** "There really are not any sphere in the heavens", attributed to Tycho's *Opera Omnia* by encyclopedia.com. The string is genuinely on that page, so it is not fabricated, but the English is corrupt (OCR, probably for "spheres") and no locus is given. **Dropped.** The Tycho quotation that is used, on Copernicus's method, reproduced cleanly.
