# Edmond Halley (1656-1742): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-07 for the Atlas of Journeys. Second Astronomer Royal, cataloguer of the southern sky, financier and editor of Newton's Principia, proposer of an Earth of nested magnetic shells, and the man who told the world a comet would come back.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by modern secondary paraphrase. Contradictions are flagged in place and gathered in section 11; none is adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. On the name: he signed himself Edmond, DNB heads its article "Halley, Edmund", and the commemorative namings use the Latinate form **[A: Wikipedia]**; the journey file uses Edmond and does not correct quoted or commemorative forms.

---

## 1. Haggerston and St Paul's, 1656-1673

- Born 8 November 1656 at Haggerston, parish of Shoreditch, son of a wealthy soap-maker and salter of the same name **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- Boyhood passion for astronomy, and his own instruments before university: **[R: MacTutor]**, settled tradition with no document named. Attested is the consequence, that he reached Queen's College already an accomplished observer with instruments **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- The father lost money in the Great Fire, 1666 **[A: MacTutor]**, dated to the year only; the journey file uses 1666-09-02, the Fire's first day, as a sort key and says so.
- St Paul's School; captain of the school at about fifteen, so about 1671 **[A: MacTutor]**. Mother Anne (née Robinson) died 1672 **[A: Wikipedia]**, no month or day.

**Gap.** Nothing on the household or the estate. The father appears twice in the whole pool: losing money in 1666, dying in 1684.

## 2. Oxford and Saint Helena, 1673-1678

- Entered The Queen's College, Oxford, July 1673 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- Corresponded with Flamsteed about errors in the tables and assisted at Greenwich, 1675 **[A: MacTutor]**, the year the Royal Observatory was founded. The chronology lens files this after the 1676 occultation; its own date is 1675 and the journey file sorts on the date. Occultation of Mars by the Moon, 11 June 1676, in the Philosophical Transactions: his first publication **[A: MacTutor]**.
- Left Oxford without a degree; sailed November 1676 for Saint Helena, backed by Charles II and the East India Company **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Set up an observatory and over roughly eighteen months fixed 341 southern stars and noted a cluster in Centaurus **[A: MacTutor]**. Returned May 1678 **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- First complete telescopic observation of a transit of Mercury, 7 November 1677 **[A: MacTutor]**. Seed of the 1716 transit method; the pool does not draw that line, and the journey file makes it as juxtaposition, not as a sourced claim.

**Contradiction of arithmetic, flagged.** November 1676 to May 1678 is eighteen months door to door and cannot also contain eighteen months ashore plus two ocean passages. Not adjudicated.

## 3. The Southern Tycho, 1678-1681

- Elected Fellow of the Royal Society 30 November 1678, aged 22, among the youngest to that date **[A: Wikipedia, giving the day; MacTutor gives a similar date]**. Oxford MA without examination by royal command, 3 December 1678, for the Saint Helena catalogue **[A: Wikipedia]**. Catalogus Stellarum Australium, 1679 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**; not consulted, the 341 count rests on two secondary sources agreeing.
- **"Southern Tycho."** Tagged **[R]** by the quotes lens as unlocated; the verifier upgraded it. Wikisource returns it in two period works on Halley: DNB says the catalogue "earned for him from Flamsteed the title of the 'Southern Tycho'", and EB1911 gives the same title in 1678 **[A: DNB; EB1911]**. Capitalise it Southern Tycho. DNB credits the coinage to Flamsteed without naming the document, so that attribution is itself reported **[R]**.
- Sent to Danzig 1679 to mediate the dispute over Hevelius's naked-eye observations **[A: Wikipedia]**; no month. European tour 1680: comet near Calais, work with Cassini in Paris, much of the following year in Italy **[A: MacTutor]**.

**Gap.** The Italian year of 1681 names no city in any reachable source, so there is no Italian stop: a real hole in the itinerary of a traveller whose atlas entry is a map.

## 4. Islington, the comet, and the Principia, 1682-1687

- Married Mary Tooke 1682; Islington; three children **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Observed the comet later named for him, September 1682 **[A: MacTutor]**.
- Presented on Kepler's third law and the inverse-square law to the Royal Society, 24 January 1684 **[A: MacTutor]**; the chronology lens lists this after the August visit and the dates order it. Father disappeared March 1684, found dead about five weeks later, circumstances never resolved **[A: MacTutor]**.
- Visited Newton at Cambridge August 1684 and asked what curve a planet traces under an inverse-square law; Newton answered an ellipse, and the exchange prompted Halley to press for the proof **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**. The atlas holds the scene already: newton.journey.json, "Trinity College, Halley's question", 52.207, 0.1146, 1684-08-01, August attested through De Moivre as recorded by Conduitt, day not given. Pin and date inherited byte for byte.
- Clerk of the Philosophical Transactions from 1685; world map of the trade winds, 1686, the first meteorological chart **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- Financed, edited and saw the Principia through the press, 1687, with a prefatory Latin ode **[A: MacTutor]**. Mechanism from the Newton file: the Society voted to print, found its budget spent on a history of fishes, and Halley took the whole cost on a clerk's salary, spending fifteen months on text and proofs and talking Newton out of suppressing Book III during the Hooke quarrel; Streater printed it in July 1687, licensed by Pepys **[A: newton.journey.json, from Royal Society Council minutes 1686]**.
- Newton's own testimony, from the Author's Preface (1729 Motte translation, Wikisource), used verbatim in the journey file **[A]**: Halley "not only assisted me with his pains in correcting the press and taking care of the schemes, but it was to his solicitations that its becoming publick is owing"; and, of the demonstrations, he "continually pressed me to communicate the same to the Royal Society".

**Correction from the verifier.** "the orbit of that Comet computed in an ellipsis by Dr. Halley" is genuine Newton but not in the body of the Principia; Wikisource returns it only in the 1729 prefatory matter, inside a list of additions, so it is a fragment of an enumeration and is not used as a quote in the journey file.

## 5. The chair refused, the hollow Earth, the Mint, 1691-1697

- Stood for the Savilian Chair of Astronomy 1691; opposed, reportedly in part by Flamsteed and on religious grounds; the chair went to David Gregory **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. The ecclesiastical opponents were Archbishop John Tillotson and Bishop Stillingfleet, objecting to his doubt that the Earth's age matched the biblical account **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Diving bell for prolonged underwater work, and marine compass design, 1691 **[A: Wikipedia]**. No location for the trials; the journey file pins London by default and says so.
- **The hollow Earth, 1692.** A Philosophical Transactions paper proposing an outer shell about 500 miles thick around two further nested concentric shells and an innermost core, each with its own magnetic poles, separated by luminous atmospheres, to explain anomalies in magnetic variation across place and time; escaping gas from the inner atmospheres produced the aurora, and the interior might be inhabited **[A as to substance: Philosophical Transactions 1692, summarised by Wikipedia, "Hollow Earth" and "Edmond Halley"]**. **The primary text was not reachable**, so Halley's wording, the 500-mile figure and the shells-plus-core structure could not be checked against anything he wrote **[R for the exact structure and figure]**.
- **A quotation that is not one.** "Halley put forth the idea of a hollow Earth consisting of a shell about 500 miles thick, two inner concentric shells and an innermost core" is a word-for-word lift of the current Wikipedia article, not Halley. Never set it in quotation marks as his. The only period wording reachable is DNB's, which quotes two words of him inside its own paraphrase: "He supposed auroræ to be occasioned by the escape of a 'luminous medium,' by which a subterranean globe was rendered habitable." **[A: DNB; only "luminous medium" is Halley's]**. The journey file uses that sentence and says in quote_source which two words are his.
- Breslau mortality tables, 1693 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**; he never went to Breslau, the work was done in London on another city's registers, and the journey file pins it in London. Papers of 1694 proposing a cometary impact as cause of the biblical Flood, too controversial, unpublished until 1724 **[A: Wikipedia]**. Deputy comptroller of the Chester branch of the Royal Mint from 1696, about two years, on Newton's recommendation **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.

## 6. The Paramour, 1698-1702

- Met Peter the Great during the Tsar's English visit, 1698 **[A: Wikipedia]**; the shipbuilding-and-science content of the conversation is **[R]**. Naval commission to command the pink Paramour the same year, with orders to "attempt the discovery of what land lies to the south of the western ocean" **[A: DNB, quoting the commission]**.
- First voyage November 1698, the first purely scientific voyage the English navy commissioned: South Atlantic, compass variation, winds and currents; cut short by the lieutenant's insubordination **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.
- After the court martial, recommissioned; sailed 24 August 1699, reached pack ice near the Falklands, surveyed the coasts of South America and Africa **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. His own account: "fell in with great islands of ice, of so incredible a height and magnitude that I scarce dare write my thoughts of it" **[A: DNB, glossing it as his run to 52 degrees south]**.
- Voyage ended 6 September 1700; the General Chart of the Variation followed **[A: Wikipedia]**. Third voyage 1701, tides and coastline of the English Channel **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. Sent by Queen Anne on harbour-fortification missions to the Adriatic and Trieste, 1702 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**.

**Gap.** No port of departure or return for any of the three voyages, and no coordinates for the southern limit beyond DNB's 52 degrees south. The journey file pins the ice stop at that latitude and calls the longitude a stand-in.

## 7. Savilian Professor of Geometry, 1703-1719

- Appointed Savilian Professor of Geometry, succeeding Wallis; well-received inaugural lecture **[A]**. **Contradiction:** Wikipedia dates it November 1703, MacTutor 1704. Oxford took him only after his theological opponents had died **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- Astronomiae Cometicae Synopsis, 1705: the comets of 1531, 1607 and 1682 share near-identical orbital elements, are one periodic body, and it will return about 1758 **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**. **Contradiction:** the chronology lens gives three apparitions, the afterlife lens four, adding 1456, both Wikipedia-derived; the journey file states the three and records the fourth in date_confidence. The Synopsis was not consulted.
- On priority, per DNB, he expected "candid posterity to acknowledge that this was first discovered by an Englishman" **[A: DNB, quoting Halley]**.
- Books V-VII of Apollonius's Conics from Arabic, 1706; complete Latin Conics and an honorary doctorate, 1710; the lost Book VIII reconstructed by him **[A: Wikipedia]**. Proper motion: Ptolemy's catalogue against current positions shows Sirius, Procyon and Arcturus have moved **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**; **contradiction**, publication 1718 against 1717 or 1718.
- Published Flamsteed's observations in 1712 against his wishes, with a critical preface, permanently damaging the relationship **[A: Wikipedia]**. Flamsteed in his own words: "lazy and malicious thief"; the catalogue "very effectually spoiled"; and to Arbuthnot, 29 March 1711, "the neglect of me, and the ill-usage I had met with, was a dishonour to the queen and the nation" **[A: DNB, "Flamsteed, John"]**. Proposed in 1716 that transits of Venus observed from widely separated points could measure the astronomical unit; carried out in 1761 and 1769 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 8. Astronomer Royal, 1720-1742

- One of the first attempts to date Stonehenge by astronomical and antiquarian reasoning, 1720 **[A: Wikipedia]**; no method detail, no resulting figure. Second Astronomer Royal on Flamsteed's death, 1720, until his own **[A: Wikipedia; MacTutor]**; no month.
- Lunar observations across a complete eighteen-year Saros to improve the Moon's tables **[A: Wikipedia]**, and kept the results, telling the Royal Society council on 2 March 1727 that he "had hitherto kept them in his own custody, that he might have time to finish the theory he designed to build upon them" **[A: DNB]**. That meeting falls two days after Newton's last presiding appearance and three weeks before Newton's death.
- Died at Greenwich, aged 85. **Contradiction, structural rather than clerical:** Wikipedia gives 25 January 1742 New Style, MacTutor 14 January 1742 Old Style **[A: both]**. England adopted the Gregorian calendar only in 1752, so Old Style is what Greenwich would have written. The journey file's calendar reads "gregorian" because the life falls after 1582; the eleven-day gap sits in the death stop's date_confidence and the file does not choose.
- Buried at old St Margaret, Lee, Blackheath, in the same vault as John Pond, with Nathaniel Bliss nearby in an unmarked grave; the church was later rebuilt and a marked grave identifying Halley remains **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 9. Afterlife

- Tombstone relocated by the Admiralty to the southern wall of the Camera Obscura at the Royal Observatory, half an hour's walk from the grave; a comet-shaped plaque in the South Cloister of Westminster Abbey **[A: Wikipedia]**. **Both undated in the pool**; the journey file gives each 1742-01-01 and says in date_confidence that the year is a sort placeholder.
- Recovered 25 December 1758 by Johann Georg Palitzsch, a farmer and amateur near Dresden; perihelion 13 March 1759, close to Clairaut's refined calculation allowing for Jupiter and Saturn, and described as the first demonstration that a body other than a planet orbits the Sun **[A: Wikipedia, "Halley's Comet"]**.
- **A legend, false.** The tradition that Halley was knighted is untrue; the error may trace to Norton's 1839 Elementary Treatise on Astronomy **[R: Wikipedia offers this as a possibility, not as documented transmission]**.
- The 16 November 1835 perihelion coincided with Samuel Clemens's premature birth at Florida, Missouri; the 20 April 1910 perihelion preceded his death at Stormfield by a day, as predicted **[A: mark_twain.journey.json]**.
- Halley Research Station, Edmund Halley Way, Road and Drive, Rue Edmund Halley, a lunar and a Martian crater; Giotto's closest recorded flyby of the nucleus, 14 March 1986; next return 28 July 2061 **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 11. Contradictions flagged, not resolved

**Death date**, 14 January 1742 OS (MacTutor) against 25 January 1742 NS (Wikipedia). **Savilian Chair of Geometry**, November 1703 (Wikipedia) against 1704 (MacTutor). **Apparitions in the Synopsis**, three against four, both Wikipedia. **Proper motion**, publication 1718 against 1717 or 1718. **Saint Helena arithmetic**, November 1676 to May 1678 cannot hold eighteen months ashore plus two passages. **1684 ordering**, the chronology lens puts the January presentation after the August visit and the dates settle it. **The Principia phrase**, real Newton but prefatory matter of 1729 and a fragment of a list. **The hollow-Earth sentence**, current Wikipedia prose submitted as a widely repeated summary, and it is neither Halley nor a summary. **Interlock coordinates**, four inherited pins disagree with the files the lens cites, and section 10 records which won.

## 12. Gaps stated as gaps

Stated in place above: the unread 1692 paper, the Italian year with no city, no ports for the Paramour voyages, no site for the diving-bell trials, no year for either commemoration, no method or figure for Stonehenge. Three more:

- Mary Tooke: named, married 1682, three children, nothing else. No death date, no names for the children, nothing about sixty years of an Islington household.
- The father's death, March 1684: "circumstances never fully resolved" is the whole of it.
- Halley's religious views: opposed as unorthodox in 1691, not opposed by 1703. What he believed is not in the pool, only what his opponents said of it.
