# Saint Pantaleon (Pantoleon; Panteleimon; San Pantaleone; Pantaleón de Nicomedia)
*Research report, 2026-08-08. Slug: `saint_pantaleon`.*

**Legend:** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

## 0. The condition of the file

There is a martyr and there is a Passio, and they are not the same object.

The **fact of a martyrdom** at Nicomedia in the Diocletianic persecution is defended by the Catholic Encyclopedia on three early witnesses: **Theodoret**, **Procopius**, and the **Martyrologium Hieronymianum** (§5). The **Lives** supplying everything anyone pictures (torches, molten lead, floating stone, beasts, wheel, melting sword, fruiting olive) are, in that same entry's words, **"the lives containing these legendary features are all late in date and valueless."** **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia (1913), 'St. Pantaleon']**

That is a contradiction inside the defending source: it keeps the martyrdom and discards every narrative of it. This report keeps both halves. **No Roman-era acta survive.** Everything narrative below is [R] unless an attestation is named.

## 1. Nicomedia: birth, medicine, the court

- Born at **Nicomedia** (modern İzmit) to **Eustorgius**, a wealthy pagan, and **Eubula**, a Christian. **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia; en.wikipedia 'Saint Pantaleon'; es.wikipedia]**
- **c. 275 is [R].** No document fixes it; every encyclopedia reports it unsourced. `0275-01-01` is a **pin-forcing placeholder**, hedged in `date_confidence`, never in prose.
- **Eubula instructs him in early childhood and dies young**; his pagan father raises him and the teaching lapses. **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia]** No ages given. **Gap.**
- **Birth name Pantoleon** ("in all things like a lion"); **Panteleimon** ("all-merciful") is **postmortem**, see §3. Studies medicine under **Euphrosinos**, then becomes **court physician**. **[A as hagiographic tradition: en.wikipedia; it.wikipedia]**
- **CONTRADICTION, NOT RESOLVED:** the Catholic Encyclopedia and en.wikipedia name **Maximian**; it.wikipedia names **Galerio**. Neither acknowledges the other. **Correction:** the pool's afterlife lens asserted "physician to co-emperor Galerius" as settled, which is the minority half of a split tradition; its own chronology lens handled it correctly. The journey states the split.

## 2. Conversion

- **Hermolaus** reconverts him. **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia; OrthodoxWiki]** **His rank is contested:** priest (Catholic Encyclopedia, OrthodoxWiki) versus "characterized as a bishop of the church at Nicomedia in the later literature" (en.wikipedia). Both carried. **Not resolved.**
- **Hermippus** and **Hermocrates**, fellow priests, are described in Orthodox tradition as survivors of a massacre of **20,000 Christians at Nicomedia in 303** **[A as tradition: OrthodoxWiki]**; the 20,000 is a calendar number, see §4.
- **The rebuke:** *"But, my friend, of what use are all thy acquirements in this art, since thou art ignorant of the science of salvation?"* **[A as text: Liguori, *Victories of the Martyrs*, via en.wikipedia; the original NOT located]**
- **The child and the viper:** *"The child revived, and the viper shattered into pieces before the eyes of Pantoleon."* **[A as text: Holy Trinity, *Lives of the Saints*, 27 July, from the Prologue/Menaion]**

**Two corrections, both self-contradictions inside the research pool.** (1) **The healing.** The chronology lens had him heal a **paralytic before the emperor**; the checkable sources have him **restore sight to a blind man**, converting **his father** (en.wikipedia). The paralytic survives only in Hammer's 1908 compilation and is not carried as event. (2) **The name.** The geography lens had him take the name Panteleimon at baptism; all three of its own cited sources, and the pool's chronology lens, put it at his **death**. **Corrected: Pantoleon at birth, Panteleimon at the block.**

- On Eustorgius's death he **inherits, frees his slaves, distributes the wealth**, then **practises without charge** and **visits imprisoned Christians**. **[A: en.wikipedia; OrthodoxWiki, "devoted all his talent to serving the poor"; it.wikipedia, "curava gratuitamente chiunque si rivolgesse a lui"]**

## 3. Denunciation, torments, execution (c. 304-305)

- **Denounced by envious fellow court physicians** **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia]**, the one detail no version drops.
- **The catalogue of instruments** (torches; molten lead; drowning weighted with a stone; beasts; the wheel), each failing, then **beheading**. **[R: the Passio-derived legend]** **The order is not stable:** en.wikipedia opens with a wheel over fire and closes with the wheel again; the Catholic Encyclopedia orders them differently, and neither notices. **Flagged.**
- **The olive tree.** The first stroke fails, *"the sword melted like wax"* **[A as text: OrthodoxWiki]**; milk is said to flow instead of blood; the tree fruits out of season. *"When the saint was beheaded, the olive tree - to which the saint was tied, at the moment of his death was covered with fruit."* **[A as text: Holy Trinity, 27 July]** **Correction:** the pool claimed the ellipsis in this quotation was in the source. It is not; the sentence is complete and is quoted in full.
- **Death c. 305** **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia, "about 305"; en.wikipedia]**; the specific **27 July 305** is **[R: it.wikipedia, tradition]**. **Panteleimon** attached in Eastern tradition for the dying prayer of forgiveness. **[A: en.wikipedia; OrthodoxWiki]**
- **Adamantius**, a witness, is said to have collected the blood, origin of the Ravello relic. **[R: it.wikipedia]** **INTERNAL STRAIN:** the same Italian material elsewhere attaches him to a **12th-century** custody chain, eight hundred years later. Not reconciled by the sources, nor here.
- **Burial at Nicomedia.** **[R]** **The grave is not located** by anything reached; Justinian's rebuilding implies it was known in the sixth century, but nothing states where.

## 4. The persecution's machinery, via the atlas's own record

The **edict** is the only part of this dossier with hard documentary standing, and it comes from outside the saint's file. Lactantius dates the razing of the Nicomedia church to **23 February 303** (the Terminalia) and the Edict to the following morning. **[A: carried byte-identically in `constantino_el_grande`, `saint_acacius`, `saint_blaise`, `saint_erasmus`]**

`saint_acacius.journey.json` also carries the **Myriads of Holy Martyrs, by the sword, at Nicomedia, 18 March**, and states in its own campa that Nicomedia "already holds ... Pantaleon the physician of the same company of fourteen." The 20,000-Christian massacre OrthodoxWiki has Hermolaus survive is the same calendar territory. **The relation between these Nicomedian dossiers is confusion in later calendars, not any documented link. [A: stated as such in `saint_acacius`]** **No source places Pantaleon at the edict-posting;** the link is thematic and is stated as thematic in `date_confidence`.

## 5. Early attestation of the cult

**Theodoret of Cyrrhus** (d. c. 457), eighth discourse on the martyrs: attests the cult, the earliest testimony identified **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia]** · the **Martyrologium Hieronymianum** (5th-6th c.): records him among early entries **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia]** · **Procopius**, *De aedificiis* (c. 554-560): **Justinian rebuilds the shrine at Nicomedia** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**CORRECTION.** The pool placed Procopius's testimony at **Constantinople**. It is **Nicomedia**. Two pool entries (Procopius; "Justinian rebuilds the shrine") are **the same testimony counted twice**, and the journey carries them as one stop.

**Gap:** the **Martyrologium Hieronymianum** has **no fixed place of compilation** in anything reached ("Western Europe" is the pool's own placeholder), so rather than mint a proxy pin the journey folds it into the Theodoret stop and says so. That pin is the **bishop's own see at Cyrrhus (36.7442 / 36.9575)**, explicitly not a Pantaleon site.

## 6. The relics: a body with no centre

**The head, claimed four times, unadjudicable.** (1) **Lyon**, Saint-Jean **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia, "at Lyons his head is venerated"; en.wikipedia]**; the **Charlemagne attribution** (it.wikipedia) is **[R]**, on no primary document, and en.wikipedia omits it; the pool stated it flatly and the journey splits the two claims. (2) **Vercelli** **[A: it.wikipedia]**. (3) **Panachrantos, Andros** **[A: OrthodoxWiki; it.wikipedia]**, arrival dated **1683** by Greek Wikipedia. (4) **Rossikon, Mount Athos** **[A: en.wikipedia lists "the head of Saint Panteleimon" among its relics]**; the pool had this "not independently corroborated", so it **upgrades**, making the conflict four-way. **No source acknowledges any other.**

- **Constantinople:** relics transferred from Nicomedia at an **undated** time; a **phial of blood said to liquefy on the feast day**. **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia]** **CONTRADICTION:** the Catholic Encyclopedia puts the liquefaction here, the Italian sources at Ravello; neither notices the other. **Correction:** the pool dated the transfer to the 8th century and added **Carthage** as a claimant; **neither is in its cited source**, and the Catholic Encyclopedia and en.wikipedia leave the transfer undated. **Both dropped.**
- **Saint-Denis:** bodily relics **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia; en.wikipedia]**, with no date, no account of arrival, no body part named. **Gap;** the journey's date is an admitted placeholder.
- **Venice, San Pantalon:** an **arm relic** **[A: it.wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the **Pantalone commedia dell'arte etymology is NOT in the cited source**. Contested folk etymology, **dropped**, with the drop stated in the campa.
- **Rome, San Pantaleo:** a **dedication, not a relic site**; it.wikipedia records the body under the high altar as **St Joseph Calasanz**. **[A]** **The stop is dropped.** **Gerace** (femur), **Lucca**, **Montoro** and **Rutigliano** hold further fragments **[A: it.wikipedia]**, not carried for length.
- **Porto:** relics carried by **Armenian refugees after the fall of Constantinople in 1453** to **São Pedro de Miragaia**, into the **cathedral in 1499** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, the most precisely dated leg of the dispersal. **Putna** (Bucovina) and **Ss. Peter and Paul, Constanța** hold relics too **[A: OrthodoxWiki; it.wikipedia]**, undated.
- **Tsri, then Amaras:** **Movses Kaghankatvatsi** has **Bishop Grigoris** bring relics to Tsri and **King Vachagan III** move them to Amaras. **[A as chronicle report, via en.wikipedia]** **CONFLATION FLAGGED:** the **Amaras** article credits that king's **489** chapel to the remains of **St Grigoris**, not Pantaleon. **Not resolved.**
- **Buenos Aires. Correction:** the pool called the church unnamed and pinned the city centre. **The cited source names it:** es.wikipedia specifies the sanctuary in **Mataderos**, holding an **arm** fragment gifted by **Cardinal Albino Luciani** (later John Paul I) through the Venetian see.

## 7. Ravello and the blood

- **Ampoule** in the Duomo, custody traced by local tradition to the **12th century**; liquefaction reported **every 27 July**. **[A as tradition: it.wikipedia]** Liguori claims to have witnessed it, "as I, the author of this work, have seen it" (quoted in full in the journey). **[A as text via en.wikipedia]**
- **1643:** Bishop **Bernardino Panicola** moves it to a new chapel in solemn procession; in the same century the glass **cracks**, still visible. **[A: it.wikipedia]**
- **Secondary ampoules. Correction:** the pool listed four recipients; it.wikipedia names **eight**: Constantinople, Montauro, **Martignano**, Limbadi, **Caiazzo**, **Lanciano**, **Irsina**, and the **Monasterio de la Encarnación, Madrid**. All eight carried.
- **Madrid. Correction:** the pool called this "a Spanish echo of the Ravello liquefaction." es.wikipedia says the Madrid ampoule liquefies on **26 July, not 27**, and its article on the **Ermita de San Pantaleón de Losa** (Burgos) says it **came from that hermitage**. **Two provenances in one encyclopedia, on different pages. Not resolved.**
- **17 March 2020:** an extraordinary liquefaction reported during a **livestreamed** service amid the COVID-19 pandemic. **[A: it.wikipedia]** **No independent report located.**

## 8. The Fourteen Holy Helpers, feasts, patronage

- Grouped after the **Black Death**; **Rhineland** origin per English scholarship, **Regensburg** per German. **[A: en.wikipedia; the disagreement already carried in `catorce_santos_ayudantes`]**
- **By about 1400** the closed sequence appears in the same order across **Regensburg, Bamberg, Wurzburg, Nuremberg**. **[A: de.wikipedia 'Vierzehn Nothelfer']**
- **Patronage. Correction:** the pool gave "physicians, apothecaries, midwives, invoked against consumption, headaches, and witchcraft." **"Witchcraft" is in no fetched source.** en.wikipedia gives **physicians and midwives, protection of domestic animals, invoked against cancer and tuberculosis**; headaches and consumption are the Catholic Encyclopedia's separate list. **Domestic animals was missing and is restored.**
- **Feasts, unharmonized:** **27 July** primary, West and Byzantine **[A]** · **28 July** in some Western calendars and **18 February** as a minor Western commemoration **[A: Catholic Encyclopedia]** · **19 Epip**, Coptic **[A: en.wikipedia]** · **26 July** for the Madrid ampoule **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **Not reconciled by any source and not here.**
- **Iconography:** martyr's palm, medicine box, long-handled probe, Gospel book or crucifix, sometimes an olive branch; a beardless young man. **[A: en.wikipedia]** No site anchors it, so no stop is minted.

## 11. The honest gaps

**No Roman-era acta**, and the defending source calls the surviving Lives valueless while keeping the martyrdom: that is the file. Open and unadjudicated below it: **which emperor** he served (Maximian or Galerius) · **Hermolaus's rank** (priest or bishop) · **the head** (four claimants) · **the liquefaction** (Constantinople or Ravello) · **Adamantius** (witness in 305, or a 12th-century figure) · **the Madrid ampoule** (Ravello or Burgos) · **the Amaras translation** (Pantaleon's relics or Grigoris's). **Undated entirely**, carried with explicit `date_confidence` placeholders: the Nicomedia-to-Constantinople transfer, Saint-Denis, Vercelli, the Venice arm, the Athos head, the Tsri translation, Putna and Constanța, the eight ampoules, the Buenos Aires fragment. **The grave at Nicomedia is not located**, and **no independent report** exists on the 2020 liquefaction.
