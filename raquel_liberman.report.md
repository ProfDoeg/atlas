# Raquel Liberman (1900-1935): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-15 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Ruchla Laja Liberman, Berdychiv 1900 to Buenos Aires 1935; the woman whose denunciation of 31 December 1929 broke open the Zwi Migdal trafficking network.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or inference. Contradictions are flagged and left open. Unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**The controlling fact:** almost nothing survives in Liberman's own hand. No diary, no letter, no memoir, no interview. Everything quoted as her speech descends from court proceedings as retold decades later in commemorative journalism, and from novels. The one primary document anybody has produced is a ship's manifest. That shapes every section below.

---

## 1. Berdychiv and Warsaw, 1900-1922

**Birth.** 10 July 1900, Berdychiv, Kiev Governorate, Russian Empire (present-day Ukraine), a town with one of the largest Jewish populations in the empire. **[A: English Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman"; Spanish Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman", agreeing on town and date.]** No street, house, or synagogue register is cited by either.

**Contradiction, unresolved:** a secondary summary drawing on Zwi Migdal historiography puts her birth in **Łódź, Poland**. Neither Wikipedia article carries this and its print source was never confirmed. Recorded, not adjudicated; the journey file pins Berdychiv.

**Name.** Transliterated at least four ways: Raquel, Rokhl Lea, Rushla Laja, Ruchla Laja Liberman. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia.]** A Yiddish/Polish name passing through Spanish-language paperwork, not a dispute about identity.

**Warsaw.** The family relocates to Warsaw while she is a child; she grows up in the Jewish quarter. **[A: English Wikipedia.]** No year, no address, no schooling. The journey file dates the move to 1905, marked inferred.

**Marriage.** 1919, Warsaw, to **Yaacov (Jacobo) Ferber**, a tailor, under Jewish religious rites. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia.]** Venue, officiant, witnesses: not named anywhere reachable.

**Children.** A son born 1920, a second child 1921. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia.]**
**Contradiction, unresolved:** the sons' names. Some accounts give **Joshúa (José) Ferber** and an unnamed second son; others **José and Moisés**. **[A for the disagreement itself: Spanish Wikipedia.]** The 1922 ship manifest cited by Spanish Wikipedia records them as **ICHIA** and **MOESCHE**, the only primary-document form of the names located.

**The husband goes first.** Ferber emigrates ahead of wife and children, the standard chain-migration pattern. **[R: Spanish Wikipedia narrates it; port of departure and ship are not recorded.]**

## 2. The crossing and Tapalqué, 1921-1923

**Her arrival.** 30 September 1922, port of Buenos Aires, with her two sons. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, citing the ship manifest; English Wikipedia gives only the year.]**

**Contradiction, noted and not treated as live:** a secondary summary states she arrived in **1918**. The 1922 date rests on a manifest; the 1918 figure has no located document behind it. Recorded so the disagreement is not silently erased.

**Port of departure.** Not located: no ship name, no port of embarkation. **[R]** The journey file's Hamburg stop is explicitly marked as reconstruction, standing for the North Sea emigrant-hall system; Bremerhaven is equally plausible and equally undocumented. Whether she passed through the Hotel de Inmigrantes is likewise **[R]**: the era's standard reception building, not an individually documented fact about her.

**Tapalqué.** The family settles in Tapalqué, Buenos Aires Province. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia.]**

**Ferber's death.** Tuberculosis, in Tapalqué. **[A]** English Wikipedia implies shortly after her 1922 arrival; Spanish Wikipedia specifies 1923. The journey file uses 1923 with the spread in `date_confidence`.

*Internal contradiction in the research pool, flagged:* the afterlife lens placed Ferber's death in Buenos Aires city. Both Wikipedia articles place it in Tapalqué. The journey file follows Tapalqué.

**The sons placed out.** Unable to support the boys as a seamstress, she leaves them with a foster family and goes to the city for work. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia.]** The foster family is not named; the arrangement is not described.

## 3. The Varsovia society / Zwi Migdal

**Legal cover.** The network registers in Avellaneda (then Barracas al Sud) as the **Sociedad Israelita de Socorros Mutuos Varsovia**, giving a trafficking ring the standing of Jewish communal charity. Registration commonly dated 1906. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia, "Zwi Migdal".]** Some sources date the trafficking milieu itself to the 1860s; 1906 is the legal registration.

**Headquarters.** A two-storey building at **Av. Córdoba 3280** with garden, synagogue, event hall, bar, dining room, and funeral parlour. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal": "un edificio de dos plantas con jardín, sinagoga, salón de fiestas, bar, comedor y sala de velatorios".]**

**Auction sites.** The **Café Parisien**, given by Spanish Wikipedia as "de la avenida Alvear 3184", and the **Hotel Palestina**. **[A for the practice and the names; the addresses are a problem.]** Today's Av. Alvear has no 3184; the period Avenida Alvear is now Av. del Libertador, whose 3184 lies far from the pool's pin. The Hotel Palestina's address was not located at all. Both stops are pinned to the city-centre marker rather than to an unsupportable street position. Whether Liberman passed through either is **[R]**; they stand for the system that processed her.

**Renaming.** The organisation drops "Varsovia" after a Polish diplomatic complaint and takes the name Zwi Migdal.
**Contradiction, unresolved:** English Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal" dates complaint and renaming to **1927**; Spanish Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal" states "En el año 1929 la embajada de Polonia se quejó a la Argentina porque tal organización llevaba el nombre Varsovia". Both years are carried in `date_confidence`; neither is preferred. Spanish Wikipedia also glosses "Zwi Migdal" as "gran fuerza" rather than solely as member Luis (Zwi) Migdal's name; both readings are recorded.

**Scale.** At its 1920s peak, roughly **430 members**, about **2,000 brothels**, about **4,000 trafficked women** nationally. **[A: English Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal".]**

## 4. Captivity, 1923-1929

**Entrapment.** She comes under the control of a trafficker named in sources as **Jaime (or José) Cissinger / Kissinger**. **[R]**
**Contradiction, unresolved:** Spanish Wikipedia sets out two competing versions, direct abduction versus deception by a false promise of marriage or work, and does not choose. Sources also disagree on whether first contact happened in Poland or only after her arrival. The journey file states that the versions compete and picks neither.

**Where.** Brothels concentrated on **Junín and Lavalle**, in what was then the city's Jewish quarter. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia.]**

**Duration. Contradiction, unresolved, three-way:** English Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman": "For at least four years Liberman was a captive". Spanish Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman": approximately six years. Spanish Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal": "11 años de prostitución forzada". Three figures circulate, not two. All three are named in `date_confidence` on the relevant stop.

**First escape.** She saves money in secret, reportedly with a client's help, and buys her way out. **[R: neither article gives a year.]** Placed around 1926, hedged in `date_confidence`. She then opens a small antique or curio shop on **Calle Callao** **[A for the fact, R for the year]**; the exact address is not preserved.

## 5. Korn, and the denunciation

**The fraudulent marriage.** Zwi Migdal member **José Salomón Korn** courts her under a false promise of marriage, performs a sham Jewish ceremony, takes her savings, and forces her back into a brothel. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia.]** 1929.

**Second escape.** **[A: English Wikipedia.]** How she managed it is not described in any reachable source. A real gap, not a summarisation loss.

**The denunciation.** 31 December 1929, to police commissioner **Julio Alsogaray**, known within the force for refusing the network's bribes. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman"; English Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal".]**

*Note on Alsogaray.* English Wikipedia's "Julio Alsogaray" page is a disambiguation page for two unrelated men. The commissioner of this case has no standalone English article, itself a measure of how thin the standing reference record is.

### The famous line: provenance chain, and why it must be hedged

1. **Spanish Wikipedia, "Raquel Liberman"**, section "Denuncia y juicio a la Zwi Migdal": "El comisario le preguntó si estaba dispuesta a declarar ante un juez y ella afirmó: 'Solo se muere una vez: la denuncia no la retiro'." Cited to its ref 7.
2. **Ref 7 was retrieved in full**: "Reconocimiento a Raquel Liberman, pionera contra la trata de personas", *diario El Argentino* (Gualeguaychú, Entre Ríos), 21 November 2011, archived at `web.archive.org/web/20140222001822/http://www.diarioelargentino.com.ar/noticias/99806/`. It reads: "'Sólo se muere una vez, la denuncia no la retiro', dicen que dijo."
3. **Three divergences.** (a) *Wording*: the source has "Sólo" accented and a **comma**; Wikipedia's colon is an editorial alteration. (b) *Hedge*: the source marks the line as hearsay, "dicen que dijo". (c) *Context*: per El Argentino, when Alsogaray asked whether she would testify before a judge, "su respuesta fue afirmativa", she simply said yes; the famous line comes **afterward**, answering the network's renewed "halagos, promesas y al fin amenazas de muerte". Wikipedia collapsed two moments into one.
4. **Provenance is weaker still.** El Argentino is not quoting a police or court file. It quotes the *fundamentos* of a 2011 congressional initiative by Diputadas Silvia Vázquez and María Luisa Storani: a commemorative text written some eighty years after the fact.
5. **The English version has no independent standing.** English Wikipedia renders it "I can only die once, I won't withdraw the complaint" and cites, as ref 8, **the same El Argentino item**. No independent English source carries these words; it is one editor's uncredited translation of an already-altered Spanish text.

**Editorial decision.** The journey file's `quote` field carries the source wording, not Wikipedia's colon version; `quote_source` names El Argentino, the 2011 *fundamentos*, and the "dicen que dijo" hedge, and states that no police or court document carrying these words has been located. The English rendering is not used as a quotation anywhere.

### The judge's line

"The very existence of the Zwi Migdal Organization directly threatens our society", attributed to Judge **Manuel Rodríguez Ocampo**. **[R at best.]** Not locatable verbatim in either Wikipedia article; no Spanish original found. **Not used as a quote in the journey file.** The substance, that Rodríguez Ocampo took the case, refused bribes, and ordered the prosecution, is **[A: English Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal"; Spanish Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman"]**.

## 6. The case, 1930-1931

**The raid.** Police raid the headquarters at Av. Córdoba 3280 on **30 May 1930**, seizing the organisation's own records and membership rolls. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal".]**

**The 108.** On **27 September 1930**, Spanish Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal": "se dictó el procesamiento de 108 de los socios de la Migdal".

**Correction carried into the journey file:** *procesamiento* is **indictment, not conviction**. English Wikipedia loosely says "convictions" while also reporting the men were released in January 1931, which is incoherent. "108 criminal convictions" overstates the record.

*Conflation flagged and undone:* the pool merged the 30 May raid and the 27 September procesamiento into one dated event. They are four months apart; the raid did not itself produce the 108.

**The fugitives.** Roughly **334** further suspects sought under warrant. **[A: English Wikipedia.]**

**The appeal.** By **January 1931** the appellate court releases most of those held; Spanish Wikipedia "Raquel Liberman" states the Cámara de Apelaciones finally convicted only **three** minor participants, for want of corroborating testimony beyond Liberman's own. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia; English Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal" corroborates the January 1931 releases.]** The articles are not strictly contradictory but differ in emphasis: English foregrounds the 108, Spanish the reversal.

**The collapse.** Public exposure destroys the mutual-aid cover. English Wikipedia "Zwi Migdal" describes the organisation as effectively ceasing to operate **by 1939**, hundreds of pimps deported to Uruguay. **[A]** The pool dated the collapse to 1930, compressing a decade; the journey file uses 1930-1939.

## 7. Last years and death

**Reunion.** Reunited with her two sons in Buenos Aires after the trial. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia.]** No address, no account of the household.

**The visa.** 1934, an application to return to Poland, apparently meaning to take the boys. The trip never happens. **[A: English Wikipedia]** for the application; **[R]** for the intent. No ticket, itinerary, or correspondence was located.

**Death.** 7 April 1935, Buenos Aires, thyroid cancer, aged 34. **[A: English Wikipedia: "on 7 April 1935, she died of thyroid cancer at the age of 34".]** The **Hospital Argerich** detail comes from the Spanish material alone and is carried as **[R]**.

**Contradiction, unresolved:** Spanish Wikipedia contradicts itself, infobox giving 7 April 1935 and body text "murió el 17 de abril de 1935". The journey file uses 7 April and states the conflict in `date_confidence`.

**Correction:** the sons' ages at her death, usually given as "14 and 15", appear in **neither** article. They are an inference from the 1920 and 1921 births; the journey file does not assert them.

**The grave: a genuine gap.** No source locates her grave, tomb, or any burial marker; targeted cemetery, tomb, and burial searches returned nothing. Stated as an open gap, not a settled absence: a record may well exist in Buenos Aires Jewish communal archives this research did not reach.

## 8. Afterlife

**The mediated voice.** What survives of her own words is institutional: police and court transcripts, filtered through officials' questions and clerks' hands, then through commemorative journalism eighty years later. No letter, diary, or interview. **[A as an absence: neither Wikipedia article cites any personal writing by her.]**

**Literature.** Myrtha Schalom's 2003 novel *La Polaca*, nine editions, adapted as the one-woman play *En el nombre de Raquel* (Mariel Rosciano); Nora Glickman, *The Jewish White Slave Trade and the Untold Story of Raquel Liberman*; Ilan Sheinfeld, *The Tale of a Ring*; Talia Carner, *The Third Daughter* (2019); Humberto Costantini's *Rapsodía de Raquel Liberman*, unfinished at his death. **[A: English and Spanish Wikipedia; the play detail from the secondary summary.]** Documentary: Gabriela Bohm, *Raquel: A Marked Woman*, 30 minutes **[A: English Wikipedia, Recognition section; the pool mis-cited this to Spanish Wikipedia]**.

**Award.** The **Premio Raquel Liberman**, created June 2010, for work in defence of survivors of gender violence and trafficking. **[A: English Wikipedia: "as of June 2010, the Raquel Liberman award was created".]**

**Telenovela.** *Argentina, tierra de amor y venganza* (2019) contains "un personaje basado en su vida". **[A: Spanish Wikipedia.]** The widely repeated attribution of the role to **María Eugenia Suárez is unsourced**: Spanish Wikipedia does not name her.

**The subway station.** **Callao-Raquel Liberman**, **Line D**, under Av. Córdoba 1800 between Callao and Rodríguez Peña, at -34.59964, -58.39311. **[A: Spanish Wikipedia, "Callao-Raquel Liberman (subte de Buenos Aires)".]** Three corrections to the pool: (a) the line is **D**, not B; (b) English Wikipedia does not mention the station at all and cannot be cited for it; (c) **contradiction, unresolved:** the station article credits a Legislature vote of **2023**, while Spanish Wikipedia's "Raquel Liberman" says **2019**. Both years are named in `date_confidence`.

## 10. Interlocks

The corpus holds several travellers whose lives touch Liberman's world: Alberto Gerchunoff (Pale to Buenos Aires, 1889), Max Glucksmann (Bukovina to Buenos Aires, 1890), César Milstein (whose father crossed in 1913), Meyer Lansky (Grodno, same push factors), Julio Argentino Roca (whose state-building produced the immigration and police apparatus). Ghislaine Maxwell and Jeffrey Epstein sit in the queue as structural parallels.

**None is named in a campa, deliberately.** Every one is a shared-port, shared-city, or shared-theme connection. Not one met Liberman, corresponded with her, influenced her, was influenced by her, wrote about her, or stood in any documented relation to her. Naming them inside her prose would assert a relation the record does not support. The interlock list returned for this subject is empty.
