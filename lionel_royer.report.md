# Lionel-Noël Royer (1852-1926): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. The Sarthe-born history painter who fought at Loigny at seventeen, was sponsored into the Beaux-Arts by the officer who found him sketching the field, painted the Vercingetorix surrender that a century of French schoolbooks reprinted, and spent his last years putting Joan of Arc on the walls of the basilica at Domrémy.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in place. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or an inference the sources allow but never state. Contradictions are flagged, **not adjudicated**. Unreachable sources are named with the reason. The journey that comes out of this is 43 stops in 8 segments, with nine verbatim quotations (every distinct one the pool held) and every hedge in `date_confidence` rather than in the campa prose.

**Two warnings first. 1.** The pool arrived with a systematic coordinate problem: nine of thirty-one geography pins wrong by 300 m to 14 km, several by copy-paste (the latitude 48.0092, Le Mans cathedral, was pasted onto Loigny twice). All corrected below.

**2.** The pool's interlock lens asserts, repeatedly, that **Royer painted murals in the Panthéon**: "Royer's Panthéon murals (Vercingetorix and the Joan of Arc cycle)", "the Panthéon's collaborative Joan of Arc mural program with Lenepveu". **No other lens supports this and two contradict it outright**: the Vercingétorix is at the Musée Crozatier, bought by that municipality, and the Joan cycle is at Domrémy. The Panthéon Joan cycle is Lenepveu's. Treated here as an error in the pool and not carried into the journey.

## 1. Château-du-Loir and the war (1852-1871)

Born **25 December 1852 at Château-du-Loir, Sarthe**. **[A]** both wikipedias; Wikidata Q1827276; the Musée Crozatier sheet. The birth house is **not identified in any consulted source**, an honest gap; the pin is the town centre at Saint-Guingalois.

**Volunteers before turning eighteen** with the **Volontaires de l'Ouest**, the corps formed from the former papal Zouaves; one source gives his age at enlistment as seventeen. **Fights at Loigny-Poupry, 2 December 1870.** **[A]** fr.wikipedia; loire1870.fr; universdesarts.com.

- *Two corrections on rank and command.* The pool called Charette a **general** at Loigny; per fr.wikipedia he was a **lieutenant-colonel** on 2 December 1870, made *général de brigade (au titre auxiliaire)* only on **14 January 1871**, and Royer's own Montreal canvas is titled *Colonel Athanase de Charette*. The afterlife lens also calls de Sonis "the general who never commanded him", which is wrong: the Volontaires were *rattachés au 17e corps d'armée*, and the 17th Corps was **commanded by General Gaston de Sonis**, who personally led the charge alongside Charette's men.

**The battlefield encounter.** Charette notices the boy sketching and offers to fund the École des Beaux-Arts. **[A]** for the encounter and the offer, which fr.wikipedia states independently of the family. **[R]** for the wording, "I will pay for your Beaux-Arts education", which descends through the grandson Pierre Edouard Torchet via the blog.

**The mass at Saint-Péravy-la-Colombe before the battle.** **[R]** loire1870.fr describes the scene as Royer painted it in 1910, the painter among the kneeling soldiers with a torch. **fr.wikipedia's articles on the commune and on the battle do not mention the mass.** The mass and his presence at it are both weakly sourced; the painting is firmly attested.

**Auvours, January 1871.** Royer paints *Bataille d'Auvours, le 10 janvier 1871* in 1874. **[A]** for the painting (Joconde 000PE009877); **[R]** for his presence there. *Contradiction flagged:* loire1870.fr titles it *le 11 janvier*, the catalogue **10 janvier**, and the battle of Le Mans ran **10 to 12 January 1871** (fr.wikipedia), so neither day is wrong about the fighting. That article **does not name the Volontaires de l'Ouest among the troops engaged**.

## 2. The Beaux-Arts and the early Salon (1871-1882)

**Studies under Alexandre Cabanel and William-Adolphe Bouguereau.** **[A]** fr.wikipedia; universdesarts; Musée d'Orsay catalogue 21309. **No matriculation date is given by any consulted source**; the journey places the stop in 1871, marked inferred.

**First shows at the Salon in 1874.** **[A]** fr.wikipedia gives "à partir de 1874". *Contradiction flagged:* the afterlife lens says he exhibited "between 1874 and 1900", which understates it. Works are recorded through 1910, a showing for 1912 and a prize for 1913.

Works entering public collections this decade, all **[A]** from fr.wikipedia's works list: *La fille de l'hôtesse* (1875, 82 x 66 cm, Gray); *Mauresque* (1876, Marseille); *Daphné changée en laurier* (1880, Tessé, Joconde 00160017326) with an undated *Diane surprise* (000PE009968). The watercolour *La cantinière héroïque* is in the Musées du Mans, dated only **circa 1874** by loire1870.fr **[R]**.

**The second grand prix de Rome, 1882.** **[A]** fr.wikipedia: "second premier grand prix de Rome en peinture de 1882".

- *Contradiction flagged, and it matters.* The afterlife lens says flatly that he "wins the Prix de Rome in 1882", following en.wikipedia; the geography lens says correctly that it was the **second** prize. **The two lenses contradict each other.** The premier grand prix went to **Gustave Popelin** (*Mattathias refuse de sacrifier aux idoles*); Royer shared the second rank with **Paul Leroy**. The Villa Médicis residency belonged to the premier prix alone, and **no consulted source places Royer in Italy**. The journey states the distinction explicitly.

## 3. The Salon career and the medals (1884-1900)

All **[A]**, fr.wikipedia and the universdesarts.com Salon record agreeing: third-class medal 1884; *Le Christ au tombeau* at the Salon of 1887, later Chartres; *La Source* in the *Salon illustré* 1888; second-class medal 1896; bronze at the Exposition Universelle of 1900; first prize for history painting 1913 (Base Palissy). *Le Chœur de la cathédrale du Mans*, 1896, Musée de Tessé, Joconde 000PE009892 **[A]**.

**Portrait of the Comte d'Adhémar de Cransac**, Dayton Art Institute. **[A]** for the work and location (en.wikipedia caption), but **the date is "c. 1890" and must not be read as anchored**; the pool listed it as 1890 flat, and the **Dayton collection endpoint returns 404**.

**Colonel Athanase de Charette, Commandant des Zouaves Pontificaux, 1885**, Basilique-cathédrale Marie-Reine-du-Monde, Montreal. **[A]** fr.wikipedia's public-collections list, sourced to the cathedral's PDF. *Correction:* the chronology lens called this undated with an unknown arrival date, "a gap". **It is not a gap**, 1885 is given, and the pool's title (*Le colonel de Charette à Loigny*) and five-metre height are **not** supported by the cathedral listing. **[R]**: nothing places Royer in Canada. Likewise ***Cupidon et Psyché*, 1893, at the World's Columbian Exposition, Chicago**: **[A]** for the work and showing (fr.wikipedia), **[R]** for his presence, and later whereabouts a gap.

**Alfred Dreyfus in his cell**, for **Le Petit Journal illustré, no. 218, 20 January 1895**. **[A]** fr.wikipedia gives issue and date. The degradation at the École militaire was 5 January; the drawing is **made in Paris from written accounts**, not from Île du Diable, and the pool is right to keep that as a stated limit. His work for newspaper supplements is separately attested (en.wikipedia).

## 4. Joan of Arc: the refused competition and the Domrémy cycle (1893-1918)

**1893: the competition for the stained-glass windows of the cathedral Sainte-Croix at Orléans.** Ten watercolours on the life of Joan of Arc at one tenth of the intended scale. **He loses**, and the windows are never made to his designs. **[A]** both wikipedias name the *concours des verrières*; the chronology lens had it only as "an unsuccessful competition entry", place "France". **1897: the ten watercolours given to the Société historique et archéologique du Maine, Le Mans. [A]** The society's **period address is not given by consulted sources**; the pin sits in central Le Mans, marked approximate.

**The Basilique du Bois-Chenu, Domrémy** (built 1881-1926).

- **Base Palissy PM88002005**: *"Ensemble de huit toiles peintes marouflées, datées et signées, de Lionel Royer entre 1911 et 1918, sur la vie de Jeanne d'Arc"*, the scenes being her first communion, Chinon, Orléans, the coronation at Reims, her last communion, the stake. **[A]** The strongest single document in the pool, closing a gap the other lenses left open.
- *Contradiction flagged, not resolved.* The chronology lens calls the execution dates a gap (following fr.wikipedia, which gives none); the afterlife lens gives **1913**, on the Yves de Saint-Jean blog alone; Palissy gives **1911 to 1918**. The journey follows Palissy and records 1913 as unsupported.
- **Stained glass with Charles Lorin of Chartres.** **[A]** for the collaboration (fr.wikipedia); **[R]** for any visit, no dated record surviving. **Lorin directed the studio at 46 rue de la Tannerie from 1899**, so the collaboration cannot predate 1899.
- **1913: first prize for history painting at the Salon** for one of the canvases. **[A]** the archival note in PM88002005, which **does not say which of the eight took the prize**, a gap.
- **1926: the basilica is consecrated**, the year construction closes and the year Royer dies. **Whether he lived to see it, and whether the ceremony fell before or after 30 June, no consulted source states.**

**Critical reading.** Chantal Bouchon (1998) is quoted at length by fr.wikipedia; two of her three sentences are used verbatim in the journey, and on the third see below.

## 5. The volunteers remembered, and undated commissions (1910-1912)

**Two monumental canvases for the rebuilt church of Loigny-la-Bataille, 1910, 6 x 3 m, both classé MH that year.** **[A]** Base Palissy PM28000776 and PM28000777; fr.wikipedia.

- *Correction to the titles.* The pool gave *L'Agonie du général de Sonis* and *La Communion des Zouaves à Saint-Péravy-la-Colombe*; the catalogue titles are ***La Communion des zouaves*** (PM28000776) and its pendant ***Le Général Gaston de Sonis*** (PM28000777). The pool's versions are descriptive variants in circulation.
- *Correction.* The afterlife lens called these works "undated"; Palissy dates them firmly to 1910, the chronology lens has it right, and Palissy settles the disagreement.
- Royer places **himself** among the kneeling soldiers in the communion scene, torch in hand. **[R]** loire1870.fr, not independently corroborated.

**Undated commissions, each a stated gap:** *Le Crucifix* at the autel du Crucifix, cathédrale Saint-Julien du Mans, **[A]** for the work (fr.wikipedia) with no date; a *Pietà* for the Visitation at Le Mans, frescoes at Mulsanne, a panel for the mairie of the 10th arrondissement, all **[R]** on universdesarts alone; *Femme en deuil* at Gap, **[A]** for the work and **undated for both painting and acquisition**. ***Volontaires de l'ouest* at the Salon of 1912**: **[R]** loire1870.fr alone, **no catalogue entry reachable**.

## 6. Vercingétorix, and what happened to it

**Painted 1899**, oil on canvas, **321 x 482 cm**, shown at the **Salon of 1899**, signed and dated lower right. **[A]** POP/Base Joconde 01160003005; utpictura18.univ-amu.fr notice 22776; Musée Crozatier didactic sheet.

- *Correction: the pool's venue for the 1899 Salon is impossible.* It gives the Palais de l'Industrie, which per fr.wikipedia served the salons *from 1857 to 1897* and whose **demolition began in 1896**. It did not exist in 1899. The Salon that year was on the Champ de Mars, and the Société des artistes français moved under the nave of the Grand Palais only in **1901**. The exhibition is solid, the venue has been replaced, and the substitution is disclosed in the journey.

**Acquisition: contradiction flagged, not resolved.** POP/Base Joconde 01160003005 gives **purchase by the municipality in 1901, inventory 1903.59**; the **Crozatier's own sheet gives 1903 as the purchase year**. 1903 is on the face of it the inventorying year, and the pool's geography and afterlife lenses take opposite sides. Both readings sit in `date_confidence`, neither is chosen. That it was a **purchase and not a gift** is explicit in the accession note **[A]**.

**The museum's own modern critique** of the picture it owns: the warhorse too large for the breeds ridden in Gaul, the oppidum of Alesia painted like a medieval fortress, the composition inverting the outcome by making the defeated chieftain the hero. **[R]** the didactic sheet, undated, a modern reassessment and not a period judgement. The same sheet notes that **Motte's *Vercingétorix se rendant à César* (1886) is in the same collection** and likewise paints the Gaul taller than the Roman **[A]**: the inversion was a convention of the genre.

## 7. Death and disappearance (1926 onward)

**Dies 30 June 1926 at Neuilly-sur-Seine.** **[A]** fr.wikipedia; Wikidata Q1827276. *Correction:* the afterlife lens gives his age as seventy-four. Born 25 December 1852, he was **seventy-three**, and fr.wikipedia files him under *Décès à 73 ans*.

**Buried at Fontenay-aux-Roses.** **[A]** fr.wikipedia; Wikidata. **The plot and section are not identified in any consulted source.** The pool gave the commune centroid; the cemetery is 600 m north, at 48.7943, 2.2896 (OSM way 22752939).

**Steady Larousse entries from 1895; the name gone from reference works by the 1940s.** **[R]** the Yves de Saint-Jean blog alone, itself a piece of the rediscovery it describes. **No retrospective and no catalogue raisonné was found in this pass**, a gap. The Vercingétorix meanwhile is reprinted in French history textbooks across a century, until it is the defining image of the surrender at Alesia **[A]** (the blog and en.wikipedia independently), though **neither names a first textbook edition**, another gap.

**Personal testimony.** Louis de Meurville, a friend, described "an open heart of gentleness with everyone, sometimes irritable and impulsive but always ready to help", and Royer is said to have repeated of himself, "I am a priest of art, I work with love". **[R]** the same blog, family memory, treated as tradition.

## A fabricated quotation, caught

The pool's quotes lens carried this French sentence, attributed to Bouchon by way of en.wikipedia:

> Par la synthèse évocatrice qu'il réussit et le sentiment profond qu'il y introduit, le peintre laisse l'interprétation ouverte à chacun.

**This French text exists in no source.** It is a back-translation of en.wikipedia's English rendering. The actual French on fr.wikipedia reads:

> Par la synthèse évocatrice qu'il réalise et le sentiment qu'il introduit dans ce cycle, le peintre laisse le jugement aux idées de chacun.

**The journey uses the fr.wikipedia wording.** If the English is wanted, en.wikipedia's own sentence should be quoted verbatim rather than translated back into French.

A second pool quote was **truncated mid-sentence**: the Vercingetorix line ends "...au moment de sa reddition (il aurait été massacré par la garde romaine)". The full sentence is used. Its **attribution was also wrong**: the pool credited the Art Contrarian blog (2018), which carries only an English translation and cannot be the source of the French. The journey credits fr.wikipedia directly.

## Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in the campa where the paths genuinely cross.

- **`joan_of_arc`**: the whole Domrémy phase. Royer's eight canvases render what the Joan file transcribes, the village, the Ladies' Tree named in her testimony, Chinon, Reims. **[A]** for the subject matter via Palissy; **[R]** for any claim that he worked from the transcript directly rather than the nineteenth-century Joan literature.
- **`pierre_bourlemont`**, **`joffrey_bourlemont`**, **`pica_bourlemont`**: the seigneurs whose fief Domrémy was, whose Fairies' Tree is the standing prop of Royer's Joan iconography. **[A]** for the family and the tree; **[R]** for the connection to Royer, shared ground and not documented influence.
- **`yolande_aragon`**: the Angers court that armed Joan. Named at Chinon and Reims, where the point is her **absence** from the composition. **[R]**
- **`falconetti`**: Dreyer's *Passion de Jeanne d'Arc*, shot at Billancourt from October 1927, sixteen months after Royer's death. **[R]**; nothing connects it to Royer.
- **The surname coincidence is flagged, not exploited.** `QUEUE.md` warns that Royer is a misnaming risk, rightly: Joan lodged at Vaucouleurs with **Henri and Catherine Le Royer**, and the census lists two Domrémy-area women named **Royer**. **No relation to the painter is asserted by any source, or here.**

**Campa pollution cleanup (this pass).** Five campas carried "this atlas"/"this corpus" self-reference (the Vercingétorix purchase, the Domrémy village stop, the Ladies' Tree, and the Chinon and Reims coronation panels); all five are rewritten to state the same fact plainly, with every named person left in place unchanged, since none of the removed language named a coincidental person, only the project itself. **Flagged for a future pass rather than resolved here:** the Bourlémont-family mention in the Domrémy village stop ("Royer arrives to paint the ground they lived on") is close to the shared-geography pattern this cleanup otherwise cuts, since Joffrey, Pierre, and Pica Bourlémont held the fief roughly six and a half centuries before Royer painted it and the campa itself already calls the Royer connection "[R]... shared ground, not documented influence." It was left in place this pass because the Ladies' Tree specifically is named in Joan's own 1456 trial testimony, which Royer worked from directly, giving the tree (if not the family generally) a documented textual source; a stricter reading might cut the three Bourlémont names from the village stop while keeping the tree.
