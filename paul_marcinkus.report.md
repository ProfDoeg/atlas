# Paul Casimir Marcinkus (1922-2006): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-18 for `paul_marcinkus.journey.json`. Cicero, Illinois → Rome → La Paz → Ottawa → Vatican City → Sun City, Arizona.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, publisher copy, or a press claim not verified at a primary source. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Where the pool carried an internal error, the correction is stated with its source and the rejected value preserved.

The subject is well documented at the level of offices and badly documented at the level of conduct: no trial was held, no deposition taken, and he gave perhaps a dozen quotable sentences in twenty-five years. Everything between the two is press reconstruction, and the file is built accordingly.

## 1. Cicero and Chicago, 1922-1953

- Born 15 January 1922 in Cicero, Illinois, youngest of five children of Lithuanian immigrants; father a window washer, mother a bakery worker. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Paul Marcinkus'; lithaz.org]**
- Seminary at St. Mary of the Lake, Mundelein, c. 1938-1943. **[R]**: no enrollment record consulted, the dates being the ordinary sequence for a 1947 ordination.
- Ordained 3 May 1947 for the Archdiocese of Chicago by Cardinal Samuel Stritch. **[A: Wikipedia; catholic-hierarchy.org]**
- Assistant pastor at St. Christina's and Holy Cross (Back of the Yards); 1949, appointed to the archdiocesan matrimonial tribunal. **[A: Wikipedia]**

**Corrections.** (1) *Birth parish*: the pool tagged the St. Anthony of Padua entry [A] while its own note conceded the parish was [R]. Journey file: birth date and city [A], parish [R]. (2) *Ordination venue*, **CONTRADICTION, unresolved**: the pool asserted Holy Name Cathedral and tagged it [A] while conceding that no source confirms it and that Chicago ordinations of that era were customarily held at the Mundelein chapel. The file keeps the cathedral pin, the same building holding his 2006 funeral, and states the problem in `date_confidence`.

**Gap.** Nothing reachable describes the parish work itself: no sermon, no anecdote, no parishioner. The years between ordination and Rome are an office record only.

## 2. The diplomatic apprenticeship, 1953-1963

- Rome, c. 1953. **CONTRADICTION, unresolved:** the dossier gives a doctorate from the Gregorian; Wikipedia specifies the programme of the Pontifical Ecclesiastical Academy. Both printed in the campa, neither preferred.
- 1955-1959, La Paz: secretary at the apostolic nunciature to Bolivia; acquires fluent Spanish. **[A: Wikipedia; Grokipedia]** Building coordinates [R], city pin.
- 1959, Ottawa: secretary at the Holy See's mission. **[A]**
- December 1959: enters the Secretariat of State, into Montini's orbit. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- Mid-1960s: drives the founding of Villa Stritch, Via della Nocetta 63, residence for American curial clergy, and keeps a room there. **[A: americamagazine.org; il Giornale]**

**Corrections.** (1) *Ottawa institution*: the chronology lens said nunciature, the geography lens said apostolic delegation. The delegation is correct, the Holy See and Canada having established full relations only in 1969. (2) *Villa Stritch*: the geography lens put it on Via della Camilluccia, a different quarter; the afterlife lens had the correct Via della Nocetta 63, which is used. More seriously, **both lenses asserted Villa Stritch sheltered him from Italian magistrates.** It could not: it is USCCB property with **no extraterritorial status**. The claim is rejected in `date_confidence` and the stop moved back to the 1960s, where his founding role belongs. In 1987 he was inside Vatican City.

## 3. Bodyguard and bishop, 1963-1970

- Interpreter for John XXIII; English voice, travel planner and informal bodyguard for Paul VI. **[A: Wikipedia; dossier]**
- 24 December 1968: named secretary of the IOR and Titular Bishop of Horta. **[A]**
- 6 January 1969: consecrated bishop by Paul VI in St. Peter's. **[A: catholic-hierarchy.org]**
- 27 November 1970, Manila airport: Benjamín Mendoza y Amor Flores, a Bolivian painter dressed as a priest, attacks Paul VI with a kris. **[A: EWTN Vatican; National Catholic Register; America Magazine; contemporaneous UPI wire caption]**

**Corrections.** (1) *Nickname*: the pool gave "the Gorilla" as documented with "the Papon" as variant. Wikipedia gives **il Papón** for the bodyguard role, "the Gorilla" being the press epithet. Both circulate; the file prints both and says the order is unsettled. (2) *Manila*: tagged **[R]** in one lens, **[A]** in another. Event, date, place and attacker are [A]. Only **who stopped Mendoza** is contested: most accounts credit Marcinkus, and President Marcos publicly claimed he disabled the man himself with a karate chop. Both printed, neither chosen.

## 4. The IOR presidency and the Sindona years, 1971-1974

- October 1971: president of the Institute for the Works of Religion, in the Torrione di Niccolò V. **[A]**
- Board member, Cisalpine Overseas Bank, Nassau, with Sindona and Calvi, c. 1971-1982. **[A: fraudauditing.blogspot.com court-document summary; dossier; `licio_gelli.journey.json`, which names him on that board]**
- 24 April 1973: questioned by US prosecutors about $14.5 million in counterfeit bonds delivered to the Vatican in July 1971. No charge. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- 8 October 1974: Franklin National Bank declared insolvent. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Michele Sindona'; TIME archive]**

**Corrections.** (1) *Age*: Wikipedia's "age 48" at appointment is impossible, since he turned 49 on 15 January 1971; the dossier inherited the error and the campa says forty-nine. (2) *Franklin National*: the pool pinned Franklin Square, where the bank originated, but its headquarters stood in Mineola from 1960 until the failure (liherald.com).

**Gap.** The IOR's Sindona losses are reported everywhere as "tens of millions" and nowhere as a figure.

## 5. Ambrosiano, 1975-1982

- Banco Ambrosiano, Milan's second private bank, on the Piazza Paolo Ferrari block; chairman Roberto Calvi, a sworn member of Licio Gelli's Propaganda Due. The IOR becomes shareholder and partner. **[A: Intesa Sanpaolo archive; Forbes; `licio_gelli.journey.json`]**
- Banca del Gottardo, Lugano: accounts of United Trading Company and the Panamanian shell structure. **[A: Tribunale di Roma accounting consultancy, philipwillan.com]**
- 6 August 1978: Paul VI dies at Castel Gandolfo. **[A]** Marcinkus's presence that evening is **not** attested; the dossier places him in the inner circle only in the days after, and the campa claims no more.
- 1 September 1981: the letters of patronage, paired with Calvi's counter-letter of 27 August 1981 indemnifying the IOR. **[A]**
- 26 September 1981: Titular Archbishop of Horta and Pro-President of the Pontifical Commission for Vatican City State. **[A: catholic-hierarchy.org]**
- April 1982: "Roberto Calvi merits our trust." **[A: Christianity Today, 'The Vatican Bank Scandal', Sept 1982, verbatim]**
- 5 June 1982: Calvi's letter to John Paul II. 10 June: Calvi flees on a false passport. June: Ambrosiano fails owing roughly $700 million to $1.5 billion, the dossier's $1.3 billion inside that range. 18 June: Calvi found under Blackfriars Bridge. **[A: Wikipedia; Forbes; MoneyWeek]**

**Corrections.** (1) *Letters of patronage*: the pool dated them 1982-1984, which is when they were litigated; they were signed 1 September 1981. (2) *1981 title*: not "vice-president of the Governorate", a later-era name, but Pro-President of the Pontifical Commission for Vatican City State, dated 26 September 1981. (3) *Lugano*: the pool's "Basel/Banca del Gottardo, Lugano" conflates two cities; the bank was headquartered in Lugano and the "Basel/" prefix is struck. (4) *Calvi's letter*: verbatim only through "gravest damage" (Irish Examiner; corroborated by Wikipedia and murdermap.co.uk). The trailing "It must be avoided at all costs." is **unattested**, surviving only in a search summary that echoed the query, and is not printed; the Forbes 2019 piece does not contain this wording. The quote stays **[R]**, a translation with no facsimile located.

**Not staged, deliberately.** Calvi's death under Blackfriars Bridge is Calvi's own stop, on his own future file (queued, QUEUE.md:826). It is named in one flat clause inside Marcinkus's Vatican stop and carried in `suggested_refs`, per the standing rule and the interlock lens's instruction.

## 6. The immunity years, 1982-1987

- 1982: the Holy See's line is that the IOR owes nothing. Cardinal Giovanni Benelli criticises Marcinkus publicly from inside the Curia. **[A: TIME 1982, verbatim]**
- 25 May 1984, Geneva: settlement signed. **AMOUNTS CONTESTED, both printed:** UPI gives $250 million from the IOR inside an accord above $500 million, $244 million of it to 120 creditor banks; Wikipedia's Calvi article gives $224 million. The attested characterisation is "in recognition of moral involvement"; the pool's "in a spirit of reciprocity" is **not attested anywhere located** and is not used.
- 25 February 1987: Milan arrest warrant for Marcinkus, Luigi Mennini and Pellegrino De Strobel as accessories to fraudulent bankruptcy, upheld on appeal in April. **[A]**
- February 1987: Vatican communiqué of "profound astonishment", invoking Article 11 of the Lateran Treaty. **[R]** on the wording, see Sources.
- 17 July 1987: the Court of Cassation holds the three to be officials of a central body of a foreign state, immune absent Holy See authorisation. The case ends here. **[A: In re Marcinkus, Mennini and De Strobel, ILR]**
- 1987: *Marcinkus v. NAL Publishing, Inc.*, 138 Misc. 2d 256, 522 N.Y.S.2d 1009 (N.Y. Sup. Ct. 1987). **[A]**

**Corrections.** (1) *The flagged contradiction dissolves*: the dossier said indicted in 1982, Wikipedia said never charged. Both describe the same 25 February 1987 warrant at different levels of formality, quashed 17 July 1987, so no indictment proceeded and no trial was held. A dating error, not a source conflict. (2) *End of the case*: the pool put the dispositive ruling at October 1988; no such ruling was located, and the operative decision is the Cassation of 17 July 1987. (3) *NAL suit*: not a pre-publication injunction as the pool had it, the novel being already published, so he sought to halt ongoing distribution; the dust jacket also carried an apparent quotation attributed to him.

**Gap, and it is the central one.** No court ever heard evidence about his conduct. Everything asserted about his intent rests on journalism, on the letters, and on his own denials.

## 7. Withdrawal and death, 1989-2006

- June 1989: the IOR presidency is abolished, a cardinals' commission and lay directorate taking over. **[A: Wikipedia]**
- 30 October 1990: resigns as Pro-President of the Pontifical Commission for Vatican City State. **[A: catholic-hierarchy.org]**
- 1990: returns to the Archdiocese of Chicago, then to Sun City, Arizona; assistant priest at St. Clement of Rome. **[A]**
- c. 2004: last substantial interview, to Philip Willan ('In search of Marcinkus'). The bungalow and cactus detail is **[R]**, Willan's own reportage.
- 20 February 2006: dies at Sun City, aged 84; cause undisclosed. **[A]**
- 2 March 2006: funeral Mass at Holy Name Cathedral, Cardinal Francis George chief celebrant; interment the same day at St. Casimir Cemetery, Section 41, Lot BP Sudvid., Grave 3, beside his parents. **[A: Chicago Catholic, 5 March 2006; Find a Grave 13445283]**

**Corrections.** (1) *Tenure*: the geography lens said fourteen years and the afterlife lens said eighteen; 1971 to 1989 is eighteen. (2) *Funeral date*: the pool said 24 February 2006, which is the date of the Daily News-Sun obituary notice; the funeral was 2 March 2006. (3) *No monument*: that there is no plaque, statue or marker to Marcinkus at the Torrione di Niccolò V is **[R]**, inferred from the failure of every consulted source to name one, not from a survey on site. It closes the file as such.
