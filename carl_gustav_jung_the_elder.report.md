# Carl Gustav Jung the elder (1794/95-1864): research report

*Basel physician, anatomist, rector, freemason; paternal grandfather and namesake of the psychiatrist Carl Gustav Jung. Rumoured, in Basel and in his own family, to be the natural son of Goethe.*

**Naming discipline.** Three men in this atlas carry a version of this name and must not be collapsed: **Carl Gustav Jung the elder** (this subject, d. 1864); **Carl Gustav Jung** the psychiatrist, his grandson (`jung.journey.json`, 1875-1961); and **Dr Carl Jung of Mainz** (`carl_jung_of_mainz.journey.json`), tracing the claimed family line to a supposed seventeenth-century Mainz ancestor. German sources spell this subject *Karl*; English and French, *Carl*; Bair, *Carl Gustav I*.

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction or tradition.

---

## 1. Birth and parents (Mannheim, 1794 or 1795)

- Born at **Mannheim**, in the **Electoral Palatinate** of the Holy Roman Empire, to **Franz Ignaz Jung** (1759-1831), a Catholic physician originally of Mainz, and **Sophie Ziegler**. **[A: en.wikipedia "Karl Gustav Jung"; fr.wikipedia for Franz Ignaz's dates and Mainz origin]**
- **The birth date is contradicted and is NOT resolved here.**
  - *7 September 1795*: en and de Wikipedia, "Karl Gustav Jung"; also the date already carried by `carl_jung_of_mainz.journey.json`, which explicitly calls the 1794 dating (there ascribed to Wikidata) wrong.
  - *7 June 1794*: fr.wikipedia, citing Frank McLynn, *Carl Gustav Jung* (1996).
  - *1794*, year only: **Deutsche Biographie**, GND 130921572, citing **NDB 10 (1974), p. 677**; the **Basler Professorengalerie** catalogue (A 40, Abb. 109, "1794-1864"); en.wikipedia's own "Carl Jung" article.
  - The scholarly apparatus favours **1794**; day-precision exists only for **1795-09-07**. The journey file carries 1795-09-07 for continuity with the existing atlas file and flags the dispute in `date_confidence`. **Not adjudicated.**
- Correction to the research pool: the fighting around Mannheim in these years was the **War of the First Coalition against Revolutionary France**, not a campaign "against Napoleon," who held no German command in 1794-95. Franz Ignaz Jung is said to have been managing a field hospital in it at the time of the birth. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Gap:** no reachable source gives the parish or house of birth. Jaffé's footnote (below) records that the **Jesuitenkirche baptismal register at Mannheim** was searched, but no reachable source prints the entry.

## 2. The Goethe legend

The reason the subject was queued, and the part of the file where the evidence must be handled with tongs.

- **The claim:** that Jung the elder was the illegitimate son of **Johann Wolfgang von Goethe** by Sophie Ziegler. **[R: family and civic tradition, flagged unverified by every source that carries it]**
- **He himself apparently enjoyed telling it.** Bair, *Jung: A Biography* (Little, Brown, 2003), ch. 1, p. 8: "He became notorious among the good burghers of Basel, not only for his liberal political views but also for the story he so enjoyed telling, that he was an illegitimate son of the poet Goethe." **[A as to Bair's statement; R as to the paternity]**
- **The grandson told it and retracted it.** The psychiatrist "entertained contemporaries with the family legend that his paternal grandfather was the illegitimate son of Goethe and his German great-grandmother, Sophie Ziegler," and "later in life he pulled back from this tale, saying only that Sophie was a friend of Goethe's niece." **[A: en.wikipedia "Carl Jung", citing Bair]**. Correction: this telling belongs to his **student years at Basel from 1895**, not to Küsnacht, where he lived only from 1909. The pool misassigned the place.
- ***Memories, Dreams, Reflections*** (ed. Aniela Jaffé, trans. R. and C. Winston, Pantheon 1963), footnote to "School Years": "The wife of my great-grandfather (Franz Ignaz Jung, d. 1831), Sophie Zegler, and her sister were associated with the Mannheim Theater and were friends of many writers. The story goes that Sophie Zegler had an illegitimate child by Goethe, and that this child was my grandfather, Carl Gustav Jung." **[A as text]**
- **The negative check is on the record.** Jaffé, same page: "No proof of this item of family tradition has been found in the available sources, the archives of the Goethehaus in Frankfurt am Main and the baptismal register in the Jesuitenkirche in Mannheim." **[A]**
- **His diaries are silent.** MDR: "My grandfather says not a word about it in his diaries, however. He mentions only that he once saw Goethe in Weimar, and then merely from behind!" **[A as text]**. That sighting is the only attested contact of any kind between the two men.
- **Contradiction, flagged not resolved:** Bair has the elder Jung *enjoying* the story; MDR has the grandson calling it "an annoying tradition." These do not agree about how the family held it.
- **Absence check on the Goethe side.** `goethe_full.journey.json` contains no mention of Jung, of an illegitimate son, or of the legend; the only string match is "jungen" in *Die Leiden des jungen Werthers*. **The legend is attested only from the Jung side.** **[R: stated as a gap]**
- Jaffé on the grandson: "Jung used to speak of this stubbornly persistent legend with a certain gratified amusement, for it might serve to explain one subtle aspect of his fascination with Goethe's Faust; it belonged to an inner reality, as it were." **[A]**
- Correction to the pool: Bair's "healthy dose of skepticism" line (p. 10) belongs to a **different** legend, the Humboldt park-bench story of how Carl Gustav I became Swiss. It must not be attached to the Goethe claim.

## 3. Heidelberg and the Burschenschaft (1815-1817)

- Enrols in medicine at **Heidelberg**. **[R: fr.wikipedia; no matriculation year in any reachable source]**
- **Converts from his family's Catholicism to Protestantism** under liberal and nationalist student influence, and joins the Burschenschaft **Teutonia**, dated 1815. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1816: doctorate in medicine and the natural sciences, summa cum laude**, dissertation *De evolutione corporis humani*. **[A: en/de/fr Wikipedia; Bair: "graduating summa cum laude with a doctorate in medicine and the natural sciences, awarded for a dissertation entitled 'De evolutione corporis humani.'"]**
- **1817: the student gathering at Jena, then the Wartburgfest** (18-19 October 1817), marking the tercentenary of the Reformation and the fourth anniversary of Leipzig. **[A: en.wikipedia; de.wikipedia "Wartburgfest"]**

## 4. Berlin: the Charité and the Hausvogtei (1818-1821)

- Trains in **surgery and ophthalmology under Johann Nepomuk Rust at the Charité**. **[A: en/fr Wikipedia; Bair: "when only twenty-four, he was called to Berlin to become surgical assistant to the legendary Charite Hospital ophthalmologist, Johann Nepomuk Rust"]**. Wikipedia gives no year, and Bair's "twenty-four" is hostage to the disputed birth year; the journey file places it 1818 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.
- **23 March 1819: Karl Ludwig Sand, Burschenschaft student and a friend of Jung's, assassinates August von Kotzebue.** In the crackdown Jung is found holding a **geological hammer Sand had given him**, is convicted, and serves **thirteen months**. **[A: en.wikipedia; Bair: "Carl Gustav I, whose crime was mere possession of the kind of hammer used in mineralogical research, a gift to him from Sand, was imprisoned for thirteen months in Hansvogtai prison."]**
- **Correction, and a gap closed:** "Hansvogtai" is a garbling. The institution is the Berlin **Hausvogtei**, the royal court prison on Hausvogteiplatz, a gaol into the mid-nineteenth century (de.wikipedia). That supplies the city the pool left blank: **Berlin, 52.5133 / 13.3958**, consistent with his being at the Charité already. **[A]**
- **1821: released, looks for research openings in Paris.** **[A: en/de Wikipedia]** The pool's "forced to flee" compresses the record: he was tried, served the sentence, then left.

## 5. Basel: chair, museum, rectorate (1822-1845)

- **1822: settles in Basel**, appointed to the medical faculty of the **University of Basel**, then seated at the **Rheinsprung**. The Kollegienhaus at Petersplatz opened only in 1939, so any pre-1939 university pin there is anachronistic. **[A: fr.wikipedia; de.wikipedia "Universität Basel"]**
- Teaches **surgery, anatomy and obstetrics**. **[A: fr/de/en Wikipedia]**
- **1823:** pamphlet *Können in Basel die nöthigsten Hülfsanstalten zur Förderung medizinischer Studien gegründet werden?* **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1824: founds the Basel anatomical collection**, ancestor of the Anatomisches Museum Basel. **[A: de.wikipedia "Anatomisches Museum Basel"]**. The museum's present home at Pestalozzistrasse 20 sits in an institute opened in 1921 and is not his building; his own theatre's site is a **gap**.
- **1824: Swiss citizenship and the Freedom of the City of Basel**, while holding assistant professorships in surgery, anatomy and midwifery. **[A: en/de Wikipedia]**
- **1828: Rector of the University of Basel**, with reforms and charitable foundations; the same year's *Über das Verhältniss der Anatomie zu der medicinischen Wissenschaft* is his **rectoral address**. **[A: en.wikipedia; corroborated by `carl_jung_of_mainz.journey.json`]**. The pool's geography lens wrongly called the rectorate undatable.
- **1831:** the drama *Die Revolution: Originaldrama in drei Akten*, under the pseudonym **Demius**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **1842: the Bürgerspital moves to the Markgräflerhof on Hebelstrasse**, becoming the 322-bed municipal hospital attached to the university. fr.wikipedia credits Jung with renovating the Bürgerspital without a date; the 1842 move is the event that fits his tenure. **[A for the move: de.wikipedia "Universitätsspital Basel"; R for identifying it with his credited renovation]**
- **1843:** *Über die seitliche Erhabenheit in dem Lateral-Ventrikel des menschlichen Gehirnes*. **1845:** *Über das Gewölbe in dem menschlichen Gehirne*. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

## 6. Freemasonry (1844-1856)

- **Venerable master of the Basel lodge Zur Freundschaft und Beständigkeit** (founded 1808). **[A: fr.wikipedia, citing *Le Guide du Franc-Maçon*, Groupe de Recherche Alpina, 1998]**. **Gap:** no reachable source gives the lodge house's address, so no located pin.
- **1844: the Grande Loge Suisse Alpina is founded**, unifying the cantonal lodges; **Jonas Furrer**, later the first president of the Confederation, is its first grand orator. **[A: fr.wikipedia; de.wikipedia "Schweizerische Grossloge Alpina"]**. **Correction:** neither article names a founding city, so the pool's "Zurich (founding seat)" is unsourced; the journey file uses the Switzerland centroid. **[R]**
- **1850-1856: second Grand Master of the Alpina.** **[A: fr/de Wikipedia; Bair: "In 1850, he was so prominent throughout Switzerland that he was elected Grand Master of the United Swiss Freemasons societies."]**. **Correction:** "Basel, civic seat of the Alpina Grand Mastership" is an invented location; no source seats the grand mastership at Basel.
- **Atlas gap, stated as a gap:** searching `*.journey.json` for "Alpina", "Grand Lodge" and "Grand Master" returns **no existing file naming the elder Jung**. `theodor_reuss.journey.json` has Reuss founding an Anational Grand Lodge at Basel in 1916, fifty-two years after Jung's death: chronology, not connection.

## 7. Internal medicine, psychological treatment, Zur Hoffnung (1855-1864)

- **First chair of internal medicine at Basel, held to his death.** Year **contradicted**: en and de Wikipedia give **1855-1864**; fr.wikipedia gives "à partir de 1854". The journey file uses 1855 and flags 1854.
- **An anonymously printed article proposing psychological treatment**, quoted by Bair (ch. 1, p. 12), who says he proposed, "indirectly and discreetly, for Basel remained conservative in the extreme", that such a department would suit any medical university: "I am not thinking of a lunatic asylum of the usual sort... but of an institute that would admit ill people of all kinds, whose cure could be attempted by means of psychological treatment." **[A: the quotation is verbatim in Bair and attributed by her to the elder Jung]**. **Correction to the pool:** the source is an anonymous printed article, not a "letter/proposal", and Bair breaks the quotation with "he wrote". **Gap:** its title, journal and year are given nowhere reachable.
- **1857: founds Zur Hoffnung**, for children with mental disabilities. **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Gap:** the site within Basel is not established.
- fr.wikipedia separately credits him with writing that "prefigured psychotherapy" without naming it; probably the same article, but that identification is **[R]**.

## 8. Household, marriages, children

- **Three marriages: Virginie de Lassaulx, Elisabeth Reyenthaler, Sophie Frey; at least thirteen children.** **[A: en.wikipedia]**
- **Gap, explicit:** no reachable source gives the marriage years or which wife bore which children.
- **Ernst Georg Jung**, son, architect. **[A: en/de Wikipedia]**
- **Johann Paul Achilles Jung (1842-1896)**, youngest son, pastor, **father of the psychiatrist**. **[A: en.wikipedia "Carl Jung"; the Deutsche Biographie GND record links him as a relation]**. The pool tagged this [R], which under-tags it; what remains unknown is **which wife was Paul's mother**.
- **Domestic manner.** Ernst described his father as "severe, a ruthless disciplinarian" (Bair, p. 13). **Correction:** the following words, "whose children were eager to leave his house," fall **outside** Bair's quotation marks and are her narration, not Ernst's speech.
- **Late public reputation.** Bair, p. 12, reproducing a characterization current in Basel and set by her in quotation marks with its own endnote: "a man of irresistible charm, kind-hearted, tactful, and humorous." She draws the contrast with the children's testimony herself, a page later.
- **The diaries.** Bair: he "kept meticulous diaries about the state of his health and records of persistent coughing, frequent fever, nausea, indigestion, and depression." These are the diaries the grandson reports as silent on Goethe. **[A: Bair]**. **Gap:** their present location is unstated.

## 9. Death and afterlife

- **Dies at Basel, 12 June 1864.** **[A: en/de/fr Wikipedia; Deutsche Biographie GND 130921572]**
- **Age at death is contradicted** because the birth year is: 68 on the 1795 dating, 70 on the 1794 dating. The pool's own lenses split, 68 against seventy, for the same death. Neither is asserted in the journey file.
- **Buried in the Wolfgottesacker, Basel.** **[A: en/fr Wikipedia]**. All three lenses of the pool gave a different and wrong pin for this one grave. The cemetery lies in eastern Basel between the Wolf goods yard and the Dreispitz depot, 47°32′25″N 7°36′30″E = **47.5403 / 7.6083** (de.wikipedia "Wolfgottesacker").
- **1876: commemorative bust commissioned from Ferdinand Schlöth; moved to the Skulpturhalle Basel in 2008.** **[A: en/de Wikipedia]**
- **Portrait in the Basler Professorengalerie, A 40, Abb. 109, dates 1794-1864.** **[A: de.wikipedia]**. **Venue correction:** the gallery hangs in the **Aula of the Museum an der Augustinergasse**, not in the Kollegienhaus. The catalogue is also an independent carrier of the 1794 birth year.
- **1875: the grandson is born at Kesswil and given his name.** **[A: en.wikipedia "Carl Jung"; `jung.journey.json`]**
- **1895: the grandson matriculates in medicine at Basel.** **[A for the year: en.wikipedia]**. The precise day 1895-04-18 appears **only** in `jung.journey.json` and could not be corroborated externally; flagged low-confidence.
- **1911: the Weimar psychoanalytical congress**, Goethe's town, with the legend in the air around the grandson. **[R: `jung.journey.json`]**
- **1956: memorial tablets at the Bollingen Tower** for the ancestors of the House of Jung, including "the Basel doctor, the legend-line of Goethe." **[R: `jung.journey.json`, citing MDR]**

## 10. Interlocks used, and interlocks refused

**Used, a real relation exists:** the **psychiatrist Carl Gustav Jung** (grandson and namesake, same faculty, carrier of the legend); **Goethe** (the claimed father, seen once from behind at Weimar, and knowing nothing of the claim in his own file); **Dr Carl Jung of Mainz** (the family's claimed ancestor, and the line through which Franz Ignaz Jung's Mainz practice descends; that file already narrates this subject's arc and should be read as the parent file).

**Refused, co-location only:** **Paracelsus** (Basel chair three centuries earlier, thematic parallel, no textual link); **Pestalozzi** (Basel civic-reform milieu, nothing connecting him to Zur Hoffnung); **Theodor Reuss** (Basel masonry from 1916); **Samuel Preiswerk** (queued; the psychiatrist's *maternal* grandfather. Their descendants married, but that marriage postdates the elder Jung's death and no source attests contact. Named here for disambiguation only, never in a campa).

**Canonical geography:** none of Kaaba (21.4225, 39.8262), Temple Mount or Buenos Aires touches this itinerary. The inherited canonical pin is **Weimar 50.9795, 11.3235** from `jung.journey.json`; Mannheim inherits `carl_jung_of_mainz.journey.json`'s 49.48778 / 8.46611, Mainz its 49.9929 / 8.2473, the Basel Rheinsprung its 47.5596 / 7.5886, all byte-identical. Paris appears only as an actual 1821 stop, 48.8566 / 2.3522.

**Other coordinates corrected in the journey file:** Heidelberg Alte Universität 49.4108 / 8.7063; Wartburg 50.96611 / 10.30639; Skulpturhalle 47.5603 / 7.5796; Kleinhüningen 47.5825 / 7.5936; Küsnacht 47.3172 / 8.5836.
