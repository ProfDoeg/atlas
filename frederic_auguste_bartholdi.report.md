# Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi (1834-1904): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, 2026-08-14. Slug: `frederic_auguste_bartholdi`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition. Contradictions are flagged and left standing, never silently split.

The brief carried one question: which Statue of Liberty replica near Buenos Aires belongs to the Bartholdi line. Section 7 answers it, and not cleanly. Two Buenos Aires Liberties exist, both dated to the same fortnight of 1886, and the "twenty-five days before New York" formula usually attached to the Barrancas de Belgrano cast belongs demonstrably to the other one.

---

## 1. Colmar and Paris, 1834-1856

**Birth.** 2 August 1834, 30 rue des Marchands, Colmar **[A: fr.wikipedia "Auguste Bartholdi"; en.wikipedia "Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi"; Musée Bartholdi]**. The house was bequeathed to the city in 1907 and opened as the Musée Bartholdi in 1922 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

**Parents.** Jean Charles Bartholdi (1791-1836), *conseiller de préfecture*, died when the boy was about two; mother Augusta Charlotte, née Beysser (1801-1891), daughter of a mayor of Ribeauvillé **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Correction:** the father was a prefectural administrative councillor, not a magistrate.

**The move to Paris.** Usually compressed into "the father died and the family moved." The sources do not support it: fr.wikipedia places the relocation at the Lycée Louis-le-Grand enrolment in **1843**, seven years after the death **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. The journey file keeps the two apart. That the family kept the Colmar house and returned each summer is museum tradition **[R]**.

**Schooling, training, studio.** Lycée Louis-le-Grand 1843-1851, baccalauréat 1852 **[A: both wikipedias]**. Painting and sculpture under Ary Scheffer on the rue Chaptal, earlier under Antoine Étex **[A: Musée de la Vie Romantique; standard biographies]**; reading Scheffer as the origin of his preoccupation with silhouette is interpretation **[R]**. His mother bought him a rue Vavin workshop in 1853, kept forty years **[A: fr.wikipedia; Musée Bartholdi archives]**.

**First Salon.** *The Good Samaritan*, Paris Salon, 1853 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Anachronism flagged:** an earlier draft placed this Salon at the Palais de l'Industrie, a building erected in **1855** and housing the Salon only from 1857 **[A: en.wikipedia "Palais de l'Industrie"]**. **The 1853 venue was not established from any reachable source; recorded as a gap.** The journey stop carries no coordinate finer than Paris.

**First public monument.** Bronze General Jean Rapp, Champ-de-Mars, Colmar, **31 August 1856** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. He was twenty-two.

## 2. Egypt, 1855-1856, and the Suez proposal, 1869

**The voyage,** with a circle of Orientalist painters including Jean-Léon Gérôme **[A: en.wikipedia; Bartholdi 1885]**. In his own words: *"When I was twenty years old I traveled in Egypt with the painters Gerome and Belly ... this country had a very considerable effect upon my taste for sculpture"* **[A: Bartholdi, *The Statue of Liberty Enlightening the World* (New York, 1885), Internet Archive `statuelibertyen00bartgoog`]**.

**Itinerary contradiction flagged.** en.wikipedia: "Egypt and Yemen." fr.wikipedia: "Égypte et Arabie." The **Aden** leg is carried **[R]**: Musée Bartholdi notes describe 1855-56 Egypt-Yemen sketches, but no reachable source documents the itinerary.

**Alexandria, Giza, Abu Simbel** are the usual anchors **[A: NPS; standard biographies]**. That he derived his rule of long-distance legibility from Abu Simbel is an interpretive commonplace repeated by the NPS **[R]**.

**Suez, 1869.** A colossal robed woman with a raised light for the canal's northern mouth, pitched to Khedive Isma'il Pasha, refused on cost **[A: en.wikipedia; NPS]**. Bartholdi: *"At this period I was expecting to execute a statue of Egypt for the Suez Lighthouse. I even laid before Ismail Pacha a project."* **[A: Bartholdi 1885]**.

**Title contradiction flagged, three ways.** fr.wikipedia: *"L'Égypte éclairant l'Orient."* en.wikipedia: *"Egypt Carrying the Light to Asia."* NPS: *"Egypt (or Progress) Carrying the Light to Asia."* An earlier draft produced a fourth form, *"L'Égypte éclairant l'Asie,"* by blending fr.wikipedia's syntax with en.wikipedia's noun. That blend is a fabrication and is retracted. No title is preferred here.

## 3. The war year and the Lion, 1870-1880

**Service.** Squadron leader of the National Guard, then attached to Giuseppe Garibaldi's Armée des Vosges **[A: both wikipedias]**. fr.wikipedia calls him *aide de camp* but specifies his function as **government liaison agent for supply**; the journey file uses the function, not the title.

**Headquarters contradiction flagged.** An earlier draft anchored the service at Dijon. Per en.wikipedia "Army of the Vosges," Garibaldi found Dijon already surrendered in November 1870 and fell back on **Autun**, besieged by the Prussians; the army re-entered Dijon in January 1871 and lost it again on 1 February. Dijon was no stable headquarters; the stop is anchored at Autun with the oscillation named.

**Colmar's defence.** That he personally organised or joined it in 1870 is **[R]**, repeated in museum material, not traced to a document.

**Lion of Belfort. Four problems, all flagged:**

1. **Dates contested.** en.wikipedia gives 1871-1880; fr.wikipedia gives 1875-1879. Not resolved; the stop uses the 1880 completion and names the disagreement.
2. **Orientation corrected.** en.wikipedia "Lion of Belfort": designed to face east toward Prussia but **reversed to face west** to avoid provoking the German Empire, only an arrow beneath its paw pointing back at the border. Accounts saying it "faces east toward Germany" invert this.
3. **Method corrected.** Not cut from living rock; assembled from separately carved red sandstone blocks brought to the site **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
4. **Coordinate corrected.** 47.6367 / 6.8647 per en.wikipedia. A circulating 47.6392 / 6.8627 is ~300 m off and discarded.

**Freemasonry.** Lodge Alsace-Lorraine, Paris, 1875 **[A: both wikipedias]**. Its 1875 meeting address was not found; the stop uses the Grand Orient building on rue Cadet as an approximate pin and says so **[R for the coordinate]**.

## 4. Laboulaye's table and the first crossing, 1865-1871

The project's seed is a remark of Édouard de Laboulaye's, reported by Bartholdi as direct speech: *"if a monument were to be built in America as a memorial to their independence, I should think it very natural if it were built by united effort, if it were a common work of both nations"* **[A: Bartholdi 1885, on the 1865 Glatigny dinner]**. Note the evidentiary shape: Laboulaye's words as recalled by Bartholdi twenty years later, with no contemporaneous record reached. Part of the scholarship treats the evening as a founding legend; the stop is carried **[R]**.

On the 1871 voyage: *"In the course of the voyage I formed some conceptions of a plan of a monument, but I can say that at the view of the harbor of New York the definite plan was first clear to my eyes"* **[A: Bartholdi 1885]**. He chose Bedloe's Island because federal ground belonging to no single state suited a monument meant for the whole union **[A: NPS]**. He met Grant and crossed the country by rail **[A: Bartholdi 1885, naming Grant, Meade, Sheridan, Peter Cooper, Cyrus Field, Agassiz and Colonel Forney among his introductions]**. **Niagara Falls [R]:** the tour is attested, the Niagara stop traditional and unconfirmed in any primary source reached.

## 5. Rue de Chazelles and the American decade, 1875-1884

**Construction.** Built full scale by Gaget, Gauthier et Cie on the rue de Chazelles in hammered copper over an iron armature designed by Gustave Eiffel's firm **[A: NPS; en.wikipedia]**. Bartholdi on his medium: *"the system of hammered copper, which, from an artistic point of view, offers elements of excellence when it is well treated"* **[A: Bartholdi 1885]**. His description of Eiffel's four-point pylon is quoted in the journey file.

**Atlas cross-reference.** `gustave_eiffel.journey.json` pins rue de Chazelles at **48.8823 / 2.3054** and Liberty Island at **40.6892 / -74.0445**, both inherited byte-identical. That file's authorship flag is carried here unresolved: fr.wikipedia credits the skeleton to Maurice Koechlin under Eiffel, en.wikipedia to Eiffel himself.

**The arm at Philadelphia, 1876** **[A: NPS; en.wikipedia "Centennial Exposition"]**. **Corrections:** the climb to the torch balcony cost **fifty cents**, not a quarter, on which en.wikipedia and es.wikipedia "Estatua de la Libertad" agree independently; and the arm reached Philadelphia only in **August 1876**, too late for the exhibition catalogue.

**Lafayette, Union Square.** Executed 1872, cast 1873, dedicated 6 September 1876 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Correction:** en.wikipedia "Marquis de Lafayette (Bartholdi)" gives the donors as **French residents of New York**, an expatriate subscription, not a gift from the French people to the city. The stated motive, gratitude for American relief sent to France during the Franco-Prussian War, sits awkwardly with the centennial framing; both readings are recorded, neither preferred.

**Madison Square.** The arm stood there until 1882, then returned to Paris **[A: NPS]**. The month of its arrival on the square is an inference from the Centennial's 10 November close, and is flagged in the stop rather than defaulted.

**Marriage. Two contradictions, neither resolved.**

- *Place.* fr.wikipedia specifies *"l'hôtel de ville de Newport (Rhode Island)."* en.wikipedia says Providence, uncorroborated. **Newport preferred**; Providence appears to be an error.
- *Year.* en.wikipedia gives 1876; fr.wikipedia's *"la même année"* attaches the 20 December date to **1884**. **1876 preferred** only because it fits his documented American stay, en.wikipedia "Statue of Liberty" having him return to Paris in 1877. That is inference, not citation. An earlier draft's claim that "the contemporaneous record" fixes 1876 is **withdrawn: none was reachable.**

**Bartholdi Fountain, Washington.** Bought by Congress for $6,000, installed at the base of Capitol Hill in 1878, removed 1926 for the Meade Memorial, moved into the United States Botanic Garden only in **1932** **[A: en.wikipedia "Bartholdi Fountain"; USBG records]**. **Corrections:** cast by **Durenne**, not Val d'Osne; the lamps were originally **gas**, electrified later in the 1880s, so "electric lights" is anachronistic for 1878; the upper vasque rests on three kneeling **tritons**, nymphs below. **Coordinate:** 38.887127 / -77.012669, discarding 38.8894 / -77.0353 (~2.9 km off) and 38.8869 / -77.0089 (~330 m off).

**The head at the Exposition Universelle, Paris, 1878** **[A: NPS]**. Bartholdi's own measurements: *"The head is 4.40 metres in height. The eye is .65 metres in width."* The torch platform, he adds, *"will easily hold twelve persons."* **[A: Bartholdi 1885]**.

**Patent.** U.S. design patent D11,023, 1879 **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**. Filing and grant dates not obtained; the stop defaults to 1879-01-01 and says so.

**Presentation. Date corrected by four years.** en.wikipedia "Statue of Liberty": *"The completed statue was formally presented to Ambassador Morton at a ceremony in Paris on July 4, 1884."* The minister was **Levi P. Morton**. An earlier draft's 4 July **1880** is wrong. A separate Morton ceremony, the first rivet, is dated 24 October 1881 by es.wikipedia; neither is 1880.

**Shipment.** Some 350 pieces in over 200 crates, loaded at Rouen onto the frigate *Isère*, reaching New York 17 June 1885 before a crowd of some two hundred thousand **[A: NPS]**.

## 6. The dedication, 1886

Bedloe's Island, 28 October 1886, President Grover Cleveland presiding, in heavy fog **[A: fr.wikipedia; NPS]**. **Correction: Bartholdi was 52, not 62.** Born 2 August 1834, he had turned fifty-two twelve weeks earlier; the circulating "sixty two" is a ten-year error. Commander of the Legion of Honour, 1886 **[A: both wikipedias]**; no exact date within the year was established.

## 7. The replicas, and the Buenos Aires question

**Barrancas de Belgrano.** A three-metre cast-iron Liberty painted to imitate bronze, in the park above the Belgrano bluffs **[A: es.wikipedia "Barrancas de Belgrano"]**. fr.wikipedia "Répliques de la statue de la Liberté" states plainly: *"Petite réplique en fer réalisée par Bartholdi dans le même moule qu'une des répliques de Paris"* **[A]**. en.wikipedia "Replicas of the Statue of Liberty" adds a base inscription *"Val d'Osne, 8 Rue Voltaire, Paris"* with the year **1884**, and an inauguration of **3 October 1886**.

**Date contradiction flagged, unresolved.** es.wikipedia reports two candidate years, **1875** and **3 October 1886**, cites neither and decides neither. 1875 would predate the New York statue entirely and cannot stand as written. The journey stop carries 1886-10-03 and names the dispute.

**The second Buenos Aires Liberty.** One crowns the façade of the **Escuela Normal Superior n.° 9 Domingo Faustino Sarmiento**, Avenida Callao 450, Balvanera, architect Carlos Morra (1854-1926) **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **Two corrections:** the institution is *not* a "Colegio Nacional Sarmiento" (no Liberty at the Colegio Nacional de Buenos Aires; checked and negative on en.wikipedia), and the building's inauguration is **3 October 1886**, not 8 September. es.wikipedia says of this façade statue that it was inaugurated *"25 days before its more famous version"* in New York.

**The finding.** In the sources reached, the "twenty-five days before New York" formula belongs to the **school façade statue**, not to the Barrancas de Belgrano cast most repetitions attach it to. Both carry 3 October 1886; only one is documented as a Bartholdi-mould casting. The Bartholdi-line answer to the brief is **Barrancas de Belgrano**; the "25 days" story is the school's. Two facts fused elsewhere, separated here.

**What "by Bartholdi" means.** The tension between fr.wikipedia's *"réalisée par Bartholdi"* and a commercial foundry mark is resolved only as **[R]**: these small cast-iron Liberties were plausibly Val d'Osne catalogue products made from his models rather than pieces he supervised. Inference, not a sourced claim.

**Villa Aberastain, San Juan.** A Liberty in Plaza Libertad, formerly Plaza de Mayo, installed **9 July 1931** **[A: es.wikipedia "Villa Aberastain"]**. The plinth bears **1810-1910** and an Argentine shield: the centenary is the **1910** centenary of the May Revolution, for which the statue was reportedly requested from France by Federico Cantoni, destined for the Parque de Mayo in San Juan city and later moved under Mayor Marcos Zalazar. An earlier draft's "installed in 1931 to mark the centenary" garbles this; 1931 marks arrival, nothing else. The France-donation element is local testimony (*"se deduce"* in the source) and stays **[R]**.

**Paris, Île aux Cygnes.** Quarter-scale (11.50 m) bronze, inaugurated by President Sadi Carnot on **4 July 1889** **[A: en.wikipedia "Île aux Cygnes"]**. **Two corrections:** the donors were the French expatriate community living **in the United States**, giving to Paris, not "the American community in Paris" (the direction is commonly inverted); and it originally faced **east**, toward the Eiffel Tower, turned west toward New York only in **1937**, so "facing downriver toward the sea" describes the post-1937 object. Also in Paris: the 1/16-scale 1889 bronze at the Musée d'Orsay, the 1878 plaster maquette at the Musée des Arts et Métiers **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

## 8. Late years, death, afterlife

Chicago, World's Columbian Exposition with his wife, 1893 **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Equestrian Vercingetorix, Clermont-Ferrand, 1903 **[A: en.wikipedia]**.

**Death.** 4 October 1904 at 82 rue d'Assas, Paris 6e, aged 70 **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Cause contested:** fr.wikipedia gives only *"maladie"*; en.wikipedia and several sketches say tuberculosis. Not settled. **Coordinate:** ~48.8437 / 2.3319; a circulating 48.8477 / 2.3299 is ~450 m north by the Luxembourg and discarded.

**Burial.** Montparnasse, 28th division, under an allegorical Renown co-sculpted with Auguste Rubin **[A: fr.wikipedia]**. **Coordinate:** 48.8386 / 2.3255; a circulating 48.8422 / 2.3266 falls outside the cemetery and is discarded.

**Musée Bartholdi.** House bequeathed 1907, museum opened 1922; its 1990 exhibition *"D'un album de voyage: Auguste Bartholdi en Égypte, 1855-1856"* showed the Egyptian travel photographs and drawings **[A: fr.wikipedia]**.

## 9. Atlas interlocks

**Named in campa** (direct relation to Bartholdi only):

- **`gustave_eiffel`**: designed the internal iron pylon and flexible armature carrying the copper skin. Direct, documented collaboration. Canonical pins inherited byte-identical.
- **`marques_de_lafayette`**: Bartholdi executed the bronze Lafayette dedicated in Union Square, 6 September 1876. A one-directional relation across a century, the sculptor studying and portraying the man.

**Deliberately not named in campa.** The map already shows co-location; shared geography is not a relation.

- 1876 Philadelphia Centennial co-presence: `alexander_graham_bell`, `elizabeth_bessie_burden`, `frank_lloyd_wright`. The 1886 dedication as backdrop or iconographic co-reference: `edward_bellamy`, `francis_bellamy`, `policarpa_salavarrieta`.
- Buenos Aires institutional or toponymic overlap: `bernardo_houssay`, `sarmiento`, `belgrano`. Colmar geography: `parzival`, `paracelsus`, `san_antonio_abad`.
- `anita_garibaldi` (Bartholdi served Giuseppe Garibaldi, who has no atlas file; Anita died 1849), `napoleon` and `lionel_royer` (Rapp was Napoleon's aide, Royer painted Vercingetorix): no relation runs through Bartholdi.
