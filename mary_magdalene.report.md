# Mary Magdalene — report

**Sources.** Canon spine: the four Gospels (Luke 8:2-3, 7:11-50; Mark 15:40-16:9; John 11, 12, 19-20; Acts 1-8) for Magdala, the Galilee ministry, the Passion watch, and the Resurrection morning. Provençal spine: Jacobus de Voragine's *Legenda Aurea*, ch. 96 (William Caxton's 1483 English, read via christianiconography.info's reader's edition), for the exile voyage, the Marseille cycle, the thirty years at the Sainte-Baume, and the death at Villalata/Saint-Maximin. Supplementary: Gregory the Great's *Homily 33* for the medieval conflation of the sinner, Mary of Bethany, and the Magdalene into one figure; Victor Saxer's *Le culte de Marie Madeleine en Occident* and Wikipedia for the Vézelay/Saint-Maximin relic rivalry and the 1279 translation under Charles II of Anjou; and this atlas's own `giles.journey.json` and `pica_bourlemont.journey.json` for the three shared Provençal pins.

**Judgment calls.** (1) I followed the curator's steer and made the Camargue/Saint-Gilles ending primary; I did not include the competing Ephesus/Eastern tradition of her death, since the brief asked for the Provençal spine, not a survey of every rival legend — the one rivalry the brief *did* ask for (Saint-Maximin vs. Vézelay) is kept honestly unresolved as two coda stops. (2) Nearly every Provence-era date (AD 36 onward) is my own chronological scaffolding — the *Golden Legend* gives sequence but almost no years — so those stops are marked `"traditional"` in the sense the sibling files use it (the event is canon-to-the-legend) rather than a claim of textual precision; only the 1279 Charles II ceremony, which is independently documented, is marked `"attested"`. (3) I placed the Luke 7:36-50 "sinful woman" scene at Capernaum by convention, since the Gospel itself never names the town — flagged in the campa text as tradition's placement, not scripture's. (4) The "rock" of the Marseille shipwreck legend has no fixed traditional coordinate in the sources I found, so I anchored it at the Château d'If / Frioul islands off Marseille as an evocative, clearly-marked "traditional" placement rather than inventing false precision.

**Gaps.** The *Legenda Aurea* gives no coordinates finer than "Marseille," "Villalata," or "the desert" — the Sainte-Baume grotto and Saint-Maximin basilica coordinates are the actual/later-consecrated sites, used as the traditional pins the pilgrimage itself now uses. I did not find a named year for Vézelay's relic claim more precise than "mid-11th century," so I used 1050 as a round anchor.

**Time-folds, deliberately marked.** The richest one: Mary Magdalene crosses the empty Camargue ground near the future Saint-Gilles-du-Gard roughly six centuries before Giles the hermit ever sets foot there — she is walking through another saint's future address, and the campa says so outright, the way `giles.journey.json` folds Caesarius of Arles and Charlemagne into one hermit's lifetime. The Saint-Gilles pin (43.6767, 4.4318) is now shared by three files: hers, `giles.journey.json`, and `pica_bourlemont.journey.json`'s 1178 pilgrimage stop.

**Five richest episodes.** (1) The seven devils cast out at Magdala — the whole rest of her canon-life pivots on one sparse Lukan sentence. (2) Noli me tangere — her own two surviving quotes, "Rabboni" and "I have seen the Lord," make her literally the first Easter preacher, to the apostles who are supposed to preach to her. (3) The rudderless boat from Jaffa, carrying Lazarus, Martha, Maximin, Marcella, Cedonius, and Sarah — persecution turned into providence. (4) The prince-and-princess-of-Marseille cycle: a storm, a widower marooned two years on a rock with a nursing infant, and a wife who wakes from death describing a pilgrimage she made unseen. (5) The thirty years in the Sainte-Baume grotto, fed at the seven canonical hours by angels instead of bread — ending in the two unreconciled tombs, Saint-Maximin and Vézelay, both left standing.

**Connections to the atlas.** Six Galilee/Jerusalem stops are pinned byte-identical to `jesus.journey.json` (Cana, Mount of Beatitudes, Nain, Jericho, the Bethany raising and anointing, Golgotha, the Tomb, the empty tomb, and the Noli me tangere garden) — she is present at, and in two cases the sole subject of, scenes already told from His side. The Sainte-Marthe/Tarascon pin is shared with `pica_bourlemont.journey.json`, which independently tells Martha's Tarasque legend from a 12th-century vantage. The Saint-Gilles pin threads three files together across a six-century gap. Together this makes Mary Magdalene one of the atlas's connective figures — Galilee to Provence, Gospel to Golden Legend, one woman's page touching four others'.
