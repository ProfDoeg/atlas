# Saint Anthony of Padua (Fernando of Lisbon), c.1195-1231, research report

*Slug: `st_anthony_of_padua_1195_1231`. Written 2026-08-18 from the verified pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock and afterlife lenses, adversarially corrected). Calendar: Julian, the life being wholly pre-1582.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, later hagiographic layer, or a claim the primitive sources do not carry. Contradictions are flagged, not resolved.

**The evidentiary spine.** Four bodies of material at four distances: (1) the **Assidua** (*Vita prima*), c.1232, the primitive life and only near-contemporary source; (2) Julian of Speyer's ***Vita secunda*** (c.1235) and the ***Dialogus*** (c.1245-46); (3) the later legendaries, above all **Rigaud's *Rigaldina*** (c.1300), sixty to seventy years downstream and sole witness for most French episodes; (4) his own sermons, critically edited only in 1979. The famous stories, without exception, sit in layer 3 or later. The one strong documentary claim sits outside the hagiographic series.

---

## 1. Lisbon: the parts of the birth that are not documented

- Born in **Lisbon**, given name **Fernando**; working date **c.1195** **[R]**. The exact date "15 August 1195" is flagged **by the Basilica's own literature** as a "very late tradition." The Assidua gives no birthdate.
- Surname **Martins de Bulhões**, parents **Vicente Martins** and **Teresa Pais Taveira** **[R]**: fifteenth-century writers, judged "less certain" by Niccolò Dal-Gal (1907). The primitive lives name no family at all.
- **Canons Regular of St Augustine at São Vicente de Fora** at about fifteen **[A, Assidua; CE; Wikipedia]**, conventionally 1210. Transferred to **Santa Cruz, Coimbra**, for roughly eight years of scriptural and Latin study **[A, Assidua]**; the stated motive, that visiting relatives broke his concentration, is **[R]**, a gloss laid over an attested move.

**Gap, stated as a gap:** eleven years as an Augustinian canon are the largest documented block of his life and the least narrated. Almost nothing survives of them beyond "he studied."

## 2. Ordination: an internal contradiction, not resolved

**[R].** No primitive source gives a year and the **Catholic Encyclopedia gives none**. Wikipedia's "ordained around age 19" puts it near **1214**; devotional chronologies print **1219**. **These are incompatible**, and the pool's chronology entry carried both at once, which the adversarial pass caught. The defensible statement is **"a priest by 1220."** The file enters 1219 only to order the segment, says so in `date_confidence`, and demotes the stop to [R], venue included. That he was made **guestmaster** is [R], one source.

## 3. The five martyrs and the change of order

- **Berard of Carbio and four companions** beheaded in Morocco, **16 January 1220** **[A, en.wikipedia]**; their major shrine is **Santa Cruz, Coimbra** **[A]**, the house where Fernando was a canon. The return of the bodies decisively affected his decision to leave the Augustinians **[A, Assidua]**.
- **Contradiction flagged:** the pool credited **King Afonso II** with ransoming the bodies. The Berard article names nobody; the commoner tradition credits **Infante Dom Pedro of Portugal**, then in Morocco. Demoted to **[R]** and kept out of the campa rather than picked.
- **Transfer to the Friars Minor and change of name, 1220. Contradiction flagged and carried in the file:** Wikipedia sites it at **Olivais**, the **Catholic Encyclopedia at Santa Cruz itself**. The file uses Olivais and records the disagreement. The name **Anthony** derives from the hermitage's dedication to **Anthony the Great** **[R, but early]**.

## 4. Morocco, the storm, Sicily

- **Mission to Morocco seeking martyrdom; grave illness; mission abandoned [A, Assidua].** The **town is not fixed by the earliest sources**; Marrakesh is later inference **[R]**.
- **Return voyage diverted by weather; landfall in Sicily [A as to the diversion, Assidua]. Honest gap:** the Assidua fixes no position. The pool supplied 35.8, -6.5 labelled "Strait crossing," which the adversarial pass showed to be open Atlantic ~100 km WNW of Larache, not the Strait. The stop was **renamed** to a notional Atlantic point rather than moved: either choice invents a geography the source does not give. **Messina** is the recovery point **[A]**.

## 5. The Chapter of Mats: what drops to [R]

- **Chapter of Mats at Assisi, 30 May 1221 [A, Catholic Encyclopedia; Pentecost did fall on 30 May].** His presence is attested.
- **The personal meeting with Francis of Assisi is [R], not [A].** The most consequential demotion in the pool. **No early source records a meeting.** The Assidua-derived narrative has him left unassigned and then taken by **Brother Gratian** into Romagna; en.wikipedia says only that he later "came to the attention of" Francis. The campa states the presence, then says that no early text records the two men speaking.
- **Pin inherited byte-identical** from `saint_francis.journey.json`: **43.0578, 12.5804**. For the operator: the interlock lens reported Francis's pin as 43.0707, 12.5697, which the file does not carry. The file was believed over the lens.

## 6. Montepaolo and Forlì

- **Montepaolo hermitage near Dovadola [A, Assidua]. Contradiction flagged:** one source gives c.1221-1222, another 1222-1227. The Italian record for the Eremo dates the stay **1221-1222 with a later return c.1228 as provincial**, resolving part of the spread.
- **The unprepared sermon at Forlì.** His emergence as a major preacher in the early 1220s is **[A]**; the discovery-of-the-gift narrative is **[R]** in form. **Sources disagree sharply on the year: Wikipedia 1222, CE 1224, Franciscan Media c.1227.** All three appear in the `date_confidence` and in the campa.

## 7. Bologna and the letter

- **Francis's short letter "to Brother Anthony"**, generally accepted as authentic, authorizing him to teach sacred theology provided study does not extinguish the spirit of prayer and devotion **[A, primary source; quoted in Benedict XVI, 10 February 2010]**. Wikipedia dates the entrustment to 1224.
- **Anachronism caught and corrected.** The geography lens sited the Bologna teaching and the stolen psalter at the **Basilica di San Francesco**, whose **groundbreaking was 1236, five years after Anthony's death**. The friars were then at the modest **Santa Maria delle Pugliole** house (from 1211). Stop renamed, correction in `date_confidence`.
- **The stolen psalter is [R]** and **not in the Assidua**. The "patron of lost things" devotion rests entirely on it.

## 8. France: the softest stretch in the record

Montpellier, Toulouse, Arles, the Limousin: all **[R]**, accepted in outline, poorly fixed in detail, largely dependent on the **Rigaldina (c.1300)**.

**Contradiction flagged, not resolved.** For 1226 the sources put him in three places: the **Catholic Encyclopedia** makes him **Custos Provincial in the Limousin**; **Wikipedia** has him at the **chapter at Arles** and preaching in Provence; both also have him returning to Italy on Francis's death. Both stops are kept, and each `date_confidence` names the other as its contradiction. The **Arles bilocation legend** (Francis seen in the doorway, absent in body) is **[R]**, a later accretion separate from the chapter attendance, told in the campa as a story that grew.

## 9. Provincial of the north: two dates the pool had wrong

- **[A]** that he held a senior charge after returning to Italy; **[R]** as to its name and year. Wikipedia: provincial minister of northern Italy/Romagna by c.1227. Catholic Encyclopedia: **Minister Provincial of Emilia**, shortly after the return.
- **Correction:** the pool read CE as electing him in **1230**. It does not. CE has him elected **shortly after his return (c.1227)** and **resigning at the chapter of Assisi on 30 May 1230. 1230 is the resignation year.**
- **Second correction:** the pool placed that resignation at **Padua**. The chapter was at **Assisi**; Padua is where he then settled, and the file puts the resignation at the Porziuncola pin. Modern historiography warns against projecting later territorial terminology backward onto the still-informal early structure; the campa says the title is heavier than the thing.

## 10. Rome, 1228

**Papal audience before Gregory IX [A].** The epithet is transmitted **in two forms** and both are given rather than one chosen: en.wikipedia has **"Ark of the Testament"**; the Italian tradition has **both** *arca del Testamento* **and** *scrigno*, the jewel case or treasury of the Bible. **Attribution corrected:** the sermon commission is credited by the Italian source to **Cardinal Rinaldo Conti** (later Alexander IV), not to Gregory IX.

## 11. Padua: the one documentary claim

The strongest evidence in the dossier, and not hagiographic. On **15 March 1231** the podestà of Padua, **Stefano Badoer**, modified the law of debt **"ad preces venerabilis fratris beati Antonii"**, barring imprisonment or exile of insolvent debtors who surrendered their assets **[A, the civic act itself, via it.wikipedia]**. The pool's hedge (statute real, causation merely traditional) was **too weak in the opposite direction from usual**: **Anthony is named in the act's own wording**, so the causal link is documented, not attributed. The date was corrected from bare "1231."

The **Lenten crowds "exceeding 30,000"** (Catholic Encyclopedia) remain **[R]**: medieval crowd figures of this kind are conventional and untestable. The campa reports the figure as a figure.

## 12. Camposampiero, Arcella, and the age at death

- **Camposampiero, the walnut-tree cell [A, Assidua, among the earliest-attested episodes of the death sequence].** The record for the **Santuario del Noce** dates the stay **c.19 May to 13 June 1231** and names **Count Tiso VI** as builder of the structure in the tree. **Ergotism** as cause is **[R]**, one source. **Death at Arcella, 13 June 1231**, in the Poor Clares' hospice **[A, agreed across all sources]**.
- **Age unstable.** EN and IT Wikipedia give **35**, the Arcella sanctuary page **36**. The campa writes "thirty-five, thirty-six by the reckoning the sanctuary itself prefers"; the pool's "around 36" overshot the majority reading.
- The **death-scene details** (the Marian hymn, "I see my Lord," the children crying the news unprompted) are **early**, from the Assidua dossier, and still **[R]**: legendary furniture, stated as such.

## 13. The legends, and a shrine attached to the wrong story

**Sermon to the fish** and **miracle of the mule**: both **[R]**, absent from the Assidua, undated within his lifetime. **Correction the pool caught:** the **Tempietto di Sant'Antonio** (Rimini) commemorates **the mule only**, not the fish sermon, which has **no fixed monument**; the afterlife lens had presented one shrine as the site of both. The file gives the mule the Tempietto pin and leaves the fish sermon on the shore with no monument claimed.

## 14. The corpus, and why the text is not closed

The securely authentic corpus is principally the ***Sermones dominicales et festivi*** **[A]**: Latin model-sermons for trained preachers, **not stenographic records of crowd-speech**, called by Centro Studi Antoniani close to "a treatise of sacred doctrine in sermon form." Early-modern attributions are far broader than what is now defensible. **Critical edition: 1979, 3 vols., Centro Studi Antoniani [A]. Amandine Postec** then identified Anthony fragments in **BnF NAL 3245**, a branch **not in the 1979 stemma**, so the critical text history is genuinely open. The 1981 stop closes on this rather than on the tomb.

## 15. Afterlife: what belongs here and what does not

Kept as stops (his own body, grave, title): the burial; **canonization at Spoleto, 30 May 1232** (352 days, [A], papal act); the basilica rising around the grave; the **1263 translation** and the tongue; **Doctor Evangelicus, 16 January 1946**; the **1981 examination**.

**Deliberately not staged as scenes**, per the rule that a stop belongs to whoever was actually there. Each is cited instead in `suggested_refs`/`sources` on his burial-and-basilica stop, which is what the rule licenses for a later one-directional engagement with an object genuinely his:

- **Donatello's** bronze high altar, corrected from "1448-1467" to **c.1446-1450** (he died 1466; the seven statues "after 1448"); **Titian's** three frescoes of the posthumous miracles, **April to December 1511**, Scuola del Santo (coordinate corrected to 45.4010, 11.8801), his first large-scale independent work.
- The **nine reliefs of the Cappella dell'Arca**, corrected from "1505-1562" to **1505-1577** (the resuscitated-youth panel is Cattaneo and Campagna, 1571-1577; Mosca's is the intact glass, with Stella, 1520-1529; the jealous-husband panel is Rubino and Cosini, not in the listed group).

Also: **completion standardized to c.1310**, not 1301. **UNESCO inscription of the frescoes, 2021 [A]**. **Iconography** (habit, book, lily, Christ Child) is **[R]**, a convention not attested from his own century, and gets no stop because there is nowhere to put it.
