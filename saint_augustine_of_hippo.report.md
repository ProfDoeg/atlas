# Saint Augustine of Hippo (354-430): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-24 for `saint_augustine_of_hippo.journey.json`. Aurelius Augustinus, born at Thagaste in Roman Numidia, died bishop of Hippo Regius in the Vandal siege. The file carries `calendar: "julian"` per the pre-1582 rule; the relic stops after 1582 are Gregorian by the world's clock.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or self-testimony with no independent witness · ***corrected*** the pool's adversarial pass overturned a gather claim, and the fix is in the journey file.

**The structural problem, stated once.** Nearly everything known about Augustine before 391 comes from one book he wrote about himself c. 397-400, ten to thirty years after the events, addressed to God rather than a reader, shaped to argue a thesis about grace. There is no second witness for the pear tree, the garden, Ostia, the friend at Thagaste, or the woman of fourteen years; Possidius, who knew him, covers those years by quoting the *Confessions* back. "Augustine reports X" and "X happened" are not one tag here.

## 1. Thagaste and the schools (354-371)

- Born Thagaste, Roman Numidia (Souk Ahras, Algeria), 13 November 354; pin 36.2864, 7.9511. Father **Patricius**, minor landholder and councillor, unbaptized until shortly before his death; mother **Monnica / Monica**, Catholic, born c. 331 (inferred from her stated age of 56 at death in 387). **[A: New Advent, 'St. Augustine of Hippo'; Wikipedia; *Confessions*]**
- Catechumen; boyhood illness, baptism requested and deferred; beatings at school and prayers not to be beaten. **[R and A for the narration: *Confessions* I.11, I.14.]** ***Corrected:*** the geography lens tagged this **A** while its own note said "recorded here as R pending direct primary-text confirmation." File carries R.
- Grammar school at **Madauros** (M'Daourouch), c. 365-369. Latin letters, Virgil above all. **[A: Wikipedia, 'Augustine of Hippo'; coords 36.0767, 7.82 from Wikipedia, 'Madauros']**
- **Weeping for Dido**, *Confessions* I.13. Famous, real, and **not in the verified pool**; not re-fetched. **[R]** Used in one clause of the Madauros campa.
- Year at home, age 15-16, 369-370, for lack of money. **[A: *Confessions* II]**
- **The pear tree.** **[R: *Confessions* II.]** *Pool contradiction, unresolved:* chronology lens tags it **R**, geography lens **A** (with a prose note splitting fact-of-narration from interpretation). File uses R in both places, on the adversarial pass's own recommendation.

## 2. Carthage (371-383)

- Arrives 371 for advanced rhetoric, financed partly by the Thagastan patron **Romanianus**. **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** Pin 36.8522, 10.3239 (Byrsa hill, the atlas's landmark for the ancient city).
- Long-term monogamous relationship with an unnamed woman from c. 371; son **Adeodatus** c. 372. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* IV, IX; years reconstructed]** **Permanent gap:** no source gives her name, origin, family, or fate after 385.
- Reads Cicero's **Hortensius**, c. 373, in his nineteenth year. **[A: *Confessions* III.4]** The dialogue is lost and survives in fragments largely by quotation.
- Manichaean **auditor** from c. 373, attached roughly nine years. **[A: New Advent; Britannica; Wikipedia]** Scholarship cautions that his later account of the sect is a polemicist's; unresolvable here.
- **Thagaste, teaching grammar, 373-374.** ***Corrected:*** the chronology lens dated this 375; Wikipedia's timeline and the geography lens give 373-374, with the **Carthage** rhetoric school running 375-383. 375 begins the Carthage period.
- **Death of the unnamed friend.** ***Corrected twice.*** Chronology lens: 376, Thagaste. Geography lens: c. 375-376, filed at **Carthage**, while its own prose said he could not bear "his native city." *Confessions* IV settles it: "when I first began to teach rhetoric in my native town, I had acquired a very dear friend," and then "I left the town of Thagaste, and came to Carthage." File: Thagaste, c. 374, grief as the reason for the move. *Not adjudicated:* the text says "rhetoric" where the timeline says "grammar."
- School of rhetoric at Carthage, nine years, 375-383. **[A: Wikipedia timeline]**
- **De pulchro et apto**, dedicated to the orator **Hierius**, never met. **[R: *Confessions* IV.13-15, self-report of a lost work; he cannot recall whether it ran to two books or three.]** The pool's 380 is a placeholder.
- **Faustus of Milevis**, eloquent and unable to answer the astronomical objections. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* V]** Sources give 382 or 383 and do not agree.
- Sails for Rome 383, having deceived Monica about the tide. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* V.8]** The harbour chapel where she prayed is not locatable; pin is the city.

## 3. Rome and Milan (383-386)

- Severe illness soon after reaching Rome. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* V.9]** **Gap:** no address for his Roman lodging is attested; pin is the Roman Forum, 41.8925, 12.4853.
- Wins the imperial chair at Milan on **Symmachus**'s recommendation, 384; takes it up late that year. **[A: *Confessions* V.13; Britannica]** Pin 45.4642, 9.19.
- **Hearing Ambrose, 384-386.** ***Corrected:*** the geography lens put this at the Basilica of Sant'Ambrogio (45.4624, 9.1758). Coordinates fit; the church is wrong. Ambrose commissioned it 379-386 in a martyrs' burial ground as the *Basilica Martyrum*; it was never his see, and his cathedral stood where the Duomo stands. File uses **45.4642, 9.1914** and drops the stop rather than stage Augustine in a building no source puts him in.
- **The panegyric and the drunk beggar**, 385. **[A: *Confessions* VI.6]** ***Corrected:*** the text names **no emperor**. "Boy-emperor Valentinian II" is a scholarly identification, and one of the 385 panegyrics was for the consul **Bauto**. File says "the emperor" and puts the identification in `date_confidence`.
- Monica arrives 385; a marriage is arranged with a girl below legal age; the woman of fourteen years is shipped back to Africa without Adeodatus and vows never to know another man; Augustine, unable to wait two years, takes another. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* VI, VI.15]** Her side is unrecoverable.
- **Libri Platonicorum**, c. 386. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* VII]** Identification with Plotinus and Porphyry in **Marius Victorinus**'s Latin is reconstruction: **[R]**. Augustine names no title, no translator.

## 4. The garden, the villa, the font (386-387)

- **Simplicianus** on Victorinus's public profession; **Ponticianus** on Antony of Egypt and the two officials at Trier. **[A as self-report: *Confessions* VIII.2, VIII.6]** Antony never met Augustine; the link runs only through the secondhand telling, and Antony is not staged.
- **The garden conversion**, late summer 386, "tolle lege," Romans 13:13-14. **[R strictly: *Confessions* VIII.12, written eleven to fourteen years later, no witness on record but Alypius. New Advent, Britannica and Wikipedia repeat his account; none corroborates it.]** `date_confidence` says "attested only as self-testimony."
- **Resignation of the chair.** ***Corrected:*** the geography lens gave "early 387," which puts it *after* the Cassiciacum winter and reverses the sequence. New Advent gives autumn 386; *Confessions* IX has him wait out the vintage vacation and cite **lung** trouble, not "chest and throat."
- **Cassiciacum**, autumn-winter 386, villa of **Verecundus**; *Contra Academicos*, *De beata vita*, *De ordine*, *Soliloquia*. **[A for the retreat and works: *Confessions* IX.4; New Advent]** **[R for the site]**: **Cassago Brianza** (45.733, 9.283) is traditional and disputed.
- **Baptism by Ambrose, Easter vigil 24-25 April 387**, with Adeodatus and Alypius. **[A: *Confessions* IX.6; Britannica; New Advent]** ***Corrected:*** drop the "335" baptistery date. Two baptisteries survive under the Duomo; octagonal **San Giovanni alle Fonti**, where Ambrose baptized, is of his own episcopate (374-397), and 335 attaches to **Santo Stefano alle Fonti**. File says "late fourth century."
- **Te Deum legend** (the hymn improvised at the font): late, no early support, rejected by hymn scholarship. **[R, doubly flagged: no hymnological source located, so even the rejection rests on the dossier.]** The campa states the legend and its lateness both.

## 5. Ostia and the return (387-391)

- **Vision at Ostia**, autumn 387. **[R: *Confessions* IX.10, no external witness]** Pin 41.7558, 12.2917.
- **Monica's death at Ostia**, autumn 387, aged 56, buried there (Santa Aurea later built over the site). **[A: *Confessions* IX.11; Wikipedia, 'Ostia Antica']** ***Corrected:*** the chronology lens inverted the disagreement. New Advent gives **387**; the **English Wikipedia** article gives 388. *Confessions* IX.11 puts the death in the thirty-third year of his life, and 56 is his own figure, not a tradition.
- Her instruction to lay her body anywhere and remember her only at the altar is *Confessions* IX.11, **not** in the verified quote pool. **[R for the wording]** Given as reported speech, never inside a `quote` field.
- Months in Rome among the monastic houses, writing *De moribus ecclesiae catholicae et de moribus Manichaeorum*. **[A: New Advent]**
- Lands in Africa 388; family property converted into a lay ascetic community holding goods in common, c. 388-391. **[A: New Advent; Britannica; Wikipedia]**
- **Death of Adeodatus.** **[R]** ***Corrected:*** chronology lens 390; Wikipedia 372-388; *De magistro* gives a terminus of 388-389. File states **c. 388-390, unfixable**, and the campa says no source records the day. *Confessions* IX.6: "quickly did You remove his life from the earth."

## 6. Hippo Regius (391-430)

**Canonical pin inherited byte-identical from `san_cristobal.journey.json`: 36.8869, 7.7539**, used at every Hippo stop. The geography and afterlife lenses used 36.8825, 7.75; the interlock lens names the san_cristobal value canonical.

- **Forced ordination as presbyter, 391**, by **Valerius**, under congregational pressure. **[A for the ordination: Britannica; New Advent; Wikipedia]** **[R for the mechanics]**: forced ordination is a late-antique literary form as well as a real pressure.
- Valerius grants a garden for a clergy monastery and lets a presbyter preach in his bishop's presence, against African custom. **[A in substance: standard biography; Wikipedia, 'Hippo Regius']**
- **Debate with Fortunatus, 392**, two days, transcript surviving as *Acta contra Fortunatum Manichaeum*; he cannot answer and leaves Hippo. **[A]** No month in reachable sources. The 393 council address on the creed (*De fide et symbolo*) **[A]** is folded into this stop.
- **Coadjutor bishop, then bishop on Valerius's death.** **[A: New Advent and Britannica c. 395/396; Wikipedia 395; both stand.]** It cuts across a Nicene canon. The **Megalius of Calama** detail (refusal, a circulated charge, then consecration) is **not in the verified pool**: **[R]**, left out.
- **Confessions**, thirteen books, c. 397-400. **[A: New Advent; Wikipedia gives 397-398]**
- **Episcopal complex and bishop's court**, 396-430: *audientia episcopalis*, ransoming captives, letters by the hundred, training **Alypius** and **Possidius**. **[A: Possidius, *Vita Augustini*]**
- **Letter 93 to Vincentius**, c. 408, defending coercion of the Donatists on Luke 14:23. **[A: Epistula 93, NPNF]** He states in the letter that he had held the opposite view and was changed by results, not by argument.
- **The Circumcellions** (armed Donatist bands, lime-blindings of Catholic clergy): standard background, **not in the verified pool**: **[R, flagged]**. One campa clause.
- **Conference of Carthage, 411.** ***Corrected, the pool's most substantive error:*** the chronology lens said Augustine **presided**. He did not. Wikipedia, 'Conference of Carthage (411)': convened by **Honorius**, presided by the commissioner **Marcellinus**, who "found in favour of the orthodox party, which led to the violent suppression of the Donatists." Augustine was the leading Catholic spokesman and drafted the case. The bishop counts (286 / 284) are **not in the pool**: **[R]**, so the campa says "hundreds."
- **De civitate Dei**, begun 413, completed c. 426, twenty-two books; occasioned by Alaric's sack of Rome in 410. **[A: New Advent; Wikipedia]** Book XVI holds both the ruling on the monstrous races (XVI.8) and the denial of the antipodes (XVI.9).
- **Pelagian controversy**, 411-430, against Pelagius, Caelestius and **Julian of Eclanum**; *Contra Iulianum opus imperfectum* unfinished at death. **Retractationes**, 426-427. **[A: New Advent]**
- **Death 28 August 430**, Vandal siege under Geiseric, fever, penitential psalms hung on the wall. **[A: New Advent after Possidius; Britannica; Wikipedia gives age 75]** Buried at Hippo; Possidius says church and library survived the later burning, and why is undocumented beyond his bare claim.

## 7. The relics (c. 484 - 1900)

- **Cagliari**, carried by the African bishops expelled by **Huneric**. **[A: Wikipedia]** ***Corrected:*** the afterlife lens gave "c. 500s"; Huneric reigned 477-484, so **c. 484**. **Gap:** the church of deposit is named nowhere reachable. File uses city coordinates 39.2278, 9.1111.
- **Pavia, San Pietro in Ciel d'Oro**, c. 720, by **Peter, bishop of Pavia**, uncle of **Liutprand**. **[A for the tradition]** ***Corrected:*** four afterlife entries used 45.1856, 9.1497, some 800 m off; the article gives **45.1914, 9.1550**. **Bede** as earliest witness is unreachable and stays **[R]**, unasserted in the file.
- **Arca di Sant'Agostino**, **Giovanni di Balduccio**, 1362-1365, three tiers, over 150 figures. In **1695** a marble box of bones, wood and glass vials is found; rival Augustinian orders dispute it; **Benedict XIII** approves the bones **1728**, ecclesiastically not forensically, with no chain of custody to Hippo. Friars expelled 1785, relics to the cathedral 1799, reinstalled 1896. **[A]**
- **Annaba, 1842:** a *cubitus* from Pavia, in a glass tube inside the arm of a recumbent marble statue. **[A: French Wikipedia, 'Basilique Saint-Augustin d'Annaba']** ***Corrected twice:*** the geography lens pinned this to the Hippo ruins, the afterlife lens to 36.8975, 7.7539 (1.9 km off); the French article gives **36.8819, 7.7447**. Basilica 1881-1900, architect **Joseph Pougnet**; ***corrected***, consecrated **29 March 1900**, not April. Reopened 19 October 2013.
- **Souk Ahras today:** no statue, monument or museum documented in the town's own reference article beyond a landmark called "the olive tree of St. Augustine." **[A as a negative finding: Wikipedia, 'Souk Ahras']** No colonial-era installation or removal date confirmable either way. Used as the file's closing stop.
