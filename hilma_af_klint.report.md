# Hilma af Klint (1862-1944): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-03 for the Atlas of Journeys. Swedish painter and medium; author of the 193-work cycle "Paintings for the Temple," withheld from view by the terms of her own will.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in line. **[R]** = reconstruction or secondary retelling not traced to a primary document this session. Where reachable sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and left standing. An earlier pass carried duplicated dates, invented precisions, and coordinates in open water; each is marked **CORRECTED** with the earlier reading given, so the passes can be compared rather than one quietly overwriting the other.

---

## 1. Karlberg and Adelsö, 1862-1879

**Birth. [A: en.wikipedia, "Hilma af Klint"]** 26 October 1862 at Karlberg Palace, Solna; fourth of five children of Victor af Klint, naval officer, and Mathilda née Sonntag.

- **CORRECTED: what Karlberg was.** The first pass called it "a naval academy." It housed the Royal War Academy (Krigsakademien), founded 1792, training army and naval cadets jointly until 1867, when the naval school moved to Skeppsholmen **[A: en.wikipedia, "Karlberg Palace"]**. Coordinates 59.34111 / 18.02194, matching the plotted 59.3411 / 18.0219.

**Hanmora. [R: sv.wikipedia]** Family summers at Hanmora on Adelsö; biographers place her earliest botanical drawing here. The farm building is not geolocated in anything reached; the pin is the island centroid.

**Valerius and Hermina. [A: TheArtStory, "Hilma af Klint: Life and Legacy"; en.wikipedia]** From 1879 she attends séances led by the medium and court photographer Bertha Valerius. Her sister Hermina dies in 1880. **Contradiction, not resolved:** the sources disagree on which came first, and so on whether the spiritualism is consequence or context.

## 2. Tekniska skolan and Konstakademien, 1880-1887

- **CORRECTED: the enrolment year.** The first pass gave 1879. **[A: sv.wikipedia]** gives 1880: "Began studies at Tekniska skolan (now Konstfack) in portrait painting under Kerstin Cardon." 1879 is the séance year duplicated onto the wrong event; the geography lens already carried 1880, so the two lenses contradicted each other inside one dossier. 1880 adopted.

**Royal Academy. [A: en.wikipedia; sv.wikipedia]** Admitted at twenty in 1882; five years of portraiture, botanical drawing, and landscape; graduation with honours in 1887 and a state stipend. Women had been accepted since 1864, so she belongs to one of the first generations, not the first. On graduation she is granted a studio in the Ateljébyggnaden at Hamngatan 5, kept for decades: every canvas of the Temple cycle is painted there.

- **CORRECTED: the Academy's address.** The first pass plotted 59.3283 / 18.0686 and noted the institution "moved premises over the twentieth century." Both wrong. **[A: en.wikipedia]** places it on Fredsgatan at 59.32861 / 18.06417, in a building held since 1780. Pin tightened to 59.3286 / 18.0642; the false note deleted.

## 3. Edelweiss, De Fem, the High Masters, 1896-1905

**De Fem. [A: en.wikipedia; sv.wikipedia]** In 1896 af Klint forms De Fem with Anna Cassel, Cornelia Cederberg, Sigrid Hedman, and Mathilda Nilsson. Sessions open with prayer, meditation, a sermon, and New Testament exegesis before contact is sought; automatic writing and drawing follow.

- **CORRECTED: the meeting address.** The first pass placed the founding at Engelbrektsgatan 31, Cassel's family apartment (59.339 / 18.0765). Unsupported: **[A: sv.wikipedia, "Anna Cassel"]** says only that Cassel lived "at various addresses on Östermalm," and nothing places De Fem at her home. Demoted to an unlocated central-Stockholm plot.
- **CORRECTED: the Edelweiss dates.** The first pass gave "c.1889-1896 ... Before De Fem." **[A: sv.wikipedia]** dates the Edelweissförbundet membership to 1896-1897, concurrent with De Fem; the 1889 start has no source. **[A: en.wikipedia]** supports precedence in principle ("The Five began their association as members of the Edelweiss Society"), so the ordering inside 1896 is unestablished. Printed as overlap.
- **CORRECTED: the name of the entities.** "The High Ones" in some first-pass entries, "the High Masters" in others. **[A: en.wikipedia]** gives the High Masters (Höga Mästare), including Gregor. Other names in the literature: Amaliel, Ananda, Clemens, Esther.
- **CORRECTED: the Kandinsky arithmetic.** The first pass called 1896 "eleven years before Kandinsky's claimed priority." His first abstraction is dated 1910 or 1911: fourteen to fifteen years. The priority framing is standard **[A: en.wikipedia; sv.wikipedia; Foundation]**; the count was wrong.
- **CORRECTED: the Theosophical Society.** 1904 in one first-pass lens, 1905 in another. **[A: Foundation]** "She joined the Theosophical Society in May 1904"; **[A: sv.wikipedia]** gives 1905. Foundation adopted, variant recorded.

**Livelihood. [R: TheArtStory]** Around 1900 she is a draughtsman for the Veterinary Institute in Stockholm. Its premises are unlocated; no pin is claimed.

## 4. Paintings for the Temple, 1906-1916

- **CORRECTED: the commission, and a claim dropped.** The first pass carried a 1904 séance directing her to paint "on the astral plane" and "proclaim a new philosophy of life." No such directive appears in en.wikipedia, sv.wikipedia, or the Foundation chronology. **[A: Foundation]** dates the Amaliel commission to January 1906, painting from November 1906. The 1904 directive is dropped; its **[R]** tag had been correct.
- **CORRECTED: when De Fem ended.** The first pass had the séances "continuing to 1906." **[A: Foundation]** says the group worked "until dissolving in early 1908"; **[A: sv.wikipedia]** gives 1896-1907. Either way it overlaps the first two years of the cycle.

**Primordial Chaos. [A: en.wikipedia; TheArtStory]** Twenty-six works from November 1906. Steiner will later single the group out as the strongest of the commission. **The Ten Largest. [A: en.wikipedia]** 1907; ten works on the four stages of life, each roughly 240 by 320 cm.

**Steiner in the studio. [A: en.wikipedia; TheArtStory; Voss 2020 at second hand]** In 1908 Steiner is brought to Hamngatan. He approves only of Primordial Chaos, "the best, symbolically," criticises her role as a passive medium, and warns that the work will not be understood for decades. **Contradiction, not resolved:** sources disagree on the length of secrecy advised, and her will's twenty years may or may not echo him. Only the fact and approximate content of the visit are consistent.

- **CORRECTED: the pause, and the caregiving.** **[A: en.wikipedia]** "apparently, stopped painting for four years"; **[A: sv.wikipedia]** "1908-1912: Production hiatus." But the first pass attached "a period she spent caring for her blind mother," which belongs to 1918-1920 **[A: sv.wikipedia; Foundation]**.
- **CORRECTED: resumption versus completion.** The first pass had 1912 as completion. **[A: en.wikipedia]** dates the cycle 1906-1915, "in two phases, with an interruption between 1908 and 1912." 1912 is the resumption; the second phase runs to 1915.
- **CORRECTED: the Swan.** Dated to 1914 alone by the first pass. **[A: en.wikipedia]** gives October 1914 to March 1915; **[A: sv.wikipedia]** adds the count, twenty-four paintings.

**Malmö. [R: TheArtStory]** In 1914 she shows a conventional landscape at a Baltic-region collective exhibition in Malmö, alongside early abstract work by Kandinsky. Hall unidentified; the pin sits on the Baltic Exhibition grounds now under Pildammsparken. One secondary account, no catalogue reached. **The end. [A: en.wikipedia; TheArtStory]** The Dove and the Altarpieces close the cycle in 1915, and af Klint records that the guidance ceases. Parsifal follows in 1916 as independent work.

## 5. Munsö and Helsingborg, 1917-1930

**The studio. [A: sv.wikipedia]** "1917: Opened new studio on Munsö at Bona fideikommiss near Furuheim summer house."

- **CORRECTED: the Munsö coordinates.** Three entries plotted Munsö at 59.452 / 17.534, roughly 6 km north of the island, in open water. **[A: en. and sv.wikipedia, "Munsö"]** give 59.40111 / 17.56167, with Bona gård just east of Munsö church. All three stops moved to about 59.405 / 17.560.
- **CORRECTED: who paid for it.** The first pass said Anna Cassel "finances" the studio. **[A: sv.wikipedia, "Anna Cassel"]** describes a loan of 3,000 kronor "which they later settled up," and **[A: Foundation]** records that af Klint had bought Furuheim in 1913.

**Studier över Själslivet. [R: TheArtStory]** From 1917, an unpublished manuscript past 1,200 pages. The page count is repeated in secondary literature, not verified against the manuscript.

**Furuheim, mother, societies. [A: sv.wikipedia; Foundation; TheArtStory]** 1918, she moves to Furuheim with her mother, who dies in 1920; in the same year af Klint leaves the Theosophical Society for Steiner's Anthroposophical Society.

**Helsingborg. [A: sv.wikipedia]** From 1920 she winters in Helsingborg on the Öresund. No address and no stated reason in anything reached; the pin is the city centre and the motive is a gap. The journey dates the stop inside 1920 without a month, to hold travel order against the Dornach visit.

## 6. Dornach, 1920-1930

**The Goetheanum. [A: en.wikipedia; sv.wikipedia]** From 1920 she travels annually to Dornach: "1920-1930: Annual visits to Dornach, Switzerland for anthroposophical research." The first pass began the sequence in 1921 in one entry, 1920 in another; 1920 adopted. **Coordinates [A: en.wikipedia]** 47.48611 / 7.62028; the atlas already carries the hill from steiner.journey.json at 47.4865 / 7.6203 (building) and 47.4872 / 7.6193 (Schreinerei), inherited byte-identical rather than re-derived.

- **CORRECTED: Elsa Cochius.** The first pass named "Elsa Cochius" as a companion met at Dornach, treated as a competing attestation against Peggy Kloppers-Moltzer. No source reached contains a Cochius. **[A: en.wikipedia]** states unhedged: "Around 1920, in Dornach ... af Klint met Dutch eurythmist Peggy Kloppers-Moltzer." Cochius removed; if Voss carries her, that is a gap, not an attestation.
- **CORRECTED: the fire and the arson claim.** The first Goetheanum burned on the night of 31 December 1922 to 1 January 1923 **[A: en.wikipedia]**, which says only that "some claim by arson" and that arson "is not proven." The first pass asserted that "contemporary police reports attribute" the fire to arson, a basis the source does not supply. Her presence on the hill is undocumented; the journey tags it **[R]**.
- **CORRECTED: London, 1928.** The exhibition at the World Conference on Spiritual Science is confirmed **[A: en.wikipedia]**, with Kloppers on the organising committee. Two attached details are not, and are dropped: the sea voyage with rolled canvases, and the story that she was "initially excluded" and added after Kloppers-Moltzer's insistence. Venue unconfirmed; Rudolf Steiner House was built in stages from 1926, so its coordinates are a flagged candidate only.

**Wendingen. [A: en.wikipedia]** Through Kloppers-Moltzer she approaches the editors of the Amsterdam magazine Wendingen about an exhibition. Nothing comes of it. The concrete second Goetheanum is completed in 1928, three years after Steiner's death. The first pass also asserted that she donated paintings to the Goetheanum archive; nothing reached corroborates it, and the journey states that as a gap.

## 7. Lund, Djursholm, the will, 1935-1944

**Lund. [A: sv.wikipedia]** In 1935 she moves to Lund with Thomasine Andersson, a nurse with her since her mother's death, who stays to the end. No address reached.

- **CORRECTED: the war watercolours.** The first pass dated "The Blitz" and "The Fight in the Mediterranean" to 1935. **[A: en.wikipedia]** says only "made in the 1930s," so the year was invented; and the titles are anachronistic, the London Blitz beginning September 1940. **[A: sv.wikipedia]** gives her late watercolour period as 1920-1941. **Contradiction, not resolved:** kept **[R]**, left open.
- **CORRECTED: how long she was in Djursholm.** The first pass had her living out "her final years" there. **[A: sv.wikipedia]** has her in Lund from 1935 and records "1944: Moved to cousin Hedvig af Klint's villa in Djursholm." Months, not years.
- **CORRECTED: the vehicle.** A bicycle in one lens, an unresolved bus-versus-tram question in another. **[A: Foundation]** states she "died on 21 October 1944 after a streetcar accident." Bicycle deleted, question closed. **[A: sv.wikipedia]** registers the death in Danderyd, the municipality containing Djursholm.
- **CORRECTED: two coordinates.** Djursholm is 59.39722 / 18.08750 **[A: en.wikipedia]**; the afterlife lens had 59.3986 / 18.0742, disagreeing with the geography lens's plot of the same death. Galärvarvskyrkogården is 59.32806 / 18.09417 **[A: sv.wikipedia]**; the first pass had 59.3258 / 18.105, in open water. Unified on 59.3972 / 18.0875 and 59.3281 / 18.0942.

**The grave and the will. [A: en.wikipedia]** Burial on Djurgården is confirmed; the family-plot detail, that she lies beside her father, is **[R]**, read across two sv.wikipedia articles. Over 1,200 paintings and roughly 125 notebooks and diaries pass to her nephew, Vice Admiral Erik af Klint, with the condition that the abstract work not be shown for at least twenty years after her death.

## 8. The embargo and the return, 1944-2023

**The boxes. [A: en.wikipedia]** Opened "at the end of the 1960s," with little public notice. Where the works stood for twenty-five years is a gap: **[A: sv.wikipedia, "Anna Cassel"]** records that by 1932 Cassel owned Villa Furuheim and af Klint's studio building, so the familiar story of the crates sitting in af Klint's own Munsö studio lacks a source.

**Moderna Museet. [A: en.wikipedia]** "In 1970, her paintings were offered as a gift to Moderna Museet i Stockholm, but the donation was declined." **CORRECTED:** the museum stands at 59.32639 / 18.08361; the first pass plotted 59.3255 / 18.0894, in the water.

- **CORRECTED: the Foundation's year.** The first pass gave 1981. **[A: sv.wikipedia]** dates Stiftelsen Hilma af Klints Verk to 1972; **[A: en.wikipedia]** gives no year. 1981 is the year Erik af Klint died (1901-1981), the likely origin of the error.
- **CORRECTED: the 2013 tour.** The first pass said it "tours Europe in 2013." **[A: en.wikipedia]** "A Pioneer of Abstraction" opened at Moderna Museet February to May 2013 and toured to Berlin, Málaga, Copenhagen, Oslo, and Tallinn through 2015. The Stockholm launch was omitted.
- **CORRECTED: Järna.** The first pass said a museum was "approved in 2017." **[A: en.wikipedia]** Snøhetta "presented plans," costs estimated at €6 to 7.5 million. No approving body is named.
- **CORRECTED: Tate Modern 2023.** Called "a major Hilma af Klint exhibition." **[A: en.wikipedia]** lists "Hilma af Klint & Piet Mondrian: Forms of Life," a two-hander, not a solo retrospective.

**Other attested stations. [A: en.wikipedia]** Åke Fant at a Nordic conference, Helsinki 1984; LACMA's "The Spiritual in Art," 1986; Arnhem 2010 (no journey stop, for length); the 55th Venice Biennale, 2013; the Hilma af Klint Room agreed with Moderna Museet, February 2018 (folded into the 2013 stop); the Guggenheim's "Paintings for the Future," 2018-2019.

## Contradictions left standing

1. Séances (1879) or bereavement (1880) first; causation undecided.
2. Theosophical Society, May 1904 (Foundation) against 1905 (sv.wikipedia).
3. De Fem's end, 1907 (sv.wikipedia) against early 1908 (Foundation).
4. The length of secrecy Steiner advised; her will's twenty years may be independent of him.
5. The war watercolours: dated to the 1930s, titled for events of 1940-1941.
6. Where the works stood 1944-1970; Cassel owned Furuheim and the studio from 1932, and "stored on Munsö" has no located source.
7. Death place: Djursholm in the accounts, Danderyd in the civil record.

## Gaps declared

- **Julia Voss, *Hilma af Klint: A Biography* (2020)**, the standard modern life, was not reachable in any form. Several first-pass claims plausibly derive from it (the Engelbrektsgatan address, Elsa Cochius, the wording of Steiner's remarks); none checkable here. The largest hole in the dossier.
- **The notebooks**, roughly 125 volumes plus Studier över Själslivet, are unpublished; nothing here rests on a primary manuscript reading.
- **No Spanish-language critical edition** of the notebooks or translated catalogue raisonné could be confirmed to exist. A gap, not a negative finding.
- **Unlocated places**, each a declared approximation in the journey: De Fem's meeting room, the Veterinary Institute, the Theosophical lodge, the Helsingborg and Lund addresses, the Hanmora farm building, the Malmö hall, the 1928 London venue, the Helsinki hall.
- **Whether the Järna building was ever built**, and **whether af Klint was at Dornach the night of the fire**: both undocumented in anything reached.

## Atlas interlocks

Genuine crossings, named in the journey campa: **Rudolf Steiner** (steiner.journey.json), who stood in her studio in 1908 and whose hill she climbed yearly for a decade; **Helena Blavatsky** (blavatsky.journey.json), whose Theosophical Society she joined in 1904; **Annie Besant** (annie_besant.journey.json), president at Adyar in exactly the years af Klint was a Stockholm member, and the pole from which Steiner's rupture pulled her away; **Goethe** (goethe_full.journey.json), for whom the Dornach building is named.

Parallels that are not crossings, recorded and not dramatised into meetings: the Monte Verità ecology of laban, ida_hoffmann, suzanne_perrottet, and mary_wigman, the same weather with no documented contact; the Paris pin already active through Blavatsky and Steiner, where af Klint never lived; and **Emma Kunz**, named in QUEUE.md line 198 as "kin to Hilma af Klint already queued" but not researched, so not yet nameable in campa.
