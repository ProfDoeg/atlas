# H. G. Wells (1866-1946) research report

*Compiled 2026-08-22 for `h_g_wells.journey.json`. 45 stops, 8 segments.*

Draper's apprentice, biology teacher, founder of the scientific romance, Fabian wrecker,
interviewer of Lenin and Stalin, author of a hundred-odd books, of which the four that will not
die were all written before he was 32.

**Legend.** [A] attested, source named. [R] reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried only by
a secondary biography. Disagreements between the three research lenses (chronology, geography,
afterlife) are flagged, never adjudicated silently, and each is also printed in the relevant
stop's `date_confidence`. Bare quoted titles are Wikipedia articles.

## 1. Bromley, 1866-1880

- Born 21 September 1866 above his father's china and cricket-goods shop, Atlas House, 162 High

  'Bromley']

## 2. The trades, 1879-1884

The most tangled stretch of the record; the lenses disagree with each other and internally.

- Wookey, Somerset, National School, pupil-teacher. Chronology 1879, geography October 1879.
  [A as to the post] **Contradiction, not resolved:** a 1879 Wookey post sits badly against the
  same article keeping him at Morley's until 1880 and at Windsor in 1880, and standard biography
  puts Windsor first and Wookey after, in winter 1880. The journey follows the dated lenses.
- Rodgers and Denyer, drapers, Windsor, 1880, lasting weeks. [R: geography lens only, on Wells's
  *Experiment in Autobiography* and Sherborne (2010); absent from Wikipedia and from chronology]
- Samuel Cowap, chemist, Midhurst, ended by the Latin examination fee, then a short spell
  boarding at Midhurst Grammar School. [A] **Order corrected:** chronology put both after
  Southsea, in 1883, but Wikipedia says that after a short apprenticeship at a Midhurst chemist
  and an even shorter stay as a boarder he signed his papers at Hyde's. Geography dated the
  boarding to 1881, which that sentence rules out. Both put at c. 1880.
- Hyde's Drapery Emporium, Southsea, 1880-1883. [A] **Lens conflict resolved on the source:**
  geography headed it 1881-1883 and its own note corrected to 1880; Wikipedia says "From 1880 to
  1883".
- Midhurst Grammar School again as pupil-teacher, 1883-1884, and the scholarship out of it. [A]

## 4. The scientific romances, 1895-1900

- *The Time Machine*: serialised The New Review January to May 1895; Holt 7 May, Heinemann 29
  May 1895; precursor essays in The National Observer, 17 March to 23 June 1894; the term "time
  machine" coined by the book. [A: 'The Time Machine'] **Chronology corrected:** it placed the
  book at Woking, where Wells arrived only in May 1895, after the serial had run and with the
  book in press. The journey gives the novel a London stop.
- 141 Maybury Road, Woking, from May 1895; marriage to Jane at the St Pancras register office,
  October 1895. [A] **Chronology corrected:** it had the marriage first and the house after.
- *The Island of Doctor Moreau*, April 1896. [A] **Chronology corrected:** it placed the book at
  Worcester Park, where the Wellses arrived only in late summer 1896.
- Horsell Common, walked from the Woking house, used as the Martian landing site. [R as to the
  walking habit: Sherborne only]; [A as to the fictional siting: the novel]
- Worcester Park, late summer 1896 to 1898; *The Invisible Man* written there, 1897. [A]
- *The War of the Worlds*: serialised Pearson's Magazine April to December 1897; Heinemann book
  1898. [A: 'The War of the Worlds'] **Both other lenses corrected:** chronology collapsed serial
  and book into 1898, and the afterlife lens dated the novel 1897, right for the serial and wrong
  for the book.

## 6. Russia, the war, Easton Glebe, 1910-1927

- Easton Glebe, Little Easton, Essex, leased from the Countess of Warwick, c. 1910-1928. [A]
- First Russian journey, January to February 1914, Saint Petersburg then Moscow, returning a
  self-described staunch Russophile. [A] **Anachronism corrected in both lenses:** both call the
  city Petrograd, but in January 1914 it was still Saint Petersburg, renamed only that August or
  September. Wikipedia's own H. G. Wells article carries the anachronism forward.
- *The World Set Free*, 1914, which names the atomic bomb. [A] **Chronology corrected on
  Szilard:** it gave 1932 for the chain-reaction work, where 'Leo Szilard' has him reading the
  novel in 1932 and conceiving the chain reaction on 12 September 1933. Szilard's reading is a
  later person's engagement with Wells's book, so it is a `suggested_ref`, not a scene.
- **War propaganda: chronology corrected, wrong building for the year.** It put Wells at
  Wellington House in 1918; that bureau was dissolved in February 1918. His 1918 work was at
  Crewe House, under Lord Northcliffe, resigning that July. [A] Gap: no coordinate for Crewe
  House was reachable, so the stop uses the generic London pin and declares it.
- 1920, Soviet Russia: lodges with Maxim Gorky in Petrograd during the famine; meets Moura
  Budberg, who becomes his lover and last companion and refused his proposals; meets Lenin, whom
  he writes up as the dreamer in the Kremlin. [A]
- *The Outline of History*, 1920 [A], folded into the Grasse stop as the income that paid for the
  house; the "race between education and catastrophe" line is [A: Chapter 41, via Wikiquote].
  Labour candidate for London University, 1922, unsuccessful [A]. Lou Pidou, Grasse, with Odette
  Keun, 1924-1933 [A].
- Jane's death, 6 October 1927, aged 55. [A] **Chronology corrected on place:** it gave London;
  Wikipedia has her dying at Dunmow, Essex, that is at Easton Glebe, cremated afterwards at
  Golders Green. The death is pinned at Little Easton; the cremation gets no stop.

## 7. The tables of power, 1930-1938

- Chiltern Court, Baker Street, 1930-1936; *The Shape of Things to Come*, 1933, predicting a
  second world war beginning January 1940 against the actual September 1939; *Experiment in
  Autobiography*, 1934. [A]
- **Madrid and Hildegart Rodriguez Carballeira: geography lens retagged [R] to [A].** It hedged
  that contact may have been by correspondence because she was killed in 1933; 'Hildegart
  Rodriguez Carballeira' states she accompanied Wells on a visit he made to Madrid and declined
  his offer to relocate to London as his secretary, which is an in-person meeting. A bound, not
  a date: her mother shot her on 9 June 1933 and nothing narrows it further, so 1932-01-01 is a
  placeholder inside the bound.
- Bridgeport, Tongue Point, 4 March 1933: the Dymaxion car. [A: inherited from
  `buckminster_fuller.journey.json`], whose campa records that Amelia Earhart and H. G. Wells came
  to ride in it and the newsreels swarmed. Pin inherited byte-identical: 41.161, -73.181.
  Roosevelt's bank holiday is the same day. The strongest attested crossing with the corpus.
- Berlin, Opernplatz, 10 May 1933: his books burned, his work banned from German libraries. [A]
  Wells was not present; the stop is written as his work at that place, not his presence.
- PEN International, founded at the Florence Restaurant, London, 5 October 1921, Galsworthy first
  president, with Conrad, Shaw and Wells among the founders [A: 'PEN International']; Wells later
  president, overseeing the expulsion of the German PEN club for refusing non-Aryan members [R].
  **Contradiction, not resolved:** 'PEN International' gives the presidency as 1932-1935, 'H. G.
  Wells' gives October 1933 to October 1936.
- 1934: received by Franklin Roosevelt in Washington in July, then interviews Stalin in the
  Kremlin for three hours on 23 July, published in the New Statesman. [A] Co-founds The Diabetic
  Association, now Diabetes UK, the same year [A]; not given a stop.

## 8. The last decade, 1936-1946

- 13 Hanover Terrace, Regent's Park, 1936 to his death; *World Brain*, 1938. [A] **Afterlife lens
  corrected:** it called this home for "the last two decades"; it was one, with Chiltern Court
  before it. The 1966 GLC blue plaque [A] is a `suggested_ref`.
- *The Rights of Man*, 1940, later feeding the 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights; on the

- The Mercury Theatre on the Air adaptation, CBS, 30 October 1938, and the exaggeration of the
  panic legend (contemporary surveys found c. 2% of listeners heard it), are [A: 'The War of the
  Worlds (1938 radio drama)']. **The broadcast is Orson Welles's event, not Wells's, and is not
  staged as a stop**; it is carried in the campa and refs of the stop for the one meeting between
  the two men, October 1940, which is [A] from the same article with Wells's remark verbatim.
  Gap: no source names the place, so that stop is pinned at Wells's own address as a placeholder.
- *Mind at the End of Its Tether*, 1945; death 13 August 1946, aged 79, at 13 Hanover Terrace,
  Moura Budberg among those near him; preferred epitaph from a 1941 preface, never carved: "I
  told you so. You damned fools."; cremated at Golders Green, 16 August 1946. [A]
- **Ashes off Old Harry Rocks: afterlife lens retagged [R] to [A].** It doubted the location, but
  'Old Harry Rocks' states the ashes were scattered in the water there and cites Anthony West,
  *H. G. Wells: Aspects of a Life*, p. 153 (1984), a biography by Wells's own son. **Relationship
  corrected:** the geography lens called West Wells's son-in-law; he was his son, by Rebecca West.

## Interlocks used

Four atlas travelers are named in campas, each on a relation rooted in Wells's own life. Shared
geography alone was never treated as a reason to name anyone.

- `buckminster_fuller`, Bridgeport stop. Direct, attested, in person: Wells rode in the Dymaxion
  car, 4 March 1933. Pin inherited byte-identical.
- `charles_darwin`, Normal School stop. Influence: Wells's formative year was under T. H. Huxley,
  who had argued Darwin's case in public for two decades, and natural selection is the engine of
  *The Time Machine* and *Moreau*.
- `jules_verne`, Time Machine and A Modern Utopia stops. Rivalry, one-directional and attested:
  Verne, interviewed by Gordon Jones in 1903 (*Temple Bar*, June 1904), distinguished his own
  fact-grounded method from Wells's inventing. He died at Amiens 24 March 1905, weeks before
  *A Modern Utopia*. No meeting is attested; none is claimed.
- `edward_bellamy`, A Modern Utopia stop. Read and criticised: Wells read *Looking Backward*,
  found its Boston too smug and finished, and framed his own utopia as kinetic against it.
  One-directional; Bellamy died 1898.

Declined. `annie_besant`: the pool itself notes Besant left the Fabians c. 1890 and Wells did not
join until 1903, so there is no overlap and no relation, only the same institution twenty years
apart. `victoria_ocampo`: no evidence of contact, only a London milieu overlap. Paris, offered as
a pin to inherit because Verne and Ocampo have Paris stops: no Wells visit is documented in any
file read, so no Paris stop was written.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

**H. G. Wells is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`.** Per the adversary's duty in that file, the
relations below are recorded rather than silently deleted, with the direction caught.

**Direction caught: FORWARD, in both cases.** Forward reach is not licensable by an agent's
judgement and is not covered by the standing exception, which is for a framework applied
backward. Neither has been written into the journey file.

1. `frank_herbert`. The pool offers *Dune* (1965) and the science-fiction tradition as descending
   from the genre Wells and Verne founded in the 1890s. Herbert's first sale was in 1952, six
   years after Wells's death, so no contact was possible. This reaches forward out of a 1946 file
   toward a 1965 artefact, which the block rule forbids. If the operator wants the lineage
   recorded it belongs on Herbert's own file, pointing back at Wells: old hash, new block, legal.
2. `philip_k_dick`. Same shape. Dick was born 1928; the offered relation is that his corpus
   inherits from the Wells and Verne founding. Forward. It belongs on Dick's file.

A third case is backward-facing and needed no exception: Borges's 1952 essay is a later
commentator's engagement with an earlier subject's books, which the standing rule makes a real,
citable, one-directional relation, so it sits as a `suggested_ref` and is named in no campa. If
the operator would rather see Borges named in prose, that is a ruling to make.
