# Roberto Calvi (1920-1982): research report

*2026-08-18. Milanese banker; general manager (1971) then president (1975-1982) of the Banco Ambrosiano, the Catholic "priests' bank" whose largest shareholder was the Vatican's Institute for the Works of Religion (IOR); P2 member; found hanging under Blackfriars Bridge, London, 18 June 1982.*

**[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, secondary repetition, or a crawl claim unconfirmed against a fetched source. Contradictions are flagged and left open, not adjudicated. Companion file: `roberto_calvi.journey.json`, 8 segments, 44 stops; hedging lives in each stop's `date_confidence`, never in the prose. Coordinates are in §8. "Earlier lens" means an earlier pass of this research, corrected on the record rather than quietly dropped.

## 1. Milan, 1920-1958

- Born Milan, 13 April 1920, son of a manager at the Banca Commerciale Italiana [A, it./en.wiki]. Birth address not reachable. 1939: enrolls in Economics and Commerce at the Bocconi [A, it.wiki].
- ***Contradiction, unresolved.*** en.wiki has him join the BCI after wartime service and only then move to the Ambrosiano; it.wiki dates only his 1947 Ambrosiano entry as a clerk and describes no BCI period. The journey's BCI stop is dated 1945-01-01 as a placeholder for "after the war," on the English article alone.
- 1947: joins the Banco Ambrosiano, Via Clerici 2 [A, it.wiki: "aveva sede a Milano, in via Clerici 2"]. 1952: marries [A, year only; no spouse named anywhere reachable].
- 1958: personal assistant to Alessandro Canesi, later the bank's president [A, it.wiki: "assistente personale di Alessandro Canesi"]. *Correction:* an earlier lens put this in 1947, which is only the year he joined as a clerk. 1960: takes over foreign-sector financial operations [A].
- *Correction:* the dossier's "spends his entire career inside this building" is false. He began at the BCI, and as chairman operated across the group's Rome, Nassau, Luxembourg and Lugano arms. (The bank itself was founded 1896 by Giuseppe Tovini, the IOR its principal shareholder [A, en.wiki].)

## 2. 1963-1975: the offshore machine

- Banca del Gottardo, Lugano, the group's Swiss arm from 1963 [A, en.wiki], predating Calvi's management: he inherits and extends it.
- Banco Ambrosiano Holding, Luxembourg: [A] that it existed, [R] as to date. *Correction:* an earlier lens gave "1963-1982"; 1963 is the Gottardo date carried over by mistake. en.wiki places the holding in the 1970s expansion without a year and it.wiki gives none, so 1970-01-01 is an explicit decade marker. Panama shells [R, it.wiki names the jurisdiction, no company, address or date], folded into the September 1981 letters-of-patronage stop.
- 16 February 1971: direttore generale [A for 1971 on both language articles; R for the day, it.wiki only]. 15 October 1975: president, after a year as vice-president [A for 1975; R for the day and vice-presidency, it.wiki].
- 1971: Cisalpine Overseas Bank, Nassau, founded by Sindona, Marcinkus and Calvi [A, via `michele_sindona.journey.json`]. Marcinkus heads the IOR from 1971, is a director of Ambrosiano Overseas, and is "involved with" Calvi "for a number of years" [A, en.wiki]; his own atlas file dates his board arrival to 1972.
- "God's Banker" / "banchiere di Dio": confirmed [A] but **undated by any fetched source [gap]**. *Correction:* an earlier lens dated the offshore expansion to 1975. It began before the chairmanship (Gottardo 1963) and ran through the 1970s.

## 3. 1974-1981: Sindona's crash, the Bank of Italy, the press

- 1974, il crack Sindona: the empire collapses after Franklin National Bank fails; Sindona had moved large sums through the Vatican Bank to Swiss banks since 1969 [A, en.wiki].
- ***The most important gap in this file.*** Sindona is universally called Calvi's "banking mentor" in the press, but the Sindona and Banco Ambrosiano pages, fetched independently, give no dated record of a direct Sindona-Calvi meeting or transaction. Not asserted in the journey; cited instead is the atlas's own Nassau/Cisalpine 1971 stop, with the gap stated on the face of it.
- 1977: liquidator Giorgio Ambrosoli finds a $5.6 million Sindona-group commission paid to "an American bishop and a Milanese banker," read by investigators as Marcinkus and Calvi [A, via `michele_sindona.journey.json`]. Nothing charged against either on the strength of it; Ambrosoli shot dead in Milan, July 1979 [A]. 1978: Bank of Italy report predicts "future disaster," triggers criminal investigations [A, en.wiki]. No month.
- 29 January 1979: magistrate Emilio Alessandrini shot dead on his way to the Palazzo di Giustizia by a Prima Linea commando (Segio, Donat-Cattin, Viscardi) [A, it.wiki]. *Correction:* an earlier lens dated this to 1978, the report's year. No source connects the killing to the bank, and the journey says so inside the campa rather than letting juxtaposition imply a link the record does not carry.
- Rizzoli / Corriere della Sera: Ambrosiano takes a stake [A that it happened, en.wiki; R as to when and how much]. **The "April 1981" date and the "40 per cent" figure could not be confirmed**; an earlier lens carried both under an [A] tag, withdrawn here.

## 4. 1981: the list, the arrest, the sentence

- 17 March 1981: searches ordered by magistrates Gherardo Colombo and Giuliano Turone, working the Sindona fake-kidnapping case, cover Villa Wanda at Arezzo, the Socam works and the Giole factory at Castiglion Fibocchi. The 962-name P2 roll is found "fra gli archivi della Giole" [A, it.wiki "Propaganda 2"]; Calvi is on it [A, en.wiki]; Forlani makes the list public 21 May 1981 [A]. *Correction:* both earlier lenses had the roll found at the villa and the search done by "Milan police." Date and count hold; site and acting authority do not.
- 21 May 1981: arrested in Milan on magistrate Gerardo D'Ambrosio's order, illegal currency export [A, it.wiki]. *Correction and source conflict:* an earlier lens gave "Rome, 20 May 1981"; en.wiki gives neither city nor day and cannot support it.
- Suicide attempt in custody [A that it happened, both articles], with corrections. *Method:* it.wiki says "ingerendo barbiturici," en.wiki says only that he attempted suicide, so neither supports "cutting his wrists" or "taking sleeping pills," both carried by earlier passes. *Sequence:* it was in custody during the trial, before release on bail; "pending appeal" inverts the order. *Gaps:* no month, so 1981-06-01 orders the segment only; the prison is unnamed everywhere, San Vittore commonly asserted and Lodi given by contemporary accounts, neither confirmed nor repeated as fact.
- 1981: four years suspended, fine of roughly $19.8 million for transferring $27 million abroad; released on bail, kept as president [A, en.wiki]. July 1981 is [R], no day anywhere.
- September 1981: the IOR letters of patronage over the Panamanian shells, Calvi's counter-letter days earlier [A via `paul_marcinkus.journey.json`, 1981-09-01]. *Citation problem:* an earlier lens tagged them [A] against en.wiki "Paul Marcinkus," which documents only his IOR presidency and Nassau directorship. The letters are real and widely reported, but need a different citation to carry an [A] tag.

## 5. June 1982: the collapse

- 1 June 1982: Ambrosiano cannot account for $1.287 billion advanced to Vatican-owned Panamanian shells [A for the figure, en.wiki; the date via `licio_gelli.journey.json`].
- 5 June 1982: letter to John Paul II warning of a "catastrophe of unimaginable proportions" [A, en.wiki, clause confirmed on two fetches; only the excerpt is printed]. The source gives the place only as "Italy"; the journey pins Milan, where the Italian chronology has him until 7 June, and notes that `paul_marcinkus.journey.json` stages this date at the Vatican, the letter's destination and Marcinkus's own ground.
- 17 June 1982: stripped of his post; his secretary Graziella Corrocher leaves a note denouncing him and jumps from a fifth-floor window [A, en.wiki, both on "the day before his body was found"]. The earlier pass left the day open between 17 and 19 June; that is removed.

***The flight: two irreconcilable routes.*** en.wiki has him vanish from a Rome flat on 10 June and travel Venice, Zurich, London by private plane. it.wiki has him leave Milan 7 June, be in Rome 9 June where Carboni assigns Emilio Pellicani as guide, fly to Venice 11 June, go by car to Trieste, cross into Yugoslav waters by motorboat, come overland through Klagenfurt, meet Carboni at Bregenz 14 June, fly Innsbruck-London 15 June. **There is no Zurich leg in the Italian account.** The journey follows the Italian chronology, far more granular and internally dated, and states the English variant on the departure stop. The false-passport name "Gian Roberto Calvini" is common to both [A].

15/16-17 June: lodges at Chelsea Cloisters with Silvano Vittor; Carboni and the Kleinszig sisters arrive from Amsterdam 16 June [A, it.wiki]. The earlier pass gave "16-18 June," overstating the far end: the body was found at 7:30 on the 18th.

## 6. 18 June 1982 and after

- 18 June 1982, c. 7:30 a.m.: body hanging from scaffolding under Blackfriars Bridge, bricks and stones in the pockets, roughly $14,000 in three currencies [A, en.wiki, both articles].
- *"Frati neri."* P2 members called themselves frati neri, black friars [A, en.wiki]; the bridge is named for the Dominican priory on the north bank. The article asserts no causal link and neither does the journey. The dossier's Newcastle masonic-lodge connection is excluded as unconfirmed.
- July 1982: first inquest, majority verdict of suicide [A]. Sir Keith Simpson's testimony of no struggle marks is [R, crawl only]; courtroom unlocated [gap]. 1982: buried at Drezzo, Colverde, Como [A, it.wiki], no date anywhere, so 1982-08-01 orders the stop after the inquest. July 1983: second inquest, open verdict [A].
- 1984: the IOR pays Ambrosiano's creditors "in recognition of moral involvement," no admission of legal liability [A that it happened]. ***The sum is contradictory and left open:*** en.wiki "Roberto Calvi" gives US$224 million to 120 creditors; en.wiki "Paul Marcinkus" gives GBP 145 million, no year; an earlier lens and `paul_marcinkus.journey.json` give $250 million, 25 May 1984, Geneva. The journey uses $224 million with the Geneva venue and date, and names all three.
- June 1989: Marcinkus leaves the IOR after 18 years, not twenty; pro-president of the Governorate since 1981, resigned that post 30 October 1990 [A, en.wiki]. *Correction* to an earlier lens's "April 1989" and "twenty years." Not staged as a Calvi stop: Calvi was seven years dead, and this is Marcinkus's scene on his own file.

## 7. 1991-2011: reinvestigation and trial

- 1991: family engages Kroll Associates, case to Jeff Katz [A]. October 1992: Kroll's report, turning on the absence of paint and rust on Calvi's shoes, reaches the home secretary and the City of London Police, who show little interest [A].
- December 1998: body exhumed on an Italian court's order (it.wiki names GIP Otello Lupacchini), a German forensic scientist commissioned [A], who is not named by the source [gap]. The Milan forensic institute and the site are [R]. After 1998: reinterred in the family edicola at Tremenico [A, it.wiki], no date. *Correction:* Tremenico is in the Valvarrone, not the Valsassina, part of the comune of Valvarrone since 1 January 2018.
- October 2002: the German report finds the neck injuries inconsistent with self-inflicted hanging [A]. September 2003: City of London Police reopen the case as a murder inquiry [A].
- 19 July 2005: Licio Gelli indicted [A, en.wiki]; his name comes off the final indictment before trial, and in May 2009 the case against him is dropped for insufficient evidence [A]. His courtroom claim that the killing was commissioned in Poland appears only in reported speech, so it is [R] as a quotation and is paraphrased.
- 5 October 2005: trial opens at Rebibbia, Rome: Calo, Carboni, Kleinszig, Diotallevi, Vittor [A]. The "aula bunker" detail is [R]: it.wiki does not mention one. 6 June 2007: all five acquitted for insufficient evidence by Judge Mario Lucio d'Andria, the court nonetheless finding the death murder rather than suicide [A].
- 7 May 2010: appeal confirms the acquittals of Calo, Carboni and Diotallevi [A]; 18 November 2011: Cassation confirms, ending the case [A]. The Piazzale Clodio venue for the appeal is [R], unconfirmed anywhere.

## 9. Quotations: what survived verification

Six are carried verbatim in the journey's quote fields, with their caveats: Calvi's "catastrophe of unimaginable proportions" to John Paul II, 5 June 1982 (en.wiki, confirmed on two fetches, only this excerpt printed) · Calvi's "Il Banco Ambrosiano non è mio" after the May 1981 trial (it.wiki) · "God's Banker," press epithet, undated · Sindona to Nick Tosches on the murder staged as a masonic ritual (it.wiki citing the interview; the Tosches original unreachable) · Carboni's trial testimony (it.wiki, truncated by an ellipsis in the source) · the Rome appeals court's motivazioni of 7 May 2010 (it.wiki).

***Rejected.*** "Calvi fu strangolato da Francesco Di Carlo su ordine di Pippo Calò," attributed to pentito Francesco Marino Mannoia (1991), is not a quotation. The it.wiki wikitext carries only indirect speech, with different content: Mannoia declared that Calvi would have been killed by Di Carlo because the banker could not recover the dirty money invested by Mannoia and by Gelli through the IOR and the Ambrosiano on behalf of Calò. There is no "strangolato" and no "su ordine": Calò is the party on whose behalf the money was invested, not the one giving the order. Demoted to [R], excluded from all quote fields.

***The Di Carlo tangle, resolved.*** The dossier had Frankie Di Carlo telling investigators in 1991 that he was ordered to kill Calvi; the date conflates two people. Di Carlo became a pentito in June 1996, denied being the killer, admitted Calò had approached him, and named the Camorristi Vincenzo Casillo and Sergio Vaccari. In July 1991 it was Mannoia who accused Di Carlo [A, en.wiki]. The item upgrades to [A] but names nobody the courts convicted, and no stop stages it as a scene.

## 10. Interlocks used, and the ones deliberately not used

Named in a campa, each with a documented direct relation to Calvi, not merely shared geography: `licio_gelli`, whose lodge Calvi was sworn into and who was indicted for his murder; `paul_marcinkus`, co-founder of the Cisalpine Overseas Bank and signatory of the letters of patronage over Calvi's Panamanian shells; `michele_sindona`, co-founder of the same Nassau bank, who called Calvi's death a murder staged as a masonic ritual.

Deliberately NOT named. `robert_maxwell`, see §11: structural comparison only, running the wrong way in time. Buenos Aires (`licio_gelli`'s Casa Rosada 1973 and Palacio San Martin 1974 stops), offered by the interlock lens as an Argentine anchor for the P2/Ambrosiano milieu: **Calvi is not documented as ever having visited Argentina**, those are Gelli's own stops on Gelli's own file, and staging them here is the error the standing rule exists to prevent. Giulio Andreotti, in press coverage of Calvi's political protection and invoked in the threatening call to Ambrosoli before his 1979 murder, has no atlas file, so there is nothing to interlock with: a future interlock, not written. And two dossier-only claims, both [R] and excluded: "the Venerable," and Giuffre's testimony that his clan killed Calvi after Carboni delivered him.

Two stops arguably somebody else's, kept and declared: the killing of Emilio Alessandrini (1979) and the death of Graziella Corrocher (1982), at neither of which Calvi was present. Both are contemporaneous events of the Ambrosiano affair, happening to people whose work was the keeping of Calvi's own books; neither is a later commentator restaging an engagement with him. Neither has an atlas file; if either is given one, these scenes belong there.
