# William the Conqueror, c.1027/28 - 1087: research report
*2026-08-04. Duke of Normandy from 1035, King of England from Christmas Day 1066. Prepared for the Atlas of Journeys; register: national mythology, the canon is true.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or single-chronicle claim the other sources do not corroborate. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**; where the journey had to pick one coordinate or date, the rejected value is printed beside it.

**Method.** Four research lenses (chronology, geography, quotation, afterlife of the body) plus an interlock pass against the atlas corpus. The chronology and geography lenses were run independently and disagree in five places, all listed in section 9. Of eleven candidate quotations, two rested on a dead citation and two were modern paraphrase; all four are corrected below. Seven coordinates were moved after gazetteer checking.

---

## 1. Origins and the minority, c.1027 - 1047

**Birth at Falaise. [R]** Illegitimate son of Robert I and Herleva. The year is **not fixed**: English Wikipedia gives c.1028, Orderic implies 1026 or 1027, biographers print 1027 or 1028. The chronology lens tagged a bare 1028 [A], which overstates the evidence; the geography lens tagged the same birth [R]. The journey carries 1028-01-01 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

Herleva is conventionally a tanner's daughter, on a reading of Orderic's *pollinctores*. **[R]** Historians dispute the reading and note her family stood too high for the trade. The tradition matters only because of the Alençon insult, itself a chronicle anecdote.

**Coordinate correction.** Falaise castle was given as 48.8942, -0.1958, which falls in the town east of the keep; it is at **48.8932, -0.2039**. The standing keep is twelfth and thirteenth century work.

**Robert's departure and death. [A]** January 1035, the magnates swear fealty to William as heir; Robert dies at Nicaea in July returning from Jerusalem; the boy of seven or eight is recognized duke at Rouen (William of Jumièges). Nicaea gets no stop: William is not there. **The anarchy. [R]** Guardians and tutors killed between 1037 and 1047; Archbishop Robert of Rouen dies March 1037 **[A]**. Orderic supplies the murders, the chroniclers disagree on sequence and place, tradition puts one plot at Vaudreuil. The journey carries Vaudreuil as one [R] stop standing for the decade rather than faking a sequence.

**Valognes and Val-ès-Dunes. [A, Poitiers for the night ride]** Warned at Valognes, William rides by night for Falaise; with Henry I of France he breaks the rebel coalition at Val-ès-Dunes in 1047 and promulgates the Truce of God after it.

**Two corrections.** (1) 10 August is traditional only: "there is no decisive evidence for this date"; Douglas argues early 1047, Bates summer 1047. (2) The lens coordinate 49.12, -0.23 sits near Argences, the wrong part of the plain; the battlefield is near Conteville and Vimont at **49.083, -0.250**.

## 2. The duchy made fast, 1047 - 1063

**Brionne, 1050. [A]** Guy of Burgundy besieged out. **Alençon, 1051. [A, Orderic and Poitiers]** The garrison mocks his mother's ancestry from the walls; he mutilates the mockers. Domfront, besieged in the same campaign 1051-52 **[A, Poitiers]**, is folded into the Alençon campa for length.

**The marriage. [A that it happened; R as to date and site]** Leo IX forbids the match at Rheims, October 1049 **[A]**; William marries Matilda anyway. The chronology lens dated it a flat 1051 and tagged it [A]; Wikipedia gives "early 1050s," the Matilda article c.1051-52, and papal approval arrives only from Nicholas II in **1059**. The geography lens places the wedding at Eu; the journey follows Eu with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**Mortemer, February 1054. [A, but misattributed by one lens]** The chronology lens has William beating Henry I here. He was not present: the battle was won by Robert of Eu, Walter Giffard, William de Warenne, Roger of Mortemer and Hugh of Gournay while William faced the king west of the Seine. The geography lens is correct and is followed. **Varaville, 1057. [A, Poitiers]** He catches part of Henry's army mid-crossing at the Dives on a turning tide.

**Tillières and Thimert, 1058. [A, direction corrected]** The chronology lens has William capturing both. The action runs the other way: the French took and held them, and his answer was a siege of Thimert that dragged two years to 1060. No stop; recorded here. **1060. [A]** Henry I and Geoffrey Martel both die, and both continental checks are gone in one year.

**Caen. [A]** Stone castle from c.1060, raising Caen into a second ducal seat. Saint-Étienne is **founded 1063**, built from **1066**, dedicated **1077**. The geography lens wrote "1063 (consecrated)" and the afterlife lens "founded around 1063"; the consecration is wrong by fourteen years and is corrected in the journey campa.

## 3. The English claim and the oath, 1051 - 1065

**Edward's promise, 1051. [R]** Norman tradition; English sources do not corroborate it. No stop.

**Harold in Normandy, 1064 or 1065. [A that the voyage and an oath happened; R as to date, site, and terms]** Harold sails from Bosham (the Tapestry names it; Poitiers gives no departure point), is wrecked in Ponthieu, held by Count Guy at Beaurain until William demands him as overlord, campaigns with him into Brittany against Conan II, and swears on relics.

Three disputes attach and all three are carried into the journey rather than smoothed. **Where**: Poitiers says Bonneville-sur-Touques, the Tapestry says Bayeux, unresolved in the sources. **When**: English Wikipedia 1064, French Wikipedia 1065 following Poitiers. **What was sworn**: Norman chroniclers say Harold promised to support the claim and no English source corroborates the terms, so the whole Norman case rests on uncorroborated content.

**Coordinate corrections.** Bosham given as 50.818, -0.863, tidal water 1.2 km south of the village, corrected to **50.8289, -0.8595**. Beaurain given as 50.4, 1.87; Beaurainville is at **50.4222, 1.9042**. Bonneville given as 49.367, 0.147; the place is at **49.3381, 0.1197**.

## 4. The crossing and Hastings, 1066

**[A throughout, Anglo-Saxon Chronicle and William of Poitiers]** Edward dies 5 January and Harold is crowned on the 6th; the barons are assembled at Lillebonne; the fleet gathers at the Dives through August, is held by wind, is driven to Saint-Valery-sur-Somme and sails 27 September behind the paraded relics of Saint Valery. Harold destroys Hardrada and Tostig at Stamford Bridge on 25 September. The Normans land unopposed at Pevensey on 28 September and kill Harold on Senlac Hill on 14 October.

**The stumble at Pevensey is [R] and its quotation was wrong twice over.** The landing is well attested; the omen is a topos also told of Julius Caesar. The lens attributed "See, I have taken England with both my hands" to Poitiers or Malmesbury via a 1956 LIFE magazine retelling. In fact **Malmesbury gives the line to a soldier**, not to William (trans. Giles, Book III, p. 274), and his own footnote doubts the story, noting the silence of Poitiers, the duke's chaplain, present at the landing; Malmesbury also puts the landing at Hastings where the Chronicle says Pevensey. **The version in which William speaks is Wace's** (Roman de Rou, trans. Taylor, 1837, pp. 130-131). The journey quotes Wace, attributes it to Wace, and flags the anecdote as legendary. The LIFE citation is dropped.

## 5. Conquest, revolt, and the Harrying, 1066 - 1072

**[A, Chronicle, Poitiers, Orderic]** Dover surrenders and part of the town burns; Canterbury submits and he falls ill there; Stigand submits at Wallingford; at Berkhamsted the English magnates offer the crown. At the Westminster coronation of 25 December 1066 the two-language acclamation is mistaken by the Norman guard for a rising and they fire the houses around the abbey. He lodges at Barking while the works that become the Tower go up, then returns to Normandy in March 1067 leaving regents; Eustace II of Boulogne attempts Dover in his absence and is repulsed.

**The Exeter contradiction. [flagged]** Chronology lens: return December 1067 and the eighteen-day siege in the same entry. Geography lens: siege 1068. The return is December 1067, the siege early 1068. The journey uses 1068 and states the divergence in `date_confidence`.

**The Matilda coronation error. [corrected]** Not Winchester: she was crowned **11 May 1068 at Westminster Abbey**, at Pentecost, by the Archbishop of York.

**Revolt and castles. [A]** Edwin and Morcar revolt in 1068; castles at Warwick, York, Nottingham, Lincoln, Huntingdon, Cambridge. Edgar attacks York in early 1069, allies with Sweyn of Denmark, and York falls in the autumn. William wears his crown in the ruins on Christmas Day 1069.

**The Harrying, 1069-70. [A as event; the casualty figure is Orderic's]** Domesday's Yorkshire "waste" entries are consistent with it. Castles at Chester and Stafford follow in the winter of 1070.

Two quotation corrections. **Orderic's judgment** was transmitted with its halves reversed; in Forester (vol. 2, 1854, p. 28) the famine toll comes first ("more than a hundred thousand souls... perished of want") and the refusal to commend follows. The journey restores the order. **The deathbed confession** was flagged [R] and unlocatable by the lens; the premise is wrong, since it is Orderic, Book VII ch. XV, Forester vol. 2, pp. 412-413, under the running head "THE CONQUEROR CONFESSES HIS CRUELTIES". What was wrong is the **wording**, a school-textbook paraphrase. Forester reads "I fell on the English of the northern counties like a raving lion." The journey uses Forester and keeps the caveat in `quote_source`: the speech is Orderic's own composition, not a transcript.

**Church and settlement, 1070. [A]** Legates crown him again at the Easter court at Winchester; Stigand and other native bishops are deposed; Lanfranc goes to Canterbury, Thomas of Bayeux to York; Battle Abbey is founded on the field. **Ely, 1071. [A]** A causeway across the fen; Morcar imprisoned. **Abernethy, 1072. [A]** Malcolm III submits and gives a son as hostage. **Cassel, February 1071. [A]** William fitzOsbern killed in Flanders; no stop, William absent.

**The Edwin conflation. [corrected]** The chronology lens welds Edwin's death to Ely. Edwin was killed in 1071 making for Scotland, betrayed by his own retinue. Only the causeway and Morcar belong to Ely.

## 6. The continental rear and the rebel son, 1073 - 1080

**[A]** Le Mans taken March 1073. The Revolt of the Earls, Ralph de Gael and Roger de Breteuil, 1075; Waltheof executed at Winchester May 1076. September 1076 he besieges Dol and **is beaten off by Philip I**, one of the few clear defeats of his career. At Gerberoy in 1079 he is wounded and unhorsed by Robert Curthose, said to have known his father's voice and spared him **[A as to the wounding, Orderic; R as to the recognition]**. A council at Lillebonne in 1080 confirms his authority over the Norman church (no stop).

**Coordinate correction.** Gerberoy given as 49.5667, 1.8667; the Oise commune is at **49.535, 1.850**. The engagement is dated late January or early February 1079.

## 7. Domesday, Mantes, and the death, 1085 - 1087

**[A, Chronicle]** The survey is commissioned at the Christmas court of 1085 at Gloucester. On 1 August 1086 every major landholder swears at Salisbury, fealty binding directly to the king over any intermediate lord. The returns are compiled and kept at Winchester **[R as to custody: the location of the book in 1086-87 is debated]**.

**Mantes, 1087. [A]** William burns the town; his horse rears on the burning ground and he is thrown against the saddle pommel. The Chronicle dates the campaign before the Assumption, that is before 15 August. **Death, 9 September 1087. [A, Orderic]** At the priory of Saint-Gervais outside Rouen; his attendants strip the room and leave the body on the floor. **Burial at Caen. [A, Orderic]** The sarcophagus is too small; forcing the body down ruptures it and the stench empties the church.

**Coordinate contradiction at Rouen. [flagged]** Afterlife lens 49.4432, 1.0999, central Rouen near the cathedral, 1.4 km wrong. Geography lens 49.4544, 1.0794, about 600 m wrong. The priory church, built extra muros to the west, is at **49.4496, 1.0827**, used in the journey.

**A three-way disagreement on the Abbaye aux Hommes. [flagged, harmonized]** Geography lens 49.1822, -0.3714; afterlife lens 49.1839, -0.3711; gazetteer 49.18167, -0.37278. A fourth figure, 49.1829, -0.3707, already exists in the atlas as richard_lionheart's city-level pin for Caen. The journey uses **49.1817, -0.3728** for every Saint-Étienne stop and does not overwrite richard_lionheart's pin, which refers to the city and not the abbey.

## 8. The afterlife of the body

**[A as sequence; R as to what survives]** 1522, the tomb opened on papal authorization, the skeleton reported intact in leather, resealed with a new inscription. 1562, Huguenot soldiers scatter the bones; a thighbone is recovered. 1642, the femur reinterred under a new monument. 1793, revolutionary mobs destroy it again. **Corrected**: what was destroyed in 1793 was the elaborate monument of c.1742, not the 1642 marker, and the present marker is not a bare slab left by the Revolution but an **early nineteenth-century white marble ledger stone** installed as a replacement. **[R]** Norman antiquarian accounts contradict each other on whether any bone survived; not adjudicated.

**Monuments.** The **Bayeux Tapestry**, roughly 70 m, is his principal visual chronicle; Kentish or Norman making is disputed and left open. The **Falaise equestrian bronze** (Louis Rochet, 1851) sets him above effigies of the six earlier dukes from Rollo. The **Dives-sur-Mer monument** lists the companions legend credited with sailing in 1066, a roster called padded since the nineteenth century **[R]**.

## 9. Contradictions carried, not resolved

1. **Birth year.** c.1027 or 1028 against 1026/7 implied by Orderic; lens tagging also conflicted.
2. **Val-ès-Dunes date.** 10 August 1047 traditional; Douglas early 1047, Bates summer 1047.
3. **Marriage.** 1051, "early 1050s," or c.1051-52; forbidden 1049, approved 1059.
4. **Oath site and date.** Bonneville (Poitiers) against Bayeux (the Tapestry); 1064 against 1065.
5. **Exeter.** Chronology lens 1067, geography 1068.
6. **Matilda's coronation.** Winchester (chronology lens) against Westminster, 11 May 1068.
7. **Mortemer.** Whether William was present. He was not; the lenses disagreed.
8. **Landing anecdote.** Three versions (Malmesbury, the Chronicle, Wace), none harmonized.
9. **Saint-Étienne and Saint-Gervais coordinates.** Five wrong or divergent values across two lenses.
10. **What lies under the Caen marker.** Local histories contradict each other.
11. **Bayeux Tapestry origin.** Kent for Odo, or Normandy.

## 10. Interlocks with the existing atlas

Named in campa where the paths genuinely cross:
- **joan_of_arc** and **catherine_alexandria** (with **hypatia** upstream of the second), at the 1035 Rouen stop: William's ducal capital is Joan's prison and trial seat in 1431, her ransom paid from the taxes of conquered Normandy, and Catherine's relics were enshrined on the côte above the same city. **[A]**
- **richard_lionheart**, at the Falaise statue: Richard defends himself at the imperial Diet "as though he sat on his own throne at Lincoln or Caen," inheriting the England-Normandy union of 1066. **[A]**
- **clotilde**, at Mantes: her monastery at Andely stands in the Vexin, the chalk river country William is burning when he takes his death wound. **[A]**
- **tolkien**, at the coronation: the Rawlinson and Bosworth Chair, and the pre-Conquest English that 25 December 1066 began to displace at court and in law. **[A]**
- **morgan-le-fay** with **san_cristobal**, at Dives: Morgan's cult migrates to Sicily "in the wake of the Norman conquest," the same wave that puts the Hautevilles in the south and an Italo-Norman fleet at Rab. **[A]**

Found but not used in campa: **gustave_flaubert**, **roland_barthes** (Normandy, eight centuries downstream, [R]); **borges** (Old English, the Maldon epitaph, [R]); **guinevere** (Carlisle's Norman keep, [R]); **giles** (a Norman foundation in London); **kyot-willehalm** (the 1066 canonization of William of Gellone, coincidence only).

**Negative result, stated as a gap.** The other named canonical pins in the corpus (the Kaaba at 21.4225, 39.8262; the Temple Mount; Buenos Aires) do not intersect this life. **No stop in this journey inherits a canonical pin byte-identically, because no stop in this life stands on the same ground as one.** The nearest case, Caen, is handled in section 7.
