# Quintilian (Marcus Fabius Quintilianus), c. 35 - c. 100 CE
*Research report for `quintilian.journey.json`. 2026-08-31.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or modern convention.
Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated. Absences are stated as absences.

The governing fact: **almost none of this life is documented from outside his own book.** Four
external witnesses exist: Martial (contemporary), Juvenal (a generation later, satirical), Ausonius
(three centuries later, one sentence), Jerome (three centuries later, one clause). Everything else is
an autobiographical aside inside the *Institutio Oratoria*, or modern inference dressed as
chronology. Where a date is convention rather than record, `date_confidence` says so in those words.

---

## 1. Calagurris: birth and boyhood

**[R]** Born at Calagurris Nassica (modern Calahorra, La Rioja), Hispania Tarraconensis. **No ancient
source gives a year.** Estimates run mid-30s to c. 40 CE; c. 35 is convention. The Hispanic origin
rests entirely on one clause in Jerome's *Chronicon*: *Quinctilianus ex Hispania Calaguritanus*.
Scholarship has also asked whether the town marks birth or only family origin, and no source settles
it. File: `0035-01-01`, all of this in `date_confidence`.

**[R]** Boyhood in a Romanized municipium with its own coinage, circus, baths and amphitheatre
(Wikipedia, "Calahorra"). No autobiographical statement about his childhood survives; Book 1's
account of infant education is a program, not a memoir. **[A]** The father: one rhetorical example
credited to *pater meus* at *Inst.* 9.3.73, the entire documentary existence of the man. No mother,
siblings, or property named anywhere.

## 2. Rome under Nero

**[A]** In Rome as a young man, early enough to hear and admire **Gnaeus Domitius Afer**, whose
sayings and case-handling he cites throughout the *Institutio*, including remarks on Afer's late
decline. **[A]** Afer died 59. **[R]** *When* he came, at whose expense, and with whom is nowhere
stated; the file's `0048-01-01` orders the segment and says so. **[R]** Remmius Palaemon as his
teacher: later scholia only, plausible, never confirmed. **[R]** His whereabouts at Afer's death are
unstated; Rome is inferred from Afer's career base.

**[R] Beyond the verified pool**, flagged for the operator: (a) Seneca the Younger as Nero's tutor
and the most imitated stylist of the period; (b) the critique of the declamation schools as *Inst.*
Book 2, written forty years later and not offered as autobiography. Both framing only.

## 3. The undocumented decade, c. 59-68

**The largest hole in the life, left as a hole.** Tradition sends him back to Hispania to practice
law for roughly nine years. **No surviving contemporary source names a residence, a case, a client,
or an occupation in this interval.** The file pins the stop at his birthplace because that is where
the tradition puts him, and says the tradition rests on Jerome's ethnic and nothing else.

## 4. The return with Galba, 68

**[A]** Galba governed Hispania Tarraconensis, capital Tarraco, from "59 or 60"; hailed by his troops
at **Carthago Nova** in April 68, where he refused the imperial title and styled himself legate of
the Senate and Roman People; received news of Nero's death at **Clunia**; formally proclaimed by the
Senate at Rome, 8 June 68 (Wikipedia, "Galba"). **Correction carried, because the error circulates
widely:** the gather material had Galba proclaimed at Clunia. He was not. The corrected sequence is
in the stop's `date_confidence`.

**[R]** Quintilian in Galba's entourage: traditional account, and **Quintilian never states it.** The
campa says so plainly, because the tradition is load-bearing for the whole chronology. **[A]** Galba
assassinated January 69. Quintilian appears in no account of that year's politics on any side, and
names no patron afterwards.

## 5. The school and the salary, 69 onward

**[A]** His own statement, preface to Book 1: twenty years of teaching before the leisure to write.
**[R]** The start year (c. 69) is derived by counting back from a retirement placed at 88-89. Neither
endpoint is given directly by any source.

**The 72 CE problem, flagged and not resolved.** **[A]** Suetonius, *Vespasian* 18: *Primus e fisco
Latinis Graecisque rhetoribus annua centena constituit*, 100,000 sesterces annually to Latin and
Greek rhetors from the fiscus. **Suetonius gives no year and does not name Quintilian anywhere in the
Life of Vespasian.** **[R]** "72 CE" is a modern placement, not attested. **[R]** Jerome's
*Chronicon* separately makes Quintilian the first at Rome to hold a public school and imperial
salary, and **some reckonings place Jerome's notice at 88**, in direct tension with the 72
convention. The file dates the stop `0072-01-01` and states the whole contradiction in
`date_confidence`. Not resolved.

**[A]** He continued as advocate alongside the teaching; the *Institutio* carries first-person case
recollection throughout. **[R]** The **Basilica Julia** pin is inference: the centumviral court met
there, and he never names the building.

**Queen Berenice. [A]** *Inst.* 4.1.19, verified verbatim: "I myself, when I appeared on behalf of
Queen Berenice, actually pleaded before her," cited by him as an example of a judge with a personal
stake. **[R]** The year (c. 75) is **not in the text**; it is inferred from Berenice's residence at
Rome under Titus, and the Palatine setting is likewise inference. Charge, forum and outcome are all
unrecorded.

**The unauthorized handbooks. [A]** *Inst.* 1 pr. 7-8: "two books on the art of rhetoric are at
present circulating under my name, although never published by me or composed for such a purpose."
These are **lecture handbooks taken down by pupils over a two-day course**, not speeches. The gather
material had "speeches and lecture material"; corrected. The speech-publication complaint is a
separate matter at 7.2.24, not used.

### Pupils and reputation
- **[R]** Pliny the Younger as pupil: widely accepted on ancient and later testimony; the pool named
  no single primary passage. **[R]** Tacitus is often listed and **rests on nothing**; the campa says
  exactly that.
- **[A]** Martial, *Epigrams* 2.90, contemporary, addressed to him by name. Quoted.
- **[A]** Juvenal, *Satires* 7.186-198 on his "vast domains." **[R]** That he held vast estates is
  not independently verifiable, and the satire postdates him (c. 110-120). The file dates that stop
  inside Quintilian's own life at `0090-01-01`, rather than staging Juvenal's writing as a scene.
- **[R]** Pliny, *Letters* 6.32, "To Quintilian": the addressee may be the rhetorician or another
  Quintilianus, disputed. Not used, because it cannot be pinned to this subject.

## 6. Retirement, the imperial commission, the honours

**[R]** Retirement c. 88-89, by counting twenty years; Wikipedia gives 88.

**[A]** *Inst.* 4 pr. 2: "Domitianus Augustus has entrusted me with the education of his sister's
grandsons" (*sororis suae nepotes*). **That is all Quintilian says.** He does not name the boys, the
renaming, Clemens, or Domitilla. **[A]** The renaming to Vespasianus and Domitianus and the parentage
(Clemens, Flavia Domitilla) come from **Suetonius, *Domitian* 15**. The gather material attributed
the whole package to Quintilian's preface; over-claiming, and the citation is split in the file.

**[R]** The *ornamenta consularia* through Clemens: recorded only by **Ausonius, *Gratiarum actio*
7.31**, three centuries later, with no year anywhere. **Contradiction flagged:** Wikipedia's
"Quintilian" states he was "made a consul by Emperor Vespasian." Double conflation, since the only
evidence is Ausonius on honorary consular *ornaments*, granted under **Domitian** through Clemens.
The file follows Ausonius and names the conflation in the campa rather than discarding it.

## 7. The house that emptied

All three deaths come from one document, the preface to Book 6. No other source exists for any of
them; no name, cause, or exact date for any of the three people.

- **[A]** The wife died **before completing her nineteenth year**. Quoted verbatim at 6 pr. **6**,
  not 5 as the gather material had it (section 5 begins "Yet for me her death alone was such...",
  6 begins "Still her children survived her, and I, too..."). Citation corrected in the file.
- **[A]** The younger son died at **barely five**, and 6 pr. 6 establishes he was **the first of the
  two to leave him**. **Correction:** the gather material said "only months after his mother's
  death." **No interval is stated anywhere in the preface.** That clause is cut and the absence is
  stated in `date_confidence`.
- **[A]** The elder son died at about nine; the loss nearly halted composition. He had been one
  intended audience of the project, alongside Marcellus Victorius's children.

The file's `0090` / `0091` / `0093` order the sequence Quintilian gives. They are not records, and
the confidence fields say so.

## 8. The twelve books

**[R]** Composed c. 92-95, published conventionally c. 95 on internal references to Domitian and the
heirs. No source dates any individual book; the month-level dates in segment 7 only keep the books in
reading order.

**Book 1 [A]** The course begins at the cradle: the nurse's speech, the tutor, letters as play, the
class preferred to the private tutor. **[R, beyond the pool]** The argument against beating pupils is
*Inst.* 1.3, standard content but not in the verified pool.

**Book 10 [A]** 10.1.112: the student who comes to delight in Cicero has made real progress, since
"Cicero" is by now not the name of a man but of eloquence itself. **[A]** 10.1: the mixed verdict on
Seneca, ingenious and morally serious but corrupting to imitate. **[R, beyond the pool, flagged]**
That the Greek list opens with **Homer** (10.1.46) and the Latin with **Virgil** (10.1.85) is
standard content of the survey but not itemized in the pool; both are atlas travelers, so the
operator should know it rests on general grounds.

**[A] Quote correction, load-bearing.** "eloquence will never attain to its full development or
robust health, unless it acquires strength by frequent practice in writing" is **not at 10.3.2**,
where the gather material placed it (Butler's 10.3.2 reads "We must therefore write as much as
possible..."). The sentence is at **10.1.2**. Cited correctly in the file.

### Book 11.2, the memory chapter
The reason the subject is queued, and the best-attested part of the file.

- **[A]** 11.2.1: "memory has been called the treasure-house of eloquence."
- **[A]** 11.2.11-**16**: the Simonides banquet-collapse narrative. **Correction:** the gather
  material gave 11.2.11-23. It is 11.2.11-16; 14-16 carries the dispute and the Scopas material,
  11.2.17 onward the method.
- **[A]** 11.2.**17**, and not inside the narrative: "it is an assistance to the memory if localities
  are sharply impressed upon the mind." The inference **drawn from** the story, so the file quotes it
  on the method stop, not the narrative stop.
- **[A]** 11.2.14-15: the disagreeing authorities. Glaucus of Carystus / Leocrates / Agatharcus /
  Scopas as honorand; Pharsalus or Crannon as the town. Apollodorus, Eratosthenes, Euphorion,
  Eurypylus against Apollas, Callimachus, Cicero. He notes Cicero's version "gained wider
  circulation" and declines to choose.
- **[A]** 11.2.17-21: loci and imagines, the ordered walk, the reusable building. **[A]** 11.2.22:
  Metrodorus and the 360 loci in the twelve zodiacal signs, quoted. **[A]** 11.2 generally: his
  reservation against the confident recommendation of the *Rhetorica ad Herennium*, which the pool
  summarizes rather than quotes, checked against the Loeb text.

**Rule applied here.** The Crannon banquet belongs to **Simonides of Ceos**, whose file already
carries it as three stops at 39.517 / 22.35 and 39.2989 / 22.3986, citing Quintilian by section.
**Nothing in this file stages Quintilian in Thessaly.**

**Book 12 [A]** 12.1.1, Cato's *vir bonus dicendi peritus*, quoted; 12.1.18-19 on the philosophers;
his own concession that the perfect orator may never have existed.

**Publication [A]** Prefatory letter to the bookseller **Trypho**; dedication to **Marcellus
Victorius**, also for his son Geta. **[R]** The **Argiletum** pin is inference: he names Trypho but
no address.

## 9. The end of the record

**[A]** Domitian executed **Titus Flavius Clemens** in 95 on a charge ancient writers describe as
atheism and adoption of Jewish practices; Flavia Domitilla was exiled. **The fate of the two boys is
unrecorded, and any effect on Quintilian is unrecorded**; his text is silent, and the silence reads
as neither approval nor ignorance. **[A]** Domitian assassinated 18 September 96; **no source says
whether Quintilian was alive.**

**[R] and an honest gap.** There is **no ancient death notice of any kind**: no year, place, manner,
will, tomb or epitaph. Modern works print c. 95, c. 96, c. 100, and "after 95." Rome is assumed only
because his adult life was spent there. **[A] consistent negative:** no authenticated ancient
likeness survives; all iconography is posthumous invention.

## 10. Afterlife of the object

**[R]** The pseudo-Quintilianic **Declamations**: 19 *maiores* and 145 *minores* survive, judged
spurious or composite (Spanish Wikipedia confirms both counts). **[R, hedged]** The **388** figure
for the original Minor set is standard in the scholarship but could not be sourced this pass; the
campa carries it as what "scholarship puts near."

**[A]** Mutilated copies circulated continuously in medieval France and Italy; scholars knew most of
the work through quotations lodged in other authors (Wikipedia, "Institutio Oratoria").
**Correction carried:** the gather material had the work surviving "only through the accident of a
single medieval manuscript chain culminating at St. Gallen." That overstates it. Poggio's find
restored the **complete** text; it did not rescue the work from loss.

**[A]** September 1416: **Poggio Bracciolini**, at the Council of Constance, found a complete text at
the abbey of **Saint Gall**; Bruni's reply is quoted. **[R]** The "buried in rubbish and dust"
phrasing reaches us through secondary citation (Wikipedia summarizing Shepherd 1837) and is treated
as tradition. **Coordinate note:** the lenses disagreed on Saint Gall (47.4228 / 9.3764 vs 47.4245 /
9.3767; Wikipedia gives 47.42306 / 9.37722). Moot here, since this file has no Saint Gallen stop;
harmonised value 47.4231 / 9.3772, for whichever file needs it.

**[A]** *Editio princeps*: **ISTC iq00024000**, Rome, **Johannes Philippus de Lignamine**, 3 August
1470, ed. **Giovanni Antonio Campano**; a second Rome edition followed within the month (ISTC
iq00025000, Sweynheym and Pannartz, after 30 August 1470, ed. Bussi). The gather material had the
right year, city and editor, the wrong printer.

**[A]** Modern statues: Calahorra, by **Antonio Loperena Eseverri**; Logroño, in the **Fuente de los
Ilustres** (Spanish Wikipedia, neither dated). The 1970 date in earlier notes is **not confirmed**;
the file uses it only to order the segment.

## Stops not written, and apparatus relations for the operator

**Saint Gallen, 1416.** Poggio travelled to the abbey, so under the standing rule that is his stop on
his own file. The recovery is carried instead on **Quintilian's own stop for his own object** ("Rome,
what became of the twelve books"), at his Roman pin, with Poggio and the abbey in `sources` and
`suggested_refs`; Bruni's letter is quoted there as testimony about the book, not a scene at a place.
**Crannon and Pharsalus:** Simonides's places, on his file. **Frances Yates:** her 1955 stop names
Quintilian directly ("Cicero has it; Quintilian has it"), which is her engagement with him twenty
centuries downstream and belongs on her file; *The Art of Memory* is a source line here, nothing more.

**Apparatus: none found, and nothing deleted.** Quintilian is not in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and no relation
in the pool required the exception. Two adjacent items, so the operator can see they were handled
rather than dropped. **Backward, already legal:** Quintilian writing about Simonides, Cicero, Homer,
Virgil, Cato and Metrodorus is a subject engaging earlier material inside his own book, old hash in a
new block. **Forward, refused:** the art-of-memory cluster in `QUEUE.md` (Camillo, Llull, Roger
Bacon, Bruno, Yates) postdates him by twelve to nineteen centuries; **none is named in any campa and
none appears in any `suggested_ref`**, that relation running the other way.
