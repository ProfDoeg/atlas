# Rabindranath Tagore (1861-1941): research report

*2026-08-24. Working record behind `rabindranath_tagore.journey.json` (9 segments, 45 stops). Compiled from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses) plus this corpus's own journey files.*

**Legend.** [A] attested, source named · [R] reconstruction or dossier-only · [X] contradiction recorded, not adjudicated · [gap] checked and not found. Bare paths like /Gitanjali are en.wikipedia.org articles.

The adversarial pass overturned much of the first gather: six coordinates wrong by between 500 m and 33 km, one death in the wrong city, one quotation assigned to the wrong lecture, one Oxford venue wrong, one translator credited to the wrong man, one date still contradicted by English Wikipedia. Those corrections are the substance of this report, and they are carried into the journey's `date_confidence` fields, never its prose.

---

## 1. Jorasanko, 1861-1877

- Born 7 May 1861 at Jorasanko, north Calcutta, youngest surviving son of Debendranath Tagore and Sarada Devi. [A /Rabindranath_Tagore; /Jorasanko_Thakur_Bari] Debendranath, the Maharshi, led the Brahmo Samaj, bought the Bhubandanga land in Birbhum in 1867 and built the prayer hall there [A /Debendranath_Tagore]; that land becomes Santiniketan.
- Pin corrected. The chronology lens carried 22.5958, 88.3639, 1.2 km north-east of the house. The Jorasanko article gives 22.5850, 88.3592, matching the geography lens. Used for every Jorasanko stop.
- 1873, at about eleven: upanayana, then months on the road with his father. The Birbhum retreat, Amritsar (devotional music at the Golden Temple), Dalhousie (Sanskrit, history, astronomy, the Upanishads). [A] Months within 1873 are unsourced; the journey's dates order the segment only.
- 1877: *Bhikharini*, cited as the first short story in Bengali, and the Bhanusimha cycle, faux-medieval lyrics that fooled some readers as to their antiquity. [A] Folded into the Brighton stop.

## 2. England and the marriage, 1878-1884

- 1878, Brighton: lodged with the family of his brother Satyendranath, first Indian in the ICS; a local public school for weeks. [A] 1878-1880, UCL: Henry Morley's lectures on English literature, law read and dropped, no degree, home in 1880. [A]
- Pin corrected. Pool 50.825, -0.159. OpenStreetMap clusters Medina Villas, Hove BN3 at 50.8250-50.8265, -0.1739 to -0.1745; the pool's longitude lands 1.1 km east, in central Hove. The journey uses 50.8258, -0.1742. Cut for stop budget, not disbelieved: Shahibaug, Ahmedabad, and Malabar Hill, Bombay (Annapurna Turkhud, whom he called Nalini), both [R], dossier only.
- Marriage, 9 December 1883 (24 Agrahayana 1290 BS) to Bhabatarini, renamed Mrinalini Devi. [A /Mrinalini_Devi] Two corrections: she was nine, not "about ten" (b. 1874, he was 22); her home is Phultala, then Jessore, now Khulna, Bangladesh, with Dakshindihi the hamlet inside it.
- 21 April 1884: Kadambari Devi, wife of his brother Jyotirindranath, dies by suicide at Jorasanko; opium named; no authenticated note survives, no motive on record. [A /Kadambari_Devi] Everything the biographies build past those facts is inference, and the journey says so.

## 3. The Padma estates, 1890-1901

- 1890: assigned the zamindari estates, principally Shelaidaha, Shahzadpur, Patisar; *Manasi* the same year. [A] *Chinnapatra*, the *Galpaguchchha* stories, and the cooperative credit and arbitration at Patisar are [R], dossier plus Dutta and Robinson, not fetched. The 1898 arrival of the family at Shelaidaha [A] is cut for stop budget.
- Pin corrected (Shelaidaha). Pool 23.9053, 89.1962; /Shilaidaha gives 23.9199, 89.2200, Kumarkhali Upazila, Kushtia District, about 3 km off. That page also independently confirms the English *Gitanjali* was begun at Shelaidaha in 1912.
- Pin corrected (Patisar), badly. Pool 24.793, 88.79, roughly 33 km off into open country. OpenStreetMap places Patisar at 24.6731, 89.0801, Raninagar Upazila, Naogaon District. The journey names Raninagar. Shahzadpur (24.166, 89.596) was not re-verified [gap]: two of the three estate pins were wrong, so treat this one as unchecked.

## 4. The school, 1901-1911

- December 1901: opens the Brahmacharyasrama at Santiniketan. [A /Santiniketan] The day is unsourced; the journey's 22 December (Poush) orders the segment. [R]
- 23 November 1902, Mrinalini Devi dies aged 28. [A /Mrinalini_Devi] The chronology lens put this at Santiniketan and was wrong: the infobox gives Jorasanko Thakur Bari. She fell ill at the school and was brought down to Calcutta. The pool's two lenses contradicted each other; the contradiction is settled on the cited source. The appendicitis suggestion is Rathindranath's later opinion. [R]
- 19 January 1905: Debendranath Tagore dies at Calcutta, aged 87. [A /Debendranath_Tagore] The pool's note that only the year was verifiable can be retired. That he died inside the Jorasanko mansion is not sourced; only "Calcutta" is. The journey uses the Jorasanko pin and says so. [X, minor]
- 1907: his youngest child Samindranath dies of cholera at Munger, aged eleven, away from Santiniketan. [R] Cut deliberately: the pool implies Tagore was not at Munger, and a stop belongs to whoever was there. If restored it belongs at Tagore's own pin as news arriving.
- 11 December 1911 written, 27 December 1911 first sung at the Calcutta Congress session, the same day as a separate song honouring George V, whence the durable myth. [A /Jana_Gana_Mana] The Beadon Square pin (22.585, 88.3639) is the geography lens's and is unverified. [gap]

## 5. Gitanjali and the Nobel, 1912-1917

- 1912: English prose versions begun at Shelaidaha in convalescence and continued at sea; Rothenstein circulates the manuscript; Yeats writes the introduction; the India Society publishes *Gitanjali: Song Offerings* in November 1912, 103 prose poems from 53 poems of the 1910 Bengali *Gitanjali* plus 50 from other books, one English piece fusing two Bengali poems (*Naivedya* 89 and 90). [A /Gitanjali]
- Pin deliberately weakened. The geography lens invented an "India Society office" at 51.5117, -0.1281 near the Strand; no fetched source gives the Society's address [gap], so the journey uses the canonical London pin, 51.5074, -0.1278, and says why.
- November 1913: the Nobel Prize in Literature, first non-European laureate. [A] He did not go to Stockholm; the British minister read his banquet message [R]. So there is no Stockholm stop: the ceremony happened where he was not, and it is a `suggested_ref` on the Santiniketan stop where the news arrived. The Academy citation is quoted in the journey and tagged [R] in its `quote_source`, since nobelprize.org returned 403 and the wording is the reproduced one.
- June 1915, Knight Bachelor in the Birthday Honours [A]. 1916, the Japan lectures, published 1917 as *Nationalism* [A].

## 6. The renunciation and the university, 1918-1922

- 24 December 1918 (8 Poush 1325): foundation stone of Visva-Bharati [A /Visva-Bharati_University]; formal establishment 1921 [A]; the journey's 23 December 1921 day is [R], an ordering device.
- 13 April 1919 Jallianwala Bagh; 31 May 1919 the letter to Viceroy Lord Chelmsford renouncing the knighthood. [A /Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre, which reproduces the text]
- Stop placement, deliberate. The interlock lens proposed inheriting Gandhi's Jallianwala Bagh pin for the renunciation stop. Rejected: Tagore was not at Amritsar in 1919, and the massacre is Gandhi's stop on Gandhi's file. The renunciation sits at Calcutta, where the letter was written, with Jallianwala Bagh in its `suggested_refs`; Tagore's own Amritsar stop is 1873, when he was there. That Chelmsford never formally accepted the renunciation is unverified [R] and is not asserted.
- 9-14 June 1921, Darmstadt: Keyserling's Tagore-Woche at the School of Wisdom, with Jung, Scheler, Driesch, Frobenius and Richard Wilhelm in the circle; Keyserling had sought Tagore out in Calcutta on his own world journey. [A internal: `keyserling.journey.json`, corroborated by `martin_buber.journey.json`]
- 1922, Surul: the Institute of Rural Reconstruction under Leonard Knight Elmhirst, in India as Tagore's secretary from November 1921. [A /Sriniketan; /Leonard_Elmhirst] Name corrected: "Dorothy Straight Elmhirst" is anachronistic for 1922, since she was Dorothy Whitney Straight until September 1925. Pin corrected: pool 23.65, 87.63; OpenStreetMap gives Surul at 23.6653, 87.6648, about 4 km off.

## 7. Buenos Aires, 1924-1925

- 6 November 1924: the ship to Peru puts in at Buenos Aires with Tagore ill; Peru abandoned. [A] es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Ocampo supplies what the first pass had only single-sourced: the Plaza Hotel first, then Miralrío, a quinta Ocampo borrowed from her cousin's husband and then had to rent when eight days became two months; departure 4 January 1925, not merely "January". [A] The *Purabi* and "Vijaya" dedication is not on that page: dossier-sourced, independently carried by `victoria_ocampo.journey.json`.
- Pins inherited byte-for-byte from `victoria_ocampo.journey.json`: port -34.608, -58.3646; San Isidro -34.45806, -58.51833. The pool's own values (-34.4708/-58.5052, -34.472/-58.506) are town-centre approximations, unused.
- Villa Ocampo stop dropped. The geography lens proposed one and had its coordinate wrong by 1.7 km besides (/Villa_Ocampo gives -34.4581, -58.5183). More decisively, the Spanish article says the house Tagore occupied was Miralrío. Villa Ocampo is where other people later commemorated him: their stop, not his. [X resolved against the pool]

## 8. Europe and the last foreign roads, 1926-1935

- 1926, Italy: Naples, Mussolini in Rome, the regime's propaganda use of his remarks [A]. The 5 August 1926 repudiation in the *Manchester Guardian*, and Rolland's part in supplying evidence, are [R]: documented in the scholarship, not confirmed by any fetched page. The journey places the repudiation inside the Rome stop, not at the newspaper's offices, where Tagore was not.
- 1926, Balatonfüred: heart treatment at the state cardiology hospital; bust gifted by India 1956, replaced 2005; promenade named for him since 1957. [A /Balatonfured] The lime tree he is said to have planted was not in the pool; the journey gives it to local memory. [R]
- 1927: Singapore, Java (Borobudur), Bali, with Suniti Kumar Chatterji [A]; *Java-Jatrir Patra* [R]. 1930, Paris: exhibition of his paintings arranged by Ocampo [R]; `victoria_ocampo.journey.json` attests only the 1930 meeting in France, and the exhibition is dossier-sourced. Kept and hedged, since the meeting is attested and the year matches.
- 1930, Oxford: Hibbert Lectures in May, published 1931 as *The Religion of Man* [A]. Venue corrected: the pool pinned the Sheldonian (51.7548, -1.2544); the Hibbert series is hosted by Manchester College, today Harris Manchester College, 51.7558, -1.2520.
- Caputh, the Einstein conversation. [X, live, not resolved by fiat.] /Rabindranath_Tagore says 14 April 1930; themarginalian.org, quoting the transcript published as "Note on the Nature of Reality", says 14 July 1930. The journey uses 14 July and states the conflict in `date_confidence`: July is the standard scholarly date, and April is impossible on an itinerary in which Oxford falls in May and Germany follows. Pin corrected from 52.366/12.955 (and the interlock lens's 52.3667/12.95) to 52.3439, 12.9886.
- Moscow, September 1930, *Russiar Chithi*. [R], dossier-only, cut for stop budget, and the first thing to restore.
- 1932: Iran at Reza Shah's invitation [A]; Iraq and the Bedouin [R]; Pune and the Poona Pact fast [R], cut. The plane sent for him and the reading at Hafez's tomb are traditional and unverified. [R]
- Ceylon: [X], and cut. /Rabindranath_Tagore dates the leg to 1933; other biographies say May 1934. The pool asserted 1934 as [A], which it is not. The anthem question is settled the other way and kept: Ananda Samarakoon composed *Namo Namo Matha* himself in 1940, adopted 22 November 1951, having abandoned his Santiniketan course after six months. [A /Ananda_Samarakoon]
- July 1935: Maximiani Portas meets Tagore at Santiniketan, converts, takes the name Savitri Devi. [A internal, `savitri_devi.journey.json`]

## 9. Illness, death, afterlife

- Late 1937, first collapse. [A] The sourced wording is "late 1937"; the pool's "September 1937" is unsourced and was demoted. The same article records a second, worse collapse at the end of 1940 from which he never recovered, which the pool missed and which makes 1937 the first of two. Both are in the journey. In 1937 Satyendra Nath Bose dedicates *Visva-Parichay* to him. [A internal]
- 30 July 1941, last poem dictated, the day before surgery; 7 August 1941, death at Jorasanko, aged eighty, complications of a urinary and prostatic illness treated surgically, no autopsy-based diagnosis. [A] That 7 August is 22 Srabon is universal Bengali usage, unverified against a fetched source. [R]
- Cremation at Nimtala. [A, upgraded from the pool's R.] The fetch that failed as "Nimtala Ghat" succeeds as /Nimtala_Crematorium: cremated there in 1941, with a Rabindranath Tagore Memorial in the compound. Pin corrected from the pool's two disagreeing values (22.596/88.357 and 22.5959/88.3554) to 22.5936, 88.3517. The memorial complicates the "no tomb at all" framing: no grave, but a marked memorial on the cremation ground, and the journey says that. Crowd disorder around the body: [R], accounts vary, not adjudicated.
- Anthems. India: first stanza adopted 24 January 1950 [A]. Bangladesh: provisionally 10 April 1971 by the Provisional Government, constitutionally 16 December 1972 [A /Amar_Sonar_Bangla]; the pool's bare "1972" is split accordingly. Jorasanko: Rabindra Bharati University founded 1961 for the birth centenary, museum status the following year [A]; the journey's 8 May 1961 is an ordering device [R].
- Nobel medal. Stolen from the Visva-Bharati vault 25 March 2004; two replacements, gold and bronze, from the Swedish Academy 7 December 2004; baul singer Pradip Bauri detained 2016 on suspicion of assisting. [A /1913_Nobel_Prize_in_Literature] Contradiction resolved: the first pass read the page as saying the medal was recovered; what was recovered was the reward. The original remains missing, and the journey says so. Santiniketan inscribed by UNESCO in 2023, 45th session, criteria iv and vi, including the Uttarayan houses and Rabindra Bhavana [A /Santiniketan]; the day is unverified and the journey's 17 September 2023 is [R].
- Jardín de los Poetas, Palermo. [gap, and written as a gap.] QUEUE.md names Tagore as one of the busts; the afterlife lens could not confirm it. es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosedal_de_Palermo says the garden holds 26 busts and the retrievable portion named only nine, Tagore not among them; the city pages 404'd. The stop is kept, dated with an explicit placeholder, and written as an open question, on the canonical pin inherited from `jose_marti.journey.json`. Settle it with a Turismo Buenos Aires or *La Nación* roster before treating it as fact.

## Quotes: checked, and three corrections

Verbatim and confirmed: Gitanjali I, X, XXXV, XCIII (Wikisource on the 1912 Macmillan edition; Gutenberg 7164); the Chelmsford passage (quoted with citation on two Wikipedia pages, not checked against the archival letter or *English Writings* vol. 8); *Nationalism* (Gutenberg 40766); Yeats's introduction (Wikisource).

1. "The spirit of conflict and conquest..." is in "Nationalism in the West", not "Nationalism in Japan" as the pool had it, and begins mid-sentence. Not used; recorded so the misattribution does not propagate.
2. The Yeats line silently turned a subordinate clause into a sentence; the original opens "But though these prose translations...". The journey quotes it with ellipses and says why in `quote_source`.
3. "The rain patters, the leaf quivers" is verbatim in Gutenberg 22217 but wrongly attributed twice over: *My Reminiscences* (1917) was translated by his nephew Surendranath Tagore, and the passage is about learning to read. Not used.

The "Mahatma" attribution is worse than unprovable: /Mahatma dates Tagore's use to 6 March 1915 while citing two earlier written records, Pranjivan Mehta's 1909 letter to Gokhale and a Jetpur document of 21 January 1915. That Gandhi called him "Gurudev" is documented nowhere reached in this pass, and stays [R]. The knighthood stop says both.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Tagore is not listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing here applies anyone's framework backward or forward. Four relations in the pool are not rooted in his own life; per the adversary's duty none was silently deleted, and each is recorded with its direction.

1. Albert Camus at Villa Ocampo, 1949: FORWARD. `albert_camus.journey.json` calls Beccar "the villa where Tagore convalesced". It postdates Tagore's death by eight years and is Camus's own travel. Not written into this file in any form; it is already correct where it sits, as a one-directional relation from Camus to Tagore. No grant needed or requested.
2. Hermann Hesse's *Siddhartha* (1922): NEITHER, dropped from prose. The interlock lens itself flags it as concurrent context, not a meeting; Hesse and Tagore have no attested contact, and naming him in a campa would be co-location dressed as relation.
3. Hedwig Lachmann's German translations, Hermsdorf, from 1903: BACKWARD, one-directional, handled. She engaged his work; he did not engage hers. That belongs as a stop on her household's file (Landauer's) and on Tagore's only as a reference. It sits in the `suggested_refs` of the Darmstadt stop. No campa mention.
4. Swami Vivekananda: no relation established. QUEUE.md line 193 pairs them as "the two great Indian voices at Western intellectual crossings", which is genuine and curatorial, but `swami_vivekananda.journey.json` carries no Tagore stop and no meeting is attested either way. Vivekananda is named in no campa; the pairing lives in the queue and here. To make it visible the honest form is a shared Calcutta and Brahmo milieu note on both files, separately sourced, not a name-drop on one.

Two further placements follow the same rule, flagged because the pool proposed otherwise. Rathindranath Tagore and Santosh Chandra Majumdar at the University of Illinois (Japan 1906, graduated 1909; the pool's "c. 1906-1909" is off at the front) is Rathindranath's travel: no stop, a `suggested_ref` on the Sriniketan stop. Stockholm, 10 December 1913: Tagore was not there; no stop, a `suggested_ref` on the Santiniketan stop.

Interlocks named in campa, ten slugs, each with a direct or documented relation evidenced in the sections above: `w_b_yeats`, `victoria_ocampo`, `keyserling`, `jung`, `giuseppe_tucci`, `mussolini`, `einstein`, `savitri_devi`, `satyendra_nath_bose`, `mahatma_gandhi`. `einstein.journey.json` carries no Tagore stop, confirmed by grep; adding one would need its own Einstein-side sourcing and is not done here, since a journey file does not reach into another.
