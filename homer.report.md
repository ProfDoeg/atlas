# Homer: research report

*2026-08-22. Written from the verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses) after adversarial verification. Slug `homer`; artifacts `homer.journey.json` and this file.*

**Legend: [A] = attested, source named · [R] = reconstruction, tradition, or legend.** Contradictions below are flagged and left standing; none is adjudicated. Sources that could not be reached are named together with the reason.

---

## 0. The governing problem

There is no biography of Homer. This report separates three things that ancient and modern writers alike run together: **the poems**, whose text, transmission and reception are documentable; **the Life**, meaning the pseudonymous *Vita Herodotea* and its relatives, which supply the itinerary furnishing most of this journey's stops, all **[R]** and all undated in the source; and **the man**, for whom the surviving record contains no document of any kind, no contemporary mention, no inscription, no artifact, no date.

The journey file's register is "national mythology: the canon is true," so the Life is narrated as a life. Evidential status lives in each stop's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose, and every vita stop says there that the year shown is an ordering device and nothing more.

**Dating, the whole spread, unresolved.** Herodotus 2.53 says Homer and Hesiod "lived but four hundred years before my time, as I believe," giving roughly 850 BCE **[A, Rawlinson, Wikisource, fetched]**. Powell reads that literally: c. 800-750 BCE. Janko's linguistic-statistical work gives the eighth century. M. L. West argued from internal echoes of Hesiod that the Iliad cannot precede c. 660-650 BCE, the Odyssey up to a generation later. All three **[R, Wikipedia 'Homer', reporting them]**. Nagy's evolutionary model names no composition date at all, describing progressive fixation through performance with an important sixth-century Panathenaic stage **[R, dossier summary; Nagy not fetched]**. **No date is asserted anywhere in the journey file.**

## 1. Before the poet: the matter

- **Wilusa and Ahhiyawa.** Hittite archival documents name a western Anatolian polity Wilusa, widely identified with Troy/Ilios, and a power called Ahhiyawa, widely identified as Mycenaean Greek. **[A as to the dossier's summary; primary tablets NOT fetched, recorded as a gap.]** The identifications are the majority reading, not a certainty, and say nothing about Homer.
- **Hisarlik.** Repeated destruction and rebuilding across Bronze Age strata **[A]**. Troy VIIa is dated 1300-1180 BC with destruction around 1180 BC **[A, Wikipedia 'Troy VII']**. *Two pool corrections:* one item gave c. 1220 BCE for that same destruction and another c. 1180, and the file follows the article at 1180; and Schliemann began digging in **1870**, 15 June **1873** being when he uncovered the gold he called Priam's Treasure, so the pool's "announced in 1873 that he had discovered Troy" is compressed.
- **Cup of Nestor, Pithekoussai.** Manufactured c. 735-720 BC, found 1954 by Giorgio Buchner in Tomb 168 of the San Montano cemetery, in a cremation grave of c. 720-710 BC **[A, Wikipedia 'Nestor's Cup (Pithekoussai)']**. The pool's citation was to a disambiguation page and its "roughly 740-730 BCE" too early. **Not used as a stop** (an object made by somebody else, where the tradition never sends Homer), but recorded as the hardest evidence that alphabetic Greek and circulating epic material coexisted in the eighth century.

## 2. Birth and childhood: Cyme, Smyrna, the Meles

All **[R]**, from the Life attributed to Herodotus, dated by its own source article to the **3rd-4th century CE** despite its Ionic-dialect disguise **[A, Wikipedia 'Life of Homer (Pseudo-Herodotus)']**.

- Conception at Cyme (Critheis, ward of Cleanax of Argos, sent to Smyrna to avoid scandal), birth beside the Meles at a women's festival, the sighted boy named **Melesigenes**, the cave at the river's source shown as his composing place, and **Phemius** the schoolmaster marrying Critheis and leaving him the school: **all [R]**, none dated in the source, the cave's location lost.
- The **Homereum** shrine on the Meles bank and Homer's head on Smyrna's civic coinage are historically real **[A, Wikipedia 'Smyrna']**, sitting inside a stop whose birth narrative is not.

**A structural point, carried in the file's confidence fields rather than argued in campa.** Phemius is the Ithacan singer of *Odyssey* I; **Mentes** and **Mentor**, below, are both disguises Athena takes in the same poem. The Life furnishes its poet's biography out of his own poems, three times over.

## 3. The voyage and the blindness

- **Mentes of Leucas** finances a voyage through "Tyrrhenia," "Iberia," and other lands **[R]**. **The source names no city anywhere in this itinerary.** Cumae and Gadir are the researcher's own placeholder pins; both stops declare this, on the same footing as the Egyptian Thebes pin below.
- **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** The same Life gives two incompatible accounts of the onset of blindness: one has the eye disease worsen on **Ithaca**, in Mentor's house, with Ithacan tradition claiming the blindness began there, the other has it recur and become permanent at **Colophon**, which the biographer himself prefers. Both are stops; each confidence field names the other.
- Larissa Phriconis and Neonteichos **[R]**. Both pins were **wrong in the pool and were corrected**. Larissa moved from 38.7889 / 26.9928 to **38.6676 / 27.0314** (Wikipedia 'Larissa (Aeolis)'), a 14 km error; Neonteichos, which inherited it, from 38.79 / 26.99 to **38.70 / 27.025**, inside the band roughly thirty stadia from Larisa on the Hermus. Neonteichos is not securely located by anyone, and the file says so.
- **Cyme, the naming.** The council refuses him a pension; an opponent objects that supporting every *homeros* would burden the city **[R]**. This is a folk etymology. The ordinary Greek noun *homeros* means hostage or pledge, an alternative no source adjudicates. **Lucian** makes the same joke from the far end: his Homer in the afterlife claims to have been a Babylonian named **Tigranes**, called Homer because he was a hostage among Greeks. **[A** that Lucian wrote it; the content is declared fiction by its own author.**]**

## 4. Chios

- **Thestorides** at Phocaea copies the poems for board and performs them on Chios as his own; Glaucus the goatherd at **Pithys**; the household at **Bolissus**; the school and two unnamed daughters at **Chios town**; the **Cypria** given to Stasinus of Cyprus as one daughter's dowry. **All [R].** No historical Thestorides is attested anywhere, and the dowry story exists to explain a real authorship dispute.
- **Three coordinate errors in the pool, all corrected.** Pithys and Bolissus at 38.4863 / 25.9126, about 5.4 km west of Volissos and in the sea, moved to **38.483 / 25.975**. Chios town at 38.3775 / 26.065, the *island* infobox centroid, 7 km inland in the mountains, moved to **38.3725 / 26.1375**. Daskalopetra pinned in two places by two lenses of the pool (38.4258 / 26.1069 and 38.4136 / 26.1394), reconciled to **38.4136 / 26.1394** at Vrontados.
- The **Homeridae** are a real institution **[A, Wikipedia 'Chios'; Pindar, Nemean 2]**. Their claim of biological descent from an individual Homer is not.
- *Live confusion worth recording:* the marble plate at the entrance to Volissos quotes "Herodotus" on Homer having lived there. The sentence is from the pseudonymous Life, not Herodotus. The file notes this rather than repeating it.

## 5. Delphi, Chalcis, Egypt

- **Delphi [R].** The oracle warning him to "be careful of the enigma of the young children," reported by Pausanias 10.24.2. **Could not verify:** Perseus and LacusCurtius would not render that section, so the wording is unconfirmed. **Fabricated citation caught and removed:** the pool cited a Wikipedia article titled **"Tomb of Homer"** here and again for the Ios tomb, and **that article does not exist** (see Sources). Both citations were dropped and replaced.
- **Chalcis [R].** The pool tagged this **[A]** with the note "traditionally placed c. 700 BCE, per Hesiod's own account." **Both are wrong and both were changed.** Hesiod, *Works and Days* 650-662, describes his one sea voyage from Aulis to Chalcis for the funeral games of Amphidamas and the tripod dedicated on Helicon, but **Homer is not mentioned in Hesiod's account at all**, and Hesiod supplies no date. The *Certamen* is a second-century CE text, naming Hadrian at line 33, possibly deriving from Alcidamas' *Mouseion* **[A, Wikipedia 'Contest of Homer and Hesiod']**. Retagged **[R]**, false date attribution deleted.
- **Egyptian Thebes [A for the passage, R for its content].** Diodorus Siculus 1.96.2 was **verified verbatim on LacusCurtius**: the Egyptian priests claimed as visitors Orpheus, Musaeus, Melampus, Daedalus, Homer, Lycurgus, Solon, Plato, Pythagoras, Eudoxus, Democritus and Oenopides. The pool's "not independently confirmed" caveat is obsolete and was dropped. **But the passage names no city**, so Karnak is a placeholder pin and the stop says so. The pool's coordinate 25.7188 / **32.6396** also sits west of the temple complex; corrected to 32.6573.

## 6. Samos and Ios

- Samos: the Apaturia phratry, the priestess of Kourotrophos, the potters' *Kiln*, the midwinter *Eiresione* **[R, Life sec. 26 and 32-34]**.
- **Contradiction, flagged and not resolved.** Pseudo-Herodotus has Homer land ill on Ios and die of that illness, and explicitly **rejects** the riddle of the lice as the cause. Other ancient Lives make failure to solve the riddle the cause. Both are ancient, both circulated, no source adjudicates. Both appear in the riddle stop's confidence field. **Two candidate tomb sites**, Plaketos and Agia Theodoti, likewise conflict.
- **Alcaeus of Messene**'s two epigrams in the Greek Anthology, c. 2nd century BCE, are the earliest literary tradition fixing the death on Ios.
- **The tomb searches [A].** Pasch van Krienen with Spyridon Valetas in **1771**, finding a stone near the chapel of Agia Aikaterini reading "here under the earth lies the sacred head of heroic Homer," then doubting it in print on grammatical grounds. Theodore and Mabel Bent, **25 January 1884**, a three-hour ride to Plaketos to see "the supposed tomb of Homer who died here on his way from Samos to Athens" **[A, Wikipedia 'Ios (island)']**. The pool under-specified this; the exact date and the Samos-to-Athens framing were added.

## 7. The cities that claim him

Argos, Salamis (Saronic) and Rhodes, alongside Smyrna, Chios, Colophon and Ios **[R]**. The classical survey is Aulus Gellius, *Noctes Atticae* 3.11, **not fetched this session**, cited at second hand. The lists vary in content between authors while the number seven stays stable. Hero-cult for Homer is reported at Smyrna, Chios, Argos and Ios **[A/R, dossier summary of Hellenistic testimony, not refetched]**. The **Hadrianic oracle genealogy** making Homer son of Telemachus and Polycaste, Nestor's daughter **[R, late, no primary text fetched]**, is the only version giving him a nameable father. The Pylos pin was corrected from 37.0192 / 21.6942 to **37.0272 / 21.6950** (Wikipedia 'Palace of Nestor'), a 900 m error.

## 8. The poems without the poet

- **Peisistratean recension [R].** Widely repeated in antiquity; modern scholarship treats it as legend, and the Wikipedia article carrying it flags the passage as unsourced.
- **Hipparchus and the rhapsodes [R for the law, A for the killing].** The Panathenaic relay law is attributed by **Pseudo-Plato, *Hipparchus* 228b**, not verified this session. The pool said Hipparchus was "targeted" in 514. **He was killed:** Harmodius and Aristogeiton organized a revolt for the Games, panicked, attacked early, and killed him; the wider coup against Hippias is what failed **[A, Wikipedia 'Panathenaic Games']**.
- **Alexandria [A].** Zenodotus, first head librarian in 284 BCE, first known critical edition, its own date unrecorded (Wikipedia 'Zenodotus'). Aristarchus of Samothrace heads the Library 153-145 BCE and produces the most influential ancient edition. The 24-book division is attributed to the Alexandrians generally, without a year. Papyri show markedly less variation from c. 150 BCE onward (Wikipedia 'Homer').
- **The Homereion.** No longer dossier-only. Wikipedia 'Apotheosis of Homer' describes the Archelaos of Priene relief as showing worshippers at an altar, representing the Alexandrine Homereion, with **Ptolemy IV and Arsinoe III** present. Retagged **[A/R]** from **[R]**. The relief is dated **c. 225-205 BC** by the British Museum, so the pool's "2nd-1st century BCE" was too late, and was found in Italy **probably in 1658**, not across the 17th-18th century. **The British Museum page returned 403/429 to direct fetch and was not read.**
- **Venetus A [A]**, Marcianus Graecus 454/822, tenth century, A scholia descending from Aristarchus, digitized May 2007. **Editio princeps [A]**, Florence **1488-1489**, not 1488 flat, editor Demetrius Chalcondyles, financed by **Giovanni Acciaiuoli** with Neri and Bernardo de' Nerli, whom the pool omitted, dedicated to Piero de' Medici. **English Homer [A]**: Chapman from 1598, complete 1616; Pope's Iliad June 1715 to May 1720, Odyssey 1725-26; Keats's sonnet 1816.

## 9. Quotations, and two bad ones caught

Quotations in the journey file are verbatim from fetched texts. Pool items needing repair, in order of seriousness:

- **Aristotle, "the art of telling lies skilfully."** Verified verbatim in Gutenberg #1974 (Butcher), **but the pool's section citation was wrong**: section **XXIV**, not IX. Not used in the end; the Poetics IV line is used instead.
- **Xenophanes B11.** The pool's English wording ("theft, adultery, and deceiving one another") **matches no published translation** and must not be presented as a quotation. The file uses **Burnet's** rendering (*Early Greek Philosophy*), the fragment attributed to Sextus Empiricus, *Adversus Mathematicos*. Two further errors fixed: Wikipedia 'Xenophanes' does **not** carry that wording, and pinning the fragment to Colophon in 540 BCE asserts more than any source does, since Xenophanes left around 545 BCE for Sicily and Magna Graecia. The file names Colophon only as his birthplace.
- **Thucydides and the Hymn to Apollo.** The pool called this "the earliest securely datable literary source" for the blind poet of Chios. **Overstated:** Wikipedia 'Homeric Hymns' has the Hymn attributed to Homer "by **Pindar** and Thucydides, who wrote around the beginning and the end of the fifth century BCE respectively." The correct citation is **Thucydides 3.104.4-6**, winter 426/425 BCE, not 431. **The primary passage was not retrievable this session**, so with the "earliest" framing false too, **this material is used nowhere in the file.**
- **Herodotus's supposed contradiction, reframed.** The pool presented the "168 years after Troy, 622 years before Xerxes" reckoning (c. 1102 BCE) as a disputed Herodotean passage. It is not: that arithmetic belongs to the **pseudonymous Life**. The contradiction is between two authors, not two readings of one, Herodotus 2.53 at c. 850 BCE against Pseudo-Herodotus at c. 1102. Neither is resolved; neither appears as a date in the file.

- **Strabo, retagged R to A.** The pool's caveat that the Strabo defense could not be verified after four failed fetches is obsolete; the passage was retrieved. **Strabo, *Geography* 1.1.10** (LacusCurtius, Loeb): against Eratosthenes' claim that a poet aims only to entertain, Strabo holds that "poetry is a kind of elementary philosophy" and defends Homer's apparent geographical errors as poetic choice. Date loosened, the Geography being c. 7 BCE to 23 CE against the pool's over-precise "-20".
- **Seventh-century poets.** The pool called the earliest allusions in Archilochus, Alcman, Tyrtaeus and Callinus **explicit**. Wikipedia 'Homer' says **implicit**. The distinction matters: none of the four names Homer.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Homer is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`. Per the standing rule, nothing was silently deleted; the following is kept and flagged for the author's ruling.

**Direction caught: FORWARD.** The final segment, "The Poems Without the Poet" (9 stops: Peisistratos and Hipparchus at Athens, the Homeridae on Chios, Zenodotus and the Homereion and Aristarchus at Alexandria, Venetus A, the Florentine editio princeps, the English Homer), together with the last stop of the Ios segment ("Ios, the grave dug for," 1771), is posthumous by between two and twenty-six centuries. None is Homer travelling. Each is an act performed by somebody else upon an object that is Homer's own: the poems, or his grave.

**The reading under which they are legal**, assumed here: the standing rule's own example licenses "an artist's stop for the object they made, citing a later critic's essay about it." The Iliad is Homer's made object, and where it goes a stop for it may go, with editors and printers named as agents but never staged as travellers with scenes of their own. Every campa in that segment is written about the poem, not an editor's biography.

**The reading under which they are not:** if a journey file is a block stamped at its own moment, a file whose clock stops in the eighth century BCE cannot embed a 2007 digitization or a 1616 folio, whatever the object. On that reading the segment is a forward reach, needing the author's grant in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, like the Burroughs and Derrida grants, or deletion.

The pool was built with a dedicated afterlife lens carrying coordinates for exactly this material, which suggests the pipeline expects it. **The author's ruling is requested.** If the answer is no, the fix is mechanical: drop segment 9 and the 1771 stop, leaving 34 stops in 8 segments, and the material survives here.

**No backward apparatus relation was found**, Homer having no theoretical framework to apply. Nothing in this file reaches back past the poems themselves.
