# Max Perelman (Max Perlman, born Meiers Perlmanis), 1909-1985

*Research report, 2026-08-22. Riga to Holon by way of Whitechapel, Corrientes, Commissioner Street, Second Avenue and Sydney. Yiddish comedian, singer and songwriter; from June 1939 a Buenos Aires performer, from the mid-1950s an Israeli resident, always a touring act.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, oral testimony or inference.

Two sources carry almost everything. **Veronica Belling, "S'iz nisht dos vos amol iz geven: Max Perlman (1909-1985)," Digital Yiddish Theatre Project (UWM), 2020** is the only substantial biography reached in any language, and much of the record rests on it alone; where a second archive corroborates or corrects her, this report says so, since "Belling says" and "Belling and the archives say" are different evidentiary states. The second is the **UBA dictionary of exiled Jewish musicians in Argentina** (Glocer et al.), I.195, I.283, II.156-157. An earlier pass called that PDF unreadable. It is readable, and extracting it produced most of §9.

---

## 1. Riga, 1909-1928

- Born **1909 in Riga**, middle-class Jewish family, birth name **Meiers Perlmanis**. **[A** Belling; year and city independently in Wikidata Q81939428, with VIAF 98494469 and LC no2019073077.**]**
- At about **six**, sings in the choir of **Cantor Rozovski**; children's roles in Yiddish theatre follow. **[A** Belling.**]**
- Studies at the **dramatic studio of the Riga Peretz Club**, in Russian and Yiddish both. **[A** Belling, "1920s", no year.**]**
- **1928**: professional career begins with the **Nayer Yidisher Teater** of Riga; works between Riga and Kovno **through 1934**. **[A** Belling; UBA II.156, "desde 1928 a 1934".**]**
- **Gaps:** no day, month, street or parents' names for the birth; the cantor is named but not his synagogue; the Peretz Club building was never located; no Kovno hall is named anywhere. All declared in the file's `date_confidence` fields.

## 2. Gita Galina and the European circuit, c. 1932-1938

- Meets **Guita (Gita) Galina** in the Riga theatre; she is already established; they marry and tour together thereafter. **[A** Belling.**]** No date for meeting or marriage anywhere; the file infers c. 1932 because joint touring precedes London. Galina was born **c. 1907 in Vitebsk**, birth name **Guta Israiley** **[A** UBA I.195; independently Wikidata Q81939577**]**.
- Itinerary: **Vienna, Czechoslovakia, Poland** (a guest engagement with the Skale Theater), **France, Belgium, England**, including **London's Pavilion**, plus **Liverpool, Leeds, Manchester, Glasgow**, and about a year in Paris and Belgium. **[A** Belling; UBA II.156, same list, undated.**]**
- The London leg is datable **only by the theatre's death**: the Pavilion at 191-193 Whitechapel Road **closed in 1935**, so the visit cannot be later than that and most plausibly follows straight on the 1934 close of the Baltic phase. **[A** closure; **[R]** the 1934-35 inference.**]**
- **1938**: **Mamele**, dir. Joseph Green and Konrad Tom, with Molly Picon, as **Dawid Samet**. **[A** National Center for Jewish Film, which spells him "Max Perelman".**]** Łódź and Ciechocinek exteriors are general film history; no source places *him* there **[R]**, so the file pins Warsaw and says so.

## 3. Buenos Aires, 1939-1947

- **7 June 1939: ARRIVAL in Argentina** with Galina aboard the **Oceanía**, having embarked at **Trieste**. **[A** UBA II.156, "El 7 de junio de 1939, llegó a la Argentina, con Gita Galina, en el buque Oceanía, habiendo embarcado en Trieste"; repeated at I.195.**]** The embarkation is undated; the file gives Trieste a declared May 1939 placeholder to order the crossing.
- **Adolf Mide** issues the invitation; the couple join the **Compañía Israelita de Operetas** and headline **Teatro Excelsior**, Avenida Corrientes 3224, facing the old Abasto market, for about three years. **[A** Belling; address and 1912-1961 dates via Teatros de Buenos Aires del Siglo XVII al XXI.**]** The city's Yiddish stage then: the commercial **Soleil, Excelsior, Ombú, Mitre**, the independent **IFT**, and vaudeville in **El Cristal** and **El Internacional** **[A** DYTP/Fordham, a **mid-1930s** count**]**.
- **9 May 1942**: at the Excelsior with Galina under **Leo Schwarz**, a JKG concert. **[A** UBA.**]**
- **14 July 1943**: **Rund um die Operette** in the **Salón "Unión Tranviarios"**, for the Sociedad Cultural Israelita; **Victor Schlichter** conducting, **André László** regisseur, **Horváth Sándor** on the gypsy orchestra; the Compañía Israelita contingent **Gita Galina, Max Perlmann, Philipp Wenning**; Offenbach, Paul Abraham, Olshanetzky, Leo Fall, Kálmán, Brodsky and Oscar Straus excerpted. Press: *Argentinisches Tageblatt* 1 July p. 4; *La Semana Israelita* IV.170, 16 July p. 9. **[A** UBA I.283.**]**
- **Through 1947**: touring **Brazil, Uruguay and Chile** **[A** UBA II.156**]**. **São Paulo** for the Brazilian leg is inference from its standing as Brazil's main Yiddish stage, documented nowhere **[R]**.
- **The Bari namesake.** A **Max Perlman** headed the JDC's relief office in **Bari**, transferred from North Africa (**JTA, 2 July 1944** **[A]**). Not the entertainer: the 1942 and 1943 dates pin the singer to Buenos Aires. Recorded because the two are routinely conflated in search.
- **Gaps:** the Salón Unión Tranviarios's address; any Brazilian, Uruguayan or Chilean venue.

## 4. South Africa, London, Southern Cone, 1948-1956

- **1948**: first South African season at **His Majesty's Theatre, Johannesburg**, under **Sarah Sylvia**, with Galina. **[A** ESAT.**]** The **eight-week run** and **20,000 attendees in two weeks** are **Belling's figures alone**; ESAT carries neither. The theatre, commissioned 1937 and completed only in **1945** after wartime interruption, replaced a 1903 house, so 1948 played the new building **[A** artefacts.co.za 423**]**.
- Tours recur **1948/49, 1951, 1972**; Belling says at least four visits; UBA II.157 says only "Entre 1948 y 1951".
- **London Grand Palais**: **1949, 1951, 1955**. **[A** Belling.**]**
- **1951**: **Menora Zahav**, about fifteen and a half, performs with him for the first time in **Johannesburg**. **[A** Belling, from Zahav interviews of 24-25 December 2018.**]** The 1951 venue is inferred from the circuit, not stated.
- **Cape Town**, mid-to-late 1950s. **[R]** Sole evidence is Oppenheimer's own hedged recollection ("mid or late fifties and perhaps early sixties"); no theatre named. Dated 1956 in the file as a midpoint.
- **Gaps:** the Grand Palais's coordinates (a declared East End placeholder is used); every Cape Town and 1972 venue.

## 5. Israel, the United States, Argentine returns, 1952-1969

- **1952**: goes to the United States, thereafter alternating it with his Israeli home **[A** UBA II.157**]**. No city is named; New York is an explicit placeholder in the file.
- **1954**: grand Israeli debut **[A** Belling**]**; the *Jewish Chronicle* of 8 January 1955 reports the visit as having run **nine months**.
- **26 August 1955**: **Der Amerikanische Littwak**, dir. Hermann Ludwig, with Galina, **Teatro Soleil**, his first documented appearance there. **[A** UBA.**]**
- **Ramat Gan**: Mayor **Avraham Krinitzi** gives them a ground-floor apartment, their permanent Israeli home. **[R]** Oral history only, Zahav via Belling, 2018; no deed or municipal record examined by anyone in the chain.
- **October 1956**: records **"No es como se quiere"** and **"Dinero y más dinero"**, words and music his own, on **Odeón**, **300 copies** registered. **[A** *Boletín Oficial*, 21 and 27 August 1957.**]** The Boletín states **no genre**; these must not be called tangos on this evidence.
- Israeli circuit, 1950s-60s: **Haifa**, **Tiberias** **[A]**; **Netanya, Nahariya, Afula**, Emek Yizrael towns **[R]**. No venue named for any; the file stages Haifa and folds the rest into its campa rather than inventing pins.
- **11 December 1960**: poster for "Max Perlman and troupe" in **Oy! Vayber Vayber!**, Tel Aviv-Jaffa area **[R]**; the hall is not established.
- **New York, 1962-1969** **[A** Belling, with NYU Second Avenue Online holdings**]**: **1962** *Honeymoon in Israel*, **Roosevelt Theater**, *NYT* review 15 October, the same year **"Kh'vil nisht keyn sakh nor a bisele"** was published, words and music his own **[A** Mlotek**]**; **1963** *Don't Worry, Brother*, Roosevelt; **1964** *Good Luck*, **Anderson Yiddish Theater, 66 Second Avenue**, music **Sholem Secunda**, with Miriam Kressyn, Jacob Jacobs, **Galina** as Zisel, Fyvush Finkel and Seymour Rexite; **1968** *Never Too Late for Happiness*, **Actors' Playhouse, 100 Seventh Avenue South**; **1969** *Oh What a Wedding!*, Anderson, Zahav taking over ingenue roles.
- **1955, 1966, 1967**: Argentine returns. **[A** UBA II.157.**]** The 1967 tour is with **Shimon Dzigan** **[A** Belling**]**.

## 6. Late seasons and death, 1971-1985

- **c. 1971-72, Jerusalem, Mitchell Theatre**: **Golda Meir** attends. **[R]** Oppenheimer's recollection via Belling; no programme, notice or security record; venue address not located.
- **1972**: returns to South Africa **[A** Belling, via Chaim Lewis in *Jewish Affairs*, May 1972**]**.
- **October 1981**: an elderly grandfather in Hirshbein's **Dem shmids tekhter**, **Norman Thomas Theater** (a high-school auditorium on East 33rd Street), with Ben Bonus and Mina Bern. **[A** Belling.**]**
- **6 January 1983**: **Gita Galina** dies of leukemia, buried at **Holon**. **[A** leukemia, burial and interval, Belling; the exact date **[R]**, Wikidata Q81939577 citing BillionGraves, unchecked against the stone.**]**
- **1984**: last New York tour, **Oy Mama! Am I in Love**, Shalom Yiddish Musical Comedy Theatre, **Town Hall**. **[A** Belling.**]**
- **1985**: final tour of the **United States, Australia and Israel** with **Menora Zahav** **[A** UBA II.157**]**; Sydney is Belling's city for the Australian leg. On returning, found to have **terminal cancer** **[A** Belling**]**. **Gap:** no disease type, hospital, physician or city named anywhere.
- **23 July 1985**: dies in Israel, buried at **Holon beside Galina**. **[A** Belling, and Belling alone; see §9.4.**]**

## 7. The songs

- **"Ven Du Lakhst"**, words and music his own, catalogued genre **Theatre/Tango**, on *Max Perlman featuring Yiddish Songs* (P-011a) and *Max Perlman at the Yiddish Theatre* (P-011b). Also his in the same catalogue: **"Ahava"** (tango/theatre), **"Ahava Dos Heyst Libe"** (waltz), **"Vos Geven Iz Geshen"** (waltz/theatre), **"Alts Tsulib Parnuse"**, **"A Mensh Mit A Maske"**, **"A Meydele Fun Tel-Aviv"**, **"Yiddishe Kapelye"**, and **"Zol Es Geyn Vi Es Geyt"** (music by **Sholem Secunda**, words by Perelman). **[A** Freedman Jewish Sound Archive, UPenn, what=7123.**]**
- **Zemereshet** (2675) holds **"Madagaskar"** and notes he recorded for the **Stinson** label in Argentina. **[A]**
- **Skura and Fiszman.** Real and correctly attributed: **Susana Skura and Lucas Fiszman, "From *shiln* to *shpiln* in Max Perlman's Songs," *Journal of Jewish Languages* 4:2 (2016), 231-251** (Crossref) **[A]**. The abstract says it analyses **three songs**, treating code mixing of Yiddish, Castidish, Spanish and Argentine slang plus moral criticism of middle- and lower-class Jewish life in Buenos Aires. It does **not** mention thirty songs, Fundación IWO, the Jewish Museum, or motifs of automobiles and gambling; those four circulating claims are **withdrawn to [R]**. Full text **403 Forbidden**; the body may carry a corpus figure, but nobody in this chain has read it.

## 8. Contradictions and open questions, not resolved

1. **Which label.** QUEUE.md's own entry says he "recorded on the **Panzer** label under the Argentine spelling of his name". The *Boletín Oficial* documents **Odeón** (1956); **Zemereshet** says **Stinson**; the 1971 LP is **Greater Recording Co.** No source reached mentions Panzer. Left standing for the operator.
2. **"Two of the best known Argentinian Tangos."** A flyer for "Tango Nuevo: A Musical Exploration of Jews & Tango" states this outright, naming neither song and citing nothing. **[R]** The page verifiably makes the claim; nothing corroborates it. Freedman classifies exactly **one** song of his as Theatre/Tango, and the 1956 Odeón pair have **no stated genre**. Kept as a circulating legend rather than deleted.
3. **First South African season**: 1948 or 1949; Belling writes "1948/49". And **Town Hall vs. an alternative venue** for *Oy Mama! Am I in Love*, 1984: a UBA-derived note in circulation names a different house.
4. **The Roosevelt Theater's address**, 1962-63: unknown. The Yiddish-programming Roosevelt at 111-117 East Houston Street closed in 1951 and was demolished in 1959; the other Manhattan Roosevelt is a Harlem cinema. Both excluded, no third candidate found.
5. **Which Buenos Aires house** he played on the 1966 and 1967 returns: unnamed in every source reached.
6. **"El Gardel judío"** belongs to **Jevel Katz**, not Perelman. The DYTP Katz page never uses the phrase; it gives Katz's March 1940 death, 25,000 mourners, and names **Perelman performing Katz material with Simón Tenovsky**. For the nickname cite **Nuriel, "Jevel Katz: Representing Yiddish Buenos Aires" (LAJSA)**.

## 9. Corrections applied to the research pool

Each verified against the source named, not settled by preference.

1. **7 June 1939 is the arrival, not the departure** from Trieste (UBA II.156).
2. **1952 US visit and the Brazilian leg upgraded [R] to [A]**, and **14 July 1943 identified**, by extracting the UBA PDF an earlier pass called unreadable.
3. **The 1985 tour was not Sydney only**: US, Australia and Israel, with Zahav. **Brazil in 1967 dropped** (UBA confines Brazil to the pre-1947 leg); **a 1953 South African tour dropped** as unsupported.
4. **Wikidata does not corroborate 23 July 1985.** Q81939428 carries only the **year**, no exact date, no place of death, no burial place, despite holding VIAF, LC, WorldCat and Zemereshet ids. The date rests on Belling alone.
5. **The 20,000/eight-weeks figures are Belling's, not ESAT's**, which carry no attendance data. **The five-theatre count is a mid-1930s figure and DYTP/Fordham's**, not a 1939 figure and not Czackis's. **Teatro Soleil moved out of the early 1940s** to 26 August 1955.
6. **Chaim Lewis named** as the "snake charms" reviewer, and **A. Misheiker** as author of the shund passage (*Jewish Affairs* 3, no. 12, 1948, 28-29); both had been recorded as unnamed.
7. **Two distinct NYT hat lines separated**: "the agile, beaming star..." belongs to *Don't Worry, Brother*, 1963; **"a comfortable evening of familiar horseplay" credited to the NYT**, not an unnamed paper.

## 10. Quotations and afterlife

Thirteen of the file's forty-five stops carry a quote, all verbatim from Belling quoting the named organ: **Chaim Lewis** (1972), **A. Misheiker** (1948), the *Jewish Chronicle* three times (including 8 January 1955), an unnamed **Habima** member (ellipsis in the source), **Bruce Oppenheimer** three times, and the *NYT* four times (1962, 1963, 1981, 1984).

Afterlife, all **[A]** unless marked: the **1971 LP** *At the Yiddish Theatre* (GRC-216), digitised by the Internet Archive from Boston Public Library vinyl; **Karsten Troyke & Trio Scho**, 2006 and 2012, five Perlman songs between them; the **Preßburger Klezmer Band** video, which exists under the stated title but whose composer credit could **not** be verified **[R]**; **Stanford Special Collections**, eighty-one Israeli Yiddish posters, thirteen featuring Perelman; **Zemereshet** and **Save the Music Archives**; the *M&G* of 18 April 1997. Belling's own verdict, her judgment and not a fact, is that he was "largely excluded from the historical canon of Yiddish performance" despite his popularity.

**Interlock actually used: Gita Galina only**, the direct family interlock the QUEUE entry was built on (QUEUE.md 407-408); she has **no journey file yet**. Every other name in the campas is a documented participant in his own scenes, and none has an atlas file.
