# Celia Sánchez Manduley (1920-1980): research report

*2026-08-15. Prepared for `celia_sanchez.journey.json`: 7 segments, 38 stops, gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or synthesis, including anything the sources assert without a citation of their own. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Coordinate problems are reported here and carried into the journey file's `date_confidence` field, where all hedging lives.

**Structural warning.** The official hagiography of Celia Sánchez is also, in large part, the only detailed record there is: the archive holding her papers is an institution she founded, and EcuRed, the fullest reachable source on her early years, is a Cuban state encyclopedia. Where the outside and Cuban records disagree, both are reported; where only the Cuban record exists, that is stated.

---

## 1. Media Luna and Pilón, 1920-1952

**Birth.** 9 May 1920 at Media Luna, Oriente, in a house her father bought in 1913, in the Central Isabel / Pueblo Nuevo quarter **[A: es.wikipedia; EcuRed]**. Father Dr. Manuel Sánchez Silveira, physician to the sugar mill and the countryside; mother Acacia Manduley, dead while Celia was a child **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Sibling count contradicted, unresolved**: en.wikipedia "one of eight children", es.wikipedia "nueve hijos". The campa says "eight of them or nine" and calls neither correct.

**Coordinates corrected.** Pool 20.1394, -77.0928; es.wikipedia gives 20°08′40″N 77°26′10″W = **20.1444, -77.4361**, the pool's pin being ~35 km east, inland toward Bayamo. Used twice (birth house; the same building as museum). **Manzanillo likewise**: pool 20.3428, -77.1275, reused in four entries; es.wikipedia gives **20.3397, -77.1086**, ~2 km off.

**Registration and baptism.** Civil register, Juzgado Municipal de Vicana, Manzanillo, 16 October 1920; baptized at the Purísima Concepción there, 22 July 1922 **[A: EcuRed]**.

**Formation.** No university, well read, politically formed by her father, "médico rural, hombre culto y comprometido con el Partido Ortodoxo liberal", named as the source of her first contact with politics; her exposure to the rural poor came through his rounds **[A: es.wikipedia]**. She joins the Ortodoxos under Chibás c. 1947-1951 **[R: es.wikipedia states the affiliation with no date, document or chapter named]**; stop dated 1947-01-01.

**Pilón.** Moves there c. 1940 and lives nearly sixteen years keeping the house and assisting the practice **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**. **Coordinates corrected**: pool 19.8497, -77.3183 sits ~6 km offshore; es.wikipedia gives **19.9053, -77.3208**. The pool also pinned Manzanillo and Pilón, 55 km apart, to one point; split here into separate stops.

**Pico Turquino, 1953.** She organizes and joins a climb carrying a bronze bust of José Martí to the summit for the centenary of his birth **[A only in the sense of being universally repeated in Cuban biography; no primary document reached]**, the one pre-war event supplying the mountain knowledge the war uses. Cuban accounts date it variously to January (Martí's centenary) and to 21 May 1953: **contradiction flagged, not resolved**; stop dated 1953-01-01.

**Ensenada de Mora, 1954.** A holiday among the cays near Pilón with her father **[R: EcuRed]**. Pool pin 19.85, -77.3 falls in open water; the file uses **19.9028, -77.3672**, approximate.

**10 March 1952.** Batista's coup radicalizes her **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**. Nothing records where she was that day; the stop is placed at Pilón, where she lived, and says so.

## 2. Norma: the coastal network, 1955-1956

**The cell.** Founding organizer of the 26th of July underground in the Manzanillo/Pilón district from 1955, at first running arms, war name **Norma** **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**. **El Marabuzal**, a farm near Manzanillo, is where her network dispatched the first armed detachment sent up to the guerrillas **[A: es.wikipedia]**; not independently pinned, so the town coordinate is used and the imprecision stated.

**The peasant network.** es.wikipedia has her organizing "una red de colaboradores campesinos" near the intended landing point, i.e. beforehand; en.wikipedia independently has her organizing the peasant family network that housed and fed the rebels *after* the landing **[A, both]**. The two agree on substance and differ on timing. Both are true in the ordinary sense that the network was built beforehand and used afterward, but **no source consulted says so**, so the file keeps two stops rather than a synthesis neither source makes.

**Niquero.** Staging point for trucks, guides and supplies **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Coordinate 20.0472, -77.5781.

**The landing, corrected.** The Granma did not land at Playa Las Coloradas. It grounded at **Los Cayuelos**, a mangrove point ~2 km away, 2 December 1956 **[A: es.wikipedia "Desembarco del Granma"; EcuRed]**. The file names Los Cayuelos and keeps the pin at **19.92, -77.67**, already canonical in `fidel_castro.journey.json` (whose own note calls it approximate). The pool's alternative, 19.8644, -77.5761, is rejected: ~20 km due south of Niquero, in water.

**Alegría de Pío, 5 December 1956, and Cinco Palmas, 18 December.** She was at neither, and **no stop is written** at either, because a stop belongs to whoever was actually there: the ambush and the survivors' reunion are the Castro brothers' scenes. The ambush enters her file only as news reaching Manzanillo, with the campa stating plainly that her whereabouts that week are undocumented. **Gap stated as a gap.** The pool's Cinco Palmas coordinate is corrected for the record anyway, being 35 km out: it lies in the Purial de Vicana zone of Media Luna municipality, near **19.99, -77.29** **[A: EcuRed "Purial de Vicana", giving Purial at 19°59′20″N 77°17′38″W]**.

## 3. The Sierra, 1957-1958

**First ascent, contradiction, flagged.** es.wikipedia: "El 19 de marzo subió a la Sierra Maestra" **[A]**. EcuRed records an *earlier* ascent in February 1957, with Frank País **[A]**. Both cannot be her first, and neither source acknowledges the other. The file writes both as stops, in order, stating the disagreement in each; the February stop is pinned at **19.9889, -77.2939** (EcuRed's Purial de Vicana, the foothill zone through which the mountains were reached), tagged approximate. The pool's further framing, that on 19 March she switched permanently to combatant status, is **rejected**: nothing supports it, and she kept moving between coast and column afterward.

**Purial de Vicana, attribution weakened.** The pool made Purial a node of her courier network **[R]**. EcuRed's Purial article ties the place to Raúl Castro's passage and **does not mention Celia Sánchez**. The file uses Purial only as the geography of the February ascent.

**El Uvero, 28 May 1957.** She takes part in the attack; in the standard Cuban account, the first woman to hold a combat position in the Rebel Army **[A: es.wikipedia; EcuRed confirms the date]**. **Coordinates corrected across provinces**: pool 19.9167, -77.1667 is in Granma near Pilón; EcuRed places El Uvero in Guamá municipality, **Santiago de Cuba**, near Chivirico. The file uses **19.95, -76.58**, approximate; no gazetteer coordinate was reached.

**The star of the Comandante, 21 July 1957.** `che_guevara.journey.json` records Celia Sánchez pinning the comandante's star to Guevara's beret after Castro promotes him **[A: corpus interlock, verbatim mention]**. The hardest personal interlock in the file. Pin **20.017, -76.75**, inherited byte-identical.

**The papers, tag corrected.** The pool tagged the wartime insistence on preserving documents **[A]** in one list and **[R]** in another, for the identical assertion. en.wikipedia documents only the **1964** founding of the archive, so the claim is **[R]** throughout. What *is* attested, from the far end of the chain, is Suárez Pérez's 2014 statement that the Sierra documents reached her Havana flat "guardados en los mismos paquetes preparados durante la guerra" **[A: es.wikipedia, citation 10]**, which implies the practice without dating it. The Sierra stop rests on that implication and says so.

**La Comandancia de La Plata.** EcuRed substantiates her role directly: she conceived the buildings, without felling a single tree or levelling any terrain, and oversaw the blankets, the flowers, and what the article calls the details of beauty and sensitivity **[A: EcuRed]**. **Coordinates flagged, canonical pin kept anyway**: the pool gave 19.9889, -76.9833; EcuRed places the site in Bartolomé Masó municipality, on the northern slope, suggesting nearer 20.02, -76.94. The file uses **19.9667, -76.9**, already canonical in `fidel_castro.journey.json`, because one site must carry one pin across the atlas. **An open problem affecting two files; fix it in both at once, not in one.**

**El Jigüe, downgraded.** The pool claimed she organized supply and care of the wounded. EcuRed dates the battle 11-21 July 1958, places it near the confluence of the Jigüe and La Plata streams in present-day Guamá, and **does not mention Celia Sánchez** **[A for the battle; the attribution to her is R and thin]**. The file keeps the stop, corrects the date, moves the pin south to ~19.96, -76.88, and writes her role as tradition rather than fact.

**Pelotón Mariana Grajales, 4 September 1958.** Formed at La Plata; she helps organize it, and Teté Puebla's testimony is quoted verbatim **[A: es.wikipedia, citation 7]**. Puebla credits Fidel Castro with teaching the women to shoot and naming them his escort, not Celia; the campa does not overwrite the witness.

**Weak stops kept with the hedge visible.** Pino del Agua, 16 February 1958, presence attested **[A: EcuRed]**, coordinate approximate. Campechuela and Bartolomé Masó, on the arms chain and the supply road, are both **[R: regional historiography, uncited]**, the latter pinned at district level.

## 4. Havana, 1959-1972

**Arrival.** Enters Havana with the column in January 1959; becomes Castro's closest aide and gatekeeper over access to him **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Teté Puebla on the Caravana de la Libertad is quoted verbatim **[A: es.wikipedia, citation 7]**. `fidel_castro.journey.json` records Castro running the government from the **Habana Libre** for three months from 19 January; her gatekeeping begins in that lobby by every biographical account, though the Castro file does not name her **[R for her presence there; A for the government's location]**. Pin **23.1392, -82.3828**, inherited byte-identical.

**The apartment.** Calle 11 entre 10 y 12, Vedado, where from the early 1960s the documents accumulated **[A: es.wikipedia, quoting Suárez Pérez]**. **Coordinates corrected**: pool 23.1372, -82.3931 is the Vedado centroid around Calles E/F; Calles 10 and 12 lie ~1.3 km west. The file uses **23.1372, -82.4045**, grid-derived, flagged as derived rather than gazetteered.

**Secretary of the Presidency of the Council of Ministers, 23 March 1962** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Three corrections. The pool's "for over twenty years" is **wrong**: March 1962 to January 1980 is under eighteen, and it is her proximity to Castro, not the post, that runs twenty-one years from 1959. After 1976 the title is Secretary of the Council of State, that body being a creation of the 1976 constitution. And **the office's location is an inference**: nothing consulted sites it at the Palacio de la Revolución. Pinned 23.1225, -82.3836.

**Oficina de Asuntos Históricos, 4 May 1964** **[A: es.wikipedia; EcuRed]**. Holdings as of 2014: more than 159,000 photographs across 28 collections and more than 56,000 documents **[A: EcuRed]**. **Address corrected**: the pool duplicated the apartment pin; EcuRed gives **Calle 8 No. 210, entre 11 y Línea**. File uses 23.1385, -82.4005, grid-derived. **Anachronism flagged**: "del Consejo de Estado" is the later name applied retroactively to a 1964 founding.

**Coppelia, 4 June 1966, claim rewritten.** The pool credited her with championing the park. en.wikipedia has the project **led by Fidel Castro**, the architect **Mario Girona**, and Celia Sánchez **naming** it after her favourite ballet **[A]**. The file writes the naming, not the championing. Coordinates corrected to ~23.1405, -82.3853, ~380 m north of the pool's pin.

**Granma offices, 23 August 1967.** She visits the newspaper with women who had been combatants **[R: EcuRed]**. Pin ~23.1256, -82.3861, approximate; the pool's 23.135, -82.3667 does not match the building.

**Parque Lenin, downgraded and corrected.** The pool said she "personally directs the design and construction", tagged **[A]**. es.wikipedia credits the design to **Antonio Quintana Simonetti**, dates the inauguration to **22 April 1972**, and **does not mention Celia Sánchez at all**. She is widely credited as promoter; that is what the file says, at **[R]**, naming Quintana. Coordinates corrected to **23.0236, -82.3569**.

## 5. Death and afterward

**Death.** Lung cancer, Havana, 11 January 1980, aged 59 **[A: en.wikipedia; es.wikipedia]**. The pool's "less than a year after being diagnosed" is in **neither** article as retrieved and is **dropped as unsourced**. No hospital is named anywhere consulted: **a gap**.

**Burial, contradiction, unresolved, carried into the campa.** es.wikipedia: Necrópolis de Cristóbal Colón. en.wikipedia: "A memorial to and mausoleum for Celia Sanchez was built in Parque Lenin", its lead suggesting she was memorialized there with remains now at Colón; its own Colón Cemetery article **does not list her among notable interments**. The stop states the contradiction rather than choosing. Three cemetery coordinates appeared across the pool; reconciled to **23.1231, -82.3986**.

**The relationship with Fidel Castro, the central unresolved question.** en.wikipedia: the intimacy of the relationship "is subject to debate, including rumours of the two sharing a romantic relationship. However, neither Sánchez nor Castro addressed these rumours during their lifetime" **[A]**. Outside accounts (Geyer, Stout) treat it as open; BBC Mundo ran a piece headlined around whether she "fue amante de Castro" **[R: fetch blocked; cited through its title as reflected in the Wikipedia articles]**. Cuban framing describes a political partnership and distinguishes it explicitly from a conjugal relationship **[R: synthesis; no primary document naming this position was reached]**. The file gives this its own stop, states both traditions, resolves nothing. She never married and left no children **[R: inference from an absence]**, and was Castro's longest-standing collaborator, roughly twenty-three years **[R: synthesis]**.

**Casa Natal museum, date contested.** EcuRed: inaugurated **11 January 1983**, third anniversary of her death, house declared a National Monument in **1990**. es.wikipedia: opened **9 May 1990**, her seventieth birthday, possibly conflating the two. **Flagged, not resolved**; stop dated 1983-01-11. Address confirmed, Ave. Raúl Podio Saborit 111; holdings 144 objects including a Colt .45, a Springfield rifle and an M-1 carbine **[A: EcuRed]**. The pool's closure-and-2015-reopening **did not appear** in the article retrieved and is **dropped as unverified**.

**Manzanillo memorial: the pool's version was a misreading, rewritten.** The pool described a tiled staircase on the seafront used as a backdrop for wedding photographs, tagged **[A]**. The raw wikitext of en.wikipedia actually says: "People of Manzanillo use the altar of the Caridad del Cobra [sic] Virgin as a marriage altar signifying her dedication to the Cuban Revolution." That is an altar of the Virgen de la Caridad del Cobre: no staircase, no seafront, no photographs. The same article separately mentions "a memorial and mausoleum in Manzanillo". The file writes only what is supported, at **[R]**, and names the confusion.

**Currency and naming: the pool's caution was itself an error.** A previous pass flagged the banknote and coin claims as possibly hallucinated and discarded the Zimbabwe detail. The raw wikitext of en.wikipedia contains all of it: her face in the banknote watermark, several varieties using her portrait as a security feature, a 10-peso coin struck ten years after her death, and schools, hospitals and community centers named for her "from Cuba to Zimbabwe" **[A: en.wikipedia, itself thinly sourced]**. Not cross-checked against a catalogue: **stated as a gap**.

**Fidel Castro's 1981 tribute** is quoted verbatim on the death stop **[A: dedication speech at the hospital named for her, 16 January 1981, at en.wikipedia citation 19]**. **Flag: the pool places the speech in Havana, but the Hospital Celia Sánchez Manduley is in Manzanillo.** Neither was verified, which is why the quote is attached to a stop rather than staged as a scene.

**Not used as stops.** The reported memorial issues of Granma and Bohemia, January 1980 **[R: unverified]**, and the 2016 documentary *Celia, la más hermosa flor* **[A: es.wikipedia]**, which has no screening location.

## Interlocks

Two atlas travelers are named inside a campa, both on a direct personal relation, not shared geography: **`che_guevara`**, whose beret she pins the comandante's star to on 21 July 1957, a verbatim mention in his own file (pin 20.017, -76.75 inherited byte-identical), and **`fidel_castro`**, the relation the whole life turns on (his pins inherited byte-identical at Playa Las Coloradas, La Comandancia de la Plata and the Habana Libre).

**Deliberately not named**, though the pool proposed them: Meyer Lansky, Allen Dulles, John F. Kennedy and Charles Luciano (shared city or structural mirror only, the last a false positive in the pool itself), and **Tamara Bunke** (institutional overlap in Havana ministries, with no meeting, correspondence or influence attested either way). The Buenos Aires canonical pin (-34.6037, -58.3816) is **not** inherited: she was never there. Conversely, `fidel_castro.journey.json` has no coordinate for Manzanillo or Pilón, the base from which the coastal reception of the Granma was run; those pins originate here.

## Honest gaps

Where she was on 5 December 1956 and on 10 March 1952. The hospital where she died. Whether the February or the March 1957 ascent was the first. Whether the museum opened in 1983 or 1990. Whether she lies at Colón, is memorialized at Parque Lenin, or both. Where her office stood. Exact coordinates for El Uvero, El Jigüe, La Comandancia de la Plata and both Vedado addresses, all derived or approximate, none from a gazetteer. And anything of her inner life not filtered through an institution she founded or a state that canonized her.
