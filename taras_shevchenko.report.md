# Taras Shevchenko (1814-1861): research report
*Compiled 2026-08-02 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Taras Hryhorovych Shevchenko, born a serf at Moryntsi in the Kyiv gubernia, died a freedman at the Imperial Academy of Arts in Saint Petersburg; poet, painter, etcher, ethnographer; the founding author of modern Ukrainian literature.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by one source only. Contradictions are flagged and **not** adjudicated. Where a coordinate in the working pool was wrong, the correction is given with its authority and the wrong pair is recorded too, because it is still in circulation.

**Method.** Two lenses gathered independently (chronology and quotation; geography and coordinates) over Encyclopedia of Ukraine, English and Ukrainian Wikipedia and two Project Gutenberg translation volumes. An adversarial pass then checked every coordinate against a named gazetteer entry and every quotation against the plain-text Gutenberg files, line by line. Corpus interlock was resolved against `~/essays` and the existing journeys in `working/journeys/`.

---

## I. The serf village, 1814-1828

**[A: Encyclopedia of Ukraine; en.wikipedia]** Born at **Moryntsi**, Zvenyhorodka county, Kyiv gubernia, 25 February Old Style / 9 March New Style 1814, a serf of the landowner Vasily Engelhardt. Both calendars are in the sources; the journey uses New Style for birth and death and says so in `date_confidence`.

**[A: en.wikipedia timeline]** The family moves to **Kyrylivka** (today Shevchenkove) in 1816. Mother Kateryna dies 1 September 1823; father Hryhorii dies 2 April 1825. Orphaned at eleven.

**[R: en.wikipedia timeline]** He runs away from the village in February 1827, aged about thirteen. Tagged [R]: the timeline asserts it, cites no document, and the standard biographies treat the flights and failed placements as a composite rather than a dated event.

**[A: en.wikipedia]** Hired out to a **deacon at Lysianka**, one of several failed placements.
**[R]** Sent to **Tarasivka** to learn from village icon painters. Tradition only; no primary source reached. The coordinate 49.25 / 30.55 is approximate and is carried into the journey with that hedge stated.

**Coordinate corrections.** *Moryntsi*: pool 49.2667 / 30.9333, correct **49.2519 / 31.0011** (en.wikipedia "Moryntsi"), ~5 km off into farmland west of the village. *Kyrylivka / Shevchenkove*: pool 49.3833 / 30.7167, correct **49.1883 / 31.0997** (uk.wikipedia "Шевченкове (Звенигородський район)"), ~34 km off and reversing the bearing, since the village lies southwest of Moryntsi rather than northwest. *Lysianka*: pool 49.2333 / 30.4667, correct **49.2569 / 30.8233** (en.wikipedia "Lysianka"), ~26 km off.

**Internal inconsistency in the pool, flagged:** Kyrylivka was pinned at 49.3833 / 30.7167 twice and at 49.3833 / **31.45** once. One village, three coordinates, none right; 31.45 puts the pin near Kaniv, some 60 km away.

## II. The master's household, 1828-1838

**[A: Encyclopedia of Ukraine; en.wikipedia]** Taken as a houseboy by **Pavel Engelhardt**, heir to the estate.

**[A for the city]** He is in **Vilnius**. Encyclopedia of Ukraine: he "served him in Vilnius (1828-31) and then Saint Petersburg." Ukrainian Wikipedia narrows it: "від осені 1829-го до початку 1831-го."

**[R for the teacher] Contradiction, not resolved.** The English timeline names **Franciszek Ksawery Lampi** as his Vilnius teacher. Lampi worked in Warsaw. The painter usually named for the Vilnius period is **Jan Rustem**. Encyclopedia of Ukraine names nobody. The journey carries Vilnius as attested and the teacher as disputed, naming both candidates and neither as fact.

**[R] Contradiction, not resolved: was there a Warsaw stay?** English Wikipedia asserts a Warsaw apprenticeship; EoU and Ukrainian Wikipedia keep him in Vilnius throughout. If it happened it belongs to 1830-31, not 1829 as the pool had it. Kept as a stop and labelled contested.

**[A]** Date correction: the household reaches **Saint Petersburg in early 1831**, not 1830. The November Uprising begins 29 November 1830 and the move follows that winter.

**[A: Encyclopedia of Ukraine; en.wikipedia]** Engelhardt apprentices him about four years to the decorative painter **Vasilii Shiriaev**. He is noticed by **Karl Briullov**.

**[A: Encyclopedia of Ukraine; en.wikipedia]** **5 May 1838**: freedom purchased for **2,500 rubles**, raised by lottery of a Briullov portrait of the poet **Vasily Zhukovsky**. The pool's afterlife lens tagged this [R] as "widely repeated tradition." That tag is wrong and is raised to [A]: EoU states the purchase and the date outright, en.wikipedia gives portrait, lottery and sum.

**Minor contradiction, flagged.** He dedicates "Katerina" to Zhukovsky "in memory of **April 22, 1838**" (Manning, Gutenberg #78316, lines 3735-3736), while the manumission is dated **5 May 1838**. Nothing reached reconciles them; that April 22 is the lottery and 5 May the deed is a guess, labelled as one.

## III. The Academy and the Kobzar, 1838-1845

- **[A]** Enters the **Imperial Academy of Arts** under Briullov's supervision, 1838. Silver medal April 1839; typhus November 1839.
- **[A: EoU; en.wikipedia "Kobzar (book)"]** **Kobzar**, Saint Petersburg 1840: eight poems, printed by E. F. Fischer, 1,000 copies.
  - **[R] Demoted clause.** "The censor removes and destroys material from the first 100 copies before sale" does not appear in the article cited for it and could not be corroborated. Kept out of the journey prose.
- **[A]** **Haidamaky**, 1841. **Hamaliia** and the etching album **Zhivopisnaia Ukraina** (Picturesque Ukraine), 1844. **Son** (The Dream), 1844, the court satire that will convict him.
- **[A: EoU]** Graduates 1845; joins the **Kyiv Archeographic Commission**, travelling Russian-ruled Ukraine to draw monuments and collect folklore.

**[A]** First return to Ukraine as a free man, May 1843; **Yahotyn**, the Repnin estate, where **Varvara Repnina** forms a lasting attachment; **Kyrylivka** in the autumn of 1843, where he sees his surviving siblings after about fourteen years, most of them still serfs.

**[A for the poem, source-thin for the site]** **"Zapovit"** is written at **Pereiaslav, 25 December 1845**. The Pereiaslav attribution is scholarly consensus, not confirmed here against a document; the poem itself was read in Manning (Gutenberg #78316, lines 7420-7423).

## IV. The Brotherhood and the sentence, 1846-1847

**[A]** Joins the **Brotherhood of Saints Cyril and Methodius** in Kyiv, 1846: a clandestine society for a federation of free Slavic peoples and the abolition of serfdom.

**Date contradiction, flagged.** The chronology lens gives the arrest as **5 April 1847**; the geography lens dates the Brotherhood arrests to **March 1847**. Both derive from the same English Wikipedia article. The journey uses 5 April for his own arrest and notes the earlier round-up date in `date_confidence`.

**[A: Manning, Gutenberg #78316]** The trial record survives in translation and is the best primary material reachable for this life. Four passages were verified against the plain text:

- Count **Lakhtenberg**, President of the Academy, lines 1093-1100: Shevchenko "has a gift for poetry," "was always considered as a moral man, perhaps something of a dreamer and an honorer of the Little Russian past, but nothing prejudicial came to the knowledge of the Academy."
- Shevchenko at interrogation, line 1104: he admits "some insolent and satirical works," "forgetting his conscience and the fear of God."
- Count **Orlov**, chief of gendarmes, lines 1107-1134: Shevchenko, "instead of feeling eternal gratitude to the persons of the Most August Family, which had deigned to free him from serfdom, composed verses... of the most revolting character."
- The sentence of **26 May 1847**, lines 1136-1144, and beneath it: "Under the strictest supervision with a prohibition of writing and sketching."

**[R]** That **Nicholas I** added that clause in his own hand is asserted by en.wikipedia and universal in the tradition; Manning prints the addition without naming who wrote it. Kept, tagged.

## V. Exile: Orenburg, the Aral, Novopetrovske, 1847-1857

**[A]** Posted as a private to **Orsk fortress**, June 1847. Attached to **Lieutenant Aleksei Butakov's Aral Sea survey**, 1848-49, and permitted unofficially to draw for the expedition's scientific record in defiance of his own sentence.

**Correction, place.** The pool's label "Raim fort (Fort Karmakchi)" conflates two posts. **Raim** (Raiym), founded 1847, stood near the Syr Darya delta close to modern Aralsk (en.wikipedia "Aralsk", 46.783 / 61.667); **Karmakchy** is ~170 km upriver. "Fort Karmakchi" is struck; the exact Raim coordinate stays [R], the site being eroded and partly submerged.

**Correction, dates.** The expedition winters at **Kos-Aral over 1848-49**, not in 1849; the Karakum crossing in the pool belongs to the 1848 overland march from Orenburg to Raim, before the sailing. He returns to **Orenburg** late in 1849, but the denunciation, the confiscation and the arrest for breaching the ban come in **April 1850** (en.wikipedia: "April 1850: Arrested for wearing civilian clothes, violating drawing ban").

**[A]** Transferred October 1850 to **Novopetrovske fortification**, Mangyshlak Peninsula, today **Fort-Shevchenko**, Kazakhstan. Nearly seven years under the ban.

**[R]** The "bootleg booklets," notebooks of verse hidden in his boots, are universal in the tradition and were not independently sourced here. One contemporaneous adjacent statement survives: the biographical note to A. J. Hunter's 1922 translation (Gutenberg #68486, lines 2946-2947), "They forbade him to write but he wrote verses secretly and concealed them." That is an editor's third-person sentence, not the poet's, and is quoted as such.

**[A: Journal, 21 July 1857, Manning lines 1313-1319]** The release in his own hand, ending: "he first greeted me with Liberty: July 21, 1857, at eleven o'clock in the morning."

## VI. The return, 1857-1859

**[A]** Released under the amnesty following the death of Nicholas I; confined first to **Nizhny Novgorod**, forbidden the capitals and Ukraine.

**Correction, date, and an internal contradiction in the pool.** The pool had him "returning to the capital" on **25 February 1858**. He does not. That is his birthday, the day he receives in Nizhny Novgorod the news that permission has been granted (en.wikipedia: "On his birthday, 25 February 1858, Shevchenko received the news..."). He leaves in March, stops at **Moscow**, reaches Saint Petersburg late March. The pool entry also contradicted its own Moscow entry dated 1858-03.

**[A: Journal, 10 March 1858, lines 1409-1412]** On meeting Butakov again: "My heart grows cold at the very memory of that wilderness."
**[A: diary, 17 March 1858, lines 1418-1421]** On Repnina in Moscow: "She has changed for the better... Has she not met in Moscow a good confessor?"

**[A]** June 1858: the Academy gives him rooms and a workshop.

**Last journey to Ukraine, 1859.** **[A]** Kyiv in June; the land search along the Dnieper with his brother-in-law Varfolomii near **Korsun**; a visit to the scholar **Mykhailo Maksymovych**; **Kyrylivka**, where his brother Mykyta and sister Iryna are still bound to the estate; the Tarnovsky estate at **Kachanivka** on the way north. The engagement to **Lykera Polusmakova**, a young former serf, dates to this year and later breaks down.

**Correction, place.** Maksymovych's **Mykhailova Hora** is at **Prokhorivka, left bank of the Dnieper opposite Kaniv** (approximately 49.78 / 31.55), not 49.75 / 31.45, which lands inside Kaniv town on the right bank. The visit itself is confirmed.

**Correction, place.** He is **not** arrested at Priorka. En.wikipedia: "in July Shevchenko was arrested following a report to the local police, which accused him of blasphemy, and transported to Kyiv via Cherkasy." The arrest happens downriver in the Cherkasy district; **Priorka is where he lodges after release**.

**Correction, coordinate.** Kachanivka Palace is at **50.8367 / 32.6556** (en.wikipedia, "Kachanivka Palace"), not 50.7667 / 32.6167, an error of ~8 km.

## VII. Death and the hill, 1860-1861

**[A]** Publishes **Bukvar Iuzhnorusskii**, a Ukrainian primer for popular literacy, 1860. Dropsy diagnosed December 1860. Final poem 14 February 1861.

**[A]** Dies in Saint Petersburg, **26 February Old Style / 10 March New Style 1861**, aged forty-seven, in his workshop at the Academy. Buried first at the **Smolensk Cemetery**. Dostoevsky, Turgenev, Saltykov-Shchedrin and Leskov attend the funeral.

**[A]** Reburied on **Chernecha Hora** (Monks' Hill, later Taras Hill) above the Dnieper near **Kaniv**, **22 May 1861** (8 May Old Style), per "Zapovit."

**Coordinate contradiction inside the pool, resolved by authority.** Two lenses pinned the grave differently: 49.7519 / 31.4581 (afterlife) and **49.7331 / 31.5147** (geography, after uk.wikipedia "Чернеча гора"). The first lands in Kaniv town, ~3.5 km off. The journey uses 49.7331 / 31.5147.

**Correction, the cross.** The 1861 burial is marked by a **wooden** cross. The **cast-iron** monument-cross paid for by **Vasyl Tarnovsky the Younger**, designed by **Viktor Sychuhov**, goes up in **summer 1884** with the first folk museum of the Kobzar beside it (uk.wikipedia); Soviet authorities dismantle it in the 1920s (en.wikipedia). The pool had the cross over the fresh grave; it was not. **[A: EoU]** The hillside becomes the core of the **Taras Shevchenko National Reserve**, 1925.

**Correction, attribution.** Shevchenko did **not** set "Zapovit" to music. EoU: "After being set to music by H. Hladky in the 1870s, the poem achieved a status second only to Ukraine's national anthem." Ukrainian Wikipedia gives **Mykola Lysenko** the first setting, 1868, for a Lviv commemoration, and dates **Hordii Hladky's** rival melody to 1868 too. The sources disagree on the decade of the Hladky tune; **not resolved**.

## VIII. The book and the monuments

- **[A: en.wikipedia "Kobzar (book)"]** A fuller *Chyhyryn Kobzar* in 1844 adds "Haidamaky." After the 1847 arrest the Kobzar is banned across the empire and confiscated from libraries, shops and private hands, which is why lifetime copies are rare. The fullest lifetime edition, 17 works, appears in 1860 at the expense of **Platon Symyrenko**. A two-volume **Prague** edition of 1876 adds posthumously recovered work. Peter Fedynsky's complete English translation follows in 2013 (Glagoslav).
- **[R]** The 1922 Canadian *The Kobzar of the Ukraine* (A. J. Hunter) and "more than 100 languages by the 1990s" are **not** in the article cited for them. The Hunter volume was read directly this session as Gutenberg #68486, which attests book and translator independently; only the citation chain was wrong.
- **[A / R] Washington, D.C., 27 June 1964.** The Taras Shevchenko Memorial is dedicated before roughly 100,000 people. **Correction:** Eisenhower was **not** president in 1964; Lyndon Johnson was. Eisenhower attended as a former president and unveiled the statue. Coordinate corrected to **38.9100 / -77.0490**, the 2200 block of P Street NW, not the generic city-centre pin.
- **[R]** A monument on the Manitoba legislature grounds in **Winnipeg**, 1961. Unverified; kept [R] and kept out of the journey.

## IX. The corpus interlock

**[A: `/home/drdoeg/essays/204_hornero.md`]** A bronze Shevchenko stands in the **Jardín de los Poetas, Parque 3 de Febrero, Palermo, Buenos Aires** (-34.575, -58.414). A **hornero**, the rufous ovenbird, has built its clay oven-nest in the statue's arms and rebuilds it within days each time the park administration removes it. **[A: `instagram/2024-04-04-monumento-a-taras-shevchenko-ciudad-autonoma.md`]** The post of 4 April 2024 photographs the monument and gives his dates and titles: poet, writer, painter, folklorist, ethnographer.

**[A: `204_hornero.md`]** **Ral Veroni's** Platense symbology takes the hornero and its oven-house, capped with a red Phrygian cap, as its emblem, and the famous instance of that nest is the one at Shevchenko's feet. Veroni is a corpus figure (census #15) but not an atlas traveler; the link runs through the monument.

**[A]** The census counts exactly **3** corpus documents naming him (`census_real_persons_2026-08-02.md`, line 166, rank #160: one essay, two Instagram posts); `QUEUE.md` line 77 uses the same framing.

## X. Gaps, stated as gaps

1. **The Vilnius teacher is unknown to this report.** Lampi is wrong or misplaced; Rustem is the literature's usual answer and nothing reached confirms it. Whether Warsaw happened at all is likewise open: one source says yes, two keep him in Vilnius.
2. **No archival document was reached for any event.** Everything primary here arrives through two 20th-century translation volumes on Project Gutenberg; the Ukrainian scholarly apparatus was not consulted. The Pereiaslav attribution of "Zapovit" rests on consensus, not on a document seen here.
3. **The Raim coordinate is approximate.** The fort site has eroded; no gazetteer entry for Raim itself was found, only for nearby Aralsk.
4. **Unverified, carried as [R]:** the 1961 Winnipeg monument; the "first 100 copies" censorship of the 1840 Kobzar; the 1848 Karakum overland march, asserted in the pool with no citation of its own.
5. **Lykera Polusmakova.** The 1859 engagement and its collapse are attested; no dates, no letters, nothing further. She is in the report and not in the journey, because there is no place to pin her.
