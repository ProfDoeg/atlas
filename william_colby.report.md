# William Egan Colby (1920-1996): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Register: national mythology: the canon is true. Calendar: gregorian.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or inference. Contradictions are flagged where they sit and are **not** adjudicated.

Colby's working life was conducted in a medium designed to leave no record, and the documentary base is lopsided in consequence: the war and the congressional year are heavily documented, while the station years between them survive as little more than a list of cities and dates. The journey says so rather than filling the middle with atmosphere.

## 1. Origins and the garrison childhood (1920-1936)

**[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** Born 4 January 1920 in St. Paul, Minnesota, only child of Elbridge Colby, an English professor who took an Army commission, and Margaret Mary Egan, of a St. Paul Democratic political family.

**[R]** Panama. Biographers list the Canal Zone among the father's postings without naming a fort or a year, and nothing from it appears in Colby's own writing. Dated in the journey to a placeholder inside the 1920s.

**[A as residence / R as to detail]** Tianjin, China. Wikipedia's William Colby article lists Tianjin among the childhood postings; Encyclopedia.com says "China."

> **Source failure flagged.** The pool cited "Wikipedia, Elbridge Colby" as an [A]-grade source for the years c. 1927-1929 and a "military and academic post." **There is no Wikipedia article on Elbridge Colby the professor-soldier (1891-1982);** the only article of that name is his great-grandson, born 1979. The residence stands; the years and the description are carried as **[R]**.

**[A: Britannica; Princeton Alumni Weekly memorial]** Burlington, Vermont, public high school, mid-1930s: the first settled stretch of the boyhood, with no documentary trace beyond the fact of attendance.

## 2. Princeton and the interrupted degree (1936-1943)

**[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]** A.B. in politics, Princeton, 1940; senior thesis on the Spanish Civil War. **[R: Princeton Alumni Weekly memorial]** That he edited the college newspaper, and that the class was a feeder for the OSS and the early CIA: true of the cohort generally, but the specific claims rest on one memorial notice.

**[A: Wikipedia]** Enters Columbia Law School 1941; volunteers for the Army; **LL.B. conferred 1947**, the intervening years being the war.

**[R, weakly sourced]** Parachute qualification at Fort Benning, 1943. The qualification itself is not in doubt, but **the site and the year are unverified:** the pool's sole source, warhistory.org, returns **HTTP 403** and no other reachable source names the jump school. Its coordinates (32.3893, -84.9663) also land in the city of Columbus, off the installation; corrected to c. 32.3526, -84.9414.

## 3. The OSS: France and Norway (1943-1945)

**[A: Wikipedia; SpyScape; USASOC Jedburgh histories]** Joins the OSS in 1943 and trains at Milton Hall, east of Peterborough, the joint OSS-SOE finishing school that put through 93 teams. Colby's team is BRUCE, with a French officer and a radio operator.

> **Date and coordinate corrected.** The pool put the training in 1943; the school ran from 1943 but Colby's course was the 1944 intake preceding his August drop. Read enlistment 1943, training 1943-44. The pool's pin, 52.61 / -0.335, is 3.5 km NNW of the house, which is at **52.5845, -0.3116**.

**[A: CENTCOM Citadel; European Center of Military History]** Team BRUCE drops from a Carpetbagger B-24 on the night of 13/14 August 1944 into the Yonne; the pilot misjudges the run and puts them roughly twenty miles from the intended field, onto German-held ground.

> **Contradiction flagged.** The pool wrote "by the second week of September the sector is liberated." The Yonne was overrun in **late August 1944** (Sens 21 August, Auxerre 24 August). The reachable sources do not resolve whether the framing is simply wrong or refers to a sector the team had moved to. The journey uses the late-August dates.

**[A: HistoryNet, "Surprise, Kill, Vanish"; Operasjon Rype (Wikipedia); U.S. Army]** 24 March 1945: Colby leads the NORSO Group (largely Norwegian-American volunteers of the 99th Infantry Battalion) onto the ice of Jævsjøen in Operation Rype.

> **Three corrections, all material.**
> 1. **"The only OSS operation on Norwegian soil" is false.** Operation Woodlark ran in parallel in spring 1945 (Jørstad bridge, April), and further NORSO parties followed. The defensible superlative, and Colby's own, is that Rype was **the only combined ski-parachute operation the U.S. Army mounted in the war.**
> 2. **Personnel figures conflict.** The pool said 31 expected, 12 arrived. HistoryNet, its own cited source, says **35** designed and **15** arrived, three B-24s having failed to find the lake and one having dropped five NORSOs into neutral Sweden. Irreconcilable from here; the journey follows the traceable figures.
> 3. **Coordinate corrected.** Pool gave 64.265, 12.32, which is Snåsa **village**, some 40 km NW. Jævsjøen is at **64.0627, 13.0505** (Wikidata Q6977515).

**[A: gjefsjo.no]** Headquarters at the Gjefsjøen mountain farm. **Coordinate corrected:** the pool gave 64.25, 12.4, Snåsa village again; the farm publishes **64.1046, 13.0848**.

**[A: HistoryNet; The Norwegian American]** **The Grana bridge was not attacked.** The 335-foot Grana bridge was Rype's assigned primary target and Colby **decided against it**: heavily guarded, 24 miles west of camp, "too risky an undertaking without his whole team." The Norwegian American: "Rather than blow up the large Nordland Railway bridge at Grana, they successfully targeted a smaller railroad bridge at Tangen."

> **Sharpest contradiction in the pool.** Its geography lens describes Grana as though the demolition happened, and its Tangen entry has the team adding "a second breach to the line already crippled at Grana." **No reachable source supports any breach at Grana.** Coordinates (64.244, 12.39) unverified; carried as approximate.

**[A: gjefsjo.no; HistoryNet]** Tangen bridge south of Snåsa blown at about 06:30 on 15 April 1945 (HistoryNet gives 14-15 April). **[A: HistoryNet]** 23 April 1945: two and a half kilometres of track destroyed in Lurudalen, the last major demolition. Coordinates for both (64.15, 11.95 and 64.35, 12.45) are unverified.

> **Contradiction flagged.** The pool had the Tangen party "reinforced by" Operation Woodlark personnel. HistoryNet has **Lt. Glen Farnsworth and three Norwegian assistants**, against four 36-foot steel I-beams over a 13-foot creek. **No source checked places Woodlark men at Tangen.**


## 4. Law, and the first stations (1945-1958)

**[A: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Marries Barbara Heinzen, 1945. LL.B. 1947, then practice at Donovan's New York firm and a move to Washington for the National Labor Relations Board.

> **Corrections.** The pool dated the NLRB move to 1947; Encyclopedia.com puts it at the **end** of the stint, c. 1949. The firm's 1947 name was **Donovan, Leisure, Newton, Lombard & Irvine.**

**[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** Joins the CIA in 1950.

> **Coordinate corrected.** The pool pinned the joining at 38.9047, -77.0163, which is Adams Morgan and matches no agency facility of any period. **In 1950 the CIA was not at Langley** (it moved in 1961) but at the **E Street Complex, 2430 E Street NW, c. 38.8951, -77.0510.**

**[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]** Stockholm, 1951-1953, embassy cover, work on the stay-behind networks later gathered under the name Gladio. **[R]** Everything beyond the fact of the posting: no specific operation appears in any open biography. **[A: Britannica; general histories of CIA funding of the Italian Christian Democrats]** Rome, 1953-1958, State Department cover, covert political funding against the largest Communist Party in Western Europe.

## 5. Vietnam, first tour, and the Far East Division (1959-1967)

**[A: Britannica]** Saigon from 1959, eventually chief of station. **[A: Wikipedia; Encyclopedia.com]** Returns to Washington in 1962 as **deputy** chief of the Far East Division and becomes **chief in 1963.**

> **Correction flagged.** The pool made him chief in 1962. Encyclopedia.com: "chief of the Far East Division from 1963 onward." Wikipedia: "1962 (deputy, then chief)." The two posts are routinely conflated. The pool also labelled the stop "Washington, D.C." while giving Langley coordinates. **Langley is in Virginia.**

**[R]** Vientiane and the secret war in Laos. His authority over the programme as division chief is undisputed, but **no reachable source places him personally in Vientiane**, and the journey stop says so in its date_confidence.

## 6. CORDS and Phoenix (1968-1972)

**[A: Encyclopedia.com; Special Forces Chapter 78; Wikipedia]** 1968: returns as deputy to Robert Komer, succeeds him at CORDS, and holds the **personal rank of ambassador from the 1968 appointment** (the pool dated the rank to 1969; Encyclopedia.com: "headed the controversial CORDS/Phoenix program as ambassador in 1968").

**[A as the programme's own reported figures]** 1968-1972: Phoenix credited with neutralising **81,740** suspected NLF members, **26,369** killed.

> **Standing dispute, not adjudicated.** These are the programme's own numbers. Historians dispute both the totals and the proportion correctly identified, and a Senate subcommittee found imprecise targeting and allegations of torture and extrajudicial killing (RAND; Readex). Colby held to his death that the great majority were combat deaths.

**[A: Wikipedia, Phoenix Program]** Colby testifies on Phoenix in the 1971 hearings on U.S. Assistance Programs in Vietnam, the series in which K. Barton Osborn called Phoenix a "sterile depersonalized murder program."

> **Tag corrected upward.** The pool graded this **[R]**, a "secondary-literature characterization." It is a documented event and is carried as **[A]**. His July 1971 House Government Operations subcommittee testimony gave **20,587** killed for January 1968 to May 1971.

**[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]** 1972: executive director-comptroller. **[A: Wikipedia]** 2 March to 24 August 1973: **Deputy Director for Operations (DDO)**.

> **Title corrected.** The pool wrote "Deputy Director of Central Intelligence for Operations," conflating the DDO with the separate office of Deputy Director of Central Intelligence. The date is right.

## 7. The Family Jewels and the directorship (1973-1976)

**[A: Wikipedia, "Family Jewels"; Commentary; Church Committee records; Brookings]** May 1973, Schlesinger orders all employees to report in writing any activity that might have fallen outside the charter, producing **693 pages**. 4 September 1973, Colby sworn in as tenth DCI and the report passes to him. October 1973, the Yom Kippur War, ruled out by American estimates. 22 December 1974, Hersh's front-page New York Times story on two decades of illegal domestic surveillance, sourced from the file; within about 48 hours Colby announces the resignation of **James Jesus Angleton**. January 1975, the Rockefeller Commission; 1975, the Church Committee, before which he discloses assassination plots, MKUltra, mail opening and domestic surveillance, and on 16 September the shellfish toxin. 30 April 1975, the fall of Saigon. 3 November 1975, the Halloween Massacre. 30 January 1976, tenure ends.

> **Arithmetic corrected.** The pool said "roughly three years." 4 September 1973 to 30 January 1976 is **two years and about five months.**

> **Conflation flagged; it would mislead any reader if left standing.** The pool's afterlife lens attached both "the Colby purge" and "the Halloween Massacre" to a supposed purge of internal CIA dissent. **These are two different events.** The Halloween Massacre was Ford's cabinet reshuffle of early November 1975, done **to** Colby by the White House. The reduction of the clandestine service sometimes called a purge happened under **Schlesinger in 1973.** Kept apart in the journey.

**[R]** That the disclosures earned lasting hostility from the old guard, who felt he had given away the family jewels: ubiquitous in the secondary literature (Commentary; Wikipedia; Just Security), but an interpretive judgment about internal sentiment rather than a documentable fact. Written in the journey as contested reputation.

## 8. After office, and the Wicomico (1977-1996)

**[A: Wikipedia]** 1977, founds Colby, Miller & Hanes; 1978, *Honorable Men: My Life in the CIA*, with Peter Forbath (Simon & Schuster); 1984, divorces Barbara Heinzen and marries the diplomat Sally Shelton; 1989, *Lost Victory* (Contemporary Books, Chicago; the pool's source field garbled the publisher as "Contegan").

**[R]** Late advocacy for nuclear arms reduction: attested only in summary form on Wikipedia, with no speech, organisation, or dated appearance traced.

**[A: Washington Post, 7 May 1996; Roanoke Times]** Last seen about 7:15 p.m. on **27 April 1996** at Rock Point, Charles County, Maryland. Canoe found beached 28 April. **Body recovered 6 May 1996**, face down on a marshy bank.

> **Coordinate corrected, and the pool contradicted itself.** Its geography lens gave Rock Point as 38.3667, -76.9833, some 17 km NW, inland, and not on the Wicomico at all; its own afterlife lens gave the right figure, **38.27194, -76.84083.** River mouth c. 38.2518, -76.8255.

> **Arithmetic contradiction flagged.** The pool says variously an "eight-day search," "nine more days," and "nine days later." Both are defensible on different anchors: **27 April to 6 May is nine days, 28 April to 6 May is eight.** The journey names the anchor rather than picking a number.

**[A: Maryland OCME via Deseret News, 11 May 1996]** Ruled **accidental**: drowning and hypothermia associated with arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease, severe blockage of the left anterior descending coronary artery, no signs of foul play. **[A: Arlington records; Find a Grave 5898372]** Buried with military honours **13 May 1996, Section 59, Grave 655.**

## 9. Afterlife

**[A: *The Man Nobody Knew* (dir. Carl Colby, First Run Features, 2011)]** September 2011: the son's documentary reframes his father as guilt-ridden over Vietnam and proposes the drowning may have been suicide. **[A: HuffPost]** His brother Jonathan disputes this sharply, calls their father "a straightforward, unrepentant soldier," and notes the body was found without shoes, consistent with kicking to stay afloat. **The disagreement is left open in the journey.**

**[R]** Murder theories persist in conspiracy literature; **no physical evidence of foul play was ever produced.** Recorded as an afterlife phenomenon, not a competing account. **[R]** No statue, named building, or monument, and he is not among the stars on the CIA Memorial Wall, reserved for officers killed in the line of duty: an inference from the wall's criteria plus a failed search. **[A: NSAEBB362]** The CIA's declassified in-house history of the directorship, posted 2011, is the touchstone for reconstructing the tenure.

## Quotations

Seven are carried verbatim in the journey. This is the audit of where each came from.

**Usable:**

1. **[A]** "I cannot agree to the transfer of sensitive material…" DCI statement, **29 September 1975**, on the Pike subpoena. NSArchive Document 29.
2. **[A]** "There are some 'bad secrets' which are properly revealed by an aggressive press…" Letter, **March 1974**, answering the clandestine service chief; NSAEBB362.
3. and 4. **[A, secondhand relay inside a primary document]** "First I had Phoenix, then Watergate, and now this," and "Yes, but I will not do anything illegal or lie in order to do so." Both relayed by Helms to Eagleburger, **memo of 7 January 1975**, FRUS 1969-1976 XXXVIII/2, Document 25.
5. **[A, with citation caveat]** "But I realize that my view of American intelligence… is much different from that of the ordinary citizen, and even from that of the well-informed minority." *Honorable Men* (1978). **The pool quoted this truncated mid-sentence without an ellipsis;** the full sentence is restored in the journey. Seen through Jeff Stein's review (archive.org, nsia-CIAColbyWilliamEHonorableMen), **not against the page image**, and the review says only "in the last few pages," so the circulating "p. 474" cite is unsupported.
6. **[A, same caveat]** "Their harsh opinion, in part, is, of course, the result of those questionable activities that have come to light…" *Honorable Men* (1978).

**Not usable:**

7. **[R, rejected]** "I believe that the figures in mid-1971 that were testified to at the time…" The **figures are genuine** (28,978 captured / 20,587 killed / 17,717 rallied) but **the wording is untraceable to any transcript** and its phrasing is retrospective, so it cannot be 1971 testimony as the pool claimed. The journey substitutes Colby's **1981 interview for *Vietnam: A Television History*** (AAPB, cpb-aacip-15-4746q1sk6s).
8. **[R, misattributed, excluded]** "The Central Intelligence Agency owns everyone of any significance in the major media." **Wikiquote lists it as Misattributed**, tracing its first appearance to David McGowan's *Derailing Democracy* (2000). It appears nowhere in the journey; recorded here so its absence is deliberate.

## Sources

**Reached and read.** Every source cited inline above was opened in this pass. Three carried disproportionate weight: **Encyclopedia.com** (the 1963 Far East date, the 1968 ambassadorial rank, the Donovan firm's 1947 name), **HistoryNet, "Surprise, Kill, Vanish"** (the corrective on Rype's personnel figures and on Grana), and **gjefsjo.no** (the farm's own published coordinates). Also read: the existing atlas files for patrice_lumumba, george_hw_bush, george_w_bush, william_casey, manuel_noriega, augusto_pinochet and adnan_khashoggi, and QUEUE.md.

**Named and not reached (honest gaps)**
- **warhistory.org, "William E. Colby"**: HTTP 403. Sole source for the Fort Benning jump school, the 1943 date, and the Silver Star and foreign decorations; all carried at reduced confidence.
- **"Wikipedia, Elbridge Colby" (the professor-soldier)**: does not exist. See section 1.
- **1971 hearing transcripts, U.S. Assistance Programs in Vietnam**: event confirmed via Wikipedia and Readex, transcript not opened, which is why the pool's quotation could not be salvaged. Likewise ***Honorable Men*** in original page images: both memoir quotations are seen only through a review's reproduction.
- **Colby's CIA personnel file, station records, CORDS reporting**: closed or classified. The years between Stockholm and Saigon are thin for that reason, not because the search was cursory.
- **Coordinates for the Grana, Tangen and Lurudalen sites**: given by no source. The journey carries approximations from narrative distances and flags each in date_confidence.
