# Saint Erasmus of Formia (Elmo): research report

*2026-08-08. Erasmo di Formia; Sant'Ermu; St Elmo. Bishop and martyr, one of the Fourteen Holy Helpers, patron of Gaeta and of sailors, invoked against pains of the belly. Feast 2 June.*

**[A] = attested, source named. [R] = reconstruction, legend, local tradition, or a placeholder coordinate.**

## Method

Two things have to be held apart here, and the sources routinely conflate them.

The first is a **cult**: documented, continuous, geographically fixed, in places still running. A body venerated in a church at Formia by the time of Gregory the Great, moved to Gaeta in 842, rediscovered in 917, installed in a cathedral consecrated in 1106, carried through the streets every second of June, patron of five Italian towns.

The second is a **life**, which is not documented at all. The Catholic Encyclopedia's verdict, carried in the English Wikipedia article, is that there appears to be **no historical basis for the passio**, the surviving narrative conflating the Formia cult figure with a separate Syrian bishop also called Erasmus **[A, Catholic Encyclopedia (1913) via en.wikipedia, "Erasmus of Formia"]**.

Everything in sections 1 to 5 is therefore **[R]** unless tagged otherwise; an **[A]** inside the legendary material attaches to *what a text says*, never to the event narrated.

**Fourteen errors in the supplied research pool are corrected inline**, each flagged where it arises: the CE misattribution and the edict date (1); the Myra coordinate (5); the Gelasius anachronism (6); "Formia Cathedral", three coordinates for one site, the unnamed 842 church and bishop, the 850 patronage, the 1108 consecration (7); Castel Sant'Elmo, the impossible Pigafetta pin, the Camoes-as-da-Gama sighting, the short list of rival names for the fire (8); the Munich skull, Bassiano, Roccagorga (10).

## 1. Origin, and the contested see

**No birth year exists in any source**; the Passio dates nothing. The **250** carried in the journey is arithmetic run backwards from a traditional death of 303, a placeholder hedged in `date_confidence` **[gap]**. **No agreed birthplace either**: en.wikipedia and CatholicSaints.info offer **Mount Lebanon**, the Italian article and the Antioch strand **Antioch in Syria (36.2023, 36.1613)** **[R, both]**.

> **CONTRADICTION ON THE SEE, FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED.** The Latin West makes him **bishop of Formia (41.2564, 13.606)** **[R, en.wikipedia; Golden Legend]**. it.wikipedia calls him outright **vescovo di Antiochia** **[R]**. en.wikipedia's explanation: "the Acts of Saint Elmo were partly compiled from legends that confuse him with a Syrian bishop Erasmus of Antioch" **[A as to the statement]**. The journey carries **both pins**, six hundred miles apart, dated the same year. Neither is preferred.

**A pool error corrected here.** The pool attributed the conflation claim to the **Catholic Encyclopedia**. The en.wiki body does not cite the CE for it; the CE appears only in the article's external-links section. The conflation statement is en.wiki's own **[A, en.wikipedia body text]**. The CE *is* correctly the source for the separate, stronger statement that the passio has no historical basis.

**The imperial frame is real and dated.** On **23 February 303** Diocletian orders the new church at **Nicomedia (40.7654, 29.9408)** razed, the day chosen because it is the Terminalia; the **Edict against the Christians is published the next day, 24 February 303** **[A, en.wikipedia, "Diocletianic Persecution"]**. The pool had the edict on the 23rd; corrected. The pin is **inherited byte-identical** from `saint_acacius.journey.json` and `saint_blaise.journey.json`. **The link to Erasmus is thematic, not documentary: no record places him at Nicomedia** **[gap]**.

## 2. The cave

He flees the persecution and hides seven years, fed by a raven, until an angel tells him to go back and preach **[R, en.wikipedia; CatholicSaints.info]**.

> **CONTRADICTION ON THE PLACE.** it.wikipedia says only "si rifugiò dapprima per sette anni in una caverna": a cave, unnamed, unlocated. The Anglophone summary names **Mount Lebanon** **[R]**. No source names a peak, a valley or a spring. The journey pins **34.2439, 36.0453**, the Qadisha and Cedars of God tract, **explicitly as an illustration and not an attested site** **[R, placeholder]**.

## 3. The trial at Antioch

Arrested and brought before Diocletian at Antioch; refuses to sacrifice; tortured; imprisoned; freed by an angel **[R, en. and it.wikipedia; CatholicSaints.info]**.

> **HISTORICAL STRAIN, FLAGGED.** Diocletian's court sits ordinarily at **Nicomedia** and used Antioch only intermittently, chiefly during the Persian campaigns of the mid-290s. **No administrative record puts the emperor in Syria in 303** **[A as an absence, en.wikipedia "Diocletian"]**. The legend needs an emperor and a bishop in one room and supplies them.

## 4. The torments (what the Golden Legend actually says)

The English text used throughout is **Caxton's translation of Jacobus de Voragine, as printed by Wynkyn de Worde in 1527**, reproduced at catholicsaints.info **[A as text]**. Thirteen verbatim passages were recovered; the journey carries ten at the corresponding stops.

The sequence: **leaden mallets** ("beaten so cruelly with leaden malles, that all his veins must break and burst"); a **pit of snakes, adders and paddocks**; a **pan seething with rosin, pitch, brimstone lead and oil**, poured into his mouth; a **burning metal coat**; **teeth drawn with iron pincers**; **skin carded with iron cards**; **roasting**, during which he says he lies on a bed strewn with sweet roses; and finally the **belly opened and the bowels wound out** **[all A as text, all R as event]**.

Two observations. **The instruments circulate**: iron carding-combs are Blaise of Sebastea's fixed emblem, and Blaise's own Passio uses them on him in the same generation of legends **[A, saint_blaise.report.md; en.wikipedia "Saint Blaise"]**. The emblem that sticks to a saint is the one his town carved first, not the one his text used exclusively. And **the barrel of spikes is not a Roman penalty**: no such execution is attested in Roman legal or narrative sources reachable here **[R; gap, no positive attestation sought or found]**.

## 5. Lycia, and the two deaths

Freed, he goes to **Lycia**, raises a citizen's dead son, and the witnesses go to baptism in a mass; Maximian hears of it and orders him retaken **[R, en.wikipedia]**. **No Lycian town is named in any source.** The journey pins **Myra, 36.2592, 29.9853** as an illustration only. *Pool coordinate corrected*: its 36.2508, 29.9744 is neither Myra nor Demre town centre (36.2440, 29.9853). Also flagged: **Myra becomes capital of Lycia only in 325** **[A, en.wikipedia]**.

> **THE CENTRAL CONTRADICTION OF THE FILE, FLAGGED, NOT RESOLVED.**
> **Version A (en.wikipedia).** Recaptured, taken to **Illyricum**, disembowelled, intestines wound on a windlass, c. 303. No town named; the journey pins **Salona, 43.5417, 16.4897** as a stand-in **[R, placeholder]**.
> **Version B (it.wikipedia, and the local cult).** The **archangel Michael** brings him to **Formia**, where he dies seven days later. **There is no windlass in this account at all.**
> Neither source yields. Both are pinned, on the same date, six hundred miles apart.

> **AND A THIRD POSITION, WHICH IS NEITHER.** it.wikipedia proposes on its own account that the disembowelment was never an event: that the windlass is a **ship's winch (argano)** from the paintings, an emblem of his adopted patronage of sailors, misread by later viewers as an instrument of death. The source offers this **as its own hypothesis, not as settled fact** **[R]**. If it is right, the most famous thing about Erasmus is a caption error.

## 6. The text

The **Passio** dates to the **sixth century** ("La Passio che narra la vita di sant'Erasmo risale al VI secolo") **[A as to the dating, it.wikipedia]**; author unknown **[gap]**.

**Pool error corrected.** The pool said no Pope Gelasius reigned in the period the text claims. That is not the problem. it.wikipedia says the passio "fu attribuita a papa **Gelasio II**", who reigned **1118 to 1119**, six centuries *after* the text; **Gelasius I** (492 to 496) sits adjacent to the sixth century and would have been possible. The defect is a **medieval misattribution to the wrong Gelasius** **[A, it.wikipedia]**.

The **Acts of Saint Elmo** are the vehicle of the Antioch conflation **[A as to the statement, en.wikipedia]**. **Do not attribute the conflation to the Golden Legend**: en.wiki credits de Voragine only with making him "a bishop at Formia over all the Italian Campania, as a hermit on Mount Lebanon, and a martyr in the Diocletianic Persecution." The pool merged the two.

## 7. The relics: Formia to Gaeta

- **c. 590.** **Gregory the Great** records the body kept **at a church in Formia**: the earliest hard anchor in the dossier **[A]**. *Pool error:* it called this "Formia Cathedral". **The see of Formiae was extinguished and its rights carried to Gaeta; no cathedral of Formia survives.** it.wikipedia says "la chiesa di Formia". What stands is the **chiesa di Sant'Erasmo Vescovo e Martire** in **Castellone** (**41.259, 13.6157**, district centroid; **no excavated plan located** **[gap]**).
- **c. 600.** Feast of **2 June** in the **Hieronymian Martyrology** **[A, it.wikipedia]**.
- **842.** **Saracen raiders destroy old Formiae** **[A]**. **Bishop John III brings the relics to Gaeta** into the **pre-existing church of Santa Maria del Parco** **[A, en.wikipedia, "Gaeta Cathedral"]**; the pool named neither church nor bishop. The journey **inherits the Gaeta pin 41.2136, 13.5717 byte-identical from `catorce_santos_ayudantes.journey.json`**, which narrates the same transfer from the company's side.
- **917.** **Bishop Bono** rediscovers the hidden relics **[A, it.wikipedia]**. *Pool error:* it dated the patronage of Gaeta to **850**; it.wikipedia places the proclamation **after** the inventio ("furono ritrovate nel 917 dal vescovo Bono. Sant'Erasmo fu quindi proclamato patrono della città").
- **22 January 1106.** **Paschal II consecrates Gaeta Cathedral**, to Saints Erasmus and Marcian with Saint Mary of the Assumption **[A, en.wikipedia]**. *Two pool errors:* the date given as 1108 and called vague across sources, against the pool's own chronology lens which said 1106; and a coordinate 1.3 km west of the building. **Correct: 41.208944, 13.586806**, rounded to 41.2089, 13.5868.

> **THE STRUCTURAL GAP.** **No original tomb of Erasmus has ever been excavated, opened or archaeologically described, at Formia or anywhere else** **[gap; an absence across all sources consulted]**. The relic history begins with a translation, not a grave: the same evidentiary shape the atlas already recorded for Blaise.

## 8. Elmo: the name, the fire, the fort

- **c. 950.** A tenth-century church of **Sant'Erasmo** on the **Vomero** above Naples (**40.8438, 14.239**). **Erasmo to Ermo to Elmo** by ordinary contraction **[A]**.
- **Castel Sant'Elmo (40.843815, 14.239005).** *Pool error:* it credited **Charles of Anjou** with converting the site into a fortress. en.wiki documents only "a structure at the site from 1275" under **Charles I**. The **fortress is commissioned by Robert of Anjou from 1329**, built by **Tino da Camaino** to his death in **1343**, called a *castrum* by **1336**; the **"castrum Sancti Erasmi" attestation of 1348** is correct **[A]**.
- **c. 1300 onward, the fire.** **St Elmo's fire**, the corona discharge at mastheads in storms, read as a sign the danger is passing **[A, en.wikipedia]**. **The naming was never universal**: Welsh *canwyll yr ysbryd (glân)*, glossed both **candles of the Holy Ghost** and **candles of St David**; Russian usage giving it to **St Nicholas** or calling it **St Peter's lights**; also **St Helen's fire** and **St Hermes' fire**. The pool gave only the Holy Ghost gloss. The journey pins a **nominal point, 36, 18**.
- **1519 to 1522, Pigafetta.** en.wiki says only that mention of the fire "can be found in Antonio Pigafetta's journal of his 1519 to 1522 voyage with Ferdinand Magellan." **No year within the voyage and no position.** The pool asserted April 1520 at **-52.6, -68.5**, the Atlantic mouth of the Strait of Magellan, **which the fleet did not enter until 21 October 1520**: an internally impossible pin. Moved to **Puerto San Julián (approx. -49.3, -67.72)** **[R, placeholder]**.
- **1552, Fort St Elmo, Valletta (35.8994, 14.5188)**, named Sant'Ermu **[A]**.
- **1572, Os Lusíadas.** *Misdated by 75 years in the pool*, which logged a sighting on **da Gama's 1497 voyage**. en.wiki: the fire "is also described in **The Lusiads (1572)**, the epic account of the voyages of discovery of Vasco da Gama." That is **Camões's poem**, not a log. Re-dated and reworded as literary attestation; pinned at Lisbon **[A]**.
- **1832, HMS Beagle.** Darwin records the phenomenon **[A]**. **No position is given**; the journey pins the Río de la Plata and says so **[R, placeholder]**.

## 9. The company of fourteen

Erasmus is one of the **Fourteen Holy Helpers**, assembled in the Rhineland and Franconia under the Black Death **[A, en.wikipedia]**. The following pins are **inherited byte-identical from `catorce_santos_ayudantes.journey.json`**:

Regensburg 49.01806, 12.09083 (1320 to 1360); Nuremberg 49.4534, 11.0775 (c. 1400); Vierzehnheiligen 50.1157, 11.0541 (1445 to 1448); Rome 41.9022, 12.4534 (1450 and 1969); Corvey 51.77885, 9.40943 (836); Buenos Aires -34.6037, -58.3816 (1900).

Two do real work. **Corvey, 836**: Vitus's relics go into German ground by deliberate policy **six years before** Erasmus is carried to Gaeta in flight, the same ninth-century wave of *translatio* on the opposite motive **[A, catorce file]**. **Rome, 1969**: the reform drops **Barbara, Catherine of Alexandria, Christopher and Margaret of Antioch** for unverifiable historicity and **leaves Erasmus**, whose passio the Catholic Encyclopedia had called baseless in 1913. **No document consulted explains the distinction** **[gap]**.

**The belly patronage.** Erasmus is invoked against **colic, abdominal and intestinal ailments, labour pains, and cattle pests** **[A, en.wikipedia]**. The sources read this as arising **from the disembowelment legend**, with **no healing episode in his life to support it** **[R as to the causal link, given by the sources as inference]**. A **1908 devotional source quoted in the catorce file** names him with **George, Pantaleon and Vitus** for animals struck by plague **[A, Hammer 1908]**.

## 10. Later cult, art, and the towns

- **1527, Wynkyn de Worde**, English Golden Legend, putting the legend into vernacular print **[A]**.
- **1628 to 1629, Nicolas Poussin, "The Martyrdom of Saint Erasmus"**, altarpiece for the north transept of **St Peter's (41.9022, 12.4539)**; a mosaic copy now in place, canvas in the Vatican Pinacoteca **[A]**.
- **The skull at Munich (48.1367, 11.5759).** *Pool overstatement.* en.wiki hedges: the skull "is **purported** to be in St. Peter's Church, Munich." The Peterskirche article says only that "relics of Munditia and Erasmus of Formia are venerated in the church". **Tag downgraded from [A] to [R].** Further relics are said dispersed across Europe and **no source names the churches** **[gap]**.
- **Patronal towns.** **Gaeta**, **Formia**, **Bassiano** (Latina), **Santeramo in Colle** (Puglia), **Roccagorga** **[A, en.wikipedia]**. *Pool errors:* **Bassiano about 8 km wrong** (correct approx. **41.550, 13.033**), **Roccagorga omitted**. Santeramo is 1.1 km off, acceptable.
- **Iconography.** Vested bishop with the **windlass**, sometimes misread as a ship's capstan **[A as convention, en.wikipedia]**, which it.wikipedia argues is the original meaning (section 5).
- **1969 and after.** Feast fixed at **2 June** in the Roman Martyrology; observance continues at Gaeta and Formia, with an annual procession of the relics **[A for the feast; R for the procession, whose start date no source gives]**.

## 11. Gaps, stated as gaps

- **No birth year and no agreed birthplace.** The 250 in the journey is a placeholder.
- **No agreed see** (Formia and Antioch both stand) and **no agreed place of death** (Illyricum and Formia both stand).
- **No named town** for the Lebanon cave, the Lycian miracle, the Lycian hillside, or the Illyrian martyrdom: four placeholder pins, each labelled.
- **No excavated grave**, anywhere, ever. **No author and no manuscript history** for the sixth-century Passio.
- **No founding year** for the Gaeta 2 June procession, and **no date** for the arrival of the purported skull at Munich.
- **No explanation** for why the 1969 reform kept Erasmus and dropped four of his company.
- **No Islamic-world stop.** The interlock lens flagged the canonical Kaaba pin (21.4225, 39.8262) as available high-traffic geography but found **no Erasmus connection in the corpus**. **Not inherited**: an unearned pin is worse than a missing one.
