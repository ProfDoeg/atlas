# Carlos María della Paolera (1890-1960): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-21 for `carlos_maria_della_paolera.journey.json`. 43 stops, 8 segments.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in the same sentence · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a dossier claim no independently fetched source corroborated. Contradictions are flagged where they occur and are **not** adjudicated. Where the web-crawl dossier was wrong on a fact or a coordinate, the correction is stated with its source; where it was right and a previous reader under-rated it, the tag is lifted and that is said too. Nine coordinate corrections were applied, all from OpenStreetMap / Nominatim, each stated in full in the relevant stop's `date_confidence` rather than repeated here.

Argentine popular journalism calls him the creator of the Avenida 9 de Julio. He was not that. He was the man who imported *urbanismo* into Argentina as a discipline, staffed it, gave it a standing office inside a municipality, and then gave the international profession a symbol and a date on the calendar.

## 1. Origins and formation, Buenos Aires 1890-1920

- Born **7 September 1890, Buenos Aires** **[A: es.wikipedia; Infobae, 16 Nov 2022]**, to **Cayetano della Paolera**, a builder, and **Ángela Buschiazzo**, kin of the architect **Juan Antonio Buschiazzo** **[A: Infobae]**. No street address is in any source reached, so the pin is the canonical Buenos Aires point.
- Attended the **Colegio Nacional de Buenos Aires** **[A: Infobae]**, which the dossier held as unverified tradition; the tag is **lifted**. No year is given anywhere, so the stop's 1903 is an ordering device with nothing behind it.
- **Graduated civil engineer, UBA, 1912** **[A: Infobae, "se graduó de ingeniero civil en la UBA en 1912"]**. The thesis title *Proyecto de provisión de agua para una ciudad de 100.000 habitantes* and the date 19 November 1912 are **[R]**. **Contradiction, flagged:** es.wikipedia states he *began* studies in 1912, which cannot stand beside graduating in 1912, so studies must have begun c. 1907-08.
- **1920, delegate of the Centro Argentino de Ingenieros to the Congreso de la Habitación** (Museo Social Argentino) **[R]**, dossier-only.

## 2. Paris, 1921-1928

- **Institut d'Urbanisme de l'Université de Paris, 1921-1928, under Marcel Poëte** **[A: journals.openedition.org/abe/10839]**. Hedge kept: some institutional biographies place the decisive move in **1924**, and only the span is independently confirmed.
- **Diploma thesis** *Contribution à l'étude d'un plan d'aménagement, d'embellissement et d'extension de Buenos Aires*, 1928 **[A: redalyc 125162706005; openedition abe/10839]**. The defence date of **31 March 1928** is **[R]**, and "first Latin American to take the institute's diploma" is **[R]**, circulating in institutional biographies with no register consulted.
- ***La Razón* articles written from Paris, 1925-1927** **[R for the series, A for one article]**. The verified item is **14 April 1927**, quoted with full citation in **Alicia Novick, "Planes versus proyectos", *Revista de Urbanismo* N°3 (2000), p.15**, a PDF read directly. It carries the distinction the career rests on, *plano* against *plan regulador*, and is one of only two quotations in the file from his own hand.
- **1924 Amsterdam congress** **[R]** and **1928 Union Internationale des Villes, Brussels** **[R]**: dossier-only; both venues are landmark guesses, labelled so in the stops. **Marriage to Marie Thérèse Rouault** and **son born Paris 25 March 1926** **[R]**: user-contributed genealogies, no certificate of either.
- **Canonical pin inherited byte-identically.** All Paris stops use **48.8566, 2.3522**, the atlas's standing central-Paris point (`le_corbusier`, `jean_claude_nicolas_forestier`, `juan_antonio_buschiazzo`). The pool's IUUP pair (48.8489, 2.3436) was deliberately not used.

## 3. Return, and Rosario, 1928-1935

- **Ten UBA lectures, *La evolución de Buenos Aires y bosquejo de un Plan Regulador*, 1928** **[R]**, venue unverified; what is attested is that **from 1928 he advocated comprehensive metropolitan planning for Buenos Aires** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The **Rosario lectures at Ángel Guido's invitation**, printed as *Dos conferencias sobre urbanismo*, are **[R]**, only a professional relationship with Guido by 1929 being confirmed (Scielo).
- **First university chair of urbanism in Argentina, Universidad Nacional del Litoral at Rosario, 1929** **[A: vitruvius arquitextos 08.087/216; iaa.fadu.uba.ar article 264]**. The dossier's "31 December" is **dropped**.
- **Ordinance No. 58 (1929) commissions the Plan Regulador y de Extensión with Guido and Adolfo P. Farengo; approved 1935; regional scope on a 20 km radius from Plaza 25 de Mayo** **[A: scielo.org.ar S2314-15492016000100003]**. The local objection that the appointment bypassed open competition is a professional grievance in the same tradition, not a legal finding.
- **Quotations from the 1935 *Memoria descriptiva y justificativa*** **[A: quoted in Galimberti (2016)]**. The memoria is **coauthored** with Farengo and Guido and the `quote_source` says so; of three verified lines one is quoted, the others paraphrased rather than given as his sole authorship.
- **Mar del Plata lectures, 1931-1932** **[R]**, venue unconfirmed, and nothing shows the municipality adopting a plan from him.
- **Werner Hegemann's 1931 tour: the dossier's single claim is split in two.** Hegemann's South American tour and his Mar del Plata appearance are **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Werner Hegemann']**; della Paolera's chairing of the hosting committee, the Buenos Aires and Rosario legs, and his prologue to *Problemas urbanos de Rosario* are **[R]**.

## 4. The municipal office and the chairs, 1932-1939

- **Head of the Dirección/Oficina Técnica del Plan de Urbanización, Buenos Aires, 1932, after pressure from a socialist group on the city council** **[A: openedition abe/10839, corroborated by Infobae]**; the ordinance date of 5 February is **[R]**. **Contradiction, flagged:** the tenure ends **1939** (es.wikipedia) or **1943** (Novick/Piccioni via the dossier), neither preferable on what was gathered, and the campa states both without choosing.
- **Chair of urbanism at UBA, 1933** **[A: vitruvius 08.087/216]**; the dossier's "25 May" is dropped. **Anachronism corrected:** the dossier placed this at a "UBA School of Architecture", but UBA had **no** architecture faculty in 1933, architecture sitting inside the **Facultad de Ciencias Exactas, Físicas y Naturales** until the Facultad de Arquitectura y Urbanismo was created in **1948**.
- **Manifiesto del Símbolo del Urbanismo, November 1934** **[A as to existence and date; the wording's chain is single-rooted]**. The famous line was **not invented**, contrary to the previous pass's suspicion, but its sourcing is thin: reproduced at `surp-aqp.blogspot.com` (2013) as *"La ciudad como el árbol no puede desligarse de la tierra que la sustenta"*, and cited in **João Soares Pena, *Arquitextos* 186.08 (2015)**, whose own note points back to that same page, so the academic citation is **not** independent corroboration. The circulating repunctuation *"La ciudad, como el árbol…"* is a variant and is not used. **No archival or scholarly edition of the manifesto was locatable.**
- **The symbol adopted at the 1935 Congress of Urbanism at Besançon, where 8 November was fixed as the day of urbanism** **[A: vitruvius 08.087/216]**. The dossier's [R] is **lifted**. Whether he travelled to Besançon in person is still unestablished, and that caveat is kept in the stop.
- Three items of 1939, all **[R]**: the **Instituto Argentino de Urbanismo** (founding venue not located) with the 1942 volume coauthored with Miguel Roca; the **Avenida San Martín at Junín** with the engineer Rodolfo Henry (es.wikipedia lists the project, the ramblas description is dossier-only); and *Tráfego, espaços livres e altura dos edificios*, **Rio de Janeiro**, a bibliography listing whose personal travel is not established.

## 5. The Avenida 9 de Julio: where the legend and the record part

- **The corridor long predates him** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Avenida 9 de Julio']**: Intendente **Francisco Seeber**'s proposal of 1889-1890, a route project by the architect **Buschiazzo** approved in **1895**, **National Law 8,855 of 1912** authorising expropriation for a **33-metre** artery, and the 1915 crisis that stalled it.
- **CORRECTION.** "First to propose expropriating a series of city blocks" is **overstated**: expropriation was authorised in 1912. What he was first to propose, in **1933**, was taking the blocks **entire**, **110 m** wide with no remnant buildings, as an *avenida parque* **[A: es.wikipedia]**. The **140 m** figure belongs to the final scheme (Infobae); the dossier ran the two together.
- **CORRECTION.** His plan did not "require" clearing the **church of San Nicolás de Bari**: that came down in **1931** for the Diagonal Norte **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Iglesia de San Nicolás de Bari']**, and the Obelisco rose on its ground in 1936. The other demolitions Infobae names (Frank Brown's circus, the Teatro del Pueblo, the Vieytes soap works) stand, and the count is **138 properties across 33 manzanas**, not 13 blocks.
- **CORRECTION.** The **1934 variant** proposed **one** government-buildings centre, at the Avenida de Mayo crossing **[A: es.wikipedia]**, not centres plural, and its "rapid-transit streets" are not in the source. Infobae records him later **rejecting** ministries on the avenue in favour of decentralisation and calling the 60 m Social Development block a *"rascacielos"* **[A]**. The 1934 node is a position he abandoned.
- **He opposed the Obelisco**, having designed a **fountain** for Plaza de la República **[A: Infobae]**, and the demolition rubble was sold at public auction **[A]**.
- **Chronology** **[A: es.wikipedia]**: demolitions **20 April 1937**; expropriation proceedings **8 May 1937**, after demolition began; construction from **1 July 1937** under **Federico Zamboni**; **first section inaugurated by President Agustín P. Justo, 12 October 1937**, **500 m between Bartolomé Mitre and Viamonte**; Mitre to Belgrano completed 1944-1947.
- **CORRECTIONS.** The dossier's "Cerrito to Carlos Pellegrini" for the 1937 opening is impossible, those being the **lateral roadways flanking** the avenue. Its "roughly forty days of clearing" is in **no located source**, the circulating 31-day figure belonging to the Obelisco's 1936 construction.
- **The legend is itself a documented fact about his afterlife** **[A: Infobae 2022]**: journalism still calls him the avenue's creator while reporting that he lost the argument over its monument. The file carries it as afterlife, never as biography.

## 6. Provinces, region, institute, 1941-1956

- Four **[R]** items, none independently reconfirmed: **Mendoza**, a 1941 proposal and the 1943 *Reorganización del sistema ferroviario* with Farengo, personal travel not established; **La Plata, Gran Centro Cívico, 1944**; the **Bañado de Flores** survey and Gran Parque del Sur, 1946; and *L'Aéroport National du Grand Buenos Aires*, c. 1950, whose relation to the actual Ezeiza airport is **not** established. All are pinned as subject-of-study locations, not documented presences, and say so.
- **Instituto Superior de Urbanismo created 11 March 1948; Della Paolera named Director 24 April 1948** **[A: isu.fadu.uba.ar/?p=29, fetched directly]**. **Gaston Bardet**, Poëte's son-in-law, gave the inaugural lectures **[A: iaa.fadu.uba.ar article 264]**.
- **World Town Planning Day proposed 1949, now kept in more than 30 countries** **[A: iaa.fadu.uba.ar article 264; es.wikipedia]**. **Contradiction, flagged:** the first source dates the **first observance to 1950**, the dossier claims **1949**, and es.wikipedia credits him as creator without separating the 1934 symbol from the observance. The ISOCARP and RTPI pages cited in the dossier could not be re-fetched.
- **Directorship ends 28 September 1956, succeeded by Arq. Sergio Fernández Pico** **[A: isu.fadu.uba.ar/?p=29]**. The previous pass marked this dossier-only; it is **on the page the dossier cites**, and the tag is lifted. Nothing there frames it as punitive and **nothing explains it**. That Argentina was a year past the 1955 coup is context, not evidence.

## 7. Death and afterlife

- **Died 15 September 1960, Buenos Aires, aged 70** **[A: es.wikipedia; Infobae has only the year]**; the dossier under-rated this, since the full date **is** sourced. **Cause, hospital, funeral and burial: genuinely unknown**, stated as a gap and not as a finding of "no burial".
- **Papers deposited with the Sociedad Central de Arquitectos and the Museo de la Ciudad** **[R]**, undated. ***Buenos Aires y sus problemas urbanos* (1977)** **[A as an edition: Open Library]**, the only collected edition of his writing that surfaced; Randle's editorship and the Oikos imprint are **[R]**, from the dossier's bibliography.
- **CEDODAL monograph, ed. Ramón Gutiérrez**, driven by his grandson **Gerardo della Paolera**, who never met him **[R for the motive, A for the quotations]**. **DATE CORRECTION:** the Télam wire story carrying the quotations is dated **7 November 2022**, not 2021; the book's 2021 imprint is a separate fact and must not be attached to the story. A second verified line, unused, prints *equilibro* for *equilibrio* and needs a [sic] if ever quoted.
- **Commemorative names**, the most genuinely new material in this pass, all from direct OSM query. **Calle Carlos M. Della Paolera** (way 83432713) and **Plazoleta Carlos María Della Paolera** (way 114352739), both in Retiro, neither in the dossier **[A]**. **CORRECTION:** es.wikipedia's unsourced street in **Tijuca, Rio de Janeiro** is **wrong**, a Brazil-wide query returning nothing in Rio; the Brazilian street is **Rua Carlos Maria Della Paolera, Jardim da Saúde, Cursino, São Paulo**. **Mar del Plata is understated everywhere:** an **Avenida Ingeniero Della Paolera** through several barrios and a **Plazoleta Ing. Carlos María Della Paolera (del Urbanismo)** in La Perla. **No naming date** was established for any of them; the stops use 1980 as an ordering device and say so.
- **Institute renamed by Resolución (CD) 579 of 16 December 2003** **[A: isu.fadu.uba.ar/?p=29]**. **CORRECTION:** dormancy ended with **Resolución 321 (CD) of 7 December 1993** appointing Arq. **Juan Manuel Borthagaray**, so **1993** and not 1994, and the page gives **no start year**, so the dossier's "1980-1994" is not asserted.
- Even the standard portrait is posthumous, taken from Diego Abad de Santillán, *Historia Argentina* (TEA, 1971) **[A: Wikimedia Commons]**.

## Claims in the pool NOT staged as stops

1. **Ciudad Jardín Lomas del Palomar.** The dossier's 1930s attribution is **wrong on date and role**: founded by **Erich Zeyen**, land and planning **1943**, and his documented connection is that in 1943 he "reconoció a esta urbanización como la primera en su tipo en Sud América", an **endorsement, not authorship** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. Not staged, because **no verified coordinate was in the pool**. It needs a geocoder pass on El Palomar, Tres de Febrero, and should be written as a 1943 endorsement.
2. **The 1977 Randle anthology**, folded into the papers stop: a book, not a place he went. **Café de las Ciudades**, "Infraestructura ecológica…" (c. 2020-21) **[R]**: see the next section.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Per `EXCEPTIONS.md`, relations not rooted in the subject's own life are flagged rather than silently deleted. **Della Paolera is not on that list.** One relation was caught.

- **Direction: FORWARD.** The *Café de las Ciudades* article of c. 2020-21 takes his 1934 symbol and its line about the city and the tree and applies them to twenty-first-century "ecological infrastructure" for the Buenos Aires metropolitan region, sixty years after his death; the family makes the same move at the 2022 presentation, framing him as an early ecologist. Forward reach is never licensed by an agent's own judgement, so **no stop applies his framework to anything after 1960**. The material is kept one level down, recorded here and available as `sources`/`suggested_refs` on his own 1934 manifesto stop; making it a stop would require adding him to the forward-grants table.
- **No backward apparatus relation was found.** He is a practising engineer, not a theorist with a portable framework, and nothing in the pool applies his method to events he never treated.
- **One stop-ownership call.** The 8 November 2022 CEDODAL presentation at the Salón Prisma is **the grandson's event at a place della Paolera never went**, so it is not staged as a scene. The book, the presentation and the quotation ride instead on his **own** stop for his **own** papers.

## Interlocks named in campas

Named only where he stands in a real relation to the person, never for shared geography.

- `juan_antonio_buschiazzo` **[A]**: **family**, his mother's kinsman (Infobae), and author of the **1895** route project for the corridor he reconceived in 1933 (es.wikipedia).
- `jean_claude_nicolas_forestier` **[A/R]**: adviser to the **1925 Noel-commission plan** that della Paolera attacked as a mere *plano*, the target of the 1927 *La Razón* article. Forestier's role and the article are [A]; della Paolera naming him personally is [R].
- `joseph_bouvard` **[A/R]**: the widened north-south avenue of Bouvard's **1909 Nuevo Plano** is documented prehistory of the corridor [A], which his own historical method obliged him to study; direct engagement with Bouvard's drawings is [R].
- `le_corbusier` **[R]**: della Paolera **explicitly criticised** Le Corbusier's clear-the-ground proposals (dossier, no dated text located). Named once, in the 1933 chair stop. No meeting is attested and none is implied.

**`carlos_thays` was considered and deliberately NOT named:** the pool establishes only that the dossier lists him among figures preceding della Paolera's claim to be "father of Argentine urbanism", which is framing, not attested engagement. **Poëte**, **Bardet**, **Guido**, **Farengo**, **Hegemann** and **Roca** are named as real teachers and collaborators but have no journey files; **Poëte and Hegemann are the clearest research gaps** in this cluster.

## Honest gaps

- **No burial**: no cemetery, tomb, funeral notice or cause of death, anywhere. **No manifesto**: the 1934 text survives, in what was reachable, only through a 2013 blog reproduction on a single-rooted chain. **No documents of the private life**: street of birth, school register, marriage certificate, birth record of the son, none located.
- **Two contradictions left standing:** the end of the municipal tenure (1939 vs 1943) and the first observance of World Town Planning Day (1949 vs 1950).
- **No naming date** for any street or square carrying his name, in three cities and two countries; **no explanation** of why the ISU directorship ended in September 1956; **no confirmed address** for the Institut d'Urbanisme in the 1920s, which is why the canonical Paris pin is used rather than a guessed building.
