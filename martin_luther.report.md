# Martin Luther (1483-1546): research report

*Atlas of Journeys, slug `martin_luther`. Register: national mythology, the canon is true. Calendar: Julian for the life, the Gregorian reform falling thirty-six years after his death; Gregorian for the single afterlife stop of 1858.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-witness recollection. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is printed and left open.

## Method and its limits

Built from a verified research pool (chronology, geography, quotes, interlock, afterlife lenses), corrected against German and English Wikipedia coordinate data and against Gutenberg texts of Luther's printed works.

The corrections are not cosmetic. **The geography pool arrived with systematic coordinate error**, up to 4.7 km in one case and 620 m in the load-bearing case, the Castle Church, which would have dropped the Ninety-Five Theses pin in the old town at the parish church rather than at the castle. Twenty-one pins are replaced.

Hedging on dates lives in the journey's `date_confidence` field, not in campa prose; this report is where the hedges are argued. **No primary manuscript was consulted.** The evidentiary floor is Wikipedia, Bainton and Brecht as relayed through the pool, and public-domain Gutenberg editions, which is a real ceiling on this file's authority.

## I. The copper country, 1483-1501

**[A: Wikipedia, Martin Luther; Brecht]** Born 10 November 1483 at Eisleben in the county of Mansfeld to Hans and Margarethe Luder, a peasant's son gone into copper and his wife. **[A: Bainton; Eisleben parish tradition]** Baptised 11 November, feast of St. Martin of Tours, which supplies the name. The family moves to Mansfeld in 1484, where Hans leases smelting furnaces, rises to *Hüttenmeister*, and becomes one of four elected town representatives. **[A: Bainton]** 1497, a year at Magdeburg with the Brethren of the Common Life. 1498, Eisenach; boards with the Cotta family, sings as a chorister at St. Georgenkirche.

**[R: Luther's *Tischreden*]** The beatings at the Mansfeld school, fifteen in one morning, and the wasted mendicant prince of Anhalt seen at Magdeburg, are Luther's own recollections given decades later: reported speech, no contemporary record. Stated in campa as the canon states them, per register; tagged here.

## II. Erfurt: the law, the storm, the cloister, 1501-1508

**[A: Brecht]** Matriculates 1501; baccalaureate 1502; master of arts 1505, second of seventeen. Begins law at his father's direction.

**[R: Luther's later account; Bainton]** 2 July 1505, the thunderstorm near Stotternheim and the vow to St. Anne: a single-witness recollection and nothing else. The pool tagged this [R] in the chronology lens and [A] in the geography lens; resolved toward caution, [R]. Documented is only the consequence: law books sold, cloister entered fifteen days later.

**[A: Brecht]** 17 July 1505, enters the Observant Augustinian friary at Erfurt. **[R: Wikipedia]** Ordained deacon 27 February 1507; tagged [R] in the pool and left there. **[A, day contested]** Ordination to the priesthood: de.wikipedia gives 4 April 1507, en.wikipedia "3-4 April". The journey uses 1507-04-03, the split recorded in `date_confidence`. Not adjudicated.

**[A: de.wikipedia; correction applied]** The pool merged the ordination with the first Mass, both lenses putting them in Erfurt Cathedral on 3 April 1507. The ordination was in the cathedral in early April; the first Mass (*Primiz*) was 2 May in the Augustinian monastery church, and it is at that feast that Hans Luder attended and made his remark about honouring father and mother. Split into two stops.

## III. Wittenberg and Rome, 1508-1517

**[A: Brecht]** 1508, a first Wittenberg stint on Aristotle's *Nicomachean Ethics* at the Leucorea, then recalled to Erfurt. 1510-1511, Rome on the order's business in the dispute over union of Observant and Conventual houses; the shock at Roman clerical irreverence and the demolition and rebuilding of St. Peter's are attested. **[R: Bainton, noting the story rests on later Table Talk]** The Scala Sancta climb on the knees and the doubt at the top: no contemporary record. Kept as an [R] stop because it is load-bearing for the canon and the register requires the canon be told; the tag carries the warning.

**[A: de.wikipedia]** September 1511, permanent transfer to Wittenberg and the Augustinian cloister. **[A: Brecht]** 19 October 1512, doctorate; succeeds Staupitz in the chair of biblical theology, held until death. **[A: Wikipedia]** 1515, provincial vicar for Saxony and Thuringia, eleven houses; folded into surrounding campas, not pinned.

## IV. The indulgence quarrel, 1517-1521

**[A: Wikipedia]** 31 October 1517, the letter to Archbishop Albert of Mainz enclosing the ninety-five propositions.

**[R: Iserloh (1961) against traditionalist readings of Melanchthon and Rörer]** The door-posting is disputed and left disputed. The nailing appears in Melanchthon's account written after Luther's death and in Georg Rörer's marginal note, and is absent from Luther's own 1517 correspondence, which describes only sending the theses to the archbishop. The journey states both without resolving. The scene is load-bearing for Protestant national mythology whatever its evidentiary status, which is the register this entry is written in.

**[A: Wikipedia]** January 1518, the Latin theses reprinted in several German cities, a German translation circulating: attested but placeless, folded into the Wittenberg campa. **[A: Brecht]** 26 April 1518, Heidelberg Disputation; Bucer, Brenz and Schnepf among the hearers.

**[A: Bainton; correction applied]** October 1518, three days before Cajetan at Augsburg, then the night flight. The pool's label read "Augsburg, St. Anna's Church (Fuggerei)", conflating two sites a kilometre apart: Luther lodged at the Carmelite house of St. Anna, Cajetan examined him at the Fugger townhouse on the Maximilianstrasse, and the Fuggerei is an almshouse quarter on the Jakoberstrasse. **[A: Wikipedia]** January 1519, the Altenburg meeting with Karl von Miltitz: no stop, no verified coordinate in the pool, and inventing one would defeat the correction pass.

**[A: Wikipedia, Leipzig Debate]** June to July 1519, the Pleissenburg: Eck against Karlstadt first, then Luther from 4 July, who concedes that some propositions condemned at Constance were Christian and evangelical. **[A: Wikipedia]** 15 June 1520, *Exsurge Domine*; 10 December, the burning outside the Elster Gate; 3 January 1521, *Decet Romanum Pontificem*.

## V. Worms, April to May 1521

**[A: Wikipedia, Diet of Worms]** Arrival 16 April; 17 April, the table of books and the request for a day; 18 April, the refusal; Edict signed 25 May, backdated to 8 May.

**[R, flagged hard]** "Here I stand, I can do no other" is not in the primary record. The pool supplied a garbled form with the clauses inverted ("Ich kann nicht anders, hier stehe ich..."), which is not even the traditional wording; that is "Hier stehe ich, ich kann nicht anders. Gott helfe mir. Amen." More important than word order: only the closing "Gott helfe mir. Amen." appears in the documented reply, and Wikipedia's own article on the phrase hedges with "according to tradition". The journey quotes only the documented ending and says in `quote_source` that the famous clause is traditional: a deliberate departure from the canon in a register that otherwise honours it, made because the pool's own verification note recommends exactly that.

*Verification limit, named:* the earliest transcript has not been compared with the first printed edition, here or in the assembling session, whose search budget ran out at 200 of 200. The status of the phrase is open, not settled against tradition.

## VI. The Wartburg and the return, 1521-1522

**[A: Wikipedia; Luther Bible]** 4 May 1521, the staged seizure near Altenstein arranged by Frederick the Wise; May 1521 to March 1522, the Wartburg, the alias Junker Jörg, the New Testament out of Greek into German in eleven weeks of draft. **[R: Wartburg-Stiftung visitor tradition; nineteenth-century travel accounts]** The inkwell thrown at the devil: no source from 1521 or 1522 records it, and the stain shown today has been renewed repeatedly because visitors chipped the plaster away as souvenirs. Kept as an explicitly [R] stop.

**[A: Kessler's *Sabbata*, cited in Bainton]** 3 March 1522, the Black Bear at Jena, the two Swiss students, the Hebrew psalter: one of very few hours of this life recorded by strangers. **[A: de.wikipedia]** 9 to 16 March, eight Invocavit sermons in the Stadtkirche against Karlstadt's pace and against iconoclasm. **[A: Wikipedia, Luther Bible]** September, the September Testament printed by Melchior Lotter with Cranach woodcuts.

## VII. The household, 1523-1534

**[A: Wikipedia, Katharina von Bora]** 4 April 1523, the Nimbschen nuns escape in Leonhard Koppe's covered wagon among the herring barrels. **[A: Wikipedia, German Peasants' War]** April 1525, *Admonition to Peace*; 15 May, Frankenhausen; later that month, *Against the Murderous, Thieving Hordes of Peasants*, never withdrawn.

**[A: Wikipedia, Katharina von Bora]** 13 June 1525, the marriage, witnesses including Justus Jonas, Bugenhagen, and Lucas and Barbara Cranach; 27 June, the public celebration under Bugenhagen. **[A]** December 1525, *De Servo Arbitrio*, answering Erasmus's *De Libero Arbitrio* of 1524. **[A: Wikipedia, Marburg Colloquy]** 1 to 4 October 1529, Marburg: agreement on fourteen articles, division on the Eucharist.

**[A: Wikipedia, Katharina von Bora]** Children: Hans 1526, Elisabeth 1527 (dies in infancy), Magdalena 1529 (dies 1542 aged thirteen), Martin 1531, Paul 1533, Margarete 1534; a miscarriage 1 November 1539. Carried in campa prose, not pinned: they share one address with stops already written.

**[A: en.wikipedia, Diet of Augsburg; correction applied twice, the pool having made one error in two lenses]** 25 June 1530. Melanchthon authored the Augsburg Confession but did not present it; the Saxon chancellor Christian Beyer read it aloud to Charles V. Luther, under the imperial ban, sat out the diet at the Veste Coburg from 23 April to 4 October. **[A: Wikipedia, Luther Bible]** 1534, the complete German Bible, seventy-five books, printed by Hans Lufft.

## VIII. The late years, 1537-1546

**[A: en.wikipedia, Smalcald Articles; correction applied]** The pool's Schmalkalden entry was wrong in substance, not only in coordinate: it had Luther travelling there in February 1537 and drafting the Smalcald Articles on the spot. He wrote them in December 1536 at Wittenberg at Elector John Frederick's request; at Schmalkalden he was too ill with kidney stones to attend; and the League declined to adopt them, taking Melanchthon's *Treatise on the Power and Primacy of the Pope* instead.

**[A: Brecht; de.wikipedia]** 5 October 1544, dedication of the chapel at Hartenfels Castle, Torgau. Hedge preserved: one of the first rooms built expressly for Protestant worship, not the first, Neuburg (1543) and Sankt Joachimsthal (1540) preceding it. **[A: Bainton]** January to February 1546, the last journey to Eisleben to arbitrate between the counts of Mansfeld, four days held at Halle by the flooded Saale. **[A: Wikipedia; Justus Jonas, present]** 18 February 1546, death at Eisleben, aged 62.

**[R: de.wikipedia, Martin Luthers Sterbehaus]** Which house he died in is disputed and left so. The Andreaskirchplatz museum has been called the death house only since the seventeenth century, fixed in that role when Prussia bought it for a memorial in 1863; recent research places the death at Markt 56, the Drachstedt house, since demolished. The pool's coordinate matched neither. The stop is tagged reconstruction and uses the museum coordinate. **[R, downgraded from the pool's [A]]** That the body was "opened, embalmed, and displayed" is unsupported; contemporary accounts describe a tin or pewter coffin, and the journey says coffin, not autopsy.

**[A: Brecht; Castle Church records]** 19 February 1546, lying in state at St. Andreas, Jonas's funeral sermon. 22 February, burial beneath the Schlosskirche pulpit, 2.4 m down, the cortege having come via Halle and Bitterfeld; Bugenhagen preaching, Melanchthon giving the Latin oration and laid beside him in 1560.

## IX. Afterlife

**[A: Wikipedia; de.wikipedia]** Katharina dies at Torgau 20 December 1552, aged 53, after fleeing plague-stricken Wittenberg and being thrown from a wagon into water; buried in the Marienkirche, some fifty kilometres from her husband.

**[A: en.wikipedia, All Saints' Church, Wittenberg]** 1760, the wooden doors destroyed when Imperial forces shell the Prussian-held Wittenberg fortress. The object on which the theses may have hung no longer exists; the posting rests entirely on textual testimony. 1858, the bronze *Thesentür* cast with the Latin text, about 1,000 kg, dedicated 10 November under Friedrich Wilhelm IV on the 375th anniversary of the birth.

**[A: de.wikipedia]** Monuments, none pinned: Schadow's, unveiled on Wittenberg's market square 31 October 1821 under a Schinkel baldachin, the oldest in Germany and the first free-standing public statue there honouring a non-noble; Rietschel's at Worms, 25 June 1868, grouping Luther with Wycliffe, Hus, Savonarola, Waldo, Melanchthon and Reuchlin, a genealogy of forerunners built backwards. Also unpinned: the 1883 jubilee, the 1996 UNESCO inscription, the 2017 quincentenary.

**[R, downgraded from the pool's [A]: de.wikipedia, *Luther in effigie*]** The death mask. German Wikipedia states that authentic casts are nowhere securely attested ("nirgends sicher bezeugt"), and the ascription to Lukas Furtenagel is a modern hypothesis of Uta Kornmeier. Nor are the objects straightforwardly "displayed today in Halle": the Halle effigy lost its wax in 1926, and the plaster casts shown at Eisleben from 1933 were withdrawn in 2011 pending authenticity checks. No stop written.

## Coordinate corrections applied

Twenty-one pins were replaced; corrected values are in the journey file. Errors ran 215 m to 4.7 km. The consequential ones: Schlosskirche 620 m, the same wrong value recurring in six other pool entries; Luthereiche 730 m and Wartburg 440 m, the pool holding two different wrong values for each; Veste Coburg 860 m, in the town rather than on the fortress hill; Mansfeld Elternhaus 2.3 km, in open country; Schmalkalden 1.2 km; Stotternheim 4.7 km; Eisleben Sterbehaus, matching neither candidate building. At Augsburg St. Anna the pin was near enough and the label was the error. Unchanged, no error found: Magdeburg, Eisenach, Erfurt Augustinerkloster, both Rome pins, Heidelberg, Jena, Marburg, Halle.

## Contradictions flagged and left open

Argued above; indexed here. (1) Ordination 3 April against 4 April 1507. (2) Ordination and first Mass conflated in the pool, separated on de.wiki alone. (3) Stotternheim tagged [R] in one lens and [A] in another within one pool. (4) The door: Melanchthon and Rörer against Luther's own 1517 silence. (5) "Here I stand": documented ending against traditional continuation. (6) The death house: Andreaskirchplatz museum against the demolished Markt 56. (7) The death mask: Furtenagel by modern hypothesis against de.wikipedia's "nirgends sicher bezeugt". (8) Two different pool coordinates for one site, twice, at the Wartburg and the bull-burning oak; unified on de.wiki, but that inconsistency is reason to treat the pool's unflagged coordinates with reserve.

## Honest gaps

- **Nimbschen** and **Frankenhausen.** Neither coordinate is in the pool. The journey uses an approximate point for Kloster Nimbschen south of Grimma, and the town of Bad Frankenhausen rather than the Schlachtberg above it, saying so in both stops' `sources`. Both should be verified.
- **Altenburg, January 1519** (the Miltitz meeting). Attested, no verified coordinate, no stop. The **1518 print diffusion** of the theses is likewise attested and placeless, folded into prose.
- **The Worms transcript question.** Not pursued; search budget exhausted at 200 of 200.

## Quotations used, with their real provenance

The pool arrived with three misattributions to Hazlitt. All three are from **PG #9841**, *Selections from the Table Talk of Martin Luther*, the **Captain Henry Bell** translation. Corrected throughout.

- **Ninety-Five Theses**, theses 1, 27, 32, 36, 62, 86, PG #274. **[A]** Thesis 27 is printed with the translator's bracketed completion, "the soul flies out [of purgatory]": the pool's truncation had dropped the object of the sentence and with it the point.
- **[A]** *Concerning Christian Liberty*, PG #1911, 1520; *An Open Letter on Translating*, PG #272, 1530; *A Treatise on Good Works*, PG #418, 1520; *Commentary on Galatians*, PG #1549, 1535.
- **Table Talk**, three sayings (the sparrows; the ravens must have a kite; all the torments), PG #9841. **[A] as printed text, [R] as Luther's words**: reported speech recorded by dinner guests, not autograph. The sparrows saying carries a speech tag in Bell ("I am persuaded, said he, that...") which the pool dropped, and the torments saying was missing two words ("and prepare"); both restored. On the ravens saying Bell names the questioner as the Elector of Saxony, then Frederick the Wise; the journey names no questioner rather than risk the wrong one.
- **The Worms reply**: only "Gott helfe mir. Amen." See section V. Fourteen of the forty-four stops carry a quote.
