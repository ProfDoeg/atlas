# Pamela Colman Smith (1878-1951): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-28 for `pamela_colman_smith.journey.json`: 45 stops, 8 segments, gregorian.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim whose source could not be reached · **[C]** contradiction between sources, flagged and left open · **Corr.** a research-pool error corrected here and in the journey file.

The standard biography is **Stuart R. Kaplan, *The Encyclopedia of Tarot*, Volume III (1990)**, which reproduces Smith's letters, her visitors' book, the *Green Sheaf* colophon, her Catholic Who's Who entry, and the Photo-Secession's exhibition report. It is also the source Wikipedia cites for the death and burial, and it contradicts Wikipedia's own lead on both. Where they disagree, Kaplan is followed and the disagreement stated. Every hedge below also lives in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`, never in campa prose.

---

## 1. Pimlico and Kingston, 1878-1893

- **[A]** Born 16 February 1878 at **28 Belgrave Road, Pimlico**, to American parents: Charles Edward Smith, merchant, and Corinne Colman, of a painting family. The street number is Kaplan's, not Wikipedia's alone, so the pool's caution on it is dropped. No birth certificate is reproduced anywhere.
- **[R]** A first decade shuttling between Britain, New England, and the Caribbean, Manchester included; her claim of twenty-five Atlantic crossings before twenty-two is unverified. **No stop:** undated, unlocated, eventless. The claim sits in the birth campa.
- **[A]** The family moves to **Kingston** c. 1889, her father taking work connected to the West India Improvement Company. No address survives. There she absorbs the **Annancy cycle**, published in 1899; the scan was read directly and four passages are carried.
- **[R]** A large 1896 toy-theatre *Henry Morgan* in Kingston: "a later reconstruction rather than a contemporaneous record", in the pool's own words. Greer documents toy-theatre performances at a **Brooklyn fair, December 1897**, not a Kingston staging. Kept as a stop, tagged [R].
- **[A]** **Corinne Colman Smith dies in Jamaica, 1896.** **Gap:** year only, no month, day, or burial place.

## 2. Pratt Institute and the New York trade, 1893-1899

- **[A]** Enrolled at **Pratt Institute**, 200 Willoughby Avenue, at about fifteen, under **Arthur Wesley Dow**. Left **1897 without a degree**, reason reported as illness, which is named nowhere. **Gap.**
- **Corr. [C] the Macbeth "first exhibition".** The pool asserted one in 1897. Kaplan documents regular *sales*, prints "sold regularly by the end of 1897, through the Macbeth Gallery on Fifth Avenue"; no one-person show appears in Kaplan, in Wikipedia's *Macbeth Gallery* article, or in the Greer post cited for it. Recast as sales; any first-exhibition claim stays **[R]**.
- **[R]** The **237 Fifth Avenue** number: Kaplan gives only "Fifth Avenue", Wikipedia no address. The coordinate assumes 237 is right.
- **[A]** **1899:** *Annancy Stories* (R. H. Russell), written and illustrated by her, with a publisher's introduction comparing her to Uncle Remus. Her father dies the same year; the source does not say where, so the fact is folded into this stop, not pinned to an invented place.
- **[R]** The wider 1898-1899 Russell output (a Yeats design, a *Shakespeare's Heroines* calendar): Greer and the pool only; no stop.

## 3. London: Lyceum, studio, temple, 1899-1903

- **Corr. [C] two events under one date.** The pool put both the return to England and the studio at 1901. Wikipedia separates them: she returned in 1899, the year her father died, and opened the studio and weekly open house in 1901. Split into two stops.
- **[A]** She works in and around the **Lyceum** household: Ellen Terry, Henry Irving, Bram Stoker. **[R]** Salaried employment there is *not* established; intermittent design, illustration, companionship, and touring is the supported form. **[R]** The nickname **"Pixie"** and its attribution to Terry: repeated everywhere, attested nowhere fetched.
- **[C] Her own address.** The 1901 visitors' book gives **13 Milborne Grove**, the colophon **14**, both quoted in Kaplan. Not adjudicated; both recorded. The locality is The Boltons, usually called South Kensington; the pool's "Chelsea" is loose.
- **[A]** Initiated into the **Isis-Urania Temple**, November 1901. **[R]** The introduction by W. B. Yeats: his own initiation (Mark Masons' Hall, 1890) is attested in his file, his introducing Smith is tradition.
- **Gap: her grade.** No certificate or roll entry was found, so her passage through the Adeptus Minor ceremony (the seven-sided vault of Christian Rosenkreuz, rebuilt as a room) is inferred from Order practice, not attested for her.
- **[R]** *A Broad Sheet* with **Jack B. Yeats**, 1902: tradition, unconfirmed; issue-level attribution between the two artists has never been worked out.
- **[A]** **1903:** the Order fractures; Waite founds the **Independent and Rectified Order R.R. et A.C.** **[R]** Smith's membership in it, and its roughly four-year duration, are unverified. Waite's file leaves the rite's founding city uncertain between London and Geneva; the London pin is used because Smith is in London.

## 4. The Green Sheaf, 1903-1906

- **[A]** *The Green Sheaf*, thirteen issues, **May 1903 to May 1904**, edited, published, hand-coloured, and sold by Smith herself from 14 Milborne Grove. The complete run was read in the Internet Archive OCR; five quotations are carried, four from the No. I prospectus and one from "The Town", signed P.C.S., Vol. XII.
- **[A]** Sold through **Elkin Mathews**, Vigo Street, and from issue seven through **Brentano's**, Union Square. **[R]** The **John Baillie** exhibition and reciprocal advertising, its Baker Street address unconfirmed: **no stop**, carried inside the Elkin Mathews campa.
- **Corr. the Knightsbridge shop had an address after all.** The pool pleaded that none survives. Kaplan quotes the magazine's own announcement, "located at Park Mansions Arcade, Knightsbridge, London"; Boston College dates it to the twelfth issue, 1904, the Green Sheaf School of Hand-Colouring's shop.
- **[A]** The **Green Sheaf Press** outlives the magazine, publishing books mostly by women, into 1906 at least. **Gap:** no list of titles, no record of a winding-up.

## 5. 291 and the music pictures, 1906-1909

- **[A]** Three surviving **music pictures**, dated 1907, at the Tate, painted while the music played. The practice is undated; the stop sits at June 1906 only to precede the January show.
- **[A]** **291 Fifth Avenue, 5-22 January 1907**, extended eight days, roughly 2,200 visitors, "far more visitors to the gallery than any of the previous photography shows" (Kaplan). The first show there devoted to a non-photographic artist. Second show **26 February to 11 March 1908**; a third in 1909, attested without dates and folded into the same stop rather than given a fabricated one.
- **[C] Did it sell out?** Wikipedia's *Alfred Stieglitz* article says all exhibited works sold; Kaplan quotes the Photo-Secession's report of thirty-three sold; the *291 (art gallery)* article says only that a substantial number sold. **The Stieglitz article is the outlier.** Not resolved.
- **Corr. the "seventy-two works" figure is unsupported**, appearing in none of the sources consulted, including the one cited for it. **Dropped, not demoted.** The platinum-print portfolio was of **22** works.
- **Corr. the Magic Spectacles date.** The pool gave 25 March 1909; Greer gives the lecture as **Tuesday night, 23 March**, Assembly Hall, Ryerson Street, *reported* on the 25th. The pool had the report date as the event date.
- **Corr. one letter, two dates.** The pool's two lenses each carried the Stieglitz letter, at 18 and at 19 November 1909. Same document, quoted in Kaplan: **19 November 1909**, written **from London**, not at the gallery. Merged into one stop and moved.

## 6. The deck and the years after, 1909-1914

- **[A]** **Waite commissions the deck**; Smith draws all seventy-eight designs between roughly April and October 1909, Waite specifying the symbolism card by card. Waite's own file, stop "London, the direction of Pamela Colman Smith", names her directly and independently records the flat fee and the initials-not-name detail.
- **Gap: the money.** No contract, fee, royalty clause, or copyright assignment has ever surfaced. "Flat fee" rests on her phrase "very little cash" and on the absence of any later payment.
- **[R] Sola Busca.** Waite's file records Sola Busca photographs deposited at the British Museum in 1907, and the parallels with her Three of Swords and Ten of Wands are real, but **no record places her in front of them**. A `suggested_ref`, not a fact in a campa.
- **[A]** **December 1909**, William Rider & Son, **164 Aldersgate Street**: maroon box, roses-and-lilies backs, with Waite's *The Key to the Tarot*; run halted c. April 1910 over cardstock, **five copies of the true first edition known to survive**. The plates burned in the war, so later editions descend from printed cards and later recolourings cannot be assumed to reproduce her palette.
- **Corr. Aldersgate, not Paternoster Row.** The pool contradicted itself, inventing a Paternoster Row point 750 m off. Reconciled at **51.5215, -0.0977**, inherited byte-identical from Waite's file.
- **Corr. the conversion resolves against 1890s.ca**, which puts it in a New York residence of 1911-1914. Kaplan quotes her **own Catholic Who's Who entry for 1939**: "Rec'd into the Church at Farm Street 1911." Farm Street is the Jesuit church in Mayfair. On her own testimony: London, 1911, a named church. The New York reading is recorded in the stop as a minority position.
- **Corr. Suffrage Atelier founders**: Laurence Housman, Clemence Housman, **and Alfred Pearse**; the pool omitted Pearse. Smith is listed among the members. **[R]** coordinate, a west-London placeholder. **Gap:** her output cannot be inventoried, the records being incomplete and most work unsigned.
- **[A]** **1911:** *The Pictorial Key to the Tarot*, all seventy-eight cards illustrated, designs credited to Smith in the text from the start; also 1911, illustrations for **Bram Stoker's** *The Lair of the White Worm*; 1913, for **Ellen Terry's** *The Russian Ballet*; 1914, Waite disbands the rite and leaves the Golden Dawn tradition.
- **Gap: after 1913.** What she published in her remaining thirty-eight years could not be traced. The thinning of her commissions is an absence of records, not a finding.

## 7. Cornwall, 1918/19 to 1951

- **Corr. date and tenure.** The pool said "1920s" and "buy". Both wrong. Kaplan puts the move "shortly after the war" and records the Parc Garland church in regular service from **February 1919**; artcornwall gives 1918 and names the uncle Theodore. Kaplan and Wikipedia both say **lease**. Corrected to c. 1919, and to a lease.
- **[C] Whose house held the priests?** Wikipedia puts the holiday home "in a neighbouring house", Kaplan in her own. Not resolved; no deed either way.
- **[A]** She served as **sacristan of Our Lady of The Lizard** and called the converted building her "Cathedral". **Gap: the coordinate.** Parc Garland resolves in neither OSM nor Nominatim, the pool gives two points for it, and Kaplan's village-church context points to **Lizard village** rather than Predannack. Used at hamlet precision, uncertainty declared.
- **[R]** Kynance and Cadgwith as her walking ground: local geography, no documented episode at either. One stop, at Kynance, with Cadgwith in the campa.
- **[A]** **1939:** financial pressure forces her and Nora Lake to **Exeter**. **Gap:** no address found. **[A] Early 1940s:** on to **Bude**. **[R]** The errand-boy anecdote (paid in pictures rather than coins) is oral evidence reaching print through late recollection, as artcornwall cautions.
- **Corr. "such fun" was nearly published as a fabricated letter-quote.** The pool had it as an incomplete quotation from an unlocated letter describing the Roman Catholic Church. It is neither: Kaplan prints it twice as a *spoken* remark recalled by the painter Alphaeus Cole, and it describes **being a Catholic**, not the Church.
- **[R] on that stop's place and date, flagged for the operator.** Kaplan has Cole recalling the visit in New York City, "probably shortly after the end of World War II". **No transatlantic crossing by Smith in the 1940s is documented anywhere consulted**, and she was then destitute in Bude, so the recollection may misplace an earlier visit. The stop carries the New York pin with that objection stated in full. If a crossing can be ruled out, it should move or go.

## 8. Death, burial, afterlives

- **Corr. the death date. 18 September 1951**, not 16. Kaplan: she "died at age 73 on September 18, 1951, at 2 Bencoolen House in Bude", Mrs Elsie T. Bates present. **[C]** Wikipedia is internally inconsistent, infobox and lead giving the 16th, its body text, citing Kaplan, the 18th. The 18th is used.
- **Corr. cause of death.** "Heart disease" overstates the record. Kaplan notes only that she "seems to have had some heart problems", citing a February 1939 letter sympathising that she was "feeling so poorly with your heart". The file names no cause.
- **[C] Which burial ground?** Kaplan, the source Wikipedia itself cites, records "her burial at the local **Catholic cemetery** in Bude, but her grave site remains unknown". Wikipedia paraphrases this as St Michael's Cemetery; artcornwall names **St Michael and All Angels**, Bude's **Anglican parish church**, odd for a devout convert. Left open. **Gap:** the coordinate is unverifiable and the plot record burned before the parish books were centralised at Truro.
- **[A]** She died in debt; effects auctioned to satisfy creditors; Nora Lake, named beneficiary, received substantially nothing. The pool's claim that the auction *destroyed the tarot originals* is speculative and **not carried**: their whereabouts were unrecorded before her death.
- **Corr. the 1971 U.S. Games claim is verifiable, not [R].** Wikipedia, *Rider-Waite Tarot*: the deck entered the US public domain in **1966**, and the U.S. Games claim on its 1971 version "only applies to new material added to the pre-existing work (e.g. designs on the card backs and the box)". The dispute is over new packaging atop a public-domain work, not over Smith's authorship.
- **[R] The Rider reissue history**, "until 1939, then again from 1971 to 1977", carries a `{{citation needed}}` tag dated January 2025. **Not stated as fact in the journey file.**
- **[A]** 1975, *To All Believers*, Delaware Art Museum, **11 September to 19 October**, around Melinda Boyd Parsons's catalogue; the Princeton leg's dates were not established, so it is named in the Delaware campa rather than given a stop with an invented date. **[A]** 2009, the **Smith-Waite Centennial**. **[A]** 2019, *Life and Work*, Pratt Libraries. **[A]** 2022-23, the Whitney's *At the Dawn of a New Age*. **[A] at one remove**, 2 January 2026, the *Overlooked No More* obituary, via Pratt's summary.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Smith is **not** in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, and nothing here required the exception: she holds no framework to apply backward, and none of anyone else's was applied to her. **Neither direction was caught.** Three relations were found that are *not* rooted in her own life; none was silently deleted.

1. **Etteilla, Paris, 1770s. Dropped from the campas; backward, genealogical only.** The deck sits in the lineage Etteilla to Levi to Mathers to Waite, but that is a genealogy of *Waite's* programme; nothing places her in contact with an Etteilla deck, and `etteilla.journey.json` never mentions her. **If the operator judges the lineage Smith's own inheritance rather than Waite's, reinstate it.**
2. **H. P. Blavatsky. Dropped.** Milieu proximity only; Yeats passed through the Esoteric Section, Smith did not, and no source connects them.
3. **Isadora Duncan. Dropped.** A structural link through a shared node, the Terry / Craig family; her file never mentions Smith and no meeting is attested.

Travelers named in campas: **`arthur_edward_waite`**, who commissioned the deck, directed its symbolism, and headed her rite from 1903; **`w_b_yeats`**, whose work appears in *The Green Sheaf*, whose plays she designed for, and whose 1901 pamphlet is the Order crisis she was initiated into the middle of (the introduction itself stays [R]); and **`christian_rosenkreuz`**, one-directional under the standing interlock rule, since she entered an order built on his legend. He is named once, at the initiation stop, whose `date_confidence` states her unrecorded grade history.
