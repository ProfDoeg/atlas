# Augusto Boal: research report
*2026-08-06. Rio de Janeiro 1931 to Rio de Janeiro 2009. Director at the Teatro de Arena, inventor of the Teatro do Oprimido, tortured and exiled in 1971, exiled again by the Argentine coup of 1976, elected vereador of Rio in 1992.*

**Method.** A gathering pass over en / pt / es Wikipedia plus primary retrieval (Open Library; the ITI's own PDF of the 2009 World Theatre Day message; ctorio.org.br; OpenStreetMap for every coordinate), then adversarial verification, which overturned eleven items. Every correction is named here, not absorbed silently. Where editions disagree and no third source adjudicates, the disagreement is printed as a disagreement.

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a date derived from a stated age rather than from a document.

## 1. Penha, and the chemistry that did not take (1931-1953)

**Birth. [A]** Augusto Pinto Boal is born **16 March 1931** in the *subúrbio da Penha*, Rio de Janeiro, to the Portuguese immigrants José Augusto Boal and Albertina Pinto (pt; date confirmed independently by en and es).

- **Correction carried.** The pool's **"Penha Circular"** is anachronistic and is struck: that *bairro* was constituted only on **23 July 1981** (pt, "Penha Circular"), fifty years after the birth. The journey reads **"Penha, Rio de Janeiro"**.

**The child director. [R]** At about **ten** he directs cousins and siblings in home theatricals (es). The journey's 1941 is arithmetic from the stated age, not a dated event. No source names a play, a room, or a witness.

**University. [R]** At **seventeen** he enrols in **chemical engineering** (*engenharia química*) at the **antiga Universidade do Brasil** (pt). Two pool errors corrected: the institution was **not** the Federal University of Rio de Janeiro in 1948, renamed UFRJ only in **1965**; and the subject was chemical engineering, not chemistry as es implies. **1948** is derived from the stated age and is **not attested**, so the [A] tag is demoted; the **1952** degree is likewise age-derived. The 1948 site of the Escola Nacional de Química is not established anywhere reachable, so the pin sits on central Rio.

## 4. The kidnapping (1971)

## 5. Buenos Aires, and the book (1971-1976)

## The quotations, and what the verifier did to them

Eight of the twelve Boal quotations in the pool were altered in transmission. Every one is corrected in the journey file.

- ***Games for Actors and Non-Actors*, the definition.** It does **not** begin "Theatre is the capacity". 1992 1st ed., p. xxvi: "In its most archaic sense, theatre is the capacity possessed by human beings—and not by animals—to observe themselves in action." The pool's "2nd ed. (Routledge, 1992/2002), p. xxvi" mixes 1st-ed. roman pagination with a 2nd-ed. label; the journey cites the **1992 1st ed.** exactly. The pool's note calling it "the opening line of the theoretical preface" is **false**: it appears several pages in.
- **The spect-actor passage**, verified in the 2002 2nd ed., **p. 15**. No leading definite article, lower case, and the third sentence ("They are spect-actors") restored. The pool's capitalised "Actors"/"Spectators" is the 1st-edition rendering.
- **The end-of-session passage**, 2nd ed. **p. 275** (1st ed. p. 245). The pool's trailing "..." is the pipeline's own truncation; its note claiming the ellipsis came from the source is false. Full text carried.
- **The frontier passage**, 2nd ed. **p. 276** (1st ed. p. 246). The internal ellipsis legitimately elides "Theatre shall never end!". Em dash restored. The pool's note calling this "the closing statement of the book" is wrong; several paragraphs follow, ending "Let's hope. Let's work for it!"
- ***The Rainbow of Desire*** (1995), p. 13: em dashes restored around "or theatricality". **Verification limit:** the text could not be obtained directly (see Sources); the quotation rests on Wikiquote's page-13 citation plus consistent aggregator attestation, and on the parallel definition in *Games*, which **was** verified.
- ***The Aesthetics of the Oppressed*** (2006): both quotations had dashes converted to commas, once producing a comma splice. Dashes restored. "We must all do theatre – to find out who we are..." is corroborated beyond Wikiquote by Routledge's own description.
- The *Canadian Theatre Review* interview (1986) and *Hamlet and the Baker's Son* (2001) passed unaltered.
- The Cherríe Moraga line (*A Xicana Codex of Changing Consciousness*, Duke, 2011) is **not Boal** and is not carried: a later writer's tribute, evidence of reception only.
