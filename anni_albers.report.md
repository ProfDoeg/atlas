# Anni Albers (1899-1994): research report

*Slug `anni_albers`. Compiled 2026-08-12 from the verified research pool (gather pass plus adversarial verification). Companion artifact: `anni_albers.journey.json`, 9 segments, 45 stops.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named in place · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or claim carried only by an unattestable source. Contradictions are flagged where they arise and **not** adjudicated.

**Shape of the evidence.** The Bauhaus years can be dated to the semester. The Latin American trips, which mattered more to her, survive largely as museum copy that would not load this session plus one unattestable user timeline, so every Mexico and Andes stop is tagged **[R]**.

---

## 1. Origins: Charlottenburg, 1899-1922

- **Birth [A].** 12 June 1899, Charlottenburg, Berlin (Wikipedia "Anni Albers"; de.wikipedia; Tate biography). Charlottenburg per Wikipedia: 52.51667, 13.30000.
- **Middle name: contradiction.** de.wikipedia gives "Annelise **Else** Frieda Fleischmann"; the pool's chronology lens submitted "Elsa" while its geography lens wrote "Else". The file uses Else and says the records disagree.
- **Family money [A, corrected].** Two sources, not one: father **Siegfried Fleischmann**, furniture manufacturer; mother **Toni**, daughter of the publisher **Leopold Ullstein** (de.wikipedia). The submitted "publishing family" was wrong as stated, publishing coming through the mother only.
- **Martin Brandenburg [A].** Private painting study under the Impressionist from 1916, Berlin (Wikipedia). Her only formal art training before the Bauhaus.
- **Kokoschka anecdote [R].** Shown a portrait of hers, Kokoschka is said to have asked only "Why do you paint?". In the Wikipedia narrative with no citation retrievable; the presumed origin, Weber's *Anni Albers: A Life*, was **not opened**. Used as a stop quote, flagged [R].
- **Hamburg [A, place corrected].** About two months at the Staatliche Kunstgewerbeschule, 1919 (Wikipedia). The school stood at **Am Lerchenfeld 2**, **53.56750, 10.03139**, in its 1911-1913 building; submitted 53.5511, 9.9937 is the Rathaus area, ~3 km off (Wikipedia, "University of Fine Arts of Hamburg").
- **Gap.** No source reached states why she left Hamburg after two months.

## 2. Weimar: the Vorkurs and the loom, 1922-1925

- **Admission [A].** Rejected on her **first** application to the Bauhaus itself, admitted on the second, **April 1922** (Wikipedia; de.wikipedia). The pool's geography lens had misattributed the rejection to another school.
- **Teachers [A].** Vorkurs and early workshops under **Johannes Itten** and **Georg Muche** (Wikipedia). Itten ran the Vorkurs 1919-1923 (`itten.journey.json`); **Paul Klee**, Form Master from 1921, taught form theory to the whole student body, weavers included (`paul_klee.journey.json`).
- **Steering into weaving [R].** That she preferred glass or wall painting and was channelled to the loom, one of the few areas open to women, is repeated throughout Bauhaus scholarship; the **mechanism is disputed** and nowhere documented as policy.
- **Klee as her decisive teacher [R].** Widely asserted; this dossier cannot produce the statement in her own words, and the campa says so.
- **Interlock [A].** Itten ran a Mazdaznan community inside the Bauhaus 1919-1923, the same student body and years she was in the Vorkurs, before his break with Gropius (`itten.journey.json`).

## 3. Dessau: cellophane, diploma, workshop, 1925-1932

- **Marriage: date reduced [flagged].** The pool submitted **1925-05-09**, unsupported: English Wikipedia, de.wikipedia, the Connecticut Women's Hall of Fame and Wikipedia's Josef Albers article all give the **year only**. Worse, 9 May is exactly **her date of death**, the signature of a contaminated field. Reduced to 1925.
- **Move to Dessau [A], coordinates corrected.** The Bauhaus Dessau building is **51.8392, 12.2272** per Wikipedia; submitted 51.8302, 12.2508 lands near the station, ~1.8 km off.
- **Meisterhäuser [A], coordinates corrected.** Ebertallee 65-71, **51.8432, 12.2205**, near Klee and Kandinsky; submitted 51.8368, 12.2447 is ~1.9 km off. The pin is inherited from `paul_klee.journey.json`. Josef took the half Moholy-Nagy vacated in 1928.
- **Diploma work [A, contradiction flagged].** Wall covering for the auditorium of the **ADGB Bundesschule at Bernau**. de.wikipedia dates it **1929/30** with materials **cotton, linen, bast and cellophane**, not "cotton chenille and cellophane" as submitted; English Wikipedia confirms the light-reflecting and sound-absorbing function. *Not resolved:* Wikipedia's ADGB Trade Union School article credits **Margaretha Reichardt** with textiles "used in the furnishings". Probably two commissions, but the campa names both and settles nothing.
- **Workshop leadership [A, corrected].** **Interim** director for **one semester from autumn 1931**, after Gunta Stölzl was forced out (de.wikipedia). The submitted claim that she ran it until the closure is **false**: **Lilly Reich** took the workshop in 1932.
- **Interlock [A].** Stölzl had worked in Itten's **Ontos** weaving and carpet workshops at Herrliberg, founded 1923 after his Bauhaus departure (`itten.journey.json`).
- **Berlin-Steglitz [A], coordinates corrected.** Rented former telephone factory, **Birkbuschstrasse 49**, near 52.449, 13.319; submitted 52.4408, 13.3244 is ~1 km south. The dissolution vote is dated **20 July 1933**, closure August, so the pool's "1933-08" is the closure, not the decision.
- **Interlock [A].** Klee dismissed and his Dessau house searched by the Gestapo in spring 1933, the same pressure that put the Alberses on a ship (`paul_klee.journey.json`).

## 4. The crossing, 1933

- **Sailing [A].** Google's Doodle page states that on **18 November 1933** she and her husband "set sail for the United States to flee Nazi Germany": a departure, not an arrival in Carolina.
- **Arrival: contradiction.** November 1933 (English Wikipedia) vs December 1933 (BMC Museum + Arts Center, faculty 1933-1949). The file dates the stop 1933-12-01 and records it.
- **Gap.** No source names the port or the ship, so the departure stop is pinned on Berlin, their last German ground, with the reason stated.
- **Philip Johnson recommendation [R, downgraded].** Standard in the literature, submitted as [A], **not corroborated** by Wikipedia's BMC article, which says only that Josef "was selected to run the art program".
- **Her role, corrected.** "Arrive to found the college's art program" overstates her position: she began as an assistant, becoming **official faculty in 1935** (Wikipedia).
- **Campus, corrected.** The pool's two Black Mountain pins (35.6187, -82.3221; 35.6323, -82.3018) match neither campus: **Blue Ridge Assembly** 1933-1941, then **Lake Eden** 1941-1957. The file uses the canonical pin **35.617, -82.323** and names the campus in prose.
- **Naturalization: contradiction.** 1937 (Wikipedia) vs 1939 (Timetoast). The file uses 1937 and records the conflict.

## 5. Black Mountain, 1933-1949

- **Teaching [A].** Weaving and textile design, 1933-1949 (Wikipedia; BMC Museum). The grass-and-scrap story, students gathering outdoor materials when looms were unavailable during a 1940s relocation, is presented on Wikipedia **as anecdote** and carried here as legend **[R]**.
- **Harriet Engelhardt Memorial Collection [A, date corrected].** Textiles "primarily from Mexico and South America, but also ... Italy, the Pacific Islands, and Africa" (Wikipedia, which gives **no year**). The pool's "1930s-1940s" is **impossible at its early end**: the collection memorialises a student who died mid-decade. Conventionally **1947**.
- **Willard Gallery [A, date and place corrected].** Not 1940: Wikipedia's exhibition list gives "**Anni Albers and Alex Reed: Exhibition of Necklaces, May 5-25, 1941**", and the Willard Gallery article lists 5-24 May 1941 while assigning the **December 1940 and 1941** jewellery shows there to **Alexander Calder**. The submitted coordinates were **MoMA's**, reused; Willard stood at **32 East 57th Street**, ~40.7625, -73.9720.
- **Interlocks, summer 1948 [A].** Josef Albers summoned **John Cage**, **Merce Cunningham** and **Buckminster Fuller**; Cage staged Satie's *Le Piège de Méduse* with Fuller acting (`merce_cunningham`, `buckminster_fuller` journey files). Fuller had apprenticed as a millwright in a Quebec textile mill in 1914. Pin and date inherited: **35.617, -82.323**, **1948-07-01**.
- **MoMA 1949 [A].** First textile designer given a solo show at the Museum of Modern Art; tours other US venues 1951-1953 (Wikipedia).
- **Departure and the Yale date [A, corrected].** They leave Black Mountain in **1949**; Josef goes to Yale in **1950** (Wikipedia "Josef Albers"; Connecticut Women's Hall of Fame; the pool's geography lens), which the pool's chronology conflated. His office was **chairman of the Department of Design**, to his 1958 retirement.

## 6. Mexico and the Andes: thinnest evidence, largest consequence

- **First Mexico trip [R, downgraded from A].** The only support for **1935** is a **Timetoast user timeline**. English Wikipedia does not date a first trip and ties the **backstrap loom** to **1939**. The "thirteen trips, 1935-1967" framing is Guggenheim copy that **could not be retrieved**.
- **Contradiction.** Backstrap loom and first trip: 1935 (Timetoast) vs 1939 (Wikipedia).
- **Mexico-derived weavings dated 1936 [R, downgraded from A].** Same user timeline, sole source. *Ancient Writing* is dated 1936, consistent but inferential. The file carries the work as attested, the derivation as [R].
- **Site visits [R].** Monte Alban, Mitla, Teotihuacan and a Havana stopover are repeated in the scholarship with no retrievable itinerary. **Teotihuacan and Havana were dropped** as weakest. Mitla corrected to **16.92722, -96.35944**; submitted 16.9236, -96.3739 is ~1.5 km west.
- **Peru and Chile [R].** A 1953 journey rests on an Albers Foundation post and the a-r-t.com Josef Albers chronology, listing 1953-54 visiting professorships in **Lima** and **Santiago** (Universidad Católica). Lima, Cusco, Machu Picchu, Paracas and Chan Chan are carried without primary itineraries; **Chan Chan was dropped**. Lima's pin **-12.0464, -77.0428** is inherited from `guaman_poma_de_ayala.journey.json`.
- **Paracas [R], hedge preserved.** Nothing establishes that she walked the Wari Kayan necropolis; she may have known its textiles through collections only. Coordinates moved west to **-13.83, -76.30**, the submitted -76.25 falling in the bay.
- **Attested [A].** That she collected pre-Columbian and especially Peruvian textiles during and after Latin American travel, and that this shaped her later pictorial weavings (Wikipedia; Metropolis).

## 7. New Haven, the books, the prints, 1950-1971

- **Haystack: the pool's largest geographic error [A, corrected].** She taught at the **Haystack Mountain School of Crafts**, Maine, 1950 (Wikipedia); the pool placed it at **Deer Isle**. Haystack was **founded in 1950 in Montville** and **moved to Deer Isle only in 1961**, so a summer 1950 session cannot have been on the island; submitted 44.1462, -68.6564 is neither place. The file pins **Montville township, 44.4269, -69.2103**; the exact ground within Montville or Liberty is unnamed.
- **Publications [A].** *On Designing*, 1959, **Pellango Press**, New Haven; Wesleyan second edition 1962; paperback 1971. *On Weaving*, **1965**, **Wesleyan University Press**, Middletown, dedicated "**to my great teachers, the weavers of ancient Peru**", pre-Columbian textiles set beside Bauhaus structural diagrams (Albers Foundation; dedication corroborated by Globalyell, Metropolis, Fiveable). Expanded **David Zwirner Books, 2017** **[R]**: publisher page empty, new material unconfirmed.
- **1966 review [R].** The Albers Foundation page quotes a 1966 review, "in this age where experiment has degenerated into mere concoction", **without naming the publication**. Not used as a stop quote.
- **MIT and awards [A].** *Pictorial Weavings*, Massachusetts Institute of Technology, 1959; AIA **Craftsmanship Medal** 1961; American Craft Council **Gold Medal** 1981 (Wikipedia).
- **Tamarind [A, coordinates corrected].** June Wayne's invitation **1963**, fellow in **1964**, portfolio **Line Involvements** (Wikipedia). Tamarind, founded 1960, stood on **Tamarind Avenue, Hollywood**, near **34.0905, -118.3336**; submitted 34.0736, -118.3499 falls in Beverly Grove, ~2.4 km off.
- **End of loom work [A].** 1970: she stops weaving and gives the looms away (Wikipedia).
- **Foundation [A, coordinates corrected].** Josef and Anni Albers Foundation, **1971**, **Bethany, Connecticut**, 88 Beacon Road, **41.42556, -72.99250** per Wikipedia; submitted 41.4001, -72.9998 is ~2.8 km south.

## 8. Death and afterlife

- **Josef Albers dies 1976 [A]**, the year major exhibitions of her work are held in Germany (Wikipedia). *Gap:* no day or month in anything reached; the file defaults to January and says so.
- **Her death [A], coordinates reconciled.** 9 May 1994, at home in **Orange, Connecticut**, aged 94. Against the pool's two Orange pins (41.2799, -73.0198; 41.2793, -72.9976) and Wikipedia's **41.27944, -73.02528**, the file uses **41.2794, -73.0253** throughout.
- **Gap.** The fate of her remains, whether cremation, burial or scattering, is **not established** by any source consulted: no tomb and no marker in Wikipedia or Albers Foundation materials accessed.
- **Connecticut Women's Hall of Fame [A].** Inducted 1994, the year of her death. **Renwick Gallery retrospective [A]:** Washington, D.C., 1985.
- **Tate Modern [A].** "Anni Albers", **11 October 2018 to 27 January 2019**, with **Kunstsammlung Nordrhein-Westfalen, Düsseldorf**; first major UK exhibition; over **350 objects**, many new to Britain, including *Ancient Writing* (1936) and *Six Prayers* (1966-67), which the campa calls only a six-panel memorial weaving since the pool names no commissioner.
- **Google Doodle [A].** 18 November 2024, marking the 1933 escape rather than her birthday (Wikipedia). Used inside the departure campa, having no place of its own.

## 9. Quotes: what survived verification

1. "In my case it was threads that caught me, really against my will..." **[A at second hand]**: 'Material as Metaphor' (1982), quoted with citation in Wikipedia, **not** checked against the Wesleyan anthology. Used at the Weimar weaving stop with the caveat in `quote_source`.
2. "Weaving is an example of a craft which is many-sided..." **[A, primary]**: scan of the primary printing, 'Work with Material', *Selected Writings on Design* (Wesleyan), p. 9.
3. "And a longing for excitement can be satisfied without external means, within oneself; for creating is the most intense excitement one can come to know." **[A, primary]**: same essay, p. 8. Aggregator versions mispunctuate it.
4. "Why do you paint?" (Kokoschka) **[R]**: about her, not by her; no citation retrievable.
5. "there is no medium that cannot serve art." **[R]**: reported as her position on Wikipedia, no essay or page cited; used at the MoMA stop with paraphrase risk stated.
6. "to my great teachers, the weavers of ancient Peru" **[A]**: dedication, *On Weaving* (1965), triple-corroborated.
7. 1966 review, "in this age where experiment has degenerated into mere concoction" **[R]**: publication unspecified, not used as a stop quote.

**Correction for the record.** The circulating "**textiles** also includes **colour**" is a **Wikipedia-altered variant**; the primary printing reads "**it** also includes **color**", the tells being broken number agreement and British spelling in an American text. Its usual attribution is wrong too: the words are from "**Work with Material**" (1937 reprint, first published *Black Mountain College Bulletin* 1938), not "On Designing", which **collects** it.
