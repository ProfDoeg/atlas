# Cristóbal Balenciaga Eizaguirre (1895-1972): research report

*Prepared 2026-08-08 for the Atlas of Journeys. Getaria to San Sebastián, Madrid, Barcelona, Paris, and back to a hillside cemetery above the Bay of Biscay.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition or inference · contradictions are flagged where they stand, and reasoning that would resolve them is labelled as reasoning, not as a source.

---

## 1. Getaria: the mariner's son (1895 - c. 1912)

**Birth.** Cristóbal Balenciaga Eizaguirre is born in Getaria, a fishing town in Gipuzkoa on the Basque coast, on **21 January 1895** **[A: Wikipedia; Metropolitan Museum of Art essay]**. Coordinate note: the research pool carried Getaria at 43.3049, -2.1998, which sits roughly 310 m east of the town centre, out toward the harbour mouth. Corrected to **43.3044, -2.2036** (en.wikipedia, Getaria) **[A]**.

**Parents.** Father **José Balenciaga Basurto**, a mariner, dies while Cristóbal is still a child **[A: Wikipedia]**; no source reachable in this pass gives the year of the father's death, and the journey file places it before the 1907 apprenticeship for sequence only, with the hedge carried in `date_confidence`. Mother **Martina Eizaguirre Embil** works as a seamstress; the boy watches her sew, and this is his first exposure to garment-making **[A: Wikipedia; thefamouspeople.com]**.

**The Marquesa de Casa Torres.** The Marquesa, a client of his mother, is said to have recognised the boy's talent and financed his formal tailoring training in Madrid. **This arrived in the pool tagged [A] and has been demoted to [R].** The Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa's own account ("Cristóbal Balenciaga: The Early Years," Google Arts & Culture) states only that Martina Eizaguirre sewed **for** the Marquesa and that the boy began learning dressmaking at the Marquesa's **Aldamar Palace in Getaria**. The onward claim about Madrid is repeated across popular biography without contemporary documentation **[R: e.g. Europeana, "Cristóbal Balenciaga, master of couture"]**. The pool's own afterlife lens tagged the same claim [R], so the pool was internally inconsistent.

**Apprenticeship.** Around age twelve, c. **1907**, he begins an apprenticeship with a tailor **[A: Wikipedia]**. The tailor is unnamed in every reachable source. Gap, stated as a gap.

**Madrid training.** Undated in every source found, and its financing is the [R] tradition above. The journey file gives it a sequence date, hedge in `date_confidence`.

## 2. The Spanish houses (1917 - 1937)

**This is the section the incoming pool got wrong.** It asserted, tagged **[A]** and sourced to Wikipedia and wunderlabel.com, that Balenciaga opened his first boutique in **1919** under his mother's name **"Eisa"**. Both halves are wrong.

- **First atelier: 1917, at 2 Calle Vergara, San Sebastián, aged 22** **[A: Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa, "Cristóbal Balenciaga: The Early Years" (Google Arts & Culture); corroborated by the V&A, which dated its 2017 retrospective to the centenary of the first San Sebastián house, and by Forbes (2024)]**.
- **"Eisa" is not the first house's name.** In **March 1927** he opened a second establishment as **"Martina Robes et manteaux"**, renamed **EISA Costura** in **October 1927**, presenting its first collection on **17 September 1927** **[A: same museum source]**.

**Contradiction flagged, not silently dropped:** es.wikipedia carries "1919, named Eisa" and the pool inherited it. The museum's archive and the V&A's centenary dating supersede it, but the erroneous version is in wide circulation. Both are recorded; the journey file follows the museum.

**Expansion.** The San Sebastián house expands with branches in **Madrid and Barcelona** over the following years **[A: Wikipedia]**. No reachable source in this pass dates either branch precisely. Gap.

**The war.** The Spanish Civil War forces the closure of his Spanish shops and Balenciaga relocates to Paris **[A: Wikipedia]**, the move dated **August 1937** **[A: Wikipedia; sansebastianturismoa.eus]**. The date of the Spanish closures themselves is not separately attested in anything reached; the journey file dates the closure stop to 1937 with the hedge in `date_confidence`.

## 3. Avenue George V (1937 - 1950)

**The house.** He opens the House of Balenciaga on **avenue George V**, Paris, in **August 1937** **[A: Wikipedia; sansebastianturismoa.eus]**, at **no. 10** **[A: V&A, "Introducing Cristóbal Balenciaga"]**.

Coordinate note: the pool carried 48.8686, 2.3009 for the salon. Avenue George V runs from Place de l'Alma in the south to 99 Champs-Élysées in the north, numbering from the Alma end (en.wikipedia, Avenue George V). No. 10 therefore lies near the **Alma end, approx. 48.8657, 2.3011**; the pool's pin falls roughly 330 m north, around nos. 30-40. Corrected **[A by inference from the street's numbering; not a surveyed address]**.

Per the interlock lens the **founding** stop inherits the canonical atlas Paris pin **48.8566, 2.3522**, byte-identical across at least 199 stop entries (Barthes, Freud, Proust, Joyce, Klee and others); the address-specific stops use the corrected avenue George V coordinate. The difference between the two is deliberate.

**Gap: the Occupation.** Nothing in this pool covers the Paris house between 1940 and 1944, when the German occupation reshaped every couture house in the city. The journey file makes no claim about those years. It is the largest single hole in the dossier.

**Wladzio d'Attainville.** Balenciaga's business partner and life partner, the Franco-Polish businessman **Wladzio Jaworowski d'Attainville** (b. 1899), dies on **14 December 1948** **[A: Find a Grave memorial 287028387; Euppublishing, "Wladzio d'Attainville and the House of Balenciaga (1924-1948)," *Costume*, 2026]**.

**The all-black mourning collection: [R], probably legend.** The pool tagged as [A] the claim that the collection following d'Attainville's death was designed entirely in black. Period press archives show the collections that followed carried vibrant designs alongside black pieces. **Demoted to [R] and written in the journey file as legend rather than as an event** **[R; the death itself remains [A]]**.

**Sexuality.** He keeps his private life undisclosed throughout his public career; biographers note it was not openly discussed in his lifetime **[R: Wikipedia characterisation, not a dated document]**. The one contemporary voice in the pool is his own dressmaker Elisa Erquiaga, on d'Attainville: "He was very handsome and well-educated and we all knew, but nobody spoke about it in the studio" **[A: V&A Blog, "Balenciaga: Love and Hats!"]**.

## 4. The silhouette loosened (1951 - 1960)

All of the following are **[A: Wikipedia]** unless noted, and none carry a day or month in any reachable source; the journey file defaults each to 01-01, hedge in `date_confidence`.

- **1951**: broadens the shoulders, removes the defined waist.
- **1955**: the tunic dress.
- **1956**: from here he schedules his shows a month after the other couture houses.
- **1957**: the high-waisted "baby doll," a draped cocoon coat, the balloon skirt, and the waistless **sack dress** **[A: thefamouspeople.com; Wikipedia]**. The *Daily Mirror* answers (quoted in the table below).
- **1958**: the chemise dress, another waistless line; and **Knight of the Legion of Honour**.
- **1959**: the Empire line, high-waisted.
- **1960**: the wedding dress worn by **Fabiola de Mora y Aragón** for her marriage to King Baudouin of Belgium, made in Paris and worn in Brussels.

## 5. The master of us all

**Dior's tribute.** The phrase "the Master of us all" is genuine and the pool's citation for it was wrong. The pool cited the V&A article "Introducing Cristóbal Balenciaga"; that page was fetched twice with targeted prompts, the phrase does not appear on it, and it carries only one Dior quotation (the orchestra one). **Correct citable source: V&A Explore The Collections, Opera Coat, museum no. O1435930**, which has Balenciaga's "rival Christian Dior not[ed] that he was the 'Master of us all'" **[A]**; also the title of **Mary Blume, *The Master of Us All* (FSG, 2013)** **[A]**. The remark is undated in everything reached.

**Quotations carried into the journey file, all [A]:**

**Givenchy, upgraded from [R] to [A].** The pool had "Balenciaga taught me everything I know…" as an [R] attribution found only in a search summary. It is carried, fetched and read directly, by **Grazia Magazine, "5 fascinating facts you didn't know about Hubert de Givenchy"** **[A]**. One wording note, recorded rather than smoothed: Grazia prints "that is was not necessary," an evident typo for "that it was," and the journey file quotes it corrected.

**How many interviews.** The pool's afterlife lens said **only one**, contradicting the pool's own chronology lens. He gave **two**: **Paris Match, 1968**, and **The Times, August 1971**, the latter at the insistence of the journalist Prudence Glynn and published as "Balenciaga and la vie d'un chien" **[A: Wikipedia for both; Prospect Magazine states he "gave only two interviews"]**. The **France-Soir, 1962** detail stands: a stolen photograph of him in his white work smock, captioned "This is Balenciaga, the mystery man" **[A: Forbes (2024); Prospect Magazine]**.

## 6. The closing of the house (1968 - 1971)

- **January 1968**: final Paris collection; by then Cardin, Courrèges, Saint Laurent and Ungaro have all moved into ready-to-wear **[A: FIT, "Paris Refashioned, 1957-1968"]**.
- **May 1968**: retirement announced **[A: Wikipedia; sansebastianturismoa.eus]**. His justification, as Wikipedia cites it: "High fashion is mortally wounded." **[A]**
- **1 July 1968**: the Paris headquarters on avenue George V and the Madrid studios close their couture operations; perfume and accessories divisions continue **[A: Wikipedia]**.
- **1970**: between retirement and death he collaborates informally with Spanish prêt-à-porter companies, with no financial investment of his own **[A: Wikipedia]**. The companies are unnamed in the source. Gap.
- **1971**: the second and final interview, to *The Times*, reported from Spain **[A: Wikipedia]**.

## 7. The last month (March 1972)

**The Franco wedding dress.** He comes out of retirement to design the gown worn by **Carmen Martínez-Bordiú**, granddaughter of Francisco Franco, at her wedding on **8 March 1972** at the chapel of El Pardo, Madrid; his final couture **[A: Wikipedia; 24 Horas Mundo]**. The pool said "days before his death." **Corrected: roughly two weeks.**

**The heart attack.** **23 March 1972**, at the **Parador de Jávea** (Xàbia, Alicante), where he was staying with the designer Ramón Esparza **[A: es.wikipedia; Vanidades]**. The pool's chronology lens gave 22 March; that is off by one day.

**The death, and the contradiction that runs through every source.** The pool contained the same conflict twice, once flagged as unresolvable and once resolved, and both versions are recorded here:

- **en.wikipedia and most English-language biographical summaries:** died **24 March 1972**, in **Valencia**, aged 77.
- **es.wikipedia, sansebastianturismoa.eus, elisa-rolle.livejournal.com, IMDb:** **23 March**, and/or **Jávea**.

**The reconciling sequence** (labelled reconstruction, since no single source reached narrates it end to end with citations): heart attack at the Parador de Jávea on 23 March; transfer to the **Sanatorio de la Sagrada Familia** in Valencia; a second attack in intensive care; death in the early hours of **24 March 1972** **[R: Vanidades, "De qué murió Cristóbal Balenciaga," read against es.wikipedia and en.wikipedia]**. On this reading both strands are partly right: 23 March and Jávea describe the collapse, 24 March and Valencia the death. **Age 77 is not in dispute.** The journey file carries the two as separate stops and does not merge them.

**The headline.** On the day of his death *Women's Wear Daily* runs **"The King is Dead"** **[A: WWD, 24 March 1972, quoted in Wikipedia]**.

**Burial.** In the cemetery of Getaria, on the hill above the town **[A: Wikipedia; sansebastianturismoa.eus]**. The plot is **[R: Contingent Magazine (2026); Find a Grave 14602094]**; the coordinate 43.3058, -2.2019 is the cemetery, not a surveyed grave.

## 8. Afterlife (2011 - 2026)

**The museum.** The **Cristóbal Balenciaga Museoa** opens in Getaria adjoining the **Palacio Aldamar**, the first major museum in the world dedicated solely to a couturier **[A: Tourism Euskadi]**, inaugurated by **Queen Sofía of Spain** with **Hubert de Givenchy** present **[A: Wikipedia; spain.info]**.

**Date contradiction, flagged:** **7 June 2011** (es.wikipedia, spain.info, Tourism Euskadi and the museum, which give inauguration and opening as one day) against **10 June 2011** (en.wikipedia, which splits them). The pool also carried an **11 June** variant, attested nowhere in this pass and recorded as unsupported rather than as a third position. The journey file leads with 7 June and the 10 June reading is kept here.

Coordinate note: the museum's own pin is **43.3015, -2.2049**, not the Getaria town centroid the pool used.

**Holdings.** More than **1,200 garments and accessories**, shown in rotation, within an archive of more than **3,000 pieces** including sketches, toiles, fabric swatches, letters and photographs; roughly 90 items are on display at a time **[A: Cristóbal Balenciaga Foundation, via Google Arts & Culture, "Preserving and Sharing a Heritage"]**. The pool's alternative figure of **"over 5,500 items"** is unsupported by anything reachable and has been dropped.

**V&A, 2017.** "Balenciaga: Shaping Fashion," the first UK retrospective, opens **May 2017**, timed to the centenary of the first San Sebastián house and the eightieth anniversary of the Paris salon; about 100 garments and 20 hats, including x-rays of hidden construction, shown alongside work by more than 30 designers he influenced **[A: Dezeen (2017)]**.

**The modern house.** Revived under Nicolas Ghesquière, Alexander Wang, and Demna Gvasalia, the label becomes in **2022** the centre of a widely covered advertising scandal **[A: SSENSE, "The A-Z Guide to Balenciaga"]**.

**Disney+, 2024.** The six-episode Spanish miniseries *Cristóbal Balenciaga* releases **19 January 2024**, Alberto San Juan in the title role, dramatising the Paris years 1937-1968 **[A: Wikipedia, "Cristóbal Balenciaga (TV series)"; IMDb]**.

## 9. Atlas interlocks

**Genuine crossing: roland_barthes.** `roland_barthes.journey.json` carries a stop "Paris, Système de la mode" (1967), where Barthes lays a structuralist grid over the written fashion press **[A: roland_barthes.journey.json]**; its groundwork, *Éléments de sémiologie* (1964-65), applies signifier and signified "not to language but to clothes, food, furniture, cars" **[A: same file]**. Barthes is in Paris 1924-1980, Balenciaga's house 1937-1968. **No meeting is attested anywhere** **[R: co-location only]**. The journey file names Barthes in one campa and links via `suggested_refs`; it invents no encounter.

**Considered and dropped: victoria_ocampo.** Ocampo's francophone Buenos Aires household and her importing of French letters to Argentina through *Sur* **[A: victoria_ocampo.journey.json]** put her in structurally the milieu, South American landed elite oriented toward Paris, that supplied much of Balenciaga's private clientele **[R: class and geography parallel only]**. No source attests her as a client. She is therefore **not** named in any campa and the Buenos Aires pin is not taken as a stop.

**Gaps in the atlas, flagged as gaps:**
- **christian_dior** is queued in QUEUE.md but has no `journey.json`; the man who called Balenciaga the master has no file to cross-reference **[A: QUEUE.md; directory listing]**.
- The **Kaaba** pin (21.4225, 39.8262, from abraham and aishah) and the **Temple Mount** pin (31.778, 35.2354, from flaubert, constantino, ibn_arabi, joffrey_bourlemont) were searched for Balenciaga relevance and have none. Not forced.
- **False positive, flagged:** the corpus's other "San Sebastián" hits are **San Sebastián de La Gomera** (`columbus.journey.json`) and the **San Sebastián colony in Urabá**, Colombia (`pizarro.journey.json`). Neither is the Basque city.
- Madrid recurs via `sarmiento.journey.json` and `serrano.journey.json`, neither of which contains Balenciaga content; the Madrid pin is used here on its own merits.
