# Jean-Baptiste Joseph Fourier (1768-1830): research report
*2026-08-09. Slug `joseph_fourier`.*

Legend: **[A]** = attested, source named · **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition · Contradictions are flagged and left standing, not adjudicated.

The spine of the reachable record is the MacTutor (St Andrews) biography, the English and French Wikipedia articles, and François Arago's éloge of 1830 (1857 English translation, Project Gutenberg #16775), the only near-contemporary narrative source reached. Arago knew Fourier as a colleague at the Academy: a witness, and a eulogist, and both facts are carried forward here.

---

## 1. Auxerre: the tailor's orphan (1768-1787)

- Born at Auxerre, Burgundy, **21 March 1768**, son of a tailor. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia EN/FR]**
- Orphaned by about age nine or ten, both parents dying in succession. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]** The intermediate step before the Benedictines is named differently in the two encyclopedias: EN Wikipedia has a "Convent of St Mark," FR Wikipedia a *pensionnat* kept by the organist **Joseph Pallais**. **Both traditions are recorded; neither is preferred here.**
- Recommended to the **Bishop of Auxerre**, who secures him a place among the Benedictines. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia FR]**
- Enters the **École royale militaire of Auxerre**, Benedictine-administered, **1780**; studies literature first, turns to mathematics at thirteen or fourteen. **[A: MacTutor]** (FR Wikipedia tags the school's precise 1780s campus as unconfirmed; the geography lens flagged its coordinates as approximate to the historic Benedictine quarter. **[R]**)
- **1783**: first prize for a study of **Bossut**'s work on mechanics. **[A: MacTutor]**

**Gap.** The birth house at Auxerre is identified by no source reached; town-centre coordinates (47.7982, 3.5697) anchor the stop.

## 2. The abbey and the first Paris paper (1787-1793)

- **1787**: enters the Benedictine abbey of **Saint-Benoît-sur-Loire** to train for the priesthood; teaches mathematics to younger students there. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia FR]**
- **Coordinate correction.** The pool gave Saint-Benoît-sur-Loire as 47.7908 / 2.2911, some 2.5 km off in open country southwest of the commune. EN Wikipedia gives **47.8103 / 2.3067**; the journey file uses the corrected pair. **[A: Wikipedia EN]**
- **1789**: leaves without taking vows; the dissolution of the religious orders returns him to civilian life. Visits Paris and reads a paper on the solution of **algebraic (numerical) equations** to the Académie royale des sciences. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1790**: teacher at the École royale militaire of Auxerre, the school of his own boyhood. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1793**: joins the local **Revolutionary Committee**. MacTutor quotes his own letter on "the sublime hope of establishing among us a free government." **[A: MacTutor, quoting Fourier]**

## 3. The Terror and the two arrests (1794-1795)

- **July 1794**: arrested on charges connected to the **Orléans incident** and imprisoned; expects the guillotine. **[A: MacTutor]** The geography lens dates the arrest **4 July 1794** **[R: Wikipedia EN/FR]**; MacTutor gives only the month. *Contradiction of precision, not of substance; left open.*
- Released after **Robespierre's execution** (9 Thermidor, 27 July 1794) and the political reversal that followed. **[A: MacTutor]**
- Nominated to the newly founded **École normale**, which opens in Paris **20 January 1795** and closes **19 May 1795**; teachers include **Lagrange, Laplace, Monge**. Sessions in the amphitheatre of the Muséum d'histoire naturelle at the Jardin des Plantes. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia FR]**
- **Second arrest and imprisonment, 1795** (FR Wikipedia: **7 June 1795**). **The cause of his release is disputed.** MacTutor explicitly records that sources disagree: the pleas of his pupils; the intervention of **Lagrange, Laplace, or Monge**; or simply a further shift in the political climate. FR Wikipedia names Lagrange and Monge. **Flagged, not resolved. [R]**
- **1795**: begins teaching at the **Collège de France** and is appointed at the **École centrale des travaux publics**, renamed **École polytechnique**. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1797**: succeeds **Lagrange** in the chair of analysis and mechanics. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**

**Correction.** The pool dated the school's move from the Palais Bourbon quarter to the Collège de Navarre site to 1805; FR Wikipedia dates it **1804**. Coordinates (48.8608, 2.3183) are correct for the seat of 1794-1804.

## 4. Egypt (1798-1801)

- **19 May 1798**: embarks at **Toulon** with Bonaparte's fleet as a scientific adviser among the *savants*. **[A: Wikipedia FR; MacTutor]**
- **10-19 June 1798**: the fleet takes **Malta** en route. **[A: MacTutor for the fleet]** Fourier's personal presence ashore is inferred from his passage with the convoy, not attested by name. **[R]**
- **1-2 July 1798**: the army lands near **Marabout**, west of Alexandria, on 1 July; **Alexandria falls on 2 July**. **[A: Wikipedia EN, "French campaign in Egypt and Syria"]**
  - **Correction.** The pool claimed "French forces take Alexandria" on 1 July, conflating landing with capture; MacTutor's looser phrasing does not support it. A second pool note preserved a "28 June" infobox contradiction that the current EN article no longer carries. Both hedges dropped.
- **21 July 1798**: **Battle of the Pyramids** at Imbaba; French entry into **Cairo** 22-24 July. Fourier travels with the savant corps; his presence at the battle itself is **not separately attested [R]**.
- **1 August 1798**: **Battle of the Nile**, Aboukir Bay. Nelson destroys the French fleet and strands the expedition. **[A: MacTutor]** *Coordinates (31.3167, 30.0667) supplied by the writer; the pool carried none for this event.*
- **22-23 August 1798**: the **Institut d'Égypte** is founded in the palace of **Hassan Kashif**; first session 23 August with **Monge president, Bonaparte vice-president, Fourier secretary, Louis Costaz assistant secretary (*secrétaire-adjoint*)**. **[A: Wikipedia FR, "Institut d'Égypte"]**
  - **Correction.** The pool made Costaz a co-equal named "alongside" Fourier. He was the assistant secretary.
  - MacTutor separately records Fourier as one of **twelve members of the mathematics division**, with Monge, Malus, and Napoleon. **[A]**
- **November 1798 onward**: charged with collating the Institut's sectional reports, the seed of the **Description de l'Égypte**; writes its **Préface historique**, of which **Louis de Fontanes** said it united the graces of Athens and the wisdom of Egypt. **[A: Wikipedia EN; Arago]**
- **19 July 1799**: **Pierre-François Bouchard** finds the Rosetta Stone at Fort Julien. **[A: `pierre_francois_bouchard.journey.json`]** **No source reached states that Fourier examined the stone personally.** A gap; he is placed only as officer of the institution that received the find.
- **1799**: Upper Egypt survey with **Louis Costaz**, attested in general terms by FR Wikipedia. The individual sites (**Aswan**, **Philae**, **Karnak**, **Dendera**) are **[R]**, reconstructed from the Description's itinerary; his presence at each is not separately attested.
- **c. 14 June 1800**: **Kléber** is assassinated at Cairo and Fourier delivers the funeral oration, an extract of which Arago prints. **[A: Arago]** The date of delivery is not established. Arago also reports Bonaparte's remark on the epithet "illustrious" applied to Kléber in the Préface historique. **[A: Arago]**
- **May-June 1801**: the **Cairo garrison capitulates** (under Belliard) and is evacuated; the savants leave with it. **[A: Wikipedia EN]**
- **2 September 1801**: **Menou** signs the capitulation of **Alexandria**. **[A]**
  - **Correction.** Two pool entries placed Fourier here and tied his return to Menou. MacTutor never mentions Menou, and the Cairo savants had sailed two to three months earlier. His presence at Alexandria is **not attested** and is flagged on the stop.

## 5. Return and the prefecture of the Isère (1801/1802-1815)

- **Year of return disputed.** MacTutor and EN Wikipedia: **1801**. FR Wikipedia: **1802**. **Left standing.** The port of landing is named by no source reached; Toulon is used as a placeholder pin because it was the port of departure. **[R]**
- **12 February 1802**: Napoleon names him **prefect of the Isère** at Grenoble. **[A: Wikipedia FR: "Le 12 février 1802, Napoléon le nomme préfet de l'Isère."]**
  - **Correction.** The pool's two lenses contradicted each other, 1801 against 12 February 1802. The documented date is used and the older tradition noted.
  - **End date corrected**: the pool said "until 1814." He held Isère into **1815**, moving to the Rhône during the Hundred Days and being removed **17 May 1815**. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**
- **Champollion.** **Jean-François Champollion** (b. 23 December 1790) reached Grenoble **27 March 1801** to live with his elder brother Jacques-Joseph. **[A: Wikipedia EN]**
  - **Correction.** Two pool entries called him eleven at the meeting; on 27 March 1801 he was **ten**.
  - The anecdote of the visit, the collection, and the boy's declaration is **[R]**: EN Wikipedia flags its truth as uncertain ("Whether or not the report of this visit is true").
  - The **alliance** is not in doubt. Arago states that Fourier kept Champollion out of conscription by investing him with the title of pupil of the School of Oriental Languages. **[A: Arago]**
- **1810**: founds the **Imperial Faculty (university) of Grenoble** and becomes its rector; hosts evening gatherings with Champollion at the **Hôtel de Lesdiguières**. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**
  - **Correction.** The pool called this an "Imperial Academy of Grenoble"; FR Wikipedia has "la Faculté (université) Impériale de Grenoble."
- **Public works.** Draining the **marshes of Bourgoin** **[A: MacTutor, verbatim]**; and a new highway out of Grenoble.
  - **Contradiction, not resolved.** MacTutor: "a new highway from Grenoble to Turin." FR Wikipedia: the road "reliant Grenoble à Briançon par le col du Lautaret." The pool's "Mont Cenis" detail is unsourced and struck.
- **c. 1804-1807**: the experimental and theoretical work on the propagation of heat in solids. **[A: MacTutor: "His work on the topic began around 1804."]** The pool's geography lens dated the experiments 1802-1807; corrected to 1804.
- **1807**: memoir *On the Propagation of Heat in Solid Bodies* completed. **[A]**
- **21 December 1807**: read at the Paris Institute; committee of **Lagrange, Laplace, Monge, Lacroix**. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1808**: **Lagrange and Laplace object** to the trigonometric series expansions. **[A: MacTutor]** The memoir is not published at the time.

## 6. The Hundred Days (1815)

- **March 1815**: Napoleon lands at **Golfe-Juan** (43.5661, 7.0839, per `napoleon.journey.json`) and marches north through the mountains toward Grenoble. **[A]**
- Fourier, who had attempted to organize opposition, **flees Grenoble** as Napoleon's forces enter. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia FR]** `napoleon.journey.json` records the march but not the flight; that gap is filled here from Fourier's own sources.
- **1815**: appointed **prefect of the Rhône** at Lyon during the Hundred Days. **[A: Wikipedia FR]** The date of appointment is not given in any source reached. **Gap.**
- **17 May 1815**: removed from the prefecture. **[A: Wikipedia FR]** The reason is not stated in the sources reached. **Gap.**
- **10 June 1815**: Napoleon grants him a pension of **6000 francs**, never received after the final defeat. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1815**: named director of the **Bureau of Statistics of the Seine**. **[A: Wikipedia EN]** Exact address not established; central Paris coordinates used. **Gap.**

## 7. Paris, the Academy, and the book (1817-1830)

- **1817**: elected to the **Académie des sciences**, seated at the Palais de l'Institut on the Quai Conti, after an earlier admission had been blocked on political grounds. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**
- **1822**: on **Delambre**'s death, elected **Permanent Secretary** of the mathematical section. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia]**
- **1822**: *Théorie analytique de la chaleur* printed at Paris by **Firmin Didot Père et Fils**, the printing arranged by Delambre before his death. **[A: MacTutor; Wikipedia EN]**
- **1824**: publishes the argument that the atmosphere raises the Earth's surface temperature above what direct radiation alone would give, strengthened by **Claude Pouillet** in 1827 and 1838. The term **greenhouse effect** is coined by **Nils Gustaf Ekholm** only in **1901**. **[A: Wikipedia EN, "Greenhouse effect"]**
- **1822-1830**: priority disputes with **Biot** and **Poisson** over the heat theory. **[A: MacTutor]**
- **1830**: elected foreign member of the **Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences**. **[A: Wikipedia EN]** *No Stockholm stop: no coordinates supplied, and he did not travel there.*
- **4 May 1830**: falls down a flight of stairs, aggravating a heart aneurism he had suffered from since Egypt. **[A: Arago, verbatim; Wikipedia EN]**
- **16 May 1830**: dies at **19 rue d'Enfer**, Paris, aged sixty-two. **[A: Wikipedia FR]** The street was later absorbed into Boulevard Saint-Michel and rue Henri-Barbusse; coordinates (48.846, 2.339) approximate it. **[R for the pin]**
- Last words as recorded by Arago: a call for vinegar. **[A: Arago]**

**Unconfirmed tradition, carried as [R].** That Fourier believed heat necessary to health after Egypt, kept his rooms overheated, and wrapped himself heavily. Widely repeated; **not found in MacTutor or in any Wikipedia article checked**. Arago's aneurism passage is the attested fact and does not mention the habit.

## 8. Afterlife

- Buried at **Père Lachaise, Division 18**, under a monument with **Egyptian motifs**. **[A: Wikipedia EN for the motifs, FR for the division]**
  - **Coordinate discrepancy inside the pool**: afterlife lens 48.8614 / 2.3931, geography lens 48.8606 / 2.3928, some 90 m apart. The afterlife pair is used, the discrepancy recorded rather than averaged. The tomb inscription was not recovered. **Gap.**
- **2 July 1830**, six weeks after the funeral: **C. G. J. Jacobi** writes to **Legendre** from Königsberg the letter that makes Fourier the foil in the argument over what mathematics is for.
  - **Correction.** The pool carried an unsourced English paraphrase. The French original is verified in Jacobi, *Gesammelte Werke* Bd. I, letter of 2 July 1830, pp. 454-455 (archive.org/details/gesammeltewerkeh01jacouoft). The journey file uses MacTutor's attested rendering (after N. Rose, 1988), keeping the antecedent clause about Fourier, without which the sentence loses its target.
- **1849**: bronze statue erected at **Auxerre**; **melted down during the Occupation** in a wartime metal drive (exact year not specified). **[R: Wikipedia FR]** Replaced by a simple **medallion**.
- **2012**: the **Centre de culture scientifique, technique et industrielle de Bourgogne** launches a national subscription for a replacement statue. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**
  - **Correction.** The pool guessed "Cap Sciences," the CCSTI of Bordeaux and Nouvelle-Aquitaine, a different region. Struck.
  - Whether a new statue now stands at Auxerre is **not established** by any source reached. **Gap.**
- **1889**: name inscribed among the **72 names on the Eiffel Tower**. **[A: Wikipedia EN/FR]** The interlock lens gives **48.8583, 2.2945** and the afterlife lens 48.8584; the interlock pair is inherited byte-identical and the one-digit divergence noted.
- **1935**: the lunar crater **Fourier** (Mare Humorum region) adopted by the IAU. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**
- **1878**: **Alexander Freeman**'s English translation, Cambridge; **1888**: corrected French re-edition by **Gaston Darboux**. **[A: Wikipedia EN]** All Fourier quotations in the journey file come from Freeman, which is noted to carry editorial corrections: this is Freeman's English, not Fourier's French.
- **Université Joseph Fourier**, Grenoble, until the **2015** merger into Université Grenoble Alpes. **[A: Wikipedia FR]**

**Not reached (named with reason)**
- Fourier's *Préface historique* to the *Description de l'Égypte*: no accessible full text located. Arago's report of Fontanes's remark on it is the only handle used.
- The original French of the 1807 memoir and the referees' report: not located; MacTutor's summary of the Lagrange and Laplace objection is the basis.
- Isère prefectural records for the Bourgoin drainage and the Grenoble road: not consulted. The projects rest on MacTutor and FR Wikipedia, which disagree about the road's destination.
- J. Herivel, *Joseph Fourier: The Man and the Physicist* (1975): not reached. The obvious next source for the 1801/1802 return, the Lyon appointment date, and the overheated-rooms tradition, all three still open.
- Tomb inscription at Père Lachaise: no transcription found.

**Open contradictions carried into the journey file (in `date_confidence`, never in prose)**
1. Return from Egypt: 1801 (MacTutor, EN) vs 1802 (FR).
2. Prefecture appointment: 1801 (MacTutor, EN) vs 12 February 1802 (FR, documented).
3. The Grenoble highway: to Turin (MacTutor) vs to Briançon by the col du Lautaret (FR).
4. Cause of the 1795 release: pupils, or Lagrange/Laplace/Monge, or the political climate (MacTutor records the disagreement itself).
5. Auxerre schooling: "Convent of St Mark" (EN) vs *pensionnat* Pallais (FR).
6. Père Lachaise coordinates: 48.8614/2.3931 vs 48.8606/2.3928 within the pool.
7. Eiffel Tower coordinates: 48.8583 (interlock, inherited) vs 48.8584 (afterlife lens).
