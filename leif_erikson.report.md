# Leif Erikson: research report

*2026-08-02. Leifr Eiriksson, called heppni, "the Lucky." Born probably in Iceland c. 970, died in Greenland between 1018 and 1025. Artifact: `leif_erikson.journey.json`, 9 segments, 44 stops.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, inference, or a coordinate placed on argument rather than on text. Contradictions between the two principal sources are flagged where they arise and are **not adjudicated**; this subject's whole difficulty is that the canon is doubled.

---

## Method, and the shape of the evidence

There are two medieval accounts of Leif Erikson and they are not two versions of one story. They are two stories about the same man, composed in the same century on the same island, disagreeing at the load-bearing points.

- **Groenlendinga saga** survives **only inside Flateyjarbok**, compiled 1387-1394, **cut into two episodes and interpolated into the Saga of Olaf Tryggvason** **[A]**. It has Bjarni Herjolfsson, Tyrker, the horse-fall, Leifsbudir, Freydis with the axe.
- **Eiriks saga rauda** survives in **Hauksbok** (14th c.) and **Skalholtsbok** (15th c.), both from a **thirteenth-century original** **[A, Wikipedia]**. It has the storm-driven discovery, Thorgunna, Straumfjord, Hop.

Both are **thirteenth-century compositions about events of c. 1000**, drawing on two centuries of oral transmission **[A]**. The repeated window "c. 1200 to 1400" is **wrong and was corrected out**: the later date belongs to the Flateyjarbok *manuscript*, not to composition. Against all this stands one piece of physical evidence, **L'Anse aux Meadows**: Norse, North American, dated, and mentioning Leif Erikson nowhere, because nothing at it mentions anybody.

## Phase 1: The outlawries, c. 950 to 985

- **[R]** Erik the Red's father **Thorvald Asvaldsson** farms at **Jaeren** until a killing forces the family to Iceland; he settles at **Drangar** on Hornstrandir, where Erik grows up (Landnamabok; Eiriks saga rauda ch. 1). No year given. The artifact pins Drangar and folds Jaeren into its campa.
- **[R]** Erik marries **Thjodhild** and builds **Eiriksstadir** in Haukadalur; tradition holds Leif was born there. **No saga states Leif's birthplace or birth year.** c. 970 is a scholarly estimate; Eiriksstadir rests on local tradition and the reconstructed farm. The date_confidence says plainly that the birth is unrecorded.
- **[A]** Erik kills **Eyjolf Saur and Hrafn the Dueller** near Vatnshorn over borrowed bench-boards, then **the sons of Thorgest at Oxney** over the boards again (Eiriks saga rauda ch. 2).
- **[A]** The **Thorsnes assembly** outlaws Erik for three years, 982. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool gave 64.833 / -23.35, on the **south** coast of Snaefellsnes, 35-40 km from the district in question. Thorsnes is the north-coast headland at Stykkisholmur; the thing site near Helgafell is approximately **65.03 / -22.79**, used by the artifact.
- **[R]** Erik sails west from Ondverdarnes, following the sighting attributed to **Gunnbjorn Ulfsson**. Departure point inferred.

## Phase 2: Greenland, 982 to 999

- **[A]** Erik winters three times in **Eiriksfjord**, returns to Iceland in 985, and names the country **Greenland** to attract settlers (Eiriks saga rauda ch. 2).
- **[A]** 986: Erik leads a settler fleet and founds **Brattahlid**; Leif, a child, is raised there. Confirmed at **Qassiarsuk**. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool repeated **61.1667 / -45.4167** at four points; Wikipedia 'Qassiarsuk' gives **61.150 / -45.517**, about 6 km southwest, used at every Brattahlid stop.
- **[A]** **Bjarni Herjolfsson**, sailing to join his father at **Herjolfsnes**, is blown past Greenland and sights three unknown coasts without landing (Groenlendinga saga ch. 2-3). *Coordinate corrected* to **59.983 / -44.700**; the supplied 60.0167 was 3.7 km too far north. **The episode does not exist in Eiriks saga rauda.**
- **[A, one saga only]** **Tyrker**, a thrall called German, is Leif's **foster-father**. *Source corrected:* the pool assigned this to Eiriks saga rauda, but Tyrker appears **only in Groenlendinga saga** and is wholly absent from the other. The most intimate relationship of Leif's childhood is missing from half the record, and the campa says so.

## Phase 3: Norway and the king, 999 to 1000

- **[A, both sagas]** Leif sails to Norway, enters **Olaf Tryggvason's** hird at **Nidaros**, and is **baptized at court** (Groenlendinga saga ch. 5; Eiriks saga rauda ch. 5). The one stretch of the life where the sagas agree.
- **[A, date corrected]** Olaf commissions Leif to carry Christianity to Greenland. The pool dated this flatly to **1000**; **Olaf died 9 September 1000 at Svolder**, and the commission must precede Leif's sailing. The artifact dates it **999 / winter 999-1000** and states the constraint in date_confidence.
- **Quote refused, flagged.** The pool carried a direct quotation: *"I believe it would be difficult to carry this mission to a successful issue in Greenland."* **This is not direct speech.** Olson (PG #18571) reports it as narration: "Leif replied that the king should decide, but gave it as his belief that it would be difficult..." The quotation marks and the "I believe" opener convert indirect discourse into speech. Dropped from the artifact; the campa renders it as reported speech.
- **[R, one saga only]** Storm-bound on the return, Leif is delayed in the **Hebrides** and has an affair with **Thorgunna**, who bears **Thorgils Leifsson**. Eiriks saga rauda ch. 5 only. Eyrbyggja saga knows a Thorgils Leifsson independently: weak corroboration of the son, none of the affair. No island is named; the coordinate marks the Outer Hebrides.

## Phase 4: The voyage west, 1000 to 1001

### The primary contradiction, not resolved

| | Eiriks saga rauda | Groenlendinga saga |
|---|---|---|
| How Vinland is found | Leif **blown off course** from Norway | **Bjarni sights it first**; Leif buys his ship |
| Intent | accident | expedition |
| Bjarni, Tyrker | absent | central |
| Named base | Straumfjord | Leifsbudir |

Scholars do not reconcile these and **treat them as competing traditions [R]**. The artifact gives the fork its own stop, with a mid-passage coordinate declared to correspond to no event either saga locates.

### The Groenlendinga itinerary

- **[A]** Leif buys Bjarni's ship, crews it with **thirty-five men**, asks Erik to lead; **Erik falls from his horse** and turns back. Absent from the other saga entirely.
- **[R]** **Helluland** (flat rock, glaciers), tentatively **Baffin Island**; **Markland** (forest), tentatively the **Labrador coast**. Namings attested; identifications are consensus, not text; coordinates approximate at the scale of a coast.
- **[R, cross-saga]** **Furdustrandir**, the Wonder Strands. *Flagged:* the name is given in **Eiriks saga rauda ch. 8**, where it belongs to **Karlsefni's** later voyage, and Groenlendinga saga never applies it to Leif. The artifact states the divergence rather than assigning the naming.
- **[R]** An island (possibly Belle Isle) and a cape (possibly Cape Bauld). Dropped as a separate stop; folded into the landfall campa.

## Phase 5: Leifsbudir, and the grape problem

- **[A]** Landfall at a headland with a river running out of a lake; **large timber houses** built and named **Leifsbudir**; salmon larger than the crew have seen. **Tyrker** then wanders off and returns having found **grapevines**, which gives Vinland its name (Groenlendinga saga ch. 5).
- **[A, wording corrected]** The winter. The pool claimed the saga says **no snow falls**. It does not. Groenlendinga saga says there was **no frost**, the grass barely withered, and livestock needed neither fodder nor housing. "No snow" is an embellishment absent from the text; removed from the campa, which states the frost claim and names the embellishment as one.

**The grape problem, the strongest argument against the site identification.** The pool proposed a pin at **51.55 / -55.6** labelled "wild grape and wheat grounds near Vinland camp." **Refused.** Wild grapes do not grow at 51.5 N in northern Newfoundland and have not in the Holocene. The physical evidence agrees: **butternuts and butternut-worked wood** were excavated at the site, and butternut's range coincides with wild grape's, in the **Gulf of St Lawrence and New Brunswick**, several hundred kilometres southwest. The artifact places a **single [R] pin in the Gulf of St Lawrence** (47.9 / -65.5), its date_confidence stating that no saga names that coast, no Norse structure has been found on it, and the pin rests on botanical range alone.

## Phase 6: Leif the Lucky, and the return

- **[A, one saga only, two corrections]** On the return Leif rescues shipwrecked people and earns the byname **heppni**. *Wrong saga:* the pool assigned the named-Thorir rescue to Eiriks saga rauda, but per Wikipedia that saga "doesn't mention Thorir at all, while the Saga of the Greenlanders explicitly names him." *Wrong nationality and person:* Thorir is a **Norwegian merchant**, not an Icelandic castaway, and **Gudrid is his wife**, not the merchant. Fifteen were taken off a **skerry**; Thorir died at Brattahlid that winter.
- **[A]** Leif preaches Christianity in Greenland; **Thjodhild** converts and builds Greenland's first church; **Erik refuses baptism**. Ruin excavated at Qassiarsuk. *Coordinate corrected:* the pool's 61.1626 / -45.4183 sits 5-6 km outside the excavated area; the church lies within the Qassiarsuk complex, a couple of hundred metres from the farm mound, and the artifact uses 61.1512 / -45.5158.
- **[R]** Erik dies "not long after 1000"; **1003 is convention, not record**. Leif succeeds as paramount chief **[A]** and makes no further Vinland voyage.

## Phase 7: The houses lent out, 1004 to 1013

- **[A]** **Thorvald Eriksson** borrows ship and houses, is killed by Skraelings, and is buried at a headland he names **Krossanes**: first Norseman recorded dead in North America (Groenlendinga saga ch. 7). Never located; the coordinate is an [R] placeholder.
- **[A, kinship error corrected]** **Thorfinn Karlsefni** leads the largest colonizing attempt. The pool called him Leif's **brother-in-law** while calling Gudrid Leif's sister-in-law in the same sentence. Wikipedia has him "a wealthy Icelandic merchant unrelated to Leif"; he married Gudrid, widow of Leif's brother **Thorstein Eiriksson**, and that is his only tie. "Brother-in-law" dropped.
- *Contradiction flagged:* the pool merged **Straumfjord** and **Leifsbudir** into one pin. Groenlendinga saga has Karlsefni **borrow Leif's houses at Leifsbudir**; Eiriks saga rauda has him **found Straumfjord**. The artifact keeps one pin, names both traditions, and does not choose.
- **[R]** **Hop**, the southern camp with the tidal lagoon and self-sown wheat, is in **Eiriks saga rauda ch. 11-12 only** and has never been identified. Coordinate is a guess.
- **[A, one saga only, kinship contradiction flagged]** **Freydis Eiriksdottir** leads a final expedition ending in a massacre of her Norse partners, killing five women herself. The pool called her Leif's **half-sister** while citing Groenlendinga saga; that saga calls her his **full sister**, and it is **Eiriks saga rauda** that makes her Erik's illegitimate daughter. The claim contradicted its own source. Both terms are given in date_confidence. Leif then has three of her people tortured for the truth and declines to punish her: his **last narrated act** in the Vinland material.

---

## Phase 8: The silence

- **[A]** Leif is mentioned alive and chief in Greenland in the **Saga of Saint Olaf**, c. 1018: the last textual reference to him living. By **1025** his son **Thorkell Leifsson** holds the chieftaincy, implying the father is dead.
- **[R]** **No saga records the date, manner, or place of Leif's death, and no grave is identified anywhere.** Scholars place it between 1018 and 1025. The artifact states this as a gap, in a stop whose subject is the absence.
- **[A, date corrected]** **Gardar** (Igaliku) becomes Greenland's ecclesiastical seat. The pool dated this "11th c." and glossed it "not long after" Leif's mission, contradicting its own note. Per Wikipedia the bishopric was petitioned by **Sokki Thorisson** in the early **twelfth** century; **Arnaldur** was ordained **1124** and arrived **1126**, roughly 125 years after Leif. Coordinate 60.9871 / -45.4229 checks out.

## Phase 9: Afterlife

Sagas composed 13th c., Flateyjarbok compiled 1387-1394: see Method. Thereafter:

- **1477 [A]** Columbus sails a hundred leagues beyond Thule and records that Iceland's harbours still carry talk of lands to the west. His own note, via Ferdinand Columbus, *Historie* ch. 4.
- **1841 / 1890 [A, framing and date corrected]** Beamish, *The Discovery of America by the Northmen*, London: "first English translation" is overstated, since Wikipedia calls it "a summary of the researches of Professor Carl Christian Rafn," i.e. of *Antiquitates Americanae* (1837), and **Toulmin Smith's 1839** volume of nearly identical title preceded it. Reeves, *The Finding of Wineland the Good*, is **1890**; the 1895 printing is the posthumous second edition. Storm's 1891 critical edition is uncorroborated and not carried into the artifact.
- **1887 [A]** Anne Whitney's Boston bronze, paid for by **Eben Norton Horsford**'s campaign to prove Vinland lay on the Charles River, unsupported by archaeology. The Milwaukee casting is **1887**, not 1893. Chicago (Humboldt Park, 1901) and St Paul / Duluth were cut for stop budget.
- **1930 / 1932 [A, anachronism corrected]** Calder's Leif, gifted for the Althing millennium, unveiled 17 July 1932 on **Skolavorduholt**. It cannot have been "placed before Hallgrimskirkja in 1930": that church was begun 1945 and rose behind a statue already standing.
- **1960-1968 [A]** Helge Ingstad, guided by resident **George Decker**, and archaeologist **Anne Stine Ingstad** uncover eight (possibly nine) Norse buildings at L'Anse aux Meadows. Historic Site 1968; UNESCO 1978.
- **1962 / 1929 / 1964 [A]** Werner's Seattle bronze, **moved to Shilshole Bay Marina 2007** at 47.67986 / -122.40595 (the pool gave "20th century" and a point 450 m off); cut for stop budget. Leif Erikson Day: Wisconsin 1929, federal proclamation from 1964, not a public holiday.
- **1957-2021 [A, dates corrected]** The Vinland Map: surfaced 1957; unveiled by Yale **11 October 1965**, the day before Columbus Day (the pool said 10 October; the holiday fell on the 12th); 1972 McCrone finds anatase, a 1920s titanium dioxide pigment, in the ink; Yale's 2018 symposium calls it a forgery, formal announcement **1 September 2021**.
- **2021 [A]** A *Nature* study uses the carbon-14 signature of the **993 CE solar storm** to date felled wood at the site to **1021**, the only securely dated European presence in the Americas before Columbus. **[R]** Whether the site is the saga's **Leifsbudir** or a later camp **remains disputed**, and **no artifact there names Leif Erikson**.

## Contradictions carried forward, not resolved

1. **How Vinland was found**: accident (Eiriks saga rauda) versus deliberate expedition after Bjarni (Groenlendinga saga).
2. **Bjarni Herjolfsson** and **Tyrker**: central to Groenlendinga saga, nonexistent in the other.
3. **Thorir's rescue**: Groenlendinga saga only; Eiriks saga rauda does not mention Thorir.
4. **Freydis's kinship**: full sister (Groenlendinga saga) versus half-sister (Eiriks saga rauda).
5. **Karlsefni's base**: Leifsbudir versus Straumfjord.
6. **Furdustrandir**: named on Karlsefni's voyage in one saga, not applied to Leif in the other.
7. **Hop**, and **Thorgunna with the Hebridean son**: Eiriks saga rauda only.
8. **Leifsbudir and L'Anse aux Meadows**: the identification is inference; the grape evidence argues against it while the butternut evidence argues for contact with grape country.

---

## Honest gaps

- **Birth**: no year, month, or place in any saga. Everything in the artifact's first stops is [R]. **Death**: no year, manner, or place, and no grave; the bound 1018-1025 rests on the son's tenure alone.
- **Appearance and character**: one narrator's sentence in Gathorne-Hardy. Nothing else.
- **Locations**: Leifsbudir's identification is unproven, and Krossanes, Hop, Straumfjord, and Furdustrandir are unlocated. Four stops carry approximate coordinates and say so.
- **Physical evidence naming Leif**: none. Not an inscription, not an object, not a bone.
- **The Norse sources were read in translation only.** No manuscript or facsimile was consulted; chapter references follow the translations below.
