# Marian Rejewski (1905-1980): research report
*2026-08-30. Prepared for `marian_rejewski.journey.json`. Slug: `marian_rejewski`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim surviving only through an intermediary I could not open. Contradictions are flagged where they stand and adjudicated only where a primary document settles them. Where two dates compete, the file carries the first value given below and records the second in that stop's `date_confidence`, never in the campa prose.

## 1. Bromberg, 1905-1923

- Born **16 August 1905** at **ul. Wileńska 6, Bydgoszcz** (then Bromberg, Prussian partition), youngest of seven children of the tobacco merchant **Józef Rejewski** and **Matylda née Thoms**. **[A: marian-rejewski.pl kalendarium and pl.wikipedia for the address; MacTutor for parents and birth order.]**
- Father **died 1 June 1921** **[A: en.wikipedia, citing Polish biographical sources; no civil register seen]**. Nothing about the family's circumstances afterwards appears in any reachable source; the file states that gap inside the campa.
- **Königliches Gymnasium** of Bromberg, Polish state classical gymnasium after the town passed to Poland in 1920, **graduated 1923** **[A: MacTutor]**. The **1914 entry year** is **[R]**, inferred from ordinary gymnasium age.

## 2. Poznań and Göttingen, 1923-1932

- **University of Poznań** from 1923, **magister filozofii 1 March 1929**, thesis on **doubly periodic functions of the second and third kinds** **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **Secret cryptology course** for German-speaking mathematics students **began 15 January 1929** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Sources disagree on its end, 22 March against May: **not resolved**, and immaterial, because Rejewski **left before it concluded** for the Göttingen scholarship **[A: marian-rejewski.pl, "1929 lato-1930 lato"]**. **Ciężki** as one of the teachers **[A: MacTutor]**.
- **Göttingen, actuarial statistics, summer 1929 to summer 1930** **[A: MacTutor, marian-rejewski.pl]**. No arrival date exists in anything reached; the file sets 1929-10-01 and flags it. The reported disappointment with the instruction is MacTutor's report of his own view: **[A]** as a report, **[R]** as to what he meant.
- **1930-1932**: assistant to **Zdzisław Krygowski** **[A: MacTutor]** while working part time at the Cipher Bureau's **Poznań outpost** on German hand ciphers **[A: en.wikipedia]**. The twelve-hours-a-week figure and the cellar under the military command are **[R]**.

## 3. Warsaw, 1932-1939

- **1 September 1932**: joins **BS-4**, German section of the Cipher Bureau, in the **Saxon Palace** **[A: en.wikipedia, marian-rejewski.pl]**. **October 1932**: given the military Enigma alone, in a separate room **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **The French documents. Contradiction, not resolved.** en.wikipedia gives **9-10 December 1932**, the dossier **8 December** citing Rejewski's recollection, and his own published account as printed by Christensen says "quite unexpectedly on 9 December 1932". The file uses **9 December**, because that is what the quoted primary text says. Provenance, Bertrand's service sourced from **Hans-Thilo Schmidt**: **[A]**.
- **Last days of December 1932**: rotor and reflector wiring reconstructed, first breaks **[A: en.wikipedia]**. No day in any reached source; 28 December is a flagged placeholder inside the attested window. The **AVA works** building Polish Enigma doubles: **[R]**.
- **Marriage to Irena Maria Lewandowska, 20 June 1934**, Bydgoszcz: **[A]** as to marriage and date (MacTutor, marian-rejewski.pl), **[R]** as to the **Church of the Sacred Heart of Jesus** as venue, deliberately downgraded (see §7.3).
- **Andrzej b. 22 June 1936, Janina b. 16 February 1939** **[A: en.wikipedia]**. **Address 1934: ul. Zajączka 7**, Żoliborz **[A: marian-rejewski.pl]**, one of three the same chronology lists for that year, with Sułkowskiego and Mickiewicza 12; the file uses one to stand for three.
- **2 November 1937**: reflector A replaced by reflector B, the card catalogue voided, the work redone **[A: Rejewski's own account, Kozaczuk Appendix D p. 264, quoted verbatim in the file]**.
- **15 September 1938**: indicator procedure changed **[A: en.wikipedia]**; the **bomba** and the **Zygalski sheets** follow, undated in every reached source, so the file's "within weeks" is flagged. **1938**: **Gold Cross of Merit** to Rejewski, Silver Crosses to Różycki and Zygalski **[A: en.wikipedia, cross-checked in the pool against Wojciech Polak's security-file study]**; no day-date, so it is folded into the 15 September stop.
- **Pyry, 25-26 July 1939**: the solution, the bomba principle and the perforated sheets disclosed to the French and British, two Polish-built Enigma doubles handed over **[A: GCHQ, "The Pyry Forest Meeting"]**. The Polish chronology gives a wider **24-27 July** window and **Denniston's report emphasises 26 July**: **not resolved**. **Alan Turing was not present**, which GCHQ states explicitly, and the file does not stage him there. Knox's reported silence on the drive back, and his later gift of silk scarves, are **[R]**; the file keeps a neutral version of the first and drops the second.

## 4. September 1939: the road to Kuty

marian-rejewski.pl gives the route as a chain: Warszawa Wileńska (5 Sept), Mińsk Mazowiecki, Siedlce, Brześć, Kowel, Łuck, Dubno, Tarnopol, Czortków, **Kuty on 17 September** and the crossing into Romania on the day of the Soviet invasion, **Bucharest 21 September**, then Yugoslavia, Italy and Modane to **Paris about 25 September**. **[A** for the chain; the towns are chronology entries, not documented sightings.**]** The file compresses this into four stops, naming the intermediate towns inside the campas rather than pinning nine near-identical waypoints. Two notes: the source names the departure point only as **"Warszawa Wileńska"**, and I could not establish whether that means the railway station or the district (gap stated in `date_confidence`, pin at the station); and **the bomby destroyed or buried near the Romanian border** rests on a recovered evacuation report cited by Borowiak and Grajek, not refetchable, so **[R]**.

## 5. France and Africa, 1939-1943

- **P.C. Bruno**, Château de Vignolles, **Gretz-Armainvilliers, from 20 October 1939** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, which also puts the château **some 40 km southeast** of Paris, not east as the pool had it.
- **Turing's January 1940 visit and his conversations with Rejewski** **[A: GCHQ]**, which states they "were fundamental to developing the thinking which led to the design and installation of the first British 'Bombe'." en.wikipedia's "PC Bruno" does not mention the visit. The claim that Bruno recovered its first wartime key on **17 January 1940** could not be confirmed anywhere and is **omitted** rather than carried as [R].
- **Evacuation to Algeria 24 June 1940** **[A: en.wikipedia]**; the summer near **Oran**, and the southward road through La Ferté, Vensant, Larche, Agen and Toulouse **[A: marian-rejewski.pl]**, compressed into the Oran stop.
- **P.C. Cadix. Pool date corrected.** The pool said "1940-09"; marian-rejewski.pl gives **Marseille with false papers on 26 September 1940** and **Cadix from 1 October 1940**, and en.wikipedia agrees on 1 October, which the file uses. Cover identity **"Pierre Ranaud, a lycée professor from Nantes"** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, spelled **"Pierre Raneau"** by the museum chronology, a contradiction flagged in the file. Cover organisation **Travaux Ruraux** **[A]**.
- **Jerzy Różycki's death, 9 January 1942. Tag upgraded [R] to [A]**: en.wikipedia confirms he died in the sinking of a French passenger ship that day, returning from Algeria to Cadix. The ship's name (**Lamoricière**) and the co-victims (**Graliński**, **Smoleński**) are standard in the literature but unconfirmed against a primary source, and stay **[R]** at that level. **House rule:** the sinking happened at sea, where Rejewski was not; the stop sits at Cadix, where he received the news.
- **Dispersal of Cadix, November 1942** **[A]**; no day, 9 November used as a flagged placeholder.
- **Pyrenees crossing, robbery by the guide, arrest near Puigcerdà, 29 January 1943** **[A: en.wikipedia, marian-rejewski.pl]**; detentions at **Bellver**, **Seo de Urgel** and **Lleida**, with the rheumatism contracted there **[A: marian-rejewski.pl]**, compressed into one Lleida stop.
- **Three contradictions, none resolved, all recorded in the file.** Release from Spanish custody: **4 May 1943** in most sources, **24 May** in a competing chronology. Arrival at Hendon: **30 July 1943** in marian-rejewski.pl, **3 August** in en.wikipedia. Demobilisation: en.wikipedia distinguishes a **15 November 1946** discharge from a **21 November** return, MacTutor gives 21 November for the demobilisation itself.
- **Inducted as a private 16 August 1943** **[A: en.wikipedia]**, which falls on his thirty-eighth birthday; **second lieutenant 10 October 1943, lieutenant 1 January 1945** **[A: MacTutor]**; **Stanmore Park** and **Felden**, heading German section **N** on SS and Sicherheitsdienst **double Playfair** **[A: marian-rejewski.pl]**. That **Bletchley never sent for him** is an argument from silence, labelled as such in the file.

## 6. Poland, 1946-1980

- **Return via Gdynia, 21 November 1946**, after seven years and two months, and **reunion at ul. Dworcowa 10, flat 2, Bydgoszcz** **[A: MacTutor, marian-rejewski.pl]**.
- **Kabel Polski cable works, 1 February 1947 to the 1950 dismissal** **[A: marian-rejewski.pl]**. **Role contradiction, not resolved**: the museum chronology makes him **head of the invoicing section**, en.wikipedia **director of sales**; the file uses the first and names the disagreement. **Security investigations c. 1949-1958 closed without a hostile-activity finding** **[A: en.wikipedia]**.
- **Death of Andrzej, 9 July 1947, poliomyelitis, aged eleven. Pool improved**: en.wikipedia has only "summer 1947", marian-rejewski.pl gives "9.07.1947", so the date now rests on the museum chronology rather than an unconfirmed dossier claim.
- **1967. Pool conflation corrected.** marian-rejewski.pl separates three events the pool had merged: **disability pension from 1 February**, **first memoir typescript in April**, **move to ul. Gdańska 10 in the summer**. The file dates the stop to the move and narrates the writing inside it. A **second memoir followed in 1974** **[A]**.
- **1969: Warsaw, ul. Gdańska 2, from 23 June** **[A: marian-rejewski.pl]**. **A flagged contradiction dissolves here:** the pool suspected a conflict between a Gdańska Street address in Warsaw and one in Bydgoszcz. There is none. **ul. Gdańska 10, Bydgoszcz (1967)** and **ul. Gdańska 2, Warsaw (1969)** are two real streets in two cities; tag raised to **[A]**.
- **1973**: Bertrand's memoir published in Paris, publicity begins **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Press visits to the Warsaw flat: **[R]**.
- **1980**: **"An Application of the Theory of Permutations in Breaking the Enigma Cipher", Applicationes Mathematicae 16.4 (1980), 543-559** **[A: MacTutor]**. No month for the issue; the file defaults to 01.
- **Died 13 February 1980, heart attack, aged 74; buried with military honours at Powązki, quarter B-39** **[A: MacTutor and en.wikipedia for the death; pl.wikipedia "kwatera B-39", locator B39-4-3, and marian-rejewski.pl for the quarter]**. The quarter is **[A]**, upgraded from the pool's [R]. **The funeral date is a gap**: no reached source gives it, and the burial stop carries the date of death. **Irena's death in 1986 and burial beside him**: **[R]**, unverified.

## 7. Afterlife, legends, disputes

1. **Grand Cross of the Order of Polonia Restituta**, posthumous, jointly with Różycki and Zygalski. **Contradiction resolved on the primary document**: pl.wikipedia quotes President **Kwaśniewski's decree of 14 February 2000**, citing **M.P. 2000 nr 13, poz. 273**. en.wikipedia's **21 July 2000** is unsourced and contradicts the Monitor Polski entry. File uses **2000-02-14**.
2. **Poznań Cryptologists Monument**, three-sided bronze, unveiled **10 November 2007** before the Imperial Castle **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia for the form]**. **Quote apparatus corrected**: the jury verdict quoted in the file belongs to the **2002 design competition**, not to the unveiling, as MacTutor's own framing shows.
3. **The 2022 Gawłowski biography** (English adaptation 2023) was attacked by the Bydgoszcz historian **Hanka Sowińska** in a review cataloguing errors of dates, family relationships, churches and addresses, with a parallel genealogical critique by Jerzy Lelwic **[R**, neither piece refetched**]**. Consequence: address- and venue-level detail sourced only to popular biography is downgraded here, which is why the marriage church is [R].
4. **Memoirs in book form**, AMU Press, Poznań, **2011**, second edition 2013, bilingual **[A: press.amu.edu.pl]**, after a shorter appearance in **Przegląd Historyczno-Wojskowy 6 (2005)**, special no. 5, pp. 71-204. English translations of the technical accounts ran in **Annals of the History of Computing 3:3 (1981)** and **Cryptologia 6:1 (1982)** **[A** per DOI records in the pool, not refetched**]**. The **Enigma Cipher Centre** opened in the Collegium Martineum, Poznań, in **2021**; the day-date 24 September rests on the AMU press release alone, not on en.wikipedia.
5. **Bydgoszcz**: **bench memorial 2005** (memorial and date **[A: en.wikipedia]**, sculptor **Michał Kubiak [R]**); **mural "Odszyfrowany Rejewski" by Juliusz Nowicki, 2017**, on the Gdańska Street building where he lived **[A: en.wikipedia]**; **Bydgoszcz Główna station named for him, reportedly 2 February 2022** **[R**: the gov.pl page did not resolve on refetch, so the date, the "ninth station" ranking and the attendee list are unconfirmed**]**.
6. **NSA Cryptologic Hall of Honor 2014** **[A: en.wikipedia, "NSA Hall of Honor"]** and **IEEE Milestone, 5 August 2014**, framed at team level **[R** as to plaque wording: ETHW returned 403**]**. Neither is staged as a stop: both are American ceremonies at which nothing of his was present.
7. **The ice-cream bomba.** The naming story is attributed to **Colonel Tadeusz Lisicki**, who was not in the pre-war Cipher Bureau and had not known Różycki (d. 1942); Rejewski's own account says only that they called the devices "bombs" for want of a better name **[R**: Christensen, "The Evolution of the Cryptologic Bombe", not refetched**]**. Kept out of the file: too thin for a campa, and not a place.
8. **A bomba handed to Britain at Pyry.** Not supported: the file says two **Enigma doubles** were handed over, per GCHQ, and that the bomby were destroyed or buried, per the evacuation report.
9. **The mother's origin in Toruń.** Popular biography only, genealogically disputed: **[R]**, and **not used in the file at all**.

## 9. Interlocks with the existing corpus

**Named in a campa: `alan_turing`, and nothing else.** The relation is direct and documented: Turing travelled to P.C. Bruno in January 1940 and talked with Rejewski about the machine and the plugboard, and GCHQ states those conversations were fundamental to the first British bombe. Both men were physically at Gretz-Armainvilliers, so the stop is legitimately Rejewski's as well as Turing's.

`alan_turing.journey.json` already flags this interlock as pending, in its Broadway Buildings stop of September 1938: "Marian Rejewski, whose work the last sentence describes, is queued in the atlas but not yet researched." Its 4 September 1939 Bletchley stop says "the Polish methods no longer reach the traffic", which is the 1938 indicator change seen from the British side. **Gap in Turing's file, for the operator, not fixed by me:** that file has **no stop for the January 1940 Bruno visit**, jumping from 4 September 1939 to 1 January 1940 at Letchworth. Per the house rule the Bruno stop belongs on both files, since both men were there; I have written it on Rejewski's and touched nothing in Turing's, as a subagent must not.

**Considered and rejected as name-drops**, recorded so a later pass does not reopen them: `elizebeth_smith_friedman` (shared node in Dilly Knox, not a relation); `william_f_friedman` (his Bletchley visit is April 1943, Rejewski reached Britain in August, so no overlap); `emmy_noether` and `john_von_neumann` (Göttingen, institutional overlap only, no documented contact); `nicolaus_copernicus` (Toruń, via a disputed claim about Rejewski's mother, four centuries apart); `marie_curie` (Warsaw); `ian_fleming` (Enigma-adjacent, no Polish content). The corpus also holds **three different Göttingen pins** across noether, klein and von_neumann; this file uses the pool's 51.5328/9.9356 and does not arbitrate that.

**Pins inherited and pins created.** The **Paris** pin (48.8566, 2.3522) is inherited byte-identical from the corpus standard. **No pin existed anywhere in the corpus for Pyry, Gretz-Armainvilliers or Uzès**; all three are fresh geocodes, sourced in their `date_confidence` fields.

## 10. Apparatus relations for the operator

**None found, in either direction.** Rejewski is not on the `EXCEPTIONS.md` list, and this pass produced nothing that would need him added: no framework of his is applied backward to material he never treated, and nothing reaches forward past his death except attested commemorations of him at places that are his own (grave, university, town).

Two items were kept out rather than silently deleted, so the operator can rule on them. **The NSA Hall of Honor and IEEE Milestone of 2014** are real and attested but placeless as far as Rejewski is concerned; they are recorded in §7.6 and appear in the file only as context, which is the standing house rule rather than an apparatus question. **The Science Museum's reconstruction of the bomba** is a later institution's object about a device he designed: per the house rule it would belong as a source or `suggested_ref` on a Rejewski stop, never as a scene, and it is omitted because the underlying article was not refetchable.
