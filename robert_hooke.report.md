# Robert Hooke (1635-1703): research report

*2026-08-29, for `robert_hooke.journey.json`. Curator of Experiments to the Royal Society 1662-1703, Gresham Professor of Geometry, City surveyor after the Great Fire, author of Micrographia.*

**[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or single-source claim. Contradictions are flagged and left open except where a fetched source demonstrably beats an unfetched one; those cases show their reasoning.

**Calendar caveat.** The file carries `"calendar": "gregorian"` because the life falls after 1582. That is a simplification: England kept the Julian calendar until 1752 and began its legal year on 25 March, so every date here is Old Style. Newton's "5 February 1675/6" carries the double year for that reason. Nothing has been converted.

## 1. Isle of Wight, 1635-1648

- **Birth [A].** 18 July 1635, Freshwater, to Cecily Gyles and John Hooke, curate of All Saints' (Wikipedia). Baptism c.26 July is standard in the biographies but unverified here, so the campa says only "within the fortnight".
- **Childhood [R].** Fossils, the brass clock rebuilt in wood, the model ship with firing guns: all from Waller (1705) and Aubrey, none a contemporary document. 1645 is a placeholder inside c.1638-1648; the campa says "tradition holds".
- **Father's death and the £40 [A].** October 1648.
- **Peter Lely [R], corrected.** The pool pinned the 1648 placement to Lely's Covent Garden Piazza studio. Anachronistic: Lely's Piazza house dates from the 1650s-60s. The coordinate works as a London pin, so the stop is named for London, not the Piazza.
- **Westminster School [A as to fact, undated within a span].** Under Busby: Latin, Greek, Euclid (MacTutor, which gives no year inside c.1648-1653). 1649 orders the segment. "Six books of Euclid in a week" is admiring biography and the campa presents it as a story.

## 2. Oxford, 1653-1662

- **Christ Church 1653 [A]** as chorister, organist, servitor; **matriculated only in 1658 [A]**; **MA 1662 [A]**, no month given, defaults to January.
- **Wadham and Wilkins's circle [A].** The experimental club in the Warden's lodgings, where Wren was met. The 1659 flight scheme laid before the Oxford Philosophical Club is separately attested and folded into this stop rather than staged as its own scene.
- **Thomas Willis [R as to premises].** The assistantship is standard; the building is not confirmed by anything fetched. Coordinate corrected, section 4.
- **Boyle and the air pump [A; dating contradiction flagged, not resolved].** MacTutor dates the engagement and the improved "Pneumatical Engine" to **1655**; the pool's geography lens gave **c.1658-1662**. Both circulate. The file uses 1658 as an ordering date and says so. *New Experiments Physico-Mechanicall* (1660) rests on Hooke's machine [A]. The **£7 a year** wage is literature-standard but carried by neither source named on that stop, so the campa omits it.

## 3. The Royal Society, 1662-1665

All [A]: Curator of Experiments Nov 1662 on Boyle's recommendation, with demonstrations at nearly every weekly meeting (MacTutor); Fellow 3 June 1663 (MacTutor); double star Gamma Arietis reported 1664; rooms taken at Gresham 1664, home and observatory for life (MacTutor); Curator for life 11 Jan 1665 and Gresham Professor of Geometry 20 March 1665; *Micrographia* Jan 1665, 38 copperplates, coining "cell" (MacTutor, Wikipedia).

**Salary: correction to the pool.** The pool asserted an "£80 package split £30 from the Society and £50 from Cutler's lectureship", citing MacTutor, which does not say that. MacTutor: the Society agreed £80 a year in 1664 and "shortly after this they arranged the position of Cutlerian Lecturer in the Mechanical Arts for him at a salary of £50 per year". The split is an artefact of the pool's summarising pass. The file states £80 promised, Cutler's £50 alongside.

**The Pepys anecdote [R as to wording].** "The most ingenious book that ever I read in my life" and the sitting up till two come from the British Library. Pepys's diary was not fetched, so the campa reports it indirectly, not as quotation.

## 4. Fire, survey, buildings, 1666-1692

- **The Fire [A].** 2-6 September 1666; Gresham survived and temporarily housed City government and the burnt Exchange's business (The London Archives). The Society was consequently displaced to **Arundel House, 1666-1673**, a span taken from `newton.journey.json`, which corrected the same venue question for Newton's 1672 election.
- **City surveyorship: date corrected.** The pool put it in 1666. The office was created by the Rebuilding Act, assented **February 1667**; standard accounts date the appointment to 1667. 1666 is kept for the Wren collaboration and the September rebuilding plan. **"90 private properties a month" [A, verbatim]** from the Science Museum; "c.3,000 of 8,394 foundations" is Wikipedia's.
- **Surveyor of the King's Works: doubtful, flagged, not asserted.** Wikipedia alone says "In 1670, he was appointed Surveyor of the Royal Works." MacTutor knows Hooke only as City Surveyor, and that office was **Wren's**, 1669-1718. The pool built a geography stop for it at Scotland Yard. **That stop is not written**; the claim sits in the Guildhall stop's `date_confidence` as contested.
- **The Monument: contradiction flagged, not resolved.** Wikipedia: "constructed between 1671 and 1677". Science Museum: 1673-1679. The file uses 1671 to order the segment and states the disagreement; the pool's third form, "1671-1679", matches neither and is dropped. The zenith-telescope shaft and its defeat by traffic vibration are confirmed by both.
- **Bethlem, Moorfields [A].** "Built rapidly and at great expense between 1675 and 1676"; Cibber's *Melancholy* and *Raving Madness* on the gate piers, surviving in the Bethlem Museum of the Mind. Facade length and subsidence are literature-standard, not carried by those sources. **Royal College of Physicians, Warwick Lane [A but undated]** folds into the same campa.
- **St Paul's and the catenary [A].** Hooke consulted Wren and stated the arch principle as an anagram at the end of *A Description of Helioscopes* (1676). Credit for the dome remains divided between Wren, Hooke, and later hands; the campa resolves nothing.
- **Aske's Hospital, Hoxton [A], date corrected.** The pool gave "1679-1690s", impossible: Aske died in 1689 and the buildings were funded by his will, "built on 21 acres in Hoxton by 1692 to the design of Robert Hooke", demolished 1824. The file uses c.1690-1692.
- **Dropped as stops, recorded so nothing is lost.** *Montagu House* [R], attribution explicitly debated. *Royal Observatory, Greenwich* [R as to Hooke]: Flamsteed House "was designed by Sir Christopher Wren, **probably** assisted by Robert Hooke". *Ramsbury Manor* [R], resting on scattered payments.

**Coordinate corrections to the pool**, checked against each place's own Wikipedia article. Willis's laboratory: `51.75, -1.25`, a placeholder reading as false precision, to **51.7512, -1.2506** (Beam Hall, Merton Street). Ragley Hall, c.6 km east of the estate, to **52.19795, -1.89611**. Willen church, c.2 km off, to **52.06234, -0.72015**. City of London Cemetery, on Wanstead Flats, to **51.56, 0.048**. Two more are kept for reuse though their stops were dropped: the Royal Observatory, where the pool copied Wikipedia's own faulty infobox although the Prime Meridian runs through the building, correctly **51.4778, -0.0015**; and the Bethlem Museum of the Mind, correctly **51.38083, -0.02944**.

## 5. The experiments

- **Ventilated dog, 1667 [A, one step down].** Dog kept alive with thorax open by bellows ventilation; fresh air reaching the blood, not lung motion, sustains life; his recorded unwillingness to repeat it. The Philosophical Transactions paper was **not fetched**, and the October 1667 demonstration date often given was not verified.
- **Gamma Draconis parallax, 1669-1674 [A as to attempt and failure].** Telescope mounted vertically through his own rooms; four observations published; the claim did not hold.
- **Newton's optics, 1672 [A].** Critique delivered as curator. The reading was at **Arundel House**, not Gresham, per `newton.journey.json`. Honoured: the stop sits at Hooke's Gresham pin, where he wrote, and `date_confidence` states where the reading happened.
- **Balance spring, 1675 [A as to dispute].** Huygens's spiral balance spring, developed in Paris with Isaac Thuret, and Hooke's counter-claim. Hooke's "1658" is his own assertion, not an attestation; the Tompion watch inscribed "R. Hooke invenit 1658" comes from `christiaan_huygens.journey.json`.
- **Hevelius, 1679 [A].** Hooke argued in print that naked-eye sights could not match telescopic ones; the Society sent the 22-year-old Halley to Danzig; the verdict went against Hooke (`edmond_halley.journey.json`).
- **Sound and vibration [A].** Flour on a vibrating glass plate, 8 July 1680; pitch as frequency by toothed brass wheels, 1681. Combined at the earlier date.
- **Memory, 1682 [A as to the lecture, standard summary as to content].** A Royal Society lecture modelling memory mechanically: encoding, association, retrieval, forgetting. The text, printed by Waller in 1705, was **not fetched**. Given the atlas's art-of-memory interest this is the item most deserving a second pass, read here at second hand.

**Elasticity: two corrections, both material.** (1) The original prints **`ceiiinosssttuu`**, double u, verified in two independent OCR scans of *Lectiones Cutlerianae* (1679): archive.org `LectionesCutler00Hook` line 15216 and `bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_lectiones-cutlerianae-o_hooke-robert_1679` line 16593. The modern `ceiiinosssttuv` normalises seventeenth-century u/v. (2) The anagram was first printed in ***A Description of Helioscopes* (1676)** and decoded in ***De Potentia Restitutiva* (1678)**; *Lectiones Cutlerianae* (1679) is the collected volume reprinting both, not the place of first appearance (corroborated at DNB vol. 27 p. 291). Do not conflate it with a second Hooke anagram a few pages later, `cediinnoopssttuu`, "Ut pondus sic tensio".

## 6. Secretaryship and the priority wars

**A ten-year error corrected.** The pool gave "acting Secretary, **13 September 1667**", citing Wikipedia. It cannot stand: Henry Oldenburg held the office until his death on **5 September 1677**. The date is **13 September 1677**, eight days after Oldenburg died, which is what makes the joint secretaryship of 19 December 1677 follow coherently. Wikipedia's "On 13 September 1667" is a typo the pool copied; it is not reproduced.

- **Joint Secretary with Nehemiah Grew, 19 December 1677 [A].** The lapse of the *Philosophical Transactions* and their replacement by Hooke's *Philosophical Collections* is literature-standard, not carried by the sources named on that stop.
- **Newton correspondence [A].** November 1679, tangential inertial motion compounded with a central attraction; 6 January 1680, the "duplicate proportion". Folded into one stop at the precisely dated letter.
- **January 1684 at Gresham [A].** Halley's derivation from Kepler's third law; Hooke's refusal to produce the proof he claimed; Wren's forty-shilling prize (`edmond_halley.journey.json`, 24 January 1684). Halley's August ride to Cambridge (`newton.journey.json`).
- **28 April 1686 [A].** Book I laid before the Society; Hooke's demand for acknowledgement; Newton's threat to suppress Book III; the scholium naming Wren, Hooke, and Halley while granting Hooke no demonstration. Newton's private line about mathematicians and drudgery was not fetched and is not quoted.
- **John Hooke the younger, Newport, 1678 [R].** Grocer, in business and legal trouble, dead in what the record treats as a suicide. Reconstructions differ; the file says so rather than picking one.

**Grace Hooke: contradiction resolved, reasoning shown.** The pool left the death year open between **1687** and **1689**. It should not stay open: two independent fetched sources give 1687 (Wikipedia; Westminster Abbey, "his niece Grace (died 1687)"), while 1689 traces only to roberthooke.org.uk, an enthusiast site. The file states 1687, tagged [A]. The relationship is stated flat in the campa and not aestheticised: the diary's coded symbols record a sexual relationship beginning when she was about sixteen, and her own account does not exist. Waller's phrase is tagged **[R]**, the 1705 text being unfetchable here.

## 7. Last years, death, afterlife

**The doctorate: wrong conferring body in the pool, corrected.** The pool said Oxford conferred an honorary MD in 1691, citing Wikipedia. "1691" appears nowhere in that article in connection with a degree; the only relevant line is the footnote "The 'MD' was an honorary degree conferred by Oxford University." The standard record (Waller's *Life*; ODNB) is a **Lambeth degree of Doctor of Physic from Archbishop John Tillotson, in London, December 1691**, an archiepiscopal grant. MacTutor and roberthooke.org.uk mention no doctorate at all. The file states the Lambeth version and stages no Oxford scene. Worth an operator's second look, since it rests on the standard record rather than on a source fetched here.

- **Cutler litigation [R as to dates].** Cutler's 1693 death and the 1696 Chancery decree come from the research trail, not a primary Chancery record.
- **Death [A].** 3 March 1703, in his rooms at Gresham, after a final year reported as blind and bedridden; no cause of death survives. Chest of about £8,000 in coin and gold; library over 3,000 books; no will; estate to his cousin Elizabeth Stephens.
- **Burial [A]; remains [A as an assumption].** St Helen's, Bishopsgate, March 1703; nineteenth-century churchyard clearance; remains **assumed** to lie in a communal reburial at the City of London Cemetery. *Assumed* is the Abbey's own word and is operative. St Helen's own article calls the 1891 remains "translated to Ilford Cemetery", a naming variant for the same ground.
- **The 1993 window [R], kept out of prose.** The pool claimed a Hooke memorial window at St Helen's was destroyed in the Bishopsgate bombing. The bombing and the damage are attested; the window is not. The campa says only that the church was wrecked and repaired.
- **Westminster Abbey memorial [A].** Unveiled 3 March 2005, tercentenary, in the lantern near Busby's grave. No inscription text given; no remains claimed.
- **Waller's *Posthumous Works* [A].** London, 1705; c.505 pages plus 15 leaves of plates; printed for the Royal Society; **dedicated to Sir Isaac Newton** (Wellcome). Counter-evidence to the "erased rival" legend, though not by itself a disproof of ill will.
- **The Hooke Folio [A], upgraded from the pool's [R].** The 2006 rediscovery and Royal Society acquisition are in the peer-reviewed record: Adams and Jardine, *Notes and Records of the Royal Society* 60 (2006), 235-239.
- **The diary [A].** 10 March 1672 to 16 May 1683; UNESCO Memory of the World; City purchase 1891; first modern printing Robinson and Adams, 1935. The shelfmark the pool carried (CLC/495/MS01758) was not re-verified and is not stated in the file.

**The missing portrait [A].** Felicity Henderson, for the Royal Society, found no order, no eyewitness, and no destruction record implicating Newton, and found the existence of any life portrait doubtful, resting on an ambiguous 1674 diary mention and a 1710 sighting by Zacharias Conrad von Uffenbach that may have misidentified a portrait of Theodore Haak. Rival modern identifications (Griffing's Mary Beale sitter, contested as Isaac Barrow; an earlier Jardine candidate reidentified as van Helmont) are unconfirmed, not asserted.

**The physical description: attribution corrected, and this is a trap.** The famous paragraph ("in person but despicable, being crooked and low of stature…") is verbatim in the **DNB, vol. 27, p. 292** (Agnes Mary Clerke), introduced there as "His biographer, Waller, describes him as…". Waller's 1705 text differs: it opens "As to his Person he was but despicable, being very crooked, tho' I have heard from himself, and others, that he was strait till about 16 [years]". The DNB condensed that opening and dropped a parenthesis without ellipsis, though the middle stretch is Waller verbatim. The file cites it as the DNB condensing Waller. Anyone quoting the paragraph as Waller is quoting Clerke.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Hooke is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so no apparatus stop is licensed on his file. One pool relation is not rooted in his own life and is recorded rather than silently deleted.

**Direction caught: FORWARD. Not licensable without the author's say-so.** The pool's interlock lens offered, tagged [R], **Charles Babbage** attacking the Royal Society for thirty years as run by titled amateurs, set against "the same body whose seventeenth-century curator-of-experiments office Hooke held for life from 1665, an office later criticized for underfunding him." Babbage was born in 1791, eighty-eight years after Hooke died, so under the block rule Hooke's file cannot embed it. If the author wants the connection made it belongs on `charles_babbage.journey.json` as Babbage's own stop looking back at the office Hooke held; that direction is legal and needs no exception.

No **backward** apparatus relation was found. Hooke's 1682 mechanical model of memory is his own work in his own lifetime and is an ordinary stop. Named so it is not mistaken for an apparatus case: the final segment carries posthumous stops (1891, 2005, 2010) in the ordinary way, as `newton.journey.json` does with Tate Britain, Sotheby's, and Jerusalem. These concern Hooke's own bones, name, and face, at places tied to him.

**Honest gaps, stated as gaps**
- No authenticated life portrait of Hooke exists. Every published likeness is a modern reconstruction from a written description that turns out to be partly the DNB's rewriting of that description.
- No individual grave, coffin plate, or identifiable remains survive. The Abbey's word is *assumed*.
- No cause of death is recorded.
- The division of architectural credit between Hooke and Wren at St Paul's, at Greenwich, at Montagu House, and arguably at the Monument is not resolvable from what survives, and is not resolved here.
- Whether Hooke ever held the Surveyorship of the King's Works is doubtful on the evidence and is left doubtful.
