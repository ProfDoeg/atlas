# Giovanni Pico della Mirandola (1463-1494): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Slug: `giovanni_pico_della_mirandola`.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by one
source only. Contradictions are flagged and **not adjudicated**. Where the research pool contained an
error a correction pass caught, both the original and the correction are recorded.

---

## 1. Mirandola and the first schools (1463-1479)

**Birth, 24 February 1463**, in the castle of the Pico at Mirandola near Modena, youngest son of
Gianfrancesco I Pico, prince of Mirandola and lord of Concordia, and of Giulia Boiardo **[A: SEP;
Wikipedia EN/IT; Britannica]**. Both principal sources agree on the day; nothing reachable dissents.
Giulia Boiardo is aunt of the poet Matteo Maria Boiardo **[A: Wikipedia]**.

*Coordinate correction.* The pool pinned the Castello dei Pico at 44.8886 / 11.0631, about 175 m west
of the castle. IT Wikipedia, 'Castello dei Pico': **44.8888 / 11.0653**.

**Childhood at the Mirandola court**, under his mother's direction, remembered for a prodigious memory,
groomed for the Church **[R: Britannica, from biographical tradition, not documents]**. **Apostolic
protonotary, 1473**, at about ten, apparently honorary and obtained through family influence
**[A: Wikipedia]**; no naming document was reachable. **Bologna, canon law, from 1477** **[A: SEP;
Wikipedia]**, SEP giving c. 1477, age fourteen; Britannica has him finding the subject arid.

*Gap, stated as a gap.* The pool records "his mother dies" under 1480. Neither SEP nor IT Wikipedia
gives the year of Giulia Boiardo's death. **The 1480 date for her death is unsupported by any cited
source and is asserted nowhere in the journey file.**

## 2. Ferrara, Padua, and the languages (1479-1482)

**Ferrara.** SEP has him leaving Bologna for Ferrara "within two years" of the 1477 enrolment, so
**c. 1479**; EN Wikipedia's "c. 1480" is the outer bound of that window. **Contradiction flagged, not
resolved**; the journey file dates the stop 1479 and puts the spread in `date_confidence`. A first
sight of Savonarola, then a young friar at Ferrara, is **[R: Wikipedia, no document]**.

**Padua, 1480-1482** **[A: SEP; Wikipedia; Britannica]**. Aristotelian and Averroist scholasticism;
he studies with **Elia del Medigo**, a Jewish Averroist, and begins **Hebrew and Arabic** under him.

*Correction carried.* The pool assigned Hebrew, Arabic *and Aramaic* to del Medigo in 1482. Two errors.
(a) EN Wikipedia attributes Hebrew and Arabic to the whole 1480-82 span, and 1482 is the year he *left*
Padua (SEP). (b) **Aramaic was not del Medigo's.** EN Wikipedia, 'Flavius Mithridates': Mithridates
"instructed him in Aramaic" and "about 1486 he lived at Fratta, near Perugia, in the house of Giovanni
Pico della Mirandola." The Aramaic clause belongs to 1486 and to Mithridates, and is placed there.

*Coordinate correction.* The pool's 45.4028 / 11.8767 lands near the Prato della Valle, some 720 m
south of the university. Palazzo Bo, the historic seat, is **45.4093 / 11.8762** (EN Wikipedia).

**Venice** during the Padua years, in del Medigo's circle, is **[R: tradition; the pool states outright
that it is not confirmed in Wikipedia or Britannica and that no itinerary survives]**. Retained as one
low-confidence stop, labelled as such. **Leaving Padua, 1482**, drawn by Florentine Platonism through
Ficino **[A: SEP]**.

*Contradiction flagged.* The pool's geography lens has him settling his inheritance on "his two
brothers" in 1482-1484. SEP dates the transfer to **1491** and names the **nephew Gianfrancesco**. Both
cannot stand. The journey file follows SEP and drops the 1482-84 clause.

## 3. Florence, Paris, and the making of the Theses (1484-1486)

**Correspondence with Poliziano and Lorenzo, 1484** **[A: SEP]**, before he arrives. **Florence,
November 1484** **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]**: he enters Lorenzo's circle and meets **Ficino** and
**Poliziano** in person, and probably **Savonarola**. Britannica preserves Ficino's recollection that
on the day Pico arrived, he himself finished his translation of Plato.

*Correction carried.* The pool's chronology held a duplicate of this meeting dated **1480**. EN
Wikipedia places it in **November 1484**. The 1480 record is wrong by four years and duplicates the
correct one; it is deleted, not kept as a second visit.

**Paris, 1485-1486** **[A: SEP; Wikipedia; Britannica]**: the leading school of scholastic theology,
its disputational method, the nominalist-realist quarrel, the first drafting of the 900 Theses. **The
letter to Ermolao Barbaro** on philosophical Latin, dated 1485 **[R: SEP; the pool tagged it R and it
stays R, no edition consulted]**. **Florence again, spring 1486** **[A: Britannica]**.

## 4. Arezzo, Perugia, Fratta: the Kabbalah (1486)

**The Arezzo affair, May 1486** **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia IT]**. He attempts to carry off Margherita,
wife of Giuliano Mariotto de' Medici; the attempt fails, he is wounded, pursued, captured, briefly
held. A companion is killed in the pursuit **[R: reconstructed from Britannica, which does not fix the
site of capture]**. He is released after Lorenzo intervenes **[R: tradition; no consulted account
names the place of release]**.

*Correction carried, and an internal contradiction in the pool.* The pool's chronology dated this
**1485**; its geography lens dated it **1486-05**. IT Wikipedia settles it verbatim: "Ad Arezzo nel
1486, ebbe effettivamente una disavventura amorosa per una certa Margherita." **1486** is used
throughout. Britannica's looser "1485-86, while returning to Florence" does not contradict May 1486.

**Perugia and Fratta, summer 1486** **[A]**. Convalescing, he meets **Flavius Mithridates**, a
converted Jewish scholar, who translates Kabbalistic manuscripts for him and instructs him in Aramaic
(EN Wikipedia, 'Flavius Mithridates', quoted above).

*Correction carried.* The pool attributed this encounter to **Rabbi Yohanan Alemanno**, called a
converted Jewish scholar, at Perugia in 1485. Three errors: the year (1486, following the May affair);
the person (Mithridates is the one documented at Fratta); the descriptor (Alemanno was a practising
Jew). EN Wikipedia calls Alemanno only "one of Pico's tutors in Kabbalah," with **no place and no
date**, and the journey file names him as a tutor without a pin or a year.

*Coordinate corrections.* Fratta, renamed Umbertide in 1863, is **43.3066 / 12.3270**; the pool's
43.3042 / 12.3378 sits about 900 m east. The pool's Perugia record was also **malformed JSON**,
carrying a stray key `"43.1107","12.3908": 12.3908` beside proper `lat`/`lng` fields; the values
(43.1107 / 12.3908) are themselves correct.

## 5. Rome: the nine hundred, and the condemnation (1486-1487)

**Arrival in Rome, autumn 1486** **[A: Britannica; Wikipedia]**, for a public disputation of nine
hundred theses drawn from Latin, Greek, Hebrew and Arabic sources.

**The Conclusiones nongentae printed at Rome, December 1486** **[A: SEP; Wikipedia]**, without a title.
The press of Eucharius Silber is pinned at 41.8986 / 12.4768 **[R: the pool itself calls the press
location approximated]**.

**The Oratio, 1486** **[A: Wikipedia; Britannica]**, written as the opening address. Not published and
not titled by Pico in his lifetime; *Oratio de hominis dignitate* is the 1496 edition's title.

**The disputation announced for 6 January 1487** **[A: Wikipedia; SEP]**.

**Innocent VIII halts it and appoints a commission, February 1487** **[A: Britannica via SEP;
Wikipedia]**.

*How many theses were condemned: contradiction, flagged.* The pool's chronology says the commission
"initially condemns thirteen"; its geography lens says "seven outright and six more suspect," then has
the Apologia defend "the thirteen contested theses." Reconcilable arithmetic, but **not the same
claim**, and no source reachable here gives the commission's own tally. The journey file says thirteen
contested, distributed seven and six *by one account*.

**The Apologia, 31 May 1487, printed at NAPLES, not Rome** **[A: SEP, "1487: Apology published
(Naples)"]**. Both pool lenses placed it at Rome; both are wrong. EN Wikipedia's "1489" is an outlier
against SEP and the standard 31 May 1487 dating, noted here and not adopted.

**The bull of 4 August 1487**, condemning all nine hundred and ordering his arrest **[A: Wikipedia;
Britannica]**.

*The bull's wording: unverified.* "In part heretical, in part the flower of heresy; several are
scandalous and offensive to pious ears..." is widely cited but surfaced here only via a general
Wikipedia query, never against the Latin; the trailing ellipsis suggests an excerpt of an excerpt.
**[R]**, used in the journey file with that hedge stated in `quote_source`.

## 6. Flight, arrest, imprisonment, release (1487-1488)

**Flight north out of Italy, late 1487** **[R: reconstructed. Neither Britannica nor Wikipedia details
the route. The pool's Genoa pin, 44.4056 / 8.9463, stands for a road, not an attested stop, and the
journey file labels it so.]**

**Arrest near Lyon, early 1488**, by officers of Philip of Savoy at the pope's request **[A: Wikipedia;
SEP; Britannica]**. The exact spot is not agreed; sources say the Lyon/Savoy region.

**Imprisonment at the Château de Vincennes, 1488** **[R for Vincennes specifically]**. Wikipedia names
it; Britannica confirms detention in France **without naming a place**; SEP dates the flight to 1487
and the imprisonment to 1488. Flagged, not resolved. **Release, 1488**, through Charles VIII and
Italian princes, with pressure from Lorenzo **[A: Wikipedia; SEP; Britannica]**. **Return to Tuscany,
summer 1488**, under Lorenzo's protection **[A: SEP; Britannica]**; the papal condemnation is never
formally lifted in Lorenzo's lifetime.

## 7. Fiesole and Florence (1488-1492)

**The villa at Fiesole**, prepared by Lorenzo **[A: Wikipedia]**: the pool gives 1488-1492 in geography
and 1489 in chronology, and the journey file settles him in 1489 and notes the span. **Heptaplus,
1489** **[A: SEP; Wikipedia]**, seven readings of the first twenty-six verses of Genesis, dedicated to
Lorenzo. **A papal inquisitorial tribunal and a formal act of submission, 1489** **[R: Wikipedia
only]**.

*Savonarola at Reggio Emilia.* The pool tagged this **[A]** and dated it **1490**; both are too strong.
EN Wikipedia mentions the Reggio Emilia disputation at which Pico heard him but gives **no year**; what
it dates to 1490 is Savonarola's reassignment to San Marco ("May or June 1490"). The encounter is
conventionally placed at the 1482 chapter. **Downgraded to [R], the 1490 stamp kept only for the
recall.** It is the weakest date in the journey file and its `date_confidence` says so at length.

**Savonarola brought to San Marco, 1490, on Pico's advocacy** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Girolamo Savonarola']**.

*Coordinate correction, and a three-way inconsistency in the pool.* The dataset carried **three
coordinates for one church**: 43.7761 / 11.2588 in the geography lens (twice) and 43.7746 / 11.2588 in
the afterlife lens. Neither is right. EN Wikipedia, 'San Marco, Florence': **43.7785 / 11.2590**, used
everywhere in the journey file.

**Transfer of the estates to the nephew Gianfrancesco, 1491** **[A: SEP]**; the pool's chronology said
1489, and see also the 1482-84 "two brothers" contradiction in §2. **De ente et uno, after 1490**
**[A: SEP, "written but unpublished during lifetime"]**: the pool said "publishes ... 1491," but it was
**written**, not published, and first appeared in the posthumous 1496 *Commentationes*. The journey
file says writes, dated c. 1491.

## 8. Savonarola, the last years, the death (1492-1494)

**Lorenzo de' Medici dies, 8 April 1492** **[A]**. The pool's summary source gives only the month; the
8/9 April date is independently well documented, and the hedge sits in `date_confidence`.

**Ferrara, 1492** **[A: Wikipedia]**, with continued movement to Florence: he renounces the Egyptian
and Chaldean texts, destroys his vernacular poetry, and begins giving away his wealth **[A:
Wikipedia]**.

**Alexander VI absolves him, 1493** **[A: SEP, "pardoned his earlier misadventures"; Wikipedia]**. The
pool's geography lens stamped **1493-06**; **the month is unsupported** by SEP, EN Wikipedia or IT
Wikipedia, and is dropped.

*Coordinate correction.* The Apostolic Palace is **41.9036 / 12.4564** (EN Wikipedia). The pool's
41.9029 / 12.4534, used for both the 1487 commission and the 1493 absolution, is about 250 m off,
landing on St Peter's rather than the palace.

**Disputationes adversus astrologiam divinatricem**, begun 1493, left unfinished **[A: SEP]**.
**Death, Florence, 17 November 1494** **[A: SEP; Wikipedia]**, at thirty-one, the same day Charles
VIII's army entered Florence; Poliziano had died shortly before. **Burial at San Marco, November
1494**, near Girolamo Benivieni, Savonarola preaching the funeral oration **[A: Wikipedia EN/IT]**.

*"His house."* The pool labels the death pin "Florence, his house" but supplies the generic
Florence-centre coordinate reused from an earlier record. **No source reachable in this pass fixes the
house.** The journey file pins the city and names the city.

*Poison.* Contemporary rumour named his own secretary, a second strand named Piero de' Medici **[R:
flagged as rumour by the source itself]**. **Ficino's line** on the death appears in Wikipedia but
**was not verified against a published edition of Ficino's correspondence** **[R]**.

## 9. Afterlife (1496-2021)

**1496, Bologna: the Commentationes** **[A: SEP, "first collection published 1496 (Bologna), edited by
nephew Gianfrancesco"]**, containing the first printing of the 1486 oration under its new title.

*Correction carried.* The pool gave the 1496 place as "the Duchy of Mirandola," taken from an EN
Wikipedia infobox publication field, which is not a press location and is anachronistic besides:
Mirandola was not raised to a duchy until 1617. Replaced with **Bologna, 44.4949 / 11.3426**.

**The 900 Theses as banned book.** EN Wikipedia calls them the first printed book universally banned by
the Church, nearly all copies burned **[A as to the source; the superlative is Wikipedia's, was not
independently checked, and the journey file attributes it as "by one account"]**. **1601, Basel: Opera
omnia**, printed by Sebastianus Henricpetri **[A: Wikipedia IT]**. **1933: Vincenzo Chiaroni
rediscovers the remains** at San Marco with those of Poliziano and Benivieni **[A: Wikipedia IT]**.

**1873: Walter Pater** gives Pico a chapter in *Studies in the History of the Renaissance* **[A:
Wikipedia EN]**. The pool gives only "England"; the journey file pins London, the book's place of
publication, and says in `date_confidence` that this is what the pin means.

**2007: exhumation** of Pico and Poliziano **[A: Wikipedia]**. A preliminary arsenic finding was
reported in 2008; **EN Wikipedia notes Poliziano's result was inconclusive.**

**2018: confirmation** **[A: Wikipedia IT, verbatim: "Nel novembre del 2018 ... i risultati di uno
studio coordinato del dipartimento di Biologia dell'Università di Pisa, del Reparto Investigazioni
Scientifiche dell'Arma dei Carabinieri di Parma e di studiosi spagnoli, britannici e tedeschi, hanno
dimostrato che Pico della Mirandola fu effettivamente avvelenato con l'arsenico."]**

*Contradiction flagged, not resolved.* The pool's chronology attributed the arsenic conclusion to
**2007**; its afterlife lens attributes confirmation to **November 2018**. Two rounds of work, and the
sources do not agree on which is decisive. The journey file **splits them into two stops**.

**A memorial plaque in the cloister of the Certosa di Bologna** **[A: Wikipedia IT]**, undated in the
source, so recorded here and given no stop. *Coordinate correction, the worst in the dataset:* the pool
gave 44.4838 / 11.3114, about 1.4 km south-southeast of the cemetery; EN Wikipedia gives **44.4964 /
11.3094**. **An epitaph by Girolamo Benivieni** on the tomb **[R: Wikipedia IT]** is named in a stop's
`suggested_refs`, not asserted.

**Mirandola and the 2012 earthquake.** The pool's "restored in recent years and now open to the public"
rests on an EN Wikipedia sentence that **predates 2012 and is stale**. IT Wikipedia, 'Castello dei
Pico': reopened 2006, then "gravemente danneggiato dal terremoto dell'Emilia del 2012," *inagibile*,
damage above ten million euros, collections evacuated to Modena and Sassuolo, still under restoration
**[A]**. The Palazzo del Comune of 1468 with Pico family portraits is confirmed **[A]**. The Gothic
church of St Francis, holding Pico family tombs, collapsed in the second shock of 29 May 2012
**[A: Wikipedia EN, 'Mirandola']**.

**2021: Einaudi**, a modern Italian critical edition of *De hominis dignitate* **[A: Wikipedia IT]**;
no place beyond "Italy," so no stop. **Later scholarship and fiction** (Busi and Campanini on the
Kabbalistic sources; Copenhaver and Hanegraaff on the critical side; Eco and Jo Walton) **[A: Wikipedia
EN]**: no places or dates attach, so no stops.

## 10. Crossings with the existing atlas

From the interlock lens, which reads the corpus's own journey files. Line references are to those files
as they stood at compilation.

- **Giulio Camillo** (named in the Basel 1601 campa). His Theatre draws its Sephirot-and-planets
  scaffolding from the Kabbalah Pico had baptized into Christian philosophy, and the file names Pico
  as the source current running through Egidio da Viterbo's Roman circle
  **[A: giulio_camillo.journey.json:142]**; his allegory of the interior man comes from Kabbalist
  teaching on the three souls **[A: :289]**; at his death in 1544 admirers call him "a second Pico
  della Mirandola," verbatim **[A: :605]**; Erasmus dismisses the Theatre's promise of "Ciceronian
  eloquence by magic and Kabbalah besides" **[A: :445]**.
- **Giordano Bruno** (named in the Rome 1486 and Basel 1601 campas): *De umbris idearum* fuses Ficino's
  Neoplatonic psychology with a hermetic ars memoriae **[R: giordano_bruno.journey.json:287]**, and he
  burns at Campo de' Fiori in 1600 in the city whose Curia examined Pico's Theses **[R: :803]**.
- **Arthur Edward Waite** (named in the London 1873 campa, the same city): *The Holy Kabbalah* (1929),
  on "what the Renaissance magicians took out of the Zohar and how much of it they got wrong in the
  taking," read up in the British Museum Reading Room **[A: arthur_edward_waite.journey.json:484]**.
- Downstream echoes rather than crossed paths, named in no campa: **Borges**
  **[R: borges.journey.json:180, :655]**, **Jung's** Eranos circle with Scholem **[R: jung.journey.json:967]**,
  **Christian Rosenkreuz** and the Fama's Fez **[R: christian_rosenkreuz.journey.json:123]**, **Dante**
  in Florence **[R: dante.journey.json:17]**.
- **Johannes Reuchlin**, "key figure (with Pico della Mirandola already queued) bringing Kabbalah into
  Renaissance scholarship" **[A: QUEUE.md:503]**, has **no journey file yet**, so although he is named
  in the Basel campa he is not counted as a crossing.

*Honest note on Paris.* The interlock lens flags that Pico's own Paris stop is a reconstruction from
outside the corpus: no atlas file attests it, only Bruno's later Paris episode. The file's Paris stops
rest on SEP, Wikipedia and Britannica.
