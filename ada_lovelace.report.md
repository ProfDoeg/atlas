# Ada Lovelace: research report

*Augusta Ada King, Countess of Lovelace, born Augusta Ada Byron. London, 10 December 1815 to 27 November 1852. Compiled 2026-08-12 for the Atlas of Journeys, slug `ada_lovelace`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named inline. **[R]** = reconstruction or tradition, resting on modern biography without a producible document. Where sources disagree, both are printed and neither adopted; where the record is empty, the emptiness is the finding. **Method:** the pipeline pool cross-checked against a corrections pass that re-derived coordinates, re-dated events and re-read three quotations against their printed sources. Twelve pool entries carried CORRECTED notes; every correction is carried into the journey file.

---

## 1. The fatherless house, 1815 to 1829

**Birth.** 10 December 1815, London, only legitimate child of Lord Byron and Annabella Milbanke, at 13 Piccadilly Terrace **[A: Wikipedia, 'Ada Lovelace'; Biography.com]**. *Coordinates corrected:* the pool's 51.5054, -0.145 falls near Green Park; the house stood at the Hyde Park Corner end, now 139 Piccadilly, **51.504, -0.1497**. *Claim rejected:* the pool's "absorbed into Hamilton Place" has no support in any source reached, the standard account being renumbering into Piccadilly.

**Kirkby Mallory.** 16 January 1816, Annabella takes the infant to her parents' Leicestershire seat **[A: Wikipedia]**. *Coordinates corrected* from 52.6046, -1.2966 to **52.600, -1.317**; Kirkby Hall demolished 1952 **[A]**.

**Separation and departure**, the first real conflict, unresolved: deed signed **21 April 1816**, Byron sailing from **Dover, 25 April 1816** **[A: Wikipedia, 'Lord Byron']**, which the pool collapsed into one event, wrong by four days; while Wikipedia's 'Anne Isabella Byron' dates a private settlement to **March 1816** **[A, contradicting]**. Printed, not adjudicated. "They never meet again" understates it: Byron last saw her **15 January 1816** **[A]**, before either date.

**Byron's death.** **19 April 1824**, of fever, at Missolonghi **[A: Wikipedia, 'Lord Byron']**; the pool gave only "1824-04." *Anachronism corrected:* not "Missolonghi, Greece" but Missolonghi, Ottoman Empire, a town held by the Greek revolution. Ada was eight: confirmed.

**Flyology.** At about twelve, bird anatomy, paper, oiled silk and feathers toward a mechanical wing, with a book planned **[R: childhood correspondence as related in Toole; no manuscript confirmed extant]**. *Pool contradiction:* **[A]** in its chronology lens, **[R]** in its afterlife lens; the journey takes the weaker tag.

**The illness.** June 1829: measles, or by some accounts a related paralytic illness, leaves her partially paralyzed, bedridden close to a year; crutches by 1831 **[A: Wikipedia]**. *Pool contradiction:* its geography lens claimed nearly three years without walking and recovery "by 1832," overstating Wikipedia and contradicting its own chronology entry. That lens also put the sickroom at Fordhook, Ealing, with an **[A]** tag it has not earned, no source naming the house; kept as a working pin, flagged in `date_confidence`, with *Fordhook coordinates* corrected throughout to about **51.5115, -0.2870**. Sea-air visits to **Brighton [R: pattern only]** are dropped for want of an anchor.

## 2. The tutors, 1825 to 1834

**The network.** William Frend, mathematician and radical, formerly Annabella's own tutor, stays in the family circle **[R]**; Somerville and later De Morgan complete the relay, and William King (the tutor, not the husband) is also named **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Mary Somerville.** Mentor and friend, hosts her for mathematics, opens scientific society to her, outlives her by almost exactly twenty years, dying at Naples 29 November 1872 **[A: Wikipedia, 'Mary Somerville']**. *Siting problem, unresolved:* the pool gave Hanover Square as 51.5142, -0.1494, Mayfair's being nearer **51.5136, -0.1424**, while Wikipedia ties Somerville's Hanover Square house to her husband's 1819 Chelsea Hospital appointment. The journey uses the Mayfair pin and flags it provisional: the weakest pin still worth keeping.

**Court and Babbage.** Presented at St James's Palace at seventeen in 1833, date not fixed **[A: Britannica]**; introduced to Babbage 5 June 1833 at one of his Saturday soirees, and later that month shown the working prototype section of the Difference Engine **[A: Wikipedia]**. **The Enchantress of Number**, attributed to a Babbage letter of about 1843, is **not verified**: a fetch over the Gutenberg text of 'Passages from the Life of a Philosopher' found no match in the available excerpt **[R]**, and the journey uses the phrase only inside a sentence saying it cannot be traced.

**The elopement.** Affair with a tutor and an attempted elopement, covered up **[R: Wikipedia, sourced to Toole]**. No house named; sited at Fordhook as a working pin. **The Midland mills**, toured under Jacquard card control in 1834 **[R: town, mill and date unconfirmed]**, are retained as the physical floor under the 1843 metaphor.

## 3. Countess of Lovelace, 1835 to 1841

**Marriage.** 8 July 1835 to William, **8th Baron King** **[A: Wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the pool called him "a steady baronet"; he was a baron. Fordhook as venue is the standard account, not in every source **[R for venue, A for date]**. **Children** Byron King-Noel 1836, Anne Isabella 1837, Ralph Gordon 1839; **earldom** 1838 **[A]**.

**The 1837 illness, unreconciled.** Wikipedia: a prolonged post-childbirth illness, called tedious and suffering, months to cure **[A]**. Biography.com: cholera, leaving asthma and digestive problems later treated with laudanum and opium **[R]**. Two illnesses, same year, two reputable secondary sources. Both appear in the campa.

**The houses.**
- **Ockham Park.** Built c. 1638, King family since the 1720s, altered by Hawksmoor 1727 to 1729 **[A]**. *Coordinates corrected* from 51.3057, -0.4649 to **51.2978, -0.4733**.
- **Ashley Combe.** *Correction:* the pool said King "begins building" it; Wikipedia records it built as a hunting lodge in **1799**, so he remodelled and extended. Honeymoon, annual summers, the fairy palace nickname and the Bristol Channel setting check out; coordinates 51.2274, -3.6247 correct **[A]**.
- **Horsley Towers.** *Major correction:* the pool credited Charles Barry with rebuilding it for the Lovelaces. Barry built the original Tudor Revival house 1820 to 1829 for the banker William Currie, and King bought the estate after Currie's death in 1829. The great hall dates to **1849**, to Lovelace's own designs; the Romanesque and Rhenish towers and the chapel **from 1859**, after her death. The pool's image of the house growing more elaborate as its mistress grows more ill describes work she did not live to see, and is cut **[A: Wikipedia, 'Horsley Towers']**. Coordinates 51.2654, -0.4272 exact.

**De Morgan.** From 1840, advanced mathematics by post with Augustus De Morgan of University College London **[A]**; coordinates 51.5246, -0.134 correct. *Quotation corrected:* the pool had him calling her power "of the first rate, comparable to any man's." His actual formulation, per the wikitext, is that her skill **might lead her to become an original mathematical investigator, perhaps of first-rate eminence**. The journey quotes him directly.

**Turin.** 1840: Babbage's seminar on the Analytical Engine, attended by Menabrea **[A]**. **Medora Leigh:** 27 February 1841, told that Byron fathered Medora Leigh on his half-sister Augusta, she writes back accepting it **[R: Wikipedia; full text of the letter not consulted]**.

## 4. The Notes, 1842 to 1843

October 1842: Menabrea's 'Notions sur la machine analytique de M. Charles Babbage' appears in the Bibliotheque universelle de Geneve **[A]**. Over roughly nine months she translates it and, at Babbage's encouragement, appends Notes A through G running about three times the length of the translation; published September 1843 in Richard Taylor's Scientific Memoirs vol. 3, signed A.A.L. **[A: Wikipedia; Biography.com]**. **Note G** sets out a method for computing Bernoulli numbers on the engine **[A: Wikipedia; Computer History Museum]**; "first computer program" and "first programmer" are **[R]**, later labels contested by historians who stress Babbage's prior unpublished sketches. The journey states the algorithm and refuses the superlative.

**The credit dispute, unresolved.** Bromley: the programs cited had already been prepared by Babbage years earlier. Wolfram: her exposition is cleaner and more sophisticated than Babbage's drafts **[A for both: Bromley, 'Difference and Analytical Engines'; Wolfram, 'Untangling the Tale of Ada Lovelace', 2015]**. Same evidence, opposite conclusions.

**Quotations.** The pool supplied ten; two had serious problems.

1. **The loom sentence** ("weaves algebraical patterns just as the Jacquard-loom weaves flowers and leaves") **[A: Note A, Scientific Memoirs vol. 3, text at fourmilab.ch/babbage/sketch.html]**. Used verbatim.
2. **The no-pretensions passage** (Note G) was misspelled in the pool and silently spliced across an omitted sentence. Three fixes, confirmed at en.wikiquote.org and psychclassics.yorku.ca **[A, corrected]**: "any thing" is two words; the dropped sentence "It can do whatever we know how to order it to perform." is restored; "anticipating **any** analytical relations or truths." The journey prints the restored text.
3. **"Thus, although it is not itself the being that reflects…"** *Rejected.* Verbatim words, wrong attribution: the sentence sits in the main body of **Menabrea's** Sketch, in her translation, not in her Note G, being present at psychclassics.yorku.ca/Lovelace/menabrea.htm and **absent** from the Notes-only lovelace.htm. A common misattribution; **not used**.
4. **The music passage** and **"an embodying of the science of operations"** **[A: Note A]**, both used verbatim. Four further Note A passages were verified clean and left unused.

## 5. A calculus of the nervous system, 1843 to 1845

**Carpenter.** Annabella assigns William Benjamin Carpenter to tutor the children and act as moral guide; he reportedly attempts an affair, which she ends **[R: Wikipedia; year given as 1843 or 1844]**.

**Andrew Crosse.** 1844 visit **[A: Wikipedia]**. Setup confirmed: apparatus for atmospheric electricity on insulated wire, some 2,500 voltaic cells observed by 1836, the local name "the thunder and lightning man" **[A: Wikipedia, 'Andrew Crosse']**. *Coordinates corrected* from 51.1173, -3.045 to **51.0806, -3.1143**. A close, possibly romantic relationship with his son John runs from about 1844 to her death **[R]**.

**The ambition.** She writes to Woronzow Greig of a plan for "a calculus of the nervous system" **[A: Wikipedia]**. Never written; recorded as unfinished. **Royal Institution** attendance is asserted on pattern with no lecture confirmed **[R]**, and is dropped.

## 6. The turf, later 1840s to 1851

More than 3,000 pounds lost on horses through the later 1840s; in 1851 a mathematical staking system attempted with a syndicate of men fails and leaves her thousands in debt **[A: Wikipedia]**.

- **Epsom Downs** is in no source reached; Wikipedia describes the scheme without naming a course. Re-tagged **[A]** to **[R]**; coordinates corrected from 51.3103, -0.2569 to about **51.3160, -0.2500**.
- **Pawning the family jewels through Babbage as discreet intermediary** is **unsupported**: no mention of jewels or pawning in the wikitext, and Babbage's documented role is different (the failed executor letter below). Cut, its absence noted in that stop's `sources`.
- **Newmarket** and **Doncaster** rest on secondary retellings of the syndicate's range, with no dated letter naming either **[R]**. Newmarket keeps one stop naming Doncaster inside its campa; Doncaster gets no pin.
- **The Great Exhibition.** Pool tagged her attendance **[A]** at 51.5033, -0.1666; the Hyde Park site is **51.50306, -0.17000**, and **her attendance is not recorded** in Wikipedia's Lovelace article, Britannica, or the Great Exhibition article. Re-tagged **[R]**. That Babbage's engines were not exhibited is consistent, the Great Exhibition article cataloguing exhibits down to Colt's revolvers without mentioning him: evidence by omission, labelled as such.

## 7. The last year, 1851 to 1852

**Diagnosis, redated.** The pool dated it to 1852; Wikipedia records a letter to Babbage of **12 August 1851**, written "when she was dying of cancer," asking him to be her executor. Recast: **1851 diagnosed, 1852 terminal decline** **[A, corrected]**. *Reversed claim:* the pool said Babbage "declines, unwilling or unable to face the paperwork"; in fact the letter "did not give him the necessary legal authority," so the appointment failed on a technicality **[A: Wikipedia]**.

**Great Malvern.** Gully's hydropathy clinic, established 1842, Darwin and Nightingale among its patients **[A: Wikipedia, 'James Manby Gully']**. Her own stay is **[R]**, resting on Toole and tradition. *Worst coordinate error in the pool:* 51.13, -2.327 is about **109 km** off, landing near Shepton Mallet; Wikipedia gives **52.1114, -2.3290**, only the latitude digit being wrong. **Harley Street** stands for consultation through 1851 and 1852, prescriptions contradicting each other across rest, opium and bloodletting **[R: no consulting room named in surviving letters]**.

**The confession.** 30 August 1852: she confesses something to her husband and he withdraws from the bedside **[A: Wikipedia]**. **No source states what was said**, and the journey says so rather than filling it.

**Death.** 27 November 1852, aged 36, of uterine cancer, understood today as likely cervical **[A: Wikipedia; Biography.com]**. *Address contradiction:* Marylebone (Wikipedia) against St James's Square (other accounts); the journey pins Marylebone and names the alternative. *Unsourced anecdote:* Dickens reading her the death of Paul Dombey is **not** in Wikipedia's article, which lists him only among her acquaintances. **Burial** beside Byron at St Mary Magdalene, Hucknall, 3 December 1852, at her own request **[A: parish register; Wikipedia]**; the pool's lenses gave longitude -1.2016 and -1.2006, and the journey uses -1.2016.

## 8. Afterlife

- **Six copies** of the 1843 first-edition printing survive: three at Harvard, one at Oklahoma, one at the U.S. Air Force Academy, one privately held **[A: Bonhams census at the 2018 sale; may be revised]**. **20 July 2018:** the private copy sells for 95,000 pounds **[A]**.
- **10 December 1980:** the Department of Defense approves the Ada reference manual, MIL-STD-1815 echoing her birth year **[A: ANSI/MIL-STD-1815A]**. **2009:** Ada Lovelace Day founded by Suw Charman-Anderson; **2012** Google Doodle, **2015** passport illustrations pairing her with Babbage, **2018** New York Times 'Overlooked' obituary **[A]**.
- **8 March 2022:** bronze by Etienne and Mary Millner, after Margaret Sarah Carpenter's 1836 portrait, unveiled at Millbank Quarter **[A: City of Westminster]**; same year Nvidia names a GPU microarchitecture Ada Lovelace **[A]**. **2025:** the National Portrait Gallery acquires the only three known photographs of her, two Claudet daguerreotypes of about 1843 and one of a Henry Wyndham Phillips portrait **[A: NPG]**.
- **Kirkby Mallory memorial**, Grade II listed **[A: Historic England]**; **St James's Square blue plaque [A]**. *Gloss corrected:* the pool said she lived at Kirkby Mallory "after her marriage"; she lived there as a **child** from January 1816, her married homes being Ockham Park, Ashley Combe and Horsley Towers **[A: Wikipedia, 'Kirkby Mallory']**.

## 9. Interlocks with the existing atlas

**Named in campa [A unless marked].** **`joseph_marie_jacquard`**, the strongest link in the corpus, whose journey already carries her loom sentence verbatim at pin **51.4978, -0.1745**, dated 1843-01-01, noting the pin sits with the woven portrait rather than at any Lovelace address: **inherited byte-identical**, with the silk portrait mistaken for an engraving. **`tony_skyrme`, `john_von_neumann`, `claude_shannon`**, the punched-card lineage carried forward there through Los Alamos, von Neumann's machines and Shannon's chess programme; the Washington stop inherits the canonical pin **38.8951, -77.0364** used there for Hollerith's census machine. **`newton` [R]**, Babbage holding the Lucasian chair 1828 to 1839, Newton's after Barrow, a tenure no file states, so the lineage is inferred and flagged. **`john_locke` [R]**, the Bodleian's Lovelace Collection being named for the Earls of Lovelace, which the corpus never states.

**Not used.** **`joseph_fourier`** (the 1805 Jacquard patent runs through the prefectural machine Fourier served; no attested contact) and **`gauss`** (Copley Medal 1838 from the Royal Society, whose Fellows included Babbage). **False positive:** **`arthur_edward_waite`** names his wife Lucasta "after Lovelace's lady," the referent being the poet **Richard** Lovelace. **Negative finding:** a corpus-wide grep for Lovelace, Babbage and Menabrea, and for the Kaaba, Temple Mount and Buenos Aires pins, returned only the Jacquard, Locke and Waite hits, so those three pins are not inherited. **Considered and not used:** **`anni_albers`** (writes about the drawloom-to-power-loom lineage a century after Lovelace's mill tour; no citation of Lovelace herself, the overlap is thematic only, removed under the house-style tightening of 2026-08-14).

## 10. Gaps, stated as gaps

Three blanks: the content of the 30 August 1852 confession, the name of the 1833 tutor, and the Flyology manuscript. Four unreconciled pairs: the death address (Marylebone against St James's Square), the 1837 illness (post-childbirth against cholera), the separation settlement (April against March 1816), Somerville's address (Mayfair against Chelsea). Two phrases attributed everywhere and traced nowhere: "Enchantress of Number" and the Dickens deathbed reading. Three sitings resting on retelling: the Midland mill, the racecourses, and her attendance at the Great Exhibition.
