# Túpac Amaru II (José Gabriel Condorcanqui): research report
*2026-08-14. Slug `tupac_amaru_ii`. Companion: `tupac_amaru_ii.journey.json`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named inline. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition: repeated in the literature, documentary chain not visible from here. Contradictions are printed as contradictions and left standing.

**Standing caution.** The reachable base is overwhelmingly encyclopedic (Spanish and English Wikipedia) plus a few primary transcriptions published by the Congreso del Perú. The five monographs everyone cites (Walker 2014, Lewin 1957, L. E. Fisher 1966, O'Phelan Godoy 1988, Thomson 2002) were **not read** in this pass; where they appear below they are cited at second hand, as the authority a reference work names.

---

## 1. Birth, name, lineage (1738 or 1742 to 1760)

**The birth year is contradictory and stays contradictory.** es.wikipedia: 19 March 1738, at Surimana, province of Canas (Cusco). en.wikipedia: c. 1742. **[A, both editions, "Túpac Amaru II"]** Baptism is reported in both as 1 May, same day of year, four years apart. That identical day-of-year suggests one register entry read by two traditions, but **no reachable source reproduces the parish register**, so it is left open. The journey file carries 1738 and puts the spread in `date_confidence`. **Parents:** the cacique Miguel Condorcanqui and Rosa Noguera **[R, secondary genealogies at second hand]**.

**Coordinate warning.** The geography pass proposed -14.3167, -71.3667 for Surimana, ~20 km southeast of Tungasuca, whose coordinates are firm at -14.157, -71.505 per es.wikipedia *Distrito de Túpac Amaru*. The proposed pin is almost certainly wrong. **The journey file uses -14.1535, -71.5749, the value already fixed in `micaela_bastidas.journey.json`**: a pin adjudicated elsewhere is not re-litigated per subject. Inherited, not independently verified.

**Orphaned about age twelve, raised by an aunt and uncle in Cusco. [A, Wikipedia]** No names, no address, no dates. A gap.

**Colegio San Francisco de Borja, Cusco**, the Jesuit school for sons of caciques: Spanish, Quechua, Latin. **[A, Wikipedia; L. E. Fisher 1966 at second hand]** Coordinates corrected to -13.5155, -71.97848, Plazoleta Tricentenario, ~400 m from the value first proposed; the institution moved during its history, so one pin is a simplification **[A, es.wikipedia]**.

**Descent from Túpac Amaru I is asserted, not documented. [R]** en.wikipedia states the lineage only as Condorcanqui's own claim, advanced in the Oropesa litigation. Túpac Amaru I, last Inca of Vilcabamba, was taken beyond Vilcabamba by a party under Martín García Óñez de Loyola and beheaded before the cathedral in Cusco's central square on **24 September 1572 [A, Wikipedia "Túpac Amaru I"]**. That is firm; the bridge across two centuries is not.

**Resonance, filed as [R]:** Francisco de Toledo, the viceroy who ordered the 1572 execution, was **Count of Oropesa**; the dignity José Gabriel litigated for was the **Marquisate of Oropesa**. Not the same title, and no source consulted asserts a legal descent from one to the other.

## 2. Curaca and muleteer (1760 to 1779)

**Marriage to Micaela Bastidas Puyucahua, 25 May 1760. [A, es.wikipedia "Micaela Bastidas"]** **Correction:** the wedding was at **Surimana**, Iglesia de Nuestra Señora de la Purificación, not Pampamarca. The geography pass conflated the venue with Micaela's birthplace: she was born at Pampamarca, 23 June 1744.

**Curaca of Surimana, Pampamarca and Tungasuca; ~350 mules; muleteer on the Cusco-Potosí roads. [A, Wikipedia]** The mule count recurs without a document ever being named: treat as **[R]**.

**Grievances, as the sources order them:** the **mita** to Potosí; the **reparto de mercancías**; the **1776 creation of the Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata**, redirecting Andean silver toward Buenos Aires; the **1778 alcabala increase** on common goods including rum and pulque. **[A, "Rebellion of Túpac Amaru II"]**

**Correction, anachronism removed.** An earlier draft tied the 1778 alcabala rise to Spain's financing of the American Revolutionary War. **Spain did not enter that war until June 1779.** The source frames the rise as Bourbon fiscal reform under Gálvez and Areche; the war-funding clause is dropped rather than hedged.

**Cold weather damaged the 1778-1779 harvests. [R, es.wikipedia only; the English edition does not carry it]**

**Litigation 1776-1780. [A]** He sues, including before the Real Audiencia in Lima, for the Marquisate of Oropesa, and petitions at Tinta, Cusco and Lima for relief from tribute and the mita. He loses; the petitions are ignored. **No date exists for the Lima journey itself**; the geography pass proposed 1777 and the journey file carries it hedged.

## 3. The rising (November 1780)

**The most consequential correction in the file.** The seizure and the execution of the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga were treated in an earlier pass as a source dispute between 4 and 10 November. It is not a dispute; en.wikipedia separates two events. **4 November 1780, Yanaoca:** after a celebration for Charles III, Condorcanqui and his supporters **seize** Arriaga **[A]**. **10 November 1780, Tungasuca:** Arriaga is **publicly hanged** before a crowd of indigenous people, mestizos and criollos, by **his own slave Antonio Oblitas**, who is himself executed on 18 May 1781 **[A]**.

**16 November 1780, Tungasuca, the bando. [A, both editions; `micaela_bastidas.journey.json`]** He proclaims the end of the reparto, the mita and the customs house, and the emancipation of enslaved Africans and Afro-Peruvians. Micaela Bastidas signs the orders carrying it into the provinces.

**Pillpinto and Tomasa Tito Condemayta. [A, date unresolved]** The cacica of Acos led a women's battalion at the **Pillpinto bridge**, not at Sangarará; the earlier draft placed her at the wrong battle. **es.wikipedia dates it 16 November 1780; the English chronology dates the "Battle of Pillpinto" 26 November 1780.** Both stand; the journey file carries 16 November with the spread in `date_confidence`.

**18 November 1780, Sangarará. [A]** A royalist militia column is destroyed. The royalists take refuge in the church; the attackers fire what remains of the roof, which collapses. **The toll is contested:** one account gives 578 royalist soldiers all killed; es.wikipedia *Distrito de Sangarará* reports ~395 bodies counted afterwards. The journey file gives the range and settles neither. Bishop Moscoso y Peralta excommunicates him for the burning **[A, via `micaela_bastidas.journey.json`]**.

**26 November 1780, proclaimed José I. [A, es.wikipedia]** But see the manifesto problem below: the date is softer than the assertion sounds.

## 4. The documents, and what is wrong with them

**(a) The Tungasuca manifesto:** "los reyes de Castilla me han tenido usurpada la corona y dominio de mis gentes cerca de tres siglos." Wording **verified verbatim**, Congreso del Perú, *La Rebelión de Túpac Amaru II*, Vol. 6, p. 161 **[A as text]**. Two problems, flagged not resolved. First, es.wikipedia frames it inside the 26 November self-proclamation, but the cited primary page and its neighbour (p. 160) carry **mid-November datelines**: Tungasuca, 15 November 1780, and a marginal note on a letter of 16 November. **26 November is not settled.** Second, the Congreso edition titles it ***"Presunto manifiesto de Túpac Amaru a la ciudadanía."*** *Presunto* means presumed: **the editors do not vouch for its authenticity.** Carried as a manifesto attributed to him.

**(b) The proclamation on the outcries**, in English translation: "...repeated outcries directed to me by the indigenous peoples of this and surrounding provinces... Justified outcries that have produced no remedy from the royal courts." **[A]** Verified verbatim in en.wikipedia, cited to Chambers and Chasteen, *Latin American Independence* (Hackett, 2010), pp. 33-34. The continuation ("I have acted... only against the mentioned abuses and to preserve the peace and well-being of Indians, mestizos, zambos, as well as native-born whites and blacks") is **the same document and page range**: one attestation, not two. **Date correction:** an earlier draft gave 4 November 1780; Wikipedia gives no date and places the proclamation **after Arriaga's execution**. Tungasuca as place of issue is plausible but **not stated in the source**. This is a modern translation, one remove from the document; the Spanish original was not checked.

**(c) The cell at Cusco:** "aquí no hay más cómplices, que tú y yo; tú, por opresor, y yo, por libertador, merecemos la muerte." **[A as text, [R] as testimony]** Verified in Congreso del Perú, Vol. 1, p. 728. It was **not** said at the capture at Langui but in the prison cell at Cusco, to Areche, who came demanding names; and it is **not** from the trial record, since the Congreso page carries *"Las memorias de Juan Bautista Túpac Amaru"*, the half-brother's memoir published 1825, decades later and frankly partisan. Right scene, wrong genre of source. The circulating English version ("There are no accomplices here other than you and I...") and the modernized Spanish paraphrase are **the same utterance in other clothes**, not independent witnesses.

**(d) The record of the torment, 29 April 1781:** "ay, ay, ay misericordia Señor...ay, ay, ya estoy perdido...ay, ay, nada más, no hay más, los indios." **[A]** *Diligencia de ejecución del tormento*, recorded by the scribe Manuel Espinavete López, via es.wikipedia. The most nearly documentary of the four: a court officer writing down what a man said under torture.

Also carried, lower confidence: **"de qué me sirve que sea Tupa Amaro que no hemos de hacer algo por nuestros paisanos"** **[A, trial record of the oidor Benito de la Mata Linares]**; **"Solo siento de los paisanos criollos... sino que vivamos como hermanos"** **[A as quoted, [R] as to occasion]**; **"que muera el mal gobierno, viva el rey y no pague alcabala, ni aduana..."** **[A as quoted in several accounts; primary not seen]**.

## 5. The campaign (December 1780 to April 1781)

**6 December 1780, Ayaviri taken. [A]** The revolt extends toward the altiplano. **8 December 1780, near the Pisac bridge [A]:** his cousin **Diego Cristóbal** leads over 6,000 followers toward Paucartambo and the Sacred Valley to encircle Cusco from the north; royalists defeat the column.

**The strategic argument. [A]** Micaela Bastidas urges an immediate assault while Cusco is thinly held; he moves south into the mining districts instead. `micaela_bastidas.journey.json` gives his reason in his own frame: he will not spend indigenous lives against a garrison fielding indigenous fighters in its front rank. Historians read the delay as the decisive missed chance. Both readings carried, neither adjudicated.

**The siege of Cusco: the circulating dates are wrong at both ends. [A, corrected]** 20 December 1780 is when he **ordered the army to depart**, not a siege start. es.wikipedia dates the operation 2 December 1780 to 9 January 1781; en.wikipedia ends the siege 11 January 1781 after "over one week." **The actual investment ran roughly 2 to 10 January 1781.** And **the defenders were not 12,000 including 8,000 reinforcements**: es.wikipedia puts them at 3,000 to 12,000, while the Lima reinforcement of 15,000 to 17,500 **entered Cusco on 24 February 1781, six weeks after the siege lifted**, and did not defend it.

**22 March 1781, Pucacasa. [A]** A surprise attack on del Valle's camp in frozen terrain is repelled. **Not mapped: no coordinates for Pucacasa were obtainable**, so it has no stop. A gap, not a guessed pin.

**5 April 1781, Combapata. [A]** Royalist cavalry routs the rebels in open ground; he escapes into the Vilcanota. Checacupe follows on 6 April. *Correction:* the geography pass dated this March 1781 and framed it as a retreat waypoint; it was a pitched defeat in April. Coordinates adjusted to -14.1011, -71.4311.

**6-7 April 1781, Langui: the betrayal. [A]** Taken by his own officers, **Ventura Landaeta and Francisco de la Cruz** (also given as Francisco Santa Cruz). Coordinates corrected to -14.4326, -71.2727 per es.wikipedia *Distrito de Langui*, a shift of ~23 km. **The exact seizure point is genuinely unsettled**: some accounts give Ninamarca or the road toward Tinta, and the Langui district article carries no mention of the capture at all.

**Micaela and the sons Hipólito and Fernando are taken at the same moment, in the Tinta and Langui district. [A]** **Correction: the clause "while trying to reach La Paz" is deleted**, unsupported and geographically implausible (~300 km across the altiplano). The party reaches Cusco on **14 April 1781** with Areche and some thirty other prisoners **[A]**.

## 6. The eighteenth of May, 1781

**15 May 1781, Areche pronounces sentence. [A]** The text, in English translation via en.wikipedia, orders the condemned tied "...to four horses who will then be driven at once toward the four corners of the plaza, pulling the arms and legs from his body." The Spanish original was not read.

**18 May 1781, Plaza de Armas, Cusco. [A, both editions]** He is made to watch: the cacica **Tomasa Tito Condemayta**; his son **Hipólito**, tongue removed, hanged; his uncle **Francisco Tupa Amaro**; his brother-in-law **Antonio Bastidas**; and **Micaela Bastidas**, tongue cut out, the garrote failing because her neck was judged too thin, finished with cords and beating. **Antonio Oblitas** is executed the same day. Then the quartering by four horses, which fails; he is beheaded and dismembered by hand. Contemporary account: *"atado a las colas de cuatro caballos que tirando en direcciones opuestas no pudieron descuartizarlo."*

**Correction, small and load-bearing:** an earlier draft credited "later forensic assessment" for the failure of the quartering. **No such assessment exists or can exist**: the body was dismembered, dispersed, and the torso burned. The attribution belongs to chroniclers alone.

**The dispersal. [A, es.wikipedia]** Head to Cusco, then Tinta. Arms to **Tungasuca** and **Ayapata** (Carabaya). Legs to **Livitaca** (Chumbivilcas) and **Santa Rosa** (Melgar). Torso burned on a hill above Cusco, ashes into the Río Watanay, so that no relic and no grave exist. **Correction: Surimana is not on the documented list**; an earlier draft placed remains at his birthplace under an **[A]** tag the sources do not support. Dropped.

**Areche's bando. [A, es.wikipedia]** After the execution he decrees the suppression of Quechua in official and public use, of indigenous dress, of hereditary Inca titles and nobility, of portraits of Inca rulers, and of any commemoration of Inca descent. `guaman_poma_de_ayala.journey.json` records the same decree from the far end of the corpus.

**Institutional aftermath. [A]** The crown abolishes the corregidor and the reparto, and suppresses the hereditary kuraka title: the institutions whose abuses provoked the revolt, ended by the revolt's defeat.

## 7. The rebellion after the man (1781 to 1783)

**Túpac Katari. [A]** February 1781, Julián Apaza takes the name **Túpac Katari**, honoring Túpac Amaru and the executed Katari brothers, and rises around La Paz with **Bartolina Sisa** and **Gregoria Apaza**. First siege of La Paz from March 1781; second 7 August to 17 October 1781, broken by José de Reseguín with 10,000 troops. Sorata besieged and sacked, August 1781. The movement is **allied but organizationally distinct**, and the journey file says so.

**26 January 1782, Sicuani. [A]** Diego Cristóbal negotiates surrender with Bishop Moscoso and José del Valle and accepts amnesty; some 30,000 rebels reportedly accept terms by February 1782 **[R on the figure]**.

**The amnesty is broken. [A, corrected]** Diego Cristóbal is captured **with his whole family at Marcapata on 15 March 1783** and executed at Cusco on **19 July 1783**: flesh torn with red-hot pincers, hanged, quartered. His wife and mother die with him; surviving relatives are exiled to Spain, most dying on the voyage. **Arrest and execution were four months and one province apart**, not simultaneous as an earlier draft had it.

**~100,000 dead across the 1780-1783 cycle, ~10,000 of them from starvation and disease during the sieges. [R]** Wikipedia relays secondary estimates that vary by historian. Not treated as a figure.

## 8. Afterlives

**Fernando Túpac Amaru.** About ten at the executions and made to watch; exiled toward Africa, **escaped**, then imprisoned at Cádiz; **died in Madrid, 30 July 1798 [A, en.wikipedia]**. **Correction: there is no shipwreck in the cited source.** The shipwreck in circulation belongs to the *San Pedro de Alcántara*, which carried Túpac Amaru relatives to Spain and wrecked off Peniche in 1786; Fernando's presence aboard could not be verified and is **not asserted**. `micaela_bastidas.journey.json` does carry a shipwreck for him: **the two files disagree, and this file records rather than resolves it.** es.wikipedia also calls the 5 April 2025 repatriation **symbolic remains** (*restos simbólicos*); his Madrid burial site is unidentified.

**Belgrano, 6 July 1816. [A, via `belgrano.journey.json`]** He proposes to the Congress of Tucumán the restoration of the Inca dynasty with its capital at Cusco: a direct afterlife of the lineage claim, and one of the two places the journey file names another traveler in a campa.

**Bolívar at Cusco, 1825. [A, via `bolivar.journey.json` and `micaela_bastidas.journey.json`]** Offered the crown of the Incas, he refuses it and sends it to Sucre. **The files record that neither he nor Sucre cites the couple executed in the same square.** Because the relation is a non-citation, Bolívar is **not** named in any campa: shared ground is not a relation.

**1969 and 1972, Lima. [A, corrected]** Velasco Alvarado elevates him to national-hero status in 1969 alongside the agrarian reform and puts his image on currency. **The Government Palace's Salón Pizarro was rebaptised Salón Túpac Amaru in 1972, not 1969**; the two acts should not be merged. Belaúnde later called the room "Túpac Pizarro." Coordinates corrected to -12.04514, -77.02989.

**Panteón de los Próceres, Lima. [A]** He and Micaela are among the **41 effigies**, not the **24 próceres whose remains rest in the crypt**: a distinction that matters precisely because there are no remains. Coordinates corrected to -12.0541, -77.0325. **Gap: the effigy's installation date is not given in any source reached.** The Panteón was dedicated in 1924; the journey file uses that year and says so.

**Avenida Túpac Amaru, Lima. [A, corrected, not mapped]** Renamed **1974**, not "the 1970s"; six districts, not three; the "25 kilometres" figure is unsupported and the Comas monument is absent from the cited source. Left out of the journey file.

**The name exported. [A]** Uruguay's **Tupamaros** (MLN-T) leaflet under a related slogan in November 1964 and claim the name for a Montevideo action in August 1965 **[R on the first claiming]**. A Venezuelan party later takes it. Peru's **MRTA**, formed 1980-1982, takes his name and image and ends as a functioning organization with the 1996-1997 seizure of the Japanese ambassador's residence in Lima. The rapper **Tupac Amaru Shakur** was born Lesane Parish Crooks on 16 June 1971 and **renamed afterwards** by Afeni Shakur, so "1971 (naming)" is wrong **[A]**. Not mapped: no defensible pin exists for an act of naming.

**Inkarrí. [R]** Andean oral tradition folds him into the pre-existing Inkarrí cycle: the dismembered body of a martyred Inca reassembling underground, to rise and restore Tahuantinsuyo. Documented by twentieth-century folklorists, notably Arguedas. The tradition is older than he is and absorbs him; it is not evidence about him. Carried as the closing stop, tagged **[R]**, pinned at Surimana.

## 9. Honest gaps

No reachable source reproduces the **parish register**, so the 1738/1742 split cannot be closed. **Pucacasa has no obtainable coordinates** and therefore no stop. The **Pillpinto date** (16 vs 26 November 1780), the **Sangarará toll** (~395 counted vs 578 asserted), and the **exact point of capture near Langui** are all live disagreements. The **Panteón effigy installation date** is unknown. Where this subject's sources are thinner than his wife's, the file leans on hers; where the two disagree (Fernando's shipwreck), both readings stand. And **none of the five standard monographs was read**.
