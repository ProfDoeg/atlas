# Jonas Savimbi: research report

*Compiled 2026-08-11 for the Atlas of Journeys. Subject: Jonas Malheiro Savimbi (1934-2002), founder and lifelong president of UNITA, Angola.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, source named in place. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, or a claim carried by secondary literature that could not be pushed back to a document. Contradictions are flagged and left standing; unreachable sources are named with the reason.

**Method.** Five lenses (chronology, geography, quotations, interlock, afterlife) were verified against English and Portuguese Wikipedia, OpenStreetMap/Nominatim, and the few press items still online, producing twenty-one substantive corrections. Coordinates were the largest failure class: four pins were wrong by more than 90 km and one placed a Congolese town in the wrong hemisphere.

---

## 1. Munhango and the mission schools (1934-1958)

Born **3 August 1934** at the railway settlement of **Munhango** on the Benguela line, Bié Province, son of **Lote (Loth) Malheiro Savimbi**, stationmaster and lay preacher of the Evangelical Congregational Church of Angola. **[A: en.wikipedia and pt.wikipedia, "Jonas Savimbi"]**

*Coordinate correction.* The geography lens gave -12.1833, **18.05**, roughly 55 km west of the town. en.wikipedia "Munhango" gives 12°10'S 18°33'E and OSM returns **-12.158, 18.555**, which is used. The chronology lens gave -12.05, 18.55 and the afterlife lens -12.1333, 18.6667: three lenses, three pins.

Schooled in the mission network, including the Congregationalist complex at **Dondi** (Currie Institute), a principal Protestant school for Ovimbundu children. **[R: Bridgland (1986), not read directly; standard in the literature, absent from Wikipedia]**

*Province correction.* The geography lens placed Dondi in **Bié** at -12.55, 16.35. Dondi is in **Huambo** Province, Cachiungo municipality; OSM locates the Hospital do Dondi at **-12.5398, 16.2347**. Province and longitude both wrong.

At twenty-four he receives a scholarship from the **United Church of Christ** to study medicine at the **University of Lisbon**. **[A: en.wikipedia, verbatim: "At age 24, he was awarded a scholarship from the United Church of Christ to study medicine at the University of Lisbon."]**

*Two corrections.* (a) The chronology lens dated the scholarship to **1950**; age 24 for a man born 3 August 1934 falls in **1958**, so 1950 is wrong by eight years. (b) The geography lens called it "a Portuguese government scholarship." It was an American missionary body, which is what distinguishes Savimbi from the Lisbon-sponsored *assimilados* of his generation.

## 2. Switzerland and exile politics (c. 1960-1962)

Under **PIDE** surveillance he leaves Portugal for **Lausanne**, then enrols at the **University of Fribourg**, switching from medicine to social and political sciences. **[A for the flight and the two cities: en.wikipedia; R for the year]**

*Date contradiction, flagged and not resolved.* The chronology lens dated the flight to **1958** and tagged it [A]. That cannot stand beside the corrected Lisbon arrival, also 1958. Wikipedia gives no year. The only hard downstream anchor is the **August 1960** meeting with Roberto, so the flight falls in **1959-1960**; the journey dates it 1960-01-01 with the range in `date_confidence`. **The [A] tag is withdrawn to [R].** The change of discipline happened at **Fribourg**, not at Lausanne as the chronology lens had it.

**August 1960**, Fribourg: he meets **Holden Roberto**, leader of the movement that becomes the FNLA. **Late September 1960**: he speaks at **Kampala** for the UDEAN students and meets **Tom Mboya**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

*Correction and an open question.* The chronology lens attached a meeting with **Jomo Kenyatta** to the same trip. Per en.wikipedia "Jomo Kenyatta," he "remained imprisoned at Lokitaung until 1959 and was then exiled to Lodwar until 1961," moving to Maralal only in April 1961 and released 14 August 1961. Either the encounter belongs to a later trip or it did not happen; **it is not dated 1960-09 in the journey file.** The quotations lens carries "J'ai été convaincu par Kenyatta," attributed to French media in late September 1960 (§9): either that date is wrong or the meeting is misplaced. **Recorded, not adjudicated.**

**December 1960**: Roberto telephones urging him to **Léopoldville**. He joins the **UPA** in 1961, attends a Non-Aligned-linked summit in **Yugoslavia**, visits the **United Nations** in September 1961, transfers to **Lausanne** that December for law and political science, and becomes secretary-general of **UNEA** at **Lucerne** in March 1962. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

## 3. The break, China, and the founding of UNITA (1962-1966)

**3 April 1962**: named foreign minister of the FNLA's exile government at Léopoldville. **[A: en.wikipedia]** *Acronym correction:* the chronology lens printed "**GREA**" while expanding it as *Governo Revolucionário de Angola no Exílio*, which yields **GRAE**. Wikipedia does not name the seat of government; Léopoldville is inferred from the FNLA's base. **[R for the seat]**

He breaks with Roberto over the GRAE's inaction, conceiving a new movement with **António da Costa Fernandes** **[A: en.wikipedia "Jonas Savimbi" / "UNITA"]**; travels to **Beijing** in 1964 seeking Chinese backing **[A]**; and receives guerrilla instruction at **Nanjing** in 1965 **[R: Bridgland (1986); academy and dates vary by source, no primary reached]**. A **Cairo** passage in 1964-65 is asserted by secondary biographies, is **[R] and undocumented**, and is omitted from the journey rather than pinned on faith.

*Chronological correction.* The chronology lens placed the Chinese training under a **1980** entry, fifteen years out of place. It belongs to **1964-65**; through the 1980s the patrons were South Africa and the United States, Chinese support having largely lapsed after 1975. The geography lens dated it correctly.

**1965**: doctorate in law and political science, **University of Lausanne**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**

**13 March 1966**: at a congress of roughly two hundred delegates at **Muangai**, Moxico, Savimbi founds **UNITA**. **[A: en.wikipedia "UNITA" for date, place, delegate count]**

*Coordinate correction, the second largest in the file.* The chronology lens gave -13.0, 20.0, about 60 km out; the geography lens gave -13.4833, 21.4333, roughly **180 km** out. Muangai (now **Lutuai**, a municipality since 5 September 2024 per pt.wikipedia) is at approximately **-12.525, 19.911** (OSM), some 80 km south of Luena, and that is used.

**25 December 1966**: UNITA's first guerrilla action, against the Benguela railway at **Teixeira de Sousa** (Luau). **[A for the date and for eastern Angola: en.wikipedia "UNITA"; R for the town, which rests on Bridgland]** **1967-68**: **Kenneth Kaunda** withdraws Zambian sanctuary after repeated attacks on the line carrying Zambian copper. **[R: Bridgland and standard accounts]**

## 4. Independence and the three movements (1975-1979)

The war of independence ends with the **Alvor Agreement**; Angola becomes independent **11 November 1975**. **[A: en.wikipedia "Jonas Savimbi", "Angolan Civil War"]** *Correction:* the chronology lens said UNITA withdrew south "by August 1975." en.wikipedia "Angolan Civil War": "In **July 1975**, the MPLA violently forced the FNLA out of Luanda, and UNITA voluntarily withdrew to its stronghold in the south."

**11 November 1975**, **Huambo**: the **Democratic People's Republic of Angola** is proclaimed. **[A: en.wikipedia]** *Attribution correction:* the geography lens credited it to UNITA alone. It was proclaimed by the **FNLA and UNITA jointly**, Huambo the de jure capital and Jamba the de facto seat.

**1977**: the MPLA formally declares itself Marxist-Leninist; the 1977-78 campaigns to destroy UNITA in the south fail to capture or kill Savimbi. **[A: en.wikipedia]** **Jamba**, in remote Cuando Cubango, becomes UNITA's capital: schools, hospital, landing strip, closed to journalists except by invitation. **[A: en.wikipedia "Jamba, Cuando Cubango"; contemporary press]**

*Coordinate and date corrections.* The geography lens gave -16.9667, 21.2333 and dated the headquarters "1979/1980-1999." en.wikipedia places Jamba at 17°27'21"S 22°36'47"E (**-17.456, 22.613**; OSM -17.508, 22.727) and gives the headquarters period as **1976-1992**, capture **24 December 1999**. The pin was about 200 km out and both ends of the range wrong. **-17.46, 22.61 is used**, span 1976-1999.

## 5. The Cold War corridor (1975-1986)

**Zaire** under **Mobutu Sese Seko** is a rear base and transit route for arms from South Africa and the United States, with **Kamina** airbase a staging point for CIA and South African resupply. **[A per the geography lens, citing Gleijeses, Wright and 1980s US press; the underlying items were not retrieved]** Meetings with South African officials in **Pretoria** through the 1980s. **[R: specific visits undocumented]**

**July 1985**: Congress repeals the **Clark Amendment**, reopening covert aid **[A: en.wikipedia "Clark Amendment"]**; a Washington lobbying firm begins receiving roughly **$600,000 a year** for UNITA **[A: en.wikipedia "Jonas Savimbi"]**. **30 January 1986**: **Reagan** receives Savimbi at the **White House** **[A: en.wikipedia; contemporary press]**; the chronology lens gave only "1986," the geography lens the day.

## 6. Cuito Cuanavale (1987-1988)

**14 August 1987 to 23 March 1988**: UNITA and the **SADF** fight **FAPLA** and Cuban troops at **Cuito Cuanavale**, the largest conventional battle in Africa since 1945, at a cost of roughly **3,000 UNITA** killed. **[A: en.wikipedia "Battle of Cuito Cuanavale" for dates, coordinates and casualties]**

*Outcome correction, contradiction left open.* The chronology lens said UNITA and the SADF "defeated" the offensive. The article states that **both sides claimed victory**: the SADF halted the advance on Mavinga, while FAPLA and Cuban forces held their positions east of Cuito Cuanavale and were not destroyed. The verb is softened to "halting." The wider historiographical dispute is **not adjudicated**.

*Conflation corrected.* The geography lens dated a Lomba ambush of FAPLA's **47 Brigade** to **6 September 1987**. en.wikipedia dates the Lomba engagement to **6 September to 7 October 1987** and the annihilation of 47 Brigade to **3 October 1987**. The journey keeps 1987-09-06 as the opening of the Lomba fighting and names the 3 October destruction in the campa.

**Mavinga** is twice the objective of Soviet-planned offensives (1985; 1987-88); **Menongue** is FAPLA's staging airbase, **Rundu** in Namibia the SADF's launch point. **[A: same article]**

## 7. Washington, Gbadolite, and the ballot (1989-1992)

**January 1989**: **George H. W. Bush**, before taking office, writes to Savimbi pledging "all appropriate and effective assistance." **[A: Washington Post, "Bush Assures Savimbi of U.S. Support," 12 January 1989; New York Times, David Rampe, January 1989; HRW World Report]** *Correction:* the quotations lens dated this to a **1990** meeting. It belongs to a **letter written before the inauguration**; a Bush aide told the Post the phrase meant continuation of covert aid then running about $15 million a year. The in-person meeting did occur in 1990, but the quotation is not from it.

**22 June 1989**: at Mobutu's palace at **Gbadolite**, Savimbi and **José Eduardo dos Santos** shake hands for the first time; the ceasefire soon collapses. **[A: en.wikipedia "Gbadolite"; contemporary press]** *Coordinate correction, the worst in the pool:* the geography lens gave **-2.95, 20.2667**. Gbadolite is at **4°17'N 21°01'E (4.283, 21.017)**, in Nord-Ubangi in the far north of the Congo: the pin was in the wrong hemisphere, some 800 km south.

**5 October 1989**: he thanks the **Heritage Foundation** in Washington for its advocacy of repeal. **January-February 1990**: wounded twice within two months; he travels to Washington and meets Bush. **[A: en.wikipedia "Clark Amendment", "Jonas Savimbi"]**

**31 May 1991**: the **Bicesse Accords**: ceasefire, demobilisation of some **152,000** fighters, multi-party elections. **[A: en.wikipedia "Bicesse Accords"]** *Venue note:* both lenses pinned the signing to central Lisbon (38.72, -9.14). The accords are named for **Bicesse**, in Alcabideche, Cascais, about 25 km west, hence the alternative name **Estoril Accords**; the journey moves the pin to approximately 38.73, -9.40.

**29-30 September 1992**: UN-supervised elections. Savimbi takes **40.07%** against dos Santos's **49.56%**, forcing a runoff. **[A: en.wikipedia "1992 Angolan general election"]** Results are announced 17 October; he rejects them as fraudulent and refuses the runoff. **[R for the precise sequence]**

**30 October to 2 November 1992**: the **Halloween Massacre**. Government forces kill UNITA members and supporters in Luanda and nationally; vice-president **Jeremias Chitunda** and senior advisor **Elias Salupeto Pena** are killed on 2 November. **[A: en.wikipedia; Human Rights Watch]** Estimates run from several thousand to tens of thousands and no source narrows them; **the range is reported as a range.**

## 8. The Protocol years and the last column (1993-2002)

**January-March 1993**: UNITA besieges and largely destroys **Huambo**, taking it after fifty-five days. **[A for the siege: contemporary press, UN and human rights reporting; Savimbi's personal presence is not confirmed and is stated as a gap]**

**20 November 1994**: the **Lusaka Protocol**: ceasefire, integration of UNITA fighters into army and police, cabinet posts. **[A: en.wikipedia "Lusaka Protocol"]** Savimbi does not attend; **Eugénio Manuvakola** signs for UNITA, Foreign Minister **Venâncio de Moura** for the government. *Correction:* the chronology lens had him declining the vice-presidency at the signing; dos Santos offered it in **1995** and Savimbi rejected it in **August 1996**.

**Bailundo** becomes the political headquarters and **Andulo** the military one **[A: contemporary press and UN reporting]**; the Protocol collapses in **December 1998**, and in **1999** **Operation Restore Order** (*Operação Restauro*) destroys most of UNITA's conventional capacity **[A: en.wikipedia]**. *Correction:* the chronology lens said the offensive captured "Jamba and Bailundo." **Bailundo and Andulo** fell in **October 1999**; **Jamba** fell separately on **24 December 1999** (en.wikipedia: "Angolan military forces captured the location on December 24, 1999"). Andulo was omitted from that lens entirely.

**1999-2002**: driven from fixed bases, he leads a mobile column through Moxico. **[R: press coverage; camp locations undocumented and treated as a gap]**

**22 February 2002**: killed in a firefight with FAA troops near **Lucusse**, Moxico, on the **Luvuei River**, with fifteen gunshot wounds to head, throat, upper body and legs. **[A: en.wikipedia and pt.wikipedia; contemporary press for the location]** **April 2002**: the **Luena Memorandum of Understanding** ceasefire; **August 2002**: UNITA demobilises its armed wing. **[A: en.wikipedia "UNITA"]**

*Geographic impossibility corrected.* The afterlife lens put the death site in the "**Cassamba/Chambinga** area." **Chambinga** is the highland ground east of Cuito Cuanavale, **600 km** away: the 1987-88 battlefield, dragged in from that material. Chambinga is deleted.

## 9. Quotations

Five were offered. All five are real strings; **three had wrong dates, speakers, or referents.**

1. **"J'ai été convaincu par Kenyatta"** ("I was convinced by Kenyatta"), attributed to French media, late September 1960. **[A for Wikipedia's quotation; R for the primary press item, not reached]** *Flagged:* per §2, the words and the date cannot both be right. Carried at the Kampala stop with the contradiction in the confidence field.

2. **"There are a lot of loopholes in that agreement. The agreement is not good at all."** *Corrected.* Verbatim from en.wikipedia "UNITA," but the pool dated it 26 October 1989 and attached it to Gbadolite. Wikipedia: "Johns reported Savimbi telling both of them during a **March 1989** visit with Savimbi in Angola." That October date is only the **publication** date of Michael Johns, "Savimbi's Elusive Victory in Angola," *Human Events*. The agreement is therefore the **New York Accords** of December 1988, **not** Gbadolite, which postdates the statement. **[A for the words via Wikipedia; the Johns article is unreachable, its Congressional Record link dead]**

3. **"he had died like a soldier, with a gun in his hand."** *Corrected.* Not an FAA communiqué and not spoken on 22 February. The speaker is **Brigadier Simão Carlitos Wala** of the FAA, to the agency **Lusa**, reported by BBC News, "Savimbi died with gun in hand," **25 February 2002**. **[A: BBC News]**

4. **"a victory that electrifies the world."** Reagan on UNITA, after the January 1986 meeting. About Savimbi, not by him. **[A for Wikipedia's quotation; the transcript was not reached]**

5. **"all appropriate and effective assistance."** Bush. *Corrected:* a **written pledge, January 1989**, not a 1990 meeting. See §7.

## 10. Gaps stated as gaps

- **The Kenyatta meeting.** Either misdated or invented. Not resolved.
- **The flight from Portugal.** No year in any reachable source; bracketed 1959-1960 from the downstream anchor.
- **Cairo, 1964-65.** Asserted in secondary biographies, undocumented. Left off the map.
- **Savimbi at the siege of Huambo, 1993.** Personal presence unconfirmed.
- **The mobile column, 1999-2002.** No camp locations documented; an approximate eastern Moxico pin is used and labelled as such.
- **Lopitanga.** No gazetteer entry; the Andulo coordinate (-11.487, 16.698) is used, marked approximate. en.wikipedia's "Andulo" article prints an erroneous longitude (15.833) placing the town in Huambo Province, 95 km west of the OSM position; the afterlife lens inherited that error.
- **Reburial date.** The geography lens gave **2019-10-25**; chronology and afterlife gave **2019-06-01**, and pt.wikipedia confirms reinterment on **1 June 2019** after a 30 May 2019 agreement between Isaías Samakuva and João Lourenço. **1 June 2019 is used**; the October date is a lens error, not a source contradiction.
- **Monuments.** No statue, museum or state monument documented anywhere consulted; commemoration is grave-centred. **[R, argument from absence]** Nor is any rival, unofficial grave tradition near Lucusse documented, though the question is not closed.
- **"Red September."** Bridgland documents allegations that Savimbi oversaw torture and killings framed as a witchcraft ritual during the 1983 purges. **[R, via Wikipedia's citation of Bridgland]** Not mapped: no location beyond "Jamba," no date beyond the year.
