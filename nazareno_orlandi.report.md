# Nazareno Orlandi (Nazzareno Orlandi), 1861-1952
*Research report, 2026-08-14. Italian-born decorative painter, naturalized Argentine; Ascoli Piceno to Buenos Aires. Slug: `nazareno_orlandi`.*

**Legend.** **[A]** = attested, with the source named. **[R]** = reconstruction, tradition, single-source claim, or inference. Contradictions between sources are printed as contradictions and are **not** adjudicated. Sources that could not be reached are named with the reason.

**The shape of the problem.** Orlandi is documented almost entirely by his own encyclopedia entries. Three articles carry him (es., en., it. Wikipedia), each with a work list, and the lists agree neither with each other nor with the articles on the buildings. In nearly every case the biography names the building and the building does not name the biography. The single exception, the Teatro Municipal of Santa Fe, is worth more than the rest of the list combined. Two dates for his arrival, ten years apart, are both live; two dates for the Cordoba cathedral nave; one commission dated eight years before its building existed. None of it is resolved here.

---

## 1. Ascoli Piceno and the Italian formation, 1861-1885

- Born **29 May 1861 at Ascoli Piceno**, in the Marche. **[A: en.wikipedia, 'Nazareno Orlandi', citing Angelo de Gubernatis, *Dizionario degli Artisti Italiani Viventi* (1889), p. 337].** es.wikipedia gives only "Ascoli" without a province; Ascoli Piceno is the reading taken by the English article and is adopted here, with the ambiguity noted. **[R for the province.]**
- Studies **design in his home town** before entering the Academy. **[A: en.wikipedia]**, undated.
- **1880: exterior decoration of the Palazzo Ferri, Ascoli Piceno, with Domenico Ferri.** **[A: it.wikipedia, 'Nazzareno Orlandi']**. His **earliest dated work** and the **only commission in Italy** any source attaches to him. Absent from both the Spanish and English articles.
- **Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence**, the academic course. **[A: en. and es.wikipedia]**. **Year of entry: none given [gap]**; the journey file uses 1881 as a placeholder.
- **Three years of Italian military service**, after which he turns to **military and battle subjects**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**. Regiment, garrison and years: **none given [gap]**, nor whether the service precedes the Academy or interrupts it; the ordering in the journey file is arbitrary and says so.
- **1883: First Prize in the painting courses at Perugia.** **[R: es.wikipedia alone]**; not carried by the English or Italian articles, and no jury, competition title or month is given.
- **1885: graduates from the Accademia in Florence.** **[R: es.wikipedia alone]**; the other two articles state the Florentine training without a year.

## 2. Exhibition, criticism, and the dictionary, 1887-1889

- **1887, Venice: exhibits 'Pro patria' and 'Mi ama, non mi ama?', plus a third canvas.** **[A for the first two titles, en. and it.wikipedia agree]**. **CONTRADICTION, unresolved:** the third title is **'Sana'** in en.wikipedia and **'Pensieri profani'** in it.wikipedia. Both are printed in the journey file's campa; neither is preferred. The exhibition catalogue would settle it and was not consulted.
- In the same period he works as an **art critic**. **[A: en.wikipedia]**, undated beyond the 1887 context.
- **1889: entry in Angelo de Gubernatis, *Dizionario degli Artisti Italiani Viventi: pittori, scultori, e architetti*, Tipi dei Successori Le Monnier, Florence, p. 337.** **[A: the volume itself, cited by en.wikipedia]**. The page was **not read** **[gap]**; it would give his Italian address and exhibition list, and it is the volume later biographies lean on for his birth data.

## 3. The crossing: 1889 or 1899

This is the central contradiction of the life and it is **not resolved**.

- **es.wikipedia:** in **1889** Orlandi travels to Buenos Aires **at the instance of the architect Francisco Tamburini** and at the request of the Argentine government under **Miguel Juarez Celman**, to work on **renovations of the Casa de Gobierno**; in **1890** he settles permanently. **[R: single source.]**
- **it.wikipedia:** says only that **"dal 1889 ricevette commissioni dal governo argentino"** and that **"si stabili a Buenos Aires nel 1899"**. No Tamburini, no Casa Rosada. **[A for the wording; the 1899 settlement is [A] in this source.]**
- **buenosaires.gob.ar, 'Murales de Buenos Aires', ficha avsantafe1860:** relocation to Argentina **in 1899**, after being contracted by the Argentine government. **[A for this source's statement.]**
- **es.wikipedia, 'Casa Rosada':** documents Tamburini's unification project **from 1886** (east facade by 1898) and credits the **Salon Blanco ceiling of 1910 to Luigi De Servi**. **It never mentions Orlandi.** **[A for the silence.]**

**Net position.** *Commissions from the Argentine government beginning 1889* is carried by two sources and is the safer claim. *Permanent settlement in 1899* has two independent sources against es.wikipedia's 1890 and is the better-supported reading. *The Tamburini summons for the Casa Rosada* rests on es.wikipedia alone and is met by silence in the building's own article; it is **[R]** throughout the journey file.

**Corroborated by-product.** **Francisco Tamburini was born in Ascoli Piceno in 1846 and died in Buenos Aires in 1890** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Francisco Tamburini']**. Architect and painter came from the same small town, fifteen years apart; no source consulted remarks on it. **CONTRADICTION:** other listings give his death as **1891**.

**Port of embarkation: [R], reconstruction only.** Genoa is the standard emigrant quay of the period; no manifest, vessel or day is attested for Orlandi, and `date_confidence` says so. The landing pin is the Retiro immigration ground (-34.5958, -58.3648), not the pool's generic dockside point.

## 4. Buenos Aires, the first decade: 1890-1899

- **1890: decoration of the Iglesia del Salvador, Buenos Aires (Callao and Tucuman, Balvanera).** **[R: es. and en.wikipedia, plus the blog 'Historia del Mural Argentino'; CONTESTED]**. it.wikipedia's work list **omits the church entirely** and gives 1890 to the ex-Palazzo della Stampa instead. **The church has no Wikipedia article of its own** (two titles tried, both 404) **[gap: no cross-check possible]**. Nominatim resolves Av. Callao 542 as "Colegio del Salvador", so church and college share one pin.
- **Salon Dorado of the Diario La Prensa building, Av. de Mayo 575, today the Casa de la Cultura.** **[A that he decorated it, es.wikipedia, plus a photograph captioned as his in 'Historia del Mural Argentino']**. **CONTRADICTION, unresolved:** it.wikipedia dates the ex-Palazzo della Stampa work to **1890**, but the building (architects **Carlos Agote and Alberto Gainza**) was **completed only in 1898** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Casa de la Cultura (Buenos Aires)']**. Either the year is wrong or it belongs to another commission. That article describes the Salon Dorado's gilt carving, mirrors, Gobelins tapestries and Athena-with-seven-muses ceiling and **names no decorator at all** **[gap, not a denial]**.
- **Teatro Rivera Indarte, Cordoba.** **[R: es.wikipedia, which itself hedges with "se le atribuye"]**, pairing Orlandi with Tamburini and a painter it calls **"Gonzaga Coni"**. **CORRECTION:** the decorator documented there is **Arturo Nembrini Gonzaga** (facade reliefs, attic quadriga, Pompeian vault); architects **Tamburini and Giuseppe Franceschi**; inaugurated **26 April 1891** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Teatro del Libertador General San Martin']**, an article that **does not mention Orlandi**. Both readings are printed in the campa.
- **1893: gold medal, World's Columbian Exposition, Chicago.** **[A: es. and en.wikipedia]**, year only. **What he exhibited, in what class, and whether the work was made in Italy or Argentina: unknown [gap].**
- **1897: honorable mention, Venice Biennale (second edition).** **[A: es. and en.wikipedia]**. No catalogue checked **[gap]**.
- **Naturalized Argentine.** **[A that it happened, es.wikipedia]**; **year, court and office: given by no source [gap]**. The journey file uses 1900-01-01 as an explicit placeholder.

## 5. The domes: 1901-1904

- **Old Biblioteca Nacional, Calle Mexico 564** (architect **Carlos Morra**, inaugurated **1901**). **[R: it.wikipedia only]**; no Spanish source carries it and the library's own history does not name him **[gap]**. The pin is the street address, approximate.
- **1903: cupola of the Iglesia de San Telmo (Nuestra Senora de Belen).** **[A: es. and en.wikipedia; it.wikipedia groups it in the same year]**.
- **1903: dome of the Teatro La Comedia.** **[A: es.wikipedia; it.wikipedia same group]**. Pin is the Rodriguez Pena street address, approximate; **no photograph of the ceiling located [gap]**.
- **1903: Circolo Italiano.** **[A: it.wikipedia, same 1903 group]**. No description of the work in any source reached **[gap]**.
- **1904: Teatro Municipal de Santa Fe (today the Teatro Municipal 1 de Mayo).** **[A, and the strongest item in the whole dossier]**. The theatre's own article states: *"en el interior se destaca un lienzo anular pintado por Nazareno Orlandi"* **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Teatro Municipal 1 de Mayo']**. Architect **Augusto Plou**, built **1903-1905**, inaugurated **5 October 1905**. 1904 falls inside the campaign and is chronologically sound. **This is the only commission where the building names the painter rather than the painter naming the building.**
- **1904: sketches for the decoration of the Cathedral of Cordoba with Emilio Caraffa and Carlos Camilloni.** **[R: es.wikipedia; CONTRADICTED]**. The cathedral's own article gives **Caraffa 1900-1914**, **Camilloni with Manuel Cardenosa in the 1920s**, the dome to **Fray Vicente Munoz**, and **never names Orlandi** **[A: es.wikipedia, 'Catedral de Cordoba (Argentina)']**, so a joint 1904 campaign of the three is unsupported. it.wikipedia dates his nave work to **1916** instead. **Two years for one nave; both entered, neither confirmed.**

## 6. Honors and the great commissions: 1907-1916

- **Teatro Colon, the boca del escenario (proscenium arch), with Domingo Fontana.** **[A that the biography specifies it, es.wikipedia; it.wikipedia independently lists the Colon among his works]**. **es.wikipedia, 'Teatro Colon', is silent on both men**, naming **Marcel Jambon** and, for the 1966 cupola, **Raul Soldi** **[gap, one-sided silence]**. **Undated** in every source; the journey file anchors it at 1907, before the 25 May 1908 inauguration. **Building identity is NOT ambiguous:** "boca del escenario" can only mean the second Colon on Cerrito (**-34.6011, -58.3831**, construction 1888-1908), not the demolished 1857 house on Plaza de Mayo at which the pool's coordinates pointed.
- **Ceilings of the old Bolsa de Comercio, Calle San Lorenzo, since demolished.** **[A: es.wikipedia]**. **Undated, and no located address survives [gap]**, so the pin falls back to the canonical Buenos Aires point.
- **1910: honorary academician of the Accademia di Belle Arti, Florence.** **[A: es. and en.wikipedia]**, year only; registers unchecked **[gap]**. **No source documents any return voyage to Italy after his emigration [gap].**
- **1910: the Sociedad Italiana asks his help organizing events for the Italian pavilion at the Exposicion Internacional del Centenario.** **[R: es.wikipedia alone]**; no exposition document consulted, pavilion pin approximate.
- **1916: Palacio Paz (later the Circulo Militar), Av. Santa Fe 750.** **[A: it.wikipedia dates it 1916; the municipal 'Murales de Buenos Aires' page also credits him there]**. **The apparent contradiction with the palace's own article dissolves once the date is fixed:** the palace was built **1902-1914** (**Louis-Marie Henri Sortais**), so 1916 post-dates construction, and the artists that article names (**Carpeaux, Larche, Cullen Ayerza, Detaille, Fromentin, Ulpiano Checa**) are **sculptors and easel painters, not mural decorators [A for the non-exclusion; the silence remains a gap]**. The **Circulo Militar bought the palace only in June 1938**, so the pool's club framing was dropped as anachronistic.
- **1916: Escuela Normal Mariano Acosta.** **[A: es.wikipedia (undated) and 'Historia del Mural Argentino' as Colegio Mariano Acosta; year from it.wikipedia's 1916 group]**.
- **1916: nave of the Cathedral of Cordoba.** **[R: it.wikipedia]**, against es.wikipedia's 1904. See section 5.

## 7. Late work, prize, death: 1919-1952

- **1919: the dome of the Teatro Gran Splendid, Av. Santa Fe 1860 (today El Ateneo Grand Splendid).** **[A, and the best-described work in the whole life]**. es.wikipedia, 'El Ateneo Grand Splendid': **20 m x 19 m x 3.65 m, oil, 1919, an allegory of peace after the First World War**, with a female figure among flowers, clouds, doves, angels and nymphs, **plus a second female figure holding a film projector**. Architects **Pero y Torres Armengol** (the pool's "Rafael Peru" is a misreading; both articles give **Pero**); the theatre **opened 14 May 1919**, i.e. after the war, not before it as the pool's campa claimed.
- **1927: First Prize at the 3rd Exposicion Comunal de Artes Aplicadas e Industriales, Buenos Aires**, opened **3 December 1927**, closed **12 February 1928**, run by the Comision Municipal de Fiestas Populares. **[A: the exhibition's own *Memoria*, Peuser, 1928, cited by es.wikipedia]**. **Venue not named [gap]**, so the canonical Buenos Aires pin is used.
- **1952: dies in Buenos Aires, aged 91.** **[A: es., en. and it.wikipedia independently]**, consistent with a birth on 29 May 1861. **No day, no month, no obituary traced [gap].** The pool's flagging of this as an unverified summary needing urgent re-verification was **wrong**: three independent encyclopedias carry it.

## 8. Afterlife: a documented absence

**No tomb, no funerary monument, no cemetery register, no legend, no rediscovery event** (no exhibition in his name, no biography, no restoration campaign named for him) is recorded in **es., en. or it.wikipedia**, in the **buenosaires.gob.ar heritage pages**, or in general web search. **[A as an absence, stated as a gap rather than filled.]** His works remain standing and cited in heritage listings; the man leaves no address in the ground. The raw pool asserted this absence while having **never read the Italian article**, which supplies four items missing from every other account (Palazzo Ferri 1880, the old Biblioteca Nacional, the 1916 grouping, the 1899 settlement).

## Interlocks

Named in the campas because Orlandi has a **direct relation** to each, not because of shared geography:

- **`francesco_tamburini`** (queued). Born Ascoli Piceno 1846, Orlandi's own town; named by es.wikipedia as the architect at whose instance he came to Argentina; architect of the Teatro Rivera Indarte, whose decoration is attributed to Orlandi; first architect of the Teatro Colon, where Orlandi worked the proscenium.
- **`max_glucksmann`** (queued). Commissioned the Teatro Gran Splendid (1919) whose dome Orlandi painted. Patron relation, direct.
- **`troiano_troiani`** (researched). Supplied the sculptural ensemble for the same Gran Splendid program, same building, same year. His file already names Orlandi and the dome; the link is now reciprocated.

Deliberately **not** named, despite overlap of place and period: `mario_palanti`, `agusti_querol_subirats`, `julio_argentino_roca`, `alvear`, `eva_peron`, `alfredo_segatori`, the urban-planning cluster, `vittorio_meano`, `jules_dormal`, `francisco_gianotti`. None has an attested relation to Orlandi himself.

Grep across `working/journeys/*.journey.json` confirms the gap: apart from `troiano_troiani.journey.json` and the QUEUE.md entries, **no file mentions Nazareno Orlandi, the Iglesia del Salvador, the Salon Dorado, the Casa de Cultura, or the Circulo Militar.**
