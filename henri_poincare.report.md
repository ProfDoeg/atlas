# Henri Poincaré (1854-1912): research report

*Compiled 2026-08-29 for `henri_poincare.journey.json`. Born Nancy 29 April 1854, died Paris 17 July 1912: mathematician, mathematical physicist, philosopher of science, and without interruption for thirty-three years an engineer of the Corps des mines.*

Legend: **[A]** attested, source named · **[R]** reconstruction, tradition, or uncorroborated first-person recollection. Pool errors are corrected here and in the relevant stop's `date_confidence`. Contradictions are flagged, not adjudicated.

---

## 1. Nancy, 1854-1873

Birth 29 April 1854, elder child of the physician Léon Poincaré, who taught in the medical faculty, and Eugénie Launois. **[A: MacTutor; Académie française notice]**

**Birthplace coordinate corrected.** The pool gave 48.6934 / 6.1817 and called the quarter "Cité Ducale". Lebon (Gutenberg 32948) puts the house facing the ducal palace, pinned by fr.wikipedia at 48.69694 / 6.18028; the pool's point is about 400 m too far south. The palace and the rue de Guise stand in the **Ville-Vieille**, and "Cité ducale" is en.wikipedia's wording, not reproduced. The street number usually given, 2 rue de Guise, is **[R]**.

**The illness, tag upgraded.** The pool tagged it [R] from "dossier tradition". fr.wikipedia states he contracted **diphtheria at five (1859)** with a paralysis of about **five months**, not merely "temporary" **[A]**. The mother's bedside teaching remains **[R]**.

**Lycée de Nancy, entered 1862, eleven years** **[A: MacTutor; SEP]**. Coordinate corrected from 48.6903 / 6.1796 to the school's own 48.6911 / 6.1783 (fr.wikipedia; 2 rue de la Visitation). Renamed **Lycée Henri-Poincaré in 1913** **[A]**. Both baccalaureates 1871 **[A: SEP]**.

**Franco-Prussian War, 1870, ambulance work with his father** **[A: Times obituary, "He served for a time in the Ambulance Corps"]**. No month, no station, no first-person account anywhere reached.

## 2. The schools of the state, 1873-1879

**École polytechnique 1873, first in the entrance competition; graduated 1875 ranked second** **[A: MacTutor; Académie française]**; the attribution of the drop to weak draughtsmanship is the pool's geography lens, not a graded record **[R]**. First paper, on the indicatrix, 1874 **[R]**, no stop written. **École des mines, November 1875 to June 1878** **[A: en.wikipedia, quoting the dates]**. **Thesis defended 1 August 1879**, jury including Darboux and Hermite **[A: MacTutor; Sorbonne record 1607]**; Darboux's remark that it held material for several dissertations is a reported judgement **[R]**. **Vesoul, March to about August 1879** **[A: Times obituary; Annales des mines]**.

**Magny: date and death toll both corrected.** The pool said **eighteen** dead in **August 1879**, following en.wikipedia. Both French sources give **sixteen** dead on **1 September 1879**: annales.org ("une explosion de grisou avait fait 16 victimes") and fr.wikipedia ("1er septembre 1879 dans le Puits du Magny, causant la mort de seize mineurs"). en.wikipedia is the weaker witness on both counts. That Poincaré went down the shaft and ran the inquiry is **[A: Annales des mines]**.

## 3. Caen, 1879-1881: the Fuchsian functions

Appointment as junior lecturer in mathematical analysis, December 1879 **[A: MacTutor; en.wikipedia; SEP]**.

**Dating of the Fuchsian work corrected.** The pool dated the breakthrough to **1881**, too late. It belongs to **1880**, the year of the Grand Prix memoir and its three supplements; the mature papers appeared in **Acta Mathematica in 1882** (en.wikipedia, "Fuchsian group"). The journey recasts the sequence as 1880-1882, anchored at Caen **[A]**.

**The two invention anecdotes.** Both quotations are verified verbatim in Halsted's *The Foundations of Science*, Gutenberg 39713. The **text** is [A]; the **events** are first-person recollection published nearly thirty years later, with no witness and no date, so the dating is **[R]**. The pool dated the omnibus "c. 1881"; it belongs with the Caen excursion and the 1880 supplements, and the journey uses 1880-06-01 as an ordering device.

**Marriage, 20 April 1881: date agreed, place contradicted, contradiction left standing.** en.wikipedia puts the wedding at **Caen**, where they met; the pool's chronology lens gives **Paris** the same day, from SEP. Caen is the pin, as the reading independently checked, and the conflict is recorded in the stop.

**Klein.** The race by post through 1881-82 over uniformization, and the strain feeding Klein's autumn 1882 breakdown, is **[A: MacTutor's Klein biography]**, already narrated from Klein's side in `felix_klein.journey.json`. The letter count in the campa is approximate **[R]**, and the June 1881 opening is an ordering device.

## 4. Paris, 1881-1890: the chairs, and King Oscar's prize

Maître de conférences d'analyse, October 1881 **[A: SEP; MacTutor]**. Analysis at the École polytechnique from 1883 **[A]**, tenure to 1897 from the pool's interlock lens **[R]**. Chair of mathematical physics and probability, succeeding Lippmann, 1886 **[A: SEP]**. Académie des sciences, geometry section, **31 January 1887, aged 32** **[A: Times obituary]**, the Times giving 33 and being wrong; president 1906 **[A]**. **The pool's superlative is dropped**: "the only member ever associated with every mathematical and physical section" is confirmed by nothing reached.

**The prize was received in Paris, not Stockholm: the largest geographic correction in the file.** The pool placed the 1889 award at Stockholm while admitting no source put him there. Per actamathematica.org/library/prize-competition the result was announced **20 January 1889**, the day before Oscar II's sixtieth birthday, and "he received his prize from the Swedish ambassador in **Paris in March 1889**." The Stockholm pin is removed, not kept with a caveat **[A]**. The competition was announced in *Acta Mathematica* in **1885**, not 1887 as MacTutor implies.

**Phragmén did not find the error. Poincaré did.** The inversion appears in **three** of the pool's lenses and is corrected in all three. Phragmén's persistent questions about the convergence of infinite series prompted Poincaré to re-examine the memoir; **Poincaré** found the mistake, his assumption that the asymptotic surfaces were closed, and telegraphed Mittag-Leffler to stop the presses on **30 November 1889**. The recall cost **over 3,500 kronor against a prize of 2,500**; the corrected memoir appeared **December 1890**, *Acta Mathematica* vol. 13; **two copies** of the first printing survive at the Institut Mittag-Leffler, one annotated "The whole edition was destroyed. M.L." **[A throughout]** Recurrence theorem 1890 **[A]**.

## 5. 1893-1903: longitudes, topology, the measure of time

Bureau des longitudes and promotion to chief engineer, 1893 **[A: Académie française]**. **"Analysis Situs", 1895**, five supplements to 1904 **[A: MacTutor]**, the fifth replacing a false homological claim with the question later called the Poincaré conjecture **[A]**. "La mesure du temps", 1898 **[A: SEP; IEP]**. RAS Gold Medal 1900 **[A: RAS list]**. Lecturing on theoretical electricity at the postal and telegraph school from 1902 **[R: IEP]**, folded into the Polytechnique stop.

**The 1900 Paris congress corrected.** The pool had him lecturing there on "the future of mathematics"; that address belongs to the **1908 Rome** congress. Lebon records him as "Président du Congrès des Mathématiciens tenu à Paris du 6 au 12 août 1900", his address being on **intuition and logic** **[A]**. That Hilbert read his problems at the same congress **appears in no source fetched here**, and the stop says so.

**Honorary doctorates: documented dates, undocumented presence.** Lebon lists Cambridge 12 June 1900, Christiania 6 September 1902, Kolozsvár 8 January 1903, Oxford 24 June 1903, Glasgow 23 April 1907, Brussels 19 November 1909, Stockholm 7 December 1909, Berlin 12 October 1910. **Not one carries evidence that he attended.** Rather than stage eight foreign journeys he may never have made, the file carries a **single** stop, Cambridge, naming the whole list and stating that attendance is unconfirmed **[A dates; R presence]**.

## 6. The Dreyfus case, 1898-1906

January 1898 petition for judicial review **[R: Edinburgh dossier, webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/dreyfus.htm; the pool itself tags this R]**. **1899 letter to the Conseil de guerre at Rennes** **[A: fr.wikipedia, "En 1899, Henri Poincaré adresse une lettre au Conseil de guerre de Rennes"]**. **1904 expert report with Darboux and Appell** for the Cour de cassation review, dismantling Bertillon **[A: Edinburgh dossier]**. 12 July 1906, conviction annulled and Dreyfus reinstated **[A: same, and `captain_alfred_dreyfus.journey.json`]**.

**Two pool sub-claims dropped.** That **Paul Painlevé** read the 1899 letter into the record at Rennes, which nothing reached confirms; and the Edinburgh dossier as source for that letter, since it **was fetched and says nothing about 1899, Painlevé, or Bertillon** in connection with Poincaré. The letter is re-cited to fr.wikipedia.

**House rule applied.** Rennes and the Cour de cassation session of 12 July 1906 are **not** Poincaré stops: he did not go to Rennes, and the 1906 hearing belongs to those who were in it, Dreyfus's own file carrying it. His authorship acts stay in Paris, and 1906 appears only as the closing fact of his 1904 stop.

## 7. Relativity, 1902-1906

*La Science et l'Hypothèse*, 1902 **[A: SEP]**, English translation 1905 by W. J. Greenstreet with a preface by Joseph Larmor **[A: Internet Archive metadata]**. "Sur la dynamique de l'électron", *Comptes rendus* 140 (1905), 1504-1508, presented **5 June 1905** **[A]**. The long memoir received at Palermo **23 July 1905**, printed *Rendiconti* 21 (1906), 129-176 **[A: arxiv.org/pdf/1710.00706]**.

**St. Louis date corrected, two papers un-conflated.** The pool dated the relativity address 17 September 1904 from Lebon, whose 17 September entry reads "Communication faite le 17 septembre 1904 au Congrès tenu à Saint-Louis **par la Société mathématique américaine**" and is a **different paper**, on geodesic lines of convex surfaces. "The Principles of Mathematical Physics" went to the **International Congress of Arts and Science**, 19-25 September 1904, conventionally dated **24 September**, which the journey uses **[A]**.

**Priority left open in both directions.** The 1904 address and the 5 June 1905 note both precede Einstein's paper, received at *Annalen der Physik* on 30 June 1905; that chronology is **[A]**. What follows from it is **not established**: no source reached shows Einstein had read the note. The journey states the twenty-five-day gap as a dated fact and stops there.

## 8. Honours, the last congress, death

Académie française, **fauteuil 24** (Sully-Prudhomme's seat), elected **5 March 1908**, second ballot, 17 of 33 votes against Charles de Pomairols and Jean Aicard; received **28 January 1909**, reply by Frédéric Masson **[A: Académie française, which also records that he began reading his discourse before the session opened]**. *Science et Méthode*, 1908 **[A: SEP]**. **Inspector general, Corps des mines, 1910**: pool tagged [R], **upgraded to [A]** on en.wikipedia's career list; fr.wikipedia is silent, a gap and not a contradiction. **First Solvay Conference**, Hotel Metropole, Brussels, **30 October to 3 November 1911**, Lorentz in the chair, Poincaré, Einstein, Curie and Planck present **[A: en.wikipedia; Comptes rendus retrospective]**, with the follow-up "Sur la théorie des quanta" **[A]**. **Death 17 July 1912**, embolism after prostate surgery, while dressing, at 63 rue Claude-Bernard **[A: Times obituary; fr.wikipedia for the address]**. **Funeral 19 July 1912 at Saint-Jacques-du-Haut-Pas**, burial in the family vault, section 16, Montparnasse: pool tagged [R], **upgraded** and the vague "July 1912" replaced by the day, on fr.wikipedia plus the Times obituary of the 17th calling it "next Friday".

**Four coordinates corrected.** Death address 48.8402 / 2.3455, reconciling the geography lens's 48.841 / 2.3428 and the afterlife lens's 48.8407 / 2.3457 for the **same building**; Saint-Jacques-du-Haut-Pas 48.8434 / 2.3420, some 200 m off in the pool; Montparnasse 48.8384 / 2.3282, the afterlife lens's value lying 320 m north of the cemetery; the Institut 48.8459 / 2.3440.

At the funeral the Times names the **Prime Minister, Raymond Poincaré, his first cousin**, the President of the Senate, most of the ministry, both academies, the Prince of Monaco, the sons of the Bey of Tunis, and Royal Society delegates **[A]**.

## 9. Afterlife carried, and afterlife left out

**Carried**, all posthumous facts about Poincaré himself: the **Carlier bronze and lycée renaming, Nancy 1913** **[A: fr.wikipedia]**, the subscription among alumni of Nancy, Colmar, Metz and Strasbourg being **[R]**, so the campa says "said to have been raised"; the **Institut Henri Poincaré, inaugurated 17 November 1928**, Borel founder and first director, Perrin and Langevin on the committee, Rockefeller and Rothschild money, placed "under the patronage of the name of Henri Poincaré" **[A: ihp.fr/en/history, every claim confirmed]**; the **lunar basin, named 1970** **[A]**; the **2004 Panthéon proposal, not enacted** **[A: en.wikipedia only]**, named without the minister since the only witness is a summary; and the **2003 resolution of the conjecture**.

**Left out.** Asteroid 2021 Poincaré, found by L. Boyer at Algiers Observatory in 1936 **[A]**: the discovery is Boyer's, so no stop. The "Poincaré discovered chaos in 1890" legend **[R]**: the journey narrates the episode and makes no chaos-theory claim.

## Apparatus relations for the operator

Poincaré is **not** listed in `EXCEPTIONS.md`, so nothing rooted outside his own life is licensed as a stop. Per that file's adversary's duty, the relations found are recorded rather than deleted, with the direction named.

1. **Edward Witten and the Henri Poincaré Prize, 2006. Direction: FORWARD.** Established 1997 by the IAMP and the Institut, awarded 2006 at the International Congress on Mathematical Physics in **Rio de Janeiro**, shared by Witten, Faddeev and Ruelle **[A: en.wikipedia]**; `edward_witten.journey.json` carries its own stop. Witten was born in 1951 and there is no biographical contact. Naming him or his slug here would write a 2006 hash into a 1912 block, so **no such reference was written**, and none should be added without the author's say-so.
2. **The Institut as archival citation elsewhere. Direction: FORWARD.** `alexandre_dumas.journey.json` sources Galois banquet material to galois.ihp.fr. The Institut is a 1928 foundation carrying the name posthumously, not a contact. This file has a stop for its **founding** and carries **no** pointer to Dumas, Galois, or their files. Perelman (2002-2010) is forward too, and harmless: not an atlas subject, so no slug to embed.
3. **Henri Bergson: checked and ruled out.** Elected February 1914, received 1918; Poincaré died July 1912. **No overlap as sitting academicians**, no joint scene staged.
4. **Cousin conflation, flagged twice in the pool.** `etienne_jules_marey.journey.json` ("Boulogne, the ashes and Poincaré", 1914) and `georges_hebert.journey.json` ("Reims, Poincaré inaugurates the college", 1913) both concern **Raymond** Poincaré and both postdate Henri's death. Neither is used. Raymond appears here once, correctly, at the funeral.
5. **Shared geography deliberately not name-dropped.** Cauchy and Galois at the same rue Descartes Polytechnique; Marie Curie at the same Sorbonne. The **canonical coordinates are inherited byte-identically** (Polytechnique 48.8455 / 2.3477; Sorbonne 48.8496 / 2.3442), and **no name is dropped in the campa**.

**Names that do appear in campas, each a real relation.** Felix Klein, correspondent and rival 1881-82. Marie Curie and Albert Einstein, co-participants at the twenty-four-person conference he attended in Brussels in 1911, Einstein's own file naming Poincaré in that room. Alfred Dreyfus, in whose case Poincaré signed the 1898 petition, wrote to the Rennes council of war, and co-authored the 1904 report. Einstein's reading of *La Science et l'Hypothèse* in Bern is one-directional and is carried as a **`suggested_ref`** on Poincaré's own stop for the book, never as a scene.

## Honest gaps

- **The inner life is missing.** No letters of his own were reached. The two most intimate passages in the file, the coffee and the omnibus, are published self-report from 1908 about 1880, addressed to a psychological society. The marriage is likewise a blank: date agreed, town contradicted, four children attested, nothing else, and Louise survives to 1934 leaving no reachable voice. So is 1870, where he carried stretchers at sixteen in an occupied city and not one sentence from him about it was fetched.
- **The Klein correspondence**: neither its exact extent nor its content was reached; the campa's figure is approximate and flagged.
- **Attendance at seven of eight honorary-degree ceremonies is unknown**, so his real travel map outside France may be much smaller or much larger than this file shows.
- **Barrow-Green (1997)**, **Galison (2003)**, and *Le Monde* on the Panthéon proposal were not read directly.
