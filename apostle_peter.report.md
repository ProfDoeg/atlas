# Apostle Peter

*2026-08-29. Simon bar Jonah of Bethsaida, called Cephas (Aramaic kepha, rock), called Peter. Fisherman; first-named disciple in every list of the Twelve; first named resurrection witness in the earliest Christian formula; the only apostle Paul is on record accusing to his face; founder-martyr of the Roman church.*

**Legend.** **[A]** attested, source named. **[R]** reconstruction or tradition. The tag attaches to the *claim*, not the source: a late text can attest **[A]** that a tradition existed by its own date while the first-century claim under it stays **[R]**.

**Structural note.** Peter's life divides at Antioch. Before it, Paul's undisputed letters give a contemporary firsthand hostile witness who ate at his table. After it, including the whole Roman phase on which the papacy rests, every source is thirty years to three centuries late.

---

## 1. Galilee: the fisherman and the name (c. 25 to 28)

- Hometown **Bethsaida**, the town of Andrew, Peter, and Philip **[A, John 1:44]**. Which mound it is remains unsettled: **el-Araj** (pinned at 32.8933, 35.6192) has produced a Byzantine church and a 2022 mosaic invoking Peter as "chief and commander of the heavenly apostles"; **et-Tell** (32.9103, 35.6306) is the older rival **[R]**.
- Married, with a household at **Capernaum [A, Mark 1:29-31 and parallels]**, corroborated much earlier by Paul's reference to Cephas's believing wife **[A, 1 Corinthians 9:5]**. One of very few Gospel details about Peter that a contemporary letter confirms.
- The excavated **house of Peter**: a first-century basalt courtyard dwelling replastered for communal use by the later first century, with a fifth-century octagonal church built so its octagon sits over those walls **[A]**. That it is *Peter's own* house is the excavators' inference **[R]**.

### Contradiction flagged, not resolved: three callings, two namings

| Account | Setting | Naming |
|---|---|---|
| Mark 1:16-20 (Matthew 4:18-22) **[A]** | nets on the shore near Capernaum | none here |
| Luke 5:1-11 **[A]** | teaching from Simon's boat, the miraculous catch | none here |
| John 1:35-42 **[A]** | Andrew brings Simon to Jesus at the Jordan | **at first meeting**: "You are to be called Cephas" |
| Matthew 16:17-19 **[A]** | Caesarea Philippi, years later, after the confession | **here**, with the keys |

Not harmonized. The file carries all four as separate stops, with the divergence stated in each `date_confidence`; the consecutive placeholder dates on the Mark and Luke stops assert no reconciliation.

**The keys are Matthew's alone.** Mark 8:29-30 has the confession and stops; Luke 9:20 abbreviates further. Matthew 16:18-19 is the sole source for "on this rock I will build my church," the keys, and binding and loosing **[A as text]**. Every later claim of Petrine primacy rests on a passage that one Gospel has and two parallel accounts of the same scene do not.

## 2. The inner circle, the denial, the restoration (c. 29 to 30)

- Peter, James, and John form an inner three: Jairus's daughter (Mark 5:37), the Transfiguration (Mark 9:2), Gethsemane (Mark 14:33) **[A across all three Synoptics]**. The Gospels name no mountain; **Mount Tabor** is **Origen's** third-century identification, which became the pilgrimage standard though a fortified town stood on the summit in the first century **[R for the site]**.
- Walking on the water is **Matthew 14:22-33 alone**; Mark 6:45-52 and John 6:16-21 have the crossing without it **[A, divergence flagged]**.
- **The sword in Gethsemane.** All four Gospels report the blow that takes off the high priest's slave's right ear. **Only John names Peter as the assailant, and only John names the slave Malchus [A, John 18:10; divergence flagged]**.
- **The denial** is the one Peter episode all four Gospels carry, agreeing on the triple denial and diverging on questioners and staging **[A]**. St Peter in Gallicantu is a pilgrimage identification attested from the fourth-century Bordeaux Pilgrim **[R for the site]**.
- **"He appeared to Cephas."** 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 relays a formula Paul says he *received*, so older than its mid-50s letter. The **earliest surviving claim of a resurrection appearance to Peter**, with his name first in the list **[A]**. Paul gives no location; the Jerusalem placement is inferred from Luke 24:34 and flagged **[R]**.
- **John 21** stages the restoration: the catch, the fire, three questions answering three denials **[A as text]**. The Mensa Christi rock at Tabgha is later devotion **[R]**.

## 3. Jerusalem and the coast: the Acts phase (c. 30 to 44)

Everything here rests on **Acts alone** except where noted. Acts is Luke's composition, the speeches are not transcripts, and no episode has external corroboration. Stated once here and in each stop's `date_confidence`.

- **Matthias** (Acts 1:15-26); the **Pentecost sermon** (Acts 2:14-41), whose three thousand baptisms are Luke's figure; **the lame man, Solomon's Portico, the Sanhedrin hearing** (Acts 3:1 to 4:22), then a second arrest and flogging (Acts 5:17-42). All **[A to Acts]**, none corroborated.
- **Ananias and Sapphira** (Acts 5:1-11) **[A to Acts]**. Acts frames the deaths as divine judgment, not as an execution Peter carries out. No external record.
- **Simon Magus** (Acts 8:14-24), the transaction behind the word *simony* **[A to Acts]**. Acts does not name the Samaritan city; **Sebaste** is conventional **[R]**. The later apocryphal expansion, in which the two contest in *Rome*, is a clear escalation between sources that **Eusebius (CH 2.13-14) does not flag [R]**.
- **Aeneas at Lydda** (Acts 9:32-35) and **Tabitha at Joppa** (Acts 9:36-43) **[A to Acts]**. The seafront "house of Simon the Tanner" is later devotion **[R]**.
- **The sheet, and Cornelius at Caesarea Maritima** (Acts 10:1 to 11:18) **[A to Acts]**, with one crucial independent support: **Paul confirms at Galatians 2:12 that Peter ate with gentiles before the Antioch dispute [A]**. That corroborates the practice; the vision and speeches remain Luke's.

### Correction to the pool: the Agrippa imprisonment is 44, not 42

The pool's chronology lens dated the arrest by **Herod Agrippa I** to `0042` while its own geography lens gave "c. 44 CE" for the same event. Acts 12 ties the arrest to the Passover **immediately preceding Agrippa's death**, which Josephus dates independently (Antiquities 19.343-350) to **44 CE [A]**. The file uses `0044-04-01`. The angelic escape is Acts-only **[R]**.

## 4. Cephas and Paul: the documented decade (c. 36 to 54)

The only stretch with a contemporary firsthand witness, and so the most secure material in the file.

- **Galatians 1:18-19 [A]**: three years after his own call Paul goes up to Jerusalem, stays with Cephas **fifteen days**, and sees no other apostle but James. The earliest independent evidence that Peter was a known, locatable figure in Jerusalem.
- **Galatians 2:1-10 [A]**: James, Cephas, and John, "acknowledged pillars," extend the right hand of fellowship and divide the mission, Paul to the gentiles and Peter to the circumcised.
- **Galatians 2:11-14 [A]**: at Antioch Peter eats with gentile believers, withdraws when men come from James, and Paul opposes him **to his face**. The strongest documented conflict involving Peter anywhere. **Paul records neither Peter's reply nor the outcome**, and I have not invented one.
- **1 Corinthians 1:12 [A]**: a Cephas faction at Corinth in the mid-50s. Proof of reputation, **not of a visit**, which is asserted only c. 170 by Dionysius **[R]**. **1 Corinthians 9:5 [A]**: earliest and only firsthand-era evidence that Peter's **wife travelled with him**.

### Contradiction flagged, not resolved: Galatians 2 against Acts 15

Whether **Acts 15** narrates the same meeting as **Galatians 2:1-10**, reshaped by Luke, or a different one, is genuinely unresolved. The file carries them as two stops, dated three months apart as an ordering device, with the dispute stated in both `date_confidence` fields. The substantive difference: Paul's account of a meeting in these years mentions **no ruling and no list of abstentions**.

### Corrections to the pool: geography tags and one bad pin

Two geography entries carried `tag: "A"` while their own notes graded them R. **Sebaste** names a city Acts never names, so **R for the site, A for the episode**. **Jaffa, "House of Simon the Tanner"** names a devotional identification, so **R for the landmark, A for the Acts 9:36 to 10:16 episode**.

Third, the pool's **Antioch coordinates (36.2076, 36.1601)** are labelled the Cave Church of St Peter but sit about 1.6 km west, in central Antakya; the cave church is at 36.2094, 36.1783. The file uses the corpus's existing **Antioch pin, 36.2047, 36.1817**, for the Galatians 2 dispute, and names the cave church in `date_confidence` and `suggested_refs`.

## 5. Rome: no contemporary witness (c. 42? to 68)

The cumulative case that Peter died at Rome is respectable. The itinerary is not. The file keeps them apart, and every stop from "Rome, the arrival nobody can date" on carries the hedge in `date_confidence`.

**For a final Roman ministry and death**, in order of date:
- **1 Clement 5-6** (c. 96), from the Roman church: Peter endured many labours and "bore his testimony" **[A as text]**. Earliest post-New Testament witness to his death, and it **names no city and no manner of death**. That silence is doing work and is not papered over.
- **Ignatius, To the Romans 4** (c. 110): "I do not, as Peter and Paul, issue commandments unto you" **[A as text]**. Earliest indirect evidence of a Petrine association with Rome; no itinerary, no death details.
- **Dionysius of Corinth** (c. 170, in Eusebius CH 2.25): both taught in Italy and "suffered martyrdom at the same time" **[A that this was said c. 170; R as to the claim]**. **Gaius of Rome** (c. 200, same passage): the "trophies" at the Vatican and on the Ostian way **[A as text]**, earliest pointer to a specific site.
- **Eusebius CH 2.25** (early 4th c.): Peter crucified at Rome under Nero **[A as text; R as history]**. He gives **no year, no day, no order relative to Paul**. The 64 to 68 range is a scholarly bracket, not a source.

**Against a long Roman residency**: no undisputed writing of Peter or Paul says plainly that Peter was in Rome, and **Romans 16 [A]**, written to a church Paul had never seen, greets a long list of named individuals and **does not greet Peter**. That does not rule out a later visit. The **twenty-five-year Roman episcopate** beginning in the 40s comes from Jerome's *On Illustrious Men* and the *Liber Pontificalis*, retrospective late-antique succession lists **[R]**.

### Three pool dates asserted from sources that do not carry them

1. **Arrival under Claudius, pool date 0044, sourced to CH 2.13-14.** Verified at newadvent.org/fathers/250102.htm: **CH 2.13-14 gives no year at all**, only "the reign of Claudius" (41-54). The precision comes from Eusebius's *Chronicle*, followed by Jerome: the **second year of Claudius, i.e. 42**. The file uses `0042-01-01` and names the Chronicle. **[R]**
2. **Mark writes the preaching down, pool date 0045, sourced to CH 2.15.** CH 2.15 supplies **no year**, only the Claudian context inherited from 2.14, so the file's date is an ordering device. The pool's own noted tension stands: **Mark is conventionally dated c. 65-75**. Clement and Eusebius also disagree on whether Peter approved the work. **[R]**
3. **The wife's martyrdom, pool date 0060, source unspecified.** Located: **Clement of Alexandria, Stromata 7.11, at Eusebius CH 3.30**, "The Apostles that were Married." **Eusebius gives no date, no place, and no name for her.** The 0060 is invented and dropped. **[R]**

### The martyrdom details, graded

- **Crucifixion at Rome under Nero**: Eusebius CH 2.25 **[A as text, R as history]**.
- **Head downward, at his own request**: first explicit in the **Acts of Peter** (late 2nd c.), repeated by **Origen** (d. 253) at Eusebius CH 3.1 **[R]**. **Tertullian**, earlier, says only that Peter's passion was "like the Lord's." The most-repeated and least-early detail in the whole tradition.
- **The Circus of Nero** location and the **quo vadis** scene: both Acts of Peter, hagiography, no first-century support **[R]**. Santa Maria in Palmis was rebuilt in 1637 over a site venerated since at least the ninth century; its footprint slab is a copy **[A for the church history]**.
- **The Mamertine imprisonment and the miraculous spring [R]**. The Catholic Encyclopedia itself notes the spring long predates any Christian prisoner and that there is little firsthand account of an imprisonment there; the campa carries that skepticism.
- **1 Peter.** The text is attested **[A]**; the authorship is not. Many critical scholars read it as a Petrine-school or posthumous composition of c. 70-100. "Babylon" for Rome is standard but **itself disputed**, and even granting it, this is textual, not itinerary, evidence.

## 6. The grave, the excavation, the bones

- **Constantine's basilica**, begun 318-322, about forty years in the building **[A]**. The strongest argument in the Roman file is structural, not textual: the site sloped hard over a working pagan necropolis and needed filling, buttressing, and the burial of whole intact mausoleums to get a level floor, all to set an altar over one modest second-century aedicula. **Strong evidence that fourth-century Christians believed the grave was there. No evidence about whose bones are in it.**
- **1939, not 1940.** The necropolis was rediscovered in **1939**, while a tomb was cut for Pius XI, and excavated under **Ludwig Kaas** through 1949; the pool's chronology lens said 1940-1949 while its own afterlife lens said 1939 **[A]**. In **1942** Kaas removed bones from a niche in the graffiti wall **without documentation [A]**.
- **The osteology is not Guarducci's.** **Venerando Correnti** of Palermo examined the fragments in **1962** and concluded they belonged to a robust man of about 60 to 70. **Margherita Guarducci**, an **epigrapher**, built the Petrine identification on that finding by reading the graffiti-wall inscriptions. The pool attributed the osteology to Guarducci; corrected **[A]**. **Antonio Ferrua**, who led the dig, said publicly he was **not convinced [A]**.
- **26 June 1968**: Paul VI announces the relics identified "in a manner we believe convincing," short of scientific certainty **[A for the announcement; R that the bones are Peter's]**. **24 November 2013**: Francis displays nine fragments publicly for the first time **[A]**.
- **San Pietro in Vincoli**, consecrated **439** under Sixtus III to house the chains **[A]**. The pool's "Empress Eudoxia gave chains from Peter's Jerusalem imprisonment to Pope Leo I, around 438-439" conflates two women and mis-dates the gift. Correctly: **Aelia Eudocia** received the chains at Jerusalem from **Juvenal**, probably 438-439; her daughter **Licinia Eudoxia** presented them to **Pope Leo I**, who reigned **from 440**. The fusion legend is **[R]**.
- **Catacombs of San Sebastiano**: third-century calendars record the apostles' memory *in catacumbas* here, conventionally 258; a marble structure may have briefly housed both apostles' relics. **Timing and reason debated [R]**.
- **Two further pool corrections.** The Latin **Codex Vercellensis** does contain the Acts of Peter martyrdom narrative, which Athos and Patmos supplement rather than replace. And the Lightfoot **1 Clement** text at earlychristianwritings.com is corrupt at a line break, printing "endured not one / not one but many labors," swallowing "nor two"; the file quotes **Roberts-Donaldson**.

## Honest gaps

1. **Birth, age, death date.** None attested. The `years` field reads `fl. c. 28 - d. c. 64-68` rather than inventing a birth year; the 0025 Bethsaida stop is an explicit placeholder.
2. **The fifteen days with Paul, and Peter's answer at Antioch.** Neither man records a word of what passed between them in Jerusalem, and whether Peter replied, conceded, or broke with Paul at Antioch is unrecoverable.
3. **His wife's name.** No source preserves it, not Clement, not Eusebius, not 1 Corinthians 9:5.
4. **Between Acts 12 and Galatians 2.** Acts says he "departed to another place" and never says where. Years of silence inside the best-documented decade.
5. **The route to Rome.** No source describes any journey. The tradition gives an arrival and a death with nothing between; there is no Petrine Acts 27.
6. **Whether the bones are his.** Unproved and, since 1942 destroyed the chain of custody, probably unprovable.
